@article
Every substance has **properties**, which are characteristics you can use to identify it. A **physical property** can be observed without changing what the substance is. Color, density, melting point, hardness and whether it dissolves in water are all physical properties. A **chemical property** describes how a substance can change into a different substance. Flammability (the ability to burn) and the tendency to rust are chemical properties.

### Physical changes

In a **physical change** the substance keeps its identity. Only its form, size or state changes. Melting an ice cube, tearing paper, crushing a can, boiling water and dissolving sugar in tea are physical changes. The molecules are the same before and after: liquid water and steam are both $\mathrm{H_2O}$. Many physical changes can be reversed, for example by freezing the water again or evaporating the tea to get the sugar back.

### Chemical changes

In a **chemical change** (a chemical reaction) one or more substances are changed into **new substances** with different properties. Burning wood, rusting iron, baking a cake, digesting food and mixing baking soda with vinegar are chemical changes. The atoms are rearranged into new combinations.

### Clues that a chemical change happened

Look for evidence:

- a gas is produced (bubbles that are not boiling),
- a color change that was not caused by simple mixing,
- a solid forms in a liquid (a **precipitate**),
- heat or light is given off, or the temperature drops,
- a new smell.

No single sign is proof. Boiling water makes bubbles, but they are steam, so the change is physical. Water changing color when you add food coloring is simple mixing. The real test is whether a **new substance** has formed, and that often takes several clues together or a further test.

### Measuring density by displacement

A physical property used to identify a solid is its density. For an irregular object you can find the volume by **displacement**: put the object in water in a graduated cylinder and read how far the level rises. A rise of $1\ \mathrm{mL}$ equals a volume of $1\ \mathrm{cm^3}$. Then divide mass by volume. Because changing the shape of a piece of metal does not change its density, density is a good way to tell metals apart.

@example
title: Classifying a change
problem: A cook leaves a slice of bread in a toaster. It turns brown, gives off a smell and cannot be turned back into soft white bread. Physical or chemical change? Give evidence.
step: Ask what happened to the substances. The bread changed color and smell, two clues of new substances.
step: Check whether it can be undone. The browning cannot be reversed, which also suggests new substances formed.
step: Heat caused the starches and sugars to react and form new compounds.
step: Conclusion: it is a chemical change, supported by the color change, new smell and irreversibility.

@mistakes
- Assuming that every change that cannot be reversed is chemical. Cutting paper cannot be undone, but it is still physical.
- Treating bubbles as proof of a chemical change. Boiling and releasing dissolved gas also make bubbles.
- Thinking that dissolving means the substance disappeared or changed into something new. Dissolved sugar is still sugar.
- Mixing up a property (what a substance is like) with a change (what happens to it).

@practice num
prompt: A metal object has a mass of $150\ \mathrm{g}$. When it is lowered into a graduated cylinder, the water level rises from $40.0\ \mathrm{mL}$ to $100.0\ \mathrm{mL}$. What is the density of the metal in $\mathrm{g/cm^3}$?
answer: 2.5
check: 150 / (100.0 - 40.0)
tol: 1%
unit: g/cm^3
hint: The volume of the object equals the rise in water level.
hint: The volume is $100.0 - 40.0$ milliliters, and $1\ \mathrm{mL} = 1\ \mathrm{cm^3}$.
solution: Volume $= 100.0 - 40.0 = 60.0\ \mathrm{cm^3}$. Density $= 150 \div 60.0 = 2.5\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$.

@quiz num
prompt: A rock has a mass of $84\ \mathrm{g}$. It raises the water level in a graduated cylinder from $50\ \mathrm{mL}$ to $80\ \mathrm{mL}$. What is its density in $\mathrm{g/cm^3}$?
answer: 2.8
check: 84 / (80 - 50)
tol: 1%
unit: g/cm^3
hint: Find the volume from the rise in the water level.
hint: Volume is $80 - 50$ in $\mathrm{cm^3}$; then divide the mass by it.
solution: Volume $= 30\ \mathrm{cm^3}$, so density $= 84 \div 30 = 2.8\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$.
