@article
Cells release the energy stored in food and store it in **ATP**. The overall equation for aerobic respiration of glucose is
$$C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 \longrightarrow 6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \text{energy (ATP and heat)}.$$
Glucose is **oxidized** (loses electrons and hydrogen) and oxygen is **reduced**. The electrons are carried by **NAD$^+$** (becomes NADH) and **FAD** (becomes FADH$_2$). Respiration has four stages.

### 1. Glycolysis (cytosol)

One glucose (6 carbons) is split into two **pyruvate** (3 carbons each). Two ATP are invested and four are made by **substrate-level phosphorylation**, a net gain of **2 ATP**, plus **2 NADH**. No oxygen is required.

### 2. Pyruvate oxidation (mitochondrial matrix)

Each pyruvate loses one carbon as $CO_2$ and becomes **acetyl-CoA**, producing 1 NADH. Per glucose: 2 $CO_2$ and 2 NADH.

### 3. Citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle, matrix)

Acetyl-CoA joins oxaloacetate to make citrate. Each turn releases 2 $CO_2$ and makes 3 NADH, 1 FADH$_2$ and 1 ATP (or GTP). Per glucose (two turns): **4 $CO_2$, 6 NADH, 2 FADH$_2$, 2 ATP**.

### 4. Oxidative phosphorylation (inner mitochondrial membrane)

NADH and FADH$_2$ donate electrons to the **electron transport chain**. As electrons pass along the chain, protons are pumped into the intermembrane space, building a **proton gradient**. **Oxygen is the final electron acceptor**, forming water. Protons flow back through **ATP synthase**, which makes ATP (**chemiosmosis**). Poisons such as cyanide block the chain and stop ATP production.

### Totals

Per glucose: 10 NADH (2 + 2 + 6) and 2 FADH$_2$, and 4 ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation (2 + 2). Common estimates are about 2.5 ATP per NADH and 1.5 per FADH$_2$:
$$10(2.5) + 2(1.5) + 4 = 32 \text{ ATP per glucose}$$
(textbooks quote 30 to 32; the number is an estimate). All six carbons of glucose leave as $CO_2$.

### Without oxygen: fermentation

Glycolysis still makes 2 ATP, but NADH must be recycled to NAD$^+$ or glycolysis stops. **Lactic acid fermentation** (muscle, some bacteria) converts pyruvate to lactate. **Alcohol fermentation** (yeast) converts pyruvate to ethanol and $CO_2$. Fermentation yields only 2 ATP per glucose and produces no ATP from the electron transport chain.

@example
title: Counting carbon and ATP
problem: One glucose is completely oxidized. How many $CO_2$ are released in pyruvate oxidation plus the citric acid cycle, and how much ATP is made if each NADH gives 2.5 ATP and each FADH$_2$ gives 1.5 ATP?
step: Pyruvate oxidation releases 2 $CO_2$ (one per pyruvate); the citric acid cycle releases 4 (two per turn, two turns). Total $2 + 4 = 6$ $CO_2$, accounting for all six carbons.
step: Electron carriers: $2 + 2 + 6 = 10$ NADH and 2 FADH$_2$.
step: ATP from carriers: $10 \times 2.5 + 2 \times 1.5 = 28$.
step: Add substrate-level ATP ($2 + 2 = 4$): $28 + 4 = 32$ ATP.

@mistakes
- Saying oxygen is used in glycolysis or the citric acid cycle; it is consumed only at the end of the electron transport chain.
- Thinking that the $CO_2$ you exhale comes from the electron transport chain; it comes from pyruvate oxidation and the citric acid cycle.
- Forgetting that glycolysis nets 2 ATP (4 made, 2 used).
- Believing fermentation makes more ATP; it makes only the 2 ATP from glycolysis.

@practice mc
prompt: Which molecule is the final electron acceptor in aerobic respiration?
choice: Glucose
choice: NAD$^+$
choice*: Oxygen
choice: Carbon dioxide
hint: What is reduced to form water?
solution: Oxygen accepts electrons at the end of the electron transport chain and combines with protons to form water.

@practice num
prompt: Glycolysis plus pyruvate oxidation plus the citric acid cycle give how many total NADH per glucose? Use 2 NADH from glycolysis, 1 NADH per pyruvate in pyruvate oxidation and 3 NADH per turn of the cycle.
answer: 10
check: 2 + 2 * 1 + 2 * 3
hint: Two pyruvate enter pyruvate oxidation and two acetyl-CoA enter the cycle.
solution: $2 + 2(1) + 2(3) = 10$ NADH.

@practice num
prompt: A yeast culture ferments 15 mol of glucose with no oxygen. Assuming each glucose gives a net 2 ATP, how many moles of ATP are produced?
answer: 30
check: 15 * 2
unit: mol
hint: Net ATP from glycolysis is 2 per glucose.
solution: $15 \times 2 = 30$ mol of ATP. Fermentation yields no extra ATP.

@practice ms

choice*: The citric acid cycle

choice: Alcohol fermentation
hint: Glycolysis and fermentation are in the cytosol; ATP synthase is in the inner membrane.
solution: Pyruvate oxidation and the citric acid cycle happen in the matrix. Glycolysis and fermentation are cytosolic; ATP synthase is embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane.

@quiz num
prompt: How many grams of $CO_2$ (molar mass 44 g/mol) are produced by complete aerobic respiration of 90 g of glucose (molar mass 180 g/mol)?
answer: 132
check: 90 / 180 * 6 * 44
unit: g
tol: 1%
hint: Find moles of glucose first, then use the 1:6 mole ratio.
solution: $90/180 = 0.5$ mol glucose gives 3 mol $CO_2$, and $3 \times 44 = 132$ g.

@quiz mc
prompt: A researcher adds a compound that makes the inner mitochondrial membrane freely permeable to protons. Compared with normal cells, the treated cells will
choice: make more ATP because protons flow faster
choice*: continue to oxidize NADH but make much less ATP because the proton gradient collapses
choice: stop glycolysis entirely because it needs the gradient
choice: produce more oxygen
hint: What does ATP synthase use to make ATP?
solution: ATP synthase depends on the proton gradient. If protons leak across the membrane, the gradient collapses and ATP synthesis falls, while electron transport and oxygen use continue.
