@article
How many units must a start-up bakery sell before it stops losing money? What happens to profit if volume rises 10%? **Cost-volume-profit (CVP) analysis** answers such questions with a simple model of how revenue, costs and operating income move with volume.

### The model and its assumptions

Let $p$ be the selling price per unit, $v$ the variable cost per unit (production, selling and administrative), $F$ total fixed cost per period and $q$ the quantity sold. Then

$$\text{Operating income} = (p - v)\,q - F.$$

The **unit contribution margin** is $CM = p - v$: what each unit contributes toward covering fixed costs and then profit. The **contribution margin ratio** is $CMR = (p - v)/p$. Assumptions: all costs are either fixed or variable within the relevant range; price and variable cost per unit are constant; units produced equal units sold; and for several products, the sales mix is constant.

### Break-even and target profit

At break-even, operating income is zero:

$$q_{BE} = \frac{F}{p - v}, \qquad \text{Sales}_{BE} = \frac{F}{CMR}.$$

For a **target pretax profit** $\pi$:

$$q = \frac{F + \pi}{p - v}.$$

For a **target after-tax profit** at tax rate $t$, first convert: $\pi_{\text{pretax}} = \pi_{\text{after}}/(1 - t)$. Break-even in whole units is rounded *up*, since a fraction of a unit cannot be sold.

### Margin of safety and operating leverage

The **margin of safety** is how far sales can fall before a loss:
$$\text{MOS \%} = \frac{\text{Sales} - \text{Sales}_{BE}}{\text{Sales}}.$$

The **degree of operating leverage (DOL)** at a given volume is
$$DOL = \frac{\text{Total contribution margin}}{\text{Operating income}}.$$
A DOL of 4 means a 1% change in sales changes operating income by about 4%. High fixed costs mean high DOL: great upside in good years, deep losses in bad ones. DOL is highest near break-even and falls as volume grows.

### Several products

With a constant sales mix, treat a bundle of products as one. The **weighted-average unit contribution margin** is $\sum w_i (p_i - v_i)$, where $w_i$ is product $i$'s share of *units* sold. Total break-even units $= F/\overline{CM}$, then split by the mix. Changing the mix toward lower-margin products raises break-even.

### Using the model

CVP is a planning tool, not a prediction: it supports "what if" questions (a price cut, a switch to automated equipment that lowers $v$ but raises $F$, a new sales commission). Always state the assumptions behind a CVP answer.

@example
title: Break-even, target profit, safety and leverage
problem: A product sells for \$50, variable cost is \$30 per unit and fixed costs are \$240,000 per year. Find (a) break-even units and dollars; (b) units for an after-tax profit of \$60,000 at a 25% tax rate; (c) margin of safety and DOL at that volume.
step: Unit contribution margin = 50 - 30 = \$20; CMR = 20/50 = 40%. Break-even = 240,000 / 20 = 12,000 units, or 240,000 / 0.40 = \$600,000 of sales.
step: Pretax profit needed = 60,000 / (1 - 0.25) = \$80,000. Units = (240,000 + 80,000) / 20 = 16,000 units, or \$800,000 of sales.
step: Margin of safety = (800,000 - 600,000) / 800,000 = 25%.
step: Contribution margin at 16,000 units = 320,000, so DOL = 320,000 / 80,000 = 4.0. Check: sales up 10% to 17,600 units gives income 17,600 × 20 - 240,000 = \$112,000, up 40% from \$80,000.
answer: (a) 12,000 units, \$600,000; (b) 16,000 units; (c) MOS 25%, DOL 4.0.

@example
title: Break-even with two products
problem: A firm sells product A (price \$40, variable cost \$24) and product B (price \$100, variable cost \$70). Units sold are 60% A and 40% B. Fixed costs are \$216,000. Find break-even units for each product.
step: Contribution margins: A = 40 - 24 = \$16; B = 100 - 70 = \$30.
step: Weighted-average CM = 0.60 × 16 + 0.40 × 30 = 9.60 + 12.00 = \$21.60.
step: Total break-even = 216,000 / 21.60 = 10,000 units. A = 0.60 × 10,000 = 6,000; B = 0.40 × 10,000 = 4,000.
step: Check: 6,000 × 16 + 4,000 × 30 = 96,000 + 120,000 = \$216,000 = fixed costs, so operating income is zero.
answer: 6,000 units of A and 4,000 units of B (10,000 in total).

@mistakes
- Using price less *fixed* cost per unit as the margin: the contribution margin uses variable cost only.
- Forgetting that a target *after-tax* profit must be grossed up by $1/(1-t)$ before adding to fixed costs.
- Dividing fixed costs by the contribution margin *ratio* and calling the result units (it is sales dollars).
- Computing a multi-product break-even with a simple average of unit margins instead of one weighted by the sales mix.

model:
```python
import math

def break_even_units(fixed, price, variable, target_profit=0):
    """Units to sell, rounded up, to earn target_profit before tax."""
    margin = price - variable
    if margin <= 0:
        raise ValueError("price must exceed variable cost")
    return math.ceil((fixed + target_profit) / margin)
```
hint: Units = (fixed + target profit) / unit contribution margin.
hint: Use `math.ceil` for the rounding up and check the margin before dividing.
solution: Compute the unit contribution margin, reject non-positive margins, then divide fixed cost plus target profit by it and round up so the target is actually reached.
