@article
A paint plant, a flour mill or a bottling line makes thousands of identical units in a continuous flow. Tracking a cost sheet for every can of paint is pointless. **Process costing** instead accumulates costs by **department** (or process) for a period and divides them by the output of that period to get a **unit cost**. Costs flow from one department to the next with the units: Work in Process (WIP), Department 1 becomes WIP, Department 2, then Finished Goods.

### Equivalent units

At period-end some units are only partly finished. If 3,000 units are 40% converted, they have absorbed the conversion effort of 1,200 finished units. An **equivalent unit** is the amount of work equal to one fully completed unit. Equivalent units (EU) are computed **separately for each cost input**, because inputs are added differently: direct materials are often added at the start of the process (100% complete for any unit started), whereas conversion costs (direct labor and overhead) are incurred evenly as work proceeds.

**Convention used in this lesson:** direct materials are added at the beginning of the process; conversion costs are incurred evenly; no units are lost or spoiled.

### Five steps of a cost of production report

1. **Physical flow**: units in beginning WIP + units started = units completed and transferred + units in ending WIP.
2. **Equivalent units** for each input.
3. **Costs to account for**: beginning WIP cost plus costs added this period.
4. **Cost per equivalent unit** for each input.
5. **Assign costs** to units transferred out and to ending WIP.

### Weighted-average method

Beginning WIP is treated as if it were started and finished this period: its costs are *pooled* with this period's costs.

$$EU_{\text{WA}} = \text{Units completed and transferred} + \left(\text{Ending WIP units} \times \text{percent complete}\right)$$
$$\text{Cost per EU} = \frac{\text{Beginning WIP cost} + \text{Costs added this period}}{EU_{\text{WA}}}$$

### FIFO method

FIFO assumes the beginning WIP units are finished first, and it separates last period's work from this period's:

$$EU_{\text{FIFO}} = \underbrace{BWIP \times (1 - \%\text{ done})}_{\text{to finish beginning WIP}} + \underbrace{\text{Started and completed}}_{} + \underbrace{EWIP \times \%\text{ done}}_{}$$

The cost per EU uses *only costs added this period*. Beginning WIP's prior-period cost is carried into the cost of units transferred out. FIFO gives a cleaner measure of current-period efficiency; weighted average is simpler. When the cost per unit is stable the two give close answers. (US GAAP and IFRS both accept either method, as long as it is applied consistently.)

### The journal entry

When Department 1 finishes units, the transferred-in cost moves to the next department: debit Work in Process, Department 2; credit Work in Process, Department 1. The last department debits Finished Goods.

@example
title: Weighted-average cost of production report
problem: A department had 2,000 units in beginning WIP (all materials, 60% converted) with costs of \$8,000 materials and \$5,400 conversion. It started 18,000 units, completed and transferred 17,000, and had 3,000 units in ending WIP (all materials, 40% converted). Costs added: materials \$72,000; conversion \$85,600. Use weighted average.
step: Physical flow: 2,000 + 18,000 = 20,000 = 17,000 + 3,000.
step: Equivalent units: materials = 17,000 + 3,000 × 100% = 20,000; conversion = 17,000 + 3,000 × 40% = 18,200.
step: Cost per EU: materials = (8,000 + 72,000)/20,000 = \$4.00; conversion = (5,400 + 85,600)/18,200 = \$5.00. Unit cost = \$9.00.
step: Assign: transferred out = 17,000 × 9.00 = \$153,000. Ending WIP = 3,000 × 4.00 + 1,200 × 5.00 = 12,000 + 6,000 = \$18,000. Check: 153,000 + 18,000 = \$171,000 = 13,400 + 72,000 + 85,600.
answer: \$153,000 transferred out; \$18,000 in ending WIP; unit cost \$9.00.

@example
title: The same department under FIFO
problem: Use the same data as the previous example (BWIP 2,000 units, materials 100% and conversion 60% done, cost \$13,400; 18,000 units started; 17,000 completed; EWIP 3,000 units, materials 100% and conversion 40% done; costs added \$72,000 materials and \$85,600 conversion), but apply FIFO.
step: Started and completed = 17,000 - 2,000 = 15,000 units.
step: EU materials = 2,000 × (1 - 1.00) + 15,000 + 3,000 × 1.00 = 18,000. EU conversion = 2,000 × 0.40 + 15,000 + 3,000 × 0.40 = 800 + 15,000 + 1,200 = 17,000.
step: Cost per EU (current costs only): materials = 72,000/18,000 = \$4.00; conversion = 85,600/17,000 = \$5.0353.
step: Ending WIP = 3,000 × 4.00 + 1,200 × 5.0353 = \$18,042.35. Transferred out = 13,400 + 800 × 5.0353 + 15,000 × (4.00 + 5.0353) = 13,400 + 4,028.24 + 135,529.41 = \$152,957.65. Total = \$171,000.00, the same total cost to account for.
answer: FIFO: \$152,957.65 transferred out and \$18,042.35 in ending WIP (weighted average: \$153,000 and \$18,000).

@mistakes
- Using one equivalent-unit figure for all inputs: materials and conversion usually have different percentages complete.
- Counting ending WIP units at 100% for conversion when they are only partly processed.
- Under FIFO, including beginning WIP's prior-period cost in the cost per equivalent unit (it belongs in the cost of units transferred out).
- Dividing costs by *physical* units started instead of equivalent units.

@quiz self
prompt: A manager says, "Weighted average and FIFO should always give the same unit cost." In three or four sentences, explain when they differ and why FIFO may be preferred for evaluating current performance.
hint: Consider what each method does with the beginning work in process and its prior-period costs.
solution: They differ whenever there is beginning WIP and unit costs have changed between periods. Weighted average pools last period's costs and work with this period's, so the unit cost blends the two. FIFO computes the unit cost from current-period costs and current-period work only, so it shows what this period actually cost to produce and is better for judging current efficiency; the prior-period costs are carried only into the cost of the units transferred out.
rubric: States that the methods differ when beginning WIP exists and costs change between periods.
rubric: Explains that weighted average pools beginning WIP costs and work with the current period.
rubric: Explains that FIFO's unit cost reflects current-period costs only, so it is better for performance evaluation.
