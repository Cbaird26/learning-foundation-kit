@article
New analysts often ask me whether the CFA charter is a license. It is not. The charter is a professional credential granted by CFA Institute. A **license** (more exactly a **registration**) is permission from regulators and self-regulatory organizations to carry out regulated activity such as selling securities or giving investment advice for pay. Many careers need both kinds of credential, and they are earned in different ways.

Rules, prerequisites, fees and exam formats change. This lesson describes what each credential is for; **always confirm current requirements at FINRA (finra.org) and NASAA (nasaa.org)** and with your employer's compliance team.

### The pieces

**SIE (Securities Industry Essentials).** A FINRA exam on the basic knowledge needed in the securities industry: products and how markets work, the regulatory structure, and the main rules of conduct. It is the entry point for the FINRA representative exams. A candidate does **not** need to be sponsored by a firm to take it, and passing the SIE does not by itself register you to do any business. Results remain valid for a limited period (check FINRA for the current length), so plan the next exam within that window.

**Series 7, General Securities Representative.** The main FINRA qualification exam for representatives at broker-dealers. It covers soliciting and selling a wide range of securities, including stocks, bonds, options, packaged products and municipal securities. A representative generally needs to pass both it and the SIE (confirm the order and prerequisites with FINRA), and the Series 7 requires a **sponsoring firm**. Commodity futures, real estate and insurance are outside its scope.

**Series 63, Uniform Securities Agent State Law Exam.** A NASAA exam on state securities law. Most states require a person who sells securities to state residents to be a registered **agent** of the broker-dealer, and the Series 63 is the usual state-law qualification.

**Series 65, Uniform Investment Adviser Law Exam.** A NASAA exam for **investment adviser representatives** (people who give investment advice for a registered adviser). It tests state law, ethics, investment vehicles and portfolio management. It does not need firm sponsorship. Many states waive the exam for people who hold certain designations, which can include the CFA charter; check your state regulator.

**Series 66, Uniform Combined State Law Exam.** A NASAA exam that combines the state-law content of the Series 63 with the adviser content of the Series 65. People who do both brokerage and advice (a dual registrant) often take the Series 7 plus the Series 66 rather than the 7, 63 and 65 separately. NASAA and FINRA set the prerequisites, so confirm them before you register.

### Other registrations you will see

- **Series 6**: a limited representative exam covering mutual funds and variable contracts.
- **Series 24**: General Securities Principal, for supervising a broker-dealer's business.
- **Series 79**: Investment Banking Representative, for advising on mergers, restructurings and capital raising.
- **Series 57**: Securities Trader.
- **Series 86 and 87**: the research analyst exams, one on analysis and one on rules and regulations.
- **Series 3**: a National Futures Association exam for those dealing in futures.

### How registration works

A broker-dealer **sponsors** its representatives and files **Form U4** to register them through FINRA's systems; a termination is reported on **Form U5**. Investment adviser representatives register with states, and advisers file **Form ADV**. Registered persons complete **continuing education**: an annual Regulatory Element and a Firm Element set by the employer. A registration can lapse after a period out of the industry, and re-qualification may be needed. Anyone can check a broker's record on **FINRA BrokerCheck** and an adviser's on the SEC's **Investment Adviser Public Disclosure** site.

### Mapping roles to credentials

| Role | Typical registration (confirm with compliance) |
|---|---|
| Retail broker (representative at a broker-dealer) | SIE, Series 7, and Series 63 or 66 |
| Investment adviser representative at an advisory firm | Series 65 or 66 (or an eligible waiver), state registration |
| Dual registrant advising and selling | Series 7 plus the Series 66, or 7, 63 and 65 |
| Research analyst at a broker-dealer | Research analyst registration (Series 86 and 87), plus SIE |
| Investment banker | Series 79 (with SIE), often a state agent registration |
| Buy-side analyst or portfolio manager at an asset manager | Often no individual exam is mandatory; employer policy and adviser rules may apply |
| Futures broker | Series 3 and NFA registration |

The CFA charter does not replace any of these, but it can qualify you for a waiver of some, and it is valued by employers.

@example
title: Which licenses for a career changer
problem: A former teacher joins a small advisory firm as an investment adviser representative who will give planning and portfolio advice for fees and will not sell securities on commission. Which exams does she need, and which does she not?
step: She gives investment advice for compensation, so she is an investment adviser representative and registers with her state. The qualifying exam is the Series 65 (or the Series 66, though that adds state agent law she may not need).
step: She does not sell securities as a broker-dealer representative, so the Series 7 and the SIE are not required for this role.
step: She should confirm with her state regulator and compliance officer whether any exemption or waiver (for example by designation) applies, and complete Form U4-type or adviser registration steps through her firm.
answer: Series 65 (confirm state rules); not the SIE or Series 7 for this advice-only role.

@example
title: A dual registrant
problem: An associate at a combined broker-dealer and advisory firm will recommend individual stocks and options to retail customers for commissions and will manage some accounts for advisory fees. Which exams make sense?
step: Selling stocks and options as a broker-dealer representative needs the SIE and the Series 7.
step: State registration: as an agent of the broker-dealer (Series 63) and as an adviser representative (Series 65), or the Series 66 that combines both.
step: So a typical path is the SIE, then the Series 7, then the Series 66, all subject to sponsorship and current prerequisites.
answer: SIE, Series 7, and Series 66 (or 63 plus 65).

@mistakes
- Treating the CFA charter as a license: it is a credential from CFA Institute and does not register you to sell securities or give advice, although it may waive some state exams.
- Believing that passing the SIE makes you licensed to sell: you also need a sponsoring firm and a representative-level exam such as the Series 7.
- Confusing the Series 63 (state law for agents who sell) with the Series 65 (advisers who advise).
- Relying on a summary like this one instead of checking FINRA and NASAA for current requirements, because exams and prerequisites change.
