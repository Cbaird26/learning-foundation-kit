@article
Clients rarely ask about Sharpe ratios. They ask whether you will call back, whether the fee is fair and whether they can trust you with a hard decision. Client service is where the technical skills of the course become a relationship, and where **fiduciary practice** shows itself: the habits that make a client's interests the organizing principle of your work.

### Two legal standards of conduct

**Investment advisers** owe their clients a federal **fiduciary duty** under the Advisers Act, which the SEC has described as having two parts:

- **Duty of care.** Give advice that is in the client's best interest, based on a reasonable understanding of the client's objectives; seek **best execution** of trades; and provide advice and monitoring at a frequency that fits the relationship.
- **Duty of loyalty.** Do not place your interests ahead of the client's. Give full and fair **disclosure of conflicts** so the client can give informed consent, and eliminate or mitigate conflicts that disclosure cannot cure. The duty cannot be waived by a blanket disclaimer.

**Broker-dealers** recommending securities or strategies to retail customers follow **Regulation Best Interest** (in effect since June 30, 2020). It requires the firm to act in the retail customer's best interest and not place its own interest ahead, through four obligations: **disclosure** of material facts about the relationship and conflicts, **care** in making recommendations, **conflict of interest** policies, and **compliance** procedures. Both advisers and broker-dealers deliver **Form CRS**, a short relationship summary that explains services, fees, conflicts and disciplinary history in plain language. The two standards are related but not identical, and firms that are both must keep clear which hat they wear for each service. The CFA Standards go further than either, requiring fiduciary-like loyalty, prudence and care in Standard III(A).

### The service cycle

1. **Onboarding.** Know your client: objectives, constraints, tax situation, liquidity needs, experience and tolerance for loss. Verify identity as required by anti-money-laundering rules. Deliver the advisory agreement, Form ADV Part 2A and Form CRS.
2. **Investment policy statement (IPS).** A written agreement of **objectives** (return and risk) and **constraints** (time horizon, liquidity, taxes, legal and regulatory limits, unique circumstances), a target asset allocation and ranges, rebalancing rules, benchmark, how performance will be reported, and the schedule for review.
3. **Implementation.** Invest according to the IPS, allocate trades fairly (Standard III(B)), and seek best execution. A manager may use client commissions to buy research and brokerage services that benefit the client within the safe harbor in Section 28(e) of the Exchange Act, with good-faith judgment that the commission is reasonable and with disclosure of the practice. Services that mainly benefit the manager belong on the manager's own account.
4. **Reporting and communication.** Fees, holdings and performance are reported clearly. Fair, accurate and complete performance presentation is the Standard III(D) duty that GIPS puts into practice.
5. **Review.** Revisit the IPS at least annually and after life events, and document the conversation.
6. **Complaints.** Treat them as information: log them, respond promptly and have compliance review patterns.

### Fees

Fees must be disclosed and understood. A common structure is a **tiered percentage of assets** that falls as assets rise. For an account of value $V$ with breakpoints, the fee is the sum of each tier's rate times the amount of $V$ in that tier, and the **effective fee rate** is fee divided by $V$:

$$\text{fee} = \sum_k r_k \times (\text{amount of } V \text{ in tier } k), \qquad \text{effective rate} = \frac{\text{fee}}{V}.$$

### Reviewing a performance presentation

A reviewer reads a presentation as a skeptic. Check: are returns linked, not summed or averaged? Is the benchmark appropriate and unchanged, or changed with explanation? Are all discretionary portfolios in the composite, including closed accounts? Are required items present (dispersion when six or more portfolios, three-year standard deviation, composite assets)? Is there a long enough record? Are compliance and verification claims worded correctly? Finally, would a reasonable client be misled by what is left out?

@example
title: A tiered fee
problem: An adviser charges 1.00% on the first \$1 million, 0.80% on the next \$4 million and 0.60% on assets above \$5 million. What annual fee and effective rate apply to an account worth \$6.5 million?
step: Tier 1: $1{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0100 = \$10{,}000$.
step: Tier 2: $4{,}000{,}000 \times 0.0080 = \$32{,}000$.
step: Tier 3: the remaining \$1.5 million at 0.60% is $1{,}500{,}000 \times 0.0060 = \$9{,}000$.
step: Total fee: $10{,}000 + 32{,}000 + 9{,}000 = \$51{,}000$, an effective rate of $51{,}000/6{,}500{,}000 = 0.785\%$, below the top tier rate.
answer: \$51,000; effective rate 0.785%.

@example
title: A conflict in a product recommendation
problem: An adviser's firm earns a revenue-sharing payment from one family of funds. The adviser's best-fit model portfolio can be built with that family's funds or with lower-cost alternatives of similar quality. What should the adviser do?
step: Duty of loyalty: the firm's revenue interest conflicts with the client's interest in low cost; it must be disclosed and either eliminated or mitigated.
step: Duty of care: the recommendation must be in the client's best interest, which includes cost among the factors, given similar quality.
step: Standards: III(A) and VI(A) (disclose the conflict plainly) and III(C).
step: Action: choose the lower-cost option unless the higher-cost fund is clearly better for the client, document the reason, and disclose the revenue arrangement.
answer: Prefer the lower-cost equivalent, document the choice, and disclose the revenue-sharing conflict (Standards III(A), VI(A)).

@mistakes
- Treating a fiduciary disclaimer in the contract as a way to waive the duty of loyalty.
- Confusing the adviser's fiduciary duty with Regulation Best Interest, or assuming that a broker-dealer's recommendation standard has no conflict obligations.
- Writing an IPS that lists only a return target and omits constraints such as liquidity, taxes and time horizon.
- Reading only the headline return in a performance presentation and ignoring what is missing (benchmark changes, left-out accounts, claims of compliance).

| Year | Composite gross return | Benchmark return | Portfolios | Composite assets | Dispersion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | -14.2% | -15.0% | 4 | \$38 million | 1.9% |
| 2023 | 18.6% | 16.1% | 5 | \$45 million | 1.4% |
| 2024 | 12.4% | 11.0% | 7 | \$61 million | not shown |

Notes: "Three-year return: 16.8%." "The benchmark is a growth index; before 2024 it was a broad market index." "Portfolios that closed during the year are excluded." "Redfern Capital is GIPS compliant except for the dispersion requirement, and this composite has been verified."
hint: Check linking, record length, dispersion, benchmark changes, left-out accounts and the wording of the compliance and verification claims.
solution: (1) The three-year return of 16.8% is the sum of the annual returns; linked it is $0.858 \times 1.186 \times 1.124 - 1 = 14.4\%$. Returns must be linked geometrically. (2) Dispersion is missing for 2024 although seven portfolios were held; for six or more it is required. (3) Only three years are shown for a composite created in 2015; the minimum presentation is five years (or since inception), building to ten. (4) The benchmark changed in 2024 and the old figures use a different index; the change must be explained and be justified, not used to improve the comparison. (5) Excluding closed accounts leaves out portfolios that were discretionary and fee-paying during the period, which biases results upward. (6) "Compliant except for dispersion" is partial compliance, which GIPS do not allow. (7) A composite cannot be "verified": verification applies to the firm, and the verification statement must be worded as the standards prescribe. (8) The fee schedule and currency, the benchmark description, the three-year standard deviation of the composite and the benchmark, and a correctly worded compliance statement are missing or incomplete. These breaches also fail Standard III(D) (fair, accurate, complete) and I(C) (misrepresentation).
rubric: Finds the summed three-year return (16.8% against a linked 14.4%).
rubric: Finds at least four more of: missing 2024 dispersion, too short a record, undisclosed benchmark change, excluded closed accounts, partial-compliance wording, composite "verified", missing statistics or fee information.
rubric: Names a principle for each error (geometric linking, GIPS requirements, Standard III(D) or I(C)).
rubric: Does not call the presentation acceptable because the individual returns look plausible.
