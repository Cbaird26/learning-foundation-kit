### Week by week

**Week 1: Professionalism.** The Code of Ethics and seven Standards of Professional Conduct apply to members and candidates. Standard I covers knowledge of the law (follow the stricter of law and Code), independence and objectivity, misrepresentation (including plagiarism), misconduct and competence. Use the five-step method: facts, duties, conflicts, options, decision.

**Week 2: Clients and employers.** Standard III: loyalty, prudence and care; fair dealing (pro rata allocation); suitability; performance presentation; confidentiality (three exceptions). Standard IV: loyalty to the employer, written consent for additional compensation, and supervisors' responsibility to prevent violations.

**Week 3: Conflicts.** Standard VI: disclose conflicts prominently and plainly; clients first, then employer, then you (no front running); disclose referral fees before the service begins. A personal-trading policy has coverage, pre-clearance, restricted lists, blackout and holding periods, IPO approval, reporting and monitoring.

**Week 4: Research.** Standard V: diligence and reasonable basis; communication (fact versus opinion, assumptions and risks); records kept at least seven years. Analyst certification and research-independence rules limit pressure from banking.

**Week 5: Compliance.** Regulators: SEC, FINRA, state regulators and NASAA, CFTC and NFA, bank regulators. An adviser needs written policies, an annual review and a chief compliance officer. MNPI: do not trade or tip, report to compliance, and use information barriers.

**Week 6: Licensing.** SIE (entry point, no sponsor), Series 7 (general securities representative), Series 63 (state agent law), Series 65 (adviser law), Series 66 (combined state law). The CFA charter is a credential, not a license. Verify at FINRA and NASAA.

**Week 7: GIPS.** Firm-wide, no partial compliance, composites with all discretionary fee-paying portfolios, time-weighted returns, a benchmark, dispersion and three-year standard deviation, at least five years of history.

**Week 8: Client service.** Fiduciary duty of care and loyalty; Regulation Best Interest and Form CRS; investment policy statement; tiered fees; review of presentations.

**Weeks 9 to 12: Careers and growth.** Buy side, sell side and advisory roles; accurate, quantified resumes; STAR stories; continuing education and the CFA experience requirement; AI tools and model risk under the Standards.

### Key formulas

- Pro rata fill: $\text{fill}_i = \text{requested}_i / \sum \text{requested} \times \text{filled}$.
- Front-running gain: $\text{shares} \times (P_{\text{sell}} - P_{\text{buy}})$.
- Expected total return: $(P_T + D)/P_0 - 1$.
- Time-weighted return: $\prod (1 + R_i) - 1$; annualized: $(1 + R)^{1/y} - 1$.
- Modified Dietz: $(V_{\text{end}} - V_{\text{begin}} - \sum CF)/(V_{\text{begin}} + \sum w_j CF_j)$.
- Composite return: asset-weighted; dispersion: $\sqrt{\sum w_i (R_i - R_c)^2}$.
- Tiered fee: sum of rate times amount in each tier.
- Precision $= TP/(TP + FP)$; recall $= TP/(TP + FN)$.

### Self-check

- Can I name the Standard for a case, and say which duty comes first when they conflict?
- Can I separate the duties of an adviser (fiduciary) from those of a broker-dealer (Regulation Best Interest)?
- Can I say which exam or registration a role needs, and where to verify it?
- Can I link returns, compute a composite and spot a non-compliant presentation?

### Common errors

- Following local law when the Code is stricter.
- Believing disclosure cures every conflict.
- Summing or averaging annual returns instead of linking them.
- Treating the CFA charter or the SIE as a license.
- Claiming "partial" GIPS compliance or a "verified composite".
- Describing a candidate's status as if it were the designation.

choice: A staff member who has passed the SIE is registered to give investment advice for a fee

choice*: An analyst who receives possible material nonpublic information should stop trading in that security and tell compliance

@exam self
prompt: A portfolio manager at your firm buys shares of a stock in a personal account the day before the firm places a large client order in the same stock, then sells the next day at a profit. A junior analyst who saw the trades asks you what to do. Write a short memo (one page) to the junior analyst that names the Standards involved, explains what the analyst should do and in what order, says what the firm's compliance officer should do, and describes two changes to the firm's personal-trading policy that would help prevent a recurrence.
hint: Use the five-step method, and remember Standards VI(B), III(A), I(A) and IV(C).
solution: Memo: This looks like front running, a breach of Standard VI(B), Priority of Transactions, and of III(A), Loyalty, Prudence, and Care, and possibly misconduct under I(D). Under I(A) you must not knowingly participate in or assist a violation and should dissociate from it. Steps: first write down what you saw (dates, quantities, prices) without accusing; then report to the compliance officer, or to senior management if the compliance officer is involved or inactive; do not discuss it with the manager or others, and do not trade the stock. The compliance officer should preserve the records, review the personal-trading reports against the client order, interview the manager, quantify and remove the gain, take disciplinary action under the policy and consider reporting to regulators if required. Policy changes: a blackout on personal trades in any security with a pending or recent client order, with pre-clearance checked against the order blotter by compliance; and a minimum holding period of 30 days with monthly review of employees' trades by someone independent of the traders. Under IV(C), supervisors must also enforce the procedures.
rubric: Names Standards VI(B) and III(A) for the trade and I(A) for the analyst's duty not to participate and to dissociate.
rubric: Gives the analyst a sequenced action plan: document, report to compliance or senior management, no discussion, no trading.
rubric: Describes the compliance officer's response: preserve records, review, quantify and recover the gain, discipline, consider regulatory reporting.
rubric: Proposes two concrete policy changes, such as a blackout checked against the order blotter and a holding period with independent review.
