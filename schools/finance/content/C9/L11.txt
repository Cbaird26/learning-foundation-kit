@article
Equity is the residual, but it is far from passive: companies issue shares, buy them back, pay dividends and compensate employees with options. Investors then boil all of this down to one per-share figure, earnings per share. This lesson covers the three steps.

### Components of equity and treasury shares

Equity has **contributed capital** (share capital and share premium under IFRS, common stock and additional paid-in capital under US GAAP), **retained earnings**, **accumulated other comprehensive income or other reserves**, and any **treasury shares**. A dividend becomes a liability when declared. Share splits and stock dividends move amounts within equity and are applied retroactively in EPS. Shares repurchased and held are **treasury shares**: under IFRS (IAS 32) they are deducted from equity at cost and no gain or loss is ever recognised in profit or loss; US GAAP is the same in effect (the cost method deducts them from equity, or the shares may be retired). Preferred shares that must be redeemed are liabilities under both frameworks. A convertible bond is split under IAS 32 into a liability component (the present value of the bond's payments at the rate for a similar non-convertible bond) and an equity component (the residual). Under US GAAP, since ASU 2020-06, a convertible bond is normally recorded as a single liability at amortised cost.

### Share-based payment

**IFRS 2** and **ASC 718** apply when an entity pays suppliers or employees with equity instruments or with cash based on the share price. For **equity-settled** awards, measure the **grant-date fair value** of the instruments (using an option-pricing model such as the Black-Scholes-Merton model or a lattice), and recognise it as compensation expense over the **vesting period**, with a credit to equity. The grant-date value is not remeasured. The expense is based on the number of awards expected to vest:

$$\text{Cumulative expense}_t = \text{awards expected to vest} \times \text{fair value} \times \frac{\text{years elapsed}}{\text{vesting period}}, \qquad \text{Expense}_t = \text{cumulative}_t - \text{cumulative}_{t-1}.$$

**Cash-settled** awards create a liability that is remeasured to fair value at every reporting date, with changes in profit or loss. Differences between the frameworks: for awards with graded vesting, **IFRS 2 treats each tranche as a separate award** (front-loading expense) while **US GAAP lets the entity choose** straight-line or graded recognition for awards with only service conditions; IFRS 2 requires an estimate of forfeitures, while US GAAP lets an entity **estimate forfeitures or account for them as they occur** (ASU 2016-09).

### Basic EPS

$$\text{Basic EPS} = \frac{\text{net income} - \text{preferred dividends}}{\text{weighted average ordinary shares outstanding}}.$$

Preferred dividends are deducted if cumulative, or if declared when non-cumulative. Shares issued are weighted by the fraction of the year outstanding, and repurchases are subtracted from their date. Stock splits and stock dividends are applied as though they occurred at the start of the earliest period shown.

### Diluted EPS

**Diluted EPS** assumes that dilutive potential ordinary shares are exercised or converted. Both **IAS 33** and **ASC 260** use:

- the **treasury stock method** for options and warrants: assumed exercise proceeds buy back shares at the average market price, so incremental shares $= N\left(1 - \frac{K}{P_{avg}}\right)$ for $N$ options with strike $K$ when $P_{avg} > K$, and zero otherwise (out-of-the-money options are antidilutive);
- the **if-converted method** for convertible bonds and preferred shares: add back the after-tax interest (or the preferred dividends) to the numerator and add the conversion shares to the denominator.

A security is included only if it is **dilutive** (it lowers EPS). Compare each security's **earnings per incremental share** (the numerator add-back divided by the extra shares) and include them from the lowest upward while diluted EPS keeps falling. Dilution is tested against profit from continuing operations. Under US GAAP, ASU 2020-06 also requires the if-converted method for convertible instruments. For share-based payment awards, both standards include in the assumed proceeds the compensation for future service that is not yet recognised.

@example
title: Basic and diluted EPS
problem: Garnet Textiles has net income of \$1,350,000 and preferred dividends of \$100,000. It has 1,000,000 shares at 1 January, issues 240,000 shares on 1 May and repurchases 60,000 shares on 1 October. It has options on 100,000 shares with a strike price of \$20 (average market price \$25), and 6% convertible bonds with a face value of \$1,000,000 convertible into 60,000 shares. The tax rate is 25%. Compute basic and diluted EPS.
step: Weighted average shares are $1{,}000{,}000 + 240{,}000 \times \frac{8}{12} - 60{,}000 \times \frac{3}{12} = 1{,}000{,}000 + 160{,}000 - 15{,}000 = 1{,}145{,}000$.
step: Basic EPS is $\frac{1{,}350{,}000 - 100{,}000}{1{,}145{,}000} = \$1.0917$, or \$1.09.
step: Options: incremental shares are $100{,}000 \times (1 - 20/25) = 20{,}000$ with no numerator effect.
step: Convertible bonds: after-tax interest is $1{,}000{,}000 \times 6\% \times (1 - 0.25) = \$45{,}000$, spread over 60,000 shares is \$0.75 per incremental share, below basic EPS, so the bonds are dilutive.
step: Diluted EPS $= \frac{1{,}250{,}000 + 45{,}000}{1{,}145{,}000 + 20{,}000 + 60{,}000} = \frac{1{,}295{,}000}{1{,}225{,}000} = \$1.0571$, or \$1.06.
answer: Basic EPS \$1.09; diluted EPS \$1.06.

@example
title: Expense for a share option award
problem: On 1 January, Hollin Systems grants 1,000 options to each of 200 employees. The grant-date fair value is \$12 per option and the awards cliff-vest after 3 years of service. At the end of year 1 it expects 10% of employees to leave before vesting; at the end of year 2 it revises that to 8%. Compute the expense in years 1 and 2 and record year 1.
step: Year 1 cumulative expense: $200 \times 0.90 \times 1{,}000 \times \$12 \times \frac{1}{3} = \$720{,}000$.
step: Year 2 cumulative expense: $200 \times 0.92 \times 1{,}000 \times \$12 \times \frac{2}{3} = \$1{,}472{,}000$. The year 2 expense is $1{,}472{,}000 - 720{,}000 = \$752{,}000$, including a catch-up for the lower forfeiture estimate.
step: Year 1 entry: debit Compensation expense 720,000; credit Equity (share-based payment reserve or additional paid-in capital) 720,000.
step: The fair value per option stays at \$12; only the number expected to vest changes.
answer: Year 1 expense \$720,000; year 2 expense \$752,000.

@mistakes
- Recognising a gain or loss in profit or loss when a company sells treasury shares or repurchases its own shares.
- Using the weighted average share count without time-weighting shares issued or repurchased during the year.
- Including antidilutive securities in diluted EPS; a convertible whose earnings per incremental share exceeds basic EPS raises EPS and is excluded.
- Remeasuring an equity-settled award's fair value each period: only cash-settled awards are remeasured.

model:
```python
def tsm_incremental_shares(options, strike, avg_price):
    """Incremental shares from options under the treasury stock method."""
    if avg_price <= strike:
        return 0
    return options * (1 - strike / avg_price)
```
hint: Out-of-the-money options are antidilutive, so handle them first.
hint: Assumed proceeds `options * strike` buy back `options * strike / avg_price` shares.
solution: If the average price is not above the strike, no incremental shares are counted. Otherwise the company issues all the option shares and repurchases as many shares as the exercise proceeds buy at the average price, so the net increase is `options * (1 - strike / avg_price)`.
