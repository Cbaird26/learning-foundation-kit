@article
A bond, a loan receivable, a share portfolio and a lawsuit all raise the same two questions: how large is the amount we should show, and where do changes in it go? Financial instruments and provisions bring together measurement, risk and judgement.

### Classification under IFRS 9

A debt instrument is classified using two tests: the **business model** for managing the asset and the **contractual cash flow characteristics** (solely payments of principal and interest, SPPI).

| Business model | SPPI met | Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Hold to collect contractual cash flows | Yes | **Amortised cost** |
| Hold to collect and to sell | Yes | **FVOCI** (fair value through OCI), with interest and impairment in profit or loss and recycling of fair value gains on sale |
| Other, or SPPI not met | No | **FVTPL** (fair value through profit or loss) |

Equity investments are FVTPL, but for equity not held for trading an entity may make an **irrevocable election** to present fair value changes in OCI; the amounts are never recycled, though dividends go to profit or loss. Most financial liabilities are measured at amortised cost; when a liability is designated at fair value, the change due to the entity's own credit risk goes to OCI.

### US GAAP

Debt securities fall into three groups: **held to maturity** (amortised cost, requires positive intent and ability), **available for sale** (fair value through OCI, with credit losses recorded through an allowance in profit or loss) and **trading** (fair value through profit or loss). Under **ASC 321**, equity securities are measured at fair value through net income; there is no FVOCI option, and a **measurement alternative** is available for equity without a readily determinable fair value.

### Impairment

**IFRS 9** applies a three-stage **expected credit loss (ECL)** model. Stage 1 (no significant increase in credit risk since initial recognition) records 12-month ECL; stage 2 (significant increase) records **lifetime ECL**; stage 3 (credit-impaired) records lifetime ECL with interest income on the net carrying amount. **CECL (ASC 326)** records **lifetime expected losses from initial recognition** for assets at amortised cost, so US GAAP typically builds a larger allowance at inception. A simple ECL is $\text{EAD} \times \text{PD} \times \text{LGD}$: exposure at default, probability of default and loss given default.

### The effective interest method

A bond liability at amortised cost carries interest expense equal to the opening carrying amount times the **effective (market) rate at issue**, while the cash coupon is face value times the coupon rate. The difference adjusts the carrying amount toward face value.

### Provisions and contingencies

**IAS 37** recognises a **provision** when there is a present obligation (legal or constructive) from a past event, an outflow is probable (more likely than not), and the amount can be estimated reliably. The measurement is the **best estimate**: for a large population (warranties) the **expected value**; for a single obligation the most likely outcome, adjusted for other outcomes. If the time value of money is material, discount at a pre-tax rate, and the unwinding of the discount is a finance cost. **ASC 450** accrues a loss contingency when a loss is **probable** (likely to occur, a higher threshold) and **reasonably estimable**; **ranges** with no best estimate give the low end under US GAAP, while IAS 37 uses the midpoint of a continuous range of equally likely outcomes. US GAAP generally does not discount. IAS 37 also requires a provision for **onerous contracts**, for which unavoidable costs exceed the benefits; US GAAP has no general equivalent. **Contingent liabilities** (possible obligations or ones not reliably measurable) are disclosed, not recognised; **contingent assets** are recognised only when realisation is virtually certain.

@example
title: A bond issued at a discount
problem: Calder Foods issues 3-year bonds with a face value of \$500,000 and a 6% annual coupon when the market rate is 8%. Find the issue price, then the year 1 interest expense and carrying amount at amortised cost.
step: Price $= 30{,}000 \times \frac{1 - 1.08^{-3}}{0.08} + 500{,}000 \times 1.08^{-3} = 77{,}312.91 + 396{,}916.12 =\$474{,}229.03$ (shown to the cent).
step: Entry at issue: debit Cash 474,229.03; credit Bonds payable 474,229.03 (face of 500,000 less a discount of 25,770.97).
step: Year 1 interest expense is $474{,}229.03 \times 8\% = \$37{,}938.32$. The coupon is \$30,000, so the discount amortised is \$7,938.32. Entry: debit Interest expense 37,938.32; credit Cash 30,000; credit Bonds payable 7,938.32.
step: The carrying amount becomes $474{,}229.03 + 7{,}938.32 = \$482{,}167.35$, moving toward the face value of \$500,000, which it reaches at maturity.
answer: Issue price \$474,229.03; year 1 interest expense \$37,938.32; carrying amount \$482,167.35.

@example
title: A decommissioning provision
problem: Tarn Energy must dismantle a platform in 3 years at an expected cash cost of \$1,000,000. The pre-tax discount rate is 5%. Record the provision at the start and the year 1 unwinding.
step: The provision is the present value: $1{,}000{,}000 / 1.05^3 = \$863{,}837.60$.
step: Initial entry (included in the asset's cost under IAS 16 and IFRIC 1): debit Platform 863,837.60; credit Decommissioning provision 863,837.60.
step: Year 1 unwinding is $863{,}837.60 \times 5\% = \$43{,}191.88$. Entry: debit Finance cost 43,191.88; credit Decommissioning provision 43,191.88.
step: The provision grows to $\$1{,}000{,}000$ by the end of year 3. Under US GAAP (ASC 410-20) a similar asset retirement obligation is measured at fair value using a credit-adjusted risk-free rate and accreted in the same way.
answer: Provision \$863,837.60; year 1 finance cost \$43,191.88.

@mistakes
- Using the coupon rate instead of the effective market rate to compute interest expense on a bond at amortised cost.
- Classifying a debt instrument by what the company intends for a single instrument without asking about the business model and SPPI tests.
- Recognising a provision for a future operating loss or a plan with no present obligation.
- Assuming the probable thresholds are identical: IAS 37 uses more likely than not, ASC 450 uses likely to occur.

answer: 12500
lead: \$
check: 80000 * 0.25 - 30000 * 0.25
hint: The asset's carrying amount exceeds its tax base by the gain, and the provision's carrying amount exceeds its tax base by its full amount.
solution: The unrealised gain is a taxable temporary difference: DTL $= 80{,}000 \times 25\% = \$20{,}000$. The provision is a deductible temporary difference: DTA $= 30{,}000 \times 25\% = \$7{,}500$. The net DTL is \$12,500.
