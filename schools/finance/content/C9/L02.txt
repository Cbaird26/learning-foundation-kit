@article
Two companies can earn the same profit and still tell very different stories if one reports "operating profit" that excludes restructuring costs and the other does not. **Presentation standards** decide which statements you see, how items are grouped and which subtotals are shown, so they shape every ratio you compute from the statements.

### The primary statements

Under IAS 1 (in force until IFRS 18 applies) a complete set of IFRS financial statements has a statement of financial position, a statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income (one statement, or two), a statement of changes in equity, a statement of cash flows and notes. US GAAP is similar: ASC 220 requires comprehensive income in one continuous statement or two consecutive statements, and ASC 210 covers the balance sheet. **Other comprehensive income (OCI)** collects gains and losses that standards direct away from profit, such as foreign currency translation of foreign operations. **Total comprehensive income** is profit plus OCI.

Both frameworks forbid presenting **extraordinary items** (IAS 1 since 2005; ASU 2015-01 removed the US GAAP category). On the balance sheet, IAS 1 requires a current and non-current split unless a liquidity ordering is more relevant, but does not prescribe the order of line items, so many IFRS reporters list non-current items first; US GAAP presentations conventionally begin with current assets.

### IFRS 18

**IFRS 18** *Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements* replaces IAS 1 for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, with comparatives restated. Net profit does not change, but the statement of profit or loss now classifies income and expenses into five categories: **operating**, **investing**, **financing**, **income taxes** and **discontinued operations**. Operating is the residual category. Two new subtotals are required:

- **Operating profit**, after the operating category.
- **Profit before financing and income taxes**, after the investing category.

For a company without a specified main business activity, income from investments (such as share of profit of associates and interest income on cash) goes in investing, and interest on borrowings and on lease liabilities goes in financing. Operating expenses may be shown by nature or by function; a company that presents by function must also disclose specified expenses by nature (depreciation, amortisation, employee benefits, impairment and inventory write-downs) in the notes. IFRS 18 also requires one note on **management-defined performance measures (MPMs)**: subtotals of income and expenses a company uses in public communication to express management's view of performance, each reconciled to the most directly comparable IFRS subtotal with the tax and non-controlling-interest effects. Amendments to IAS 7 make operating profit the starting point of the indirect cash flow reconciliation.

### US GAAP presentation and recent updates

US GAAP does not prescribe categories or subtotals; SEC registrants follow Regulation S-X, which gives a format by function (revenue, cost of sales, operating expenses). "Operating income" is common but undefined, and since ASU 2017-07 the non-service components of pension cost sit outside it. Two recent updates add disclosure: **ASU 2023-07** requires public entities to disclose significant segment expenses regularly provided to the chief operating decision maker, and **ASU 2024-03** requires disaggregation of certain expense captions (such as purchases of inventory, employee compensation, depreciation and intangible amortisation) in the notes, for annual periods beginning after December 15, 2026.

### Other differences

| Topic | IFRS | US GAAP |
|---|---|---|
| Interest paid | Operating or financing (IAS 7 choice, removed by the IFRS 18 amendments) | Operating |
| Interest and dividends received | Operating or investing (IAS 7 choice) | Operating |
| Dividends paid | Operating or financing (IAS 7 choice) | Financing |
| Discontinued operation | A major line of business or geographical area (IFRS 5) | A disposal that represents a strategic shift with a major effect (ASC 205-20) |
| Expense analysis | By nature or function | Generally by function (SEC) |

@example
title: Building the IFRS 18 statement
problem: Larkspur Outfitters reports (in thousands of dollars): revenue \$8,400; cost of sales \$5,040; selling and distribution \$1,050; administrative \$630; share of profit of an associate \$35; interest income \$25; interest on borrowings \$140; interest on lease liabilities \$20. The tax rate is 25% of profit before tax. Compute the IFRS 18 subtotals and profit.
step: Operating category: revenue less all operating expenses, $8{,}400 - 5{,}040 - 1{,}050 - 630 = 1{,}680$. **Operating profit** is \$1,680.
step: Investing category: the associate (35) and interest income (25) total 60, so **profit before financing and income taxes** is $1{,}680 + 60 = 1{,}740$.
step: Financing category: borrowings (140) and lease interest (20) total 160, so profit before tax is $1{,}740 - 160 = 1{,}580$.
step: Income tax is $25\% \times 1{,}580 = 395$, so profit for the year is $1{,}580 - 395 = 1{,}185$.
step: Under a typical US GAAP format the same company would show income from operations of 1,680 and place the associate, interest income and interest expense under other income (expense); pre-tax income of 1,580 and net income of 1,185 are the same.
answer: Operating profit \$1,680; profit before financing and income taxes \$1,740; profit \$1,185 (thousands).

@example
title: Adding other comprehensive income
problem: In the same year Larkspur has a foreign currency translation gain of \$30 and a loss of \$12 on remeasuring a defined benefit pension plan, both recognised in OCI (thousands of dollars, no tax effect assumed). Compute total comprehensive income.
step: Net OCI is $30 - 12 = 18$.
step: Total comprehensive income is profit plus OCI: $1{,}185 + 18 = 1{,}203$.
answer: \$1,203 (thousands).

@mistakes
- Forgetting that IFRS 18 changes the structure and subtotals but not total profit or total comprehensive income.
- Putting interest on lease liabilities in operating expenses: under IFRS 18 it belongs in the financing category, and under IFRS 16 it is an interest expense.
- Stating that US GAAP allows interest paid in financing activities: under ASC 230 it is an operating cash flow.
- Mixing up dates: IFRS 18 applies to annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, while ASU 2024-03 applies to US GAAP annual periods beginning after December 15, 2026.
