@article
A company's accounting profit and its taxable profit are different numbers, because tax law and accounting standards measure revenue, depreciation and provisions in different ways. If you simply reported the tax payable as the tax expense, profit after tax would swing with every timing difference. Income tax accounting solves this by recognising the tax effect of those timing differences in the period when the underlying transaction is recorded.

### The balance sheet liability method

Both **IAS 12** and **ASC 740** use the **balance sheet liability method**. For each asset and liability compare the **carrying amount** in the financial statements with its **tax base** (the amount attributed to it for tax purposes). The difference is a **temporary difference**:

| | Carrying amount compared with tax base | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Asset | Carrying amount **higher** than tax base | Taxable temporary difference: **deferred tax liability (DTL)** |
| Asset | Carrying amount lower than tax base | Deductible temporary difference: **deferred tax asset (DTA)** |
| Liability | Carrying amount **higher** than tax base | Deductible temporary difference: **DTA** |
| Liability | Carrying amount lower than tax base | Taxable temporary difference: DTL |

Deferred tax equals the temporary difference times the tax rate expected to apply when the difference reverses, using the rate that is **enacted or substantively enacted** under IFRS and **enacted** under US GAAP. Total **income tax expense** is current tax plus deferred tax:

$$\text{Income tax expense} = \text{taxable income} \times t + \Delta\text{DTL} - \Delta\text{DTA}.$$

**Permanent differences**, such as non-deductible fines or tax-exempt interest, never reverse, create no deferred tax and appear only in the **effective tax rate** reconciliation, where the effective rate equals income tax expense divided by pre-tax income.

### Recognition of deferred tax assets

**IAS 12** recognises a DTA only to the extent it is **probable** that future taxable profit will be available against which it can be used. **ASC 740** recognises the DTA in full and then reduces it with a **valuation allowance** if it is **more likely than not** (a probability above 50%) that some or all of it will not be realised. Under both frameworks, deferred taxes are presented as non-current (ASU 2015-17 in US GAAP) and are not discounted. A change in tax rates is recognised in income tax expense when the new rate is enacted (or substantively enacted under IFRS): DTLs and DTAs are remeasured. Deferred tax on items recognised in OCI or equity, such as a revaluation surplus, is recognised there too.

### Uncertain tax positions

**IFRIC 23** applies when it is uncertain whether the tax authority will accept a treatment. If acceptance is probable, use the amount in the tax filing. If not, measure the effect of the uncertainty with the **most likely amount** or the **expected value**, whichever better predicts the resolution. **ASC 740-10** uses a two-step process: recognise a benefit only if it is **more likely than not** to be sustained on its technical merits, then measure the largest amount that is more than 50% likely to be realised on settlement.

### Disclosures and recent changes

**ASU 2023-09** requires more detailed rate reconciliation categories and income taxes paid disaggregated by jurisdiction, effective for public business entities for annual periods beginning after December 15, 2024. IAS 12 contains a temporary exception, from May 2023, that stops entities recognising deferred taxes related to the OECD Pillar Two top-up taxes. A 2021 amendment to IAS 12 means the initial recognition exception does not apply to transactions, such as leases and decommissioning obligations, that give rise to equal taxable and deductible temporary differences, so deferred tax is recognised on both.

@example
title: From pre-tax income to tax expense
problem: Cordwain Industries reports pre-tax accounting income of \$200,000 in year 1. Tax depreciation exceeds book depreciation by \$10,000. A warranty provision of \$15,000 was expensed but is deductible only when paid. The tax rate is 25%. Compute current tax, deferred taxes and income tax expense, and record the entry.
step: Taxable income is $200{,}000 - 10{,}000 + 15{,}000 = \$205{,}000$, so current tax is $205{,}000 \times 25\% = \$51{,}250$.
step: The depreciation difference is a taxable temporary difference (the asset's carrying amount exceeds its tax base by \$10,000): DTL $= 10{,}000 \times 25\% = \$2{,}500$.
step: The warranty provision is a liability with a carrying amount \$15,000 above its tax base of zero, a deductible temporary difference: DTA $= 15{,}000 \times 25\% = \$3{,}750$.
step: Income tax expense is $51{,}250 + 2{,}500 - 3{,}750 = \$50{,}000$, exactly 25% of pre-tax income. Entry: debit Income tax expense 50,000; debit Deferred tax asset 3,750; credit Income tax payable 51,250; credit Deferred tax liability 2,500.
answer: Current tax \$51,250; income tax expense \$50,000.

@example
title: A valuation allowance and a rate change
problem: Dunmere Corp. (US GAAP) has a DTA of \$80,000 and judges it more likely than not that 30% of it will not be realised. It also has a DTL of \$120,000 computed at 30%, and a law is enacted lowering the rate to 25%. What are the valuation allowance and the effect of the rate change on the DTL and income tax expense? (Ignore the effect of the rate change on the DTA.)
step: The valuation allowance is $30\% \times 80{,}000 = \$24{,}000$, leaving a net DTA of \$56,000. The entry debits Income tax expense 24,000 and credits Valuation allowance 24,000. (Under IAS 12 the DTA would simply be recognised at only the \$56,000 probable of use.)
step: The temporary difference behind the DTL is $120{,}000 / 0.30 = \$400{,}000$.
step: At 25% the DTL is $400{,}000 \times 25\% = \$100{,}000$, a reduction of \$20,000. Entry: debit Deferred tax liability 20,000; credit Income tax expense 20,000.
answer: Valuation allowance \$24,000; the rate change cuts the DTL, and tax expense, by \$20,000.

@mistakes
- Treating a permanent difference such as a non-deductible fine as a temporary difference and recording deferred tax on it.
- Using the current rate for deferred taxes instead of the enacted (or substantively enacted) rate that will apply when the difference reverses.
- Recognising a DTA in full under IFRS without testing whether future taxable profit is probable.
- Confusing the direction: an asset with a carrying amount above its tax base gives a DTL, while a liability with a carrying amount above its tax base gives a DTA.

@quiz code
prompt: Write `net_deferred_tax(items, rate)` that returns the net deferred tax position (DTA minus DTL), positive for a net asset and negative for a net liability. Each item is a tuple `(kind, carrying_amount, tax_base)` where `kind` is `"asset"` or `"liability"`; `rate` is a decimal tax rate. An asset with a carrying amount above its tax base creates a DTL; a liability with a carrying amount above its tax base creates a DTA. For example, `net_deferred_tax([("asset", 80000, 60000)], 0.25)` returns `-5000.0`.
starter:
```python
def net_deferred_tax(items, rate):
    pass
```
tests:
```python
import math
assert math.isclose(net_deferred_tax([("asset", 80000, 60000)], 0.25), -5000)
assert math.isclose(net_deferred_tax([("liability", 24000, 4000)], 0.25), 5000)
assert math.isclose(net_deferred_tax(
    [("asset", 90000, 80000), ("liability", 18000, 2000)], 0.25), 1500)
assert math.isclose(net_deferred_tax([("asset", 50000, 70000)], 0.20), 4000)
assert net_deferred_tax([], 0.25) == 0
```
model:
```python
def net_deferred_tax(items, rate):
    """Net deferred tax position: positive is a net DTA, negative a net DTL."""
    total = 0.0
    for kind, carrying, tax_base in items:
        if kind == "asset":
            total += (tax_base - carrying) * rate
        else:
            total += (carrying - tax_base) * rate
    return total
```
hint: For an asset the temporary difference is carrying amount minus tax base; for a liability the signs are reversed.
hint: A positive result is a net asset, so an asset with a carrying amount above its tax base must contribute a negative amount.
solution: Loop through the items and add the tax effect of each. For an asset a carrying amount above the tax base is a taxable difference, a DTL, so its contribution is negative; for a liability the same excess is deductible, a DTA, so its contribution is positive. The fourth test is an asset with a tax base above its carrying amount, which is a DTA.
