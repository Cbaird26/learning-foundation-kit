@article
Before 2019 a company could lease a fleet of aircraft for 15 years and show neither the aircraft nor the obligation on its balance sheet, as long as the lease was classed as an operating lease. Analysts adjusted for it by hand. **IFRS 16** (effective 2019) and **ASC 842** (effective 2019 for public US companies) moved nearly all leases onto the lessee's balance sheet, but they did not converge on the income statement.

### Lessee accounting under IFRS 16

A contract is a lease if it conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period in exchange for consideration. IFRS 16 uses a **single lessee model**. At commencement the lessee recognises:

- a **lease liability**: the present value of unpaid lease payments (fixed payments, variable payments tied to an index or rate, expected residual value guarantees, a purchase option if reasonably certain to be exercised, and termination penalties if the term reflects exercise), discounted at the rate implicit in the lease or, if that cannot be readily determined, the lessee's **incremental borrowing rate**;
- a **right-of-use (ROU) asset**: the liability plus payments made before commencement and initial direct costs, less incentives received.

Each period the lessee records **interest** on the liability (liability × rate) and **depreciation** of the ROU asset, usually straight-line over the shorter of the lease term and the asset's useful life. Total expense is therefore **front-loaded**. Two exemptions are allowed: leases of 12 months or less with no purchase option (**short-term leases**), and leases of **low-value assets**; payments are then expensed, usually straight-line.

### Lessee accounting under ASC 842

ASC 842 puts every lease longer than 12 months on the balance sheet (ROU asset and liability) but keeps two classes. A lessee classifies a lease as a **finance lease** if any of five criteria is met: ownership transfers; a purchase option is reasonably certain to be exercised; the lease term is for the major part of the asset's remaining economic life (75% or more is the usual benchmark); the present value of payments plus guaranteed residual is substantially all of the asset's fair value (90% or more); or the asset is so specialised that it has no alternative use. A **finance lease** is accounted for as under IFRS 16 (depreciation plus interest). Otherwise it is an **operating lease**, and the lessee records a **single straight-line lease cost** in operating expenses, while the liability still accretes at the discount rate and the ROU asset is reduced by the difference between the straight-line cost and the accretion. Private companies may elect the risk-free rate as the discount rate.

### Lessor accounting

Under **IFRS 16** the lessor classifies each lease as a **finance lease** (substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership transfer) or an **operating lease**. In a finance lease it derecognises the asset, records a **net investment in the lease** (the present value of lease payments plus any unguaranteed residual, at the rate implicit in the lease), and recognises interest income. **ASC 842** classifies lessor leases as **sales-type** (if any of the same five criteria is met), **direct financing** or **operating**; a sales-type lease is accounted for much like an IFRS 16 finance lease, with any selling profit recognised at commencement.

### Comparison

| Point | IFRS 16 | ASC 842 |
|---|---|---|
| Lessee classes | One (finance-like) model | Finance and operating |
| Lessee P&L | Depreciation and interest (front-loaded) | Finance: depreciation and interest; operating: straight-line lease cost |
| Lessee cash flow | Principal in financing; interest per policy | Finance: principal in financing, interest in operating; operating: all operating |
| Lessor classes | Finance and operating | Sales-type, direct financing, operating |

@example
title: A lessee records a five-year lease
problem: Ashford Logistics leases equipment for 5 years, paying \$20,000 at the end of each year. Its incremental borrowing rate is 6%. Record commencement and year 1 under IFRS 16, and compare year 1 expense with an ASC 842 operating lease.
step: Lease liability: $20{,}000 \times \frac{1 - 1.06^{-5}}{0.06} = 20{,}000 \times 4.212364 = \$84{,}247.28$. At commencement, debit ROU asset 84,247.28; credit Lease liability 84,247.28.
step: Year 1 interest is $84{,}247.28 \times 6\% = \$5{,}054.84$. The payment of \$20,000 pays that interest and reduces principal by \$14,945.16, so the liability falls to \$69,302.12. Entry: debit Interest expense 5,054.84; debit Lease liability 14,945.16; credit Cash 20,000.
step: Depreciation is $84{,}247.28 / 5 = \$16{,}849.46$. Entry: debit Depreciation expense 16,849.46; credit Accumulated depreciation (ROU asset) 16,849.46. Year 1 expense is $5{,}054.84 + 16{,}849.46 = \$21{,}904.30$.
step: Under ASC 842 as an operating lease the year 1 cost is the straight-line \$20,000. Entry: debit Lease cost 20,000; debit Lease liability 14,945.16; credit Cash 20,000; credit ROU asset 14,945.16. Over five years total expense is \$100,000 under both approaches; only the timing differs (IFRS 16 is higher early and lower late).
answer: IFRS 16 year 1 expense \$21,904.30 against \$20,000 for an ASC 842 operating lease.

@example
title: The lessor's side
problem: Bramble Equipment Leasing leases equipment (carrying amount \$100,000, fair value \$108,929.92) for 3 years at \$40,000 a year in arrears. The rate implicit in the lease is 5%, and the lease transfers substantially all risks and rewards (an IFRS 16 finance lease and an ASC 842 sales-type lease). Record commencement and year 1.
step: Net investment in the lease is the present value of the payments: $40{,}000 \times \frac{1 - 1.05^{-3}}{0.05} = \$108{,}929.92$.
step: At commencement the lessor derecognises the equipment: debit Net investment in lease 108,929.92; credit Equipment 100,000; credit Selling profit 8,929.92.
step: Year 1 interest income is $108{,}929.92 \times 5\% = \$5{,}446.50$. On receipt of \$40,000: debit Cash 40,000; credit Interest income 5,446.50; credit Net investment in lease 34,553.50.
step: The net investment falls to $108{,}929.92 - 34{,}553.50 = \$74{,}376.42$. After three payments it is zero, and total interest income is $120{,}000 - 108{,}929.92 = \$11{,}070.08$.
answer: Net investment \$108,929.92, selling profit \$8,929.92, year 1 interest income \$5,446.50.

@mistakes
- Discounting at the market rate for the lease payments instead of the rate implicit in the lease or the lessee's incremental borrowing rate.
- Showing a single straight-line expense under IFRS 16 for an ordinary lease: IFRS 16 has no operating lease class for lessees, so the expense is depreciation plus interest.
- Mixing the lessee and lessor models: a lessee records a liability and an ROU asset, while a lessor with a finance lease derecognises the asset and records a receivable.
- Reducing the lease liability by the whole payment instead of the payment less interest.
