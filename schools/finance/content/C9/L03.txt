@article
A company ships a machine, installs it and promises two years of support for one price. When did it earn the revenue? Before 2018 the answer depended on the industry and on which of many rules applied. **IFRS 15** and **ASC 606** replaced those rules with one model built on a single principle: recognise revenue to depict the transfer of promised goods or services to customers in an amount that reflects the consideration the entity expects to be entitled to. The two standards were developed jointly and are largely converged.

### The five steps

1. **Identify the contract.** There must be an approved agreement, identifiable rights and payment terms, commercial substance, and collection of the consideration must be probable.
2. **Identify the performance obligations.** A promise is a separate **performance obligation** if the good or service is distinct: the customer can benefit from it on its own or with readily available resources, and it is separately identifiable within the contract.
3. **Determine the transaction price**: the amount the entity expects to be entitled to, net of amounts collected for third parties such as sales taxes.
4. **Allocate the transaction price** to each performance obligation in proportion to **standalone selling prices (SSP)**.
5. **Recognise revenue** when (or as) each obligation is satisfied, that is, when control of the good or service passes to the customer.

### Variable consideration

Discounts, rebates, refunds, bonuses and penalties make the price variable. Estimate it with the **expected value** (a probability-weighted sum, suited to many similar contracts) or the **most likely amount** (suited to two outcomes). Then apply the **constraint**: include variable consideration only to the extent a significant reversal of cumulative revenue is not expected. IFRS 15 words the test as "highly probable" and ASC 606 as "probable", but ASC 606 defines probable as likely to occur, so the two thresholds are intended to be the same. For sales with a right of return, recognise revenue for the goods not expected to be returned, a **refund liability** for the rest, and an asset for the right to recover the goods, measured by reference to their former carrying amount.

### When revenue is recognised

Revenue is recognised **over time** if any one condition is met: the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefits; the entity's work creates or enhances an asset the customer controls; or the asset has no alternative use to the entity and the entity has an enforceable right to payment for work done. Otherwise revenue is recognised at the point in time when control transfers. Progress for over-time obligations is measured with an **output method** (units delivered, milestones) or an **input method** (such as costs incurred to total expected costs):

$$\text{Cumulative revenue} = \text{transaction price} \times \frac{\text{costs incurred to date}}{\text{total expected costs}}.$$

A **contract asset** is the right to consideration that is conditional on something other than the passage of time; a **receivable** is unconditional; a **contract liability** is an obligation to transfer goods or services for which payment has been received or is due.

### Where IFRS and US GAAP differ

| Topic | IFRS 15 | ASC 606 |
|---|---|---|
| Collectability in step 1 | "Probable" means more likely than not | "Probable" means likely to occur, a higher bar |
| Shipping and handling after control passes | Generally a separate service | Policy election to treat as a fulfilment cost |
| Sales and similar taxes | Excluded from the transaction price as collected for third parties | Policy election to exclude |
| Impairment of capitalised contract costs | Reversal required when conditions improve | Reversal prohibited |

@example
title: A bundle sold for one price
problem: Alder Systems sells a machine, installation and 24 months of support to a customer for \$12,000 cash on 1 January. Standalone selling prices are machine \$10,000, installation \$1,500 and support \$2,500. The machine is delivered and installation is complete on 1 January. How much revenue does Alder recognise in the first year?
step: The total of the SSPs is $10{,}000 + 1{,}500 + 2{,}500 = 14{,}000$, more than the price of 12,000, so the customer received a discount of 2,000 that is spread across all three obligations.
step: Allocate by the SSP share: machine $12{,}000 \times 10/14 = \$8{,}571.43$; installation $12{,}000 \times 1.5/14 = \$1{,}285.71$; support $12{,}000 \times 2.5/14 = \$2{,}142.86$. The total is \$12,000.00.
step: On 1 January: debit Cash 12,000; credit Revenue (machine) 8,571.43; credit Revenue (installation) 1,285.71; credit Contract liability 2,142.86.
step: Support is delivered evenly over 24 months, so after 12 months one half is earned: debit Contract liability 1,071.43; credit Revenue (support) 1,071.43.
step: First-year revenue is $8{,}571.43 + 1{,}285.71 + 1{,}071.43 = \$10{,}928.57$, and \$1,071.43 stays in the contract liability.
answer: \$10,928.57 of revenue in year 1; contract liability of \$1,071.43 at year end.

@example
title: A construction contract over two years
problem: Brightwater Builders contracts to build a facility for \$6,000,000. The customer controls the asset as it is built, so revenue is recognised over time using costs incurred as the input measure. Total expected cost is \$4,500,000. Costs incurred are \$1,800,000 in year 1 and \$1,350,000 in year 2. Find revenue and gross profit in each year.
step: Year 1 progress is $1{,}800{,}000 / 4{,}500{,}000 = 40\%$. Revenue is $40\% \times 6{,}000{,}000 = \$2{,}400{,}000$; gross profit is $2{,}400{,}000 - 1{,}800{,}000 = \$600{,}000$.
step: At the end of year 2, cumulative costs are $3{,}150{,}000$ and progress is $3{,}150{,}000 / 4{,}500{,}000 = 70\%$, so cumulative revenue is \$4,200,000.
step: Year 2 revenue is cumulative revenue less revenue already recognised: $4{,}200{,}000 - 2{,}400{,}000 = \$1{,}800{,}000$. Gross profit is $1{,}800{,}000 - 1{,}350{,}000 = \$450{,}000$.
answer: Year 1 revenue \$2,400,000 (gross profit \$600,000); year 2 revenue \$1,800,000 (gross profit \$450,000).

@mistakes
- Allocating the discount to one obligation only: unless specific criteria are met, the discount is spread in proportion to standalone selling prices across all obligations.
- Recognising revenue when cash is received or the invoice is issued instead of when control transfers.
- Including variable consideration in full without applying the constraint, which can lead to a later reversal of revenue.
- Computing year 2 revenue from year 2 progress alone: progress is cumulative, and period revenue is cumulative revenue minus revenue already recognised.

model:
```python
def allocate_price(price, ssps):
    """Allocate a transaction price in proportion to standalone selling prices."""
    total = sum(ssps)
    return [price * ssp / total for ssp in ssps]
```
hint: First add up all the standalone selling prices.
hint: Each obligation gets `price * ssp / total`.
solution: Compute the total of the SSPs once, then give each obligation the transaction price times its share of that total. The allocated amounts add back to the price, whether the price is above or below the sum of the SSPs.
