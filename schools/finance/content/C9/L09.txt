@article
A promise of a pension is a liability that may be paid decades later. How much to record, and where the changes in that liability appear in the statements, are two questions with different answers in IAS 19 and ASC 715.

### Types of benefits and plans

**IAS 19** covers short-term benefits (wages and compensated absences, expensed as the service is given), post-employment benefits, other long-term benefits and termination benefits. Post-employment plans are of two kinds:

- In a **defined contribution (DC) plan** the employer pays fixed contributions and bears no further obligation. Expense equals the contribution for the period, with a liability only for contributions unpaid.
- In a **defined benefit (DB) plan** the employer promises a benefit, typically based on salary and years of service, and bears the investment and actuarial risk.

For a DB plan the **defined benefit obligation (DBO)**, called the **projected benefit obligation (PBO)** under US GAAP, is the present value of benefits earned, discounted at a rate based on high-quality corporate bonds. The plan's assets are measured at fair value. The balance sheet shows the funded status:

$$\text{Net defined benefit liability} = \text{DBO} - \text{fair value of plan assets}.$$

If assets exceed the DBO, an asset is recognised, limited by IFRS to the present value of benefits available as refunds or lower contributions (the asset ceiling).

### The DBO and asset roll-forwards

$$\text{DBO}_{end} = \text{DBO}_{begin} + \text{service cost} + \text{interest cost} + \text{past service cost} \pm \text{actuarial (gains) losses} - \text{benefits paid},$$

$$\text{Assets}_{end} = \text{Assets}_{begin} + \text{actual return} + \text{employer contributions} - \text{benefits paid}.$$

### IAS 19 recognition

Changes in the net liability are split into three parts:

1. **Service cost** (current service cost, past service cost and settlement gains and losses) in profit or loss.
2. **Net interest** in profit or loss: the **discount rate** times the opening net defined benefit liability (or asset). The return on plan assets that is recognised in profit or loss is therefore the discount rate times assets, not an expected rate.
3. **Remeasurements** in **OCI**: actuarial gains and losses on the DBO, and the return on plan assets above or below the discount-rate interest on assets. They are **never reclassified to profit or loss** (a transfer within equity is allowed). **Past service cost** from a plan amendment is recognised in profit or loss immediately.

### ASC 715 recognition

US GAAP also reports the funded status on the balance sheet, but profit is measured differently. **Net periodic pension cost** consists of service cost, **interest cost** on the PBO (discount rate times PBO), a deduction for the **expected return on plan assets** (a long-term expected rate times assets), and amortisation of amounts held in accumulated OCI. Actuarial gains and losses, and the difference between actual and expected return on assets, go to OCI first. Net gains and losses outside a **corridor**, 10% of the greater of the PBO and the market-related value of plan assets, are amortised into pension cost over the average remaining service period. **Prior service cost** goes to OCI and is amortised over the remaining service period of active employees. Since **ASU 2017-07**, only service cost is presented with other employee compensation in operating income; the other components of net periodic cost are presented outside it.

### Summary

| Item | IAS 19 | ASC 715 |
|---|---|---|
| Return on plan assets in P&L | Discount rate (net interest) | Expected long-term rate |
| Actuarial gains and losses | OCI, never recycled | OCI, then amortised outside the corridor |
| Past service cost | Immediately in P&L | OCI, amortised |
| Balance sheet | Net liability or asset (ceiling applies) | Funded status |

Analysts often treat the interest and expected-return components as financing in nature and keep only service cost in operating profit, which is the presentation ASU 2017-07 requires.

@example
title: Pension cost under both frameworks
problem: A plan has a DBO of \$1,000 thousand and plan assets of \$900 thousand at the start of the year. The discount rate is 5%. During the year: current service cost \$80 thousand, benefits paid \$60 thousand, employer contributions \$70 thousand, actual return on plan assets \$63 thousand, and an actuarial loss on the DBO of \$25 thousand. Under US GAAP the expected long-term return on assets is 6%. Compute the closing net liability and the cost and OCI under each framework.
step: Closing DBO is $1{,}000 + 80 + 50 + 25 - 60 = \$1{,}095$ thousand (interest cost is $5\% \times 1{,}000 = 50$). Closing assets are $900 + 63 + 70 - 60 = \$973$ thousand. Net liability is $1{,}095 - 973 = \$122$ thousand, up from \$100 thousand.
step: IAS 19: service cost 80 plus net interest $5\% \times (1{,}000 - 900) = 5$, so profit or loss bears \$85 thousand. Remeasurements: interest on assets was $5\% \times 900 = 45$, so the asset gain is $63 - 45 = 18$; net with the actuarial loss of 25, OCI is a loss of \$7 thousand.
step: IAS 19 entry: debit Service cost 80; debit Net interest expense 5; debit OCI remeasurement loss 7; credit Cash 70; credit Net defined benefit liability 22.
step: ASC 715: expected return is $6\% \times 900 = 54$, so net periodic cost is $80 + 50 - 54 = \$76$ thousand. OCI: actuarial loss 25 less the asset gain $63 - 54 = 9$, a net loss of \$16 thousand. Assuming no earlier balance in accumulated OCI, the loss of 16 is within the corridor (10% of \$1,000 is \$100), so nothing is amortised.
step: ASC 715 entry: debit Pension cost 76; debit OCI 16; credit Cash 70; credit Net pension liability 22. Both frameworks show the same liability change of \$22 thousand, but the split between profit and OCI differs (\$85 against \$76 thousand).
answer: Net liability \$122 thousand; IFRS cost \$85 thousand and OCI loss \$7 thousand; US GAAP cost \$76 thousand and OCI loss \$16 thousand.

@example
title: A plan amendment
problem: An employer amends its plan, raising the DBO by \$120 thousand for service already rendered. Active employees have an average of 15 years of remaining service. Compare year 1 profit effects under IFRS and US GAAP, ignoring tax.
step: Under IAS 19 the increase is **past service cost**, recognised in profit or loss immediately: \$120 thousand expense in year 1.
step: Under ASC 715 the increase is **prior service cost**, recognised in OCI and amortised over 15 years: $120/15 = \$8$ thousand of pension cost a year.
step: Year 1 profit is therefore \$112 thousand lower under IFRS than under US GAAP. The liability rises by \$120 thousand in both.
answer: IFRS \$120 thousand in year 1; US GAAP \$8 thousand a year over 15 years.

@mistakes
- Using an expected rate of return on plan assets under IFRS: IAS 19 uses the discount rate.
- Recycling IFRS remeasurements to profit or loss later: IAS 19 remeasurements stay in OCI.
- Forgetting that the DBO grows with interest cost, and that employer contributions go into plan assets and do not reduce the DBO, while benefits paid reduce both the DBO and the assets.
- Treating a DC plan like a DB plan: the DC expense is simply the contribution, with no actuarial estimates.

@quiz num
prompt: Under US GAAP, a plan has an opening PBO of \$2,000 thousand and opening plan assets of \$1,800 thousand. The discount rate is 6% and the expected long-term return on assets is 7%. Current service cost is \$150 thousand and there is no amortisation. What is net periodic pension cost, in thousands of dollars?
answer: 144
lead: \$
check: 150 + 0.06 * 2000 - 0.07 * 1800
hint: Net periodic cost = service cost + interest cost - expected return on assets.
solution: Interest cost is $6\% \times 2{,}000 = 120$ and expected return is $7\% \times 1{,}800 = 126$. Net periodic cost is $150 + 120 - 126 = \$144$ thousand.
