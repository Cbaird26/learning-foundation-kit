@article
When the numbers change after they were published, the reader needs to know why. A change in a method, a better estimate and a mistake look alike in the arithmetic but are different in substance, and standards treat each one differently so that the comparison between periods stays reliable.

### The three situations

- **Change in accounting policy** (a **change in accounting principle** under US GAAP): switching from one acceptable method to another, such as FIFO to weighted average for inventory.
- **Change in accounting estimate**: a revision of a judgement because of new information or developments, such as a change in useful life, residual value, expected credit losses or warranty cost.
- **Prior period error**: an omission or misstatement in earlier financial statements from a failure to use reliable information that was available, a mathematical mistake, or misuse of facts.

When it is hard to tell a policy change from an estimate change, treat it as an estimate. A change in depreciation method is a change in estimate under both frameworks (US GAAP calls it a change in estimate effected by a change in accounting principle).

### IAS 8

An entity changes a policy only if an IFRS requires it or the change gives reliable and **more relevant** information. The change is applied **retrospectively**: comparative amounts are restated as if the new policy had always applied, and the opening balance of retained earnings (or other equity) of the earliest period presented is adjusted. If it is impracticable to determine the effect, the policy is applied from the earliest date practicable. A third statement of financial position is presented at the start of the earliest comparative period when the restatement has a material effect. A change in estimate is applied **prospectively**, in the period of the change and any future periods affected. A material prior period error is corrected **retrospectively** by restating the comparatives. On first application of a new standard the entity follows its transition provisions; **IFRS 18** is applied retrospectively, with comparatives restated for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027.

### ASC 250

US GAAP permits a change in accounting principle only if required by a new standard or if the entity justifies the new principle as **preferable**. It is applied retrospectively to all prior periods presented, with a cumulative effect adjustment to opening retained earnings of the earliest period presented and disclosure of the effect on income and EPS. A change in estimate is prospective. Errors are corrected through **restatement**. SEC registrants distinguish a restatement of previously issued statements that were materially misstated from a smaller **revision** when the error is immaterial to prior periods but would be material if corrected in the current period; errors immaterial to both are corrected in the current period. **ASU 2023-08** (certain crypto assets measured at fair value through net income, for fiscal years beginning after December 15, 2024, with early adoption allowed) is adopted by a cumulative-effect adjustment to opening retained earnings at the start of the year of adoption. In contrast, IFRS has no specific standard for holders of crypto assets, which are generally intangibles (IAS 38) or inventory held for sale in the ordinary course (IAS 2).

### Comparison

| Item | IAS 8 | ASC 250 |
|---|---|---|
| Voluntary policy change | Only if more relevant and reliable | Only if preferable |
| Application | Retrospective | Retrospective |
| Estimate change | Prospective | Prospective |
| Error | Retrospective restatement | Restatement (or revision or current-period correction by materiality) |

@example
title: A change in estimate
problem: Ingleby Manufacturing bought a machine for \$90,000 with an 8-year life and a \$10,000 residual value, depreciated straight-line. After 3 years it revises the total life to 10 years and the residual value to \$4,000. How should the change be accounted for and what is the new annual depreciation?
step: Original depreciation is $(90{,}000 - 10{,}000)/8 = \$10{,}000$ a year. After 3 years, accumulated depreciation is \$30,000 and the carrying amount is \$60,000.
step: A change in useful life or residual value is a change in estimate, applied prospectively. Prior years are not restated.
step: The remaining life is $10 - 3 = 7$ years, so new depreciation is $(60{,}000 - 4{,}000)/7 = \$8{,}000$ a year from year 4.
answer: Prospective change; depreciation of \$8,000 a year from year 4.

@example
title: A change in inventory policy
problem: Pelham Retail changes from FIFO to weighted average cost voluntarily and justifies it as more relevant. Inventory at the start of the earliest period presented was \$600,000 under FIFO and \$570,000 under weighted average. The tax rate is 25%. Record the effect on opening retained earnings.
step: A policy change is retrospective: restate as if weighted average had always been used. Opening inventory falls by $600{,}000 - 570{,}000 = \$30{,}000$.
step: The tax effect is $30{,}000 \times 25\% = \$7{,}500$, a reduction in the deferred tax liability (or taxes payable), so opening retained earnings fall by $30{,}000 - 7{,}500 = \$22{,}500$.
step: Adjustment to opening balances: debit Retained earnings 22,500; debit Deferred tax liability 7,500; credit Inventory 30,000. The comparative periods' cost of goods sold and net income are restated too.
step: The notes disclose the nature of the change, why it is preferable or more relevant, and the amount of the adjustment for each line item and for EPS.
answer: Opening retained earnings decrease by \$22,500.

@mistakes
- Restating prior periods for a change in estimate: estimates are changed prospectively.
- Treating the correction of an error as a change in estimate: an error is a failure to use reliable information that existed, and requires restatement.
- Forgetting the tax effect when computing the opening retained earnings adjustment for a policy change.
- Applying a change in accounting principle prospectively because it is convenient: voluntary policy changes are retrospective unless impracticable.
