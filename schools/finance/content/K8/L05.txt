@article
**Matter** is anything that has mass and takes up space. A rock, water and even the air in a balloon are matter.

### Properties
A **property** tells about matter. Color, shape, hardness, and whether it floats or sinks are properties. Two properties can be measured:
- **Mass** tells how much matter is in something. We measure it in grams (g) with a scale.
- **Volume** tells how much space something takes up. We measure liquid volume in milliliters (mL) with a measuring cup.

### Three states
Matter is usually a solid, a liquid or a gas.

| State | Shape | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| **Solid** (a block of wood) | keeps its own shape | stays the same |
| **Liquid** (juice) | takes the shape of its cup | stays the same |
| **Gas** (air in a balloon) | spreads out to fill its container | fills the whole container |

Tiny pieces called **particles** make up all matter. In a solid, they are packed close and only jiggle. In a liquid, they slide past each other. In a gas, they zoom around far apart.

### Changes of state
Heat can change matter from one state to another. Water is a good example:
- **Melting**: ice (solid) becomes water (liquid).
- **Freezing**: water becomes ice. Water freezes at $0$ °C.
- **Boiling and evaporation**: water becomes water vapor, a gas. Water boils at $100$ °C.
- **Condensation**: water vapor cools and becomes drops of liquid, like the drops on a cold glass.

It is the same water in every state. Only the state changes.

### Gas has mass
An empty balloon weighs a little less than the same balloon filled with air. Air is matter!

@example
title: Name the state
problem: Is milk in a cup a solid, a liquid or a gas? How can you tell?
step: Milk pours and takes the shape of its cup.
step: It does not spread out to fill the whole room, so it is not a gas.
step: Milk is a liquid.

@example
title: Add the masses
problem: A toy block has a mass of 40 g. A second block has a mass of 25 g. What is the mass of both together?
step: Write the numbers: $40$ and $25$.
step: Add them: $40 + 25 = 65$. The mass is 65 g.

@mistakes
- Thinking that air is nothing. Air is matter. It has mass and takes up space.
- Mixing up mass (how much matter) with volume (how much space).
- Thinking that melted ice is a different substance. It is still water.
