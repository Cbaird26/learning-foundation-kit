@article
Lessons 1 to 3 described output in the long run, when prices adjust and output is set by capital, labor and technology. In the **short run**, prices are sticky and output is determined by demand. The **IS-LM model** shows how the goods market and the money market together determine output $Y$ and the interest rate.

### Assumptions

The price level is fixed (set $P = 1$), the economy is closed, and expected inflation is zero, so the nominal and real interest rates are both $r$ (written in percentage points: $r = 5$ means 5%). Output is demand-determined: firms produce whatever is demanded.

### The IS curve: the goods market

Expenditure is $Y = C + I + G$ with

$$C = c_0 + c_1(Y - T), \qquad I = b_0 - b_1 r,$$

where $c_1$ is the **marginal propensity to consume** ($0<c_1<1$), $G$ and $T$ are government purchases and net taxes, and investment falls when the interest rate rises. Substituting and solving gives the **IS curve**:

$$Y = \frac{c_0 - c_1 T + b_0 + G}{1 - c_1} - \frac{b_1}{1 - c_1}\,r.$$

The IS curve slopes down in $(Y, r)$ space: a higher interest rate reduces investment and, through the multiplier, output. The factor $\dfrac{1}{1-c_1}$ is the **government purchases multiplier**: at a given interest rate, a rise $\Delta G$ shifts the curve right by $\Delta G/(1-c_1)$. The **tax multiplier** is $-c_1/(1-c_1)$, smaller in size because part of a tax change is absorbed by saving. A balanced-budget increase ($\Delta G = \Delta T$) raises $Y$ by exactly $\Delta G$.

### The LM curve: the money market

People demand real money balances $L(Y, r) = kY - hr$: more with higher income (transactions), less when the interest rate, the opportunity cost of holding money, is higher. The central bank sets the real money supply $M/P$. The **LM curve** is the combinations of $(Y, r)$ with $M/P = kY - hr$:

$$Y = \frac{M/P + hr}{k}.$$

It slopes up: higher income raises money demand, which requires a higher interest rate to restore equilibrium. A rise in $M/P$ shifts LM right (down). In practice the central bank targets an interest rate and supplies the reserves to hit it; the LM curve is the same relationship viewed from the money supply.

### Equilibrium and policy

Equilibrium is where IS and LM cross. **Fiscal expansion** shifts IS right: output rises but so does the interest rate, which lowers investment. This is **crowding out**, so the total increase in $Y$ is less than the simple multiplier. **Monetary expansion** shifts LM right: the interest rate falls, and investment and output rise. The effectiveness of each depends on slopes: if money demand is very sensitive to the interest rate (very large $h$), LM is nearly flat and fiscal policy is powerful; if investment is insensitive to $r$ (small $b_1$), IS is steep and monetary policy is weak.

A **liquidity trap** occurs when the interest rate is at the effective lower bound: people are willing to hold any amount of money at that rate, LM is flat, and further expansion of the money supply does not lower the rate, leaving fiscal policy (or unconventional policy) as the tool.

### Fixed prices only

IS-LM holds the price level fixed. Lesson 8 lets prices move by deriving aggregate demand from the model.

@example
title: Solving for equilibrium
problem: $C = 200 + 0.75(Y - T)$, $I = 400 - 20r$, $G = 500$, $T = 400$, and money demand $L = 0.5Y - 40r$ with $M/P = 1{,}000$. Find the IS curve, the LM curve and the equilibrium.
step: IS: $Y = 200 + 0.75(Y - 400) + 400 - 20r + 500 = 800 + 0.75Y - 20r$, so $0.25Y = 800 - 20r$ and $Y = 3200 - 80r$.
step: LM: $1000 = 0.5Y - 40r$, so $Y = 2000 + 80r$.
step: Set IS equal to LM: $3200 - 80r = 2000 + 80r$, so $r = 7.5$ and $Y = 3200 - 600 = 2600$. Check the LM: $2000 + 80(7.5) = 2600$.
step: Components: $C = 200 + 0.75(2200) = 1850$, $I = 400 - 150 = 250$, $G = 500$; the sum is $2600$.
answer: $r = 7.5\%$ and $Y = 2600$.

@example
title: Fiscal expansion and crowding out
problem: In the economy above, government purchases rise by 100. Find the new equilibrium and the amount of crowding out.
step: The multiplier is $1/(1 - 0.75) = 4$. At a given interest rate, IS shifts right by $4 \times 100 = 400$: $Y = 3600 - 80r$.
step: Equilibrium: $3600 - 80r = 2000 + 80r$, so $r = 10$ and $Y = 2800$.
step: Output rises by 200, only half the simple multiplier effect of 400, because the interest rate rises 2.5 points. Investment falls from $400 - 150 = 250$ to $400 - 200 = 200$.
step: Check the components: $C = 200 + 0.75(2800 - 400) = 2000$, $I = 200$, $G = 600$: sum 2,800. Consumption rose by 150, government by 100 and investment fell by 50, for a total of 200.
answer: $Y = 2800$, $r = 10\%$; output rises 200, with investment crowded out by 50.

@mistakes
- Using the simple multiplier $1/(1-c_1)$ for the total change in output, ignoring the rise in the interest rate that crowds out investment.
- Shifting the wrong curve: a change in $G$, $T$ or confidence shifts IS; a change in the real money supply shifts LM.
- Using the government purchases multiplier for tax changes; the tax multiplier is $-c_1/(1-c_1)$.
- Applying IS-LM when prices are flexible and the economy is at full employment; it describes demand-determined output at a fixed price level.

prompt: In an economy with marginal propensity to consume $c_1 = 0.75$, government purchases rise by 50. By how much does the IS curve shift to the right at any given interest rate? Give the horizontal shift in output.
answer: 200
check: 50 / (1 - 0.75)
hint: The shift equals $\Delta G$ times the multiplier $1/(1 - c_1)$.
solution: $\Delta Y = 50/(1 - 0.75) = 200$ at an unchanged interest rate. The actual change in equilibrium output is smaller because of crowding out.

@practice num
prompt: With $c_1 = 0.75$, taxes are cut by 100. By how much does output change at an unchanged interest rate (the horizontal shift of IS)?
answer: 300
check: 0.75 * 100 / (1 - 0.75)
hint: The tax multiplier is $-c_1/(1 - c_1)$ and the tax change is $-100$.
solution: $\Delta Y = -\dfrac{0.75}{0.25} \times (-100) = +300$. The tax cut raises disposable income by 100, of which 75 is spent in the first round.

@practice mc
prompt: Which statement correctly describes a liquidity trap?
choice*: The interest rate is at its lower bound, LM is flat, and expanding the money supply does not lower the interest rate further.
choice: Money demand does not depend on income, so LM is vertical.
choice: Investment does not depend on the interest rate, so IS is vertical.
choice: Fiscal policy is ineffective because it crowds out all private investment.
hint: Think about what happens to the interest rate when it cannot fall.
solution: In a liquidity trap, increases in $M/P$ are absorbed into money holdings with no fall in $r$, so monetary expansion does not stimulate investment; fiscal expansion raises output with no crowding out, because the interest rate does not rise.

answer: 2920
check: (200 - 0.75 * 400 + 400 + 580) / (1 - 0.75) - (20 / (1 - 0.75)) * (0.5 * ((200 - 0.75 * 400 + 400 + 580) / (1 - 0.75)) - 1160) / (0.5 * (20 / (1 - 0.75)) + 40)
hint: IS becomes $Y = 3520 - 80r$ and LM becomes $Y = 2320 + 80r$.
solution: Equating $3520 - 80r = 2320 + 80r$ gives $r = 7.5$ and $Y = 2920$. The interest rate is unchanged from the original equilibrium of $r = 7.5$ with $Y = 2600$ (the intercept of IS rises by $4 \times 80 = 320$ and the intercept of LM by $160/0.5 = 320$), and output rises by the full simple multiplier effect of 320, which shows how monetary accommodation can prevent crowding out.

@quiz num
prompt: An economy has $C = 150 + 0.8(Y - T)$, $I = 300 - 25r$, $G = 400$ and money demand $L = 0.4Y - 50r$ with $M/P = 800$, where $r$ is in percent. Taxes are cut from 250 to 200. What is the new equilibrium output?
answer: 2725
check: (800 + 50 * root(lambda r: (150 - 0.8 * 200 + 300 + 400 - 25 * r) / 0.2 - (800 + 50 * r) / 0.4, 0, 20)) / 0.4
hint: With $T = 200$, the IS curve is $Y = (150 - 0.8 \times 200 + 300 + 400 - 25r)/0.2$; the LM curve is $Y = (800 + 50r)/0.4$.
solution: IS: $0.2Y = 690 - 25r$, so $Y = 3450 - 125r$. LM: $800 = 0.4Y - 50r$, so $Y = 2000 + 125r$. Setting them equal gives $250r = 1450$, so $r = 5.8$ and $Y = 2725$. Before the cut ($T = 250$) the IS curve was $Y = 3250 - 125r$, giving $r = 5$ and $Y = 2625$, so the tax cut raised output by 100.
