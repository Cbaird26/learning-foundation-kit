@article
A country is like a very large household that trades with everyone else. Over a year it sells goods, services and assets to foreigners and buys the same from them. The **balance of payments** (BOP) is the statistical record of all those transactions between residents and non-residents over a period. It uses double-entry bookkeeping: every transaction has two sides, so the whole record sums to zero apart from measurement error.

### The accounts

The IMF's *Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual* (sixth edition) divides the record into three accounts.

- **Current account (CA):** trade in goods, trade in services, **primary income** (compensation of employees, interest, dividends and profits earned on foreign assets or paid on foreign liabilities) and **secondary income** (transfers with nothing received in return, such as workers' remittances and grants).
- **Capital account (KA):** small items: capital transfers such as debt forgiveness and the purchase or sale of non-produced, non-financial assets such as patents or land rights.
- **Financial account (FA):** transactions in financial assets and liabilities: **direct investment** (a lasting interest, conventionally an ownership stake of at least 10% of the voting power), **portfolio investment** (equity below 10% and bonds), **other investment** (loans, deposits, trade credit) and **reserve assets** (foreign currency, gold and similar assets held by the central bank).

### Sign convention and the identity

Exports and income received are **credits**; imports and income paid are **debits**. This lesson measures the financial account as the **net capital inflow**: new liabilities to foreigners (foreigners buying domestic assets or lending to residents) minus new foreign assets acquired by residents, with reserve assets included. With this convention,

$$CA + KA + FA + EO = 0,$$

where $EO$ is **net errors and omissions**, the statistical discrepancy. The IMF manual reports the financial account the other way round, as net acquisition of assets minus net incurrence of liabilities, so in its tables $CA + KA + EO = FA$: net lending from the current and capital accounts equals net lending measured in the financial account, apart from errors and omissions. Always check which convention a table uses before combining numbers.

The meaning is plain: a country with a current account deficit pays for its excess of spending abroad by selling assets or borrowing, so it runs a financial account surplus (a net inflow) of the same size.

### Saving and investment

National income is spent on consumption, investment and net exports, so the current account (ignoring the small capital account) satisfies

$$CA = S - I,$$

where $S$ is national saving and $I$ is domestic investment. A country that invests more than it saves must borrow the difference from the rest of the world. A deficit is therefore not good or bad by itself: it may finance productive investment or consumption.

### Flows and stocks

The BOP records **flows**. The **net international investment position** (NIIP) is the **stock** of foreign assets owned by residents minus the stock of domestic assets owned by foreigners. Ignoring the small capital account and errors, the change in the NIIP over a year equals the current account balance plus valuation changes (price and exchange-rate effects on the existing stock). Persistent deficits push the NIIP toward more negative values.

For current figures use the IMF and national statistical agencies; the numbers in this course are invented.

@example
title: Reading Tavira's balance of payments
problem: The fictional country Tavira reports these items for a year, in billions of its currency units: goods exports 420, goods imports 465, services exports 130, services imports 95, primary income received 60, primary income paid 75, secondary income received 20, secondary income paid 38, capital account balance +2, and net errors and omissions -3. Find the current account balance and the financial account balance (net inflow convention).
step: Balance on goods: $420 - 465 = -45$. Balance on services: $130 - 95 = +35$.
step: Primary income: $60 - 75 = -15$. Secondary income: $20 - 38 = -18$.
step: Current account: $-45 + 35 - 15 - 18 = -43$, a deficit of 43.
step: Use $CA + KA + FA + EO = 0$: $-43 + 2 + FA - 3 = 0$, so $FA = 44$. Tavira had a net capital inflow of 44, which financed the deficit.
answer: CA = -43; FA = +44 (net inflow).

@example
title: A deficit from the saving side
problem: Tavira's GDP is 1,000, national saving is 220 and domestic investment is 260 (same units, same year). Ignoring the capital account, what is the current account as a percent of GDP, and what does it say about borrowing?
step: $CA = S - I = 220 - 260 = -40$.
step: As a share of GDP: $-40/1{,}000 = -4.0\%$.
step: Tavira invests 40 more than it saves, so it must attract a net capital inflow of about 40 (4% of GDP) from the rest of the world.
answer: CA = -40, or -4.0% of GDP, financed by a net capital inflow of about 40.

@mistakes
- Mixing sign conventions: adding the IMF-style financial account (net acquisition of assets) to the current account as if it were a net inflow.
- Treating a purchase of foreign shares by a resident as a current account item; it is a financial account transaction.
- Reading a current account deficit as "the country lost money": it means residents spent more abroad than they earned, financed by borrowing or by selling assets.
- Confusing the flow (the BOP) with the stock (the NIIP).

@practice num
prompt: A country has a current account balance of +35, a capital account balance of 0, net errors and omissions of -4, and a net private capital flow (all financial account items except reserve assets) of -20, an outflow (all in billions). By the identity $CA + KA + FA + EO = 0$ with reserves counted in $FA$, by how much do its reserve assets rise? Give the increase in billions (positive for a rise).
answer: 11
tol: 0.01
check: 35 + 0 - 4 - 20
hint: Total financial account (net inflow) is the private flow minus the increase in reserve assets.

solution: $FA = -(35 + 0 - 4) = -31$. Private flows contribute $-20$, so the reserve component is $-11$: reserves rose by 11 billion. The surplus on the current account not lent out privately was absorbed by the central bank.

@quiz num
prompt: At the start of a year a country's net international investment position is -300 (billions). During the year its current account balance is -50 and valuation changes (prices and exchange rates) raise the value of its net position by 20. Ignoring the capital account and errors, what is the NIIP at the end of the year, in billions?
answer: -330
tol: 0.01
check: -300 + (-50) + 20
hint: The change in the NIIP is the current account balance plus valuation changes.
solution: $\Delta \text{NIIP} = CA + \text{valuation} = -50 + 20 = -30$, so the end-of-year NIIP is $-300 - 30 = -330$.

@quiz self
prompt: In four or five sentences, explain why a country with a current account deficit must also have a financial account surplus (net capital inflow), and how this relates to national saving and investment.
hint: Start from the balance-of-payments identity and the fact that the record is double-entry.
solution: Every transaction is recorded twice, so the accounts sum to zero apart from errors: $CA + KA + FA + EO = 0$. If the current account is in deficit, residents bought more goods, services and income abroad than they sold, and they paid the difference by selling assets or borrowing from foreigners. That is a net capital inflow, a financial account surplus in this lesson's convention. In terms of saving and investment, $CA = S - I$: a deficit means domestic investment exceeds national saving, and the shortfall is financed by foreign savings.
rubric: States the identity (or the double-entry logic) that the accounts sum to zero apart from errors and omissions.
rubric: Explains that the deficit is paid for by selling assets or borrowing, which is a net capital inflow.
rubric: Links the deficit to investment exceeding national saving, $CA = S - I$.
