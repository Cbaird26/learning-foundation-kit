objective: Plan and give a short, clear presentation.

@article
When you use facts from a source, you give **credit**. Giving credit is called **citing** the source. It shows where you learned the fact and is honest.

### A simple citation

A **citation** lists the source. For a book, write:

**Author. *Title*. Year.**

Example: Lee, Ana. *My Body*. 2020.

For a website, write the group or author, the page title and the website name:

Example: Kids Health Club. "How the Heart Works." Body Facts Website.

Ask your grown-up to help find the author and year.

### Credit inside a sentence

You can name the source as you write:

- "According to *My Body*, the heart is a muscle."
- "The website Body Facts says the heart pumps blood."

### A source list

At the end of your report, make a list called **Sources**. Put each citation on its own line, in A-B-C order by the first word.

### Presenting

When you present, you **share** your report out loud.

1. **Plan:** Pick your 3 main facts. Write each on a card with a few words.
2. **Practice:** Say it out loud two or three times.
3. **Speak:** Look at your listeners, talk slowly and loudly, and do not rush.
4. **Show:** Hold up a drawing or a model to help.
5. **Finish:** Say thank you and ask, "Any questions?"

### Listening

A good listener looks at the speaker, keeps quiet and thinks of one question to ask at the end.

@example
title: Cite two sources
problem: You used a book called "Tiny Bones" by Raj Patel, 2019, and a website page called "Why Bones Are Strong" from Body Facts Website. Write a source list and a sentence with credit.
step: Write the book citation: Patel, Raj. Tiny Bones. 2019.
step: Write the website citation: Body Facts Website. "Why Bones Are Strong."
step: Write a sentence: "According to Tiny Bones, bones give the body its shape."

@mistakes
- Forgetting to name the source for a fact.
- Reading your report word for word instead of talking to the listeners.
- Speaking too fast or too quietly.
