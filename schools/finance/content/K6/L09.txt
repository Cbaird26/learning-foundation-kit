@article
**Informative writing** teaches the reader facts about a topic. A five-paragraph essay is a good plan for it.

### The plan

1. **Paragraph 1, introduction:** Tell the topic. Start with a fact or question that grabs the reader. End with your **main idea**, also called a thesis.
2. **Paragraph 2, body:** Tell the first big point, with facts.
3. **Paragraph 3, body:** Tell the second big point, with facts.
4. **Paragraph 4, body:** Tell the third big point, with facts.
5. **Paragraph 5, conclusion:** Remind the reader of the main idea. Say the big idea in new words.

### Main idea

A good main idea sentence names the topic and your three points. Example: "Dogs make great pets because they are loyal, playful and helpful."

The three points become your three body paragraphs: loyal, playful, helpful.

### Body paragraphs

Each body paragraph has:

- A **topic sentence** that tells the point.
- Two or three **facts** or examples.
- A **closing sentence**.

### Transition words

**Transitions** link ideas. Try: *first, second, also, for example, in addition, finally, in conclusion.*

### Plan before you write

Make a small chart:

| Paragraph | Point |
|---|---|
| Intro | Dogs make great pets |
| Body 1 | Loyal |
| Body 2 | Playful |
| Body 3 | Helpful |
| Conclusion | Dogs are great pets |

Write the plan first. Then write one paragraph at a time. Last, read it aloud and fix mistakes.

@example
title: Write a main idea and topic sentence
problem: Your topic is bees. Your three points are making honey, helping flowers and living in hives. Write a main idea and a topic sentence for the first body paragraph.
step: Name the topic and the three points in one sentence: "Bees are important because they make honey, help flowers and live in hives."
step: The first body paragraph is about honey. Start with a topic sentence: "First, bees make honey."
step: Add facts after it. For example, "Bees collect nectar from flowers, and they turn it into honey."

@mistakes
- Starting with the main idea and having no introduction to the topic.
- Putting two big points into one paragraph.
- Ending with a new fact instead of reminding the reader of the main idea.
