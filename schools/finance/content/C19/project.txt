import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(19)
vol = np.array([0.011, 0.007, 0.004, 0.015, 0.009])
corr = np.array([[1.0, 0.3, -0.1, 0.5, 0.2],
                 [0.3, 1.0, 0.4, 0.2, 0.1],

                 [0.2, 0.1, 0.0, 0.3, 1.0]])
cov = np.outer(vol, vol) * corr
chol = np.linalg.cholesky(cov)
df = 5
z = rng.standard_normal((500, 5)) @ chol.T
w = np.sqrt((df - 2) / rng.chisquare(df, size=(500, 1)))
returns = z * w          # 500 days by 5 positions, daily simple returns

### Key ideas, week by week

1. **Risk and the process**: identify, measure, treat (avoid, reduce, transfer, retain), monitor. Expected loss is frequency times severity.
2. **Market risk**: returns, volatility, the square-root-of-time rule (needs iid returns and constant volatility), portfolio variance with correlation, beta, duration and DV01.
3. **VaR**: the loss not exceeded with probability $c$ over horizon $h$, losses positive. Parametric $V(z_c\sigma\sqrt h - \mu h)$; historical (the $\lceil cn\rceil$-th smallest loss); Monte Carlo (full revaluation, sampling error).
4. **Expected shortfall and backtesting**: ES is the average loss beyond VaR, and is coherent; normal ES is $V\sigma\varphi(z_c)/(1-c)$. Exceptions are binomial; Basel traffic lights 0 to 4, 5 to 9, 10 or more; Kupiec LR against 3.841.
5. **Credit risk**: PD (one year), LGD (fraction of EAD), EAD (drawn plus CCF times undrawn); EL $=$ PD $\times$ LGD $\times$ EAD; UL for one loan; cumulative PD; credit triangle; Merton.
6. **Counterparty and liquidity risk**: exposure $\max(V, 0)$, netting, collateral, CVA; LVaR with half the spread; LCR and NSFR.
7. **Operational and model risk**: seven event types, Poisson frequency with lognormal severity, SR 11-7, validation and effective challenge.
8. **Stress testing**: sensitivity, historical, hypothetical and reverse tests; post-stress capital ratio.
9. **Bank capital**: CET1, tier 1, total capital over RWA; minimums 4.5%, 6%, 8%; buffers; leverage ratio; three pillars.
10. **Insurance**: pooling, principles, deductibles, coinsurance, pure and gross premium, state-based regulation in the United States.
11. **Insurer risk**: underwriting, reserve, catastrophe, combined ratio, reinsurance, duration mismatch, Solvency II (99.5% one-year VaR) and RBC.
12. **ERM**: risk appetite, limits, three lines, RAROC, component VaR, culture.

### Key formulas

$\sigma_h = \sigma\sqrt h$; $\sigma_p^2 = \sum_i\sum_j w_iw_j\rho_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j$; $\text{VaR} = V z_c\sigma\sqrt h$ (zero mean); $\text{EL} = \text{PD}\,\text{LGD}\,\text{EAD}$; $\text{RWA} = 12.5\times$ capital charge; $\text{CET1 ratio} = \text{CET1}/\text{RWA}$; $G = (\text{pure premium} + F)/(1 - v)$; $\text{RAROC} = (\text{revenue} - \text{costs} - \text{EL})/\text{economic capital}$.

### Common errors

Forgetting conventions (sign, level, horizon); using volatility where variance belongs; using $z_{0.99}$ for a 95% VaR; missing the CCF; confusing recovery with LGD; adding unexpected losses; forgetting the factor 12.5; dividing by assets instead of RWA; dividing by $1 + v$ instead of $1 - v$ in premiums; applying the coinsurance penalty when it does not apply.

Assume joint normal, iid returns (losses positive). What is the 10-day expected shortfall at the 97.5% confidence level, scaling the one-day figure by the square-root-of-time rule? Round to the nearest dollar.
answer: 572184
lead: \$
tol: 2
check: ((4000000 * 0.012) ** 2 + (6000000 * 0.008) ** 2 + 2 * 0.3 * 4000000 * 0.012 * 6000000 * 0.008) ** 0.5 * NormalDist().pdf(ninv(0.975)) / 0.025 * 10 ** 0.5
hint: Dollar volatilities are \$48,000 and \$48,000; combine them with the correlation.
hint: One-day ES is $\sigma_p\,\varphi(z_{0.975})/0.025$, which is $2.3378\,\sigma_p$; then multiply by $\sqrt{10}$.
solution: $\sigma_p = \sqrt{48{,}000^2 + 48{,}000^2 + 2(0.3)(48{,}000)(48{,}000)} = \$77{,}398$. One-day 97.5% ES is $2.3378 \times 77{,}398 = \$180{,}940$, and the 10-day figure is $180{,}940 \times \sqrt{10} = \$572{,}184$, valid only if returns are iid with constant volatility.
