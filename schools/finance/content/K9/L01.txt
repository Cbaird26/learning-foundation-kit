@lesson
title: Families and communities
summary: Tell what a family and a community are, and describe how communities can be alike and different.
objective: Say that families come in many shapes and sizes.
objective: Explain what a community is and name some places in one.
objective: Tell the difference between a city, a suburb and a rural community.
reading: National Geographic Kids: look up articles about families around the world and about different kinds of communities (city, town, farm).
@article
A **family** is a group of people who care for each other. Some families have two parents. Some have one parent. Some have grandparents, aunts, uncles, foster parents or step-parents. Some families are big and some are small. All of them are real families.

Families can be different in other ways too. They may speak different languages, eat different foods, and celebrate different holidays. A **tradition** is something a family or group does again and again, like a birthday song or a special meal.

### What is a community?

A **community** is a group of people who live, work or play in the same place. Your family is part of a community. So are your neighbors, your school, and the people at the park.

A community has many places. It may have homes, a school, a library, a store, a fire station and a park. People in a community share these places and help each other.

### Different kinds of communities

- A **city** (urban community) has many people, tall buildings and busy streets. Many people take buses or trains.
- A **suburb** is a quieter place near a city. It has many houses and yards.
- A **rural** community is in the countryside. It has farms, fields and fewer people. Neighbors may live far apart.

Every kind of community has good things about it. People choose where to live for many reasons.

### Try this

With a grown-up, look out a window or take a walk. Count the homes, stores and trees you can see. Which kind of community do you live in?

@example
title: Which kind of community?
problem: Sam lives where there are big farms, tall barns and only a few houses. The nearest store is far away. Is this a city, a suburb or a rural community?
step: Look for clues. Farms and barns are in the countryside.
step: Few houses and a far-away store mean few people live close together.
step: These clues tell us it is a rural community.

@example
title: Same and different
problem: Mia's family has a grandmother, a dad and two kids. Leo's family has one mom and one kid. Are both families real families?
step: A family is a group of people who care for each other.
step: Both groups do that.
step: Yes. Both are real families, even though they are different sizes.

@mistakes
- Thinking a family must look one way. Families come in many shapes and sizes.
- Thinking a community is only a building. A community is the people and the places they share.
- Thinking one kind of community is better than the others. Each kind has good things about it.
