**Week by week**

1. **Capital market expectations.** Grinold-Kroner: $D/P - \Delta S + i + g + \Delta(P/E)$. Bond building blocks: yield + rolldown + expected price change - credit losses. Singer-Terhaar blends integrated and segmented premiums. Taylor rule. Forecasting biases.
2. **Asset allocation.** Asset-only, liability-relative, goal-based; portfolio return and variance; surplus return; Black-Litterman, risk budgeting, rebalancing bands.
3. **Equity construction.** Active share, tracking risk, $IR = TC \times IC \times \sqrt{BR}$, core-satellite and budgets.
4. **Fixed-income construction.** Duration and convexity, BPV, immunization conditions, cash flow matching, contingent immunization, rolldown, spread duration.
5. **Derivatives and currency.** Futures for beta and duration, covered interest parity, hedging carry, collars and overlay risks.
6. **Trading, performance and selection.** Implementation shortfall, Brinson, Sharpe, $M^2$, information ratio, manager due diligence.
7. **Private wealth.** Human capital, ability versus willingness, taxes by account type, estates and gifts (labeled figures change yearly).
8. **Institutional investors.** Required return, funded ratio, spending rules, banks, insurers and sovereign funds.
9. **Pathways.** Waterfalls, DPI, RVPI, TVPI, J-curve, direct capitalization, denominator effect, factor and ESG approaches.
10. **Ethics and GIPS.** Name the Standard by number, the fact and the action; time-weighted and composite returns.
11. **Constructed-response technique.** Command words, minutes per point, answer first, numbers, reason.
12. **Timed practice.** Mock under exam conditions, error log by cause, weighted plan.

**Self-check:** Can you compute a required return, a funded ratio and a surplus return? Can you size a futures overlay in both directions? Can you list the immunization conditions? Can you run a Brinson table that sums to the active return? Can you name a Standard by number for a case?

@exam num
prompt: A foundation spends 4.5% of assets a year, expects inflation of 2.8% and has investment costs of 0.5%. Its candidate portfolio is 60% equities with an expected return of 8.2% and 40% bonds with an expected return of 3.9%. By how many percentage points does the portfolio's expected return fall short of the compounded required return? Give it to two decimal places.
answer: 1.48
unit: percentage points
tol: 0.01
check: ((1 + 0.045) * (1 + 0.028) * (1 + 0.005) - 1) * 100 - (0.6 * 8.2 + 0.4 * 3.9)
hint: Compute the required return by compounding the three factors, then the portfolio's expected return.
hint: Required return is about 7.96%; the portfolio earns less.
solution: Required return $= (1.045)(1.028)(1.005) - 1 = 7.96\%$. Portfolio expected return $= 0.6(8.2) + 0.4(3.9) = 6.48\%$. The shortfall is $7.96 - 6.48 = 1.48$ percentage points, so the portfolio does not meet the return objective without more risk or a lower spending rate.

@exam mc
prompt: A manager at Fenwick Asset Management (fictional) runs a US large-cap composite. A client leaves, and a portfolio that was in the composite with a poor return is removed from the composite's history so that the published composite return rises from 6.8% to 7.4%. The manager also presents the firm as "GIPS-compliant for the large-cap strategy only". Which statement is correct?
choice: Both practices are acceptable if the manager discloses them in the presentation footnotes.
choice*: Removing the terminated portfolio violates GIPS, and compliance cannot be claimed for one strategy only, since a firm claims compliance for the whole firm; presenting the inflated record also raises Standard III(D), Performance Presentation.
choice: Removing the portfolio is acceptable because clients leave, but the strategy-only claim violates GIPS.
hint: Terminated portfolios stay in the history, and compliance is firm-wide.
solution: GIPS requires that terminated portfolios stay in the composite history up to termination and that a firm claim compliance for the whole firm, not a single strategy. A published return that is raised by removing a poor performer is not fair, accurate and complete, which engages Standard III(D). Disclosure does not cure the violation.

@exam self
prompt: Case: Halloran Hospital Trust (fictional) has assets of \$240 million, all in bonds with a modified duration of 5.2, and liabilities of \$220 million with a modified duration of 9.0. The sponsor is financially strong. Bond futures have a BPV of \$80 per contract. The chief investment officer, a CFA charterholder, plans to route all futures orders to a broker owned by his brother, whose commissions are higher than those of competing brokers, and has not told the board. (a) Calculate the funded ratio and the BPV of assets and liabilities. (b) Calculate the futures position that matches the BPV of the assets to that of the liabilities. (c) Explain the risk the trust faces if rates fall and give two risks of the overlay. (d) Identify the Standards raised by the broker choice and say what the CIO should do.
hint: Match BPVs: contracts = (liability BPV - asset BPV) divided by the futures BPV.
hint: For (d), consider a conflict of interest and the duty to put the client's interest first.
solution: (a) Funded ratio $= 240/220 = 109.1\%$. Asset BPV $= 240{,}000{,}000 \times 5.2 \times 0.0001 = \$124{,}800$; liability BPV $= 220{,}000{,}000 \times 9.0 \times 0.0001 = \$198{,}000$. (b) $N_f = (198{,}000 - 124{,}800)/80 = 915$, so buy 915 contracts. (c) The liabilities are more rate sensitive than the assets, so if rates fall liabilities rise by more than the assets and the \$20 million surplus erodes; the overlay removes most of this exposure. Risks of the overlay: basis risk, because futures do not move one-for-one with the bond portfolio and curve shifts may be non-parallel, and margin or liquidity risk, because falling prices if rates rise require variation margin in cash that the all-bond portfolio would need to raise by selling bonds. (d) Standard VI(A), Disclosure of Conflicts, requires disclosure of the brother's ownership, and Standard III(A), Loyalty, Prudence and Care, requires him to act for the trust's benefit, including seeking best execution and reasonable commissions. He should disclose the relationship to the board, compare brokers on cost and execution, and route orders by merit.
rubric: Computes the funded ratio (109.1%) and both BPVs (\$124,800 and \$198,000). 1 point.
rubric: Computes the number of contracts as 915 and gives the direction (buy). 1 point.
rubric: Explains the surplus risk if rates fall and names two overlay risks tied to the case. 1 point.
rubric: Identifies Standard VI(A) and Standard III(A) and the corrective actions (disclose, compare brokers). 1 point.
