@article
Ethics is tested at every level and at Level III it appears in item sets and in constructed-response cases for institutions and individuals. The questions are short stories: someone did something; you must say which Standard applies, whether it was violated and what the member should have done. Learn the Standards by number and name, in your own words, and practice applying them to facts.

### The Standards by number

**I Professionalism:** A Knowledge of the Law (follow the stricter of law and Standards), B Independence and Objectivity, C Misrepresentation, D Misconduct, E Competence. **II Integrity of Capital Markets:** A Material Nonpublic Information, B Market Manipulation. **III Duties to Clients:** A Loyalty, Prudence and Care, B Fair Dealing, C Suitability, D Performance Presentation, E Preservation of Confidentiality. **IV Duties to Employers:** A Loyalty, B Additional Compensation Arrangements, C Responsibilities of Supervisors. **V Investment Analysis, Recommendations and Actions:** A Diligence and Reasonable Basis, B Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients, C Record Retention. **VI Conflicts of Interest:** A Disclosure of Conflicts, B Priority of Transactions, C Referral Fees. **VII Responsibilities as a CFA Institute Member or CFA Candidate:** A Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs, B Reference to CFA Institute, the CFA Designation and the CFA Program.

In a case: find the facts, name the Standard, say whether it was violated and give the corrective action. Typical corrective actions: disclose the conflict, stop trading on the information, put client trades first, document the reasonable basis, update the IPS and suitability analysis, and report to a supervisor or compliance.

### Level III ethics themes

- **Duties to clients** (III): suitability must be assessed against the IPS, with clients' circumstances updated; fair dealing means no favoring of some clients in recommendations or trade allocation; loyalty means placing client interest ahead of the member's and the employer's.
- **Conflicts** (VI): disclose compensation, referral arrangements and ownership; client and employer trades follow the clients.
- **Performance** (III D, and GIPS): present performance fairly, accurately and completely.
- **Supervision** (IV C): supervisors need reasonable procedures and must act on violations.
- **Compliance with the law** (I A): when the laws of different countries conflict, follow the stricter.

### GIPS (2020 edition)

The **Global Investment Performance Standards** are voluntary, ethical standards for fair representation and full disclosure of investment performance. Key points: a **firm** claims compliance for the whole firm, not for a single product, and the claim cannot be partial. Returns are **time-weighted** to remove the effect of client cash flows. **Composites** group portfolios managed to the same strategy and must include all fee-paying discretionary portfolios; terminated portfolios remain in the composite history until their termination. Cherry-picking portfolios is a violation. Presentations include composite returns, the benchmark, the number of portfolios and a measure of dispersion, and disclose fees. **Verification** by an independent third party is optional but gives credibility. The standards also address pooled funds and asset owners. **Portability** requires continuing a track record only when the people and process that produced it have moved to the new firm.

### Return calculations

The **time-weighted return** links sub-period returns between external cash flows: $R_{TW} = \prod(1 + R_k) - 1$. A **composite return** is the asset-weighted average of the member portfolios' returns, using beginning-of-period values (or an equivalent method).

### Exam technique

Name the Standard with its number and title ("Standard VI(B), Priority of Transactions"), cite the fact that triggers it, state violation or no violation, and give the action. For GIPS items, say which requirement was breached (composite construction, claim of compliance, calculation or presentation).

@example
title: Time-weighted return and a composite
problem: A portfolio starts at \$100 million, grows to \$110 million just before an inflow of \$20 million, and ends at \$140 million. What is its time-weighted return? Separately, a composite has two portfolios: \$60 million returning 8.0% and \$40 million returning 5.0%. What is the composite return?
step: Sub-period 1 return: $110/100 - 1 = 10.0\%$.
step: After the inflow the value is \$130 million; sub-period 2 return: $140/130 - 1 = 7.69\%$.
step: Link: $1.10 \times 1.0769 - 1 = 18.46\%$.
step: Composite: asset-weighted with beginning values, $(60 \times 8.0 + 40 \times 5.0)/100 = 6.8\%$.
answer: Time-weighted return 18.46%; composite return 6.8%.

@mistakes
- Naming a Standard without the fact that triggers it, or giving only the principle ("act ethically").
- Computing a money-weighted return when GIPS requires time-weighted returns.
- Removing a terminated, underperforming portfolio from a composite's history.
- Claiming partial GIPS compliance: compliance is firm-wide.

@practice num
prompt: A composite contains three portfolios with beginning values of \$120 million, \$80 million and \$50 million, returning 7.2%, 5.5% and 9.0%. What is the composite's asset-weighted return? Give it as a percent to two decimal places.
answer: 7.02
unit: %
tol: 0.01
check: (120 * 7.2 + 80 * 5.5 + 50 * 9.0) / 250
hint: Weight each return by its beginning value and divide by the total.
solution: $(120 \times 7.2 + 80 \times 5.5 + 50 \times 9.0)/250 = 1{,}754/250 = 7.016\%$, about 7.02%.

@practice mc
prompt: A portfolio manager at Alder Asset Management (fictional) buys shares of a small-cap company for her own account the day before she places a large order for the same shares for her clients, expecting the order to push the price up. Which Standard is most directly violated?
choice*: Standard VI(B), Priority of Transactions, because client transactions must come before the member's own.
choice: Standard IV(B), Additional Compensation Arrangements, because she gained a benefit outside her salary.
choice: Standard II(A), Material Nonpublic Information, because she traded before the order was public.
hint: Whose trade should be done first?
solution: Standard VI(B), Priority of Transactions, requires that clients' and the employer's transactions have priority over personal ones. Buying first for herself to profit from the expected client price impact is the classic violation. Material nonpublic information (Standard II(A)) concerns facts about the issuer, not her own order.

@practice num
prompt: A fund begins at \$400 million. Just before an inflow of \$50 million it is worth \$436 million; at the end of the period, after the inflow, it is worth \$510 million. What is its time-weighted return? Give it as a percent to two decimal places.
answer: 14.38
unit: %
tol: 0.01
check: ((436 / 400) * (510 / (436 + 50)) - 1) * 100
hint: Split at the cash flow: before the inflow, and after it on the larger balance.
solution: Sub-period 1: $436/400 - 1 = 9.00\%$. After the inflow the value is \$486 million, so sub-period 2: $510/486 - 1 = 4.94\%$. Linked: $1.09 \times 1.0494 - 1 = 14.38\%$.

@practice mc
prompt: A firm calls itself GIPS-compliant for its large-cap strategy. In building the strategy's composite, it dropped a portfolio that was terminated after a poor year and kept only portfolios still under management. Which statement is correct?
choice: This is acceptable if the firm discloses that terminated portfolios were removed.
choice*: This violates GIPS: terminated portfolios must remain in the composite's history up to their termination date.
choice: This is acceptable because only current clients are relevant to prospective clients.
hint: Think about survivorship bias in composites.
solution: GIPS requires that all fee-paying discretionary portfolios be in a composite, and that terminated portfolios stay in the history through the last period they were managed, to avoid survivorship bias. Disclosure does not cure the omission.

@practice self
prompt: Case: Ines Calder (fictional), a portfolio manager at a private bank, recommends a structured note to a retired client whose IPS says she needs capital preservation and income. She does so because the note's issuer pays her bank a placement fee, which she does not mention to the client. She has not updated the client's circumstances since the IPS was written three years ago. Identify the Standards violated and say what she should do.
hint: There are at least two facts here, a conflict and a suitability gap; name a Standard for each.
solution: Standard VI(A), Disclosure of Conflicts: the placement fee is a conflict that she must disclose to the client in plain language before the recommendation, and she did not. Standard III(C), Suitability: she must consider the client's objectives, constraints and circumstances and update them regularly; a structured note whose capital is at risk is likely unsuitable for a client whose IPS requires capital preservation, and the IPS is stale. Standard III(A), Loyalty, Prudence and Care, is also in play because she placed the bank's fee interest ahead of the client's. She should update the client's profile, assess the note against the IPS, disclose the placement fee, and recommend the note only if it is suitable.
rubric: Identifies Standard VI(A) and the undisclosed placement fee. 1 point.
rubric: Identifies Standard III(C) and the stale IPS and capital-preservation mismatch. 1 point.
rubric: Mentions loyalty (Standard III(A)) or the client's interest versus the bank's. 1 point.
rubric: States corrective actions (update the profile, disclose, reassess suitability). 1 point.

@quiz mc
prompt: At lunch, an analyst at Wren Capital (fictional) hears from a friend who works for a supplier that a listed customer will be acquired at a large premium in two days; the deal is confidential and not public. The analyst's fund holds the customer's shares. Which action is consistent with the Code and Standards?
choice: Increase the fund's position, because the information came from a friend and not from the company.
choice*: Not trade on the information, and bring it to the compliance department, because it is material and nonpublic (Standard II(A)).
choice: Sell the position only for clients who are sensitive to risk, to treat clients fairly.
hint: Is the information material, and is it public?
solution: A confidential takeover premium is material and nonpublic, so Standard II(A), Material Nonpublic Information, prohibits trading or causing others to trade on it. The analyst should refrain and consult compliance. Selectively trading for some clients would also breach fair dealing.

@quiz self
prompt: Case: A manager joins Elm Advisors (fictional) from another firm and wants to advertise the five-year track record of the fund she managed there, which was a team effort with two other managers who did not join. In four or five sentences, say whether the record can be used under GIPS and the Standards, and what disclosure or conditions apply.
hint: Ask whether the people and process that produced the record moved with her.
solution: Under GIPS portability, a record can be linked to a new firm only if the decision-makers and process responsible for the record are substantially the same and the records are available to support it. Because two of the three managers who produced the results did not move, the record is not portable as her own and presenting it as such would be a misrepresentation under Standard I(C) and a failure of fair and complete performance presentation under Standard III(D). She may describe her prior role factually, with clear disclosure that it was a team record achieved at another firm, but not present it as Elm Advisors' composite.
rubric: States the portability test (the same decision-makers and process) and concludes the record cannot simply be used. 1 point.
rubric: Names Standard I(C) Misrepresentation and Standard III(D) Performance Presentation. 1 point.
rubric: Gives a permitted alternative, such as factual disclosure that it was a team record at another firm. 1 point.
