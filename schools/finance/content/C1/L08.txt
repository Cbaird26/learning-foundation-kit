@article
A **liability** is a present obligation arising from past events whose settlement is expected to require an outflow of resources. A **current liability** is due within one year or the operating cycle, whichever is longer: accounts payable, wages and taxes payable, unearned revenue, short-term notes and the **current portion of long-term debt**. The rest are **long-term liabilities**. Liabilities are initially measured at the amount owed (or, for debt, at present value) and interest accrues as time passes. A note's interest is $\text{principal} \times \text{rate} \times \text{time}$.

### Contingent liabilities

A **contingency** is a possible obligation that depends on a future event, such as a lawsuit. Under US GAAP (ASC 450), a company accrues a loss when it is **probable** and the amount can be **reasonably estimated**, discloses it when it is **reasonably possible**, and ignores remote items. Under IFRS (IAS 37), a **provision** is recorded when an outflow is more likely than not and a reliable estimate can be made. Gain contingencies are not recorded until realized.

### Bonds payable

A **bond** is a long-term borrowing sold to many investors. It promises (1) the **face value** (principal) at maturity and (2) periodic **coupon** payments equal to $\text{face} \times \text{stated rate}/\text{payments per year}$. The **market rate** (yield) is what investors require today. Because the coupon is fixed, the **issue price** is the present value of the promised cash flows discounted at the market rate per period:

$$P = C\,\frac{1 - (1+i)^{-n}}{i} + F(1+i)^{-n},$$

where $C$ is the coupon per period, $F$ the face value, $i$ the market rate per period and $n$ the number of periods. This lesson assumes semiannual coupons, so $i$ is half the annual market rate and $n$ is twice the years.

- Market rate = stated rate: issued at **par** (price = face).
- Market rate > stated rate: issued at a **discount** (price < face).
- Market rate < stated rate: issued at a **premium** (price > face).

The **carrying amount** is face value minus the unamortized discount (or plus the unamortized premium). Under the **effective interest method**, each period:

$$\text{Interest expense} = \text{Carrying amount at start} \times i,$$

$$\text{Cash paid} = \text{Coupon}, \qquad \text{Change in carrying amount} = \text{Interest expense} - \text{Coupon}.$$

For a discount bond, expense exceeds the coupon, so the carrying amount grows toward face. For a premium bond, expense is below the coupon, so it falls toward face. At maturity, carrying amount equals face value, which is repaid. Total interest expense over the bond's life equals total cash paid (coupons plus face) minus the amount received at issue. US GAAP presents debt issuance costs as a deduction from the debt's carrying amount, and when the difference is not material it allows straight-line amortization in place of the effective interest method.

@example
title: A bond issued at a discount
problem: On January 1, Arrowwood Co. issues \$500,000 of five-year bonds with a 6% stated rate and semiannual coupons, when the market rate is 8% a year (4% per half-year). Find the issue price, and record the issuance and the first two interest payments (June 30 and December 31).
step: Coupon = \$500,000 × 0.06 / 2 = \$15,000. With $i = 0.04$ and $n = 10$, the price is $P = 15{,}000 \times 8.1109 + 500{,}000 \times 0.67556 = 121{,}664 + 337{,}782 \approx \$459{,}446$. The discount is \$500,000 − \$459,446 = \$40,554.
step: Issuance: debit Cash 459,446 and Discount on Bonds Payable 40,554; credit Bonds Payable 500,000.
step: June 30. Interest expense = \$459,446 × 0.04 = \$18,378 (rounded). Cash paid is \$15,000, so \$3,378 of discount is amortized and the carrying amount is \$462,824. Entry: debit Interest Expense 18,378; credit Cash 15,000 and Discount on Bonds Payable 3,378.
step: December 31. Interest expense = \$462,824 × 0.04 = \$18,513. Discount amortized = \$3,513. Carrying amount = \$466,337.
step: Over the ten periods, interest expense totals 10 × \$15,000 + \$40,554 = \$190,554.
answer: Issue price \$459,446; interest expense \$18,378 and \$18,513; carrying amount \$466,337 after two payments.

@example
title: A bond issued at a premium
problem: A company issues \$100,000 of two-year bonds with an 8% stated rate paying semiannually when the market rate is 6% a year. Find the price and the first period's entry.
step: Coupon = \$100,000 × 0.08 / 2 = \$4,000. With $i = 0.03$ and $n = 4$, the price is $4{,}000 \times 3.7171 + 100{,}000 \times 0.88849 \approx \$103,717$, a premium of \$3,717.
step: Issuance: debit Cash 103,717; credit Bonds Payable 100,000 and Premium on Bonds Payable 3,717.
step: First period: interest expense = \$103,717 × 0.03 = \$3,112. The coupon is \$4,000, so \$888 of premium is amortized. Entry: debit Interest Expense 3,112 and Premium on Bonds Payable 888; credit Cash 4,000. The carrying amount falls to \$102,829.
answer: Price \$103,717; first-period interest expense \$3,112.

@mistakes
- Discounting the cash flows at the stated (coupon) rate rather than the market rate. The coupon sets the cash flows; the market rate sets the price.
- Using the annual market rate for a semiannual period: divide it by 2 and double the number of periods.
- Computing interest expense on face value. Under the effective interest method, expense is the market rate times the carrying amount.
- Letting the discount increase interest expense by a fixed amount that does not change with the carrying amount (that is straight-line amortization, not effective interest).

model:
```python
def bond_schedule(face, coupon_rate, market_rate, years):
    """Carrying amount after each semiannual period (effective interest)."""
    periods = 2 * years
    i = market_rate / 2
    coupon = face * coupon_rate / 2
    carrying = sum(coupon / (1 + i) ** t for t in range(1, periods + 1))
    carrying += face / (1 + i) ** periods
    schedule = []
    for _ in range(periods):
        interest = carrying * i
        carrying += interest - coupon
        schedule.append(carrying)
    return schedule
```
hint: First compute the issue price: the sum of the discounted coupons plus the discounted face.
hint: Each period, interest expense is the carrying amount times the periodic market rate, and the carrying amount changes by interest minus coupon.
solution: Compute the issue price as the present value of the coupons and the face value at half the market rate. Then loop through the periods: interest is the opening carrying amount times the periodic rate, and the carrying amount changes by interest minus the coupon. Discounts accrete up to face; premiums amortize down to it.
