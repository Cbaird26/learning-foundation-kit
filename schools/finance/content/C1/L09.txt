@article
A corporation raises equity by selling shares of stock to owners. Because shareholders are paid only after creditors, equity is the residual claim, and the corporation's balance sheet splits it into **contributed capital** and **retained earnings**. This lesson follows how each moves.

### Stock

**Common stock** carries voting rights and a residual claim on earnings. **Preferred stock** usually has a fixed dividend and priority over common stock in dividends and liquidation. Preferred dividends can be **cumulative**: unpaid dividends (**dividends in arrears**) must be paid before any common dividend. Companies state three share counts: **authorized** (the maximum allowed by the charter), **issued** (sold at some time) and **outstanding** (issued less treasury shares held by the company).

Stock has a **par value**, a nominal legal amount per share (often \$0.01 to \$1). When a corporation issues shares, it credits Common Stock for the par value and credits **Additional Paid-In Capital** (APIC) for the excess received:

$$\text{APIC} = (\text{Issue price} - \text{Par}) \times \text{Shares}.$$

### Treasury stock

When a company buys back its own shares, it records **treasury stock** at cost (the cost method) as a contra-equity account, a deduction from total equity. It is not an asset, and a company cannot make a gain or loss by trading in its own shares. If treasury shares are reissued above cost, the excess is credited to APIC from treasury stock; if below cost, the shortfall reduces that APIC and then retained earnings. Treasury shares receive no dividends and have no vote.

### Dividends

Three dates matter. On the **declaration date**, the board creates a liability: debit Retained Earnings (or Dividends), credit Dividends Payable. On the **record date**, shareholders on the register are determined, with no entry. On the **payment date**, debit Dividends Payable and credit Cash. Dividends are paid only on shares outstanding.

A **stock dividend** distributes additional shares. For a small stock dividend (generally under 20–25% of shares outstanding), the company transfers the market value of the new shares from Retained Earnings to Common Stock (par) and APIC. Total equity does not change. A **stock split** increases the shares and reduces par proportionally (a 2-for-1 split doubles the shares and halves par); there is no journal entry beyond a memo, and retained earnings are unaffected.

### Retained earnings and earnings per share

$$\text{RE}_{\text{end}} = \text{RE}_{\text{begin}} + \text{Net income} - \text{Dividends declared}.$$

The **statement of changes in equity** shows the beginning balance, each change (stock issued, repurchases, net income, dividends) and the ending balance of every component, including accumulated other comprehensive income. **Basic earnings per share** is

$$\text{EPS} = \frac{\text{Net income} - \text{Preferred dividends}}{\text{Weighted-average common shares outstanding}}.$$

Shares issued during the year are weighted by the fraction of the year they were outstanding.

@example
title: Issuing, repurchasing and reissuing shares
problem: Cedar Ridge Corp. issues 10,000 shares of \$1 par common stock for \$18 per share. Later it buys back 1,000 shares at \$22, and then reissues 400 of them at \$25. Record the three entries.
step: Issuance: cash is 10,000 × \$18 = \$180,000. Common Stock is 10,000 × \$1 = \$10,000 and APIC is \$170,000. Debit Cash 180,000; credit Common Stock 10,000 and APIC 170,000.
step: Repurchase: cost is 1,000 × \$22 = \$22,000. Debit Treasury Stock 22,000; credit Cash 22,000.
step: Reissue: cash is 400 × \$25 = \$10,000. The cost of these shares is 400 × \$22 = \$8,800. Debit Cash 10,000; credit Treasury Stock 8,800 and APIC from Treasury Stock 1,200. No gain is reported in income.
answer: Common stock \$10,000 and APIC \$170,000; treasury stock \$22,000 then \$8,800 reissued; APIC from treasury stock \$1,200.

@example
title: Cash and stock dividends
problem: Thornfield Inc. has 50,000 shares of \$1 par common stock outstanding. It declares a \$0.40 per share cash dividend. Later it declares a 10% stock dividend when the market price is \$30. Record both declarations and the cash payment.
step: Cash dividend: 50,000 × \$0.40 = \$20,000. Declaration: debit Retained Earnings 20,000; credit Dividends Payable 20,000. Payment: debit Dividends Payable 20,000; credit Cash 20,000.
step: Stock dividend: new shares = 10% × 50,000 = 5,000. Market value = 5,000 × \$30 = \$150,000. Debit Retained Earnings 150,000; credit Common Stock (5,000 × \$1) 5,000 and APIC 145,000.
step: The stock dividend moves \$150,000 between equity accounts. Total equity is unchanged, but 55,000 shares now share it.
answer: Cash dividend \$20,000; stock dividend of 5,000 shares debits Retained Earnings \$150,000.

@mistakes
- Crediting the whole proceeds of a stock issue to Common Stock instead of splitting par value and APIC.
- Treating treasury stock as an asset or reporting a gain on reissuing it. It is a deduction from equity and gains go to APIC.
- Paying dividends on all issued shares, including treasury shares. Only outstanding shares receive dividends.
- Recording a stock split or stock dividend as a cash outflow or as an expense.

@quiz self
prompt: A manager says, "We bought back \$500,000 of our own stock, so we now own an asset, and if we resell it for more we will record a profit." In four or five sentences, explain where treasury stock appears in the financial statements and why the manager's statements are wrong.
hint: Ask whether a company can own a claim on itself, and how reissue proceeds above cost are reported.
solution: Treasury stock is reported as a deduction from total stockholders' equity, not as an asset, because a company cannot own a claim against itself: repurchasing shares returns capital to owners, reducing both cash and equity. When the shares are reissued above cost, the excess is credited to additional paid-in capital, not income, since a company's transactions in its own shares with its owners are equity transactions rather than earnings. If reissued below cost, the shortfall reduces APIC from treasury stock and then retained earnings.
rubric: States that treasury stock is a contra-equity account that reduces total equity, not an asset.
rubric: States that reissue above cost is credited to APIC and is not a gain in net income.
rubric: Explains why: transactions with owners in the company's own stock are equity transactions, not earnings.
