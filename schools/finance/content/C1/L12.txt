@article
Every US public company files an annual report on **Form 10-K** with the SEC, free to read on **SEC EDGAR**. A 10-K is long, but its layout is standard, so you can find what you need quickly. Between 10-Ks, companies file quarterly **10-Qs** and current reports on **Form 8-K** for major events.

### Anatomy of a 10-K

- **Item 1, Business:** what the company sells, its customers and competitors, and employees.
- **Item 1A, Risk Factors:** the risks management considers material.
- **Item 7, Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A):** management's explanation of results, liquidity, capital resources and critical accounting estimates.
- **Item 8, Financial Statements and Supplementary Data:** the audited balance sheet, income statement, statements of comprehensive income, equity and cash flows, the **notes**, and the auditor's report.
- **Item 9A, Controls and Procedures:** management's assessment of internal control over financial reporting.

Later items cover executive compensation and governance (often incorporated from the proxy statement) and exhibits. The **auditor's report** gives an opinion on the statements and discusses critical audit matters. The **notes** are indispensable: they explain accounting policies (revenue recognition under ASC 606, inventory method, depreciation), details of debt and leases (ASC 842 puts most leases on the balance sheet), income taxes, contingencies and segments (which now show more segment expenses under ASU 2023-07). Companies may also report **non-GAAP measures** such as adjusted earnings; SEC rules require a reconciliation to the nearest GAAP measure, and you should check what was excluded.

### Ratios

Ratios put figures of different sizes on a common footing. Use averages for balance sheet items measured against a period's flow.

| Ratio | Formula |
|---|---|
| Gross margin | (Revenue − COGS) / Revenue |
| Operating margin | Operating income / Revenue |
| Net margin | Net income / Revenue |
| Current ratio | Current assets / Current liabilities |
| Quick ratio | (Cash + short-term investments + receivables) / Current liabilities |
| Debt to equity | Total liabilities / Total equity |
| Interest coverage | EBIT / Interest expense |
| Inventory turnover | COGS / Average inventory |
| Return on assets (ROA) | Net income / Average total assets |
| Return on equity (ROE) | Net income / Average equity |

Definitions vary across analysts (for instance, debt to equity might use only interest-bearing debt), so always state yours and compare a company with its past and with similar companies.

### The DuPont decomposition

$$\text{ROE} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{Net income}}{\text{Revenue}}}_{\text{net margin}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Average assets}}}_{\text{asset turnover}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Average assets}}{\text{Average equity}}}_{\text{equity multiplier}}.$$

It shows whether a high return comes from profitability, from efficient use of assets or from **financial leverage**. Leverage raises ROE but also risk.

@example
title: Ratios for a fictional company
problem: Juniper Ridge Foods reports: revenue \$820,000; COGS \$512,000; operating expenses \$196,000; interest expense \$12,000; income tax \$25,000; net income \$75,000. Balance sheet at year end: cash \$45,000; receivables \$70,000; inventory \$110,000 (\$90,000 at the start); other current assets \$15,000; total assets \$560,000 (\$500,000 at the start); current liabilities \$150,000; total liabilities \$250,000; equity \$310,000 (\$270,000 at the start). Compute the ratios.
step: Operating income = 820,000 − 512,000 − 196,000 = 112,000. Gross margin = 308,000 / 820,000 = 37.56%. Operating margin = 112,000 / 820,000 = 13.66%. Net margin = 75,000 / 820,000 = 9.15%.
step: Current assets = 45,000 + 70,000 + 110,000 + 15,000 = 240,000, so the current ratio = 240,000 / 150,000 = 1.60. Quick ratio = (45,000 + 70,000) / 150,000 = 0.77. Debt to equity = 250,000 / 310,000 = 0.81. Interest coverage = 112,000 / 12,000 = 9.33 times.
step: Average assets = 530,000 and average equity = 290,000. ROA = 75,000 / 530,000 = 14.15%. ROE = 75,000 / 290,000 = 25.86%.
step: Inventory turnover = 512,000 / 100,000 = 5.12 times a year, about 71 days of inventory (365 / 5.12).
answer: Gross margin 37.56%; current ratio 1.60; interest coverage 9.33; ROE 25.86%.

@example
title: DuPont decomposition
problem: Decompose Juniper Ridge Foods' return on equity.
step: Net margin = 75,000 / 820,000 = 9.146%.
step: Asset turnover = 820,000 / 530,000 = 1.547 times. Equity multiplier = 530,000 / 290,000 = 1.828.
step: ROE = 9.146% × 1.547 × 1.828 = 25.86%, the same as 75,000 / 290,000.
step: Interpretation: the return comes from a moderate margin, a healthy asset turnover and moderate leverage (assets are 1.8 times equity).
answer: ROE 25.86% = 9.15% × 1.547 × 1.828.

@mistakes
- Dividing by year-end balances only when a flow measure (net income, COGS) should be compared with the average balance, and not stating which was used.
- Including inventory in the quick ratio, or using total liabilities where the question defines a ratio with only interest-bearing debt.
- Taking non-GAAP "adjusted" figures at face value without reading the reconciliation to the GAAP numbers.
- Judging a ratio in isolation. A number means little without a comparison with prior years and peers in the same industry.

import math
m, t, l = dupont(75000, 820000, 530000, 290000)
assert math.isclose(m, 75000 / 820000)

model:
```python
def dupont(net_income, revenue, avg_assets, avg_equity):
    """Return (net margin, asset turnover, equity multiplier)."""
    if revenue <= 0 or avg_assets <= 0 or avg_equity <= 0:
        raise ValueError("revenue, assets and equity must be positive")
    return (net_income / revenue,
            revenue / avg_assets,
            avg_assets / avg_equity)
```
hint: Each factor has a different denominator; the product cancels revenue and assets.
solution: Return net income over revenue, revenue over average assets and average assets over average equity, after checking that the denominators are positive. Multiplying the three factors cancels revenue and average assets, leaving net income over average equity.
