@article
Cash does not change hands exactly when revenue is earned or an expense is incurred. A company may pay for a year of insurance in October, receive a customer's prepayment in November or owe wages for three days at year end. If it recorded only cash events, its December statements would be wrong. At the end of each period, accountants therefore record **adjusting entries** so that revenues are recognized in the period they are earned and expenses in the period they help earn revenue (the **matching** idea). Adjusting entries update the accounts before the statements are prepared. Each affects at least one income statement account and one balance sheet account, and none involves the Cash account.

### Deferrals

A **deferral** is a cash payment or receipt made before the expense or revenue is recognized.

- **Prepaid expense** (an asset). The company paid cash first and uses the benefit later: insurance, rent, supplies. The adjustment moves the used part from the asset to an expense: debit Expense, credit the asset. If a \$2,400 policy covers 12 months from October 1, then at December 31 three months have expired and Insurance Expense is $2{,}400 \times 3/12 = \$600$. For supplies, expense = beginning supplies + purchases − supplies on hand.
- **Unearned revenue** (a liability). The company received cash first and owes service: debit Unearned Revenue, credit Revenue for the part earned.

### Accruals

An **accrual** is a revenue earned or an expense incurred before any cash moves.

- **Accrued expense** (a liability). Debit Expense, credit a payable (Wages Payable, Interest Payable). Interest on a note is $\text{principal} \times \text{annual rate} \times \text{time in years}$; this lesson uses whole months out of 12.
- **Accrued revenue** (an asset). Services have been performed but not billed or collected: debit a receivable, credit Revenue.

### Depreciation

Equipment helps earn revenue for years. **Depreciation** allocates its cost over its useful life; it is an allocation, not a measure of market value. For straight-line depreciation, $\text{expense} = (\text{cost} - \text{salvage})/\text{life}$. The credit goes to **Accumulated Depreciation**, a contra-asset account whose credit balance offsets the equipment account. The **book value** (carrying amount) is cost minus accumulated depreciation. Lesson 7 covers other methods.

### Summary of effects

| Adjustment | Debit | Credit | If omitted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prepaid expense used | Expense | Asset | Expenses understated, assets overstated |
| Unearned revenue earned | Unearned Revenue | Revenue | Revenue understated, liabilities overstated |
| Accrued expense | Expense | Payable | Expenses understated, liabilities understated |
| Accrued revenue | Receivable | Revenue | Revenue understated, assets understated |
| Depreciation | Depreciation Expense | Accumulated Depreciation | Expenses understated, assets overstated |

After adjusting, the company prepares an **adjusted trial balance**, which feeds the financial statements.

@example
title: Year-end adjustments
problem: Harbor Lane Studio adjusts its books on December 31. (1) A 12-month insurance policy costing \$2,400 was bought on October 1 and debited to Prepaid Insurance. (2) Supplies on hand were \$800 on January 1, purchases were \$1,100 and a count shows \$650. (3) Equipment costing \$24,000 has a five-year life and no salvage value. (4) Employees earned \$240 a day for the last 3 days of December, unpaid. (5) On November 1 the studio received \$3,600 for six months of service and credited Unearned Revenue. (6) It borrowed \$10,000 on November 1 at 9% a year. (7) Services worth \$900 are performed but not yet billed. Prepare the entries.
step: Prepaid insurance: \$2,400 × 3/12 = \$600. Supplies used: \$800 + \$1,100 − \$650 = \$1,250. Depreciation: \$24,000 / 5 = \$4,800 for the year.
step: Wages: 3 × \$240 = \$720. Unearned revenue: \$3,600 × 2/6 = \$1,200 earned in November and December. Interest: \$10,000 × 0.09 × 2/12 = \$150.
step: Entries:

```text
Dec 31  Insurance Expense               600
          Prepaid Insurance                      600
Dec 31  Supplies Expense              1,250
          Supplies                             1,250
Dec 31  Depreciation Expense          4,800
          Accumulated Depreciation             4,800
Dec 31  Wages Expense                   720
          Wages Payable                          720
Dec 31  Unearned Revenue              1,200
          Service Revenue                      1,200
Dec 31  Interest Expense                150
          Interest Payable                       150
Dec 31  Accounts Receivable             900
          Service Revenue                        900
```
step: None of the entries touches Cash. Each pairs an income statement account with a balance sheet account.
answer: Expenses of \$7,520 (600 + 1,250 + 4,800 + 720 + 150) and revenue of \$2,100 (1,200 + 900).

@example
title: Effect on net income
problem: Before adjustments, Harbor Lane Studio's net income is \$26,000. Its seven year-end adjustments are: insurance expense \$600, supplies expense \$1,250, depreciation expense \$4,800, wages expense \$720, interest expense \$150, unearned revenue earned \$1,200 and accrued service revenue \$900. What is net income after adjusting?
step: Adjustments add revenue of \$1,200 + \$900 = \$2,100.
step: They add expenses of \$600 + \$1,250 + \$4,800 + \$720 + \$150 = \$7,520.
step: Adjusted net income = \$26,000 + \$2,100 − \$7,520 = \$20,580. Skipping the adjustments would overstate net income by \$5,420.
answer: \$20,580.

@mistakes
- Recording the full payment as an expense (or the full receipt as revenue) and never adjusting, so a later period's cost lands in this one.
- Crediting Cash in an adjusting entry. Adjusting entries never involve cash.
- Forgetting the time fraction: a note outstanding for two months bears two months' interest, not a full year's.
- Treating depreciation as a drop in market value, or crediting the Equipment account directly instead of Accumulated Depreciation.

@quiz num
prompt: On October 1 a gym received \$9,000 for an 18-month membership program and credited Unearned Revenue. What is the Unearned Revenue balance after the December 31 adjustment? Round to the nearest dollar.
answer: 7500
lead: \$
check: 9000 - 9000 * 3 / 18
hint: Revenue is earned evenly over the 18 months. How many months have passed?
solution: Three of 18 months are earned: $9{,}000 \times 3/18 = \$1{,}500$. The remaining liability is $9{,}000 - 1{,}500 = \$7{,}500$.

@quiz self
prompt: At year end, a company owes employees \$3,000 of wages but makes no adjusting entry; it pays the wages in the next year. In three or four sentences, explain the effect of the omission on this year's expenses, net income, liabilities and equity.
hint: Compare what the books show with what should have been recorded.
solution: Wages Expense is understated by \$3,000, so this year's net income and year-end equity (through retained earnings) are overstated by \$3,000, and liabilities are understated by \$3,000 because Wages Payable is missing. The expense lands in next year's statements when the wages are paid, so next year's expense is overstated and its net income understated, unless the company corrects the error.
rubric: States that expenses are understated and net income overstated by \$3,000.
rubric: States that liabilities are understated and equity overstated by \$3,000.
rubric: Notes that the expense appears in the next period instead, distorting that period too.
