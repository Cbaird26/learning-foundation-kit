@article
After the adjusting entries, the **adjusted trial balance** holds every figure needed to report. The four primary statements answer four questions. The **income statement** shows profitability over a period: revenues minus expenses is net income. The **statement of changes in equity** shows why equity changed. For a company with only contributed capital and retained earnings, this can be shown as a statement of retained earnings. The **balance sheet** shows financial position at one date: assets, liabilities and equity. The **statement of cash flows** explains the change in cash, and Lesson 10 covers it.

### Order and links

Prepare them in this order: income statement, equity statement, balance sheet. Net income from the first feeds ending retained earnings in the second, whose ending balance feeds the third. The cash on the balance sheet must equal the ending cash on the cash flow statement. Under US GAAP and IFRS, notes to the statements are part of the package.

$$\text{RE}_{\text{end}} = \text{RE}_{\text{begin}} + \text{Net income} - \text{Dividends}.$$

### Classified balance sheet

A classified balance sheet groups items. **Current assets** will be turned into cash or used within one year or the operating cycle, whichever is longer (cash, receivables, inventory, prepaid expenses). **Property, plant and equipment** is shown net of accumulated depreciation. **Current liabilities** are due within one year or the operating cycle. Two common measures use the groups: **working capital** = current assets − current liabilities, and the **current ratio** = current assets / current liabilities.

On the income statement, a **single-step** format lists all revenues then all expenses; a **multi-step** format shows subtotals such as gross profit and operating income. IFRS 18, which replaces IAS 1 for annual periods beginning on or after January 1, 2027, requires IFRS income statements to classify income and expenses into operating, investing and financing categories with defined subtotals. This lesson uses a simple format.

### Closing the books

Revenue, expense and dividend accounts are **temporary (nominal) accounts**: they measure one period and start the next at zero. Asset, liability and equity accounts are **permanent (real) accounts**. **Closing entries** transfer temporary balances to retained earnings:

1. Close each revenue account to **Income Summary** (debit Revenue, credit Income Summary).
2. Close each expense account to Income Summary (debit Income Summary, credit each expense).
3. Close Income Summary to Retained Earnings: a profit is a credit to Retained Earnings, a loss a debit.
4. Close Dividends to Retained Earnings (debit Retained Earnings, credit Dividends).

Income Summary is a temporary holding account used only during closing. The **post-closing trial balance** contains only permanent accounts and proves that the books balance before the next period starts. (Income taxes are ignored in this lesson.)

@example
title: Statements from an adjusted trial balance
problem: Marlowe Design Co. has this adjusted trial balance at December 31 (taxes ignored). Debits: Cash 18,400; Accounts Receivable 7,600; Supplies 600; Prepaid Insurance 1,200; Equipment 30,000; Dividends 4,000; Wages Expense 28,000; Rent Expense 9,000; Supplies Expense 1,800; Insurance Expense 900; Depreciation Expense 3,000; Interest Expense 480. Credits: Accumulated Depreciation 6,000; Accounts Payable 5,580; Wages Payable 900; Unearned Revenue 2,500; Notes Payable (due in two years) 8,000; Common Stock 20,000; Retained Earnings, January 1, 5,000; Service Revenue 57,000. Prepare the statements.
step: Income statement. Revenue 57,000. Expenses: 28,000 + 9,000 + 1,800 + 900 + 3,000 + 480 = 43,180. Net income = 57,000 − 43,180 = 13,820.
step: Retained earnings. 5,000 + 13,820 − 4,000 = 14,820.
step: Balance sheet.

| Assets | | Liabilities and equity | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | 18,400 | Accounts payable | 5,580 |
| Accounts receivable | 7,600 | Wages payable | 900 |
| Supplies | 600 | Unearned revenue | 2,500 |
| Prepaid insurance | 1,200 | **Total current liabilities** | **8,980** |
| **Total current assets** | **27,800** | Notes payable | 8,000 |
| Equipment | 30,000 | **Total liabilities** | **16,980** |
| Less accumulated depreciation | (6,000) | Common stock | 20,000 |
| Equipment, net | 24,000 | Retained earnings | 14,820 |
| | | **Total equity** | **34,820** |
| **Total assets** | **51,800** | **Total liabilities and equity** | **51,800** |
step: The balance sheet balances. Working capital is 27,800 − 8,980 = 18,820, and the current ratio is 27,800 / 8,980 = 3.10.
answer: Net income 13,820; ending retained earnings 14,820; total assets 51,800.

@example
title: Closing entries
problem: Record the closing entries for Marlowe Design Co.
step: Close revenue: debit Service Revenue 57,000, credit Income Summary 57,000.
step: Close expenses: debit Income Summary 43,180, credit Wages Expense 28,000, Rent Expense 9,000, Supplies Expense 1,800, Insurance Expense 900, Depreciation Expense 3,000 and Interest Expense 480. Income Summary now has a credit balance of 57,000 − 43,180 = 13,820.
step: Close Income Summary: debit Income Summary 13,820, credit Retained Earnings 13,820.
step: Close dividends: debit Retained Earnings 4,000, credit Dividends 4,000. Retained Earnings is now 5,000 + 13,820 − 4,000 = 14,820, which agrees with the statement.
answer: Retained Earnings after closing is 14,820; all temporary accounts have zero balances.

@mistakes
- Closing Dividends to Income Summary. Dividends are not an expense, so they close directly to Retained Earnings.
- Listing Accumulated Depreciation as a liability or an expense. It is a contra-asset that reduces equipment to its book value.
- Preparing the balance sheet before the equity statement and then having no ending retained earnings to report.
- Closing permanent accounts such as Accounts Receivable or Unearned Revenue; only temporary accounts are closed.

hint: Follow the flow of net income, then ending retained earnings, then cash.
solution: Net income from the income statement is added to beginning retained earnings (and dividends are subtracted) in the statement of retained earnings. The statement of cash flows explains the change in the balance sheet's cash account, starting from net income in the indirect method, and its ending cash must equal the cash reported on the balance sheet.
