@article
A company sells services all year, borrows money, buys equipment and pays its staff. Months later, a bank, an investor and a tax authority each want to know how the company did. **Financial accounting** is the system that records the company's economic events and summarizes them in standard reports, the **financial statements**, for people outside the company: investors, lenders, regulators and others. (Managerial accounting serves decision makers inside the firm and is not bound by the same rules.)

### Rules and institutions

Outside users need statements prepared on a common basis. In the United States, public companies follow **US GAAP** (generally accepted accounting principles), set by the Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB) and collected in the Accounting Standards Codification. Most other countries use **IFRS** (International Financial Reporting Standards), set by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB). The SEC requires public US companies to file audited annual reports (Form 10-K) and quarterly reports (Form 10-Q), which anyone can read on SEC EDGAR. An independent auditor gives an opinion on whether the statements are fairly presented in accordance with the framework.

Four basic assumptions underlie the statements. The **economic entity** assumption keeps the business separate from its owners. The **going concern** assumption says the business will continue operating. The **monetary unit** assumption records only items that can be measured in money, normally at historical cost. The **periodicity** assumption slices the life of the business into reporting periods such as quarters and years.

### The accounting equation

Every business owns resources and those resources were financed by someone. The **accounting equation** states this:

$$\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}.$$

**Assets** are resources the entity controls that are expected to provide future benefit (cash, receivables, inventory, equipment). **Liabilities** are obligations to transfer assets or provide services to others (accounts payable, loans). **Equity** is the owners' residual claim, $\text{Assets} - \text{Liabilities}$. For a corporation, equity has two parts: **contributed capital** (stock issued to owners) and **retained earnings** (profits kept in the business).

Retained earnings change each period through the **expanded equation**:

$$\text{RE}_{\text{end}} = \text{RE}_{\text{begin}} + \text{Net income} - \text{Dividends}, \qquad \text{Net income} = \text{Revenues} - \text{Expenses}.$$

**Revenues** are inflows from delivering goods or services, **expenses** are the costs of earning them, and **dividends** are distributions of profit to owners. Dividends are not an expense.

### Accrual accounting

Under **accrual accounting**, revenue is recorded when it is earned and expenses when they are incurred, not when cash moves. A repair shop that fixes a customer's machine in December and is paid in January records December revenue and a receivable. Under **cash-basis accounting**, nothing would be recorded until January. GAAP and IFRS require the accrual basis because it matches effort and results in the same period.

### Analyzing a transaction

A **transaction** is an event with a measurable effect on the equation. Whatever happens, both sides stay equal. Buying equipment for cash swaps one asset for another, so total assets do not change. Borrowing cash raises assets and liabilities by the same amount. Earning revenue raises assets (cash or a receivable) and equity (through net income). Paying wages lowers assets and equity. Hiring an employee or signing a contract with no money changing hands is usually not yet a transaction.

@example
title: Seven transactions in the equation
problem: Tern Landscaping Inc. begins business and has these transactions. (1) Owners invest \$30,000 cash for stock. (2) It borrows \$10,000 from a bank. (3) It buys equipment for \$14,000 cash. (4) It buys supplies for \$1,200 on account. (5) It earns \$6,000 of service revenue in cash. (6) It pays \$2,500 of wages. (7) It pays a \$500 dividend. Show the equation after the last transaction.
step: Transactions 1–3. Cash is \$30,000 + \$10,000 − \$14,000 = \$26,000 and equipment is \$14,000, so assets are \$40,000. The bank loan is a liability of \$10,000 and contributed capital is \$30,000. The equation holds: \$40,000 = \$10,000 + \$30,000.
step: Transaction 4. Supplies (an asset) rise by \$1,200 and accounts payable (a liability) rise by \$1,200. Assets are \$41,200; liabilities are \$11,200; equity is \$30,000.
step: Transactions 5–7. Cash becomes \$26,000 + \$6,000 − \$2,500 − \$500 = \$29,000. Revenue of \$6,000 raises equity, wages of \$2,500 lower it, and the dividend of \$500 lowers it as a distribution.
step: Final position.

| Assets | Amount | Liabilities and equity | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | \$29,000 | Notes payable | \$10,000 |
| Supplies | \$1,200 | Accounts payable | \$1,200 |
| Equipment | \$14,000 | Contributed capital | \$30,000 |
| | | Retained earnings (\$6,000 − \$2,500 − \$500) | \$3,000 |
| **Total** | **\$44,200** | **Total** | **\$44,200** |
answer: Total assets \$44,200 = liabilities \$11,200 + equity \$33,000; net income is \$3,500.

@example
title: Finding net income from the change in equity
problem: Cobalt Bakery's assets were \$150,000 and its liabilities \$80,000 at the start of the year. At year end, assets were \$210,000 and liabilities \$100,000. During the year, owners invested \$20,000 more and the company paid \$12,000 in dividends. What was net income?
step: Beginning equity = \$150,000 − \$80,000 = \$70,000. Ending equity = \$210,000 − \$100,000 = \$110,000. Equity rose by \$40,000.
step: Equity changes by new investment, plus net income, minus dividends: \$40,000 = \$20,000 + NI − \$12,000.
step: Solve: NI = \$40,000 − \$20,000 + \$12,000 = \$32,000.
answer: Net income was \$32,000.

@mistakes
- Treating dividends as an expense. Dividends are a distribution of profit and do not enter net income.
- Believing that a purchase of equipment or inventory for cash changes total assets. It only swaps one asset for another.
- Recording revenue when cash arrives rather than when it is earned, which is cash-basis thinking.
- Calling equity "profit". Equity is contributed capital plus accumulated profits, and it also includes the effect of dividends and other changes.

@quiz self
prompt: Brightwater Plumbing finishes a repair job on December 28 and bills the customer \$900, payable in January. In three or four sentences, explain how accrual accounting and cash-basis accounting each treat the job in the December financial statements, and why GAAP requires the accrual basis.
hint: Ask what each basis uses as the trigger for recording revenue.
solution: Under accrual accounting, Brightwater records \$900 of revenue and a \$900 account receivable in December, because the work was done and the right to payment exists. Under cash-basis accounting, nothing is recorded until the cash arrives in January, so December would show no revenue even though the effort and costs occurred then. GAAP requires accrual accounting because it matches revenue with the expenses of earning it in the same period, which gives a better picture of performance.
