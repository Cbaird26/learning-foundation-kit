@review
### Week by week

1. **Accounting equation.** Assets = liabilities + equity; equity rises with contributions and net income, and falls with dividends. Accrual accounting records revenue when earned and expenses when incurred.
2. **Debits and credits.** Debits increase assets, expenses and dividends; credits increase liabilities, equity and revenues. Every entry balances; the trial balance checks the ledger.
3. **Adjusting entries.** Deferrals (prepaid expenses, unearned revenue) and accruals (accrued expenses and revenues), plus depreciation. None involves cash.
4. **Statements and closing.** Income statement, then equity statement, then classified balance sheet; close temporary accounts to retained earnings.
5. **Revenue and receivables.** The five-step model and allocation by standalone selling prices; the allowance method with percentage of sales or aging.
6. **Inventory.** FIFO, LIFO and weighted average; effects in rising prices; lower of cost and net realizable value.
7. **Long-lived assets.** Capitalize cost; straight-line, double-declining-balance and units of production; gain or loss on disposal; impairment.
8. **Liabilities and bonds.** Price = present value at the market rate; effective interest expense = carrying amount × market rate per period.
9. **Equity.** Par and APIC, treasury stock, cash and stock dividends, splits, preferred dividends in arrears, EPS.
10. **Cash flows.** Indirect method; operating, investing, financing; free cash flow.
11. **Controls and ethics.** COSO components, segregation of duties, SOX, the fraud triangle, bank reconciliations.
12. **Annual reports.** The Form 10-K, ratios and the DuPont identity.

### Key formulas

$$A = L + E, \qquad \text{RE}_{\text{end}} = \text{RE}_{\text{begin}} + NI - \text{Div}, \qquad \text{COGS} = \text{BI} + \text{Purchases} - \text{EI},$$

$$\text{SL} = \frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Salvage}}{\text{Life}}, \qquad \text{DDB}_t = \frac{2}{\text{Life}}\,BV_{t-1}, \qquad \text{ROE} = \text{margin} \times \text{turnover} \times \text{multiplier}.$$

- Can you compute each of the five kinds of adjusting entry from a short description?
- Can you state which statement each account appears on and how the statements connect?
- Can you compute COGS three ways and say which method gives the highest profit when prices rise?
- Can you price a bond, and trace its carrying amount for two periods?
- Can you build a cash flow statement from two balance sheets and an income statement?

### Common errors

- Debit means increase for every account (it does not).
- Dividends treated as an expense; cash-basis timing; revenue recorded on collection of a receivable.
- Bad debt expense at write-off under the allowance method.
- Salvage value subtracted in the DDB rate; gain on sale of equipment left in operating cash flow.
- Using the stated rate instead of the market rate to price a bond.
- Wrong signs on working-capital changes in the cash flow statement.

prompt: Calder Hardware has these year-end facts (income taxes ignored). It sold 1,000 units at \$100 each, all on credit. It began the year with 400 units at \$50, bought 600 units at \$55 and then 500 units at \$60, and uses FIFO with a periodic system. An aging schedule requires an allowance of \$4,200, and the Allowance for Credit Losses has a \$900 credit balance before adjustment. Equipment costing \$36,000 with a \$6,000 salvage value and a five-year life was bought on April 1; use straight-line depreciation by months to December 31. A \$20,000 note at 6% a year was issued on September 1 and interest has not been recorded (use whole months). Other operating expenses were \$21,000. What is income before taxes? Round to the nearest dollar.
answer: 17800
lead: \$
check: 1000 * 100 - (400 * 50 + 600 * 55) - (4200 - 900) - (36000 - 6000) / 5 * 9 / 12 - 20000 * 0.06 * 4 / 12 - 21000
hint: Work through the income statement line by line: revenue, FIFO COGS (the oldest 1,000 units), bad debt expense, depreciation, interest and other expenses.
hint: FIFO COGS uses all 400 units at \$50 and 600 units at \$55. Bad debt expense is the required allowance minus the existing credit balance.
solution: Revenue is \$100,000. FIFO COGS is 400 × 50 + 600 × 55 = \$53,000. Bad debt expense is 4,200 − 900 = \$3,300. Depreciation is (36,000 − 6,000)/5 = 6,000 a year, for 9 months \$4,500. Interest is 20,000 × 0.06 × 4/12 = \$400. Income before taxes is 100,000 − 53,000 − 3,300 − 4,500 − 400 − 21,000 = \$17,800.

prompt: On January 1, a company issues \$200,000 of three-year bonds with a 6% stated rate paying coupons semiannually, when the market rate is 8% a year (compounded semiannually). Using the effective interest method with exact (unrounded) values, what is the total interest expense recognized in the first year (the first two coupon periods)? Round to the nearest dollar.
answer: 15224
lead: \$
tol: 1
check: bond_price(200000, 0.06, 0.08, 3) * 0.04 + (bond_price(200000, 0.06, 0.08, 3) * 1.04 - 6000) * 0.04
hint: Find the issue price first: the present value of six coupons of \$6,000 and the \$200,000 face at 4% per period.
hint: Period 1 interest is 4% of the issue price. The carrying amount then rises by interest minus \$6,000, and period 2 interest is 4% of that.
solution: The issue price is about \$189,516. Period 1 interest is 189,516 × 0.04 = \$7,581, so the carrying amount becomes 189,516 + 7,581 − 6,000 = \$191,096. Period 2 interest is 191,096 × 0.04 = \$7,644. Total for the year is about \$15,224, which exceeds the \$12,000 of coupons paid because of the discount amortization.

@exam self
prompt: Redfern Cycles reports net income of \$54,000 for the year, but its cash balance fell from \$22,000 to \$15,000. The facts: depreciation \$6,000; accounts receivable rose \$48,000; inventory rose \$30,000; accounts payable rose \$9,000; wages payable fell \$3,000; equipment purchased for cash \$25,000; notes payable borrowed \$40,000; dividends paid \$10,000. Prepare a statement of cash flows by the indirect method (in summary form) and write a short memo paragraph (four or five sentences) explaining to the owners why a profitable year reduced cash.
hint: Compute operating cash flow from net income and the working-capital changes, then add investing and financing flows.
solution: Operating: 54,000 + 6,000 − 48,000 − 30,000 + 9,000 − 3,000 = −12,000. Investing: −25,000. Financing: +40,000 − 10,000 = +30,000. Net change: −12,000 − 25,000 + 30,000 = −7,000, which takes cash from 22,000 to 15,000. Memo: Redfern earned a profit of \$54,000, but profit is measured on the accrual basis. Customers owe \$48,000 more than a year ago and the company holds \$30,000 more inventory, so cash was tied up in receivables and inventory, and operating activities used \$12,000 of cash. The company also bought \$25,000 of equipment and paid \$10,000 of dividends, which were funded partly by \$40,000 of new borrowing. Management should look at collection of receivables, inventory levels and how much debt the growth requires.
rubric: Operating cash flow is computed as −12,000 with the correct sign for each adjustment.
rubric: Investing is −25,000, financing is +30,000, and the net change is −7,000, reconciling 22,000 to 15,000.
rubric: The memo attributes the cash decline to growth in receivables and inventory, noting the investing and financing flows.
