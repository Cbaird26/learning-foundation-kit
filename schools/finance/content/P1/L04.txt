### Demand, supply and elasticity

The **price elasticity of demand** is the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in price. With the **midpoint method**, percentage changes use the average of the old and new values: $E = \frac{(Q_2 - Q_1)/\bar Q}{(P_2 - P_1)/\bar P}$. Demand is elastic when $|E| > 1$ (a price rise lowers revenue), inelastic when $|E| < 1$. Other elasticities: **income elasticity** (positive for normal goods, negative for inferior goods) and **cross-price elasticity** (positive for substitutes, negative for complements). **Consumer surplus** is the area between the demand curve and the price; **producer surplus** is the area between the price and the supply curve; a price ceiling set below equilibrium creates a shortage.

### Market structure

| Structure | Firms | Pricing power | Long-run economic profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perfect competition | Many | None (price taker) | Zero |
| Monopolistic competition | Many, differentiated | Some | Zero (entry) |
| Oligopoly | Few, interdependent | Considerable | Possible |
| Monopoly | One | Largest | Possible (barriers) |

Profit is maximized where marginal revenue equals marginal cost. **Concentration** is measured by the N-firm concentration ratio and by the **Herfindahl-Hirschman Index** (HHI), the sum of squared market shares expressed in percentage points, so a monopoly scores 10,000. A higher HHI means a more concentrated market.

### Macroeconomics

**GDP** by expenditure is $C + I + G + (X - M)$. **Real** GDP growth is nominal growth adjusted for the GDP deflator: $1 + g_{\text{real}} = (1 + g_{\text{nominal}})/(1 + \pi)$. The business cycle moves through expansion, peak, contraction and trough. The unemployment rate counts people without a job who are actively seeking one, as a share of the labor force. Inflation can be demand-pull or cost-push.

**Monetary policy** is run by the central bank: raising the policy rate tightens financial conditions, slows demand and lowers inflation. A **neutral policy rate** equals the trend real growth rate plus the inflation target. **Fiscal policy** (taxes and government spending) changes aggregate demand directly; expansionary fiscal policy raises deficits. Policy mixes: expansionary fiscal with tight monetary policy raises interest rates and the public sector's share of output, with an uncertain effect on total output; fiscal and monetary stimulus together raise output strongly, with a risk of inflation. Central banks differ in independence, credibility and transparency. The Federal Reserve sets a target range for the federal funds rate and steers it mainly with administered rates (the interest rate on reserve balances and the overnight reverse repurchase facility); current settings change, so read them on the central bank's own site.

### Exchange rates and trade

An exchange rate quoted as price currency per base currency (USD per EUR) means an increase is appreciation of the base. **Covered interest rate parity** links spot $S$ and forward $F$ rates (price per base) to interest rates over the period: $F = S\,\frac{1 + i_{\text{price}}\,t}{1 + i_{\text{base}}\,t}$ when quoted simple rates are used. The currency with the higher interest rate trades at a **forward discount**. A country has a **comparative advantage** in the good for which its opportunity cost is lower; both countries gain from trade even if one has an absolute advantage in everything. Tariffs and quotas raise domestic prices and protect producers at a cost to consumers.

### Technique

Draw supply and demand shifts before you answer; for FX, first write which currency is the price currency: its interest rate goes in the numerator of the forward formula and the base currency's rate in the denominator.

@example
title: A forward exchange rate
problem: The spot rate is 0.8000 GBP per EUR. The one-year simple interest rate is 4.5% in GBP and 3.0% in EUR. What is the one-year forward rate?
step: The price currency is GBP, so $i_{\text{price}} = 4.5\%$ and $i_{\text{base}} = 3.0\%$.
step: $F = 0.8000 \times \frac{1.045}{1.030} = 0.8000 \times 1.014563$.
step: $F = 0.8117$ GBP per EUR. The EUR trades at a forward premium, because GBP interest rates are higher.
answer: 0.8117 GBP per EUR.

@mistakes
- Using the starting price rather than the midpoint in a midpoint elasticity, or forgetting that elasticity is negative along a demand curve (the exam usually reports its absolute value).
- Inverting covered interest parity: the forward rate is spot times (1 + price-currency rate) over (1 + base-currency rate).
- Treating a monopoly or oligopoly as always earning economic profit: it is possible, not guaranteed.
- Confusing nominal and real GDP growth, or adding the inflation rate to real growth when the question wants the deflator-adjusted growth.

@practice num
prompt: A coffee shop raises the price of a drink from \$4.00 to \$4.40 and its weekly sales fall from 1,000 to 900 drinks. Using the midpoint method, what is the absolute value of the price elasticity of demand? Give it to two decimal places.
answer: 1.11
tol: 0.01
check: abs(((900 - 1000) / 950) / ((4.40 - 4.00) / 4.20))
hint: Use the average quantity and the average price as the bases for the percentage changes.
hint: Percentage change in quantity $= -100/950$; in price $= 0.40/4.20$.
solution: Quantity changes by $-100/950 = -10.53\%$ and price by $0.40/4.20 = 9.52\%$, so $|E| = 0.10526/0.09524 = 1.1053 \approx 1.11$. Since this exceeds 1, demand is elastic and the price rise lowers revenue.

@practice num
prompt: Four firms in a market have shares of 40%, 30%, 20% and 10%. What is the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index? Use percentage points for shares (so a 40% share counts as 40).
answer: 3000
check: sum(s ** 2 for s in [40, 30, 20, 10])
hint: Square each firm's share in percentage points.
solution: $\text{HHI} = 40^2 + 30^2 + 20^2 + 10^2 = 1600 + 900 + 400 + 100 = 3000$, a highly concentrated market.

@practice num
prompt: The spot rate is 1.2500 USD per EUR. The one-year simple interest rate is 5.0% in USD and 3.0% in EUR. What is the one-year forward rate in USD per EUR? Give it to four decimal places.
answer: 1.2743
tol: 0.0001
check: 1.25 * (1 + 0.05) / (1 + 0.03)
hint: USD is the price currency, so its rate goes in the numerator.
hint: Multiply the spot rate by $1.05/1.03$.
solution: $F = 1.2500 \times 1.05/1.03 = 1.2743$ USD per EUR. The EUR trades at a forward premium because its interest rate is lower.

@practice mc
prompt: A government adopts a large expansionary fiscal policy while its central bank follows a tight monetary policy. Which outcome is most likely?
choice: Interest rates fall and private investment rises
choice*: Interest rates rise and the public sector's share of output rises, while the effect on total output is uncertain
choice: Output and inflation both fall sharply because the two policies cancel exactly
hint: Both policies push in the same direction on the cost of borrowing.
solution: Larger deficits increase borrowing needs and tight monetary policy raises the policy rate, so interest rates rise. Government spending takes a larger share of output and private investment is crowded out; total output depends on the relative strength of the two policies.

@practice num
prompt: Over a year a country's nominal GDP grows by 5.6% and its GDP deflator rises by 2.1%. What is real GDP growth? Give it as a percent to two decimal places.
answer: 3.43
unit: %
tol: 0.01
check: (1.056 / 1.021 - 1) * 100
hint: Real growth deflates nominal growth, it does not just subtract.
hint: Compute $1.056/1.021 - 1$.
solution: $1 + g_{\text{real}} = 1.056/1.021 = 1.03428$, so real growth is 3.43% (the approximation 5.6 - 2.1 = 3.5% is slightly high).

@quiz num
prompt: Real GDP of an economy is 2,000 and the GDP deflator is 1.25 (base year = 1.00). Government spending is 18% of nominal GDP. What is nominal government spending, in the same currency units?
answer: 450
tol: 0.5
check: 2000 * 1.25 * 0.18
hint: Nominal GDP is real GDP times the deflator.
hint: Take 18% of nominal GDP.
solution: Nominal GDP is $2000 \times 1.25 = 2500$, and government spending is $0.18 \times 2500 = 450$.

@quiz mc
prompt: In one hour, Country A can produce 6 units of cloth or 4 units of wheat. Country B can produce 2 units of cloth or 3 units of wheat. Which statement is correct?
choice*: Country B has a comparative advantage in wheat, and Country A in cloth, even though A is more productive in both goods
choice: Country A has a comparative advantage in both goods, because it is more productive in both
choice: Country B has a comparative advantage in cloth, because its opportunity cost of wheat is higher
hint: Compare the opportunity cost of wheat, in cloth, in each country.
solution: A gives up $6/4 = 1.5$ cloth per wheat; B gives up $2/3 = 0.67$ cloth per wheat. B has the lower opportunity cost of wheat, so B has the comparative advantage in wheat, and A (opportunity cost of cloth $4/6 = 0.67$ wheat versus B's $1.5$) in cloth. Absolute advantage does not decide trade patterns.
