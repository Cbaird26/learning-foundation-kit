### Week by week

**Weeks 1-2, Ethics.** Know the seven areas and what each requires. The best answer protects clients and the market first, then the employer, then the member: disclose, seek written consent, escalate. The stricter of the law and the Code governs; the mosaic theory is allowed; client trades precede employer and personal trades; do not exaggerate the designation.

**Week 3, Quantitative methods.** Effective annual rate, geometric versus arithmetic mean, time- versus money-weighted return, z-scores, Bayes, the t-statistic and p-value logic, regression $R^2$ and the slope t-test.

**Week 4, Economics.** Midpoint elasticity, market structures, HHI, real versus nominal growth, policy mixes, covered interest parity and comparative advantage.

**Weeks 5-6, Financial statement analysis.** Indirect CFO, FIFO versus LIFO and the LIFO reserve, depreciation methods, revenue recognition, impairment, leases, ratios, DuPont, cash conversion cycle, deferred taxes, EPS and FCFF.

**Week 7, Corporate issuers.** NPV and IRR, WACC with after-tax debt, DOL and DFL, MM Proposition II, trade-off theory.

**Week 8, Equity.** Order types, price-weighted index divisors, forms of efficiency, Gordon growth, CAPM, EV/EBITDA, residual income.

**Week 9, Fixed income.** Bond pricing, spot and forward rates, duration and convexity, effective duration, credit and securitization risks.

**Week 10, Derivatives and alternatives.** Cost-of-carry forwards, put-call parity, binomial pricing, swaps, option payoffs, hedge fund fees, cap rates.

**Week 11, Portfolio management.** Two-asset risk, Sharpe, Treynor, Jensen's alpha, safety-first, IPS and behavioral biases.

### Key formulas

$$\text{EAR} = (1 + r_s/m)^m - 1,\qquad \text{WACC} = w_d r_d (1 - t) + w_e r_e,\qquad V_0 = \frac{D_1}{r - g}$$

$$\%\Delta P \approx -\text{ModDur}\,\Delta y + \tfrac{1}{2}\,\text{Conv}\,(\Delta y)^2,\qquad p_0 = c_0 - S_0 + \frac{K}{(1+r)^T}$$

$$\sigma_p^2 = w_1^2\sigma_1^2 + w_2^2\sigma_2^2 + 2w_1w_2\rho_{12}\sigma_1\sigma_2,\qquad \text{ROE} = \text{margin} \times \text{turnover} \times \text{leverage}$$

### Most common errors

Using the annual rate as a periodic rate; forgetting the tax shield in WACC; using $D_0$ in the Gordon model; mixing up the direction of LIFO effects; applying duration to a large yield change without convexity; leaving a question blank; and choosing "do nothing" in an ethics question.

@exam num
prompt: Bellwether Foods has a net profit margin of 5%, an asset turnover of 1.6 and an equity multiplier of 1.5. It retains 50% of earnings and expects to keep this return on equity indefinitely. Next year's earnings per share are expected to be \$4.00 and its payout ratio is 50%. The risk-free rate is 3.0%, its beta is 1.2 and the market risk premium is 4.5%. Using the sustainable growth rate from DuPont ROE and the Gordon growth model with the CAPM cost of equity, what is the value per share? Round to the nearest cent.
answer: 83.33
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: 4.00 * 0.5 / ((0.03 + 1.2 * 0.045) - 0.5 * (0.05 * 1.6 * 1.5))
hint: ROE is margin times turnover times leverage, and the sustainable growth rate is the retention rate times ROE.
hint: ROE = 12%, so g = 6%; the CAPM cost of equity is $3.0\% + 1.2 \times 4.5\% = 8.4\%$.
solution: $\text{ROE} = 0.05 \times 1.6 \times 1.5 = 12\%$ and $g = 0.5 \times 12\% = 6\%$. The cost of equity is $r = 3.0\% + 1.2(4.5\%) = 8.4\%$. The dividend is $D_1 = 0.5 \times 4.00 = 2.00$, so $V_0 = 2.00/(0.084 - 0.06) = \$83.33$.

@exam num
prompt: A company funds itself 60% with equity and 40% with debt at market value. The risk-free rate is 3%, the equity beta is 1.1 and the market risk premium is 5%. Debt costs 6% before a 25% tax rate. A project costs \$1,000,000 today and returns \$350,000 at the end of each of the next four years. Using the CAPM for the cost of equity and the WACC as the discount rate, what is the project's NPV? Round to the nearest dollar.
answer: 188205
lead: \$
tol: 1
check: npv(0.6 * (0.03 + 1.1 * 0.05) + 0.4 * 0.06 * (1 - 0.25), [-1000000] + [350000] * 4)
hint: Find the cost of equity first, then the after-tax cost of debt, then the WACC.
hint: $r_e = 8.5\%$, the after-tax cost of debt is 4.5%, so WACC = 6.9%.
solution: $r_e = 3\% + 1.1(5\%) = 8.5\%$; $\text{WACC} = 0.6(8.5\%) + 0.4(6\%)(0.75) = 5.1\% + 1.8\% = 6.9\%$. The present value of the four \$350,000 payments at 6.9% is \$1,188,205, so $\text{NPV} = 1{,}188{,}205 - 1{,}000{,}000 = \$188{,}205$. It is positive, so accept.
