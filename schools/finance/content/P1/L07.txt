@article
Corporate Issuers carries 6-9% of the 2026 Level I exam (weights change each year; see cfainstitute.org). It is the bridge between accounting and valuation: how a company chooses projects, finances them and governs itself. Practice the formulas until they are quick.

### Governance and stakeholders

A firm's stakeholders (shareholders, creditors, managers, employees, customers, regulators) have different claims. The **agency problem** arises when managers act in their own interest rather than the shareholders': remedies include an independent board and audit committee, aligned compensation, shareholder voting rights and disclosure. Boards with staggered terms, supermajority rules and poison pills can entrench management, which is why investors watch them. **ESG** factors (environmental, social, governance) are considered by many investors as sources of risk and return; regulation and disclosure standards on sustainability are changing quickly (the EU's rules were being simplified in 2025-2026 and the SEC's 2024 climate rule is not in effect), so check current requirements.

### Capital budgeting

For a project with cash flows $CF_t$, **net present value** is $\text{NPV} = \sum_{t=0}^{n} CF_t/(1+r)^t$, and the **internal rate of return** is the $r$ that sets NPV to zero. Accept when NPV > 0 (IRR > cost of capital). NPV is the preferred rule because it measures value added in money and handles mutually exclusive projects and unconventional flows; IRR can mislead with different scales, timing or multiple sign changes. **Payback** is the time to recover the outlay (it ignores the time value of money and later cash flows); **discounted payback** uses discounted flows. Use incremental, after-tax cash flows, include changes in net working capital, ignore sunk costs, and include opportunity costs and externalities.

### Cost of capital

$$\text{WACC} = w_d\,r_d\,(1 - t) + w_e\,r_e,$$

with market-value weights. The cost of equity may come from the CAPM, $r_e = r_f + \beta(E[R_M] - r_f)$, or the bond-yield-plus-risk-premium method. The tax shield makes debt cheaper after tax.

### Leverage and capital structure

The **degree of operating leverage** is $\text{DOL} = \frac{Q(P - V)}{Q(P - V) - F}$, the percentage change in operating income per percentage change in sales; the **degree of financial leverage** is $\text{DFL} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{EBIT} - \text{interest}}$; the product is the degree of total leverage. **Modigliani-Miller**: without taxes or distress costs, firm value is independent of capital structure (Proposition I) and the cost of equity rises linearly with leverage (Proposition II): $r_e = r_0 + (r_0 - r_d)\,D/E$. With corporate taxes, debt adds a tax shield worth $tD$ and $r_e = r_0 + (r_0 - r_d)(1 - t)\,D/E$. The **static trade-off theory** says the optimal capital structure balances the marginal tax benefit of debt against the marginal expected cost of financial distress; the **pecking order theory** says firms prefer internal funds, then debt, then new equity, because of information asymmetry.

### Working capital and payout

Liquidity sources are primary (cash, short-term funds) and secondary (selling assets, renegotiating debt). Efficient working capital shortens the cash conversion cycle. Dividends versus **share repurchases**: both distribute cash; repurchases are flexible and affect EPS, dividends signal commitment. Merger analysis distinguishes horizontal, vertical and conglomerate deals, and a target's value is the standalone value plus synergies.

### Technique

Write the timeline of cash flows with signs before using the NPV formula. For payback questions, cumulate cash flows year by year and interpolate within the year of recovery.

@example
title: NPV, IRR and WACC
problem: A project costs 2,000 and returns 800, 900 and 1,000 at the ends of years 1 to 3. A firm funds itself 50% with equity costing 12% and 50% with debt costing 5% before a 20% tax rate. Is the project worth doing at the firm's WACC, and what is its IRR?
step: WACC $= 0.5(12\%) + 0.5(5\%)(1 - 0.20) = 6.0\% + 2.0\% = 8.0\%$.
step: Discount the flows at 8%: $800/1.08 + 900/1.08^2 + 1000/1.08^3 = 740.74 + 771.60 + 793.83 = 2{,}306.17$.
step: NPV $= 2{,}306.17 - 2{,}000 = 306.17 > 0$, so accept.
step: Solving $\text{NPV} = 0$ numerically gives IRR $\approx 15.98\%$, well above the 8% hurdle, which agrees with the NPV decision.
answer: Accept; NPV = 306.17 and IRR is about 15.98%.

@mistakes
- Using the pretax cost of debt in the WACC.
- Using book-value weights instead of market-value (or target) weights.
- Counting sunk costs, or leaving out the working capital investment and its recovery.
- Preferring the project with the higher IRR when the projects are mutually exclusive and differ in scale; use NPV.

prompt: A project requires an outlay of \$5,000 today and returns \$1,800, \$2,200 and \$2,400 at the end of years 1, 2 and 3. The discount rate is 9%. What is the NPV? Round to the nearest cent.
answer: 356.31
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: npv(0.09, [-5000, 1800, 2200, 2400])
hint: Discount each inflow by $1.09^t$.
hint: PV of inflows $= 1800/1.09 + 2200/1.09^2 + 2400/1.09^3$, then subtract 5,000.
solution: $1651.38 + 1851.69 + 1853.24 = 5356.31$, so $\text{NPV} = 5356.31 - 5000 = \$356.31$. It is positive, so accept.

prompt: A firm has \$600 million of equity (cost of equity 11%) and \$400 million of debt (pretax cost 6%) at market value. The tax rate is 25%. What is its WACC? Give the answer as a percent to one decimal place.
answer: 8.4
unit: %
tol: 0.1
check: (0.6 * 11 + 0.4 * 6 * (1 - 0.25))
hint: The weights are 60% and 40%.
hint: Use the after-tax cost of debt, $6\% \times 0.75 = 4.5\%$.
solution: $\text{WACC} = 0.6(11\%) + 0.4(4.5\%) = 6.6\% + 1.8\% = 8.4\%$.

prompt: A company sells 10,000 units a year at \$50 per unit. Variable cost is \$30 per unit and fixed operating cost is \$120,000. What is the degree of operating leverage at this sales level? Give it to two decimal places.
answer: 2.50
unit: times
tol: 0.01
check: (10000 * (50 - 30)) / (10000 * (50 - 30) - 120000)
hint: Contribution margin is price minus variable cost per unit, times units.
hint: DOL = contribution / (contribution - fixed costs).
solution: Contribution is $10{,}000 \times 20 = 200{,}000$ and operating income is $200{,}000 - 120{,}000 = 80{,}000$; $\text{DOL} = 200{,}000/80{,}000 = 2.50$. A 10% rise in sales raises operating income about 25%.

@practice num
prompt: An unlevered firm's cost of equity would be 10%. The firm borrows at a pretax rate of 5%, with a target debt-to-equity ratio of 0.5 and a tax rate of 25%. Using Modigliani-Miller Proposition II with taxes, what is its cost of equity? Give the answer as a percent to two decimal places.
answer: 11.88
unit: %
tol: 0.01
check: 10 + (10 - 5) * (1 - 0.25) * 0.5
hint: $r_e = r_0 + (r_0 - r_d)(1 - t)\,D/E$.
solution: $r_e = 10\% + (10\% - 5\%)(0.75)(0.5) = 10\% + 1.875\% = 11.875\% \approx 11.88\%$.

@quiz num
prompt: A project costs \$900 and returns \$400 at the end of each of the next three years. The discount rate is 10%. What is the discounted payback period, in years, using linear interpolation within the year of recovery? Give it to two decimal places.
answer: 2.68
unit: years
tol: 0.01
check: 2 + (900 - 400 / 1.1 - 400 / 1.1 ** 2) / (400 / 1.1 ** 3)
hint: Discount each inflow and cumulate until the outlay is recovered.
hint: After two years the cumulative discounted flow is 694.22; the remainder is recovered in year 3.
solution: Discounted flows are 363.64, 330.58 and 300.53, cumulating to 694.22 after two years. The shortfall is $900 - 694.22 = 205.78$, which is $205.78/300.53 = 0.685$ of year 3, so the discounted payback is 2.68 years.

@quiz mc
prompt: According to the static trade-off theory of capital structure, how does a firm determine its optimal debt level?
choice: It issues debt only after exhausting internal funds and equity, because of information asymmetry
choice*: It adds debt until the marginal tax benefit equals the marginal expected cost of financial distress
choice: It chooses any mix, because firm value is unaffected by capital structure when taxes exist
hint: Two opposing effects of debt are balanced.
solution: The static trade-off theory balances the tax shield of interest against the expected costs of financial distress. The choice about exhausting internal funds first describes the pecking order theory; the choice that any mix is fine describes Modigliani-Miller without taxes.
