@article
Last week gave you the statements; this week you interrogate them. Financial Statement Analysis is 11-14% of the 2026 Level I exam (weights change each year; see cfainstitute.org), and ratio questions are often the quickest points on the paper if you know the definitions precisely and the direction each ratio should move.

### Ratio families

| Family | Key ratios |
|---|---|
| Liquidity | Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities; quick ratio = (cash + marketable securities + receivables) / current liabilities |
| Activity | Receivables turnover = revenue / average receivables; inventory turnover = COGS / average inventory; payables turnover = purchases / average payables |
| Solvency | Debt-to-equity; interest coverage = EBIT / interest expense; financial leverage = average assets / average equity |
| Profitability | Gross, operating and net margin; ROA = net income / average assets; ROE = net income / average equity |

Days measures: **days sales outstanding** (DSO) $= 365/\text{receivables turnover}$, **days of inventory on hand** (DIO) $= 365/\text{inventory turnover}$ and **days payables outstanding** (DPO) $= 365/\text{payables turnover}$. The **cash conversion cycle** is $\text{DSO} + \text{DIO} - \text{DPO}$: the days cash is tied up in operations. State your convention: unless a question says to use averages, year-end balances are acceptable if the problem supplies only those.

### The DuPont decomposition

$$\text{ROE} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{NI}}{\text{Revenue}}}_{\text{net margin}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Assets}}}_{\text{asset turnover}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Assets}}{\text{Equity}}}_{\text{leverage}}.$$

It shows whether a high ROE comes from profitability, efficiency or borrowing. The five-step version splits net margin into the tax burden (NI/EBT), interest burden (EBT/EBIT) and EBIT margin. A rising ROE driven only by leverage is lower quality than one driven by margins.

### Income taxes

A **deferred tax liability** (DTL) arises when taxable income is lower than accounting pretax income today, so more tax will be paid later, for example from faster depreciation on the tax return than in the books. A **deferred tax asset** (DTA) arises when tax paid today exceeds tax expense, for example from warranty expense or a loss carryforward, and it is recognized if future benefit is probable (IFRS) or net of a valuation allowance (US GAAP). The effective tax rate is income tax expense divided by pretax income; permanent differences change it, temporary differences reverse.

### Per-share and cash flow measures

**Basic EPS** $= (\text{net income} - \text{preferred dividends})/\text{weighted-average common shares}$. New shares are weighted by the fraction of the year they were outstanding; stock splits and stock dividends restate all periods. Diluted EPS adds the effect of options, convertibles and warrants (treasury stock method for options). **Free cash flow to the firm**: $\text{FCFF} = \text{CFO} + \text{interest}(1 - t) - \text{capital expenditures}$ under US GAAP, where interest paid is in CFO. Free cash flow to equity: $\text{FCFE} = \text{CFO} - \text{FCInv} + \text{net borrowing}$.

### Quality of earnings

Warning signs: receivables growing much faster than revenue (aggressive recognition), inventory growing faster than sales, capitalizing expenses that peers expense, a persistent gap between net income and CFO, unusual gains in earnings, frequent "one-time" charges and changes in estimates (useful lives, allowances) that coincide with missed targets. IFRS 18 (replacing IAS 1 for annual periods beginning on or after January 1, 2027) adds operating, investing and financing categories and subtotals to the income statement and requires disclosure of management-defined performance measures, which makes some of these comparisons easier. Quality means **sustainable and repeatable** earnings, backed by cash.

### Technique

Write ratio definitions on your scratch pad at the start of the session. When a question gives only year-end balances, use them. Always sanity-check direction: higher inventory days means slower turnover.

@example
title: Cash conversion cycle and DuPont
problem: A firm has annual revenue of 4,000, COGS of 2,920, receivables of 400, inventory of 600 and payables of 240 (year-end balances, 365-day year). Net income is 160 and total assets are 2,000 with equity of 800. Find the cash conversion cycle and ROE.
step: DSO $= 400/4000 \times 365 = 36.5$ days. DIO $= 600/2920 \times 365 = 75$ days. DPO $= 240/2920 \times 365 = 30$ days.
step: Cash conversion cycle $= 36.5 + 75 - 30 = 81.5$ days.
step: Net margin $= 160/4000 = 4\%$; asset turnover $= 4000/2000 = 2.0$; leverage $= 2000/800 = 2.5$.
step: ROE $= 0.04 \times 2.0 \times 2.5 = 20\%$, which equals $160/800$.
answer: CCC = 81.5 days; ROE = 20%.

@mistakes
- Using revenue instead of COGS to compute inventory and payables days.
- Forgetting that the DuPont equity multiplier is assets over equity, so ROE rises with leverage even if margins fall.
- Calling every deferred tax difference a liability: whether it is an asset or a liability depends on whether tax will be paid later or was prepaid.
- Weighting new shares by the number of months remaining without dividing by 12, or forgetting the preferred dividends in basic EPS.

@practice num
prompt: A company has a net profit margin of 8%, an asset turnover of 1.25 and an equity multiplier (assets divided by equity) of 2.0. What is its return on equity? Give the answer as a percent to one decimal place.
answer: 20.0
unit: %
tol: 0.1
check: 0.08 * 1.25 * 2.0 * 100
hint: ROE is the product of margin, turnover and leverage.
solution: $\text{ROE} = 0.08 \times 1.25 \times 2.0 = 0.20 = 20.0\%$.

@practice num
prompt: A company has annual revenue of \$7,300 thousand, cost of goods sold of \$5,110 thousand, year-end receivables of \$840 thousand, year-end inventory of \$910 thousand and year-end payables of \$532 thousand. Using a 365-day year and year-end balances, what is the cash conversion cycle, to the nearest day?
answer: 69
unit: days
tol: 1
check: 840 / 7300 * 365 + 910 / 5110 * 365 - 532 / 5110 * 365
hint: DSO uses revenue; DIO and DPO use cost of goods sold.
hint: DSO = 42 days, DIO = 65 days and DPO = 38 days.
solution: $\text{DSO} = 840/7300 \times 365 = 42$, $\text{DIO} = 910/5110 \times 365 = 65$, $\text{DPO} = 532/5110 \times 365 = 38$, so the cash conversion cycle is $42 + 65 - 38 = 69$ days.

@practice num
prompt: A company reports net income of \$276 thousand. Its tax rate is 25% and its interest expense is \$92 thousand. What is its interest coverage ratio, defined as EBIT divided by interest expense?
answer: 5.0
unit: times
tol: 0.01
check: (276 / (1 - 0.25) + 92) / 92
hint: First go from net income to pretax income, then add back interest to get EBIT.
solution: Pretax income $= 276/0.75 = 368$; EBIT $= 368 + 92 = 460$; coverage $= 460/92 = 5.0$ times.

prompt: A company depreciates equipment straight-line in its financial statements but uses accelerated depreciation on its tax return. All else equal, what does this create in the early years?
choice*: A deferred tax liability, because taxable income is lower than pretax accounting income and the difference will reverse
choice: A deferred tax asset, because tax depreciation is larger than book depreciation
choice: No deferred tax item, because depreciation differences are permanent differences
hint: Is tax paid today smaller or larger than the tax expense on the books?
solution: Faster tax depreciation lowers taxable income now, so cash taxes are lower than book tax expense; the company will pay the difference later when the temporary difference reverses. That is a deferred tax liability.

@practice num
prompt: A firm reports cash flow from operations of \$640 million under US GAAP, after deducting interest paid of \$80 million. Its tax rate is 30% and it spent \$300 million on capital expenditures. What is its free cash flow to the firm, in millions of dollars?
answer: 396
lead: \$
tol: 0.5
check: 640 + 80 * (1 - 0.30) - 300
hint: Add back interest after tax, because CFO is after interest.
solution: $\text{FCFF} = 640 + 80(1 - 0.30) - 300 = 640 + 56 - 300 = \$396$ million.

@quiz num
prompt: A company reports net income of \$18,000 and pays preferred dividends of \$1,200. It has 5,000 common shares outstanding at the start of the year and issues 1,200 new shares on April 1. What is basic EPS? Round to the nearest cent.
answer: 2.85
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: (18000 - 1200) / (5000 + 1200 * 9 / 12)
hint: The new shares were outstanding for 9 of the 12 months.
hint: Weighted average shares $= 5{,}000 + 1{,}200 \times 9/12 = 5{,}900$.
solution: Earnings available to common are $18{,}000 - 1{,}200 = 16{,}800$; the weighted average is 5,900 shares; $16{,}800/5{,}900 = \$2.85$.

@quiz mc
prompt: Over two years a company's revenue is flat, but its year-end receivables rise 40% and its net income remains well above its operating cash flow. Which interpretation is most appropriate for an analyst?
choice: Earnings quality has improved, because accounts receivable are an asset
choice*: The pattern is a warning sign of aggressive revenue recognition or weaker collections, and warrants a closer look at the notes
choice: It shows the firm has become more efficient at collecting cash from customers
hint: Receivables should track revenue unless something has changed in how sales are booked or collected.
solution: Receivables growing far faster than revenue, together with net income exceeding CFO, suggests that earnings include credit sales not turning into cash, which signals possibly aggressive recognition or deteriorating credit quality.
