@article
Knowing the Standards is half of the job; the other half is reading a story quickly and finding the one fact that matters. Level I ethics questions are short stories with three endings. Your task is to pick the ending a careful charterholder would choose.

### A five-step framework

1. **Facts**: who is the actor, who is affected, and what has already happened? Separate what the stem says from what you assume.
2. **Standards**: which Standards could apply? Most stories engage two or three; pick the most direct one first.
3. **Violation test**: did the actor breach the duty? Check the safe harbors (mosaic theory, client consent, disclosed differences in service, public information).
4. **Best action**: if there is a problem, what is the most protective response? Typical order: stop the harm, protect clients and the market, disclose, escalate to a supervisor or compliance, document.
5. **Eliminate**: remove choices that do nothing, that shift blame, that conceal, or that over-react by breaking confidentiality when a lesser step was available.

### Patterns that recur

**Conflicts and gifts.** A gift, a relative, a personal holding or an issuer relationship that could affect judgment calls for disclosure (VI(A)) and sometimes for refusal (I(B)). If the gift or fee comes from someone other than the employer, ask whether it needs employer consent (IV(B)).

**Material nonpublic information.** Information is material if a reasonable investor would likely use it in a decision, and nonpublic if not yet widely disseminated. If you hold it, do not trade, tip or recommend on it (II(A)). A firm should use information barriers; the member should escalate to compliance rather than make a personal judgment.

**Market manipulation.** Trades or statements intended to move price or mislead, such as buying at the close to lift a valuation or spreading false rumors, breach II(B). Legitimate trading in size is not manipulation by itself: look for intent to distort.

**Diligence.** Before recommending or acting, you need a reasonable basis (V(A)). A vendor model, a third-party report or a subadviser can be relied on only if you have understood and checked its limits. Changes in the investment process must be communicated (V(B)).

**Confidentiality.** Client information is private (III(E)), with three exceptions: the client permits disclosure, the law requires it, or the information concerns illegal activity by the client. In the last case disclosure is permitted, not an obligation to publish widely: go to the appropriate authority.

**Supervision.** A supervisor who learns of a violation must act promptly, stop it and report within the firm's procedures (IV(C)); a written compliance system alone is not enough if it is not enforced.

**Performance.** Presentations must be fair, accurate and complete (III(D)). The Global Investment Performance Standards (the 2020 edition is current) are voluntary, are claimed by a **firm** (not an individual) and require composites of similar portfolios and, for most strategies, time-weighted returns; a firm either complies with all requirements or may not claim compliance, and verification is optional.

### Writing the case log

Your deliverable for these two weeks is to answer forty ethics cases and **explain each answer**. A good log entry has four parts: the Standard (with its number), the one fact that triggers it, why the winning choice beats the others, and the action you would take. Write the explanation before you look at the solution, then compare, and note why any miss happened (misread fact, wrong Standard, too-aggressive remedy, too-passive remedy).

@example
title: A tip at a dinner
problem: At a dinner, a friend who is a director of a listed company tells you in confidence that the company will announce a takeover bid next week, at a large premium. You manage a client portfolio. What should you do?
step: Facts: the information is specific, would move the price (material) and has not been announced (nonpublic). It comes from an insider with a duty of confidence.
step: Standard: II(A) Material Nonpublic Information. Trading, or recommending the stock, on this information would breach it. The mosaic theory does not apply: this is a single material nonpublic fact.
step: Best action: do not trade or tip, and do not use the information for any account. Tell compliance if the firm's procedures require it, and consider adding the stock to a restricted list. You may still trade on public information later, after the announcement.
answer: Standard II(A): do not act or cause others to act; escalate to compliance as procedures require.

@mistakes
- Choosing a response that fixes nothing (for example, "keep an eye on it") when a problem exists and escalation is available.
- Breaking client confidentiality when a smaller step (escalating to a supervisor or compliance) is the best action.
- Missing the safe harbor: public information, client consent, or the mosaic theory can make a plainly suspicious story acceptable.
- Applying a Standard that sounds right but is not the most direct one (for instance, citing I(D) Misconduct when the fact pattern is really about V(A) Diligence).

@practice mc
prompt: A portfolio manager directs most client trades to a broker that offers the manager complimentary vacations, although other brokers offer better prices for the clients. Which Standard is violated?
choice*: Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care, because client brokerage must be used for the clients' benefit
choice: Standard V(C) Record Retention, because the vacations were not recorded
choice: No Standard, because the manager did not receive cash
hint: Whose benefit should drive the choice of broker?
solution: Standard III(A) requires the manager to put clients' interests first, including in the use of brokerage. Steering trades to a broker for the manager's personal benefit, at a cost to clients, violates it; the vacations may also engage I(B) and VI(A).

@practice mc
prompt: An analyst copies several paragraphs of text and two charts from a free industry-association white paper into a published research report, without naming the source. The numbers on the charts are correct. Which Standard is violated?
choice: Standard V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis, because the numbers should have been recalculated
choice*: Standard I(C) Misrepresentation, because presenting another party's work as one's own is plagiarism
choice: No Standard, because the white paper was available free of charge
hint: Accuracy of the numbers is not the issue; credit is.
solution: Standard I(C) prohibits plagiarism: copying or using others' material without attribution, whether or not it is free and accurate. Crediting the association (and verifying the data) would have been acceptable.

@practice mc
prompt: In the final minutes of a quarter, a fund trader buys small lots of a thinly traded stock the fund already holds, at rising prices, so that the closing price lifts the fund's reported quarter-end value. Which Standard is violated?
choice: Standard III(D) Performance Presentation, because the quarter-end report was wrong in form
choice*: Standard II(B) Market Manipulation, because the trades were intended to distort the price
choice: Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information, because the trader knew about the fund's holdings
hint: Look for intent to move the price rather than to invest.
solution: Standard II(B) prohibits trading practices intended to distort prices or artificially inflate trading volume. Buying at the close of a thin market to boost the fund's valuation is a classic example.

@practice mc
prompt: An investment adviser notices that a client's account shows a pattern of large cash deposits and immediate transfers to foreign accounts that strongly suggests money laundering. Which statement best describes the adviser's obligations under the Standards?
choice: Standard III(E) forbids any disclosure of client information, so the adviser must stay silent
choice*: Standard III(E) permits disclosure to the proper authorities, because the information concerns possible illegal activity by the client
choice: Standard III(E) requires the adviser to warn the firm's other clients about the account
hint: Confidentiality has exceptions.
solution: Standard III(E) preserves confidentiality except where the client consents, the law requires disclosure, or the information concerns illegal activity. The adviser may escalate to compliance and report to the appropriate authority; telling other clients would itself be an improper disclosure.

@practice self
prompt: Case-log exercise. A research analyst is about to initiate coverage of a mid-cap manufacturer. The analyst's spouse owns shares in the company, bought two years ago. The analyst has not told anyone at the firm. Write a case-log entry in four parts: the Standards engaged, the key fact, what the analyst should do, and what the firm should do.
hint: Decide whether the holding could reasonably affect the analyst's objectivity, and who needs to know.
solution: Standards: VI(A) Disclosure of Conflicts and I(B) Independence and Objectivity (and V(A) Diligence, since the report must have a reasonable basis). Key fact: the analyst's household holds shares in the subject company, which could reasonably be seen as affecting objectivity. Analyst: disclose the holding to the employer before initiating coverage and in the report, and follow the firm's procedure on recusal or restricted trading. Firm: decide whether the analyst may cover the stock, require disclosure to readers, and monitor trading in the stock.
rubric: Names VI(A) Disclosure of Conflicts and I(B) Independence and Objectivity.
rubric: Identifies the spouse's shareholding as the fact that creates the conflict and says it must be disclosed to the employer and to report readers.
rubric: States a concrete action for the analyst (disclose, follow recusal or restricted-list procedures) and a role for the firm or compliance.

@quiz mc
prompt: A portfolio manager licenses a bond risk model from a vendor and pastes the vendor's risk model output into client reports without testing the model or understanding how it treats embedded options. The model later proves to understate risk on callable bonds. Which Standard has the manager most directly violated?
choice*: Standard V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis
choice: Standard II(B) Market Manipulation
choice: Standard IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements
hint: The failure is about checking the basis for a conclusion before using it.
solution: Standard V(A) requires a reasonable and adequate basis, supported by appropriate research. Relying on a vendor model without understanding its limits is not diligence, and V(B) is also engaged because the clients were not told of the limitation.

@quiz mc
prompt: A supervisor learns that a trader on her team has been buying a stock for his own account just before large client orders in the same stock. What should the supervisor do?
choice: Privately warn the trader and take no further action, since this is the first time it has come to her attention
choice*: Stop the activity, report it promptly to the firm's compliance or management under its procedures, and make sure client accounts are reviewed
choice: Wait for the firm's annual compliance review to detect the problem
hint: A supervisor's duty is to prevent and stop violations, not only to warn.
solution: Standard IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors requires reasonable efforts to prevent and detect violations and prompt action when one is known. The trader's pattern also breaches VI(B) Priority of Transactions and III(A), so the supervisor must stop it, escalate and protect clients.
