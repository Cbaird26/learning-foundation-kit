Ethics can decide a Level I attempt, because it is the largest topic and one of the most learnable. Candidates who treat it as "common sense" lose points on questions where the common-sense answer is not the Standards' answer. This lesson gives you the map; the next one drills cases.

### The 2026 exam in one paragraph

Level I is computer based: 180 multiple-choice questions with **three** answer choices, in two sessions of 2 hours 15 minutes (90 questions each), so about 1.5 minutes per question. For the 2026 exams the topic weights are Ethical and Professional Standards 15-20%, Quantitative Methods 6-9%, Economics 6-9%, Financial Statement Analysis 11-14%, Corporate Issuers 6-9%, Equity Investments 11-14%, Fixed Income 11-14%, Derivatives 5-8%, Alternative Investments 7-10% and Portfolio Management 8-12%. From the February 2027 exams the weights change (Ethics 10-15%, Quantitative Methods 11-14%) and GIPS stops being a separate Level I reading. **Weights, rules and fees change every year**: confirm them at cfainstitute.org before you plan. CFA Institute recommends about 300 hours of study per level. This course is independent study material and is not affiliated with or endorsed by CFA Institute.

### Structure

The **Code of Ethics** is a short set of principles: act with integrity, competence and respect; put the integrity of the profession and clients' interests above your own; use reasonable care and independent judgment; promote the integrity of capital markets; and keep your competence current. The **Standards of Professional Conduct** (updated effective January 1, 2024) turn those principles into seven areas:

| Area | Standards |
|---|---|
| I Professionalism | (A) Knowledge of the Law, (B) Independence and Objectivity, (C) Misrepresentation, (D) Misconduct, (E) Competence |
| II Integrity of Capital Markets | (A) Material Nonpublic Information, (B) Market Manipulation |
| III Duties to Clients | (A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care, (B) Fair Dealing, (C) Suitability, (D) Performance Presentation, (E) Preservation of Confidentiality |
| IV Duties to Employers | (A) Loyalty, (B) Additional Compensation Arrangements, (C) Responsibilities of Supervisors |
| V Investment Analysis, Recommendations, and Actions | (A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis, (B) Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients, (C) Record Retention |
| VI Conflicts of Interest | (A) Disclosure of Conflicts, (B) Priority of Transactions, (C) Referral Fees |
| VII Responsibilities as a CFA Institute Member or CFA Candidate | (A) Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs, (B) Reference to CFA Institute, the CFA Designation, and the CFA Program |

### The core duty in each area

- **I(A)**: follow the **stricter** of applicable law and the Code and Standards. Do not knowingly take part in a violation, and dissociate from one (and consider reporting it).
- **I(B)**: keep judgment free of pressure. Gifts that could reasonably impair objectivity must be refused or disclosed; issuer-paid research needs disclosure.
- **I(C)**: no misstatements of fact, and no **plagiarism** (copying others' work or ideas without credit). Using third-party research is fine if you attribute it and have verified it reasonably.
- **I(D)**: no conduct involving dishonesty, fraud or deceit, or that reflects adversely on your professional reputation.
- **I(E)**: keep and improve competence; do not claim skills you lack.
- **II(A)**: do not act or cause others to act on **material nonpublic information**. The **mosaic theory** lets an analyst combine public information and nonmaterial nonpublic pieces into a conclusion.
- **II(B)**: no price or volume distortion, and no spreading of false rumors, intended to mislead the market.
- **III(A)**: duty of loyalty, prudence and care to clients, who come first. Brokerage (including soft dollars) must be used for the clients' benefit.
- **III(B)**: treat clients fairly when disseminating recommendations and allocating trades; do not favor some clients.
- **III(C)**: make recommendations suitable to the client's objectives, constraints and risk tolerance, documented in an investment policy statement (IPS) and updated regularly.
- **III(D)**: performance presentations must be fair, accurate and complete.
- **III(E)**: keep client information confidential, except when the client permits disclosure, the law requires it, or the information concerns illegal activity.
- **IV(A)**: do not harm your employer; do not take client lists or work product when you leave. **IV(B)**: get written employer consent for compensation from others that could conflict with your employer's interests. **IV(C)**: supervisors must make reasonable efforts to prevent and detect violations.
- **V(A)**: a reasonable basis and diligence behind every recommendation. **V(B)**: communicate the process and limitations. **V(C)**: keep records supporting your work; seven years is the recommended minimum when no rule requires longer.
- **VI(A)** disclose conflicts. **VI(B)**: client transactions first, then employer's, then your own. **VI(C)**: disclose referral fees.
- **VII(A)**: do not cheat or disclose exam content. **VII(B)**: do not exaggerate what the designation or the program implies.

### Exam technique for ethics

1. Name the Standard before you read the choices. 2. The best answer usually **protects clients and the market first**, then involves the employer, then the member. 3. The strongest answers are proactive and conservative: disclose, seek written consent, escalate to compliance, decline. Doing nothing, or doing something clever, is rarely best. 4. Watch for the **stricter rule** and for words such as "must", "may" and "should": a Standard may recommend rather than require.

@example
title: Spotting the Standard
problem: A portfolio manager's brother is chief executive of a small-cap company. The manager plans to add the company's shares to several client portfolios. Identify the Standards engaged and the best action.
step: Read for the conflict: the manager has a family relationship with an issuer. This engages VI(A) Disclosure of Conflicts and I(B) Independence and Objectivity, and the purchases for clients also touch III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care.
step: Ask whether the relationship could reasonably impair independence. It could, because the manager might favor a relative's company over better alternatives.
step: Choose the conservative action: disclose the relationship to the employer and to the affected clients, document the reasonable basis for the investment (V(A)), and let the firm's compliance process decide whether the manager should recuse.
answer: Standards VI(A), I(B), III(A) and V(A); disclose, document the basis, and follow compliance's decision.

@mistakes
- Picking the answer that sounds practical or profitable instead of the one that protects clients and the market first.
- Treating a recommendation (such as keeping records for seven years) as an absolute rule, or a requirement as optional.
- Forgetting that the stricter of the law and the Code governs: neither "follow local law only" nor "ignore the law" is right.
- Confusing the mosaic theory (allowed: public and nonmaterial information combined) with trading on material nonpublic information (prohibited).

@practice mc
prompt: A member works in a country whose law is less demanding than the Code and Standards on a particular duty. Which course of action is required?
choice: Follow local law, because the law of the country where the member works always governs
choice*: Follow the stricter of local law and the Code and Standards, which here is the Code and Standards
choice: Follow the Code and Standards even where local law is stricter, since the Code overrides all law
hint: Two sources of rules apply to you at once.
hint: The member is bound by whichever of them asks more.
solution: Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law requires compliance with the stricter of applicable law and the Code and Standards. Here the Code is stricter, so it governs; if the law were stricter, the law would govern.

@practice mc
prompt: A client offers an investment manager a cash bonus, paid directly to the manager, if the client's portfolio beats its benchmark by 2 percentage points this year. The bonus is in addition to the fee the firm charges. What must the manager do before accepting?
choice: Nothing, because the bonus is paid by a client and rewards good performance for that client
choice*: Disclose the arrangement to the employer and obtain written consent
choice: Disclose the arrangement to the client's other advisers only
hint: Think about who could be harmed if the manager's effort shifts toward this one account.
solution: Standard IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements requires written consent from the employer for compensation or benefits from third parties, including clients, that could create a conflict with the employer's interests.

@practice mc
prompt: An analyst covering a retailer combines the company's public filings, industry data and several conversations with its suppliers, who describe general order trends without revealing confidential figures. The analyst concludes that earnings will exceed expectations and issues a buy recommendation. Which statement is most accurate?
choice: The analyst violated Standard II(A) because some of the information was not public
choice*: The analyst did not violate Standard II(A), because combining public and nonmaterial nonpublic information is consistent with the mosaic theory
choice: The analyst violated Standard I(C) because suppliers were not named in the report
hint: Ask whether any single piece of information was both material and nonpublic.
solution: Standard II(A) prohibits acting on material nonpublic information. Under the mosaic theory, an analyst may build a conclusion from public information and nonmaterial nonpublic pieces. Nothing here was both material and nonpublic, so there is no violation.

@practice mc
prompt: A research analyst plans to leave to start a competing firm. While still employed, the analyst does one of three things. Which action violates Standard IV(A) Loyalty?
choice: Registers the new firm's name with the business registry on a weekend
choice: Rents an office for the new firm and pays for it personally
choice*: Copies the employer's client list from the firm's database to take to the new firm
hint: Preparing to compete is allowed; using the employer's property or time is not.
solution: Standard IV(A) allows a member to prepare to compete if the preparation does not use the employer's time, property or confidential information. The client list belongs to the employer, so copying it violates the Standard. The registration and the office lease do not use employer resources.

@practice mc
prompt: A research analyst downgrades a stock from buy to sell. The analyst telephones the firm's largest institutional clients first, and the firm's other clients receive the change by email three hours later. The firm has no disclosed policy on distribution. Which Standard is most directly at issue, and is it violated?
choice*: Standard III(B) Fair Dealing is violated, because clients were not given a fair opportunity to act on the change
choice: Standard III(B) Fair Dealing is not violated, because larger clients may always receive faster service
choice: Standard V(B) Communication is violated, because the downgrade was not published in writing first
hint: The issue is how a recommendation change reaches different clients.
solution: Standard III(B) requires fair treatment of clients when disseminating recommendations; informing favored clients first, without a disclosed policy, gives them an advantage. Differences in service levels are acceptable only if disclosed and they do not disadvantage other clients.

@quiz mc
prompt: A portfolio manager wants to buy 20,000 shares of a stock for a personal account. Client portfolios the manager runs are about to buy the same stock in a large order that will probably move the price. What does the Code and Standards require?
choice: The manager may buy first, because the personal order is small relative to the clients' order
choice*: Client orders must be completed first, then the employer's, and the manager's personal purchase last
choice: The manager may trade alongside the clients if the trade is disclosed afterwards
hint: The Standard sets an order of priority for transactions.
solution: Standard VI(B) Priority of Transactions places clients' and the employer's transactions ahead of those of the member, so the personal purchase must wait until the client orders are done.

@quiz mc
prompt: Which statement about reference to the CFA Program is permitted under Standard VII(B)?
choice*: "I passed Level I of the CFA Program."
choice: "I am a Level I CFA, so my advice is more reliable than other analysts' advice."
choice: "I passed all three exams on the first attempt, which shows I will deliver superior investment results."
hint: A factual statement is fine; a title or a claim of superior skill is not.
solution: Standard VII(B) permits a factual statement of the exams passed. Calling oneself a "Level I CFA" implies a designation not yet earned, and claiming that exam results predict superior investment performance exaggerates what the program implies.
