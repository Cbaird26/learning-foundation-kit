@article
A **scale drawing** is a picture of a real object, such as a map or a floor plan, in which every length is multiplied by the same number. Because every length is scaled equally, the shape stays the same and only the size changes.

### Reading a scale

A scale tells you how a length on the drawing compares with the real length. It can be written two ways:

- With units: $1\text{ cm} = 5\text{ m}$ means each centimeter on the drawing stands for 5 meters in real life.
- As a ratio: $1 : 50\,000$ means one unit on the drawing is $50\,000$ of the same unit in real life. With no units, this works for any unit.

The scale is a proportional relationship, so a drawing length and an actual length have a constant ratio.

### Finding actual lengths

Multiply the drawing length by the scale. If $1\text{ cm} = 5\text{ m}$ and a road measures $4$ cm on the map, the real road is $4 \cdot 5 = 20$ m long.

To make a drawing, go the other way and divide. A wall of $18$ ft at the scale $1\text{ in} = 4\text{ ft}$ is $\frac{18}{4} = 4.5$ inches on paper.

### Using a proportion

You can write $\frac{\text{drawing}}{\text{actual}} = \frac{1}{5}$ and solve. Keep the units on the same side: drawing units on top, actual units on the bottom.

### Converting units

For ratio scales, convert at the end. With $1 : 50\,000$, a $3$ cm line stands for $3 \cdot 50\,000 = 150\,000$ cm. Since $100\,000$ cm $= 1$ km, that is $1.5$ km.

### Scale and area

If lengths are multiplied by $k$, areas are multiplied by $k^2$. With the scale $1\text{ cm} = 2\text{ m}$, each centimeter of length stands for $2$ m, so each square centimeter on the drawing stands for $2 \cdot 2 = 4$ square meters. Never multiply an area by just the scale.

### Making your own scale drawing

Choose a scale that fits the paper, divide each real length by it, and draw the lengths exactly. Label the scale on your drawing, since a scale drawing without its scale cannot be used to find real sizes.

@example
title: Actual size from a drawing
problem: On a floor plan with scale $1\text{ in} = 6\text{ ft}$, a room is drawn $3.5$ in wide and $2$ in long. Find the actual width and length, and the actual floor area.
step: Multiply each drawing length by $6$: width $3.5 \cdot 6 = 21$ ft, length $2 \cdot 6 = 12$ ft.
step: The actual dimensions are $21$ ft by $12$ ft.
step: The floor area is $21 \cdot 12 = 252$ square feet.
step: Check with the area rule: the drawing area is $3.5 \cdot 2 = 7$ square inches, and each stands for $6^2 = 36$ square feet, so $7 \cdot 36 = 252$.

@mistakes
- Multiplying an area by the scale instead of the scale squared.
- Dividing when you should multiply (or the reverse). Ask whether the answer should be bigger than the drawing.
- Forgetting to convert units, such as centimeters to kilometers on a map.

prompt: A room is $12$ ft by $9$ ft. It is drawn at the scale $1\text{ in} = 3\text{ ft}$. What is the area of the room on the drawing, in square inches?
answer: 12
unit: square in
check: (12 / 3) * (9 / 3)
hint: First find the drawing's length and width in inches.
hint: The drawing is $4$ in by $3$ in.
solution: Length $\frac{12}{3} = 4$ in and width $\frac{9}{3} = 3$ in, so the area is $4 \cdot 3 = 12$ square inches.
