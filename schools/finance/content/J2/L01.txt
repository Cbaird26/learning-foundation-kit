@article
Two quantities are in a **proportional relationship** when one is always the same multiple of the other. If 1 pound of apples costs \$2, then 2 pounds cost \$4, 3 pounds cost \$6, and so on. The cost is always $2$ times the weight.

### The constant of proportionality

That fixed multiple is called the **constant of proportionality**, written $k$. For a proportional relationship between $x$ and $y$,

$$k = \frac{y}{x} \qquad\text{and so}\qquad y = kx.$$

The number $k$ is also the **unit rate**: the value of $y$ when $x = 1$. In the apple example $k = 2$ dollars per pound.

### Spotting proportionality in three forms

**In a table.** Divide $y$ by $x$ in every row. If you always get the same number, the relationship is proportional. If the ratios differ, it is not.

| $x$ | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| $y$ | 4 | 8 | 12 |

Every ratio $\frac{y}{x}$ equals $4$, so $k = 4$.

**On a graph.** The graph of a proportional relationship is a **straight line that passes through the origin** $(0, 0)$. A straight line that misses the origin is not proportional, and neither is a curve. On the line, $k$ is the $y$-value at $x = 1$, or the ratio $\frac{y}{x}$ at any point.

**In an equation.** An equation is proportional if it can be written as $y = kx$. So $y = 5x$ is proportional, but $y = 5x + 2$ is not, because it has an added constant and its graph crosses the $y$-axis above the origin.

### Using the equation

Once you know $k$, you can find any value. If a machine fills 3 bottles every 2 minutes, then $k = \frac{3}{2}$ bottles per minute, and in $10$ minutes it fills $y = \frac{3}{2}\cdot 10 = 15$ bottles.

A quick check for any point: the pair $(x, y)$ is on the line only if $\frac{y}{x} = k$. The point $(0, 0)$ is always on it, since zero of one quantity goes with zero of the other.

@example
title: From a word problem to an equation
problem: Four pounds of apples cost \$6. Write an equation for the cost $y$ of $x$ pounds, and find the cost of $10$ pounds.
step: The relationship is proportional (the price per pound is fixed), so find the unit rate: $k = \frac{6}{4} = 1.5$ dollars per pound.
step: Write the equation: $y = 1.5x$.
step: Substitute $x = 10$: $y = 1.5 \cdot 10 = 15$.
step: So $10$ pounds cost \$15. Check: the point $(4, 6)$ gives $\frac{6}{4} = 1.5$, and $(10, 15)$ gives $\frac{15}{10} = 1.5$. Same ratio, so both fit.

@mistakes
- Using $y - x$ instead of $\frac{y}{x}$: differences do not give the constant of proportionality.
- Calling any straight line proportional. The line must also pass through the origin.
- Dividing $x$ by $y$ in one row and $y$ by $x$ in another, so the ratios do not match. Always divide in the same direction.

@practice num
prompt: A table shows the pairs $(2, 6)$, $(5, 15)$ and $(8, 24)$ for $x$ and $y$. The relationship is proportional. What is the constant of proportionality $k$?
answer: 3
check: 15 / 5
hint: Divide $y$ by $x$ in any one row.
solution: $k = \frac{y}{x} = \frac{15}{5} = 3$. Every row gives $3$.

@practice num
prompt: A recipe uses 3 cups of flour for every 12 muffins. How many cups of flour are needed for 20 muffins?
answer: 5
check: 3 * 20 / 12
hint: Find the flour per muffin: $k = \frac{3}{12}$.
hint: Multiply $k$ by $20$.
solution: $k = \frac{3}{12} = \frac{1}{4}$ cup per muffin. For $20$ muffins, $\frac{1}{4}\cdot 20 = 5$ cups.

@quiz num
prompt: Seven notebooks cost \$10.50 at a constant price each. What do 12 notebooks cost, in dollars?
answer: 18
check: 10.5 / 7 * 12
hint: Find the price of one notebook.
hint: The unit price is $\frac{10.50}{7}$. Multiply by $12$.
solution: One notebook costs $\frac{10.50}{7} = 1.50$ dollars, so $12$ cost $1.50 \cdot 12 = 18$ dollars.

@quiz mc
