@project
title: Floor plan, paint budget and a probability game
overview: You will draw a scale floor plan of a room, compute its area and the cost of painting its walls including sales tax, and design and test a probability game. The project pulls together proportional reasoning, percent, equations, area and probability.
goal: Make an accurate scale drawing and use it to find real areas.
goal: Use percent and equations to budget a paint job.
goal: Compare the theoretical and experimental probabilities of a game you design.
step: Use this room: it is a rectangle $4.5$ m long and $3.6$ m wide with walls $2.5$ m high. It has one door $0.9$ m wide and $2$ m high, and one window $1.2$ m wide and $1$ m high. Choose a scale (for example $1\text{ cm} = 0.5\text{ m}$) and compute the drawing length and width.
step: Draw the floor plan on paper with a ruler. Mark the door and window, and label the scale.
step: Find the actual floor area, then check it using the drawing: measure the drawing area and multiply by the scale squared.
step: Compute the wall area to be painted: perimeter times wall height, minus the area of the door and the window. A circular rug of diameter $2$ m is to go on the floor; find its area using $\pi$ and what fraction of the floor it covers.
step: Budget the paint. One can covers $10$ square meters per coat, you need $2$ coats, and a can costs \$28 before $8\%$ sales tax. Find the number of whole cans (round up) and the total cost with tax. Then write and solve an equation or inequality for how many cans you could buy with a budget of \$250.
step: Design a game with a spinner or two dice. State the rules, then compute the theoretical probability of winning and the probability of each prize.
step: Play the game at least $50$ times (or simulate it with dice or coins), record the results in a table, and compute the experimental probability. Compare it with the theoretical probability and explain any difference.
step: Write a one-page summary that explains how you found each answer.
deliverable: The scale floor plan with the scale labeled and the floor area computed two ways.
deliverable: A paint cost table showing wall area, cans, price, tax and total, with each step explained.
deliverable: A game description with theoretical probability, results of at least $50$ plays and a comparison.
rubric: The scale drawing uses one consistent scale and its measurements are accurate within $1$ mm.
rubric: Areas, percent and tax calculations are correct, and units are shown (m, m$^2$, dollars).
rubric: The equation or inequality for the budget is set up correctly, solved, and checked in the story.
rubric: The probability game has clear rules, a correct theoretical probability, and an honest comparison with the experimental results.
rubric: The summary uses math vocabulary correctly and each answer is explained.
safety: This project is planning only. Do not climb or paint anything. If you build a spinner, use a pencil and paper clip with a grown-up's help.
tutor: Can you check my scale conversion and explain why area uses the scale squared?
tutor: How can I tell whether my game is fair, and what does a fair game mean here?
tutor: My experimental probability differs from the theoretical one. How many more plays would help?

@review
**Week 1 to 2: proportions and percent.** A proportional relationship has $y = kx$; the table has a constant ratio $\frac{y}{x} = k$, and the graph is a line through the origin. Tax, tips and discounts are multipliers: $1.08$ for $8\%$ tax and $0.80$ for $20\%$ off. Percent change is $\frac{\text{new} - \text{original}}{\text{original}}$.

**Week 3 to 4: rational numbers.** Subtraction is adding the opposite, and distance is $|a - b|$. Products and quotients with the same signs are positive and with different signs negative. Divide by a fraction by multiplying by its reciprocal.

**Week 5 to 6: expressions and equations.** Combine like terms, distribute to every term, and factor out a common factor. Undo operations in reverse order, and flip an inequality sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative number. Open circle for $<$ and $>$, closed for $\leq$ and $\geq$.

**Week 7 to 8: scale and circles.** Real length is drawing length times the scale; areas scale by the square. $C = 2\pi r$, $A = \pi r^2$, and $d = 2r$.

**Week 9 to 10: angles and solids.** Complementary angles add to $90^\circ$, supplementary to $180^\circ$, vertical angles are equal, and triangle angles add to $180^\circ$. Triangle inequality: the two shorter sides must add to more than the longest. Volume of a prism is base area times height; surface area is the sum of all faces.

**Week 11 to 12: probability and sampling.** Probability is favorable over total, between $0$ and $1$. Multiply for independent stages. Random samples estimate populations; compare data sets using mean, median and MAD.

**Self-check:** Can you find $k$ from a table, graph and equation? Can you explain the sign rules with a number line? Can you solve $px + q = r$ and graph an inequality? Can you find a real area from a scale drawing?

**Common errors:** dividing by the new value in percent change, wrong sign when subtracting a negative, distributing to only one term, not flipping an inequality, using the diameter as the radius, multiplying an area by the scale instead of its square, and forgetting units.

@exam num
prompt: A room is $5$ m long, $4$ m wide and $2.5$ m high, with no doors or windows to subtract. You paint all four walls with one coat. One can covers $12$ square meters and costs \$30 before $7\%$ sales tax. Cans can only be bought whole. What is the total cost with tax, in dollars?
answer: 128.4
unit: dollars
tol: 0.01
check: ceil(2 * (5 + 4) * 2.5 / 12) * 30 * 1.07
hint: Find the wall area from the perimeter and the height.
hint: The wall area is $45$ square meters. Divide by the coverage of one can and round up.
solution: Wall area is $2(5 + 4) \cdot 2.5 = 45$ m$^2$. $\frac{45}{12} = 3.75$, so $4$ cans are needed. The cost is $4 \cdot 30 = 120$ dollars, and with tax $120 \cdot 1.07 = 128.40$ dollars.

@exam num
prompt: On a scale drawing with the scale $1\text{ cm} = 0.4\text{ m}$, a circular rug is drawn with a diameter of $5$ cm. The rug sells for \$20 per square meter and is $10\%$ off. What is the sale cost of the rug, in dollars, rounded to the nearest cent? Use the $\pi$ key on a calculator.
answer: 56.55
unit: dollars
tol: 0.01
check: 20 * pi * (5 * 0.4 / 2)**2 * 0.9
hint: Convert the drawn diameter to an actual diameter, then find the radius.
hint: The diameter is $2$ m, so the radius is $1$ m and the area is $\pi$ square meters.
solution: Actual diameter is $5 \cdot 0.4 = 2$ m, so $r = 1$ m and $A = \pi$ m$^2$. Before discount the cost is $20\pi \approx 62.83$ dollars. With $10\%$ off, $62.83 \cdot 0.9 \approx 56.55$ dollars.

@exam num
prompt: A bag has $5$ red marbles and some number $x$ of blue marbles. The probability of drawing a red marble at random is $\frac{1}{4}$. How many blue marbles are in the bag?
answer: 15
check: root(lambda x: 5 / (5 + x) - 1 / 4, 0, 100)
hint: Write an equation: red marbles over total marbles equals $\frac{1}{4}$.
hint: $\frac{5}{5 + x} = \frac{1}{4}$. Cross multiply.
solution: $\frac{5}{5 + x} = \frac{1}{4}$ gives $20 = 5 + x$, so $x = 15$. Check: $\frac{5}{20} = \frac{1}{4}$.
