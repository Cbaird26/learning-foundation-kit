## Week-by-week key ideas

1. **Income**: gross pay is wage times hours (overtime at 1.5 times above 40 hours a week); net pay = gross - taxes - deductions. FICA is 7.65% (6.2% Social Security plus 1.45% Medicare). A match is part of total compensation.
2. **Budgeting**: build from net pay; cash flow = income - expenses; savings rate = saved/income; 26 biweekly paychecks a year; pay yourself first.
3. **Banking**: APY is the effective annual rate; FDIC covers \$250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category; emergency fund = essential monthly expenses times 3 to 6.
4. **Taxes (2026, single)**: taxable income = income - \$16,100; marginal brackets 10%, 12%, 22%...; refund = withheld - tax; a credit beats a deduction of the same size.
5. **Credit**: payment history (about 35%) and utilization (about 30%) matter most; utilization = balances/limits; interest each month = APR/12 times the balance.
6. **Loans**: $M = P\,i/(1 - (1+i)^{-n})$; total interest = $nM - P$; longer terms lower the payment but raise interest.
7. **College**: net price = cost of attendance - grants and scholarships; interest accrues on unsubsidized loans during school.
8. **Housing**: PITI, PMI under 20% down, the 28% guideline on gross income, total rent with annual increases.
9. **Insurance**: you pay the smaller of deductible + coinsurance share and the out-of-pocket maximum; premiums exceed expected losses, but insurance protects against large losses.
10. **Investing**: holding-period return; geometric mean for growth; diversification reduces company risk, not market risk; fees compound.
11. **Retirement**: traditional (pre-tax) versus Roth (after-tax); 2026 limits of \$24,500 (401(k)) and \$7,500 (IRA); $FV = P\,((1+i)^n - 1)/i$.
12. **Protection**: scam red flags, credit freeze, \$50 liability limits, APR of short-term loans, opportunity cost.

- I can go from a wage or salary to net pay and a monthly budget.
- I can compute taxable income, tax from marginal brackets, and a refund or balance due.
- I can compute a loan payment and total interest, and compare two loans.
- I can compare two college plans by net price and explain student-loan interest.

- I can project a retirement balance and state its assumptions.

## Common errors

- Using gross instead of net pay in a budget, or the annual rate as a monthly rate.
- Applying a tax rate to all income instead of marginal slices, or forgetting the standard deduction.
- Comparing loans by payment instead of total interest.
- Comparing schools by sticker price instead of net price.
- Mixing nominal and real values, or forgetting to state compounding and timing in a projection.
