@article
College is one of the largest purchases most people make, and the advertised price is rarely what a student pays. Learning to compute the real price, and what you will owe afterwards, is a skill that can save tens of thousands of dollars.

### Sticker price and net price

The **cost of attendance** (COA) is the school's estimate of one year's total cost: tuition and fees, housing and food, books and supplies, transportation and personal expenses. It is the "sticker price." The **net price** is what you pay after subtracting **free money**, aid you do not repay:

$$\text{net price} = \text{cost of attendance} - \text{grants} - \text{scholarships}.$$

Every school must offer a **net price calculator** on its website, which gives an estimate based on your family's information. Compare schools by net price, not sticker price.

### Types of aid

- **Grants**: need-based aid (federal, state or school) that is not repaid.
- **Scholarships**: awards based on merit or other criteria; also not repaid.
- **Work-study**: a part-time job, with earnings paid to you as a paycheck.
- **Loans**: borrowed money that must be repaid with interest. Federal loans come from the government; private loans come from lenders.

To apply for federal and many state and school aid, you file the **FAFSA** (Free Application for Federal Student Aid). It produces a **Student Aid Index (SAI)**, a number schools use to decide need-based aid. Federal student loan rules changed for loans first made on or after July 1, 2026 (new repayment plans and borrowing limits), so check studentaid.gov for current terms.

### Student loan interest

On an **unsubsidized** loan, interest starts accruing as soon as the money is paid out, even while you are in school. For simplicity we assume simple interest accrues each year on the loan balance and is added to the amount you owe when repayment begins. Borrowing \$10,000 at 6% accrues $0.06 \times 10{,}000 = \$600$ a year, or \$2,400 over four years, so you start repayment owing \$12,400. Paying interest during school keeps this from growing. A **subsidized** loan, available only to undergraduates with financial need, does not accrue interest while you are in school at least half time (the government pays it).

### Comparing plans

To compare two paths, add each year's net price over the whole plan. A common path is two years at a community college and then transfer to a university to finish a bachelor's degree. Check that credits transfer, because lost credits mean extra time and money. Also remember **opportunity cost**: an extra year in school is also a year of lost earnings.

This is general education; aid rules and figures change, so always use the school's own estimates and official sources.

@example
title: Two plans for a four-year degree
problem: Plan A is four years at a university with a cost of attendance of \$28,000 a year and grants and scholarships of \$9,000 a year. Plan B is two years at a community college (COA \$14,000, grants \$5,000 a year) and then two years at the same university (COA \$30,000, grants \$6,000 a year). Compare the total net price.
step: Plan A net price per year is $28{,}000 - 9{,}000 = \$19{,}000$. Over four years: $4 \times 19{,}000 = \$76{,}000$.
step: Plan B, years 1 and 2: $14{,}000 - 5{,}000 = \$9{,}000$ a year, so $2 \times 9{,}000 = \$18{,}000$. Years 3 and 4: $30{,}000 - 6{,}000 = \$24{,}000$ a year, so $2 \times 24{,}000 = \$48{,}000$.
step: Plan B total is $18{,}000 + 48{,}000 = \$66{,}000$, which is $76{,}000 - 66{,}000 = \$10{,}000$ less than Plan A (assuming all credits transfer and aid is as stated).
answer: Plan A \$76,000; Plan B \$66,000, so Plan B costs \$10,000 less.

@example
title: Paying back a loan after graduation
problem: A student borrows \$10,000 at 6% simple interest accrued over four years, with the accrued interest added to the balance when repayment starts. Repayment is level monthly payments at the end of each month over 10 years at a 6% APR. Find the starting balance and the monthly payment.
step: Accrued interest is $0.06 \times 10{,}000 \times 4 = \$2{,}400$, so the starting balance is \$12,400.
step: Monthly rate $i = 0.06/12 = 0.005$ and $n = 120$.
step: $M = 12{,}400 \times \dfrac{0.005}{1 - 1.005^{-120}} = \$137.67$. Total repaid is $137.67 \times 120 = \$16{,}519.85$ (unrounded payment), so interest in repayment is \$4,119.85.
answer: Starting balance \$12,400; payment \$137.67 a month.

@mistakes
- Comparing schools by sticker price instead of net price after grants and scholarships.
- Counting loans as aid you will not repay: only grants and scholarships are free money.
- Forgetting that interest accrues on unsubsidized loans while you are in school.
- Assuming all community college credits transfer; check with the receiving school.

@quiz num
prompt: A student borrows \$5,000 at the start of each of four school years, on an unsubsidized loan with 5% simple interest a year. No payments are made while in school, and graduation comes at the end of year 4. The interest on each \$5,000 accrues from the day it is borrowed until graduation. What is the total owed at graduation? Round to the nearest dollar.
answer: 22500
lead: \$
tol: 1
check: 4 * 5000 + 0.05 * 5000 * (4 + 3 + 2 + 1)
hint: The first \$5,000 accrues interest for 4 years, the next for 3, then 2, then 1.
solution: Principal is $4 \times 5{,}000 = \$20{,}000$. Interest is $0.05 \times 5{,}000 \times (4 + 3 + 2 + 1) = 250 \times 10 = \$2{,}500$. Total owed is \$22,500.
