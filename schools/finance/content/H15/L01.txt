@article
You get a job offer: "\$18.50 an hour." Then the first paycheck arrives and it is smaller than you expected. Where did the money go? To answer, you need to separate **gross pay** (everything you earned) from **net pay** (the "take-home" amount that lands in your account).

### Gross pay

An **hourly** worker earns the wage rate times the hours worked. Many hourly workers are covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, which requires **overtime** pay of at least 1.5 times the regular rate for hours above 40 in a workweek. A **salaried** worker earns a fixed amount per year, divided over the pay periods (26 for biweekly pay, 24 for twice-monthly pay, 12 for monthly pay). Assume a full-time year is 2,080 hours (40 hours times 52 weeks), so a \$52,000 salary is \$25 an hour.

### From gross to net

Several amounts are taken out of each paycheck:

- **Social Security tax**: 6.2% of wages, up to a yearly wage base (\$184,500 in 2026).
- **Medicare tax**: 1.45% of all wages. Together these two are the employee share of **FICA** taxes, 7.65% of wages for most workers. Your employer pays a matching 7.65% on top of your wages; you never see that part.
- **Federal income tax withholding**: an estimate of your income tax, based on the Form W-4 you give your employer. It is a prepayment, settled when you file a return (Week 4).
- **State and local taxes**, where they exist.
- **Voluntary deductions**: health insurance premiums, retirement contributions and so on.

$$\text{net pay} = \text{gross pay} - \text{taxes withheld} - \text{other deductions}.$$

A traditional (pre-tax) 401(k) contribution lowers the wages used to compute income tax withholding, but Social Security and Medicare tax are still charged on the full wage. (Tax rules change every year and differ by state: this is general education, not tax advice.)

### Benefits are pay too

Compare job offers by **total compensation**: salary plus the value of benefits. Common benefits are employer-paid health insurance premiums, paid time off, and a retirement **match**. In a match such as "100% of the first 4% of pay," the employer adds money to your 401(k) when you contribute. Contributing less than the matched percentage leaves free money unclaimed.

| Item | What it is |
|---|---|
| Gross pay | Earnings before anything is taken out |
| FICA | Social Security (6.2%) plus Medicare (1.45%) |
| Withholding | Prepayment of income tax |
| Match | Employer deposit into your retirement account |
| Net pay | What you actually receive |

@example
title: An hourly paycheck with overtime
problem: Maya earns \$18.50 an hour. One week she works 44 hours, and the first 40 are paid at the regular rate and the other 4 at 1.5 times. Federal withholding is \$62.00 and state withholding is \$25.00. Find her gross pay, FICA tax and net pay (assume no other deductions).
step: Regular pay: $40 \times 18.50 = \$740.00$. Overtime rate: $1.5 \times 18.50 = \$27.75$, so overtime pay is $4 \times 27.75 = \$111.00$.
step: Gross pay is $740.00 + 111.00 = \$851.00$.
step: FICA is $7.65\%$ of gross: $851.00 \times 0.0765 = 65.10$ (Social Security \$52.76 plus Medicare \$12.34, rounded to cents).
step: Net pay is $851.00 - 65.10 - 62.00 - 25.00 = \$698.90$.
answer: Gross \$851.00; FICA \$65.10; net pay \$698.90.

@example
title: Valuing an employer match
problem: Sam's salary is \$52,000. Sam's employer matches 50% of employee contributions up to 6% of pay. Sam contributes 6% of pay. How much goes into the 401(k) in a year, and how much of that is the employer's?
step: Sam's contribution is $0.06 \times 52{,}000 = \$3{,}120$.
step: The match is 50% of that amount: $0.50 \times 3{,}120 = \$1{,}560$. Contributing exactly 6% earns the full match.
step: The total deposited is $3{,}120 + 1{,}560 = \$4{,}680$, and one-third of it is the employer's contribution.
answer: \$4,680 in total, of which \$1,560 comes from the employer.

@mistakes
- Treating the hourly wage as the whole story: net pay is smaller than gross pay because of taxes and deductions.
- Applying the overtime rate to all hours instead of only the hours above 40.
- Thinking a pre-tax 401(k) contribution also avoids Social Security and Medicare tax; it does not.
- Comparing job offers by salary alone and ignoring benefits such as the employer's health premium and the retirement match.

answer: 1487
lead: \$
tol: 1

solution: The 401(k) is $0.05 \times 2{,}000 = \$100$. FICA is $0.0765 \times 2{,}000 = \$153$. Net pay is $2{,}000 - 100 - 153 - 190 - 70 = \$1{,}487$.
