@article
Most big purchases, a car, a degree or a home, are financed with an **installment loan**: you borrow a **principal** $P$, then repay it in equal monthly payments over a **term** of $n$ months at an annual rate. The lender's quoted rate is the APR, and we assume interest is charged monthly at $i = \text{APR}/12$ on the unpaid balance, with payments at the end of each month.

### The payment formula

The level payment that exactly pays off the loan is

$$M = P\,\frac{i}{1 - (1+i)^{-n}}.$$

For a \$22,000 car loan at 7% APR for 60 months, $i = 0.07/12 = 0.0058333$, and $M = \$435.63$. Total paid is $60 \times 435.63$ and **total interest** is total paid minus principal. Using the unrounded payment, total paid is \$26,137.58 and interest is \$4,137.58. (Real lenders round each payment to the cent, so their totals differ by a few cents.)

### Amortization

**Amortization** means paying off a loan gradually. In each month:

1. Interest = balance $\times\, i$.
2. Principal repaid = payment $-$ interest.
3. New balance = old balance $-$ principal repaid.

| Month | Starting balance | Interest | Principal | Ending balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | \$22,000.00 | \$128.33 | \$307.29 | \$21,692.71 |

Because interest is charged on the balance, early payments are mostly interest, and the principal share grows as the balance falls. Paying extra toward principal early saves the most interest.

### Term, rate and total cost

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest, since you owe money for longer. A lower rate reduces interest, but a long term can erase the benefit. Always compare loans by **total interest paid** (and by whether the payment fits your budget), not just by the payment.

### Student loans

Student loans are installment loans too. **Federal** student loans carry rates set by law and repayment options that differ from private loans; rules changed for loans first made on or after July 1, 2026, so check studentaid.gov for current terms. A **private** loan comes from a bank or other lender, with terms based on your credit. Whatever the loan, the same payment formula and the same logic apply.

@example
title: Comparing two car loan terms
problem: You borrow \$22,000 at 7% APR. Compare a 48-month loan with a 72-month loan.
step: Monthly rate $i = 0.07/12 = 0.0058333$.
step: 48 months: $M = 22{,}000 \times \dfrac{0.0058333}{1 - 1.0058333^{-48}} = \$526.82$. Total paid is $526.82 \times 48 = \$25{,}287.23$ (unrounded), so interest is \$3,287.23.
step: 72 months: $M = \$375.08$. Total paid is \$27,005.63, so interest is \$5,005.63.
step: The longer loan saves \$151.74 a month but costs $5{,}005.63 - 3{,}287.23 = \$1{,}718.40$ more in interest.
answer: 48 months: \$526.82 a month, \$3,287.23 interest; 72 months: \$375.08 a month, \$5,005.63 interest.

@example
title: Interest and principal in a student loan
problem: A student loan of \$30,000 has a 6.5% APR and a 10-year (120-month) term, with level end-of-month payments. Find the payment and the total interest.
step: $i = 0.065/12 = 0.0054167$ and $n = 120$.
step: $M = 30{,}000 \times \dfrac{0.0054167}{1 - 1.0054167^{-120}} = \$340.64$.
step: Total paid is $340.64 \times 120 = \$40{,}877.27$ (unrounded), so interest is $40{,}877.27 - 30{,}000 = \$10{,}877.27$.
answer: Payment \$340.64 a month; about \$10,877.27 total interest.

@mistakes
- Comparing loans by the monthly payment only: a lower payment from a longer term can cost thousands more in interest.
- Using the annual rate as the monthly rate: use APR/12 when payments are monthly.
- Thinking each payment splits evenly between interest and principal; early payments are mostly interest.
- Forgetting that fees, taxes and add-ons financed into the loan raise the principal and the interest.

model:
```python
def total_interest(principal, apr, months):
    """Total interest on a level-payment monthly loan."""
    if apr == 0:
        return 0.0
    i = apr / 12
    payment = principal * i / (1 - (1 + i) ** -months)
    return payment * months - principal
```
hint: First compute the monthly payment with $M = P\,i/(1-(1+i)^{-n})$.
hint: Total interest is the payment times the number of months minus the principal; handle a zero rate separately to avoid dividing by zero.
solution: Compute the level payment from the formula, multiply it by the number of months to get everything repaid, and subtract the principal. A zero rate makes the formula divide by zero, so return `0.0` for it directly.
