@article
A bond is worth the present value of its promised cash flows. The only question is the discount rate. A bond's **yield to maturity (YTM)** is the single discount rate that makes the present value of its remaining cash flows equal to its market price. It is an internal rate of return, and it is only a promised return: it assumes the bond is held to maturity, no default, and reinvestment of every coupon at the same yield.

**Conventions used throughout this lesson.** Prices are per 100 of par unless stated. Coupons are paid semiannually and yields are **bond-equivalent yields (BEY)**: the periodic rate $y/2$ times 2, quoted as an annual rate with semiannual compounding. Valuation dates are coupon dates unless stated.

### The pricing formula

For a bond with par $F$, annual coupon rate $c$, $m$ payments a year and $N$ periods remaining, the coupon per period is $C = cF/m$, the periodic yield is $i = y/m$, and

$$P = \sum_{t=1}^{N} \frac{C}{(1+i)^t} + \frac{F}{(1+i)^N} = C\,\frac{1-(1+i)^{-N}}{i} + \frac{F}{(1+i)^N}.$$

Three rules follow. (1) Price and yield move in **opposite** directions. (2) If coupon rate $>$ yield, the bond sells at a **premium** (above par); if coupon rate $<$ yield, at a **discount**; if equal, at par. (3) **Pull to par**: if the yield stays constant, the price of a premium or discount bond moves toward par as maturity approaches. The price-yield relationship is **convex**: a fall in yield raises price by more than an equal rise in yield lowers it.

### Between coupon dates

When a fraction $w$ of a coupon period remains until the next coupon, the **full price** discounts every cash flow back by $w$ periods plus whole periods:

$$P_{\text{full}} = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \frac{C}{(1+i)^{w+k}} + \frac{F}{(1+i)^{w+N-1}}, \qquad P_{\text{flat}} = P_{\text{full}} - AI,$$

where $N$ is the number of coupons remaining and the accrued interest is $AI = C(1-w)$ (for a bond whose period is measured by the same day count). The flat price is what dealers quote.

### Yield measures

- **Current yield** $= $ annual coupon $/$ price. It ignores gains or losses at maturity.
- **YTM (BEY)** $= 2 \times$ the periodic yield. The **effective annual yield** is $(1 + y/2)^2 - 1$, which is higher than the BEY.
- **Discount-rate quotes for bills.** A Treasury bill pays 100 at maturity and is quoted on a **bank discount yield** $d = \frac{360}{t}\cdot\frac{100-P}{100}$, where $t$ is days to maturity. The price is $P = 100(1 - d\,t/360)$. The discount yield is not a true return: it divides by par rather than by price and uses a 360-day year. The bill's **bond-equivalent yield** is $\frac{365}{t}\cdot\frac{100-P}{P}$ for bills of 182 days or less.

There is no closed-form solution for a coupon bond's YTM, so spreadsheets and code solve it numerically (bisection or Newton's method). Calculators use the same idea: guess, price, adjust.

@example
title: Pricing a 10-year bond
problem: A 10-year bond pays a 5% coupon semiannually, with par 100. Find its price at a YTM of 6% (BEY), and at 4%.
step: There are $N = 20$ periods, a coupon $C = 2.50$ and a periodic yield $i = 0.03$.
step: The present value of the coupons is $2.50 \times \frac{1 - 1.03^{-20}}{0.03} = 2.50 \times 14.8775 = 37.1937$.
step: The present value of par is $100/1.03^{20} = 55.3676$, so $P = 37.1937 + 55.3676 = 92.5613$: a discount, because the coupon rate is below the yield.
step: At 4%, $i = 0.02$ and $P = 2.50 \times 16.3514 + 100/1.02^{20} = 40.8785 + 67.2971 = 108.1757$, a premium.
answer: 92.5613 at 6%; 108.1757 at 4%.

@example
title: Solving for a yield, and pricing between coupons
problem: (a) A 7-year, 4.5% semiannual bond has a price of 95.50. Find its YTM. (b) A 3-year, 6% semiannual bond has 120 of 180 days left until the next coupon (30/360). Find its flat price at a YTM of 5%.
step: (a) Try $y = 5.25\%$: the price is 95.6536, which is too high. Try $y = 5.30\%$: the price is 95.3719. The target 95.50 lies 54.5% of the way from the first to the second, so $y \approx 5.25\% + 0.545 \times 0.05\% = 5.28\%$ (BEY).
step: (b) There are 6 coupons of 3.00 left, $w = 120/180 = 2/3$ and $i = 0.025$. Discounting each cash flow gives a full price of 103.6033.
step: Accrued interest is $3.00 \times (1 - 2/3) = 1.00$, so the flat price is $103.6033 - 1.00 = 102.6033$.
answer: (a) about 5.28% BEY. (b) 102.60 flat (103.60 full).

@mistakes
- Using the annual yield as the periodic yield: for a semiannual bond use $y/2$ and $2T$ periods.
- Quoting the periodic yield (such as 2.64%) as if it were the annual BEY, or forgetting that the BEY understates the effective annual yield.
- Believing a bond's price is fixed by its coupon: price depends on the market yield and moves inversely to it.
- Treating the bill discount yield as a return on the amount invested: it is computed on par over a 360-day year.

model:
```python
def bond_price(par, coupon_rate, ytm, years, freq=2):
    """Price of a fixed-rate bond on a coupon date."""
    periods = round(years * freq)
    coupon = par * coupon_rate / freq
    i = ytm / freq
    value = sum(coupon / (1 + i) ** t for t in range(1, periods + 1))
    return value + par / (1 + i) ** periods
```
hint: There are `years * freq` periods; the periodic yield is `ytm / freq`.
hint: Discount each coupon by its period number and add the discounted par.
solution: Sum the discounted coupons for periods 1 to N, then add par discounted N periods; using the periodic yield `ytm / freq` and periodic coupon `par * coupon_rate / freq` handles any frequency, a zero-coupon bond and a zero yield.

answer: 4.24
unit: %
tol: 0.01
check: 365 / 182 * (100 - 100 * (1 - 0.041 * 182 / 360)) / (100 * (1 - 0.041 * 182 / 360)) * 100
hint: First find the price per 100: $P = 100(1 - d\,t/360)$.
hint: With $P = 97.9272$, the BEY is $\frac{365}{182} \times \frac{100 - P}{P}$.
solution: $P = 100(1 - 0.041 \times 182/360) = 97.9272$. Then BEY $= \frac{365}{182} \times \frac{2.0728}{97.9272} = 4.24\%$, higher than the discount yield because it divides by price and uses 365 days.
