@article
Everything so far (prices, yields, durations, spreads, options, prepayments) is a tool. A **portfolio strategy** says how to combine the tools to meet an objective. The objective may be to beat an index, to fund liabilities or to earn a return from a particular view. This lesson is general education, not investment advice.

### Passive and active strategies

**Indexing** aims to track a bond index. A full replication of a large index is impractical (it holds thousands of illiquid bonds), so managers use **stratified sampling**: divide the index into cells by duration, sector, credit quality and coupon, then hold a few bonds in each so the portfolio matches the index's cell weights, duration and convexity. The risk is **tracking error**: the standard deviation of the portfolio's return minus the index's return.

**Active** managers take positions relative to the benchmark:

- **Duration positioning**: longer duration than the index if rates are expected to fall.
- **Yield-curve positioning**: a **bullet** (maturities clustered at one point), a **barbell** (short and long bonds) or a **ladder** (equal amounts maturing at regular intervals). With equal duration, the barbell has more convexity and a bullet has less, so they differ in the effect of slope and curvature changes. **Key rate durations** measure sensitivity to the yield at individual maturities, which shows exposure to non-parallel shifts.
- **Credit and sector selection**: overweighting issuers or sectors whose spreads are expected to tighten.
- **Carry and rolldown**: earning the coupon and the price gain from a bond rolling down an upward-sloping curve. **Riding the yield curve** buys a longer bond than the horizon and sells it before maturity.

### Liability-driven investing

An institution with liabilities (a pension fund, an insurer) cares about the **surplus** (assets minus the present value of liabilities) more than the assets alone. Strategies:

- **Cash-flow matching (dedication)**: buy bonds whose cash flows (coupons and principal) match each liability in amount and date. It removes interest-rate risk if held to maturity, but is costly and constrained by what is available.
- **Immunization**: match present value and duration (and keep dispersion at least as high) so a parallel yield shift leaves assets sufficient. It needs periodic rebalancing.
- **Contingent immunization**: pursue an active strategy while the portfolio's value stays above a floor (the amount needed to immunize at the current rate); if the portfolio falls to the floor, switch to full immunization.

### Hedging with DV01 and leverage

To neutralize a position's rate risk, take an offsetting position in an instrument with the same DV01:

$$\text{hedge face} = \frac{\text{position DV01}}{\text{hedge DV01 per unit of face}}, \qquad \text{DV01 per unit of face} = \frac{P_{\text{hedge}}}{100}\,D_{\text{Mod}}\,(0.0001).$$

This hedges parallel shifts only. **Leverage** funds extra bonds with borrowing, often **repo**. If equity is $E$, borrowing is $B$, the asset return is $r_A$ and the cost of borrowing is $r_B$:

$$r_E = r_A + \frac{B}{E}\,(r_A - r_B).$$

Leverage multiplies both gains and losses, and the duration of equity rises: with assets of 300 at duration 5 funded by 100 of equity and 200 of short-term borrowing (duration about 0), the equity's duration is $300 \times 5/100 = 15$.

### Mortgage securities in portfolios

An MBS offers a yield above Treasuries, but its prepayment risk means its effective duration shortens when rates fall and lengthens when rates rise (negative convexity). A manager with a duration target measures it with effective duration under prepayment models, and projects pool cash flows as in the last lesson.

@example
title: Cash-flow matching and a DV01 hedge
problem: (a) A trust owes \$2 million in 1 year, \$3 million in 2 years and \$5 million in 3 years. Annual spot (zero-coupon) yields are 3.0%, 3.5% and 4.0%. What does it cost to match the liabilities exactly with zero-coupon bonds? (b) A \$50 million portfolio has a modified duration of 6.2. A hedge bond trades at 98.50 per 100 of par with a modified duration of 9.1. How much face value must be sold short to neutralize its DV01?
step: (a) Cost $= 2/1.03 + 3/1.035^2 + 5/1.04^3 = 1.94175 + 2.80053 + 4.44498 = 9.18726$ million dollars.
step: (b) The portfolio's DV01 is $\$50{,}000{,}000 \times 6.2 \times 0.0001 = \$31{,}000$.
step: The hedge bond's DV01 per \$1 of face is $(98.50/100) \times 9.1 \times 0.0001 = \$0.00089635$.
step: Face to sell short: $31{,}000/0.00089635 = \$34{,}584{,}705$, about \$34.58 million.
answer: (a) \$9.187 million; (b) about \$34.58 million of face value.

@example
title: Leverage and riding the yield curve
problem: (a) A fund has \$100 million of equity and borrows \$200 million in repo at 4%, investing all \$300 million in bonds that earn 6%. Find the return on equity. (b) A 5-year, 4% annual-coupon bond is bought at a yield of 3.8% and sold one year later when it is a 4-year bond at a yield of 3.6% (the curve is unchanged). Find the holding-period return.
step: (a) $r_E = 6\% + (200/100)(6\% - 4\%) = 10\%$. If the bonds earned only 3%, the return would be $3\% + 2(3\% - 4\%) = 1\%$: leverage magnifies losses too.
step: (b) The purchase price is $4/1.038 + 4/1.038^2 + 4/1.038^3 + 4/1.038^4 + 104/1.038^5 = 100.8954$.
step: The sale price is $4/1.036 + 4/1.036^2 + 4/1.036^3 + 104/1.036^4 = 101.4658$, and the first coupon of 4 is received.
step: The return is $(101.4658 + 4)/100.8954 - 1 = 4.53\%$, more than the 3.8% initial yield because the bond rolled down the curve.
answer: (a) 10%; (b) 4.53%.

@mistakes
- Hedging with equal face values instead of equal DV01: bonds with different durations and prices need different amounts.
- Treating cash-flow matching as free of reinvestment and default risk, or immunization as a one-time fix.
- Ignoring that leverage also raises the equity's duration and its losses.
- Assuming a duration hedge removes all rate risk: it covers parallel shifts only.

model:
```python
def hedge_face(position_value, position_duration, hedge_price, hedge_duration):
    """Face value of a hedge bond with the same DV01 as the position."""
    position_dv01 = position_value * position_duration * 0.0001
    hedge_dv01_per_face = hedge_price / 100 * hedge_duration * 0.0001
    return position_dv01 / hedge_dv01_per_face
```
hint: The position's DV01 is value times duration times 0.0001.
hint: The hedge's DV01 per dollar of face is its price over 100 times its duration times 0.0001.
solution: Matching DV01s means face times the hedge's DV01 per unit of face equals the position's DV01, so face is the ratio of the two. If the hedge has the same price and duration as the position, the face equals the position's value.

@quiz self
prompt: A pension fund is overfunded: its assets are 8% above the present value of its liabilities. Its trustees want to pursue a higher return with active duration and credit positions but cannot accept a shortfall. In four or five sentences, describe how contingent immunization could satisfy both goals, and name two risks that remain.
hint: What is the floor, and what happens when the portfolio value reaches it?
solution: The fund can run an active strategy while its assets stay above a floor, the amount needed to immunize the liabilities at the prevailing immunization rate. The cushion is the excess of assets over that floor. As long as the cushion is positive, the manager may take active positions. If losses reduce the assets to the floor, the manager switches the whole portfolio to a classical immunization (or cash-flow match) that locks in the liabilities' funding. Risks that remain include a rapid loss that gaps below the floor before the switch, non-parallel curve shifts and credit losses, and the cost of rebalancing.
rubric: Defines the floor (the value needed to immunize the liabilities at current rates) and the cushion above it.
rubric: Explains that active management continues while assets exceed the floor and the portfolio switches to immunization when assets reach it.
rubric: Names two remaining risks, such as gap risk, non-parallel yield curve shifts or credit risk.
