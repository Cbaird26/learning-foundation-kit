@article
Day and night, the seasons, the Moon's changing shape and eclipses all come from three motions: **Earth spins**, **Earth orbits the Sun** and **the Moon orbits Earth**.

### Rotation and revolution

**Rotation** is spinning on an axis. Earth rotates once every 24 hours, which gives day and night. That is $360^\circ$ in 24 hours, or $15^\circ$ per hour, so places $15^\circ$ of longitude apart differ by one hour of local solar time. **Revolution** is orbiting. Earth revolves around the Sun once in about 365.25 days (a year).

### Seasons

Earth's axis is tilted about **23.4°** from the vertical and keeps pointing the same way as Earth orbits. When the Northern Hemisphere tilts toward the Sun, it gets more direct sunlight and longer days, so it is summer there (and winter in the Southern Hemisphere). Seasons are **not** caused by distance from the Sun: Earth is actually closest to the Sun in early January. The **solstices** (about June 21 and December 21) are the longest and shortest days; at the **equinoxes** (about March 20 and September 22) day and night are nearly equal.

### Phases of the Moon

The Moon makes no light of its own; we see sunlight reflected from it. Half of the Moon is always lit by the Sun. As the Moon orbits Earth, we see different amounts of the lit half. The cycle is: **new moon**, waxing crescent, **first quarter**, waxing gibbous, **full moon**, waning gibbous, **third quarter**, waning crescent, and back to new. One cycle takes about **29.5 days**. The Moon takes about 27.3 days to orbit Earth once, but Earth also moves along its orbit, so it takes an extra two days to line up with the Sun again. The Moon rotates once per orbit, so the same face always points toward Earth.

### Eclipses

A **solar eclipse** happens at new moon when the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth and its shadow falls on Earth. A **lunar eclipse** happens at full moon when Earth is between the Sun and the Moon and Earth's shadow falls on the Moon. We do not see eclipses every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted about 5° compared with Earth's orbit, so the three bodies usually do not line up exactly.

### Tides

Tides are caused mainly by the Moon's gravity (and partly the Sun's). Most coasts have two high tides and two low tides each day. **Spring tides** (the biggest range) occur at new and full moon, when the Sun, Moon and Earth line up. **Neap tides** (the smallest range) occur at the quarter moons.

### Modeling at home

In a dim room, a flashlight (or lamp) stands for the Sun, a ball for the Moon and your head for Earth. Hold the ball at arm's length at different positions around you and watch the lit part change. Move it between you and the light, or into your own shadow, to see how eclipses happen.

@example
title: Rotation and clock time
problem: Two cities are $75^\circ$ apart in longitude. How many hours apart are their local solar times?
step: Earth turns $360^\circ$ in $24$ hours, which is $\frac{360}{24} = 15^\circ$ per hour.
step: Divide the longitude difference by that rate: $\frac{75}{15} = 5$.
step: The local solar times differ by $5$ hours, and the city to the east is ahead.

@example
title: Time from full moon to the next first quarter
problem: The cycle of phases takes about 29.5 days. About how many days pass from a full moon to the next first quarter moon?
step: From full moon the cycle continues through third quarter, new moon and then first quarter. Full moon to full moon is the whole cycle.
step: First quarter is one quarter of a cycle after new moon, and new moon is half a cycle after full moon, so the total is $\frac{3}{4}$ of a cycle.
step: Compute $\frac{3}{4} \times 29.5 \approx 22.1$ days.

@mistakes
- Saying seasons are caused by Earth being closer to or farther from the Sun. The cause is the tilt of Earth's axis.
- Thinking moon phases are caused by Earth's shadow. Phases come from our viewing angle of the Moon's lit half; Earth's shadow causes lunar eclipses only.
- Believing there is a "dark side" of the Moon that never gets sunlight. It is the far side, and it gets as much sunlight as the near side.
- Forgetting that solar eclipses occur at new moon, not full moon.

answer: 3

unit: h

solution: $360 \div 24 = 15^\circ$ per hour, so $\frac{45}{15} = 3$ hours.

@practice self
prompt: Plan and describe a model of lunar phases and eclipses with a flashlight, a ball and a dim room. Say what each object stands for, where the ball is for new moon, first quarter, full moon and third quarter, and what you would do to model a solar eclipse and a lunar eclipse. Explain why eclipses do not happen every month.
hint: Put the light in one place, yourself in the middle, and move the ball around you. Note where the lit half faces.
solution: The flashlight is the Sun, the ball is the Moon and your head is Earth. For new moon hold the ball between you and the light (the lit half faces away from you). For first quarter move a quarter of the way around so you see half of the lit half. For full moon hold the ball on the far side of you from the light, so the lit half faces you (raise it slightly so your head does not block the light). For third quarter move three quarters of the way around. To model a solar eclipse, put the ball exactly in line between the light and your eyes, so it shadows you. For a lunar eclipse put the ball directly behind your head, so that your head's shadow falls on it. Eclipses are not monthly because the Moon's orbit is tilted about 5 degrees to Earth's orbit, so the Moon usually passes above or below the line from the Sun to Earth.
