@article
Ethical and Professional Standards is a topic that appears at all three levels, and at Level II it changes shape. You are no longer asked "what does Standard III(B) require?" You are given a short story, usually a paragraph or two, and asked "which Standard did the analyst violate?" or "what should the analyst do?" A candidate who has memorized the Standards but never practiced vignettes loses points on exactly these questions. **Exam facts change every year** (the number of item sets, the weights, the timing), so check cfainstitute.org before you plan; what does not change is the method below.

### The structure you are applying

The Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct has seven Standards. Learn them as a map, because the exam answer choices will name them:

| Standard | Covers |
|---|---|
| I Professionalism | Knowledge of the Law; Independence and Objectivity; Misrepresentation; Misconduct; Competence |
| II Integrity of Capital Markets | Material Nonpublic Information; Market Manipulation |
| III Duties to Clients | Loyalty, Prudence, and Care; Fair Dealing; Suitability; Performance Presentation; Preservation of Confidentiality |
| IV Duties to Employers | Loyalty; Additional Compensation Arrangements; Responsibilities of Supervisors |
| V Investment Analysis, Recommendations, and Actions | Diligence and Reasonable Basis; Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients; Record Retention |
| VI Conflicts of Interest | Disclosure of Conflicts; Priority of Transactions; Referral Fees |
| VII Responsibilities as a CFA Institute Member or CFA Candidate | Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs; Reference to CFA Institute, the CFA Designation, and the CFA Program |

The Standards were last revised effective January 1, 2024 (Standards of Practice Handbook, 12th edition); the next change would be announced by CFA Institute.

### A four-step method for vignettes

1. **Read the question first.** Is it asking for a violation, for the action to take, or for whether a described action is permitted? The same facts can support different questions.
2. **Mark the facts that carry the ethics.** Who knows what, who benefits, what was disclosed, who got the information first, whose money it is.
3. **Name the Standard before you look at the choices.** Then match. Exam choices often pair the right action with the wrong Standard, or the right Standard with an inadequate action.
4. **Prefer the action that fixes the problem and protects clients and markets.** The recommended action is rarely "do nothing," rarely "quit," and rarely "tell the press." It is usually: stop the conduct, escalate to a supervisor or compliance, disclose, and document.

### Look-alike Standards

Most errors come from confusing neighbors. **Material nonpublic information** (II(A)) is about information that is both nonpublic and likely to affect the price; mosaic-theory work, which combines public and immaterial nonpublic pieces, is allowed. **Fair dealing** (III(B)) is about treating clients fairly when disseminating recommendations or allocating trades, not about giving everyone identical results. **Priority of transactions** (VI(B)) puts client trades ahead of employer and personal trades. **Misrepresentation** (I(C)) covers plagiarism and false statements; **Diligence and Reasonable Basis** (V(A)) covers the quality of the work behind a recommendation. **Loyalty to employers** (IV(A)) forbids taking the employer's property, such as client lists, to a new job, but permits ordinary preparation to compete after leaving.

A useful instinct: where the Code and local law differ, follow the stricter one (Standard I(A), Knowledge of the Law). Where an action is legal but harms clients, the Code can still be violated.

### Time and scoring

Ethics vignettes are short, so you can win time here for the harder valuation sets. A good target is to read, mark and answer in about two minutes; if you cannot name a Standard, eliminate choices that are clearly wrong (an action that benefits the analyst, or one that conceals information) and move on. All questions are multiple choice with three choices, so a guess is worth taking.

@example
title: Name the Standard, then the action
problem: Dara Okafor, a portfolio manager at Bellwether Asset Management, learns at a dinner that a friend who is a senior executive at a listed supplier has just seen internal figures showing a large earnings shortfall that will be announced next week. The friend says it is not public. Dara manages several client accounts that hold the supplier. Which Standard is involved, and what should Dara do?
step: Mark the facts. The information comes from an insider, it is nonpublic ("not public"), and a large earnings shortfall is likely to move the price, so it is material.
step: Name the Standard. Acting on material nonpublic information is covered by Standard II(A), Material Nonpublic Information.
step: Decide the action. Dara should not trade, or cause others to trade, on the information, including by quietly selling client holdings. Dara should tell compliance so the security can be placed on a restricted or watch list, and should encourage no further disclosure of the information.
step: Check the tempting wrong choices: selling only client positions to "protect" clients still uses the information; waiting a day does not make it public.
answer: Standard II(A); refrain from trading and notify compliance.

@example
title: A neighbor Standard
problem: An analyst at Redwood Capital publishes a report, and one section reproduces three paragraphs and a chart from a consultant's paper without crediting the consultant. Which Standard applies?
step: The issue is presenting another party's work as your own, not the quality of the analysis or a conflict of interest.
step: Passing off others' work is plagiarism, which falls under Standard I(C), Misrepresentation.
step: V(A), Diligence and Reasonable Basis, is a neighbor, but it concerns whether the recommendation has a reasonable basis. The right fix here is attribution.
answer: Standard I(C), Misrepresentation.

@mistakes
- Answering with the Standard you know best instead of the one the facts point to; always map the facts first.
- Choosing an action that helps the client but still uses material nonpublic information, or that hides a conflict instead of disclosing it.
- Treating "disclosure" as a cure-all: disclosure fixes some conflicts (VI(A)) but never makes trading on material nonpublic information acceptable.
- Forgetting that the stricter of the Code and local law governs (I(A)).

@practice mc
prompt: Maya Ortiz is a research analyst at Sandpiper Securities. On Monday she buys shares of a small-cap company for her personal account. On Tuesday her firm publishes her new buy recommendation on the company, which she had drafted last week. Which Standard is most likely violated?
choice*: VI(B) Priority of Transactions
choice: V(C) Record Retention
choice: I(E) Competence
hint: Think about whose trade should come first when the firm is about to publish a recommendation.
solution: Maya traded for herself before clients and the market could act on a recommendation she knew was coming, which is a violation of Standard VI(B), Priority of Transactions. Record retention and competence are not at issue in these facts.

@practice mc
prompt: Hollis Ng is head of equity research at Marlowe Partners. He changes the firm's rating on a stock from buy to sell. Before the written note is distributed to all clients, an assistant telephones the firm's five largest institutional clients with the news. Which Standard has the firm most clearly violated?
choice: III(C) Suitability
choice*: III(B) Fair Dealing
choice: V(B) Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients
hint: The question is about who received a change in recommendation first.
solution: Giving some clients a changed recommendation before others violates Standard III(B), Fair Dealing, which requires firms to disseminate recommendations fairly. Suitability concerns matching investments to a client's profile, and Standard V(B) concerns how analysis is presented, neither of which is the issue here.
@practice mc
prompt: Elena Voss, a financial planner, refers clients to a mortgage broker. The broker pays Elena \$500 for each client who completes a loan. Elena has not told her clients or her employer about the payments. Which Standard is most directly violated?
choice: IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements
choice*: VI(C) Referral Fees
choice: III(D) Performance Presentation
hint: Look for the Standard that is specifically about payments for referring clients to a third party.
solution: Standard VI(C), Referral Fees, requires disclosure to employers, clients and prospects of compensation received for recommending services. IV(B) deals with outside compensation for services that compete with the employer's interests, which is a nearby Standard, but VI(C) is the specific one for referral payments.

@practice mc
prompt: Tobias Lindqvist, a candidate at Carrow Advisers, is preparing a client presentation. His valuation is based on a sell-side analyst's earnings forecast, which he copies into his own model without reading how it was built and describes in the presentation as "my own forecast." Which Standard is most directly violated?
choice: II(B) Market Manipulation
choice*: I(C) Misrepresentation
choice: III(E) Preservation of Confidentiality
hint: The presentation attributes someone else's work to Tobias.
solution: Presenting another person's forecast as your own work is a misrepresentation under Standard I(C). Tobias also risks V(A), Diligence and Reasonable Basis, because he did not understand the forecast, but I(C) is the most direct violation of the facts as stated.

@practice mc
prompt: Priya Nair is chief compliance officer at a small asset manager. Several portfolio managers have been trading ahead of client orders, and Priya has learned that the firm has no written trading or supervision procedures and has never monitored personal trading. Priya has lectured the staff about honesty, but nothing has changed. What should Priya do to best meet the Standards?
choice: Rely on the lecture, because each portfolio manager is individually responsible for compliance
choice*: Adopt written compliance procedures, begin monitoring trades, and take action against the violators, as Standard IV(C) requires of supervisors
choice: Report the managers to the press, because internal action is not enough under Standard IV(C)
hint: A supervisor must make reasonable efforts to detect and prevent violations.
solution: Standard IV(C), Responsibilities of Supervisors, requires reasonable procedures and systems to prevent and detect violations, followed by action when violations are found. Lectures alone are not enough, and going to the press is not the first step; the supervisor's duty is to fix the system and discipline violators, with escalation to senior management or regulators only where needed.

@quiz mc
prompt: Sana Rahim, a portfolio manager at Quillon Capital, plans to leave and start her own firm. Before resigning she copies the firm's client list, which she did not create, to a personal drive, and she emails clients from the list the day after she leaves. She did not take any records of clients' investment positions. Which Standard is most likely violated?
choice: V(C) Record Retention
choice*: IV(A) Loyalty
choice: VI(A) Disclosure of Conflicts
hint: Think about what a departing employee may and may not take from an employer.
solution: Copying the employer's client list and using it to solicit clients violates Standard IV(A), Loyalty. Leaving to compete is permitted, and so is contacting clients from memory or from public information after departure, but the firm's client records belong to the employer.

@quiz mc
prompt: Amit Rao passed the Level II CFA exam in August and had not yet taken Level III. His new business card reads "Amit Rao, CFA Level II" and his email signature reads "Amit Rao, CFA candidate." Which statement is most accurate?
choice*: The business card violates Standard VII(B), because passing a level does not entitle him to use the CFA designation in a title; he may state that he passed the Level II exam and is a candidate in the program
choice: The email signature violates Standard VII(B), because a candidate may never mention the CFA Program
choice: Neither item violates Standard VII(B), because candidates may use the designation in a title once they pass any level
hint: Candidates may refer to the program accurately but may not imply they hold the designation.
solution: "CFA Level II" implies a partial designation and violates Standard VII(B), Reference to CFA Institute, the CFA Designation, and the CFA Program. A candidate may truthfully say that he passed the Level II exam or is a candidate in the program (provided his candidacy is current), but may not claim partial designation.
