## Key ideas week by week

**Week 1, ethics.** Map facts to a Standard first, then choose the action that stops the conduct, escalates and discloses. The stricter of the law and the Code governs.

**Week 2, quantitative methods.** t-test $= (\hat b - H_0)/s$; $F = \text{MSR}/\text{MSE}$; adjusted $R^2 = 1 - \frac{n-1}{n-k-1}(1 - R^2)$. Heteroskedasticity and serial correlation distort standard errors; multicollinearity inflates them. AR(1): mean-reverting level $b_0/(1 - b_1)$; unit root means first-difference.

**Week 3, economics.** $F = S(1 + i_P t)/(1 + i_B t)$; the high-rate currency trades at a forward discount; growth accounting and Solow level versus growth effects.

**Week 4, FSA I.** Pick the method by influence; equity method (cost plus share of income less dividends); goodwill; IFRS pension cost is service cost plus net interest.

**Week 5, FSA II.** Current rate method sends the translation gain to OCI; the temporal method sends it to net income; accruals ratios flag low quality.

**Week 6, corporate issuers.** Accretion depends on P/E; WACC uses market weights and an after-tax cost of debt; residual dividends; poison pill, white knight, greenmail.

**Week 7, DDM and free cash flow.** Gordon growth uses $D_1$; FCFF at WACC less debt; FCFE at the cost of equity.

**Week 8, multiples and residual income.** Justified P/E $= p/(r - g)$; justified P/B $= (\text{ROE} - g)/(r - g)$; $V_0 = B_0 + RI_1/(r - g)$.

**Week 9, fixed income.** Forward rates from spot rates; callable $=$ straight minus call; OAS $=$ Z-spread minus option cost; effective duration $(V_- - V_+)/(2V_0\Delta y)$.

**Week 10, derivatives.** Forward $= (S_0 - PV(\text{income}))(1 + r)^T$; swap rate $= (1 - Z_n)/\sum Z_i$; put-call parity; risk-neutral binomial; delta hedge.

**Week 11, alternatives and portfolios.** Value $= NOI/\text{cap rate}$; TVPI and DPI; fund fees; $IR \approx IC\sqrt{BR}\,TC$.

**Week 12, practice.** Pace, flag, eliminate by direction, and log errors by type.

## Common errors

Using $D_0$ for $D_1$; discounting FCFE at WACC; adding the call to a callable bond; calling Durbin-Watson below 2 negative correlation; putting a translation gain in net income under the current rate method; ignoring the base and price currency of a quote.

This year the subsidiary's revenue grew 6% in euros, and the euro depreciated 9% against the dollar on average and over the year, while the subsidiary kept positive net assets and paid no dividends. Which statement is most accurate under the current rate method?
choice*: The subsidiary's revenue in dollars fell about 3.5%, and a negative cumulative translation adjustment is reported in other comprehensive income
choice: The subsidiary's revenue in dollars rose 6%, and a translation gain is reported in net income
choice: The subsidiary's revenue in dollars fell about 3.5%, and a remeasurement loss is reported in net income
hint: Multiply the growth factor by one minus the depreciation, and recall where translation adjustments go.
solution: Dollar revenue changed by $1.06 \times 0.91 - 1 = -3.5\%$. Under the current rate method with the local currency as the functional currency, the weaker euro reduces the dollar value of positive net assets, giving a negative cumulative translation adjustment in OCI, not a remeasurement loss in net income.

@exam self
prompt: Quillfeather Inc. trades at \$48. It just paid a dividend of \$2.00, which analysts expect to grow 8% a year for three years and then 4% a year forever. The required return is 9%. (a) Compute the two-stage dividend discount value. (b) In four or five sentences, state what the result implies about the price, and explain which assumption the valuation is most sensitive to, showing the effect of reducing the long-run growth rate to 3%.
hint: Compute $D_1$ to $D_3$, the terminal value at year 3, and discount.
solution: (a) $D_1 = 2.16$, $D_2 = 2.3328$, $D_3 = 2.5194$; $V_3 = 2.5194 \times 1.04/(0.09 - 0.04) = 52.40$. The value is $2.16/1.09 + 2.3328/1.09^2 + 2.5194/1.09^3 + 52.40/1.09^3 = \$46.36$. (b) The model value of about \$46.36 is about 3% below the \$48 price, so the stock looks slightly overvalued on these assumptions, but the gap is small relative to the uncertainty in the inputs. The value is most sensitive to the long-run growth rate because the terminal value is more than 80% of the total: lowering the long-run growth to 3% reduces the value to about \$39.29, a 15% fall. The conclusion therefore depends on whether the market's price embeds a higher long-run growth or lower required return than assumed.
