@article
Equity valuation is the center of Level II, and the weights of the topic areas change each year, so check cfainstitute.org. Most equity vignettes follow one pattern: describe a company in a few sentences, give some forecasts and a required return, and ask for a value, a model choice, or the effect of changing an assumption. Learn the models by what they discount and at what rate.

### The dividend discount model

The value of a share is the present value of expected future dividends at the cost of equity $r$. For a stable firm growing at a constant rate $g < r$, the **Gordon growth model** is

$$V_0 = \frac{D_1}{r - g} = \frac{D_0(1 + g)}{r - g}.$$

Use it when the firm pays dividends that are a reasonable proxy for cash flow, and growth is stable and below the growth of the economy. It is very sensitive to $r - g$. A **sustainable growth rate** is $g = b \times \text{ROE}$, where $b$ is the retention ratio ($1 -$ payout).

A **multistage** model handles growth that fades: forecast dividends explicitly during the high-growth stage, then add a terminal value from the Gordon growth model at the end of that stage:

$$V_0 = \sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{D_t}{(1+r)^t} + \frac{D_{n+1}/(r - g_L)}{(1+r)^n}.$$

The **H-model** approximates a linearly declining growth rate. Exam traps: using $D_0$ where the model needs $D_1$, and applying the terminal value formula with the wrong year's dividend.

### Free cash flow models

When dividends do not reflect cash generation (no dividend, or payout far from capacity), value free cash flow. **Free cash flow to the firm (FCFF)** is the cash available to all capital providers:

$$\text{FCFF} = NI + NCC + \text{Int}(1 - t) - FCInv - WCInv,$$

where $NCC$ is non-cash charges such as depreciation, $FCInv$ is fixed capital investment (net of asset sales) and $WCInv$ is the increase in net working capital. Equivalently $\text{FCFF} = CFO + \text{Int}(1-t) - FCInv$ under US GAAP, where interest is in operating cash flow. **Free cash flow to equity (FCFE)** is the cash available to shareholders:

$$\text{FCFE} = CFO - FCInv + \text{Net borrowing} = \text{FCFF} - \text{Int}(1-t) + \text{Net borrowing}.$$

Match discount rate to cash flow: **discount FCFF at the WACC**, giving firm value; **subtract the market value of debt** (and preferred stock and minority interest where relevant, and add any cash not already counted) to reach equity value. **Discount FCFE at the cost of equity**, giving equity value directly. A common error is discounting FCFE at the WACC. Use FCFF when leverage is changing or negative FCFE would be hard to forecast; use FCFE when leverage is stable.

### Private companies

A private company has no market price, so the analyst applies the same models and then adjusts. The **discount for lack of marketability** reflects that the shares cannot be sold quickly; the **discount for lack of control** applies to a minority stake that cannot direct policy; a **control premium** applies to a controlling stake. The required return is often built up from public-market data plus a size and company-specific premium, and the choice of public comparables is a major source of judgment.

### Practical steps for vignettes

1. Identify the cash flow (dividends, FCFE, FCFF) and the matching rate.
2. Check the timing: is the first forecast $D_1$, or is the vignette giving $D_0$?
3. Compute the terminal value at the end of the explicit stage and discount it with the same factor as that year's cash flow.
4. Reread the question: it often asks for the sensitivity or the model choice, not the number.

@example
title: Two-stage DDM
problem: Halvorsen Foods just paid a dividend of \$1.50. Dividends are expected to grow 12% a year for three years and then 4% a year forever. The required return is 10%. What is the value of the stock?
step: Dividends: $D_1 = 1.50 \times 1.12 = 1.68$; $D_2 = 1.8816$; $D_3 = 2.1074$.
step: Terminal value at year 3: $D_4 = 2.1074 \times 1.04 = 2.1917$, so $V_3 = 2.1917/(0.10 - 0.04) = 36.528$.
step: Discount: $1.68/1.10 + 1.8816/1.10^2 + 2.1074/1.10^3 + 36.528/1.10^3 = 1.527 + 1.555 + 1.583 + 27.445 = 32.11$.
answer: About \$32.11 a share.

@example
title: From FCFF to a share value
problem: Marlow Co. is expected to generate FCFF of \$120 million next year, growing 3% a year forever. Its WACC is 8%, its debt has a market value of \$700 million, and it has 100 million shares. What is the value per share?
step: Firm value: $120/(0.08 - 0.03) = \$2{,}400$ million.
step: Equity value: $2{,}400 - 700 = \$1{,}700$ million.
step: Per share: $1{,}700/100 = \$17.00$.
answer: \$17.00 a share.

@mistakes
- Using $D_0$ as the numerator of the Gordon growth formula instead of $D_1 = D_0(1+g)$.
- Discounting FCFE at the WACC or FCFF at the cost of equity.
- Forgetting to subtract debt after discounting FCFF at the WACC.
- Computing the terminal value correctly but discounting it with the wrong year (it is discounted with the same factor as the last explicit cash flow).

prompt: A utility just paid an annual dividend of \$1.80. Dividends are expected to grow 3.5% a year indefinitely, and investors require 8.5%. What is the value of a share, to the nearest cent?
answer: 37.26
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: 1.80 * 1.035 / (0.085 - 0.035)
hint: The model needs next year's dividend.
hint: $D_1 = 1.80 \times 1.035$.
solution: $V_0 = 1.80 \times 1.035/(0.085 - 0.035) = 1.863/0.05 = \$37.26$.

choice: 6.0%
choice: 15.0%

@practice mc
prompt: An analyst projects free cash flow to the firm for a company with a stable capital structure and 3% perpetual growth. The company has debt with a market value of \$300 million. Which procedure is correct to arrive at the value of equity?
choice: Discount the FCFF at the cost of equity to obtain equity value directly
choice*: Discount the FCFF at the WACC to obtain firm value, then subtract the market value of debt
choice: Discount the FCFF at the after-tax cost of debt, then add the market value of debt
hint: FCFF belongs to all capital providers.
solution: FCFF is the cash flow to all investors, so it is discounted at the WACC to obtain firm value, and the market value of debt is subtracted to reach equity.

@practice num
prompt: A company just paid a dividend of \$2.00. Dividends will grow 10% a year for two years and then 3% a year forever. The required return is 8%. What is the value of a share, to the nearest cent?
answer: 46.85
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: 2.00 * 1.1 / 1.08 + (2.00 * 1.1 ** 2 + 2.00 * 1.1 ** 2 * 1.03 / (0.08 - 0.03)) / 1.08 ** 2
hint: Compute the terminal value at the end of year 2 from the year 3 dividend.
hint: $D_3 = 2.42 \times 1.03$; $V_2 = D_3/(0.08 - 0.03)$.
solution: $D_1 = 2.20$, $D_2 = 2.42$, $D_3 = 2.4926$, $V_2 = 49.852$. The value is $2.20/1.08 + (2.42 + 49.852)/1.08^2 = 2.037 + 44.815 = \$46.85$.

@quiz num

lead: \$
tol: 0.1
