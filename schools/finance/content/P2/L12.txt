### What the exam looks like

Level II is a computer-based exam of **item sets**: a vignette (a page of text, with tables or exhibits) followed by several multiple-choice questions, each with three choices. The ten topic areas of Level I recur, each weighted 5–10% or 10–15% in the current outline, and the emphasis is **asset valuation**, so equity, fixed income and derivatives questions dominate the calculations. Recent exams have run about 88 questions in two sessions, which works out to roughly three minutes per question; confirm the current numbers. CFA Institute recommends about 300 hours of study per level. There is no penalty for a wrong answer, so never leave a question blank.

The path: pass Levels I, II and III in order. Level III uses item sets and constructed-response (essay) questions, with a core curriculum and one specialized pathway (Portfolio Management, Private Markets or Private Wealth). To earn the charter you also need 4,000 hours of relevant work experience over at least 36 months, membership, and references.

### Working an item set

1. **Skim the questions first** (just the stems), then read the vignette with the questions in mind. You are hunting for the numbers and the one fact that decides each question.
2. **Answer in order of ease** within the set: the exam lets you flag and return. A two-minute question that you can answer should not wait behind a five-minute one.
3. **Work the calculation with one clean pass.** Write the formula, substitute, and compare your result with the choices. If the answer is not among the choices, check the units, the timing ($D_0$ versus $D_1$) and the discount rate before redoing everything.
4. **Use direction to eliminate.** Before computing, ask whether the answer should rise or fall; many of the three choices fail the direction test.
5. **Budget time.** Know your target minutes per set and a checkpoint at the halfway mark. If you fall more than 5 minutes behind, speed up by guessing on the most expensive questions.

### The full-length practice exam

Take at least two full-length timed exams, in one sitting per session, in conditions close to the real exam: the same time limit, no notes, a calculator that is permitted on the exam (the exam has a short list of approved models; check the current list), and the break between sessions. Original item sets are best, because reused questions test memory. For each session, record the result by topic and by error type.

### The error log

Every missed or guessed question goes into a log with five columns: topic area, subtopic, **error type**, the correct reasoning, and the fix. Use four error types:

| Error type | Example | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge gap | Did not know the IFRS pension components | Relearn from the text; one flashcard |
| Method slip | Used $D_0$ instead of $D_1$ | Practice 10 questions of that type; write the formula first |
| Reading error | Missed that the rate was after tax | Underline the units and the question stem |
| Time pressure | Guessed in the last 10 minutes | Practice pacing; skip and return |

After two practice exams, sort the log. If most misses are method slips in one topic, drill that topic. If misses are spread across topics but mostly reading errors, change your process, not your content. Revisit topics by weight: a topic with a heavy weight and a weak score gets the next study block.

### Final month plan

Weeks four to three: one weak topic block per day, plus one full item-set practice session. Weeks two to one: full exams under timed conditions, review, ethics refresh, formula sheet. The last three days: light review of the error log, and rest. This course is general education and is not affiliated with or endorsed by CFA Institute.

@example
title: Pacing a session
problem: A practice session has 44 questions and 132 minutes. After the first 10 questions you have used 40 minutes. How many minutes per question can you spend on the rest to finish exactly on time?
step: Time left: $132 - 40 = 92$ minutes.
step: Questions left: $44 - 10 = 34$.
step: Pace: $92/34 = 2.71$ minutes per question, versus the plan of 3. You are about 0.3 minutes behind per question already, so skip and flag the longest calculations.
answer: About 2.7 minutes per question.

@example
title: One error-log entry
problem: You missed a question that asked for the value of a share using a two-stage dividend discount model: you used the dividend just paid as the first forecast dividend. Write the log entry.
step: Topic and subtopic: Equity valuation, dividend discount model.
step: Error type: method slip, since you knew the model.
step: Correct reasoning: the first forecast dividend is $D_0(1 + g_1)$; the terminal value is built from the next dividend, not the last.
step: Fix: write the dividend timeline before computing, and drill 10 two-stage questions.
answer: Method slip: timing of the first dividend; drill and write timelines.

@mistakes
- Spending too long on early questions, so the last sets get guesses.
- Reviewing only what you got wrong by content, not by error type.
- Practicing with untimed sets and then expecting to be fast on exam day.
- Studying only your favorite topics: heavy-weight topics you avoid cost the most points.

@practice mc
prompt: Cassian Moreau is a research analyst who owns shares of a company he covers. The law of his country does not require him to disclose the holding, but the CFA Institute Code and Standards, which his firm follows, do. What should he do?
choice: Follow the local law, since it is the law of his country
choice*: Follow the stricter of the two, the Code, and disclose the holding, as Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law and Standard VI(A) Disclosure of Conflicts require
choice: Disclose the holding to his employer only, since clients need not be told when the law is silent
hint: When the law and the Code differ, the stricter governs.
solution: Standard I(A), Knowledge of the Law, says to comply with the stricter of applicable law and the Code. Disclosing a holding in a covered company is also a Standard VI(A) obligation, and VI(A) requires full and fair disclosure to clients and prospective clients as well as to the employer, so Cassian should disclose it in his research, not only to his firm.

@practice num
prompt: A company will pay out 40% of next year's expected earnings of \$5.00 per share as dividends and retain the rest. Its return on equity on retained earnings is 12% and the required return is 10%. Assuming growth equals the sustainable growth rate forever, what is the value per share (Gordon growth), to the nearest cent?
answer: 71.43
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: (5.00 * 0.40) / (0.10 - 0.60 * 0.12)
hint: Compute the growth rate first, then the dividend.
hint: $g = 0.60 \times 0.12$; $D_1 = 0.40 \times 5.00$.
solution: $g = 7.2\%$ and $D_1 = \$2.00$, so $V = 2.00/(0.10 - 0.072) = \$71.43$.

@practice num
prompt: A company's expected FCFF next year is \$90 million, growing 3% a year forever. Its WACC is 9% and its debt has a market value of \$600 million. What is the equity value, in millions of dollars?
answer: 900
lead: \$
tol: 0.5
check: 90 / (0.09 - 0.03) - 600
hint: Value the firm first.
hint: $90/(0.09 - 0.03)$, then subtract debt.
solution: Firm value is $90/0.06 = \$1{,}500$ million; equity value is $1{,}500 - 600 = \$900$ million.

@quiz num
prompt: One-year and two-year spot rates are 2.5% and 3.1% (annual compounding). What is the one-year forward rate one year from now, as a percent to two decimal places?
answer: 3.70
unit: %
tol: 0.01
check: (1.031 ** 2 / 1.025 - 1) * 100
hint: $(1 + S_2)^2 = (1 + S_1)(1 + f(1,1))$.
hint: $1.031^2/1.025 - 1$.
solution: $f(1,1) = 1.062961/1.025 - 1 = 3.70\%$.

@quiz mc
prompt: Rhea Castellan manages a client's portfolio. The client's written investment policy statement calls for capital preservation and a maximum equity allocation of 30%. Rhea, convinced of a strong market outlook, raises the client's allocation to 55% without consulting the client. Which Standard is most clearly violated?
choice: V(C) Record Retention
choice*: III(C) Suitability
choice: II(B) Market Manipulation
hint: Compare the action with the client's stated objectives and constraints.
solution: Acting against the client's written objectives and constraints violates Standard III(C), Suitability. Standard III(A), Loyalty, Prudence, and Care, is also relevant, but the best answer is III(C).
