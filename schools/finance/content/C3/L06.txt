@article
Why do firms in competitive markets produce where price equals marginal cost, and why do their profits vanish in the long run? This week answers both and delivers this block's goal: a firm's supply curve, derived from its cost function.

### The competitive model

A market is **perfectly competitive** when (1) there are many buyers and sellers, each too small to affect the price; (2) the product is homogeneous; (3) buyers and sellers have full information about prices; and (4) there is free entry and exit in the long run. Each firm is a **price taker**: it faces a horizontal demand curve at the market price $p$, so its marginal revenue is $MR = p$.

### Profit maximization

Profit is $\pi(q) = pq - TC(q)$. Setting $d\pi/dq = 0$ gives

$$p = MC(q).$$

The second-order condition requires $MC$ to be rising ($d^2\pi/dq^2 = -MC'(q) < 0$), so the firm produces on the *rising* part of its marginal cost curve.

### The shutdown rule and the firm's supply curve

In the short run, fixed cost is sunk and is paid whether or not the firm produces. Producing is better than shutting down if revenue covers variable cost: $pq \ge VC(q)$, i.e. $p \ge AVC(q)$. So:

- If $p \ge \min AVC$, the firm produces where $p = MC$.
- If $p < \min AVC$, it produces zero and loses $FC$.

The firm's **short-run supply curve** is therefore the part of the $MC$ curve above the minimum of $AVC$ (and zero below it). The **break-even price** is $\min ATC$: at that price, profit is zero. Between $\min AVC$ and $\min ATC$ the firm makes a loss but operates, because the loss is smaller than $FC$.

For $TC(q) = F + aq + bq^2$ (with $b > 0$): $MC = a + 2bq$, so supply is $q = (p - a)/(2b)$ for $p > a$ and zero otherwise.

### Market supply and equilibrium

**Market supply** is the horizontal sum of the firms' supply curves: at each price, add up quantities. With $n$ identical firms, $Q_S(p) = n\,q(p)$. Equilibrium is the price at which $Q_S(p) = Q_D(p)$. **Producer surplus** is revenue minus variable cost, the area above the supply curve and below the price.

### The long run

When firms make positive economic profit, new firms enter, market supply shifts out and price falls. When firms make losses, some exit, supply shifts in and price rises. Long-run equilibrium requires $p = MC$ (profit maximization) and zero economic profit, so $p = ATC$. Both hold only at the minimum of $ATC$:

$$p^{LR} = \min ATC = MC.$$

Each firm then produces at the **efficient scale**, and the long-run number of firms is $n = Q_D(p^{LR})/q^{*}$. Economic profit is zero because opportunity costs, including the owners' required return, are counted in cost; firms stay because they earn that normal return.

@example
title: Deriving a supply curve
problem: A firm has $TC(q) = 90 + 6q + q^2$. Derive its short-run supply curve. How much does it produce and earn at $p = \$30$? What is its break-even price?
step: $MC = 6 + 2q$ and $AVC = 6 + q$. Since $AVC$ is increasing, $\min AVC = 6$ at $q = 0$.
step: Supply: set $p = MC$ to get $q = (p - 6)/2$ for $p \ge 6$, and zero below.
step: At $p = 30$: $q = 12$. Revenue is $30 \times 12 = 360$; $TC = 90 + 72 + 144 = 306$; profit $= 360 - 306 = \$54$.
step: Break-even: $ATC = 90/q + 6 + q$ is minimized where $90/q^2 = 1$, so $q = \sqrt{90} = 9.487$ and $\min ATC = 6 + 2\sqrt{90} = \$24.97$.
answer: $q = (p - 6)/2$ for $p \ge 6$; at $p = 30$, $q = 12$ and profit is \$54; break-even price \$24.97.

@example
title: Short-run and long-run equilibrium
problem: One hundred identical firms each have $TC = 90 + 6q + q^2$. Market demand is $Q_D = 1100 - 20p$. Find the short-run equilibrium and each firm's profit. What happens in the long run?
step: Each firm supplies $q = (p - 6)/2$, so market supply is $Q_S = 100(p - 6)/2 = 50p - 300$.
step: Set $50p - 300 = 1100 - 20p$: $70p = 1400$, so $p = \$20$ and $Q = 700$, with $q = 7$ per firm.
step: Profit per firm: revenue $20 \times 7 = 140$; $TC = 90 + 42 + 49 = 181$; profit $= -\$41$. The firm makes a loss but $p = 20 > AVC = 13$, so it covers variable cost and loses less than its fixed cost of \$90: it operates.
step: Because $p = 20$ is below $\min ATC = 24.97$, firms exit in the long run. Supply shifts in and price rises until $p = 24.97$, where each firm produces $q = 9.487$. Demand at that price is $1100 - 20(24.97) = 600.5$, so about $600.5/9.487 = 63.3$ firms remain (63 or 64 in practice, since firms are indivisible).
answer: Short run: $p = \$20$, $Q = 700$, loss of \$41 per firm. Long run: $p = \$24.97$, about 63 firms, zero profit.

@mistakes
- Shutting down whenever profit is negative. The short-run rule is to shut down only if $p < AVC$, because fixed cost must be paid either way.
- Using $ATC$ instead of $AVC$ in the shutdown rule (or the reverse for the long-run entry rule).
- Deriving supply from the whole $MC$ curve, including the part below $\min AVC$ where the firm shuts down.
- Believing zero economic profit means owners earn nothing. It means they earn exactly their opportunity cost.

model:
```python
def firm_decision(price, fixed_cost, a, b):
    """Short-run output and profit for TC = fixed_cost + a*q + b*q**2."""
    if price <= a:
        return (0, -fixed_cost)
    q = (price - a) / (2 * b)
    profit = price * q - (fixed_cost + a * q + b * q * q)
    return (q, profit)
```
hint: Marginal cost is `a + 2*b*q`; set it equal to the price.
hint: The firm shuts down only when the price is at or below `a`, not whenever profit is negative.
solution: Setting price equal to marginal cost gives `q = (price - a) / (2 * b)`. If the price does not exceed `a` (minimum AVC), the firm produces nothing and loses `fixed_cost`. Otherwise compute revenue minus total cost; this can be negative but is never worse than `-fixed_cost`, which is why a loss alone is not a reason to shut down.

@quiz self
prompt: In long-run competitive equilibrium economic profit is zero. In three or four sentences, explain why firms do not leave the industry.
hint: Think about what costs are included when economists measure profit.
solution: Economic profit subtracts all opportunity costs, including the return the owners could earn on their own capital and labor in the best alternative use. Zero economic profit therefore means the owners earn exactly that normal return. They have no better use of their resources, so they have no reason to exit, and because there is no excess return no new firms have a reason to enter. Price equals minimum average total cost, and the industry is stable.
rubric: Says that economic profit includes implicit (opportunity) costs such as the owners' required return.
rubric: Explains that zero economic profit means a normal return, equal to the best alternative.
rubric: Concludes that neither entry nor exit is attractive, so the industry is stable.
