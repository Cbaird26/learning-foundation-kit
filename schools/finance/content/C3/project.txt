| Structure | Quantity (thousands) | Price | Profit per firm (thousands of dollars) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monopoly ($MR = 200 - 2Q = 50$) | 75 | \$125 | 5,625 |
| Cournot duopoly ($q = (200 - 50)/3$ each) | 100 (50 each) | \$100 | 2,500 |
| Perfect competition ($P = MC$) | 150 | \$50 | 0 |

## Week-by-week key ideas

- **Weeks 1-2, consumer choice.** Preferences (complete, transitive, monotone, convex) are represented by utility; indifference curves have slope $-MRS$. The budget line $p_1x_1 + p_2x_2 = m$ has slope $-p_1/p_2$. At an interior optimum $MRS = p_1/p_2$ and the budget is exhausted. Cobb-Douglas $x_1^{a}x_2^{1-a}$: $x_1 = am/p_1$. Check corners for perfect substitutes and kinks for perfect complements.
- **Week 3, income and substitution effects.** Slutsky income $m' = m + (p_1' - p_1)x_1^{A}$; $SE = x_1(p_1', m') - x_1(p_1, m)$; $IE = x_1(p_1', m) - x_1(p_1', m')$. SE is opposite to the price change; Giffen goods are inferior with an IE larger than the SE.
- **Week 4, production.** $MP_L$, $AP_L$, $MRTS = MP_L/MP_K$, returns to scale (Cobb-Douglas: sum of exponents).
- **Weeks 5-6, costs and competition.** $MC$ crosses $ATC$ and $AVC$ at their minima. Cost minimization: $MRTS = w/r$. A competitive firm sets $p = MC$ and supplies along $MC$ above $\min AVC$; the long-run price is $\min ATC$ with zero economic profit.
- **Week 7, monopoly.** $MR = MC$, $MR$ twice as steep as linear demand, Lerner index $= 1/|\varepsilon|$, deadweight loss, third-degree discrimination.
- **Week 8, games.** Nash equilibrium as mutual best responses, mixed equilibria by indifference, Cournot $q = (a-c)/(3b)$ each, grim trigger $\delta \ge (\pi_d - \pi_c)/(\pi_d - \pi_n)$.
- **Week 9, welfare.** $CS$, $PS$, $DWL = \tfrac12 t\,\Delta Q$ for a tax, Pigouvian tax $= MEC$ at the efficient quantity, public goods: sum marginal benefits vertically.
- **Week 10, uncertainty.** $EU = \sum \pi_i u(x_i)$; risk aversion means concave $u$; $CE = u^{-1}(EU)$; risk premium $= E[x] - CE$; maximum premium $= w_0 - CE$.
- **Week 11, information.** Adverse selection (hidden type) and moral hazard (hidden action); remedies: screening, signaling, deductibles, monitoring.
- **Week 12, factors.** Hire where $MRP = MFC$; monopsony pays less than $MRP$; Gini coefficient.

## Common errors

- Reversing the price ratio in the tangency condition.
- Computing the income effect at the old price.
- Using $ATC$ where the shutdown rule needs $AVC$.
- Setting price equal to $MR$ for a monopolist.
- Treating $E[u(x)]$ as a certainty equivalent.

prompt: A firm produces output with $q = \sqrt{KL}$. Both inputs are variable, the wage is \$4 per unit of labor and capital rents for \$1 per unit. The firm minimizes the cost of each output level and is the only seller of its product, facing inverse demand $P = 64 - 2Q$ (with $Q = q$). It maximizes profit. What is its maximum profit, in dollars?
answer: 450
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: (lambda c: ((64 - c) / 4) * (64 - 2 * ((64 - c) / 4) - c))(2 * (4 * 1) ** 0.5)
hint: First find the long-run cost function. With $a = 0.5$ the minimum cost is $2q\sqrt{wr}$.
hint: Then maximize profit with $MR = 64 - 4Q$ equal to the constant marginal cost.
solution: Minimizing cost with $MRTS = K/L = w/r = 4$ gives $K = 4L$ and $q = 2L$, so $L = q/2$, $K = 2q$ and $C(q) = 4(q/2) + 2q = 4q$: constant marginal cost \$4. Then $MR = 64 - 4Q = 4$ gives $Q = 15$, $P = 64 - 30 = \$34$ and profit $= (34 - 4)(15) = \$450$.

@exam num
prompt: A market has 200 identical consumers, each with utility $U = x^{0.5}y^{0.5}$ and income \$100. Good $y$ is the numeraire with price \$1; good $x$ has price $p$. Good $x$ is supplied by 50 identical price-taking firms, each with total cost $TC(q) = q^2/2$. What is the equilibrium price of good $x$, in dollars? Round to the nearest cent.
answer: 14.14
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: (200 * 0.5 * 100 / 50) ** 0.5
hint: Each consumer spends half of her income on $x$, so her demand is $x = 50/p$. A firm supplies where $p = MC = q$.
solution: Market demand is $200 \times 50/p = 10{,}000/p$. Each firm has $MC = q$, so $q = p$ and market supply is $50p$. Setting $10{,}000/p = 50p$ gives $p^2 = 200$ and $p = \$14.14$ (with total quantity 707.1). Firms have no fixed cost, so they operate at any positive price.

@exam self
prompt: Two firms, Aldermoor and Bellwether, each choose a High or a Low price every year. Annual profits in millions of dollars, listed as (Aldermoor, Bellwether), are: (High, High) = (12, 12); (High, Low) = (3, 16); (Low, High) = (16, 3); (Low, Low) = (6, 6). (a) Find the Nash equilibrium of the one-shot game. (b) If the firms interact every year forever with discount factor $\delta$, find the condition under which a grim trigger strategy sustains the (High, High) outcome. (c) In a short paragraph, explain what this implies for consumers and why antitrust authorities care about firms' ability to coordinate.
hint: Check each firm's best response to High and to Low.
hint: Compare the value of cooperating forever with deviating once and then facing the one-shot equilibrium forever.
solution: (a) Low is dominant for each firm (16 > 12 and 6 > 3), so (Low, Low) with profits (6, 6) is the unique Nash equilibrium, although both would earn 12 at (High, High). (b) Cooperation is worth $12/(1 - \delta)$; deviating gives $16 + 6\delta/(1 - \delta)$. Cooperation is sustained if $12 \ge 16(1 - \delta) + 6\delta$, i.e. $\delta \ge (16 - 12)/(16 - 6) = 2/5 = 0.4$. (c) Cooperation keeps prices high, so consumers pay more and buy less than in the one-shot equilibrium, creating a deadweight loss. Where the structure makes the condition easy to meet (few firms, frequent interaction, transparent prices), coordination is more likely, which is why authorities watch concentration and communication among rivals.
rubric: Identifies (Low, Low) as the unique Nash equilibrium and shows that Low is dominant.
rubric: Derives the condition $\delta \ge 2/5$ from comparing the value of cooperation with the value of deviation.
rubric: Explains the consumer harm (higher prices, deadweight loss) and why coordination is a concern for competition policy.
