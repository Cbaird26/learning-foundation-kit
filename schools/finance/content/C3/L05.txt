@article
A firm's cost curves come from two things: its production function and the prices of its inputs. This week we build the cost curves; next week we use them to decide how much a competitive firm produces.

### Which costs count

Economic cost is **opportunity cost**: the value of the best alternative use of the resources. It includes **explicit costs** (payments to others) and **implicit costs** (such as the return the owner could earn by investing her own funds elsewhere). A **sunk cost** has already been paid and cannot be recovered; it is irrelevant to decisions about the future.

### Short-run cost curves

In the short run, **fixed costs** $FC$ do not vary with output, and **variable costs** $VC(q)$ do. Total cost is $TC(q) = FC + VC(q)$. Then:

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| Average fixed cost | $AFC = FC/q$ (falls as $q$ rises) |
| Average variable cost | $AVC = VC(q)/q$ |
| Average total cost | $ATC = TC(q)/q = AFC + AVC$ |
| Marginal cost | $MC = dTC/dq = dVC/dq$ |

**Why $MC$ crosses $ATC$ at its minimum.** If marginal cost is below average cost, the next unit pulls the average down; if above, it pulls the average up. So the average is falling when $MC < ATC$, rising when $MC > ATC$, and at its minimum when $MC = ATC$. The same argument applies to $AVC$. Formally, $\dfrac{d}{dq}\dfrac{TC}{q} = \dfrac{MC - ATC}{q}$, which is zero where $MC = ATC$.

### Cost minimization in the long run

In the long run the firm chooses $K$ and $L$ to minimize $wL + rK$ subject to producing $q$, where $w$ is the wage and $r$ is the rental rate of capital. The **isocost line** $wL + rK = C$ has slope $-w/r$ in $(L, K)$ space. At an interior optimum the isoquant is tangent to the lowest isocost line:

$$MRTS = \frac{MP_L}{MP_K} = \frac{w}{r}, \qquad \text{equivalently} \qquad \frac{MP_L}{w} = \frac{MP_K}{r}.$$

The firm equalizes the extra output per dollar across inputs. For $q = K^{a}L^{1-a}$, the tangency gives $K = \dfrac{a}{1-a}\dfrac{w}{r}L$ and the minimum cost is

$$C(q) = q\left(\frac{r}{a}\right)^{a}\left(\frac{w}{1-a}\right)^{1-a}.$$

Tracing the optimal input mixes as $q$ grows gives the **expansion path**; the minimum cost at each $q$ is the **long-run total cost** $LRTC(q)$, and $LRAC = LRTC/q$.

### Economies of scale and the envelope

$LRAC$ falls where there are **economies of scale**, is flat under constant returns and rises under **diseconomies of scale**. The output at which $LRAC$ reaches its minimum is the **minimum efficient scale**. With constant returns to scale and constant input prices, $LRAC$ is flat.

For each fixed capital level $\bar{K}$ there is a short-run average cost curve $SRAC(q;\bar{K})$. The firm can always choose the capital that is best for the output it wants, so $LRAC(q) \le SRAC(q;\bar{K})$ for every $q$, with equality at the output where $\bar{K}$ is the optimal capital stock: $LRAC$ is the **lower envelope** of the short-run curves.

@example
title: Short-run cost curves
problem: A firm's total cost is $TC(q) = 50 + 10q + 0.5q^2$ (dollars). Find $FC$, $VC$, $MC$, $AVC$ and $ATC$, and the output that minimizes $ATC$.
step: $FC = 50$ and $VC = 10q + 0.5q^2$. $MC = dTC/dq = 10 + q$. $AVC = 10 + 0.5q$. $ATC = 50/q + 10 + 0.5q$.
step: Minimize $ATC$: $\dfrac{d\,ATC}{dq} = -\dfrac{50}{q^2} + 0.5 = 0$, so $q^2 = 100$ and $q = 10$.
step: At $q = 10$: $ATC = 5 + 10 + 5 = 20$ and $MC = 10 + 10 = 20$. They are equal, as the theory says.
step: Check either side: at $q = 5$, $ATC = 10 + 10 + 2.5 = 22.5$ and $MC = 15$ (below ATC, so ATC is falling); at $q = 20$, $ATC = 2.5 + 10 + 10 = 22.5$ and $MC = 30$ (above ATC, so ATC is rising).
answer: $ATC$ is minimized at $q = 10$, where $ATC = MC = \$20$.

@example
title: Cost-minimizing inputs
problem: A firm has $q = K^{0.5}L^{0.5}$, with wage $w = \$4$ and capital rental rate $r = \$1$. Find the cost-minimizing inputs and the minimum cost of producing $q = 36$, and the long-run total cost function.
step: With $a = 0.5$, $MRTS = K/L$. Setting $K/L = w/r = 4$ gives $K = 4L$.
step: Put $K = 4L$ into the output constraint: $(4L)^{0.5}L^{0.5} = 2L = 36$, so $L = 18$ and $K = 72$.
step: Cost: $wL + rK = 4(18) + 1(72) = 72 + 72 = \$144$. Spending is split equally between the two inputs, as it must be with equal exponents.
step: For general $q$: $2L = q$ gives $L = q/2$ and $K = 2q$, so $LRTC(q) = 4(q/2) + 2q = 4q$. Check with the formula: $q(r/a)^{a}(w/(1-a))^{1-a} = q(2)^{0.5}(8)^{0.5} = 4q$. Constant returns to scale give a constant $LRAC = \$4$ and $LRMC = \$4$.
answer: $L = 18$, $K = 72$, minimum cost \$144; $LRTC(q) = 4q$.

@mistakes
- Including sunk costs when deciding how much to produce. Only costs that change with the decision matter.
- Using $MRTS = w/r$ with the ratio upside down or with $MP_K/MP_L$: the condition is $MP_L/MP_K = w/r$.
- Thinking marginal cost crosses average cost at the minimum of marginal cost. $MC$ crosses $ATC$ and $AVC$ at *their* minima.
- Forgetting that fixed cost vanishes only in the long run: in the short run $ATC$ includes $FC/q$, so $ATC > AVC$ at every $q$.

prompt: A firm's total cost is $TC(q) = 200 + 8q + 0.25q^2$ dollars. What is its marginal cost at $q = 20$, in dollars per unit?
answer: 18
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: 8 + 0.5 * 20
hint: Differentiate $TC$ with respect to $q$.
solution: $MC = dTC/dq = 8 + 0.5q$, so $MC(20) = 8 + 10 = \$18$. Because $MC = 18 < ATC = 23$ at this output, average total cost is falling there.

@practice num
prompt: A firm's total cost is $TC(q) = 200 + 8q + 0.25q^2$ dollars. What is the minimum value of average total cost, in dollars per unit? Round to the nearest cent.
answer: 22.14
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: 8 + 2 * (200 * 0.25) ** 0.5
hint: $ATC = 200/q + 8 + 0.25q$. Set its derivative to zero to find $q$.
solution: $d\,ATC/dq = -200/q^2 + 0.25 = 0$ gives $q^2 = 800$ and $q = 28.28$. Then $ATC = 200/28.28 + 8 + 0.25(28.28) = 7.07 + 8 + 7.07 = \$22.14$. At that output $MC = 8 + 0.5(28.28) = 22.14$ as well.

@practice num
prompt: A firm has the production function $q = K^{0.25}L^{0.75}$. The wage is \$3 and the rental rate of capital is \$2. What is the minimum cost of producing 100 units? Round to the nearest cent. (Both inputs are variable and the firm minimizes cost.)
answer: 475.68
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: 100 * (2 / 0.25) ** 0.25 * (3 / 0.75) ** 0.75
hint: Use $MRTS = (0.75/0.25)(K/L) = w/r$ to express $K$ in terms of $L$, then use the output constraint.
solution: $3K/L = 3/2$ gives $K = L/2$. Then $q = (L/2)^{0.25}L^{0.75} = 2^{-0.25}L = 100$, so $L = 100 \times 2^{0.25} = 118.92$ and $K = 59.46$. Cost $= 3(118.92) + 2(59.46) = 356.76 + 118.92 = \$475.68$. The closed form $q(r/a)^a(w/(1-a))^{1-a} = 100 \times 8^{0.25} \times 4^{0.75} = 475.68$ agrees.

@quiz num
prompt: A firm has the production function $q = \sqrt{KL}$. In the short run its capital is fixed at $K = 16$, rented at \$3 per unit. Labor costs \$5 per unit. What is its short-run total cost of producing 24 units? Give the answer in dollars.
answer: 228
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: 3 * 16 + 5 * (24 ** 2 / 16)
hint: With $K = 16$, $q = 4\sqrt{L}$. Solve for the labor needed.
solution: $q = 4\sqrt{L}$ gives $L = q^2/16 = 576/16 = 36$. Cost $= 3(16) + 5(36) = 48 + 180 = \$228$. In general $SRTC(q) = 48 + 0.3125q^2$.

@quiz self
prompt: Explain in four or five sentences why long-run average cost can never exceed short-run average cost at the same output, and when the two curves touch.
hint: In the long run the firm may choose its capital stock; in the short run it may not.
solution: A short-run cost curve is built with one particular fixed capital stock $\bar{K}$. In the long run the firm can choose any capital stock, including $\bar{K}$, so the cheapest way to produce $q$ in the long run can be no more expensive than producing it with $\bar{K}$. Hence $LRAC(q) \le SRAC(q;\bar{K})$ for every $q$. The two are equal exactly at the output for which $\bar{K}$ is the cost-minimizing capital stock; at all other outputs the short-run curve lies above. The long-run curve is therefore the lower envelope of the short-run curves.
rubric: States that the long-run firm can choose any capital stock, including the fixed one, so long-run cost is no higher.
rubric: Says the curves touch at the output for which the fixed capital stock is the cost-minimizing one.
rubric: Describes the long-run curve as the lower envelope of the short-run curves.
