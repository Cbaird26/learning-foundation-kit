@article
Geometry starts from a few ideas that are not defined in terms of anything simpler. A **point** is a location with no size. A **line** is a straight path through two points that extends forever in both directions. A **plane** is a flat surface that extends forever. Everything else is built from these.

### Pieces of a line

A **line segment** $\overline{AB}$ is the part of a line between points $A$ and $B$, including both endpoints; its length is written $AB$. A **ray** $\overrightarrow{AB}$ starts at $A$ and passes through $B$, going on forever. The **midpoint** $M$ of a segment is the point that splits it into two segments of equal length, so $AM = MB$ and $AB = 2\,AM$.

### Angles

An **angle** is formed by two rays with a common endpoint, the **vertex**. Picture two rays leaving the origin: one along the positive $x$-axis, one pointing up and to the right. The amount of turn between them is the angle measure, in degrees. Key types:

- **acute**: between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$
- **right**: exactly $90^\circ$ (drawn with a small square)
- **obtuse**: between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$
- **straight**: exactly $180^\circ$ (the two rays form a line)

An **angle bisector** is a ray from the vertex that splits the angle into two equal angles.

### Angle pairs

- **Complementary** angles add to $90^\circ$. **Supplementary** angles add to $180^\circ$.
- A **linear pair** is two adjacent angles whose outer rays form a line; they are supplementary.
- **Vertical angles** are the opposite angles formed when two lines cross. Draw two lines crossing at a point: the four angles come in two equal pairs, because each angle forms a linear pair with both of its neighbours, so each equals $180^\circ$ minus the same quantity.

### Logical reasoning

A **conditional statement** has the form "If $p$, then $q$," where $p$ is the **hypothesis** and $q$ the **conclusion**. From it you can form:

| Name | Form |
|---|---|
| Converse | If $q$, then $p$ |
| Inverse | If not $p$, then not $q$ |
| Contrapositive | If not $q$, then not $p$ |

A conditional and its **contrapositive** are always logically equivalent (both true or both false). A conditional and its converse are **not** equivalent: "If a figure is a square, then it is a rectangle" is true, but its converse is false.

To show a conditional is false you need only one **counterexample**: a case where the hypothesis is true and the conclusion is false. To show it is true in general you need a proof, and examples alone never prove a general claim. This is the idea that the rest of the course builds on.

@example
title: Angle bisector and a linear pair
problem: Ray $BD$ bisects $\angle ABC$. The measures are $m\angle ABD = (3x + 6)^\circ$ and $m\angle DBC = (5x - 10)^\circ$. Find $x$ and $m\angle ABC$.
step: A bisector makes the two angles equal, so set $3x + 6 = 5x - 10$.
step: Subtract $3x$ and add $10$ to both sides: $16 = 2x$, so $x = 8$.
step: Each small angle is $3(8) + 6 = 30^\circ$. Check with the other: $5(8) - 10 = 30^\circ$.
step: The whole angle is the sum: $m\angle ABC = 30^\circ + 30^\circ = 60^\circ$.

@mistakes
- Treating a statement and its converse as the same claim, for example assuming "if a figure is a rectangle then it is a square" because the reverse is true.
- Forgetting that complementary angles add to $90^\circ$ and supplementary angles add to $180^\circ$ (mixing up the two words).
- Using several examples that work as if they were a proof; one counterexample disproves a claim but many examples never prove it.
- Assuming angles that look equal in a sketch are equal without being told.

@practice num
prompt: An angle measures $38^\circ$. What is the measure of its complement, in degrees?
answer: 52
check: 90 - 38
hint: Complementary angles add to $90^\circ$.
solution: $90 - 38 = 52$, so the complement is $52^\circ$.

@practice num
prompt: Two angles form a linear pair. Their measures are $(2x + 10)^\circ$ and $(3x + 20)^\circ$. Find $x$.
answer: 30
check: root(lambda x: (2*x + 10) + (3*x + 20) - 180, -100, 100)
hint: A linear pair is supplementary.
hint: Set $(2x + 10) + (3x + 20) = 180$ and combine like terms.
solution: $5x + 30 = 180$, so $5x = 150$ and $x = 30$. The angles are $70^\circ$ and $110^\circ$.

@practice mc
prompt: Which statement is the converse of "If a figure is a square, then it is a rectangle"?
choice: If a figure is not a square, then it is not a rectangle.
choice*: If a figure is a rectangle, then it is a square.
choice: If a figure is not a rectangle, then it is not a square.
choice: A figure is a square if and only if it is not a rectangle.
hint: The converse swaps the hypothesis and the conclusion.
solution: Swapping the two parts gives "If a figure is a rectangle, then it is a square" (which happens to be false). The version with both parts negated is the inverse, and the version that swaps and negates is the contrapositive.

@practice num
prompt: Point $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{AB}$. $AM = 4x - 3$ and $MB = 2x + 9$. Find the length $AB$.
answer: 42
check: 2 * (4 * ((9 + 3) / 2) - 3)
hint: A midpoint makes $AM = MB$. Solve for $x$ first.
hint: $4x - 3 = 2x + 9$ gives $x = 6$. Now find $AM$, then double it.
solution: $x = 6$, so $AM = 4(6) - 3 = 21$ and $MB = 2(6) + 9 = 21$. Then $AB = 21 + 21 = 42$.

@practice mc
prompt: Which value of $x$ is a counterexample to the claim "If $x^2 = 25$, then $x = 5$"?
choice: $x = 5$
choice*: $x = -5$
choice: $x = 25$
choice: $x = 0$
hint: A counterexample makes the hypothesis true and the conclusion false.
solution: For $x = -5$, $x^2 = 25$ is true but $x = 5$ is false. The choice $x = 5$ satisfies both parts, and $x = 25$ and $x = 0$ do not make the hypothesis true.

@quiz num
prompt: Two lines intersect. One of the four angles measures $(5x - 12)^\circ$ and the angle vertical to it measures $(3x + 18)^\circ$. Find the measure of that angle in degrees.
answer: 63
check: 5 * ((18 + 12) / 2) - 12
hint: Vertical angles are equal.
hint: Solve $5x - 12 = 3x + 18$, then substitute back.
solution: $2x = 30$, so $x = 15$. The angle is $5(15) - 12 = 63^\circ$; the other is $3(15) + 18 = 63^\circ$.

@quiz mc
prompt: Which statement is the contrapositive of "If a quadrilateral is a rhombus, then it has four congruent sides"?
choice: If a quadrilateral has four congruent sides, then it is a rhombus.
choice: If a quadrilateral is not a rhombus, then it does not have four congruent sides.
choice*: If a quadrilateral does not have four congruent sides, then it is not a rhombus.
choice: If a quadrilateral is a rhombus, then it does not have four congruent sides.
hint: Swap the hypothesis and conclusion, then negate both.
solution: Negating and swapping gives "If a quadrilateral does not have four congruent sides, then it is not a rhombus." The contrapositive is always equivalent to the original statement.
