@article
A **circle** is the set of all points in a plane at a fixed distance, the **radius**, from a fixed point, the **center**. A **chord** joins two points on the circle; a chord through the center is a **diameter**, of length $2r$.

### Arcs and angles

A **central angle** has its vertex at the center. The arc it cuts off (its **intercepted arc**) has the same degree measure as the angle. A whole circle is $360^\circ$.

An **inscribed angle** has its vertex on the circle and its sides are chords. Its measure is **half** of its intercepted arc:
$$m\angle = \tfrac{1}{2}\, m(\text{arc}).$$
So an inscribed angle is half of the central angle on the same arc. Two inscribed angles on the same arc are equal. An angle inscribed in a **semicircle** (it intercepts a diameter's arc of $180^\circ$) is a right angle. The opposite angles of an inscribed quadrilateral add to $180^\circ$.

### Chords

- A radius perpendicular to a chord **bisects** the chord. If the chord has length $2c$ and the circle has radius $r$, then the distance $d$ from the center to the chord satisfies $d^2 + c^2 = r^2$.
- Chords of equal length are equal distances from the center and cut off equal arcs.
- **Intersecting chords:** if two chords cross inside a circle and cut one chord into pieces $a$ and $b$ and the other into $c$ and $d$, then $ab = cd$.

### Tangents

A **tangent** touches the circle at exactly one point. It is **perpendicular to the radius** at that point of tangency, which gives a right angle to use with the Pythagorean theorem. Two tangent segments drawn from the same outside point are **equal in length**.

### Arc length and sector area

For a central angle of $\theta$ degrees, the arc length is $\dfrac{\theta}{360}\cdot 2\pi r$ and the sector area is $\dfrac{\theta}{360}\cdot \pi r^2$.

### A proof about parallelograms

*Given:* $ABCD$ is a parallelogram. *Prove:* $AB = CD$ and $BC = DA$.

Draw the diagonal $\overline{AC}$. Because $\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{CD}$, the alternate interior angles $\angle BAC$ and $\angle DCA$ are equal. Because $\overline{AD} \parallel \overline{BC}$, the alternate interior angles $\angle BCA$ and $\angle DAC$ are equal. The side $AC$ is shared. So $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA$ by ASA, and CPCTC gives $AB = CD$ and $BC = DA$. The same triangles also show that opposite angles are equal.

@example
title: Distance from a point to the circle's center using a tangent
problem: A circle has center $O$ and radius $6$. From an outside point $P$, a tangent segment touches the circle at $T$, and $PT = 8$. Find $OP$.
step: A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency, so $\angle OTP = 90^\circ$ and $\triangle OTP$ is a right triangle.
step: The legs are $OT = 6$ (a radius) and $PT = 8$; the hypotenuse is $OP$.
step: Pythagorean theorem: $OP^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 100$.
step: So $OP = 10$.

@mistakes
- Setting an inscribed angle equal to its intercepted arc instead of half of it.
- Using the central angle formula for an angle whose vertex is on the circle.
- Forgetting that the tangent is perpendicular to the radius, and so missing the right triangle.
- Treating a half-chord as the whole chord when using $d^2 + c^2 = r^2$.

@practice self
prompt: Given: $ABCD$ is a parallelogram with diagonal $\overline{AC}$. Write a two-column proof that $AB = CD$ and $BC = DA$. Name the congruence criterion you use.
hint: Use the parallel sides to find equal alternate interior angles with transversal $\overline{AC}$.
hint: The two triangles formed by the diagonal share a side.
solution: 1. $ABCD$ is a parallelogram (Given). 2. $\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{CD}$ and $\overline{AD} \parallel \overline{BC}$ (Definition of parallelogram). 3. $\angle BAC \cong \angle DCA$ (Alternate interior angles, $AB \parallel CD$, transversal $AC$). 4. $\angle BCA \cong \angle DAC$ (Alternate interior angles, $BC \parallel AD$, transversal $AC$). 5. $AC = AC$ (Reflexive property). 6. $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA$ (ASA, lines 3, 5, 4). 7. $AB = CD$ and $BC = DA$ (CPCTC).
rubric: Draws the diagonal and uses parallel lines to get two pairs of equal angles.
rubric: Identifies the shared side $AC$ and names ASA (or AAS).
rubric: Writes the congruence with vertices in matching order.
rubric: Uses CPCTC for the final side conclusion.
