### Key ideas, week by week

1. **Functions, limits, continuous compounding.** A limit describes behavior near a point; $\left(1 + \frac rm\right)^{mt} \to e^{rt}$ as $m \to \infty$, so $A = Pe^{rt}$ and $\text{EAR} = e^r - 1$.
2. **Derivatives and marginal analysis.** $MC = C'(q)$, $MR = R'(q)$; profit peaks where $MR = MC$ and profit is concave.
3. **Exponentials and logarithms.** $\frac{d}{dx}e^{kx} = ke^{kx}$, $\frac{d}{dx}\ln x = \frac1x$; continuous rate $r = \frac1T\ln\frac{V_T}{V_0}$; log returns add over time.
4. **Integrals.** $\int_a^b f = F(b) - F(a)$; a continuous stream has $PV = \int_0^T f(t)e^{-rt}\,dt = \frac cr(1 - e^{-rT})$ for constant $c$.
5. **Series.** $S_n = a\frac{1 - r^n}{1 - r}$, $S_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r}$; annuity $PV = C\frac{1 - (1+i)^{-n}}{i}$, perpetuity $\frac Ci$, growing perpetuity $\frac{C_1}{i - g}$.
6. **Partial derivatives.** Hold other variables fixed; $\nabla f = (f_x, f_y)$; $\Delta f \approx f_x\Delta x + f_y\Delta y$.
7. **Vectors and matrices.** Dot product, norm, $\mu_p = w^T\mu$, row-by-column multiplication, $AB \neq BA$, $(AB)^T = B^TA^T$.
8. **Linear systems.** $Ax = b$, elimination, $\det\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} = ad - bc$, $x = A^{-1}b$ when $\det A \neq 0$; state prices.
9. **Portfolio variance.** $\sigma_p^2 = w^T\Sigma w$; diversification; risk contributions $w_i(\Sigma w)_i$.
10. **Unconstrained optimization.** $f' = 0$ and $f'' > 0$ for a minimum; $\nabla f = 0$ with $D = f_{xx}f_{yy} - f_{xy}^2 > 0$; two-asset $w_1^* = \frac{\sigma_2^2 - \sigma_{12}}{\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 - 2\sigma_{12}}$.
11. **Lagrange multipliers.** $\mathcal L = f - \lambda(g - c)$; $\lambda = df^*/dc$; minimum variance $w^* = \frac{\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1}{\mathbf 1^T\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1}$.
12. **Applications.** Target-return portfolios add a second constraint $\mu^Tw = m$ and a second multiplier $\gamma$.

### Most common errors

- Using a percent instead of a decimal in $e^{rt}$, in $i$ or in a covariance.
- Setting price equal to marginal cost when the firm faces downward-sloping demand (use $MR$).
- Forgetting the factor 2 on covariance cross terms, or adding standard deviations instead of variances.
- Multiplying matrices in the wrong order, or dimension mismatches.
- Forgetting to rescale to sum to 1, or ignoring the second-order check.

answer: 6.12
tol: 0.01
unit: %
check: sum((1 / v) / sum(1 / u for u in [0.01, 0.04, 0.09]) * m for v, m in zip([0.01, 0.04, 0.09], [5, 8, 12]))
hint: For uncorrelated assets the minimum-variance weights are proportional to $1/\sigma_i^2$.
hint: The inverse variances are $100$, $25$ and $11.11$; rescale them to sum to 1, then take the dot product with the expected returns.
solution: The inverse variances sum to $136.11$, so $w = (0.7347, 0.1837, 0.0816)$. The expected return is $\mu^Tw = 0.7347(5) + 0.1837(8) + 0.0816(12) = 3.673 + 1.469 + 0.980 = 6.12\%$. (The variance is $1/136.11 = 0.00735$, a standard deviation of 8.57%.)

@exam self
prompt: Two assets have variances $\sigma_1^2$ and $\sigma_2^2$ and covariance $\sigma_{12}$. (a) Use a Lagrange multiplier to derive the weights of the fully invested minimum-variance portfolio. (b) Show that the multiplier equals twice the minimum variance. (c) Explain, in words, why the weights do not depend on expected returns, and check your formula with $\sigma_1^2 = 0.16$, $\sigma_2^2 = 0.04$ and $\sigma_{12} = 0.02$.
solution: (a) Minimize $w_1^2\sigma_1^2 + w_2^2\sigma_2^2 + 2w_1w_2\sigma_{12}$ subject to $w_1 + w_2 = 1$ with $\mathcal L = \dots - \lambda(w_1 + w_2 - 1)$. The conditions are $2(w_1\sigma_1^2 + w_2\sigma_{12}) = \lambda$ and $2(w_1\sigma_{12} + w_2\sigma_2^2) = \lambda$. Equating them and using $w_2 = 1 - w_1$ gives $w_1(\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 - 2\sigma_{12}) = \sigma_2^2 - \sigma_{12}$, so $w_1^* = \frac{\sigma_2^2 - \sigma_{12}}{\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 - 2\sigma_{12}}$ and $w_2^* = 1 - w_1^*$. (b) Multiply the first condition by $w_1$, the second by $w_2$, and add: $2(w^T\Sigma w) = \lambda(w_1 + w_2) = \lambda$, so $\lambda = 2\sigma^2_{\min}$. (c) The objective and the budget constraint contain only $\Sigma$ and the weights; expected returns never enter, so they cannot affect the minimizer. Check: $w_1 = \frac{0.04 - 0.02}{0.16 + 0.04 - 0.04} = 0.125$, $w_2 = 0.875$, with variance $0.0375$ and $\lambda = 0.075$.
rubric: Sets up the Lagrangian with the budget constraint and obtains both first-order conditions correctly.
rubric: Solves for $w_1^*$ and $w_2^*$ and verifies the numerical check ($12.5\%$ and $87.5\%$, variance $0.0375$).
rubric: Shows $\lambda = 2\sigma^2_{\min}$ by multiplying the conditions by the weights and adding.
rubric: Explains that expected returns do not appear in the objective or constraint.
hint: Write one first-order condition for each weight and equate their right-hand sides.
