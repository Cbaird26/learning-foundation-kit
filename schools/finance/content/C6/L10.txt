@article
**Optimization** means choosing inputs to make an output as large or as small as possible. Maximizing profit, minimizing risk and fitting a model to data are all optimization problems. A function without side restrictions on its inputs is **unconstrained**.

### One variable

A **critical point** of $f$ is a value $x^*$ where $f'(x^*) = 0$ (or $f'$ does not exist). At a smooth interior maximum or minimum the tangent line is flat, so every such point is a critical point. Not every critical point is an extremum, so classify it with the **second-derivative test**:

- $f''(x^*) > 0$: the graph curves upward, a **local minimum**.
- $f''(x^*) < 0$: the graph curves downward, a **local maximum**.
- $f''(x^*) = 0$: the test is inconclusive.

For $f(x) = 2x^3 - 9x^2 + 12x$, $f'(x) = 6x^2 - 18x + 12 = 6(x - 1)(x - 2)$, so the critical points are $x = 1$ and $x = 2$. Since $f''(x) = 12x - 18$, we get $f''(1) = -6 < 0$ (a local maximum, with $f(1) = 5$) and $f''(2) = 6 > 0$ (a local minimum, with $f(2) = 4$). A function with $f'' > 0$ everywhere is **convex**, and any critical point is then the global minimum.

### Two variables

For $f(x, y)$, a critical point satisfies $\nabla f = (f_x, f_y) = (0, 0)$, two equations in two unknowns. Classify with the **Hessian** matrix of second partials,
$$H = \begin{pmatrix} f_{xx} & f_{xy} \\ f_{xy} & f_{yy} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad D = f_{xx}f_{yy} - f_{xy}^2 = \det H.$$
At a critical point:

- $D > 0$ and $f_{xx} > 0$: local **minimum**.
- $D > 0$ and $f_{xx} < 0$: local **maximum**.
- $D < 0$: a **saddle point**, neither maximum nor minimum.
- $D = 0$: inconclusive.

For example, $f(x,y) = x^2 - y^2$ has a critical point at the origin with $D = (2)(-2) - 0 = -4 < 0$: a saddle, rising in the $x$ direction and falling in the $y$ direction.

### Portfolio variance as an optimization problem

Two assets with weights $w$ and $1 - w$ (fully invested, shorting allowed) have variance
$$V(w) = w^2\sigma_1^2 + (1 - w)^2\sigma_2^2 + 2w(1 - w)\sigma_{12}.$$
Setting $V'(w) = 2w\sigma_1^2 - 2(1-w)\sigma_2^2 + 2(1 - 2w)\sigma_{12} = 0$ and solving gives the **minimum-variance weight**
$$w_1^* = \frac{\sigma_2^2 - \sigma_{12}}{\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 - 2\sigma_{12}}.$$
Since $V''(w) = 2(\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 - 2\sigma_{12})$, which is twice the variance of the difference of the returns and so positive, this critical point is the global minimum. The weight depends only on the variances and covariance, not on expected returns. Eliminating $w_2 = 1 - w_1$ by substitution turns a constrained problem into an unconstrained one in a single variable; the next lesson handles the general case.

Optimization needs care: a critical point must also be checked against the **boundaries** of the allowed region, and real portfolios often forbid negative weights, which changes the answer.

@example
title: Minimizing a quadratic function of two variables
problem: Find and classify the critical point of $f(x, y) = x^2 + xy + y^2 - 9x - 6y$, and compute its value there.
step: Set the gradient to zero: $f_x = 2x + y - 9 = 0$ and $f_y = x + 2y - 6 = 0$.
step: From the first equation $y = 9 - 2x$. Substituting gives $x + 18 - 4x = 6$, so $x = 4$ and $y = 1$.
step: The second partials are $f_{xx} = 2$, $f_{yy} = 2$, $f_{xy} = 1$, so $D = 4 - 1 = 3 > 0$ and $f_{xx} = 2 > 0$: a local minimum. Because the Hessian is the same everywhere, it is the global minimum.
step: The minimum value is $f(4, 1) = 16 + 4 + 1 - 36 - 6 = -21$.
answer: Minimum at $(4, 1)$ with value $-21$.

@example
title: The minimum-variance mix of two assets
problem: Asset 1 has $\sigma_1 = 20\%$, asset 2 has $\sigma_2 = 30\%$ and their correlation is $0.25$. Find the weight in asset 1 that minimizes portfolio variance, and the resulting standard deviation.
step: The variances are $0.04$ and $0.09$, and $\sigma_{12} = 0.25 \times 0.2 \times 0.3 = 0.015$.
step: The denominator is $0.04 + 0.09 - 2(0.015) = 0.10$, and the numerator is $0.09 - 0.015 = 0.075$, so $w_1^* = 0.75$ and $w_2^* = 0.25$.
step: Check the second derivative: $V'' = 2(0.10) = 0.20 > 0$, a minimum.
step: $V = 0.5625(0.04) + 0.0625(0.09) + 2(0.75)(0.25)(0.015) = 0.0225 + 0.005625 + 0.005625 = 0.03375$, so $\sigma_p = 18.37\%$. That is lower than either asset alone (20% and 30%).
answer: $w_1^* = 75\%$, $w_2^* = 25\%$, with $\sigma_p = 18.37\%$.

@mistakes
- Treating every critical point as a maximum or minimum without applying the second-derivative or Hessian test.
- Forgetting the boundaries: the extreme of a function on an interval can occur at an endpoint.
- Calling a point a minimum when $D > 0$ but $f_{xx} < 0$ (that is a maximum), or using $f_{xx}f_{yy}$ without subtracting $f_{xy}^2$.
- Assuming the minimum-variance portfolio depends on expected returns; it depends only on the covariance matrix.

answer: 5
tol: 0.001
check: max(2 * (k / 1000)**3 - 9 * (k / 1000)**2 + 12 * (k / 1000) for k in range(0, 1501))

@practice num
prompt: Three assets have the covariance matrix below (annual) and a portfolio holds weights $w = (0.3, 0.5, 0.2)$. Find the portfolio variance as a decimal to four places.

$$\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix} 0.090 & 0.020 & 0.010 \\ 0.020 & 0.040 & 0.012 \\ 0.010 & 0.012 & 0.160 \end{pmatrix}$$
answer: 0.0341
tol: 0.0001
check: (lambda w, S: sum(w[i] * w[j] * S[i][j] for i in range(3) for j in range(3)))([0.3, 0.5, 0.2], [[0.09, 0.02, 0.01], [0.02, 0.04, 0.012], [0.01, 0.012, 0.16]])
hint: Compute $\Sigma w$ first, then $w^T(\Sigma w)$.
hint: Row 1 of $\Sigma w$ is $0.09(0.3) + 0.02(0.5) + 0.01(0.2) = 0.039$.
solution: $\Sigma w = (0.039, 0.0284, 0.041)^T$: row 1 is $0.027 + 0.01 + 0.002$; row 2 is $0.006 + 0.02 + 0.0024$; row 3 is $0.003 + 0.006 + 0.032$. Then $w^T\Sigma w = 0.3(0.039) + 0.5(0.0284) + 0.2(0.041) = 0.0117 + 0.0142 + 0.0082 = 0.0341$.

@quiz num
prompt: Find the minimum value of $f(x, y) = x^2 + 3y^2 - 2xy - 4x$.
answer: -6

check: min(x**2 + 3*y**2 - 2*x*y - 4*x for x in [k / 10 for k in range(-50, 101)] for y in [j / 10 for j in range(-50, 101)])
