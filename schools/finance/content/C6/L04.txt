@article
Differentiation answers "how fast is it changing?" Integration answers the reverse: "given the rate, how much has accumulated?" A function $F$ is an **antiderivative** of $f$ if $F' = f$. Two antiderivatives of the same function differ only by a constant, so the family is written $\int f(x)\,dx = F(x) + C$.

### Basic antiderivatives

| $f(x)$ | $\int f(x)\,dx$ |
|---|---|
| $x^n$, $n \neq -1$ | $\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C$ |
| $e^{kx}$, $k \neq 0$ | $\frac{1}{k}e^{kx} + C$ |
| $\frac1x$ | $\ln\lvert x\rvert + C$ |

### The definite integral

Slice the interval $[a, b]$ into many thin pieces of width $\Delta x$ and add up $f(x)\Delta x$ over the pieces. As the pieces get thinner this sum approaches the **definite integral** $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$, the signed area between the graph and the axis. The **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus** gives a way to compute it without summing anything:
$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a), \quad \text{where } F' = f.$$
For example, $\int_0^4 (3x^2 + 2x)\,dx = \left[x^3 + x^2\right]_0^4 = 64 + 16 = 80$, and $\int_0^2 e^{3t}\,dt = \frac{e^{6} - 1}{3} \approx 134.14$.

### Continuous income streams

Suppose money arrives continuously at a rate of $f(t)$ dollars per year at time $t$ (rent collected every day, interest accruing, a factory's output). In a tiny interval of length $dt$ the amount received is about $f(t)\,dt$, so the total received from time $0$ to $T$ is
$$\text{Total} = \int_0^T f(t)\,dt.$$
Money later is worth less than money now. With a continuously compounded discount rate $r$, a dollar received at time $t$ is worth $e^{-rt}$ today. Discounting each slice and adding gives the **present value** of the stream:
$$PV = \int_0^T f(t)\,e^{-rt}\,dt.$$

**Constant stream** $f(t) = c$: since $\int e^{-rt}dt = -\frac{1}{r}e^{-rt}$,
$$PV = c\int_0^T e^{-rt}\,dt = \frac{c}{r}\left(1 - e^{-rT}\right).$$

**Growing stream** $f(t) = ce^{gt}$, with $g \neq r$: the integrand is $ce^{-(r-g)t}$, so
$$PV = \frac{c}{r - g}\left(1 - e^{-(r-g)T}\right).$$

**Perpetuity.** If $r > g$, then $e^{-(r-g)T} \to 0$ as $T \to \infty$ and the present value of a stream lasting forever is finite: $PV = \frac{c}{r - g}$, or $\frac{c}{r}$ for a constant stream. The distant future is discounted heavily enough that the sum converges. If $r \le g$, the integral diverges and the formula does not apply.

### Computing it numerically

When $f$ has no convenient antiderivative, you approximate the integral by the same thin slices. The **midpoint rule** evaluates the integrand at the middle of each of $n$ slices of width $\Delta t = T/n$:
$$PV \approx \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} f(t_k)\,e^{-rt_k}\,\Delta t, \qquad t_k = \left(k + \tfrac12\right)\Delta t.$$
In Python this is one loop over $k$. It is far more accurate than using the left end of each slice, and it lets you check an analytic answer.

@example
title: Present value of a constant continuous stream
problem: Cedar Ridge Mills' mill is expected to generate \$120,000 per year, received continuously, for 10 years. The continuous discount rate is 6%. Find the present value, and compare it with the undiscounted total.
step: Use $PV = \frac{c}{r}(1 - e^{-rT})$ with $c = 120{,}000$, $r = 0.06$, $T = 10$.
step: $rT = 0.6$, and $e^{-0.6} = 0.548812$, so $1 - e^{-0.6} = 0.451188$.
step: $PV = \frac{120{,}000}{0.06} \times 0.451188 = 2{,}000{,}000 \times 0.451188 = \$902{,}376.73$.
step: The undiscounted total is $120{,}000 \times 10 = \$1{,}200{,}000$, so discounting removes about \$297,623, or roughly 25% of face value.
answer: \$902,376.73, versus \$1,200,000 undiscounted.

@example
title: Present value of a growing stream
problem: A royalty stream starts at \$50,000 per year and grows continuously at 3% per year for 5 years. The continuous discount rate is 8%. Find the present value.
step: The flow is $f(t) = 50{,}000e^{0.03t}$, so the discounted integrand is $50{,}000e^{-0.05t}$ because $r - g = 0.08 - 0.03 = 0.05$.
step: $PV = \frac{50{,}000}{0.05}\left(1 - e^{-0.05 \times 5}\right) = 1{,}000{,}000\left(1 - e^{-0.25}\right)$.
step: $e^{-0.25} = 0.778801$, so $PV = 1{,}000{,}000 \times 0.221199 = \$221{,}199.22$.
answer: \$221,199.22

@mistakes
- Forgetting the discount factor and integrating $f(t)$ alone, which gives the undiscounted total.
- Using the nominal rate in the exponent with the wrong sign: the discount factor is $e^{-rt}$, not $e^{rt}$.
- Applying the perpetuity formula when $r \le g$, where the integral diverges.
- Dropping the lower limit: evaluate $F(b) - F(a)$, not just $F(b)$.

model:
```python
import math

def pv_stream(f, rate, years, steps):
    """Present value of a continuous stream by the midpoint rule."""
    dt = years / steps
    total = 0.0
    for k in range(steps):
        t = (k + 0.5) * dt
        total += f(t) * math.exp(-rate * t) * dt
    return total
```
hint: The midpoint of slice `k` is `(k + 0.5) * dt`.
hint: Accumulate `f(t) * math.exp(-rate * t) * dt` in a loop over the slices.
solution: Evaluate the discounted flow at the midpoint of each thin slice and multiply by the slice width; the tests compare the sum with the closed forms for constant and growing streams, and a zero rate checks the undiscounted total.
