@article
A **function** assigns to each input in its **domain** exactly one output. In finance, functions are everywhere: the balance $A(t)$ of an account after $t$ years, the total cost $C(q)$ of producing $q$ units, the price of a bond as a function of its yield. Writing a quantity as a function lets you ask how it changes, which is what calculus is for.

### Limits

Suppose you want the value a function *approaches* as the input gets close to a number $a$, even if the function is not defined at $a$. We write
$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$$
when $f(x)$ can be made as close to $L$ as you like by taking $x$ close enough to $a$ (but not equal to $a$). The limit ignores what happens exactly at $a$.

The standard algebraic tool is to cancel a factor that causes the $0/0$ form. For $f(x) = \frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3}$, the function is undefined at $x = 3$, but for $x \neq 3$ it equals $x + 3$, so $\lim_{x\to 3} f(x) = 6$. When a square root causes the trouble, multiply top and bottom by the conjugate.

A function is **continuous** at $a$ if $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$: no jump, gap or hole. Polynomials and exponentials are continuous everywhere; a rational function is continuous wherever its denominator is nonzero.

### From periodic to continuous compounding

Invest principal $P$ at a nominal annual rate $r$ (a decimal) compounded $m$ times per year. After $t$ years the balance is
$$A = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{m}\right)^{mt}.$$
More frequent compounding earns slightly more, but the gain shrinks. Take $P = \$5{,}000$, $r = 6\%$, $t = 3$:

| Compounding | $m$ | Balance after 3 years |
|---|---|---|
| Annual | 1 | \$5,955.08 |
| Quarterly | 4 | \$5,978.09 |
| Monthly | 12 | \$5,983.40 |
| Daily | 365 | \$5,986.00 |

The balances climb toward a ceiling. To find it, let $n = m/r$, so $m = nr$ and $\left(1 + \frac{r}{m}\right)^{mt} = \left[\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^{n}\right]^{rt}$. The number $e \approx 2.71828$ is defined by $\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1 + \frac1n\right)^n = e$. As $m \to \infty$, $n \to \infty$ as well, so
$$\lim_{m\to\infty} P\left(1 + \frac{r}{m}\right)^{mt} = Pe^{rt}.$$
This is the **continuous-compounding** formula. In the table, $5{,}000\,e^{0.18} = \$5{,}986.09$, just above the daily figure.

### Effective annual rate

The **effective annual rate** (EAR) is the single annual rate that gives the same balance after one year. With $m$ periods, $\text{EAR} = (1 + r/m)^m - 1$; with continuous compounding, $\text{EAR} = e^{r} - 1$. A nominal 6% compounded continuously is an EAR of $e^{0.06} - 1 = 6.18\%$. Always compare accounts on EAR, never on nominal rates with different compounding conventions.

Continuous compounding is a model: real accounts compound at discrete dates. Its value is that exponentials are far easier to differentiate and integrate than powers of $(1 + r/m)$, which is why derivatives pricing and continuous-time finance are built on it. In this course, rates are decimals in formulas and percents when quoted.

@example
title: Compounding more often, and then continuously
problem: Larkspur Outfitters deposits \$5,000 at a nominal annual rate of 6%. Find the balance after 3 years with quarterly compounding and with continuous compounding, and the extra interest that continuous compounding earns.
step: Quarterly compounding has $m = 4$ and $mt = 12$ periods at $r/m = 0.015$ per period: $A = 5000(1.015)^{12} = \$5{,}978.09$.
step: Continuous compounding uses $A = Pe^{rt}$ with $rt = 0.06 \times 3 = 0.18$: $A = 5000\,e^{0.18} = \$5{,}986.09$.
step: The extra interest is $5{,}986.09 - 5{,}978.09 = \$8.00$ over three years. The gain from compounding more often is small and bounded by the continuous limit.
answer: Quarterly \$5,978.09; continuous \$5,986.09; difference about \$8.00.

@example
title: A limit with a removable hole
problem: Find $\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^2 + x - 12}{x - 3}$.
step: Substituting $x = 3$ gives $\frac{0}{0}$, so the expression is undefined at $3$ but a limit may still exist.
step: Factor the numerator: $x^2 + x - 12 = (x + 4)(x - 3)$.
step: For $x \neq 3$, cancel $(x - 3)$ to get $x + 4$.
step: The limit ignores $x = 3$ itself, so it equals $3 + 4 = 7$.
answer: 7

@mistakes
- Using the nominal rate $r$ as the rate per period instead of $r/m$ when compounding $m$ times a year.
- Believing that compounding faster has no ceiling: the balance converges to $Pe^{rt}$, it does not grow without bound.
- Saying a limit "does not exist" because the function is undefined at the point; the limit depends only on nearby values.
- Entering the rate as a percent (6) instead of a decimal (0.06) in $e^{rt}$.

@practice num
prompt: Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 4} \frac{x^2 - 16}{x - 4}$.
answer: 8
tol: 0.001
check: ((4 + 1e-7)**2 - 16) / 1e-7
hint: Substituting $x = 4$ gives $0/0$. Factor the numerator.
hint: $x^2 - 16 = (x - 4)(x + 4)$. Cancel and substitute.
solution: For $x \neq 4$ the quotient equals $x + 4$, so the limit is $4 + 4 = 8$.

@practice num
prompt: Cedar Ridge Mills deposits \$20,000 for 10 years at a nominal 6% per year. Account X compounds monthly and account Y compounds continuously. How much more does account Y hold at the end, to the nearest cent?
answer: 54.44
tol: 0.01
lead: \$
check: 20000 * (exp(0.06 * 10) - (1 + 0.06 / 12) ** 120)
hint: Find each balance separately. Monthly compounding has $12 \times 10 = 120$ periods at $0.06/12$ per period.
hint: $X = 20000(1.005)^{120}$ and $Y = 20000\,e^{0.6}$; subtract.
solution: $X = 20000 \times 1.819397 = \$36{,}387.93$ and $Y = 20000 \times 1.822119 = \$36{,}442.38$, so $Y - X \approx \$54.44$.

@quiz num
prompt: Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{x + 9} - 3}{x}$. Give a decimal to three places.
answer: 1/6
tol: 0.001
check: (sqrt(1e-6 + 9) - 3) / 1e-6
hint: Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate $\sqrt{x+9} + 3$.
hint: The numerator becomes $(x + 9) - 9 = x$, which cancels with the denominator.
solution: The expression equals $\frac{x}{x(\sqrt{x+9} + 3)} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x+9} + 3}$ for $x \neq 0$, and its limit is $\frac{1}{3 + 3} = \frac16 \approx 0.167$.

@quiz expr
prompt: A principal $P$ is invested at a nominal annual rate $r$ (a decimal), compounded $m$ times per year, for $t$ years. Write the final balance $A$.
vars: P, r, m, t
lead: $A =$
answer: P*(1 + r/m)^(m*t)
check: lambda P, r, m, t: P * (1 + r / m) ** (m * t)

hint: Each period earns the rate $r/m$, and there are $mt$ periods.
