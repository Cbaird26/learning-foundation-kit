@article
Why does the price of strawberries drop in June and rise in December? Because the amount farmers want to sell and the amount shoppers want to buy change, and the price moves until the two match.

### Demand and the law of demand

**Quantity demanded** is the amount buyers are willing and able to buy at a given price. The **law of demand** says that, holding everything else constant, a higher price lowers quantity demanded, so the **demand curve** slopes down. Suppose the demand for pizzas in a town is $Q_d = 100 - 2P$ (pizzas per day, with $P$ in dollars). At $P = 20$, $Q_d = 60$.

### Supply and the law of supply

**Quantity supplied** is the amount sellers are willing to sell at a given price. The **law of supply** says that a higher price raises quantity supplied, so the **supply curve** slopes up. Suppose $Q_s = -20 + 4P$. At $P = 20$, $Q_s = 60$.

### Equilibrium

**Equilibrium** is the price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. Set $100 - 2P = -20 + 4P$, so $120 = 6P$, $P^* = 20$ and $Q^* = 60$.

- At a price **above** equilibrium, say \$25, $Q_d = 50$ and $Q_s = 80$: a **surplus** of 30, which pushes the price down.
- At a price **below** equilibrium, say \$15, $Q_d = 70$ and $Q_s = 40$: a **shortage** of 30, which pushes the price up.

### Movement along versus shift of a curve

A change in the good's **own price** moves you *along* a curve. A change in anything else shifts the whole curve.

- **Demand shifters:** consumers' income (for a **normal good** demand rises with income; for an **inferior good** it falls), prices of **substitutes** and **complements**, tastes, expectations about future prices and the number of buyers.
- **Supply shifters:** input prices, technology, the number of sellers, taxes and subsidies, expectations and weather.

An increase in demand shifts the curve right: equilibrium price and quantity both rise. An increase in supply shifts it right: price falls and quantity rises. When both curves move, one of the two outcomes is certain and the other depends on which shift is larger (both increase: quantity rises, price is ambiguous).

### Price ceilings

A **price ceiling** is a legal maximum price. If it is set below equilibrium, it is binding and creates a shortage: at a \$15 ceiling in the market above, buyers want 70 pizzas and sellers offer only 40. A ceiling at or above \$20 has no effect. Economists weigh the benefit to those who can buy at the low price against the shortage, lower quality and search costs it can cause, and policymakers sometimes accept those trade-offs for goals such as affordability.

### Reading the news

To predict a price move: (1) decide whether the event affects buyers or sellers, (2) decide which curve shifts and in which direction, (3) read the new equilibrium. Draw the curves in your head, as the site shows no graphs.

@example
title: A rise in demand
problem: In the pizza market $Q_d = 100 - 2P$ and $Q_s = -20 + 4P$. A new office park raises demand to $Q_d = 130 - 2P$. Find the new equilibrium.
step: Set quantity demanded equal to quantity supplied: $130 - 2P = -20 + 4P$.
step: Solve: $150 = 6P$, so $P^* = 25$.
step: Quantity: $Q^* = -20 + 4(25) = 80$. Check with demand: $130 - 2(25) = 80$.
step: The demand curve shifted right, so both price (\$20 to \$25) and quantity (60 to 80) increased. The supply curve did not shift: the price rise is a movement along it.
answer: $P^* = \$25$ and $Q^* = 80$.

@example
title: A binding price ceiling
problem: With $Q_d = 100 - 2P$ and $Q_s = -20 + 4P$, the town caps the pizza price at \$15. What is the result?
step: The equilibrium price is \$20, so a \$15 ceiling is below it and binds.
step: At \$15, $Q_d = 100 - 30 = 70$.
step: At \$15, $Q_s = -20 + 60 = 40$.
step: Only 40 pizzas are sold, so the shortage is $70 - 40 = 30$ pizzas. The quantity traded is determined by the smaller side, supply.
answer: A shortage of 30 pizzas, with only 40 sold.

@mistakes
- Shifting the curve when the good's own price changes: a price change is a movement along the curve.
- Saying that an increase in supply and an increase in demand both make the price rise or fall without checking directions: if both increase, quantity rises but the price change is ambiguous.
- Mixing up which curve a shifter moves: input prices move supply, and income or the price of a substitute moves demand.
- Calling a shortage the result of any price above equilibrium: above equilibrium there is a surplus.
