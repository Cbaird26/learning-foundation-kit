@article
Your first slice of pizza is great, the second is good, the fourth is a chore. And a pizzeria that tries to serve more customers with one oven finds that each extra cook adds less than the one before. Both ideas, diminishing marginal utility and diminishing marginal returns, drive the curves of demand and supply.

### Utility and the consumer

**Utility** is the satisfaction a consumer gets from consuming goods. **Marginal utility (MU)** is the extra utility from one more unit. The **law of diminishing marginal utility** says that, as you consume more of a good in a period, its marginal utility eventually falls.

A consumer has a **budget constraint**: spending on all goods cannot exceed income. The consumer maximizes total utility by choosing the bundle, within the budget, that satisfies the **utility-maximizing rule**:

$$\frac{MU_A}{P_A} = \frac{MU_B}{P_B}$$

(extra utility per dollar is equal across goods), and the budget is spent entirely. If $MU_A/P_A$ were higher than $MU_B/P_B$, shifting a dollar from B to A would raise utility. Since a lower price raises $MU/P$, the consumer buys more: this is one reason demand slopes down.

### Costs of production

In the **short run** at least one input (such as the factory) is fixed; in the **long run** all inputs can change. The short-run cost concepts for output $Q$ are:

- **Total fixed cost (TFC)** does not change with output; **total variable cost (TVC)** does. $TC = TFC + TVC$.
- **Marginal cost** $MC = \Delta TC/\Delta Q$, the cost of one more unit.
- **Average fixed cost** $AFC = TFC/Q$, **average variable cost** $AVC = TVC/Q$ and **average total cost** $ATC = TC/Q = AFC + AVC$.

The **law of diminishing marginal returns** says that, when one input is added to fixed amounts of the others, its marginal product eventually falls. That is why $MC$ eventually rises with output.

**Marginal cost and average cost:** if $MC$ is below an average, the average falls; if $MC$ is above it, the average rises. So $MC$ cuts through both $AVC$ and $ATC$ at their minimum points. **Sunk costs** (such as fixed costs already paid) should not enter a decision about the next unit. In the long run, **economies of scale** lower the ATC as a firm grows, while **diseconomies of scale** raise it at very large sizes.

@example
title: Spending a budget wisely
problem: Ruth has \$50 to spend each month on pizzas (\$5 each) and movies (\$10 each). Her marginal utility (in utils) from each additional unit is in the table. Which bundle maximizes her utility?
| Unit | MU of pizza | MU/P (pizza) | MU of movie | MU/P (movie) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | 50 | 10 | 80 | 8 |
| 2nd | 40 | 8 | 60 | 6 |
| 3rd | 30 | 6 | 40 | 4 |
| 4th | 20 | 4 | 20 | 2 |
| 5th | 10 | 2 | — | — |
step: Divide each marginal utility by its price to get utils per dollar (the MU/P columns).
step: Buy units in order of highest MU/P while the budget allows: pizza 1 (10, \$5), pizza 2 and movie 1 (both 8; \$5 and \$10), pizza 3 and movie 2 (both 6), then pizza 4 and movie 3 (both 4). Total spending is $4 \times \$5 + 3 \times \$10 = \$50$, so the budget is used up.
step: At 4 pizzas and 3 movies, $MU/P = 4$ for both goods, so the rule holds. Total utility is $(50+40+30+20) + (80+60+40) = 140 + 180 = 320$ utils.
answer: 4 pizzas and 3 movies (320 utils).

@example
title: A firm's cost table
problem: A bakery has fixed cost \$60 a day. Its total variable cost at output 0 to 5 trays is 0, 30, 50, 75, 105 and 145. Compute TC, MC, AVC and ATC.
| Trays | TVC | TC | MC | AVC | ATC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30 | 90 | 30 | 30.00 | 90.00 |
| 2 | 50 | 110 | 20 | 25.00 | 55.00 |
| 3 | 75 | 135 | 25 | 25.00 | 45.00 |
| 4 | 105 | 165 | 30 | 26.25 | 41.25 |
| 5 | 145 | 205 | 40 | 29.00 | 41.00 |
step: $TC = 60 + TVC$, so TC is 60 at zero trays, then 90, 110, 135, 165 and 205.
step: $MC$ is the change in TC: 30, 20, 25, 30, 40. MC first falls (specialization) and then rises (diminishing marginal returns).
step: $AVC = TVC/Q$ and $ATC = TC/Q$: for 4 trays, $105/4 = 26.25$ and $165/4 = 41.25$.
step: Check the rule: from 2 to 3 trays, MC is 25 and equals AVC (25), the minimum of AVC. From 4 to 5 trays MC (40) is still below ATC (41.25 falling to 41.00), so ATC is still falling.
answer: The table above; MC meets AVC at its minimum and ATC keeps falling while MC is below it.

@mistakes
- Equating marginal utility itself across goods instead of marginal utility per dollar.
- Including fixed costs in marginal cost: $MC$ depends only on how total cost changes, and fixed cost does not change.
- Saying that average cost rises whenever marginal cost rises: average cost rises only when MC is above it.
- Treating a sunk cost, or total utility, as the guide to the next unit instead of the marginal benefit and marginal cost.

prompt: A shop has total fixed cost of \$200 and a total variable cost of \$480 when it produces 8 units. What is its average total cost per unit, in dollars?
answer: 85
lead: \$
check: (200 + 480) / 8
hint: Total cost is fixed plus variable cost.
solution: $TC = 200 + 480 = \$680$ and $ATC = 680/8 = \$85$. (Average variable cost is \$60 and average fixed cost \$25.)

lead: \$
check: 8 / (24 / 6)

solution: Burgers give $24/6 = 4$ utils per dollar. Fries must give the same: $8/P = 4$, so $P = \$2$.
