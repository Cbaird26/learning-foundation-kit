@review
### Week-by-week key ideas

1. **Scarcity and opportunity cost.** Every choice gives up the best alternative; the PPF shows attainable, efficient and unattainable points; comparative advantage (lower opportunity cost) explains gains from trade.
2. **Supply and demand.** Equilibrium is where $Q_d = Q_s$; shifters move curves, own price moves along them; a binding price ceiling creates a shortage.
3. **Elasticity.** Midpoint elasticity; elastic ($>1$) means price and revenue move in opposite directions; income and cross-price elasticities by sign.
4. **Consumer choice and costs.** $MU_A/P_A = MU_B/P_B$; $MC$ cuts $AVC$ and $ATC$ at their minimums.
5. **Market structures.** Produce where $MR = MC$; a competitive firm has $MR = P$; a monopoly charges above MC and has a deadweight loss.
6. **Market failure.** Efficient quantity at $MSB = MSC$; Pigouvian tax or subsidy; public goods and common resources.
7. **GDP.** $C + I + G + (X - M)$; real GDP from the deflator; growth rates.
8. **Unemployment and inflation.** Unemployment and participation rates; CPI and inflation; Fisher equation.
9. **AD-AS and fiscal policy.** Gaps; multipliers; deficits versus debt.
10. **Money and banking.** Multiplier $1/rr$; balance sheets; the Fed's tools.
11. **Monetary policy.** Rates, investment, AD; bond prices; Taylor rule; $MV = PY$.
12. **Trade and exchange rates.** Tariffs and deadweight loss; exchange-rate conversion; a stronger dollar lowers net exports.

### Key formulas

- Midpoint elasticity: $\dfrac{\Delta Q/\bar{Q}}{\Delta P/\bar{P}}$ using averages of the two points.
- $ATC = TC/Q$, $MC = \Delta TC/\Delta Q$, profit $= (P - ATC) \times Q$.
- Real GDP $= \dfrac{\text{nominal GDP}}{\text{deflator}} \times 100$; inflation $= \dfrac{\text{CPI}_2 - \text{CPI}_1}{\text{CPI}_1}$; $1 + i = (1 + r)(1 + \pi)$.
- Spending multiplier $\dfrac{1}{1 - MPC}$; tax multiplier $-\dfrac{MPC}{1 - MPC}$; money multiplier $\dfrac{1}{rr}$.
- Zero-coupon price $= \dfrac{F}{(1 + y)^n}$.

### Self-check

- Can you shift a curve correctly for any news event and say what happens to price and quantity?
- Can you compute an equilibrium, an elasticity, a monopoly outcome and a deadweight loss from equations?
- Can you go from nominal GDP and a deflator to real growth, and from a CPI to inflation?
- Can you trace a deposit through the banking system, and a policy rate change through to AD?
- Can you explain, without notes, how a stronger dollar affects exports?

### Common errors

- Shifting a curve when only the own price changed.
- Reading price off marginal revenue, not demand, for a monopolist.
- Dividing unemployment by the population, not the labor force.
- Using nominal GDP to measure growth, or mixing the signs of the exchange rate.
- Using multipliers without stating the fixed-price-level assumption.

@exam num
prompt: An economy has nominal GDP of 20,400 billion dollars and a GDP deflator of 102 (base year = 100). Potential real GDP is 20,600 billion dollars. The marginal propensity to consume is 0.75 and the price level is fixed. By how many billion dollars should taxes be cut to close the gap (assume the tax cut is the only policy)?
answer: 200
unit: billion dollars
check: (20600 - 20400 / 102 * 100) / (0.75 / (1 - 0.75))
hint: First find real GDP, then the gap, then use the tax multiplier.
hint: Real GDP is 20,000, so the gap is 600; the tax multiplier has size $0.75/0.25 = 3$.
solution: Real GDP is $20{,}400/102 \times 100 = 20{,}000$ billion. The gap is $20{,}600 - 20{,}000 = 600$. The tax multiplier has size $MPC/(1 - MPC) = 3$, so a tax cut of $600/3 = 200$ billion closes it, assuming a fixed price level and no crowding out.

@exam self
prompt: Suppose the Federal Reserve raises its target range for the federal funds rate in an economy that is above potential output. Using the models from this course, trace the effects through interest rates, investment, aggregate demand, real GDP, the price level and the exchange rate and net exports. Then give one reason economists might disagree about how strongly this policy works.
hint: Follow the chain: policy rate, market rates, investment and interest-sensitive spending, AD, then the exchange rate.
solution: Higher policy rates raise market interest rates, including real rates. Investment and interest-sensitive consumption fall, so aggregate demand shifts left, reducing real GDP toward potential and lowering inflationary pressure on the price level. Higher US rates also attract foreign funds, so the dollar appreciates, which lowers exports and raises imports, reducing net exports and reinforcing the fall in AD. Economists may disagree because of lags (effects take time and vary), because expectations of inflation can change how much real rates move, and because they estimate the output gap and the neutral rate with uncertainty. The result also depends on the assumption of an economy above potential: if the economy were in recession, tightening would worsen unemployment.
rubric: Correct direction for interest rates, investment, aggregate demand and real GDP.
rubric: Explains the exchange-rate channel: appreciation lowers net exports.
rubric: States the effect on the price level and the role of the output gap.
rubric: Gives a valid reason for disagreement, such as lags, expectations or uncertainty in estimating the gap.
