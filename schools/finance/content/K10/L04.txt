@lesson
title: Comparing prices: which is the better buy?
summary: Find the cost of one item from a pack price and decide which pack is the better buy.
objective: Find the price of one item when you know the price of a pack.
objective: Compare two packs by the price of one.
objective: Buy three items in a pretend store and make change.
reading: Money as You Grow (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau): look up the activities about spending wisely and comparing prices.

@article
Grown-up: have some pretend items ready for a store. Price tags help.

Two stores sell apples. Store A sells 4 apples for \$2. Store B sells 5 apples for \$3. Which is the better buy?

You cannot tell just by looking, because the packs are not the same size. So find the price of **one** apple in each pack.

### Price of one

To find the price of one, **share the money equally** among the items. That is dividing.

Store A: \$2 is 200¢. Share 200¢ among 4 apples. Each apple is 50¢.

Store B: \$3 is 300¢. Share 300¢ among 5 apples. Each apple is 60¢.

An apple at Store A costs less. Store A is the **better buy**: you pay less for each apple.

### Rules for a better buy

- Compare the price of **one** item, not the price of the pack.
- A lower price for one is the better buy.
- Ask: do I really need that many? A big pack is not a good deal if you throw some away.
- Ask: is it the same kind of thing? A cheap thing that breaks right away is not a good buy.

### Play store

Now use what you know. In a pretend store, add up what you buy, pay, and count the change. Doing it all together is great practice.

@example
title: Which pencils?
problem: 3 pencils cost 90¢. 5 pencils cost \$1.25. Which pack is the better buy?
step: Pack of 3: 90¢ shared among 3 is 30¢ for each pencil.
step: Pack of 5: \$1.25 is 125¢. Shared among 5 is 25¢ for each pencil.
step: 25¢ is less than 30¢, so the pack of 5 is the better buy.
answer: The pack of 5 pencils.

@example
title: Same price for each?
problem: A bag of 10 stickers costs \$1. A bag of 5 stickers costs 50¢. Which is the better buy?
step: Bag of 10: 100¢ shared among 10 is 10¢ each.
step: Bag of 5: 50¢ shared among 5 is 10¢ each.
step: Both are 10¢ each, so the price for each sticker is the same.
answer: They are the same: 10¢ for each sticker.

@mistakes
- Picking the pack with the smaller price tag. A smaller pack can cost more for each item.
- Dividing the wrong way around. Share the money among the items.
- Forgetting that you only need as many items as you will use.

@practice num
prompt: Store One sells 5 balloons for 90¢. Store Two sells 4 balloons for 60¢. How many cents less does one balloon cost at the better store?
answer: 3
unit: ¢
check: 90 // 5 - 60 // 4
hint: Find the price of one balloon at each store first.
hint: Store One is 90 shared by 5. Store Two is 60 shared by 4. Then subtract.
solution: Store One: 90 / 5 = 18¢ each. Store Two: 60 / 4 = 15¢ each. Store Two is the better buy, and 18 - 15 = 3, so a balloon costs 3¢ less there.

@practice self
prompt: Play store. With your grown-up, set up a pretend store with at least four things and price tags. Buy three items. Add up the total. Pay with a pretend \$5 bill or other pretend money. Make change by counting up. Your grown-up checks the total and the change.
hint: Add the prices one at a time. Then count up from the total to what you paid.
solution: A good answer lists the three items with their prices, the total, the money paid, and the change. For example, items at 75¢, 90¢ and 60¢ total \$2.25. Paying with a \$5 bill, the change is \$2.75.
rubric: Three items and their prices are written down, and the total is added correctly.
rubric: The money paid is at least the total.
rubric: The change is counted up from the total and the grown-up agrees it is correct.
