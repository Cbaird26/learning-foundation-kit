@article
Grown-up: use pretend money or a jar with a few real coins if you like. This lesson is about the idea, not about the family's money.

To **save** means to keep money for later instead of spending it now. A **savings goal** is something you save for. It could be a toy, a book or a game.

### Three questions

1. **How much does it cost?** That is the goal amount.
2. **How much do I have now?**
3. **How much more do I need?** Take the money you have away from the goal.

### A savings jar

Put your saved money in a jar. Each time you add money, you can see the pile grow. It is like climbing stairs. Each step takes you closer to the top.

### How many weeks?

If you save the same amount every week, you can plan. Divide the money you still need by the amount you save each week.

A toy costs \$12. You have \$4. You still need 12 - 4 = 8 dollars. You save \$2 each week. Then 8 divided by 2 is 4. It will take 4 weeks.

### Tips

- Save a little each week. Small steps add up.
- Put the jar where you can see it, but do not open it for small wants.
- If you save more each week, you reach your goal sooner.
- If a goal takes too long, pick a smaller goal first or find a way to earn a bit more.

@example
title: How many weeks?
problem: You want a toy that costs \$12. You have \$4 and save \$2 each week. How many weeks will it take?
step: How much more do you need? 12 - 4 = 8 dollars.
step: You save \$2 each week. Share 8 into groups of 2: 8 / 2 = 4.
step: Check by counting: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12. That is 4 weeks of saving \$2.
answer: 4 weeks.

@example
title: Saving small amounts
problem: You want a \$3 book. You save 50¢ each week. How many weeks will it take?
step: \$3 is 300¢. Count by fifties: 50, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300.
step: You said six numbers, so it is 6 weeks.
answer: 6 weeks.

@mistakes
- Forgetting to take away what you already have before dividing.
- Dividing the goal by the weekly amount when you already have some money saved.
- Giving up too soon. Small, steady saving works.

answer: 4

@practice num
prompt: You save \$2 each week for 5 weeks. How many dollars do you have?
answer: 10
lead: \$
check: 2 * 5
hint: Count by twos, five times.
solution: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. You have saved \$10.

answer: 3
unit: weeks
check: (15 - 3) // 4
hint: First find how much more you need.
hint: You need 15 - 3 = 12. Share 12 into groups of 4.
solution: You need 15 - 3 = 12 dollars. Saving \$4 a week: 4, 8, 12. It takes 3 weeks.

@practice mc
prompt: Which choice helps you reach your savings goal sooner?
choice*: Saving a little more each week
choice: Spending some of your savings jar on candy
choice: Picking a goal that costs a lot more
hint: What makes the jar fill faster?
solution: Saving more each week makes the jar fill faster. Spending from the jar or picking a more expensive goal makes the goal take longer.

@practice self
prompt: Pick a pretend savings goal with a price under \$20, like a toy or a book. Write the price. Choose how much you start with and how much you save each week. Make a table that shows each week until you reach the goal. Your grown-up checks the adding.
hint: Make columns for Week, Saved this week and Total saved.
solution: A good table starts at the amount already saved and adds the weekly amount each row until the total reaches the price. For a \$10 goal with \$2 saved and \$2 a week: week 1 \$4, week 2 \$6, week 3 \$8, week 4 \$10, so 4 weeks.
rubric: The goal and its price are named, and the price is under \$20.
rubric: The table adds the same amount each week and the totals are correct.
rubric: The table ends when the total reaches the goal, and the number of weeks is stated.

@quiz num
prompt: Your goal is \$18. You have \$6 and save \$3 each week. How many weeks will it take?
answer: 4
unit: weeks
check: (18 - 6) // 3
hint: How much more do you need? Then share it into groups of 3.
solution: You need 18 - 6 = 12 dollars. Saving \$3 a week: 3, 6, 9, 12. It takes 4 weeks.

@quiz num

answer: 25
unit: ¢
check: 100 - 75
hint: Count up from 75 to 100 with a quarter.
solution: From 75¢ to 100¢ you need one more quarter, 25¢.
