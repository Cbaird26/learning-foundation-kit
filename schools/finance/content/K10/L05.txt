@lesson
title: Needs and wants
summary: Tell the difference between needs and wants, and spend on needs first.
objective: Say what a need is and what a want is.
objective: Sort everyday things into needs and wants.
objective: Use pretend money to pay for needs before wants.
reading: Money as You Grow (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau): look up the activities about making spending choices.

@article
Grown-up: read this aloud and stop to talk. Some things are not clear-cut, and it is fine to discuss them.

### Needs

A **need** is something you must have to stay safe and healthy. Needs are things like:

- food and clean water,
- a home to sleep in,
- clothes that keep you warm and dry,
- medicine and a doctor when you are sick,
- things for school, like a pencil and paper.

### Wants

A **want** is something that is nice to have, but you can live without it. Wants are things like:

- a new toy,
- candy or a treat,
- a video game,
- a sparkly backpack.

Wants are not bad! They make life fun. But we do not need them to stay safe and healthy.

### Some things are in between

You need a warm coat in winter. That is a need. But you do not need a coat with lots of sparkles. The sparkles are a want. A need can come with wants stuck on.

### Why does it matter?

Money does not last forever. When you have only a little, you spend on **needs first**. Then, if some money is left, you can pick a want.

Ask yourself two questions before you buy. "Do I need it?" and "What happens if I do not get it?"

@example
title: Needs first
problem: You have \$10 of pretend money. Lunch costs \$4. A school notebook costs \$3. How much is left for wants?
step: Lunch and the notebook are needs. Pay for them first.
step: Add the needs: 4 + 3 = 7. That is \$7.
step: Take that from \$10: 10 - 7 = 3.
answer: \$3 is left for wants.

@example
title: Need or want?
problem: Is a bottle of water a need or a want? Is a toy robot a need or a want?
step: Ask "Do I need it to stay healthy?" You need water to live.
step: Ask the same about the robot. You can live without it, and it is fun.
step: Water is a need. The toy robot is a want.
answer: Water is a need and the toy robot is a want.

@mistakes
- Saying a want is a need because you want it a lot. A strong wish does not make it a need.
- Thinking wants are bad. It is okay to buy wants after the needs are paid for.
- Forgetting that a need can come with wants added on, like sparkles on a coat.

prompt: Which of these is a need?
choice*: Clean water to drink
choice: A new video game

hint: Ask yourself: do I need this to stay safe and healthy?
solution: Clean water is a need because people cannot live without it. A video game and a toy robot are wants.

choice*: A warm coat in winter

hint: Which things keep you fed, warm, safe or healthy?
solution: Soup (food) and a warm coat (clothes) are needs. Candy and a video game are wants.

@practice num
prompt: You have \$10 of pretend money. Lunch costs \$4 and a school notebook costs \$3. These are needs. How many dollars are left for wants?
answer: 3
lead: \$
check: 10 - (4 + 3)
hint: Add the two needs first.
solution: The needs cost 4 + 3 = 7 dollars. Then 10 - 7 = 3. There is \$3 left for wants.

@practice mc
prompt: Mia needs a warm coat for winter. Which choice puts needs first?
choice*: Buy the warm coat first, then think about a sparkly extra
choice: Buy a toy first and hope there is enough money for the coat
choice: Skip the coat and buy only sparkly things
hint: A need comes before a want.
solution: The warm coat is the need, so it comes first. Extras and toys are wants and can wait until the need is paid for.

@practice self
prompt: With your grown-up, find three needs and three wants in your home or in a picture book. Say each one out loud and tell why it is a need or a want. Write or draw them in two columns.
hint: A need keeps you safe and healthy. A want is nice to have.
solution: A good answer has two columns with three items each, such as needs: food, a bed, a coat; wants: a game, candy, a toy. Each choice has a reason, like "I need food to grow."
rubric: There are three needs and three wants in two separate columns.
rubric: Each need helps stay safe or healthy.
rubric: The child says a reason for each choice, with the grown-up helping.

@quiz num
prompt: You have \$15 of pretend money. Dinner food costs \$6 and school shoes cost \$7. These are needs. How many dollars are left for wants?
answer: 2
lead: \$
check: 15 - 6 - 7
hint: Add the needs: 6 and 7.
solution: Needs cost 6 + 7 = 13. Then 15 - 13 = 2. There is \$2 left for wants.
