@article
Grown-up: talk about ways your family shares or helps others. For this lesson, any giving is pretend or done with things and time, not with the family's real money.

To **give** means to let someone have something without getting anything back. To **share** means to let someone use part of what you have.

### Ways to give

You do not need money to give.

- **Time**: reading to a younger child or helping a neighbor.
- **Things**: toys or books you no longer use, which you can give to someone who will use them.
- **Kind words**: a card that says thank you.
- **Money**: a little from your Give jar.

### Charities

A **charity** is a group that works to help people, animals or places. Some charities give food to families. Some take care of animals. When many people give a little, together they can help a lot.

### Sharing equally

When you share fairly, everyone gets the same amount. To find it, **divide**.

You have 12 stickers and 4 friends. Share 12 equally among 4: 12 / 4 = 3. Each friend gets 3 stickers.

You can check: 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12.

### Giving is a choice

You give after needs are paid for. Your Give jar is the place to start. Talk with your grown-up about what you want to give and why it matters to you.

@example
title: Share the Give jar
problem: Your Give jar has \$9. You want to share it equally among 3 groups that help people. How much does each group get?
step: Share \$9 equally among 3. Think: 3 groups times what equals 9?
step: 3 x 3 = 9, so each group gets \$3.
step: Check: 3 + 3 + 3 = 9.
answer: \$3 for each group.

@example
title: Fair stickers
problem: You have 12 stickers to share equally among 4 friends. How many stickers does each friend get?
step: Divide 12 by 4. Count by fours: 4, 8, 12. That is 3 fours.
step: Each friend gets 3 stickers.
answer: 3 stickers each.

@mistakes
- Thinking you can only give if you have money. Time and kindness count too.
- Sharing so that one friend gets more. Fair sharing means equal parts.
- Giving away money that is needed for something you must buy. Needs come first.

@practice mc
prompt: Which is a way to give without using any money?
choice: Buying more candy for yourself
choice*: Helping a neighbor carry groceries
choice: Keeping all your toys in a box
hint: Giving can use time and kindness.
solution: Helping a neighbor gives your time and help, so it costs no money. The other choices do not give anything to anyone.

@practice num
prompt: Your Give jar has \$8. You give half of it away. How many dollars do you give?
answer: 4
lead: \$
check: 8 // 2
hint: Half means split into 2 equal parts.
solution: 8 split into 2 equal parts is 4 and 4. You give \$4.

@practice num
prompt: You have 15 stickers to share equally among 5 friends. How many stickers does each friend get?
answer: 3
unit: stickers
check: 15 // 5
hint: Count by fives until you reach 15.
solution: 5, 10, 15 are three fives, so each friend gets 3 stickers.

@practice num
prompt: You earn \$10. You save \$5, spend \$3 and put the rest in your Give jar. How many dollars go in the Give jar?
answer: 2
lead: \$
check: 10 - 5 - 3
hint: Add the save and spend amounts first.
solution: 5 + 3 = 8. Then 10 - 8 = 2. The Give jar gets \$2.

@practice self
prompt: Plan one pretend or real way to give with your grown-up. It can be time, a thing you no longer use or a kind note. Write or draw who you want to help, what you will give, and how it will help them. Your grown-up decides if and when you really do it.
hint: Think about who needs help and what you could do or give.
solution: A good answer names a person or group, what is given, and one way it helps. For example, "I will give a book I have finished to the library box so another child can read it."
rubric: The plan says who will be helped.
rubric: The plan says what will be given (time, a thing, a kind note or pretend money).
rubric: The child explains how it helps, with the grown-up's help.
