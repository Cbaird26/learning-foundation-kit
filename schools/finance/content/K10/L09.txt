@article
Grown-up: the bank in this lesson is a pretend bank, a notebook you keep at home. Your child does not need to give any real account details to anyone.

A **bank** is a safe place to keep money. A bank has strong vaults and careful rules, so your money is not lost or stolen the way it could be in a pocket or a drawer.

### Accounts

When you put money in a bank, it goes into an **account**. The bank writes down how much is yours. The amount in your account is your **balance**.

### Deposit and withdrawal

- A **deposit** is putting money in. The balance goes **up**.
- A **withdrawal** is taking money out. The balance goes **down**.

You start with a balance of \$5. You deposit \$3. Add: 5 + 3 = 8. The balance is \$8. Then you withdraw \$2. Take away: 8 - 2 = 6. The balance is now \$6.

### Why use a bank?

- Your money is kept safe.
- The bank keeps a record, so you know your balance.
- It is harder to spend by accident when the money is not in your pocket.
- Some accounts pay a little extra, called interest. You learn about that next week.

In the United States, a government group called the **FDIC** protects money in insured banks. If an insured bank has trouble, the FDIC protects each person's deposits up to \$250,000 at each insured bank. That is a lot of protection, much more than you will have for a long time.

### A pretend bank notebook

Make columns for the date, deposit, withdrawal and balance. Every time money goes in or out, write a new line.

@example
title: Deposit and withdrawal
problem: Your balance is \$5. You deposit \$3 and then withdraw \$2. What is the new balance?
step: Deposit means put in. Add: 5 + 3 = 8.
step: Withdraw means take out. Subtract: 8 - 2 = 6.
step: The balance is \$6.
answer: \$6

@example
title: A notebook line by line
problem: Your pretend account starts at \$4. Fill in the balance after each line.
step: Line 1, deposit \$2: the balance is 4 + 2 = \$6.
step: Line 2, withdrawal \$1: the balance is 6 - 1 = \$5.
step: Line 3, deposit \$4: the balance is 5 + 4 = \$9.
answer: \$6, then \$5, then \$9.

@mistakes
- Adding on a withdrawal. A withdrawal makes the balance smaller.
- Thinking the bank keeps your very own coins in a special box. The bank keeps a record of what is yours.
- Forgetting to write down each deposit and withdrawal.
