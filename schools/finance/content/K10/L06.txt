@article
Grown-up: read this aloud. Talk about jobs you know, such as a baker, a nurse or a bus driver.

To **earn** money means to get paid for doing work. People have **jobs**. A baker bakes bread. A bus driver drives people where they need to go. A teacher helps children learn. They get money for their work.

### Kids can earn too

Some children earn money by helping. A neighbor might pay you to water plants. Some families pay a small allowance for helping at home. A lemonade stand earns money from the cups it sells.

Earned money is not the same as a gift. A gift is something someone gives you. Earned money is pay for work you did.

### Work and pay

Many jobs pay for each thing you do. Say you are paid 25¢ for each plant you water. For 4 plants, add 25 four times: 25, 50, 75, 100. That is 100¢, or \$1.

For jobs that repeat, **multiply**. The pay for each time times the number of times. 4 plants and 25¢ each: 4 x 25 = 100¢.

### What you can do with earned money

You can spend it, save it or share it. In the next weeks you will learn to plan all three. Remember: needs come first.

### Sorting needs and wants

Using pictures is a good way to think about needs and wants. Cut or draw ten pictures of things, then sort them into two piles.

@example
title: Pay for jobs
problem: You are paid \$3 for each car you wash. You wash 4 cars. How much do you earn?
step: Each car pays \$3. You washed 4 cars.
step: Add \$3 four times: 3, 6, 9, 12.
step: Or multiply: 4 x 3 = 12.
answer: \$12

@example
title: Pay for chores
problem: You earn 50¢ for each chore. You do 3 chores. How many cents do you earn?
step: Count by fifties, once for each chore: 50, 100, 150.
step: That is 150¢, which is \$1.50.
answer: 150¢

@mistakes
- Thinking a gift is earned. Earned money is pay for work you did.
- Adding the pay only once when the job was done many times.
- Spending all the pay right away without thinking about needs first.

@practice mc
prompt: Which is a way to earn money?
choice*: Getting paid to help a neighbor rake leaves
choice: Wishing for money
choice: Buying a toy at a store
hint: Earning means getting paid for work you did.
solution: Raking leaves for pay is work, so the pay is earned. Wishing is not work, and buying a toy spends money instead of earning it.

@practice num
prompt: You sell 8 cups of lemonade for 50¢ each. How many dollars did you earn?
answer: 4
lead: \$
check: 8 * 50 / 100
hint: Two cups earn 100¢, which is \$1.
solution: Each pair of cups earns 100¢ (\$1). Eight cups is 4 pairs, so you earned \$4.

@practice num
prompt: You earn \$3 on Monday and \$4 on Tuesday. Then you spend \$2. How many dollars do you have left?
answer: 5
lead: \$
check: 3 + 4 - 2
hint: First add what you earned. Then take away what you spent.
solution: 3 + 4 = 7 dollars earned. Then 7 - 2 = 5. You have \$5 left.

@practice self
prompt: Sort ten pictures into needs and wants. Cut out or draw ten pictures of things, such as food, a toy, a coat, a game and a bed. Put them in two piles, needs and wants. Pick any two pictures and explain to your grown-up why you put each in its pile.
hint: Ask "Do I need this to stay safe and healthy?" for each picture.
solution: A good answer has ten pictures in two piles, with at least some in each pile. For two of them the child gives a reason, like "A coat keeps me warm, so it is a need" and "A game is fun, but I can live without it, so it is a want."
rubric: Ten pictures are sorted into a needs pile and a wants pile.
rubric: Needs are things that keep a person safe and healthy.
rubric: Two pictures are explained with a reason, and the grown-up agrees the reasons make sense.
