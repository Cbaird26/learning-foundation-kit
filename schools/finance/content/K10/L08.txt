@article
Grown-up: use pretend money for the plans. Your child decides how to split it.

A **spending plan** is also called a **budget**. It is a plan for what you will do with your money before you spend it.

### Three jars

A simple plan uses three jars.

- **Save**: money for a goal later.
- **Spend**: money for things you buy now.
- **Give**: money to share with others.

Pretend you earned \$8. Your plan could be:

| Jar | Amount |
|---|---|
| Save | \$3 |
| Spend | \$4 |
| Give | \$1 |
| Total | \$8 |

Add the jars. If they add up to the money you have, the plan works.

### When one part is missing

If you know the total and two jars, find the last jar by taking away. You have \$10. Save is \$4 and Spend is \$5. Give is what is left: 10 - 4 - 5 = 1. Give gets \$1.

### Checking the plan

After the week, look back. Did you spend what you planned? If you planned to spend \$4 but spent \$6, you went over by \$2. Next time you can plan more for Spend, or buy a smaller thing.

### A savings chart

A chart on paper shows your saving week by week. Make columns for the week, how much you save and the total so far. Color in a bar or a picture of a jar as the total grows.

@example
title: Find the missing jar
problem: You have \$12. Your Spend jar gets \$6 and your Save jar gets \$4. How much goes in the Give jar?
step: Add the jars you know: 6 + 4 = 10.
step: Take that away from \$12: 12 - 10 = 2.
step: The Give jar gets \$2.
answer: \$2

@example
title: Did the plan work?
problem: You planned to spend \$5 this week but you spent \$7. How much more than the plan did you spend?
step: Compare the two amounts: spent 7, planned 5.
step: Take away: 7 - 5 = 2.
step: You spent \$2 more than planned. Next week, plan carefully.
answer: \$2 more than planned.

@mistakes
- Making jars that add up to more than the money you have.
- Forgetting the Save jar. Pay your future self first.
- Never checking the plan afterwards, so you never learn what to change.

@practice num
prompt: You have \$10. Your plan puts \$4 in Save and \$5 in Spend. How many dollars go in the Give jar?
answer: 1
lead: \$
check: 10 - 4 - 5
hint: Add Save and Spend. Then take that from \$10.
solution: 4 + 5 = 9. Then 10 - 9 = 1. The Give jar gets \$1.

@practice num
prompt: You have \$12. The Spend jar gets \$6 and the Save jar gets \$4. The rest goes in the Give jar. How many dollars go in Give?
answer: 2
lead: \$
check: 12 - 6 - 4
hint: Find the part that is left.
solution: 6 + 4 = 10, and 12 - 10 = 2. The Give jar gets \$2.

@practice mc
prompt: Your plan said spend \$4. You spent \$6. What happened?
choice*: You spent \$2 more than the plan
choice: You spent \$2 less than the plan
choice: You followed the plan exactly
hint: Compare 6 and 4.
solution: The plan was 4 and you spent 6. That is 6 - 4 = 2 more dollars than planned.

@practice num
prompt: You put \$3 in your Save jar each week. How many dollars are in the Save jar after 6 weeks?
answer: 18
lead: \$
check: 3 * 6
hint: Count by threes, six times.
solution: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18. After 6 weeks the Save jar has \$18.

@practice self
prompt: Make a savings chart for a goal you choose. Pick a pretend goal and write its price (under \$20). Make a table with columns Week, Save, Spend, Give and Total saved. Fill in four weeks of a plan. Your grown-up checks that each week's Save, Spend and Give add up to the money you had.
hint: Choose an amount for each week, then add to the running total in the last column.
solution: A good chart has four rows. For example, with \$6 each week split as Save \$3, Spend \$2 and Give \$1, the Total saved column reads \$3, \$6, \$9, \$12. The goal and price are written above the chart.
rubric: The goal and its price are named and the price is under \$20.
rubric: In every week, Save plus Spend plus Give equals the money for that week.
rubric: The Total saved column grows correctly by the Save amount each week.

@quiz num

answer: 2
lead: \$
check: 15 - 7 - 6
hint: Add what you know, then take it from 15.
solution: 7 + 6 = 13. Then 15 - 13 = 2. The Give jar gets \$2.
