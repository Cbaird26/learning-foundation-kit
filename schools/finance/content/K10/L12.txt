@article
Grown-up: look at ads together in a magazine, on a cereal box, or on TV. Do not click on anything or buy anything.

An **ad** is a message that wants you to buy something. Ads are on TV, on boxes, on signs and in magazines. Ads are not always bad. They can tell you what is for sale. But an ad's job is to get you to buy.

### Tricks to spot

- **Rushing you**: "Only today! Hurry!" It wants you to buy before you think.
- **Cartoon friends**: a fun character on a box makes the thing look more fun. The character does not make the thing better.
- **"Everyone has one!"** Not everyone does, and you do not have to.
- **"Sale!"** Check the price. A sale can still cost more than you need to spend.
- **"Free" extras**: "Buy 2, get 1 free" means you must buy 2 first. Do the math.

### Do the math

Stickers cost 30¢ each. "Buy 2, get 1 free." You pay for 2: 30 + 30 = 60¢. You get 3 stickers. That is 60 divided by 3, which is 20¢ each. A good deal, if you wanted 3 stickers.

### Ask four questions

1. What does this ad want me to do?
2. Do I need it, or do I want it?
3. How much does it cost, and do I have enough?
4. Can I wait a day and think?

If you wait and still want it, and it fits your plan, it may be a smart choice.

@example
title: Buy 2, get 1 free
problem: Toy cars cost 30¢ each. A sign says "Buy 2, get 1 free." How many cents do you pay for 3 cars, and how many cents is that for each car?
step: You pay for only 2 cars: 30 + 30 = 60¢.
step: You get 3 cars. Share 60¢ among 3 cars: 60 / 3 = 20¢.
step: You pay 60¢ in all, 20¢ for each car.
answer: 60¢ in all, 20¢ for each car.

@example
title: A sale sign
problem: A sign says "Sale! Was 80¢, now 60¢." How much do you save? Is it a smart buy?
step: Take the new price from the old price: 80 - 60 = 20. You save 20¢.
step: Now ask the four questions. Do you need it? Does it fit your plan?
step: A sale is only smart if you need it or it is in your plan.
answer: You save 20¢. It is smart only if you need it and it fits your plan.

@mistakes
- Thinking a "sale" always means you should buy. Ask if you need it.
- Believing "Hurry, only today!" Waiting a day is almost always okay.
- Thinking "free" means you pay nothing at all. You often have to buy something first.
