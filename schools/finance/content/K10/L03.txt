@lesson
title: Adding prices and making change
summary: Add the prices of things you want to buy and work out the change you get back.
objective: Add two or three prices to find the total cost.
objective: Find change by counting up from the price to the money you paid.
objective: Set up a pretend store and practice paying and making change.
reading: Money as You Grow (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau): look up the activities about spending and paying for things.

@article
Grown-up: this lesson uses a pretend store. Put price tags on a few toys or snacks, with prices in cents or whole dollars.

### Adding prices

The **price** is how much something costs. When you buy more than one thing, you add the prices. The answer is the **total**.

A sticker costs 20¢ and a pencil costs 35¢. Add them: 20 + 35 = 55. The total is 55¢.

### What is change?

Sometimes you pay with more money than the price. The store gives back the extra money. That money is called **change**.

### Count up to find change

Here is a way to make change that does not need subtracting. Start at the price. Count up to the money you gave.

A toy costs 35¢. You pay 50¢. Start at 35. Add a nickel: "40." Add a dime: "50." The store gave back a nickel and a dime. The change is 15¢.

You can check with subtraction too: 50 - 35 = 15.

### Pay, then check

Always do two things. Add the prices to find the total. Then find the change. When you finish, put the change in your hand and count it. It should match.

If you do not have enough money for the total, you cannot buy everything. You might choose one thing, or save up for later.

@example
title: Making change
problem: A toy costs 35¢. You pay with 2 quarters, which is 50¢. How much change do you get?
step: Start at the price, 35. Count up to 50 with coins.
step: Add a nickel to get to 40. Add a dime to get to 50.
step: The change is a nickel and a dime. That is 5 + 10 = 15¢.
answer: 15¢

@example
title: Two things, then change
problem: A pencil costs 45¢ and an eraser costs 30¢. You pay with a \$1 bill, which is 100¢. How much change do you get?
step: Add the prices: 45 + 30 = 75. The total is 75¢.
step: Count up from 75 to 100: add a quarter, and you are at 100.
step: The change is 25¢, one quarter.
answer: 25¢

@mistakes
- Giving back the price as change instead of the extra money.
- Forgetting to add all the items before you pay.
- Counting up past the money you gave. Stop when you reach the amount you paid.

@quiz num
prompt: Gum costs 15¢, a pencil costs 25¢ and a ruler costs 30¢. What is the total in cents?
answer: 70
unit: ¢
check: 15 + 25 + 30
hint: Add two prices first, then add the third.
solution: 15 + 25 = 40. Then 40 + 30 = 70. The total is 70¢.
