@article
Grown-up: the pretend bank in this lesson is a made-up game with easy numbers. Real banks pay much less, and their rates change. Your child can ask the grown-up to look up how real interest works, but nothing needs to be signed up for.

When you keep money in a bank, the bank can use it for a while. For example, it lends money to other people. The bank says thank you by paying you a little extra. That extra money is called **interest**.

### The Pretend Bank rule

In our game, the Pretend Bank pays **1¢ for each whole dollar** in your account at the end of every week.

- \$10 in the account pays 10¢.
- \$7.05 has 7 whole dollars, so it pays 7¢. Ignore the extra 5¢.

### More money, more interest

A bigger balance earns more interest. \$3 earns 3¢ in a week. \$7 earns 7¢ in a week.

### Watching it grow

Start with \$10 and do not touch it. At the end of week 1 you get 10¢: \$10.10. At the end of week 2, you still have 10 whole dollars, so 10¢ more: \$10.20. After 4 weeks you have \$10.40.

If you add a deposit, the interest goes up too, because there are more whole dollars. Keep a table with the week, deposit, interest and balance so you can see the changes.

### Real interest

Real banks pay much less than our game. Their rate can change. The more you save and the longer you leave it, the more interest it earns. People who borrow money from a bank pay interest to the bank.

@example
title: Four quiet weeks
problem: You put \$10 in the Pretend Bank and do not add or take out anything. The bank pays 1¢ for each whole dollar each week. What is your balance after 4 weeks?
step: Each week the interest is 10 whole dollars times 1¢, which is 10¢.
step: After 4 weeks you have 4 interest payments: 10¢ four times is 40¢.
step: The balance is \$10 + 40¢ = \$10.40.
answer: \$10.40

@example
title: A deposit in week 2
problem: You start with \$5. At the end of week 1 the bank pays interest. At the start of week 2 you deposit \$2. At the end of week 2 the bank pays interest again. What is the balance?
step: End of week 1: 5 whole dollars pay 5¢. The balance is \$5.05.
step: Deposit \$2: the balance is \$7.05.
step: End of week 2: 7 whole dollars pay 7¢. The balance is \$7.12.
answer: \$7.12

@mistakes
- Counting the cents in the balance as dollars. Only whole dollars earn interest in our game.
- Forgetting to add the interest to the balance each week.
- Thinking real banks pay as much as the Pretend Bank. They pay much less.
