objective: Count a handful of mixed coins and say the total in cents.

@article
Grown-up: have a pile of real or pretend coins ready. Your child moves each coin as it is counted.

How do you count a pile of mixed coins? Use three steps.

1. **Sort.** Put the coins in groups: quarters, dimes, nickels, pennies.
2. **Biggest first.** Start with the coin worth the most.
3. **Count on.** Say the total so far, then add the next coin.

### Count on

Say you have a quarter, a dime and a nickel. Start with the quarter and say "25." Add the dime. Count by tens from 25: "35." Add the nickel. Count by fives: "40." The total is 40¢.

Why biggest first? Because counting by 25s, then 10s, then 5s, then 1s is easier than jumping around.

### Bills and coins together

Count the bills first. Then count the coins and keep going. A \$5 bill and 2 quarters is "five dollars, five twenty-five, five fifty." We write that as \$5.50. The dot is called a **decimal point**. Dollars go on the left of the dot. Cents go on the right.

### Check your work

Count again, in a different order. If you get the same total, you are probably right!

@example
title: Count a handful
problem: You have 1 quarter, 1 dime, 1 nickel and 2 pennies. How many cents is that?
step: Biggest first: the quarter. Say "25."
step: Add the dime and count on by tens: "35." Add the nickel: "40."
step: Add the pennies one at a time: "41, 42."
answer: 42¢

@example
title: Bills and coins
problem: You have one \$5 bill, two \$1 bills and 2 quarters. How much money is that?
step: Count the bills first: five, six, seven. That is \$7.
step: Count on the quarters: "seven twenty-five, seven fifty."
step: Write it with a decimal point: \$7.50.
answer: \$7.50

@mistakes
- Counting each coin as 1, so a quarter becomes 1 instead of 25¢.
- Starting with the pennies, then losing track. Start with the biggest coins.
- Forgetting to keep the running total. Say the total each time, not just the new coin.

answer: 8.50
lead: \$
check: (500 + 300 + 50) / 100
hint: Count the bills first. Then count on the two quarters.
solution: The bills are 5, 6, 7, 8, so \$8. Two quarters are 50¢. The total is \$8.50.
