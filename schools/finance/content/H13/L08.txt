@article
**Imperialism** is the extension of one state's control over other territories and peoples. Empires existed for millennia, but from about 1870 to 1914 a burst of expansion by European states, the United States and Japan is called the **new imperialism**. By 1914, European powers controlled most of Africa and much of Asia.

### Motives

- **Economic:** raw materials, markets, investment opportunities, and cheap labor.
- **Strategic:** naval bases, coaling stations and routes such as the Suez Canal (opened 1869).
- **Ideological:** nationalism and prestige; belief in cultural or racial superiority, including the claim of a "civilizing mission," which colonized peoples and many later historians have criticized as a justification; missionary goals.
- **Technology and medicine:** steamships, rifles, the telegraph, and **quinine** (which reduced deaths from malaria) made inland expansion easier for Europeans.

### Cases

**India.** The British East India Company gained power over much of India through the 1700s and 1800s. After the Indian Rebellion of 1857, the British Crown took direct rule (the Raj). Railways, courts and an English-language education system were built, and revenue systems and famines affected rural Indians. Indian national organizations, such as the Indian National Congress (1885), grew.

**China.** The Opium Wars (1839 to 1842, 1856 to 1860) began as conflicts over British opium sales and trade restrictions, and led to treaties giving foreign powers trading ports and concessions. Chinese officials and writers debated reform and resistance.

**Africa.** At the Berlin Conference (1884 to 1885), European powers set rules for claiming African territory without African delegates. In 1870 European states controlled roughly 10 percent of Africa, and by 1914 only Ethiopia and Liberia were fully independent. Many African societies resisted, among them Ethiopia, which defeated Italy at Adwa in 1896. Colonial rule often drew borders across existing communities and brought taxes, forced labor and cash-crop economies, while some Africans took part in colonial administration, schools and missions. Colonial medicine fought sleeping sickness and yellow fever, but some campaigns used coercion and forced relocation.

**Japan.** After American ships pressed Japan to open its ports in 1853 to 1854, Japan's leaders undertook the Meiji Restoration (1868), industrializing and building a modern army. Japan then built its own empire, taking Taiwan (1895) and Korea (1910).

### Nationalism

**Nationalism** is the belief that people sharing a language, history or culture should form a political community. It can unify (as in Italy, 1861, and Germany, 1871) or divide multiethnic empires such as Austria-Hungary and the Ottoman Empire. It also shaped anti-colonial movements.

### Perspectives

Colonial officials emphasized order, infrastructure and economic development. Colonized peoples and many historians emphasize loss of self-rule, unequal laws and extraction of wealth; others note that colonial effects varied by place, period and group. Historians continue to debate how to weigh these.

@example
title: Measuring the speed of the scramble
problem: Europeans controlled about 10 percent of Africa in 1870 and about 90 percent in 1914. How many years did that change take, and by how many percentage points did it rise?
step: Elapsed time: $1914 - 1870 = 44$ years.
step: Change in share: $90 - 10 = 80$ percentage points.
step: Average change: $80 / 44 \approx 1.8$ percentage points per year, though most of it came after the Berlin Conference.

@mistakes
- Treating all colonial experiences as identical across Africa and Asia.
- Treating colonized people only as victims; they resisted, adapted, negotiated and shaped colonial societies.
- Saying the Berlin Conference "divided Africa"; it set rules for claims, and conquest followed over decades.
- Presenting the "civilizing mission" claim as a fact rather than as a justification used by colonizers.
