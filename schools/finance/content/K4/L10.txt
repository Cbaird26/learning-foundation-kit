@lesson
title: Characters, setting and events
summary: Read a short story and say who is in it, where it happens and what happens.
objective: Name the characters in a story.
objective: Name the setting, which is where and when a story happens.
objective: Tell the events, which are the things that happen.
reading: Reading Rockets, "Phonics and decoding": look up the topic "story elements" (characters, setting, plot) under comprehension.

@article
A story has three big parts.

### Characters

**Characters** are the people or animals in the story. Ask: Who is in the story?

### Setting

The **setting** is where and when the story happens. It might be a park, a farm or a kitchen. It might be at night or on a rainy day. Ask: Where are they? When is it?

### Events

**Events** are the things that happen. Stories have a **beginning**, a **middle** and an **end**. Ask: What happens first? What happens next? How does it end?

### A small story

Here is a short story to read with your grown-up.

Pip the duck sat by the pond. It was a sunny day. Pip saw a red ball in the grass. Pip said, "I will get it." Pip ran and grabbed the ball. Then Pip gave the ball to Sam the frog. Sam said, "Thank you!"

In this story, the characters are Pip and Sam. The setting is by the pond on a sunny day. The events: Pip sees a ball, Pip gets it, and Pip gives it to Sam.

### Try it with your grown-up

Read the story aloud. Then ask each other the three questions: Who? Where? What happened?

@example
title: Find the parts of a story
problem: Read: "Mia the cat sat on the porch. It began to rain. Mia ran inside." Who is the character, and what is the setting?
step: Ask "Who?" The story is about Mia the cat. Mia is the character.
step: Ask "Where and when?" Mia is on the porch, and it is a rainy day.
step: The setting is the porch on a rainy day.

@mistakes
- Mixing up the setting and the characters. Characters are who. The setting is where and when.
- Naming only one thing that happened. Name the first, next and last events.
- Making up parts that are not in the story. Look back at the words.

rubric: You named both characters.
rubric: You told where the story happens.
