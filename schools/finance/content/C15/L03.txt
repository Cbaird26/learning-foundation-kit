@article
A company is not worth nothing after the last year of your forecast, and you cannot forecast forever. The **terminal value** (TV) condenses all later cash flows into one number at the end of year $N$. In most DCFs it accounts for well over half of enterprise value, so its assumptions deserve the most care.

### The Gordon growth (perpetuity growth) terminal value

If FCFF grows at a constant rate $g$ forever after year $N$ and the WACC is $r$ (written WACC in the rest of the course), then

$$TV_N = \frac{\text{FCFF}_{N+1}}{r - g} = \frac{\text{FCFF}_N(1 + g)}{r - g}, \qquad r > g.$$

The cash flow in the numerator is the **next** year's flow, $\text{FCFF}_N(1+g)$, not the last forecast flow. The present value is $TV_N/(1+r)^N$.

**Choosing $g$.** A firm cannot outgrow the economy forever, or it would eventually become larger than the economy. The terminal growth rate is therefore normally set at or below the long-run nominal growth of the economy in which the cash flows are earned (and below the discount rate). Look up current long-run growth and government bond yield figures on FRED or Treasury, and state your choice.

The result is very sensitive to $r$ and $g$. For $\text{FCFF}_5 = 73$:

| WACC | $g = 2\%$ | $g = 3\%$ | $g = 4\%$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8% | 1,241.0 | 1,503.8 | 1,898.0 |
| 9% | 1,063.7 | 1,253.2 | 1,518.4 |
| 10% | 930.8 | 1,074.1 | 1,265.3 |

### The value-driver formula: growth needs reinvestment

Growth is not free. If the firm earns a return on new invested capital (ROIC) and wants to grow NOPAT at $g$, it must reinvest a fraction $g/\text{ROIC}$ of NOPAT. Then $\text{FCFF}_{N+1} = \text{NOPAT}_{N+1}(1 - g/\text{ROIC})$ and

$$TV_N = \frac{\text{NOPAT}_{N+1}\,(1 - g/\text{ROIC})}{r - g}.$$

This exposes a key insight. If $\text{ROIC} = r$ (new investment earns exactly its cost of capital), the formula collapses to $TV_N = \text{NOPAT}_{N+1}/r$: growth adds **no** value. Growth creates value only when ROIC exceeds the WACC. A terminal assumption of high growth with high returns forever is a claim of permanent competitive advantage; in competitive markets analysts often let ROIC fall toward the WACC in the terminal period.

### The exit multiple method

Alternatively, set $TV_N = \text{multiple} \times \text{EBITDA}_N$ using a multiple observed for mature comparable firms (week 4). The multiple method imports market pricing, which may be too high or too low. Cross-check each method with the other:

- **Implied multiple** of the Gordon value: $TV_N/\text{EBITDA}_N$.
- **Implied growth** of a multiple-based value, from $TV_N = \text{FCFF}_N(1+g)/(r-g)$: $g = \dfrac{TV_N\, r - \text{FCFF}_N}{TV_N + \text{FCFF}_N}$.

If the implied growth is 6% for a mature firm, the multiple is too high.

### Mid-year timing

If FCFF arrives evenly through each year, the cash flows after year $N$ arrive at $N + 0.5$, $N + 1.5$, and so on. The Gordon value then equals $TV_N(1+r)^{0.5}$ at the end of year $N$, so its present value is $TV_N/(1+r)^{N - 0.5}$. An exit-multiple value is a price at year $N$ and is discounted $N$ years.

### How much comes from the terminal value?

Report the TV share of enterprise value. A share above about 75% to 80% says the valuation depends mainly on the terminal assumptions, so a longer explicit forecast (to let growth and margins fade) may help.

@example
title: A Gordon growth terminal value
problem: Larkspur Outfitters has FCFF forecast at \$52, \$58, \$64, \$69 and \$73 million in years 1 to 5. After year 5, FCFF is expected to grow at 3% forever. The WACC is 9% and cash flows are discounted at year end. Find the terminal value, enterprise value and the terminal value's share of enterprise value.
step: $\text{FCFF}_6 = 73 \times 1.03 = 75.19$. Terminal value at year 5: $TV_5 = 75.19/(0.09 - 0.03) = \$1{,}253.17$ million.
step: Discount it five years: $1{,}253.17/1.09^5 = 1{,}253.17/1.538624 = \$814.47$ million.
step: Present value of the explicit flows: $52/1.09 + 58/1.09^2 + 64/1.09^3 + 69/1.09^4 + 73/1.09^5 = \$242.27$ million.
step: $EV = 242.27 + 814.47 = \$1{,}056.74$ million. The terminal value is $814.47/1{,}056.74 = 77.1\%$ of $EV$.
answer: $TV_5 = \$1{,}253.17$ million; $EV = \$1{,}056.74$ million; the terminal value is 77.1% of $EV$.

@example
title: Growth with and without excess returns
problem: Bellwether Foods is expected to earn NOPAT of \$120 million in year 6. Afterwards NOPAT grows 3% a year forever. The WACC is 9%. Compute the terminal value at the end of year 5 if the return on new investment is 12%, and if it equals the WACC.
step: With ROIC of 12%, the reinvestment rate is $g/\text{ROIC} = 0.03/0.12 = 25\%$, so $\text{FCFF}_6 = 120(1 - 0.25) = 90$.
step: $TV_5 = 90/(0.09 - 0.03) = \$1{,}500$ million.
step: With ROIC equal to the WACC of 9%, the reinvestment rate is $0.03/0.09 = 33.3\%$, so $\text{FCFF}_6 = 120 \times 0.6667 = 80$, and $TV_5 = 80/0.06 = \$1{,}333.33$ million.
step: This equals $\text{NOPAT}_6/\text{WACC} = 120/0.09 = 1{,}333.33$: with no excess return, 3% growth adds nothing to value. The extra \$166.67 million in the first case is the value of earning 12% on capital that costs 9%.
answer: \$1,500 million with ROIC of 12%; \$1,333.33 million with ROIC equal to the WACC.

@mistakes
- Capitalizing the last explicit flow $\text{FCFF}_N$ instead of $\text{FCFF}_{N+1} = \text{FCFF}_N(1+g)$.
- Setting $g$ at or above the WACC, or above any plausible long-run growth of the economy (the formula then gives a meaningless or negative value).
- Assuming high terminal growth while holding reinvestment at a low level; growth must be paid for with capital.
- Using a high exit multiple from fast-growing peers for a firm that is assumed to be mature, without checking the implied growth.

answer: 2080
lead: \$
tol: 1
check: 100 * 1.04 / (0.09 - 0.04)
hint: Use next year's flow, $100 \times 1.04$.
hint: Divide by $r - g = 0.05$.
solution: $TV_5 = 100(1.04)/(0.09 - 0.04) = 104/0.05 = \$2{,}080$ million.

@practice num
prompt: An analyst values a firm's operations at year 8 using an exit multiple of 8.0 times EBITDA of \$150 million. The firm's FCFF in year 8 is \$80 million and its WACC is 10%. What perpetual growth rate of FCFF does this terminal value imply? Give it as a percent to two decimal places.
answer: 3.13
unit: %
tol: 0.01
check: (8 * 150 * 0.10 - 80) / (8 * 150 + 80) * 100
hint: $TV = 8 \times 150 = 1{,}200$. Solve $TV = 80(1+g)/(0.10 - g)$ for $g$.
hint: $g = (TV \cdot r - \text{FCFF}_N)/(TV + \text{FCFF}_N)$.
solution: $TV = 1{,}200$. Then $g = (1{,}200 \times 0.10 - 80)/(1{,}200 + 80) = 40/1{,}280 = 3.125\%$, or 3.13% to two places.

choice*: The value is too low by the factor $1 + g$, because the numerator should be next year's flow, $\text{FCFF}_N(1+g)$.
choice: The value is too high, because the last flow is larger than the average future flow.
choice: There is no effect, because the growth rate already appears in the denominator.
choice: The value is correct only if the WACC is below the growth rate.
hint: The formula values a stream whose first flow arrives one year after the valuation date.
solution: $TV_N = \text{FCFF}_{N+1}/(r-g)$ values flows beginning one year after $N$. Using $\text{FCFF}_N$ omits one year of growth, so the result is too low by the factor $1+g$. The model also requires $r > g$.

Cash flows arrive evenly through each year and are valued with the mid-year convention, so the flows after year 5 arrive at 5.5, 6.5, and so on. The WACC is 8%. @quiz self
prompt: A firm will earn NOPAT of \$100 million in the first year after the forecast and then grow 4% a year forever. The WACC is 10%. Compute the terminal value (at the end of the forecast) with the value-driver formula if the return on new investment is 10%, and if it is 15%. Then explain in two or three sentences why the two values differ and what each says about growth.
hint: The reinvestment rate is $g/\text{ROIC}$ and $\text{FCFF} = \text{NOPAT}(1 - \text{reinvestment rate})$.
solution: With ROIC of 10%, the reinvestment rate is 40%, FCFF is 60, and $TV = 60/(0.10 - 0.04) = \$1{,}000$ million, which equals $\text{NOPAT}/\text{WACC} = 100/0.10$. With ROIC of 15%, the rate is 26.67%, FCFF is 73.33, and $TV = 73.33/0.06 = \$1{,}222$ million. When ROIC equals the WACC, growth requires reinvestment that earns only its cost of capital, so it adds no value. When ROIC exceeds the WACC, each dollar reinvested earns more than it costs, so growth creates value.

rubric: Notes that with ROIC equal to the WACC the value equals NOPAT divided by the WACC, so growth adds nothing.
