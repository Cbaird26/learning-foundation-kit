### Week by week

1. **Free cash flow.** $\text{FCFF} = \text{EBIT}(1-t) + \text{D\&A} - \text{CapEx} - \Delta\text{NWC}$; $\text{FCFE} = \text{FCFF} - I(1-t) + \text{net borrowing}$. Operating NWC excludes cash and debt.
2. **DCF.** Discount FCFF at the WACC to get enterprise value; subtract debt, preferred and noncontrolling interests, add cash, divide by diluted shares. Mid-year timing multiplies the PV of the forecast flows by $(1+\text{WACC})^{0.5}$.
3. **Terminal value.** $TV_N = \text{FCFF}_{N+1}/(r-g)$; value-driver form $\text{NOPAT}_{N+1}(1-g/\text{ROIC})/(r-g)$; growth adds value only if ROIC exceeds the WACC; cross-check with the implied multiple and implied growth.
4. **Multiples.** Enterprise multiples with enterprise value; equity multiples with equity value; justified P/E $=(1-g/\text{ROE})/(r-g)$; median or harmonic mean.
5. **Modigliani-Miller.** $V_L = V_U$; $r_E = r_0 + (D/E)(r_0 - r_D)$; the WACC is constant without taxes.
6. **Taxes and distress.** $V_L = V_U + tD$ for permanent debt; $r_E = r_0 + (D/E)(1-t)(r_0-r_D)$; $\text{WACC} = r_0(1 - tD/V)$; Hamada $\beta_L = \beta_U[1+(1-t)D/E]$; the trade-off theory.
7. **Payout.** Irrelevance in perfect markets; buybacks cut the share count; EPS rises when the earnings yield exceeds the after-tax return forgone; signaling, taxes and agency matter.
8. **Residual income.** $\text{RI}_t = NI_t - r_E B_{t-1}$; $V_0 = B_0 + PV(\text{RI})$; persistence terminal value $\omega\text{RI}_N/(1+r_E-\omega)$; economic profit $= \text{NOPAT} - \text{WACC}\times IC$.
9. **M&A.** Synergy $S$; premium; cash-deal NPV $= S - \text{premium} - \text{costs}$; exchange ratio, ownership and accretion.
10. **LBOs.** Sources and uses; exit equity $=$ exit EV $-$ net debt; $\text{MOIC}$ and $\text{IRR} = \text{MOIC}^{1/n}-1$; the sources of return.
11. **Governance.** Agency problems of managers, creditors and controlling holders; board, pay, disclosure, shareholder rights and control market.
12. **Real options.** Expanded NPV $=$ static NPV $+$ option value; risk-neutral $p = (1+r_f-d)/(u-d)$; the Black-Scholes-Merton formula for delay.

### Self-check

- Can you compute FCFF three ways and show that they agree?
- Can you explain why FCFF is discounted at the WACC and FCFE at the cost of equity?
- Can you state what each of your terminal-value assumptions implies for ROIC and the exit multiple?
- Can you show with numbers why leverage raises the cost of equity but not (without taxes) the WACC?
- Can you split an LBO's gain into EBITDA growth, multiple change and debt paydown?

### Common errors

Discounting FCFF at the cost of equity; using $\text{FCFF}_N$ instead of $\text{FCFF}_{N+1}$ in the terminal value; counting the tax shield twice; pairing enterprise value with net income; forgetting net debt; using book values for market weights; ignoring the $(1-t)$ in Proposition II with taxes; computing an IRR from the profit instead of the ratio of exit to entry equity; using real-world probabilities in a binomial tree.

After year 3, FCFF grows 3% a year forever (the Gordon growth model, applied to FCFF in year 3, year-end discounting). Its target capital structure has market values of equity \$600 million and debt \$400 million, with a cost of equity of 11% and a pretax cost of debt of 6%. Net debt is \$350 million and there are 50 million diluted shares. Compute the value per share to the nearest cent.

| Item | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| EBIT | 120 | 132 | 144 |
| D&A | 40 | 42 | 44 |
| CapEx | 50 | 52 | 54 |
| Increase in operating NWC | 6 | 6 | 6 |

answer: 24.78
lead: \$
tol: 0.1%
check: (sum(f / 1.084 ** t for t, f in enumerate([120 * 0.75 + 40 - 50 - 6, 132 * 0.75 + 42 - 52 - 6, 144 * 0.75 + 44 - 54 - 6], 1)) + (144 * 0.75 + 44 - 54 - 6) * 1.03 / (0.6 * 0.11 + 0.4 * 0.06 * 0.75 - 0.03) / 1.084 ** 3 - 350) / 50
hint: Compute FCFF for each year, then the WACC from the weights 0.6 and 0.4 with the after-tax cost of debt.
hint: FCFF is 74, 83 and 92 and the WACC is 8.4%; the terminal value at year 3 is $92(1.03)/(0.084 - 0.03)$.
solution: FCFF $= \text{EBIT}(0.75) + \text{D\&A} - \text{CapEx} - \Delta\text{NWC}$ gives 74, 83 and 92. $\text{WACC} = 0.6(11\%) + 0.4(6\%)(0.75) = 8.4\%$. The forecast flows have present value \$211.13 million. $TV_3 = 92(1.03)/0.054 = \$1{,}754.81$ million, worth \$1{,}377.66 million today. $EV = \$1{,}588.79$ million, equity is $1{,}588.79 - 350 = 1{,}238.79$, and the value per share is $\$24.78$.

@exam num
prompt: A firm with an unlevered beta of 0.9 plans a capital structure with a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.5 (so debt is one-third of firm value). Its debt costs 5.5% before tax and the tax rate is 25%. Use the Hamada equation (debt beta of zero) for the equity beta, the CAPM with a risk-free rate of 4% and a market risk premium of 5% (hypothetical figures) for the cost of equity, and market-value weights for the WACC. The firm's FCFF is expected to be \$49 million next year and to grow 2% a year forever. Compute the enterprise value in millions of dollars to the nearest whole number.
answer: 795
lead: \$
tol: 1
check: 49 / ((2 / 3) * (0.04 + 0.9 * (1 + 0.75 * 0.5) * 0.05) + (1 / 3) * 0.055 * 0.75 - 0.02)
hint: Relever the beta, find the cost of equity, then the WACC.
hint: $\beta_L = 1.2375$, $r_E = 10.19\%$ and the WACC is 8.17%.
solution: $\beta_L = 0.9[1 + 0.75(0.5)] = 1.2375$, so $r_E = 4\% + 1.2375 \times 5\% = 10.1875\%$. With weights $2/3$ and $1/3$, $\text{WACC} = (2/3)(10.1875\%) + (1/3)(5.5\%)(0.75) = 6.7917\% + 1.375\% = 8.1667\%$. $EV = 49/(0.081667 - 0.02) = \$794.6$ million, or \$795 million.

@exam self
prompt: Fairmont Gear is a possible acquisition target. Its market price is \$18.00. Your valuation work gives these values per share: DCF \$21.40, EV/EBITDA multiple \$18.90, P/E multiple \$19.60, residual income model \$22.10. You estimate that the acquirer could realize synergies worth \$4.50 per share (present value, after tax and after integration costs). In a memo of six to eight sentences, give a reconciled standalone value range, explain two reasons the methods may differ, state the maximum price per share the acquirer should be willing to pay and the premium it implies, and say what you would do if the target's board demanded \$26.
hint: Standalone value is the range of the four methods; the walk-away price adds the synergies.
solution: The four methods give a standalone range of about \$18.90 to \$22.10, with a central value near \$20.50 (the DCF and the residual income model carry the most weight if their inputs are better supported than peer pricing). The methods differ because multiples reflect the market's current pricing of peers, which may be high or low, and differ in growth and risk from the target, while the DCF depends on forecast cash flows and a terminal value that can make up most of the value; the residual income model rests on book value quality and the persistence of excess returns. The maximum price the acquirer should pay is the standalone value plus the synergies, about \$20.50 + \$4.50 = \$25.00 (using the high end, \$22.10 + \$4.50 = \$26.60 is a ceiling), which is a premium of about 39% over the \$18.00 market price. At \$26 almost all of the synergy goes to the target's holders, and if the standalone value is nearer \$20.50 the acquirer's NPV is negative (\$25.00 - \$26.00 = -\$1.00 a share); the acquirer should walk away unless it can show that a standalone value near \$22 is supportable and that the synergies are credible, rather than pay above its walk-away price.
rubric: Gives a standalone range consistent with the data (about \$18.90 to \$22.10) and a central estimate with a reason for the weighting.
rubric: Explains two sources of difference between the methods, such as peer pricing and comparability, terminal value dependence, or book value quality.
rubric: States the walk-away price as standalone value plus synergies (about \$25 at a central value of \$20.50, up to \$26.60 at the high end) and the implied premium over \$18.00 (about 39%).
rubric: Concludes that paying \$26 gives away nearly all or more than all of the synergy, with a negative NPV at the central value, and states what the acquirer should do.
