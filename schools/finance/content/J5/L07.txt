@article
**Reproduction** is how living things make new organisms of their own kind. **Heredity** is the passing of traits from parents to offspring.

### Asexual reproduction

In **asexual reproduction** one parent makes offspring that are genetically identical to it, using mitosis. Bacteria split in **binary fission**, yeast and hydras grow **buds**, and many plants send out runners or grow from cuttings. It is fast and needs no partner, but offspring all share the same weaknesses.

### Sexual reproduction

In **sexual reproduction** two parents each contribute a **sex cell** or **gamete**: a **sperm** from the male parent and an **egg** from the female parent. When they join in **fertilization**, they form a **zygote**, the first cell of a new organism. Offspring get a mix of genes from both parents, so they differ from each other and from their parents. This **variation** helps populations survive changes.

### Meiosis keeps the number steady

Human body cells have 46 chromosomes, arranged as 23 pairs. If a sperm and an egg also had 46 each, the zygote would have 92. Instead, sex cells are made by **meiosis**, a type of cell division that produces four cells with **half** the chromosomes. Human gametes have 23. Fertilization restores the full set:

$$23 + 23 = 46.$$

### DNA, genes and chromosomes

**DNA** is a long molecule shaped like a twisted ladder (a **double helix**). The rungs are made of pairs of four bases, abbreviated A, T, C and G. The pairing rule is:

$$\text{A pairs with T}, \qquad \text{C pairs with G}.$$

A **gene** is a segment of DNA that holds instructions for making a protein. Genes are located on **chromosomes**, which are tightly coiled DNA found in the nucleus. The order of bases is the code.

Because of the pairing rule, in double-stranded DNA the amount of A equals the amount of T, and C equals G. If 30% of the bases are A, then 30% are T, so the remaining 40% are shared by C and G: 20% each.

### Traits

Some traits are mostly inherited, like blood type. Others depend on both genes and environment, like height, which is also affected by nutrition. Identical twins have the same DNA, but small differences in their lives make them slightly different.

@example
title: Chromosome counts through a life cycle
problem: A dog body cell has 78 chromosomes. How many chromosomes are in a dog egg, and how many in the zygote after fertilization?
step: Meiosis halves the number: $78 \div 2 = 39$, so each egg and each sperm has 39 chromosomes.
step: The zygote receives one gamete from each parent: $39 + 39 = 78$.
step: The zygote has the same number as the body cells of its parents, so the species number stays constant across generations.

@mistakes
- Saying gametes have 46 chromosomes in humans; they have 23.
- Thinking sexual reproduction makes offspring identical to a parent; only asexual reproduction does.
- Mixing up the base pairs, for example pairing A with C instead of A with T.
