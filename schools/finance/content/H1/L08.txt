@lesson

@article
A **polynomial** is a sum of **terms**, each a number (the **coefficient**) times a whole-number power of the variable, such as $4x^3 - 7x + 2$. The **degree** is the highest power present, here $3$, and the **leading term** is the one with that power, $4x^3$. Polynomials with one, two or three terms are called monomials, binomials and trinomials.

### Start with a picture

A rectangle has width $x + 3$ and height $x + 2$. What is its area? Cut it into four pieces: an $x$ by $x$ square ($x^2$), an $x$ by $2$ strip ($2x$), a $3$ by $x$ strip ($3x$) and a $3$ by $2$ corner ($6$). The total is

$$x^2 + 2x + 3x + 6 = x^2 + 5x + 6.$$

That is all multiplication of polynomials means: every piece of the first factor meets every piece of the second.

### Adding and subtracting

Combine **like terms**, which are terms with exactly the same variable part. Only the coefficients are added: $3x^2 + 5x^2 = 8x^2$, but $3x^2 + 5x$ cannot be combined.

When you subtract, the minus sign changes the sign of **every** term inside the bracket that follows it:

$$(5x^2 + 3x - 4) - (2x^2 - x + 6) = 5x^2 + 3x - 4 - 2x^2 + x - 6 = 3x^2 + 4x - 10.$$

### Multiplying

Use the distributive law: every term of the first factor multiplies every term of the second, then combine like terms. For two binomials this is often remembered as FOIL (First, Outer, Inner, Last):

$$(x + 3)(x + 2) = x^2 + 2x + 3x + 6 = x^2 + 5x + 6.$$

Three patterns turn up so often that you should recognise them on sight:

$$(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2, \qquad (a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2, \qquad (a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2.$$

Notice that $(a + b)^2$ is **not** $a^2 + b^2$: there is a middle term $2ab$. Test with numbers: $(2 + 3)^2 = 25$, but $2^2 + 3^2 = 13$. The missing $12$ is exactly $2ab = 2\cdot 2\cdot 3$.

### Simplifying mixed expressions

When an expression mixes operations, expand the brackets and powers first, then collect like terms, and keep brackets around anything that follows a minus sign until you have expanded it.

### Test a value to catch errors

Here is the most useful safety net in algebra. Pick an easy number such as $x = 2$ and evaluate the **original** expression and your **simplified** one. They must give the same number. If they differ, there is an error somewhere. If they agree, you are very likely right (one test cannot prove it, but a second value makes a mistake very unlikely). The same test lets you repair someone else's working: find the line where the values stop agreeing.

@example
title: Expand, subtract and simplify
problem: Simplify $(2x - 3)^2 - (x + 1)(x - 4)$.
step: Expand the square with the pattern $(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$: $(2x - 3)^2 = 4x^2 - 12x + 9$.
step: Expand the product: $(x + 1)(x - 4) = x^2 - 4x + x - 4 = x^2 - 3x - 4$.
step: Subtract, keeping the second result in brackets so the minus sign reaches every term: $(4x^2 - 12x + 9) - (x^2 - 3x - 4) = 4x^2 - 12x + 9 - x^2 + 3x + 4$.
step: Combine like terms: $3x^2 - 9x + 13$.
step: Test $x = 2$. The original is $(1)^2 - (3)(-2) = 7$. The result is $12 - 18 + 13 = 7$. They agree.

@example
title: Repairing a worked solution
problem: A student writes $(x + 4)^2 - (x - 1)(x + 1) = x^2 + 16 - x^2 - 1 = 15$. Find the errors and give the correct simplification.
step: Test $x = 2$. The original expression is $6^2 - (1)(3) = 33$, but the student's answer is $15$. So there is an error.
step: First error: $(x + 4)^2$ is $x^2 + 8x + 16$. The student left out the middle term $2ab = 8x$.
step: Second error: $(x - 1)(x + 1) = x^2 - 1$, and subtracting it gives $- x^2 + 1$. The student wrote $- x^2 - 1$ and did not change the sign of the $-1$.
step: Corrected working: $x^2 + 8x + 16 - x^2 + 1 = 8x + 17$.
step: Test $x = 2$: $8(2) + 17 = 33$, which matches the original expression.

@mistakes
- Writing $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2$ and losing the middle term $2ab$.
- Not changing every sign inside a bracket that follows a minus sign.
- Combining unlike terms, such as $2x^2 + 3x = 5x^3$.
- Multiplying only the first term of a factor, as in $3(x - 2) = 3x - 2$.

@practice expr
prompt: Simplify $(4x^2 - 2x + 7) - (x^2 + 3x - 5)$ and write the result as a polynomial.
vars: x
form: expanded
answer: 3x^2 - 5x + 12
check: lambda x: (4*x**2 - 2*x + 7) - (x**2 + 3*x - 5)
hint: Put the minus sign in front of every term of the second bracket.
hint: You get $4x^2 - 2x + 7 - x^2 - 3x + 5$. Now combine the $x^2$ terms, the $x$ terms and the numbers.
solution: $4x^2 - 2x + 7 - x^2 - 3x + 5 = 3x^2 - 5x + 12$. Test $x = 1$: the original is $(4 - 2 + 7) - (1 + 3 - 5) = 9 - (-1) = 10$, and $3 - 5 + 12 = 10$.

@practice expr
prompt: Expand $(3x - 2)(x + 5)$.
vars: x
form: expanded
answer: 3x^2 + 13x - 10
check: lambda x: (3*x - 2)*(x + 5)
hint: Multiply each term of the first bracket by each term of the second: four products in all.
hint: The four products are $3x \cdot x$, $3x \cdot 5$, $-2 \cdot x$ and $-2 \cdot 5$. Then combine the two $x$ terms.
solution: $3x^2 + 15x - 2x - 10 = 3x^2 + 13x - 10$. Test $x = 1$: $(1)(6) = 6$ and $3 + 13 - 10 = 6$.

@practice mc
prompt: A student simplifies $3(x - 2) - (x - 4)$ and writes $3x - 6 - x - 4 = 2x - 10$. Which statement identifies the error and the correct result?
choice*: The minus sign must change both terms of $(x - 4)$, so the correct result is $3x - 6 - x + 4 = 2x - 2$.
choice: $3(x - 2)$ should expand to $3x - 2$, so the correct result is $2x + 2$.
choice: The student should have added the brackets instead of subtracting, so the correct result is $4x - 10$.
choice: There is no error; $2x - 10$ is correct.
hint: Test $x = 2$ in the original expression and in the student's answer. Do they agree?
hint: The original at $x = 2$ is $3(0) - (-2) = 2$, but the student's answer gives $-6$. Look at what $-(x - 4)$ becomes.
solution: $-(x - 4) = -x + 4$, not $-x - 4$. So the correct working is $3x - 6 - x + 4 = 2x - 2$. At $x = 2$ this gives $2$, matching the original expression, while the student's $2x - 10$ gives $-6$.

@practice num
prompt: When $(2x - 1)(x^2 + 3x - 4)$ is fully expanded, what is the coefficient of the $x^2$ term?
answer: 5
check: np.polymul([2, -1], [1, 3, -4])[1]
hint: Which pairs of terms multiply to give a power $x^2$? One uses the $2x$, the other uses the $-1$.
hint: Pair $2x$ with $3x$ and pair $-1$ with $x^2$, then add the two results.
solution: $2x \cdot 3x = 6x^2$ and $-1 \cdot x^2 = -x^2$, so the $x^2$ coefficient is $6 - 1 = 5$. The full expansion is $2x^3 + 5x^2 - 11x + 4$.

@practice expr
prompt: Simplify $(3x + 1)^2 - (3x - 1)^2$.
vars: x
form: expanded
answer: 12x
check: lambda x: (3*x + 1)**2 - (3*x - 1)**2
hint: Expand each square with the pattern for $(a \pm b)^2$, keeping the second one in brackets.
hint: You get $(9x^2 + 6x + 1) - (9x^2 - 6x + 1)$. Remove the brackets, changing every sign in the second one.
solution: $9x^2 + 6x + 1 - 9x^2 + 6x - 1 = 12x$. Test $x = 1$: $16 - 4 = 12$.

@quiz expr
prompt: Expand and simplify $(x + 2)(x^2 - 2x + 4)$.
vars: x
form: expanded
answer: x^3 + 8
check: lambda x: (x + 2)*(x**2 - 2*x + 4)
hint: Multiply the $x$ of the first bracket by every term of the second, then do the same with the $2$.
hint: You get $x^3 - 2x^2 + 4x + 2x^2 - 4x + 8$. Which terms cancel?
solution: $x^3 - 2x^2 + 4x + 2x^2 - 4x + 8 = x^3 + 8$. Test $x = 1$: $3 \cdot 3 = 9$ and $1 + 8 = 9$.
