@article
Your first job is exciting, and so is your first surprise: the paycheck is smaller than you expected. That is because of deductions.

### Gross pay and net pay

**Gross pay** is what you earn before anything is taken out. For hourly work: $\text{gross pay} = \text{hours} \times \text{hourly rate}$. A salaried worker is paid a fixed yearly amount, split into pay periods. **Net pay**, or take-home pay, is what you actually receive:

$$\text{net pay} = \text{gross pay} - \text{deductions}.$$

### Common deductions

- **Social Security tax**: 6.2% of gross pay (up to a yearly wage limit that is far above a typical teen's earnings). It helps fund retirement, disability and survivor benefits.
- **Medicare tax**: 1.45% of gross pay, which helps fund health insurance for older people.
- **Federal income tax withholding**: an amount your employer sends to the government for you. It depends on your pay and on the Form W-4 you fill out. A teen with a small income often owes little or no federal income tax and can get withheld money back by filing a return.
- **State and local income tax**: some states and cities charge it; others do not.

Social Security and Medicare together are called **FICA** taxes, 7.65% of gross pay. These rates are the 2026 employee rates. This is general education, not tax advice; ask an adult or check the IRS for your situation.

Other deductions can include health insurance or retirement plan contributions.

### Reading a pay stub

A pay stub lists hours, rate, gross pay, each deduction, net pay, and year-to-date totals. This sample is hypothetical; the federal and state amounts are invented for practice.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Hours | 20 |
| Rate | \$12.00 per hour |
| Gross pay | \$240.00 |
| Social Security (6.2%) | \$14.88 |
| Medicare (1.45%) | \$3.48 |
| Federal withholding | \$12.00 |
| State tax (3%) | \$7.20 |
| **Net pay** | **\$202.44** |

### From salary to a pay period

A yearly salary is divided by the number of pay periods: 26 for every two weeks (biweekly), 24 for twice a month, 12 for monthly, 52 for weekly.

@example
title: Net pay from a pay stub
problem: Sofia works 20 hours at \$12 an hour. Her employer takes Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%), \$12.00 federal withholding and 3% state tax. Find her net pay.
step: Gross pay: $20 \times \$12 = \$240$.
step: Social Security: $0.062 \times 240 = \$14.88$. Medicare: $0.0145 \times 240 = \$3.48$. State tax: $0.03 \times 240 = \$7.20$.
step: Total deductions: $\$14.88 + \$3.48 + \$12.00 + \$7.20 = \$37.56$.
step: Net pay: $\$240 - \$37.56 = \$202.44$.
answer: Net pay is \$202.44.

@example
title: Salary per pay period
problem: A full-time worker has a salary of \$31,200 a year and is paid every two weeks. What is the gross pay for each paycheck?
step: Paid every two weeks means 26 paychecks a year.
step: Divide: $\$31{,}200 \div 26 = \$1{,}200$.
answer: \$1,200 gross per paycheck.

@mistakes
- Thinking net pay is the same as gross pay; budget with net pay, since that is what lands in your account.
- Taking 7.65% off twice, or using 6.2% for Medicare. Medicare is 1.45%, and 7.65% is the two together.
- Dividing a salary by 12 for a biweekly paycheck instead of 26.
- Assuming a large refund is a bonus; it just means too much was withheld during the year.

@quiz num
prompt: Amir works 20 hours at \$15 an hour. His employer withholds Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%) and \$20.00 of federal income tax; there is no state tax. What is his net pay? Give your answer in dollars and cents.
answer: 257.05
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: 20 * 15 * (1 - 0.062 - 0.0145) - 20
hint: Gross pay is 20 times 15. FICA is 7.65% of gross.
solution: Gross: $20 \times \$15 = \$300$. FICA: $0.0765 \times 300 = \$22.95$. Net: $\$300 - \$22.95 - \$20.00 = \$257.05$.
