@article
When you save, the bank pays you for using your money. When you borrow, you pay the lender. That payment is **interest**. Interest is the price of money over time.

### The simple interest formula

Three pieces of information set the interest:

- the **principal** $P$: the amount you deposit or borrow;
- the **rate** $r$: the percent per year, written as a decimal (5% is $0.05$);
- the **time** $t$: the number of years.

**Simple interest** is paid only on the original principal:

$$I = P \times r \times t, \qquad A = P + I.$$

Here $I$ is the interest and $A$ is the **amount** (the balance at the end). In this lesson the rate is a yearly rate, time is in years, and the interest is **not** added to the principal during the term. For a time in months, divide by 12, so 6 months is $t = 0.5$.

A word of warning: most real savings accounts use **compound** interest, which you will study next. Simple interest is still worth knowing: some loans use it, and it is the foundation for compound interest.

### Solving for other pieces

The formula can be turned around:

$$r = \frac{I}{P \times t}, \qquad t = \frac{I}{P \times r}.$$

### Comparing two accounts

To compare accounts fairly, use the same time period for both. A higher rate does not always win: the deposit amount matters too. Work out the interest each one earns and compare the dollars.

| Account | Principal | Rate | Time | Interest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| X | \$600 | 4% | 2 years | \$48 |
| Y | \$500 | 5% | 2 years | \$50 |

Account Y earns \$2 more even though it started with \$100 less, because its rate is higher.

@example
title: Interest and balance
problem: You deposit \$500 in an account paying 4% simple interest. How much interest do you earn in 3 years, and what is the balance?
step: Convert the rate to a decimal: $4\% = 0.04$.
step: Apply the formula: $I = 500 \times 0.04 \times 3 = \$60$.
step: Add the interest to the principal: $A = \$500 + \$60 = \$560$.
answer: Interest \$60; balance \$560.

@example
title: A time in months
problem: You deposit \$800 for 6 months at 3% simple interest per year. How much interest do you earn?
step: Six months is half a year, so $t = 6 \div 12 = 0.5$.
step: $I = 800 \times 0.03 \times 0.5 = \$12$.
step: Check: a full year would earn \$24, and half a year earns half of that.
answer: \$12.

@mistakes
- Using 5 instead of 0.05 for a 5% rate, which makes the interest 100 times too large.
- Using months as years: 6 months is $t = 0.5$ years, not 6.
- Reporting the interest when the question asks for the final balance (or the other way around).
- Comparing accounts over different time periods, or looking only at the rate and ignoring the deposit.

@quiz num
prompt: You deposit \$750 for 8 months at 6% simple interest per year. How much interest do you earn? Give your answer in dollars.
answer: 30
lead: \$
check: 750 * 0.06 * 8 / 12
hint: Convert 8 months to years first.
solution: $t = 8 \div 12 = 2/3$ year, so $I = 750 \times 0.06 \times \tfrac{2}{3} = \$30$.
