@article
Borrowing lets you have something now and pay for it later, but the lender charges interest for waiting. The **cost of borrowing** is the extra you pay beyond the amount borrowed:

$$\text{cost of borrowing} = \text{total repaid} - \text{amount borrowed}.$$

### Loans

A loan has a **principal** (the amount borrowed), an **interest rate**, and a **term** (how long you have to repay). With a simple interest loan repaid in one payment, the math is the same as earlier: borrow \$1,000 at 6% simple interest for 2 years and you repay $1000 + 1000 \times 0.06 \times 2 = \$1{,}120$. With an **installment loan** you repay in equal payments; add them all up to get the total.

A longer term means smaller payments but more total interest. The lender also looks at your credit score when setting the rate. If you cannot repay, you can lose what the loan is secured by, and your credit score drops.

### Credit card balances month by month

On a card, each month works like this (we use one-twelfth of the APR as the monthly rate and charge interest on the balance at the start of the month, then subtract the payment):

$$\text{new balance} = \text{old balance} + \text{interest} - \text{payment}.$$

Example: \$500 at 24% APR (2% a month), paying \$50 a month and not charging anything new:

| Month | Start | Interest | Payment | End |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | \$500.00 | \$10.00 | \$50.00 | \$460.00 |
| 2 | \$460.00 | \$9.20 | \$50.00 | \$419.20 |
| 3 | \$419.20 | \$8.38 | \$50.00 | \$377.58 |

The pattern continues, with interest shrinking as the balance falls. After month 11 the balance is \$13.24, and month 12's last payment is \$13.50. Total paid: \$563.50, so the borrowing cost was \$63.50, and it took 12 months. Interest is rounded to the cent each month.

### The minimum payment trap

A **minimum payment** is the smallest amount you must pay. If you pay only \$25 a month on that same \$500 balance, the same method shows the debt lasts 26 months and costs a total of \$644.96, or \$144.96 of interest. Cutting the payment in half more than doubled the interest cost. And if you keep charging purchases to the card, the balance may never go down.

Paying more than the minimum, or paying the full statement balance, is the way to keep borrowing cheap.

@example
title: Cost of an installment loan
problem: You borrow \$600 and repay \$25 a month for 30 months. What is the total repaid, and what does the borrowing cost?
step: Total repaid: $30 \times \$25 = \$750$.
step: Borrowing cost: $\$750 - \$600 = \$150$.
answer: \$750 repaid; \$150 borrowing cost.

@example
title: Two months of a card balance
problem: You owe \$300 on a card with a 24% APR (2% a month). You pay \$60 at the end of the month. What is your balance after one month?
step: Interest: $\$300 \times 0.02 = \$6.00$.
step: Add the interest: $\$300 + \$6 = \$306$.
step: Subtract the payment: $\$306 - \$60 = \$246$.
answer: \$246.

@mistakes
- Looking only at the monthly payment and not at the total cost over the whole term.
- Forgetting that the balance grows by the interest before the payment is subtracted.
- Thinking the minimum payment is the amount you "should" pay; it is just the least the lender allows.
- Adding new purchases while paying down a balance and then wondering why it never shrinks.
