@article
Your biggest financial asset right now is your future ability to earn. The choices you make about school, training and work affect your income for decades. This lesson helps you think about those choices with numbers.

### Jobs and income

Pay depends on many things: the skills a job needs, how many people can do it, the place and the industry. The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes the Occupational Outlook Handbook, with typical pay, required education and job outlook for hundreds of careers. Use it, not guesses, when you compare careers.

### Education as an investment

Education and training cost money and time, but on average workers with more education earn more and are less likely to be unemployed. Averages hide big differences, so look at the specific path. A simple way to think about it is the **break-even time**:

$$\text{break-even time} = \frac{\text{total cost of the training}}{\text{extra income per year}}.$$

After that many years of working, the training has paid for itself in extra pay. The numbers here are hypothetical. If money is borrowed for school, the loan's interest is part of the cost; federal student aid rules change, so check current terms at studentaid.gov, along with scholarships and grants, which do not have to be repaid.

### Planning a goal

A good plan names the goal, the amount, the deadline and the steps. For a goal five years away, you might deposit the same amount at the end of each year in an account. Assume a **fixed 3% yearly interest rate** compounded once a year, with each deposit made at the **end** of the year. Depositing \$300 a year gives:

| End of year | Calculation | Balance |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | \$300 | \$300.00 |
| 2 | $300 \times 1.03 + 300$ | \$609.00 |
| 3 | $609 \times 1.03 + 300$ | \$927.27 |
| 4 | $927.27 \times 1.03 + 300$ | \$1,255.09 |
| 5 | $1255.09 \times 1.03 + 300$ | \$1,592.74 |

Total deposits are \$1,500, so interest adds about \$92.74. In this table each year's balance is rounded to the cent; carrying more decimals gives the same final amount to the cent.

### Your one-page plan

Write down: (1) the goal and why it matters, (2) the cost, (3) the deadline, (4) how much you will save each month or year, (5) where you will keep the money, (6) what could go wrong and what you will do, and (7) when you will check your progress. Goals change, so review the plan every few months.

@example
title: Break-even for training
problem: A training program costs \$12,000 and raises your pay by \$8,000 a year compared with having no training. How long until the extra pay covers the cost?
step: Divide the cost by the extra yearly income: $\$12{,}000 \div \$8{,}000 = 1.5$.
step: It takes 1.5 years of working after the training to earn back the cost.
step: This ignores interest on any loan and the pay you give up while studying, which would lengthen the time.
answer: 1.5 years (a rough, hypothetical estimate).

@example
title: A five-year plan with deposits
problem: You need about \$1,500 in five years. You deposit \$300 at the end of each year at a fixed 3%, compounded annually. Do you reach your goal?
step: Follow the balance year by year: \$300.00, \$609.00, \$927.27, \$1,255.09.
step: At the end of year 5: $1255.09 \times 1.03 + 300 = \$1{,}592.74$.
step: Compare with the goal: $\$1{,}592.74 - \$1{,}500 = \$92.74$ more than needed, so yes.
answer: Yes, \$1,592.74 (about \$92.74 above the goal).

@mistakes
- Guessing at pay and costs instead of looking them up from a reliable source.
- Treating a hypothetical break-even as exact; real costs and pay vary, and loans add interest.
- Making a goal with no deadline or no yearly amount, so it never turns into action.
- Forgetting to review the plan when income, costs or goals change.

prompt: You deposit \$150 at the end of each year for 3 years in an account paying 4% a year, compounded annually. What is the balance right after the third deposit? Give your answer in dollars and cents.
answer: 468.24
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: 150 * 1.04 ** 2 + 150 * 1.04 + 150
hint: The first deposit earns 2 years of interest, the second earns 1, and the third earns none.
solution: $150 \times 1.04^2 = \$162.24$, $150 \times 1.04 = \$156.00$, and the last deposit is \$150. The sum is \$468.24.
