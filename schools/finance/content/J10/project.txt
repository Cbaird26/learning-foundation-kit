@review
### Key ideas by week

1. **Income, needs, wants, goals**: income is money you receive; needs are required; wants are optional. Turn a goal into an amount per week or month.
2. **Budget**: income minus spending and saving; surplus, deficit, balanced. Percent of income = category $\div$ income $\times$ 100%.
3. **Banks and credit unions**: checking is for spending, savings is for saving; FDIC and NCUA insure deposits up to \$250,000 per depositor, per insured institution, per ownership category; watch fees; keep a check register.
4. **Simple interest**: $I = P r t$ and $A = P + I$ (rate as a decimal, time in years).
5. **Compound interest and the rule of 72**: $A = P(1 + r)^t$ with annual compounding; years to double $\approx 72 \div$ rate.
6. **Saving and investing**: investing can grow faster but can lose value and is not insured; time matters.
7. **Paychecks**: net pay = gross pay $-$ deductions; Social Security 6.2% and Medicare 1.45% (7.65% together); salary $\div$ pay periods.
8. **Spending**: unit price = price $\div$ units; discount first, then tax; cost per use.
9. **Debit, credit and scores**: debit uses your money, credit borrows; monthly interest = balance $\times$ APR $\div$ 12 (simplified); utilization = balance $\div$ limit; FICO range is 300 to 850.
10. **Borrowing**: cost of borrowing = total repaid $-$ amount borrowed; new balance = old balance + interest $-$ payment; small payments last longer and cost more.
11. **Scams and fraud**: red flags are urgency, secrecy, odd payment methods and requests for codes; strong unique passwords; report quickly.
12. **Planning**: break-even = cost $\div$ extra income per year; deposits at the end of each year grow with $\text{balance} \times (1 + r) + \text{deposit}$.

### Self-check

- Can I make a balanced budget and compute a percent of income?
- Can I tell simple from compound interest, and compute both?
- Can I read a pay stub and find net pay?
- Can I compare two packages by unit price?
- Can I follow a card balance for three months?
- Can I name three scam red flags?

### Common errors

- Using 5 instead of 0.05 for a 5% rate.
- Using months as years in an interest formula.
- Using the yearly APR as the monthly rate.
- Forgetting savings in the budget, or paying a deficit with a loan.
- Comparing stated rates when the compounding or the principal differs.
- Treating a hypothetical rate or return as guaranteed.

answer: 15
unit: months
check: 420 / (0.20 * (4 * 18 + 68))
hint: First find total monthly income, then the 20% savings amount.
hint: Divide the goal by the monthly savings.
solution: Income is $4 \times \$18 + \$68 = \$140$. Savings are $0.20 \times \$140 = \$28$ a month. The goal takes $\$420 \div \$28 = 15$ months.

prompt: You deposit \$1,000 in Account A, which pays 4% simple interest per year, and \$1,000 in Account B, which pays 3.5% a year compounded once a year. Neither account has fees and you make no other deposits or withdrawals. How much more is Account B worth than Account A after 10 years? Give your answer in dollars and cents.
answer: 10.60
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: 1000 * 1.035 ** 10 - (1000 + 1000 * 0.04 * 10)
hint: Account A: principal plus $P \times r \times t$. Account B: $P(1 + r)^t$.
hint: Find both balances for 10 years, then subtract A from B.
solution: Account A: $1000 + 1000 \times 0.04 \times 10 = \$1{,}400$. Account B: $1000 \times 1.035^{10} = \$1{,}410.60$. B is worth \$10.60 more, even though its rate is lower, because of compounding.

@exam self
prompt: Cam works 15 hours a week at \$13 an hour. The employer takes Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%) from each paycheck, and no income tax is withheld. Cam wants a \$300 phone. Option 1: save \$60 a week until there is enough. Option 2: put it on a credit card with a 24% APR (2% a month, charged on the start-of-month balance) and pay \$25 a month, which takes 14 months and costs about \$346.48 in total. Find Cam's weekly net pay, compare the two options in dollars and time, and recommend what a careful person would consider.
hint: Compute gross pay, then FICA at 7.65%, then net pay.
hint: Compare total cost: Option 1 costs \$300; for Option 2 use the total given and subtract the price.
solution: Gross pay is $15 \times \$13 = \$195$. FICA is $0.0765 \times 195 = \$14.92$, so net pay is about $\$195 - \$14.92 = \$180.08$ a week. Option 1 takes $300 \div 60 = 5$ weeks and costs \$300 with no interest. Option 2 takes 14 months and costs about \$346.48, which is \$46.48 of interest, and it uses a card the person would need an adult to co-sign. A careful person would see that saving costs less, that \$60 is about a third of net pay and fits the budget, and that waiting 5 weeks avoids borrowing for a want. If the phone were an urgent need, they might weigh the cost of borrowing against the cost of going without.
rubric: Calculates gross pay (\$195), FICA (about \$14.92) and net pay (about \$180.08).
rubric: Compares both options with numbers: 5 weeks and \$300 versus 14 months and about \$346.48 (interest about \$46.48).
rubric: Gives a reasoned recommendation that considers need versus want and the cost of borrowing, without naming products.
