@article
Money is a tool. To use it well, you need to know three things: how much comes in, where it goes, and what you are saving it for.

### Income

**Income** is money you receive. For a teen, income often comes from an allowance, pay for chores or odd jobs (babysitting, mowing lawns), a part-time job when the law allows it, and gifts. Some income is **earned** (you worked for it) and some is not (a gift). Either way, it counts when you plan how to spend.

If you earn money by the hour, the pay is $\text{hours} \times \text{hourly rate}$. Working 5 hours at \$10 an hour earns \$50.

### Needs and wants

A **need** is something you must have to stay safe, healthy and able to function: food, a place to live, basic clothing, school supplies, and transportation to school or work. A **want** is something that is nice to have but that you could live without: a new game, a trendy jacket, a streaming subscription.

The line is not always sharp. A warm coat in winter is a need, but a coat that costs \$250 because of its label includes a lot of want. A phone may be a need for staying in touch with family, but a new model every year is a want. When you are unsure, ask: "What happens if I go without this?"

### Goals

A **goal** is something you want to reach with your money. Good goals are **specific**, have a **target amount**, and have a **deadline**. "Save some money" is vague. "Save \$180 for a bike in 12 weeks" is a plan you can act on.

To find how much to save each period:

$$\text{amount per week} = \frac{\text{target amount}}{\text{number of weeks}}.$$

Short-term goals (a few weeks or months) are reached quickly and keep you motivated. Long-term goals (a year or more, like a first car or college) need steady saving over time. In this lesson we ignore interest, so savings simply add up. Later lessons show how interest changes the picture.

@example
title: A month of income
problem: Mia gets an allowance of \$20 a week. This month has 4 weeks. She also babysat once and earned \$45. Her spending was \$30 on school lunches, \$18 on a game and \$12 on a bus pass. How much income did she have, and how much was left after spending?
step: Allowance for the month: $4 \times \$20 = \$80$.
step: Total income: $\$80 + \$45 = \$125$.
step: Total spending: $\$30 + \$18 + \$12 = \$60$. The lunches and the bus pass are needs (\$42); the game is a want (\$18).
step: Money left: $\$125 - \$60 = \$65$.
answer: Income \$125; spending \$60 (needs \$42, wants \$18); \$65 left.

@example
title: Planning for a goal
problem: Dev wants to buy a \$180 bike. He has \$30 saved and can save \$15 a week. How many weeks will it take?
step: Find how much more he needs: $\$180 - \$30 = \$150$.
step: Divide by what he saves each week: $\$150 \div \$15 = 10$.
step: Check: $\$30 + 10 \times \$15 = \$180$.
answer: 10 weeks.

@mistakes
- Counting a want as a need because "everyone has one"; ask what would happen if you went without it.
- Forgetting some income (gifts and odd jobs) when adding up the month, which makes the plan too pessimistic.
- Setting a goal with no deadline, so there is no way to tell how much to save each week.
- Forgetting to subtract money already saved before dividing by the weekly amount.

@practice num
prompt: In one month Sam receives \$18 a week in allowance for 4 weeks, a \$35 gift and \$50 for mowing lawns. Sam spends \$62 on needs and \$48 on wants. How much money is left? Give your answer in dollars.
answer: 47
lead: \$
check: (4 * 18 + 35 + 50) - (62 + 48)
hint: First add all the income, then add all the spending.
hint: Income is $4 \times 18 + 35 + 50$; spending is $62 + 48$. Subtract spending from income.
solution: Income is $4 \times \$18 + \$35 + \$50 = \$157$. Spending is $\$62 + \$48 = \$110$. Left over: $\$157 - \$110 = \$47$.

@quiz num
prompt: Lena wants \$360 for a school trip in 9 months and already has \$90. How much must she save each month to reach her goal exactly? Give your answer in dollars.
answer: 30
lead: \$
check: (360 - 90) / 9
hint: Subtract what she already has from the target first.
solution: She still needs $\$360 - \$90 = \$270$. Over 9 months that is $\$270 \div 9 = \$30$ a month.

@quiz self
prompt: A friend says, "A phone is a need, so my \$900 phone is a need." In three or four sentences, explain how you would sort this purchase into needs and wants.
hint: Think about what part of a phone is required and what part is extra.
solution: A phone can be a need because it lets you reach family and get help, but the need is met by a basic working phone. The extra cost of a top-end model buys features that are nice to have, which is a want. So part of the purchase is a need and the rest is a want, and the friend can meet the need for much less than \$900.
rubric: Says the basic purpose (staying in touch or safe) can be a need.
rubric: Separates the extra cost of the expensive model as a want.
rubric: Concludes that the same item can include both a need and a want, or suggests a cheaper way to meet the need.
