@article
Every time you buy something, you trade money you earned for something you want. **Comparison shopping** means checking more than one option before you buy, so that your money goes further.

### Unit price

Packages come in different sizes, so the bigger sticker price is not always the bigger cost. The **unit price** is the price for one unit (one ounce, one item, one sheet):

$$\text{unit price} = \frac{\text{total price}}{\text{number of units}}.$$

The item with the lower unit price is the better buy per unit. Stores often print the unit price on the shelf tag. Remember that a bigger package is only a better deal if you will use it before it goes bad or gets old.

### Discounts and sales tax

A **discount** lowers the price by a percent. For 25% off a \$48 item:

$$\text{sale price} = \text{price} \times (1 - 0.25) = 48 \times 0.75 = \$36.$$

**Sales tax** is a percent added at the register. Rates vary by state and city, so the examples here use hypothetical rates. Compute the discount first and then the tax:

$$\text{total} = \text{sale price} \times (1 + \text{tax rate}).$$

Check the **total** cost, which may also include shipping for online orders.

### Cost per use

A cheap item that wears out quickly can cost more than an expensive one that lasts. The **cost per use** (or per month) is the price divided by the number of uses (or months you will use it). A \$65 pair of shoes that lasts 20 months costs \$3.25 per month, and a \$40 pair that lasts 10 months costs \$4.00 per month.

### Smart shopper habits

- Decide whether it is a need or a want first.
- Compare at least two stores or websites.
- Wait a day before a big want to see if you still want it.
- Ask whether an ad is trying to make you feel you need it.

@example
title: Which package is the better buy?
problem: A 12 oz box of cereal costs \$3.00. A 20 oz box of the same cereal costs \$4.60. Which has the lower price per ounce?
step: The small box: $\$3.00 \div 12 = \$0.25$ per ounce.
step: The large box: $\$4.60 \div 20 = \$0.23$ per ounce.
step: The large box costs 2 cents less per ounce, so it is the better buy per unit, provided you will eat it all.
answer: The 20 oz box (\$0.23 per ounce against \$0.25).

@example
title: Sale price and tax
problem: A jacket costs \$48 and is on sale for 25% off. The sales tax rate is 7% (a hypothetical rate). What do you pay at the register?
step: Sale price: $\$48 \times 0.75 = \$36.00$.
step: Add tax: $\$36.00 \times 1.07 = \$38.52$.
step: The tax is \$2.52, and the discount saved \$12.
answer: \$38.52.

@mistakes
- Comparing sticker prices of different sizes instead of the unit prices.
- Applying the tax before the discount, or adding the discount percent to the tax percent.
- Buying a larger size just because it is cheaper per unit, then wasting part of it.
- Forgetting shipping, which can turn an online "deal" into a worse price.

@quiz mc
prompt: A store sells a pack of 6 snack bars for \$7.50 and a pack of 10 for \$11.50. Which statement is correct?
choice*: The 10-pack has the lower price per bar, so it is the better buy per bar if you will eat them all
choice: The 6-pack is cheaper per bar because its total price is lower
choice: Both packs cost \$1.25 per bar
choice: The 10-pack costs more per bar because it has more bars
hint: Divide each total price by the number of bars.
solution: The 6-pack costs $7.50 \div 6 = \$1.25$ per bar and the 10-pack costs $11.50 \div 10 = \$1.15$ per bar. A lower total price does not mean a lower unit price.
