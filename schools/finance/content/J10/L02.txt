@article
A **budget** is a plan for your money over a period of time, usually a month. It lists the income you expect and decides in advance where each dollar goes. Without a plan, money tends to disappear on small things, and the big goals never get funded.

### The steps

1. **List your income** for the month: allowance, pay, gifts you expect.
2. **List your spending** by category: food, transportation, phone, fun, clothing.
3. **Add savings as a category.** Treat saving like a bill you pay to your future self, not what is left over.
4. **Compare.** Total spending plus savings should not be more than income.

### Surplus, deficit and balance

If income is greater than planned spending, you have a **surplus**. If planned spending is greater than income, you have a **deficit**, which means you would run out of money or have to borrow. A **balanced budget** has income equal to spending plus savings, so every dollar has a job.

$$\text{surplus (or deficit)} = \text{income} - \text{total planned spending and saving}.$$

A positive result is a surplus; a negative result is a deficit.

### Percent of income

It helps to see each category as a percent of income:

$$\text{percent of income} = \frac{\text{category amount}}{\text{income}} \times 100\%.$$

One popular rule of thumb is the 50/30/20 guideline: about 50% of income to needs, 30% to wants and 20% to savings. It is only a starting point. Your own numbers depend on your situation, and a teen with few bills may save much more than 20%.

### Fixing a deficit

Cut wants first, because needs are harder to change. You can also raise income (extra chores, a job) or move the timing of a purchase. Do not fix a deficit by borrowing for wants.

### A sample budget

| Category | Type | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Allowance and jobs | Income | \$200 |
| Lunches and bus | Needs | \$90 |
| Phone plan share | Needs | \$10 |
| Games and fun | Wants | \$60 |
| Savings | Savings | \$40 |

Spending and saving add to \$200, the same as income, so the budget is balanced.

@example
title: Percent of income
problem: Maya's monthly income is \$200. She plans \$60 for wants. What percent of her income goes to wants, and is that more or less than the 30% in the 50/30/20 guideline?
step: Divide the category by income: $60 \div 200 = 0.30$.
step: Multiply by 100 to get a percent: $0.30 \times 100\% = 30\%$.
step: The guideline gives 30% to wants, so Maya matches it exactly.
answer: 30%, which equals the guideline.

@example
title: Fixing a deficit
problem: Eli's income is \$150 a month. He plans needs of \$70, wants of \$72 and savings of \$20. Is the budget balanced? If not, how large is the problem, and how could he fix it?
step: Total planned spending and saving: $\$70 + \$72 + \$20 = \$162$.
step: Compare with income: $\$150 - \$162 = -\$12$, so there is a deficit of \$12.
step: Wants are the easiest to cut. Reducing wants from \$72 to \$60 removes the deficit: $\$70 + \$60 + \$20 = \$150$.
answer: Deficit of \$12; cut wants by \$12 (or earn \$12 more).

@mistakes
- Leaving savings out of the budget, so nothing is left for goals.
- Using last month's spending as this month's income. Budget with money you actually expect.
- Fixing a deficit by cutting savings first instead of wants.
- Treating the 50/30/20 guideline as a law instead of a starting point.
