@article
A company's consolidated margin is a weighted average. A profitable consumer brand may be hiding a loss-making division, or a single country may supply most of the earnings. **Segment** and **geographic** disclosures let you take the average apart. For multinationals, the statements are also affected by exchange rates, which are an additional source of change that may have nothing to do with operations.

### Segment reporting

Under ASC 280 (US GAAP) and IFRS 8, large public companies report by **operating segments** using the **management approach**: the segments are the components that the chief operating decision maker reviews. An operating segment is a **reportable segment** if it meets any one of three 10% tests:

1. **Revenue** (including intersegment revenue) is at least 10% of the combined revenue of all operating segments.
2. The absolute value of its **profit or loss** is at least 10% of the greater of (a) the combined profit of all profitable segments and (b) the absolute combined loss of all loss-making segments.
3. Its **assets** are at least 10% of the combined assets of all operating segments.

In addition, reportable segments together must account for at least **75% of consolidated external revenue**; if not, further segments are added. Other segments are combined as "all other." US GAAP's ASU 2023-07 (effective for annual periods beginning after December 15, 2023) requires more disclosure of significant segment expenses and the measure of profit management uses.

Analysts compute for each segment:

$$\text{Segment margin} = \frac{\text{Segment operating profit}}{\text{Segment revenue}}, \qquad \text{Segment ROA} = \frac{\text{Segment operating profit}}{\text{Segment assets}}, \qquad \text{Segment asset turnover} = \frac{\text{Segment revenue}}{\text{Segment assets}},$$

(using year-end or average segment assets, stated). They also look at **geographic concentration** (a country with political or currency risk), **customer concentration** (a customer above 10% of revenue must be disclosed) and mix shifts: consolidated margin can fall even when every segment's margin is steady if sales shift toward lower-margin segments.

### Foreign currency translation

A foreign subsidiary keeps its books in its **functional currency**, the currency of its primary economic environment. The parent consolidates in its **presentation currency**, so the statements must be converted. The method depends on the functional currency.

- **Current rate method** (translation), used when the functional currency is the local currency. Assets and liabilities are translated at the **current (closing) rate**, income and expenses at the **average rate** for the period, and equity at **historical rates**. The balancing item, the **cumulative translation adjustment** (CTA), goes to other comprehensive income and accumulates in equity, not in net income. Ratios computed from a single translated statement are unchanged, except those that mix rates (such as a return on average equity).
- **Temporal method** (remeasurement), used when the functional currency is the parent's currency, or when the economy is highly inflationary under US GAAP. **Monetary** items (cash, receivables, payables, debt) are translated at the current rate, **nonmonetary** items (inventory at cost, PPE, equity) at historical rates, and the **remeasurement gain or loss goes to net income**, making earnings more volatile. Net monetary assets lose value when the foreign currency weakens.

An economy is highly inflationary under US GAAP when cumulative three-year inflation reaches about 100%; IFRS (IAS 29) instead restates the statements for inflation before translation.

For the net assets of a foreign subsidiary under the current rate method, with an exchange rate quoted in parent-currency units per unit of foreign currency:

$$\text{CTA} = \text{Beginning net assets} \times (S_{end} - S_{begin}) + \text{Net income} \times (S_{end} - S_{avg}),$$

assuming no dividends or other equity changes. A foreign currency that appreciates produces a translation gain on positive net assets.

### Constant-currency growth

To separate underlying growth from currency, restate the current year's foreign revenue at the previous year's exchange rates:

$$\text{Constant-currency growth} = \frac{\text{Current revenue at prior-year rates}}{\text{Prior-year revenue}} - 1.$$

@example
title: Which segments are reportable?
problem: Fenwick Industries has five operating segments (millions of dollars). Apply the three 10% tests and the 75% revenue test, and compute margins for the reportable segments.

| Segment | Revenue | Operating profit | Assets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer | 1,800 | 270 | 1,500 |
| Industrial | 1,200 | 150 | 1,100 |
| Medical | 600 | 120 | 700 |
| Other | 150 | -30 | 100 |
| Services | 250 | 15 | 300 |
step: Totals: revenue 4,000; assets 3,700; profit of profitable segments $270 + 150 + 120 + 15 = 555$; loss of loss-making segments 30. The profit test uses the greater, 555, so its threshold is 55.5. Revenue threshold is 400; asset threshold is 370.
step: Consumer, Industrial and Medical pass every test. Other: revenue 150, profit 30, assets 100 all fall short. Services: revenue 250, profit 15, assets 300 all fall short. So Consumer, Industrial and Medical are reportable.
step: Their revenue is $1800 + 1200 + 600 = 3{,}600$, or $90\%$ of 4,000, above 75%, so no more segments are added.
step: Margins: Consumer $270/1800 = 15.0\%$, Industrial $150/1200 = 12.5\%$, Medical $120/600 = 20.0\%$. Segment ROA (year-end assets): 18.0%, 13.6% and 17.1%.
answer: Consumer, Industrial and Medical are reportable (90% of revenue); margins 15.0%, 12.5% and 20.0%.

@example
title: Translation adjustment and constant currency
problem: A foreign subsidiary (currency FC) has net assets of FC 1,000 at the start of the year and earns FC 100 in net income evenly through the year, with no dividends. The parent's currency rates (parent units per FC) are 1.20 at the start, 1.25 on average and 1.30 at the end. Compute the cumulative translation adjustment under the current rate method. If the subsidiary's revenue is FC 800 this year and FC 760 last year, with last year's average rate 1.20 and this year's 1.25, compute reported growth and constant-currency growth in parent currency.
step: Ending net assets are FC 1,100, translated at 1.30 to 1,430. Beginning net assets translate to $1000 \times 1.20 = 1{,}200$. Net income translates at the average rate to $100 \times 1.25 = 125$.
step: $\text{CTA} = 1430 - 1200 - 125 = 105$, a gain recorded in other comprehensive income. Check with the formula: $1000 \times 0.10 + 100 \times 0.05 = 105$.
step: Revenue in parent currency: this year $800 \times 1.25 = 1{,}000$; last year $760 \times 1.20 = 912$. Reported growth is $1000/912 - 1 = 9.65\%$.
step: At last year's rate, this year's revenue is $800 \times 1.20 = 960$, so constant-currency growth is $960/912 - 1 = 5.26\%$ (equal to local growth, $800/760 - 1$). About 4.4 points of reported growth came from the stronger currency.
answer: CTA gain 105; reported growth 9.65%, constant-currency growth 5.26%.

@mistakes
- Forgetting that a segment meeting any one of the three 10% tests is reportable.
- Putting the translation adjustment in net income under the current rate method; it goes to other comprehensive income. Remeasurement gains and losses under the temporal method go to net income.
- Reading reported revenue growth as organic growth when currency movements may account for much of it.
- Assuming segment profit is measured like consolidated net income: segments usually report an internal measure before interest and tax, so reconcile it to the consolidated results.

@practice num
prompt: A foreign subsidiary has beginning net assets of FC 800 and earns net income of FC 60 evenly through the year, with no dividends. Parent-currency rates per FC: 1.10 at the start, 1.14 average, 1.20 at year-end. Under the current rate method, what is the cumulative translation adjustment for the year, in parent currency to one decimal place?
answer: 83.6
tol: 0.1
check: 860 * 1.20 - 800 * 1.10 - 60 * 1.14
hint: Translate ending net assets at the closing rate, subtract translated beginning net assets and translated net income.
solution: Ending net assets FC 860 at 1.20 are 1,032; beginning net assets are $800 \times 1.10 = 880$; net income is $60 \times 1.14 = 68.4$. CTA $= 1032 - 880 - 68.4 = 83.6$, a gain in other comprehensive income.
