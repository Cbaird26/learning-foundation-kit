This is a learning exercise in applying the course, not an investment recommendation.

step: Choose two public companies in the same industry (so that the ratios are comparable) with at least five years of annual reports. Download the Form 10-K filings from SEC EDGAR (or the equivalent regulator's site for a non-US company) and look up any current market data yourself; do not rely on this course for current figures. Record the accounting framework (US GAAP or IFRS), the inventory method and the fiscal year-end of each.

step: Compute the ratios in the ratio sheet below (use the definitions given, with average balances), a three-step and five-step DuPont decomposition of ROE for each year, and the cash conversion cycle.
step: Analyze cash flow: CFO to net income, FCFF and FCFE (state the definitions you use), and the accruals ratio. Compute the red-flag indices (DSRI, GMI, SGI) for the latest year. Read the revenue recognition, inventory, lease, segment and debt footnotes.

Run at least one sensitivity (for example growth plus or minus two points and gross margin plus or minus one point).
step: Write a four-page report: purpose and audience, company and industry context, key findings from each analytical area (with two or three tables or charts), the forecast and sensitivity, risks and a conclusion that follows from the evidence. Put definitions and adjustments in an appendix.
material: **Ratio sheet (definitions to use; state any variation).**

| Ratio | Definition |
|---|---|
| Gross, operating and net margin | Gross profit, EBIT and net income divided by revenue |
| ROA, ROE | Net income divided by average total assets and average equity |
| Asset turnover, leverage | Revenue / average assets; average assets / average equity |
| DSO, DIO, DPO | 365 divided by receivables, inventory and payables turnover (average balances; revenue for receivables, COGS for the others) |
| Current, quick | Current assets / current liabilities; (cash + securities + receivables) / current liabilities |
| Debt to equity, net debt to EBITDA | Interest-bearing debt (and lease liabilities in an adjusted version) / equity; (debt - cash and securities) / EBITDA |
| Interest coverage | EBIT / interest expense |
| CFO to net income, accruals ratio | CFO / NI; (NI - CFO) / average total assets |

## Week-by-week
1. **Framework and sources.** Six steps, starting with purpose; 10-K (audited, annual), 10-Q (unaudited), 8-K (events), proxy; the audit opinion gives reasonable, not absolute, assurance; $A = L + E$ and the equity roll-forward.
2. **Common-size and trend.** Income statement items over revenue, balance sheet items over total assets; base-year index; $\text{CAGR} = (X_n/X_0)^{1/n} - 1$ with $n$ periods.
3. **Profitability and DuPont.** Margins, ROA, ROE with average balances; $\text{ROE} = \text{margin} \times \text{turnover} \times \text{leverage}$; five-step adds tax burden and interest burden.
4. **Activity.** Turnover and days (DSO, DIO, DPO); $\text{CCC} = \text{DIO} + \text{DSO} - \text{DPO}$; payables on COGS or purchases.
5. **Liquidity and solvency.** Current, quick, cash, defensive interval; debt-to-equity, interest and fixed charge coverage, net debt to EBITDA.
6. **Cash flow.** Indirect CFO; $\text{FCFF} = \text{CFO} + \text{Int}(1-t) - \text{FCInv}$ (interest paid inside CFO, as under US GAAP); $\text{FCFE} = \text{CFO} - \text{FCInv} + \text{net borrowing}$; US GAAP and IFRS classification.
7. **Earnings quality.** Accruals ratio $(\text{NI} - \text{CFO})/\text{avg assets}$; DSRI, GMI, SGI; recurring "one-time" items; non-GAAP reconciliations.
8. **Adjustments.** LIFO to FIFO ($R$, change in $R$, $R(1-t)$); capitalizing versus expensing; IFRS and US GAAP differences.
9. **Inventory, long-lived assets, leases.** NRV write-downs; average age, total life and remaining life; ASC 842 and IFRS 16, adjusted debt and EBITDA.
10. **Segments and international.** The 10% tests and 75% rule; current rate and temporal methods; CTA; constant-currency growth.
11. **Credit analysis.** Four Cs; debt to EBITDA, FFO to debt; covenant headroom; Altman Z; seniority and structural subordination.
12. **Forecasting.** Drivers, roll-forwards, cash or revolver as the plug, AFN, circularity, sensitivity.

## Self-check
- Can I say which definition (average or ending balance, debt, EBITDA) each ratio uses?
- Can I decompose ROE both ways and explain a change?
- Can I restate a LIFO company, an expensing company and a lease?
- Can I tell a profit story from a cash story?
- Does my forecast balance, and does the cash flow check agree?

## Common errors
- Mixing average and ending balances, or definitions across companies.
- Subtracting percentages as growth rates, or counting data points instead of periods in a CAGR.
- Forgetting the tax effect in the LIFO equity adjustment; using the reserve rather than its change for COGS.
- Treating red flags as proof and the Z-score as a probability.

@exam num
prompt: Sable Brands reported revenue of \$2,400 million and net income of \$180 million. Total assets were \$2,000 million at the start of the year and \$2,200 million at the end; shareholders' equity was \$900 million at the start and \$1,000 million at the end. Using the three-step DuPont identity with average balances, compute return on equity as a percent to two decimal places.
answer: 18.95
unit: %
tol: 0.01
check: 180 / 2400 * (2400 / ((2000 + 2200) / 2)) * (((2000 + 2200) / 2) / ((900 + 1000) / 2)) * 100
hint: Net margin is $180/2400$; average assets are 2,100 and average equity is 950.
hint: The three factors are net margin, revenue over average assets, and average assets over average equity.
solution: Net margin $7.50\%$, asset turnover $2400/2100 = 1.1429$, leverage $2100/950 = 2.2105$. ROE $= 0.075 \times 1.1429 \times 2.2105 = 18.95\%$, equal to $180/950$.

@exam num
prompt: Sable Brands uses LIFO. It reports EBIT of \$300 million, depreciation and amortization of \$120 million, total debt of \$900 million and cash and securities of \$150 million. Its LIFO reserve was \$90 million at the start of the year and \$120 million at the end. Restate to FIFO and compute net debt to EBITDA to two decimal places, using FIFO EBIT.
answer: 1.67
unit: times
tol: 0.01
check: (900 - 150) / ((300 + (120 - 90)) + 120)
hint: FIFO EBIT is LIFO EBIT plus the change in the LIFO reserve.
hint: EBITDA is FIFO EBIT plus depreciation and amortization; net debt is debt minus cash and securities.
solution: The reserve increased by 30, so FIFO EBIT is $300 + 30 = 330$ and FIFO EBITDA is $330 + 120 = 450$. Net debt is $900 - 150 = 750$, so the ratio is $750/450 = 1.67$ (against $750/420 = 1.79$ as reported).

prompt: Sable Brands reports the figures below (millions of dollars). Write a memo of about 150 to 200 words for a credit committee that computes at least three indicators, states what they suggest about earnings quality and credit risk, and lists the first two things you would check in the filings.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 2,100 | 2,400 |
| Cost of goods sold | 1,300 | 1,500 |
| Receivables (year-end) | 210 | 290 |
| Inventory (year-end) | 250 | 320 |
| Net income | 150 | 180 |
| Cash flow from operations | 170 | 120 |
| Total debt | 700 | 900 |
| Total assets (year-end) | 2,000 | 2,200 |
hint: Compare growth in revenue with growth in receivables and inventory, and net income with cash flow from operations.
hint: Compute DSRI, CFO to net income and the change in debt.
solution: Revenue grew 14.3% but receivables grew 38.1% (DSRI 1.21: receivables to revenue rose from 10.0% to 12.1%) and inventory grew 28.0%, so working capital absorbed cash. Net income rose 20% while CFO fell from 170 to 120, so CFO to net income dropped from 1.13 to 0.67 and the Year 2 accruals ratio, $(180 - 120)/2100$, is about 2.9% of average assets (using year-end Year 1 and Year 2 assets). Debt rose from 700 to 900 (28.6%), funding the shortfall, so leverage is rising while operating cash weakens. Together these suggest weaker earnings quality and a thinner debt cushion, though not proof of manipulation. First checks: the revenue recognition and customer credit terms disclosures (and the allowance for doubtful accounts) and the inventory note for aging, write-downs and the LIFO reserve; then covenant terms on the new debt.
