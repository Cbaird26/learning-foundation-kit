@article
Two companies both earn a 14.8% return on shareholders' equity. One does it by earning a thin margin on very high sales volume with modest debt; the other earns a fat margin but leans heavily on borrowed money. Same ROE, different businesses and risks. **DuPont analysis** takes a return apart so you can see which.

### Margins

Each margin divides a level of profit by revenue:

$$\text{Gross margin} = \frac{\text{Revenue} - \text{COGS}}{\text{Revenue}}, \quad \text{Operating margin} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Revenue}}, \quad \text{Net margin} = \frac{\text{Net income}}{\text{Revenue}}.$$

EBIT is earnings before interest and taxes (operating income, in the simple case where there are no other items). EBITDA margin adds back depreciation and amortization first.

### Returns, and which definition we use

Returns divide profit by the capital that produced it. Capital is measured at a point in time but profit accrues over the year, so **this course uses average balances** (beginning plus ending, divided by two) unless stated; using ending balances flatters or penalizes fast-growing companies and is a common variant.

$$\text{ROA} = \frac{\text{Net income}}{\text{Average total assets}}, \qquad \text{ROE} = \frac{\text{Net income}}{\text{Average shareholders' equity}}.$$

Variants of ROA exist. Because assets are financed by both creditors and owners, **ROA before financing** adds back after-tax interest to the numerator: $(\text{NI} + \text{Interest}\times(1-t))/\text{Average assets}$. State which you use. Likewise, **return on invested capital** (ROIC) divides net operating profit after tax, $\text{EBIT}(1-t)$, by average invested capital (debt plus equity, often net of cash).

### The three-step DuPont identity

$$\text{ROE} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{NI}}{\text{Revenue}}}_{\text{net margin}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Avg. assets}}}_{\text{asset turnover}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Avg. assets}}{\text{Avg. equity}}}_{\text{financial leverage}}.$$

The identity is exact (the terms cancel) when the same averages are used throughout. Net margin measures pricing and cost control, turnover measures how hard assets work, and leverage measures how much of the assets owners finance with other people's money. Leverage raises ROE when the return on assets exceeds the cost of debt, but it also raises risk.

### The five-step DuPont identity

Splitting net margin further isolates taxes and interest:

$$\text{ROE} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{NI}}{\text{EBT}}}_{\text{tax burden}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{EBT}}{\text{EBIT}}}_{\text{interest burden}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Revenue}}}_{\text{EBIT margin}} \times \text{Asset turnover} \times \text{Leverage}.$$

The tax burden is one minus the effective tax rate. The interest burden is 1 if there is no interest; it falls as interest consumes more of EBIT. A falling ROE with a stable EBIT margin often shows up as a falling interest burden: operations are fine, but financing costs are eating the return.

### Reading the decomposition

Compare the factors, not only the product. Low-margin, high-turnover retailers and high-margin, low-turnover capital-intensive firms can earn similar ROA. Sustained ROE built on rising leverage deserves scrutiny, and ROE can be distorted when equity is small or negative after buybacks or losses.

@example
title: Same ROE, two different routes
problem: Compute the three-step and five-step DuPont factors for these two companies (millions of dollars, averages for the balance sheet items).

| | Bellwether Foods | Larkspur Outfitters |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 3,200 | 2,000 |
| EBIT | 320 | 160 |
| Pretax income (EBT) | 256 | 132 |
| Net income | 192 | 99 |
| Average total assets | 3,200 | 1,750 |
| Average equity | 1,300 | 670 |
step: Bellwether: net margin $192/3200 = 6.00\%$, turnover $3200/3200 = 1.000$, leverage $3200/1300 = 2.4615$. ROE $= 0.06 \times 1.000 \times 2.4615 = 14.77\%$, which equals $192/1300$.
step: Larkspur: net margin $99/2000 = 4.95\%$, turnover $2000/1750 = 1.1429$, leverage $1750/670 = 2.6119$. ROE $= 0.0495 \times 1.1429 \times 2.6119 = 14.78\%$, equal to $99/670$.
step: Five-step for Bellwether: tax burden $192/256 = 0.750$, interest burden $256/320 = 0.800$, EBIT margin $10.0\%$. For Larkspur: $99/132 = 0.750$, $132/160 = 0.825$, EBIT margin $8.0\%$.
step: Both earn about 14.8%. Bellwether has the higher EBIT margin and the lower turnover and leverage; Larkspur turns assets faster and uses slightly more leverage but earns a thinner operating margin. Bellwether's interest burden (0.800) is lower, meaning interest takes a larger share of its EBIT.
answer: ROE is 14.77% for Bellwether and 14.78% for Larkspur, reached through different margin, turnover and leverage combinations.

@example
title: ROA with and without financing
problem: Bellwether Foods earned net income of 192 and paid interest of 64, with a 25% tax rate and average assets of 3,200 (millions of dollars). Compute ROA both ways.
step: ROA on net income: $192/3200 = 6.00\%$.
step: After-tax interest is $64 \times (1 - 0.25) = 48$, so ROA before financing is $(192 + 48)/3200 = 7.50\%$.
step: The 7.50% measures how productive the assets are regardless of how they were financed, which is the fair number to compare with the cost of debt.
answer: ROA is 6.00% on net income and 7.50% before financing costs.

@mistakes
- Mixing definitions across companies or years: ending balances for one company and averages for another changes the ratio, especially for growing firms.
- Computing the DuPont factors with ending balances in some terms and averages in others, so the product no longer equals ROE.
- Saying higher leverage is "good" because it raised ROE, without noting that it also raises financial risk.
- Treating ROA on net income as if it excluded the effect of debt; interest has already been deducted from the numerator.

model:
```python
def dupont5(net_income, ebt, ebit, revenue, avg_assets, avg_equity):
    """Five-step DuPont decomposition of return on equity."""
    tax_burden = net_income / ebt
    interest_burden = ebt / ebit
    ebit_margin = ebit / revenue
    asset_turnover = revenue / avg_assets
    leverage = avg_assets / avg_equity
    roe = (tax_burden * interest_burden * ebit_margin
           * asset_turnover * leverage)
    return {
        "tax_burden": tax_burden,
        "interest_burden": interest_burden,
        "ebit_margin": ebit_margin,
        "asset_turnover": asset_turnover,
        "leverage": leverage,
        "roe": roe,
    }
```
hint: Each factor is one ratio of two inputs; compute them one by one.
hint: ROE is the product of all five factors, and it should equal net income divided by average equity.
solution: Compute the five ratios, multiply them for ROE, and return all six values in a dictionary. The intermediate terms cancel, so the product equals net income over average equity, which the tests confirm for two data sets.
