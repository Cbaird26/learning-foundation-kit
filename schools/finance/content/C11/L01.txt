@article
A friend asks whether a regional retailer is "a good company." You cannot answer until you know the question: a lender asks whether the retailer can repay a loan, an equity investor asks whether its profits will grow, and a supplier asks whether it will pay its invoices on time. **Financial statement analysis** is the disciplined use of a company's reported information to answer a specific decision question. The numbers are the same; the questions, and so the ratios and adjustments that matter, differ.

### A six-step framework

Analysts commonly work through the same sequence, and you will use it all course:

1. **Define the purpose and context** of the analysis: who the audience is, what decision it supports, how deep it must be.
2. **Collect the data**: financial statements, footnotes, industry and economic data, and discussions with management where possible.
3. **Process the data**: compute ratios and common-size statements, make adjustments for comparability, build forecasts.
4. **Analyze and interpret** the processed data against history, competitors and the economy.
5. **Develop and communicate conclusions**: a report or recommendation that states assumptions and limits.
6. **Follow up**: update the analysis as new information arrives.

Most mistakes come from skipping step 1 (computing ratios with no question in mind) or from stopping at step 3 (reporting ratios with no interpretation).

### Where the information is

For a US public company, the **SEC** requires periodic disclosure, and every filing is free on **EDGAR**. For other countries use the national regulator or the company's investor relations page.

| Source | What it contains |
|---|---|
| Form 10-K | Annual report: audited financial statements, footnotes, management's discussion and analysis (MD&A), risk factors, business description. Due 60, 75 or 90 days after fiscal year-end, depending on the company's size (large accelerated, accelerated, other filers). |
| Form 10-Q | Quarterly report for each of the first three quarters; unaudited, condensed statements. Due 40 days after quarter-end for large accelerated and accelerated filers, 45 days for others. |
| Form 8-K | Report of major events (acquisitions, executive departures, results announcements), generally within four business days. |
| Proxy statement (DEF 14A) | Executive compensation, board composition, related-party transactions, shareholder proposals. |

The **footnotes** are part of the statements. They give accounting policies, details of debt, leases, income taxes, segments, contingencies and subsequent events; many warning signs and adjustments in later weeks come from them. **MD&A** is management's own explanation of results, liquidity and critical accounting estimates: useful, but written by the people being analyzed. The IFRS equivalent of an annual report is read the same way, and under IFRS 18, which replaces IAS 1 for annual periods beginning on or after January 1, 2027, the income statement will gain new operating, investing and financing categories (these standards change, so check the current text).

### The auditor's report

An independent auditor gives an opinion on whether the statements are fairly presented. An **unqualified (clean) opinion** says the statements present fairly, in all material respects, the financial position and results under the applicable framework (US GAAP or IFRS). A **qualified opinion** flags a specific departure or limitation; an **adverse opinion** says the statements are materially misstated; a **disclaimer** means the auditor could not form an opinion. The report may also contain an explanatory paragraph on substantial doubt about **going concern**, and for US public companies **critical audit matters** (CAMs), the areas of most challenging judgment. A clean opinion gives reasonable, not absolute, assurance; it is not a guarantee against fraud and says nothing about whether the company is a good investment.

### Statements must fit together

The statements are linked by identities you can use as checks:

$$\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}$$

$$\text{Ending equity} = \text{Beginning equity} + \text{Net income} - \text{Dividends} - \text{Repurchases} + \text{Stock issued or stock compensation} + \text{Other comprehensive income}$$

For retained earnings alone, ending $=$ beginning $+$ net income $-$ dividends. When a line you computed does not reconcile, you have either missed an item or found something worth asking about.

@example
title: Checking that the statements fit together
problem: Fernhill Outfitters (millions of dollars) reports retained earnings of 340 at the end of Year 1, net income of 130 and dividends of 70 in Year 2. Its Year 2 year-end balance sheet is below. Confirm that the balance sheet balances and that the retained earnings line is consistent.

| Assets | | Liabilities and equity | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | 120 | Accounts payable | 190 |
| Accounts receivable | 210 | Accrued liabilities | 110 |
| Inventory | 330 | Current debt | 60 |
| Other current assets | 40 | Long-term debt | 640 |
| Property, plant and equipment, net | 900 | Other long-term liabilities | 100 |
| Goodwill | 150 | Common stock and APIC | 300 |
| Other long-term assets | 50 | Retained earnings | 400 |
step: Add the assets: $120 + 210 + 330 + 40 + 900 + 150 + 50 = 1{,}800$.
step: Add liabilities: $190 + 110 + 60 + 640 + 100 = 1{,}100$. Add equity: $300 + 400 = 700$. Liabilities plus equity is $1{,}100 + 700 = 1{,}800$, so the balance sheet balances.
step: Roll retained earnings forward: $340 + 130 - 70 = 400$, which matches the balance sheet line.
answer: Both checks hold: assets and liabilities plus equity are each 1,800, and retained earnings roll forward to 400.

@example
title: Reading a filing calendar
problem: Bellwether Foods is a large accelerated filer with a December 31 fiscal year-end. A lender wants the company's audited annual financials and its latest unaudited quarterly results. Which filings should the lender read, and what limits apply to each?
step: Audited annual financials are in the Form 10-K, due within 60 days of year-end for a large accelerated filer (about March 1).
step: The latest quarter is in the Form 10-Q, due within 40 days of quarter-end. The 10-Q is condensed and unaudited (reviewed, not audited), so it has fewer footnotes.
step: Between filings, a Form 8-K reports events such as an acquisition or the departure of the chief financial officer within four business days, and results press releases are often furnished under it.
answer: Read the 10-K for audited annual data, the latest 10-Q for interim data (unaudited, condensed), and 8-Ks for events since.

@mistakes
- Computing ratios before defining the question: a lender, a shareholder and a supplier need different measures.
- Treating a clean audit opinion as a guarantee that the company is sound or that no fraud exists.
- Ignoring the footnotes and MD&A, where leases, debt terms, contingencies and accounting-policy changes are disclosed.
- Mixing up the filings: the 10-K is audited and annual; the 10-Q is unaudited and quarterly (there is no 10-Q for the fourth quarter).

@practice self
prompt: A bank is deciding whether to lend to a mid-sized manufacturer. Walk through the six steps of the analysis framework for this decision, naming at least one specific piece of information you would collect and one measure you would compute.
hint: Begin with the decision the bank must make, then follow the steps in order.
solution: Purpose: assess whether the manufacturer can service and repay the loan, so the audience is a credit committee and the horizon is the loan term. Collect: three to five years of audited statements and footnotes (debt terms, leases, contingencies), the latest 10-Q or interim statements, industry data and any existing covenants. Process: compute liquidity and solvency ratios such as the current ratio, debt to EBITDA and interest coverage, common-size statements, and cash flow from operations to debt; adjust for leases and one-time items. Analyze: compare with prior years and with competitors, and stress the forecast cash flow for a downturn. Communicate: write a credit memo with a recommendation, key assumptions and conditions such as covenants. Follow up: monitor quarterly filings and covenant compliance.
rubric: States the purpose and audience (credit decision, ability to repay) before listing computations.
rubric: Names specific data to collect and specific measures, such as interest coverage or debt to EBITDA, appropriate to a lender.
rubric: Covers all six steps in order, including communicating conclusions and following up.
