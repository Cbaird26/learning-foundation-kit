@project
title: Initiation report on a public company
overview: Research a real public company of your choice from its SEC EDGAR filings and write a 10 to 15 page initiation report with a tested model, a valuation, a price target, risks and an ESG and governance review. Revise it after a peer review, record a ten-minute pitch and answer five hard questions. This is a learning exercise to practice the craft of research, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security.
goal: Produce a professional-quality initiation report whose sections all serve one stated thesis.
goal: Build a linked forecast model and valuation that you have tested and can defend, with a price target shown as a range and a rating on a framework you state.
goal: Practice review, revision and oral defense: respond to a peer's comments in a revision log and answer hard questions honestly.
step: Choose the company (weeks 1 and 2). Pick a US-listed company whose business you can explain in a paragraph, with at least five years of filings on SEC EDGAR. Screen it with market capitalization, enterprise value, EV/EBITDA and earnings yield from the latest 10-K or 10-Q and current data you look up and date. Write the one-page proposal using the template in the material below: company, research question, why it matters to value, sources, schedule and the biggest risk to your plan.
step: Business, industry and moat (weeks 3 and 4). From the 10-K's Business section, segment note and MD&A, build revenue from drivers, size the market, compute concentration or share where data exist, and apply the five forces and life cycle. Compute NOPAT, invested capital, ROIC and economic profit for five years, and write the moat assessment with supporting and contradicting evidence. Draft these sections.
step: Financial analysis and model (weeks 5 and 6). Put five years of statements in a spreadsheet, compute growth, common-size statements, DuPont, DSO, DIO, DPO, the cash conversion cycle, CFO to net income and free cash flow conversion, and write what, why and so what for each finding. Build the assumptions sheet, schedules and linked three statements with a Checks tab. Optionally cross-check the DCF with a short Python function on your own computer.
step: Valuation and risks (weeks 7 and 8). Compute FCFF, WACC (record every input and its date and source), terminal value, equity value per share and the terminal value share. Cross-check with peer multiples, combine into a twelve-month price target and apply a stated rating framework. Build bull, base and bear scenarios with probabilities, a WACC and growth sensitivity table, a reverse DCF, three to five ranked risks and a dated catalyst calendar. Present the draft valuation to a peer or mentor for feedback.
step: ESG, governance and full draft (weeks 9 and 10). From the 10-K and the proxy statement (DEF 14A), find the control structure, board independence, pay design, say-on-pay result, auditor tenure and fees, and the material environmental and social exposures. Present facts neutrally and with balance, quantify at least one exposure with a stated hypothetical assumption, and check the current status of any disclosure rule you cite. Assemble the full report with a front page, exhibits, sources, disclosures and certification, then run the number audit.
step: Review, revise and pitch (weeks 11 and 12). Exchange drafts with a peer, mentor or study group if you can; otherwise review your own draft after a week away, using the review checklist. Write a revision log with each comment, its importance, your response (fix, counter or decline) and what changed. Record a ten-minute pitch and write and rehearse answers to the five hard questions.

## Key ideas by week

**Weeks 1 and 2: choosing and planning.** Choose a company you can explain in a paragraph, with filings on SEC EDGAR (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A) and a specific question tied to value. Screen with market cap, EV $= \text{market cap} + \text{debt} + \text{preferred} + \text{noncontrolling interests} - \text{cash}$, EV/EBITDA and earnings yield. Plan backward from the deadline with a buffer; the proposal has no price target. Standards V(A), V(B), V(C), I(C); FINRA Rule 2241 and Regulation AC for broker-dealer research.

**Weeks 3 and 4: business, industry, moat.** Revenue is volume times price; market share is company over market; $HHI = \sum s_i^2$; the five forces and life cycle support the forecast. $ROIC = NOPAT/\text{invested capital}$, economic profit $= (ROIC - WACC) \times IC$; moat sources need evidence and counter-evidence; $LTV = g/(1 + d - \rho)$.

**Weeks 5 and 6: analysis and model.** DuPont, $DSO$, $DIO$, $DPO$, the cash conversion cycle, CFO to net income, accruals, FCF conversion, and reconciliation of non-GAAP measures. Model with one input sheet, drivers, schedules, interest on average or beginning balances and checks that show zero.

**Weeks 7 and 8: valuation and risk.** $FCFF = EBIT(1-t) + D\&A - \text{capex} - \Delta NWC$; $WACC$; $TV = FCFF_N(1+g)/(WACC - g)$; equity bridge; $P_{12} = V_0(1+k_e) - D$; expected return and rating framework. Scenarios $E[V] = \sum p_i V_i$, upside/downside ratio, sensitivity tables, reverse DCF, ranked risks and dated catalysts.

**Weeks 9 and 10: ESG, governance, writing.** Materiality first; the proxy statement for control, board, pay and audit; voting power versus economic ownership; hypothetical carbon price scenarios; balanced, factual language; SEC climate rule not in effect, EU rules being simplified (check current). A front page that works alone, a number audit, attribution and disclosures.

**Weeks 11 and 12: review and pitch.** Review top-down (thesis, evidence, model, numbers, disclosures, language); rank comments; respond with fix, counter or decline; keep a revision log. Pitch in ten minutes (about 1,400 words), lead with the answer, prepare the five hard questions and never bluff.

## Self-check list

- Can I state my thesis in two sentences and name its biggest risk?
- Does my balance sheet balance and cash tie in every year?
- Is the terminal value share reported, with a sensitivity table?
- Does every number on the front page match the model?
- Are my ESG statements facts, sourced, with balance?

## Common errors

Discounting FCFF at the cost of equity; terminal growth at or above WACC; subtracting net debt twice; using revenue for DIO and DPO; treating "one-time" charges that recur as one-time; stating judgments as facts; inconsistent numbers; scenario probabilities set to fit a rating; bluffing in Q&A.

prompt: Ridgeway Foods (fictional) forecasts next year's EBIT of \$240 million, a 21% tax rate, depreciation and amortization of \$60 million, capital expenditures of \$85 million and a \$15 million increase in net working capital. A single-stage DCF uses a WACC of 8% and perpetual growth of 3%, with FCFF growing at 3% from next year's level. Net debt is \$600 million and there are 80 million diluted shares. What is the equity value per share? Round to the nearest cent.
answer: 29.9
lead: \$
tol: 0.01
check: ((240 * 0.79 + 60 - 85 - 15) / (0.08 - 0.03) - 600) / 80
hint: First compute FCFF as EBIT(1 - t) plus D&A minus capex minus the increase in working capital.
hint: Enterprise value is $FCFF_1/(WACC - g)$; then subtract net debt and divide by shares.
solution: $FCFF_1 = 240(0.79) + 60 - 85 - 15 = 189.6 - 40 = \$149.6$ million. $EV = 149.6/0.05 = \$2{,}992$ million. Equity value is $2{,}992 - 600 = \$2{,}392$ million, and per share $2{,}392/80 = \$29.90$.

@exam num
prompt: A fictional stock trades at \$36.00 and is expected to pay a \$1.00 dividend over the next twelve months. Your twelve-month values are \$52 in the bull case (20% probability), \$38 in the base case (50%) and \$20 in the bear case (30%). What is the expected total return over twelve months, as a percent to one decimal place?
answer: 1.1
unit: %
tol: 0.1
check: ((0.2 * 52 + 0.5 * 38 + 0.3 * 20 + 1.0) / 36 - 1) * 100
hint: Compute the probability-weighted price first.
hint: Expected total return is $(E[P_{12}] + D)/P_0 - 1$.
solution: $E[P_{12}] = 0.2(52) + 0.5(38) + 0.3(20) = 10.4 + 19 + 6 = \$35.40$. Total return $= (35.40 + 1.00)/36.00 - 1 = 1.1\%$. On a framework with Overweight above +10% and Underweight below -10%, this is Equal-weight.

@exam self
prompt: You are the reviewer. A draft front page for fictional Ridgeway Foods reads: "We initiate at Buy with a price target of \$44 (price \$36), implying 28% upside. Our 2027 EPS estimate of \$2.20 implies a target multiple of 18 times. Our DCF uses a WACC of 8% and terminal growth of 3.5%; the terminal value is 90% of enterprise value. The company has a wide moat because margins were high last year. Governance is poor." Write a ranked review (about 200 words) with at least five specific comments, each with what, why it matters and a suggestion, and compute any numbers you cite.
hint: Check the upside and the implied multiple, then look at terminal value, moat evidence and the governance claim.
solution: Must fix: (1) Upside is $44/36 - 1 = 22.2\%$, not 28%; correct it everywhere it appears. (2) The implied multiple is $44/2.20 = 20.0$ times, not 18; check whether the target or the EPS is wrong, since the valuation tab drives the front page. (3) Terminal value is 90% of EV with growth of 3.5% only 4.5 points below WACC, so the result is extremely sensitive; show a WACC and growth table, add an exit-multiple cross-check and consider a longer explicit forecast. Should fix: (4) "Wide moat" rests on one year of margin; a moat needs ROIC against WACC over a cycle, a named source (switching costs, brand, cost) and contrary evidence. (5) "Governance is poor" is a judgment stated as fact; replace it with sourced facts from the proxy statement (control structure, board independence, pay, auditor) and note where views differ. Also: state the rating framework behind Buy and show the expected return, and check that the certification and disclosures are present.
rubric: Computes the corrected upside (22.2%) and implied multiple (20.0 times) and says to fix them wherever they appear.
rubric: Identifies the terminal value problem (90% of EV with growth close to WACC) and suggests sensitivity, an exit-multiple cross-check or a longer forecast.
rubric: Challenges the moat claim for lack of multi-year ROIC evidence and the governance claim as an unsourced judgment, with factual replacements.
rubric: Ranks the comments and gives each a location or reason it matters and a suggested fix, in constructive language.
