@article
Valuation is where the report commits. Readers forgive a rating they disagree with if your method is clear and your assumptions are visible; they do not forgive a price target that appears from nowhere. Use at least two methods and show where they agree and where they do not.

### Free cash flow to the firm

The cash available to all capital providers after reinvestment is

$$FCFF = EBIT(1 - t) + \text{D\&A} - \text{capex} - \Delta NWC.$$

Your model from week 6 produces this line for each forecast year. Check that it moves sensibly: a company growing 8% should be reinvesting.

### WACC

Discount FCFF at the **weighted average cost of capital**:

$$WACC = \frac{E}{V}\,k_e + \frac{D}{V}\,k_d (1 - t),$$

with market-value weights. The cost of equity usually comes from the CAPM, $k_e = r_f + \beta(ERP)$, where you state the risk-free rate (say, the 10-year Treasury yield on a date you record), the beta and its source, and your equity risk premium. Use hypothetical or current inputs you look up and cite; they change. WACC estimates are imprecise, so show a range in the sensitivity analysis (week 8).

### Terminal value

After the explicit forecast, the **Gordon growth** terminal value is

$$TV_N = \frac{FCFF_N (1 + g)}{WACC - g},$$

where $g$ must be below WACC and consistent with long-run nominal growth of the economy. It is discounted back $N$ years. The cross-check is an **exit multiple**: what EV/EBITDA does the terminal value imply, and is that plausible against mature peers? In many DCFs the terminal value is 60% to 80% of enterprise value, so report the share and defend it.

### From enterprise value to a share price

$$\text{Equity value} = EV - \text{debt} - \text{preferred} - \text{noncontrolling interests} + \text{cash}, \qquad \text{value per share} = \frac{\text{equity value}}{\text{diluted shares}}.$$

Treat leases and pension deficits consistently with the cash flows: if the full lease cost is already deducted in EBIT (as with operating leases under US GAAP), do not also subtract lease liabilities; if leases are treated as debt (as under IFRS 16, where EBIT excludes the interest part), subtract them with the other debt. Use diluted shares from the treasury stock method.

### Relative valuation

Compare multiples with peers selected for similar business, growth and risk: P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/sales and free cash flow yield. Peers' current multiples come from your data source on the date you state; adjust for differences in growth and margins rather than averaging blindly. A target multiple times your forecast of the matching metric gives a value: $\text{target price} = \text{target P/E} \times \text{EPS}$.

### Price target and rating

A **price target** is the expected price in twelve months, so roll a present value forward: $P_{12} = V_0(1 + k_e) - D$, where $D$ is dividends expected in the year. Combine methods with stated weights, for example half DCF and half multiples. The **expected total return** is $(P_{12} + D)/P_0 - 1$. A rating maps that return onto bands. Firms define their own bands; the ones in this lesson's example are invented. What matters is that you state yours, apply them consistently, and say what you would need to see to change the rating. Also disclose how the target could fail. This work is a learning exercise and not advice.

@example
title: DCF for Corvane
problem: Corvane Instruments (fictional) forecasts FCFF of \$118, \$131, \$144, \$156 and \$166 million for 2026 to 2030. WACC is 8.4%, terminal growth is 2.5%, net debt is \$275 million (debt \$410 million less cash \$135 million) and there are 62.5 million diluted shares. Find the value per share.
step: Discount each flow at 8.4%: the present values are 108.86, 111.48, 113.05, 112.98 and 110.91, which sum to \$557.28 million.
step: Terminal value at 2030: $166 \times 1.025/(0.084 - 0.025) = \$2{,}883.9$ million; its present value is $2{,}883.9/1.084^5 = \$1{,}926.8$ million.
step: Enterprise value: $557.28 + 1{,}926.79 = \$2{,}484.1$ million. Equity value: $2{,}484.1 - 275 = \$2{,}209.1$ million.
step: Per share: $2{,}209.1/62.5 = \$35.35$. The terminal value is $1{,}926.8/2{,}484.1 = 77.6\%$ of EV, which is within the usual range but means the result leans on WACC and growth.
answer: \$35.35 per share; terminal value 77.6% of enterprise value.

@example
title: From value to price target and rating
problem: Corvane's DCF value is \$35.35 today, its cost of equity is 9.6% and it expects to pay a \$0.60 dividend. Its 2027 EPS forecast is \$2.62 and you choose a target P/E of 18. The share price is \$48.20. Weight the DCF and multiple methods equally, find the price target and the expected total return, and apply this framework: Overweight above +10%, Underweight below -10%, otherwise Equal-weight.
step: Twelve-month DCF value: $35.35 \times 1.096 - 0.60 = \$38.14$.
step: Multiple method: $18 \times 2.62 = \$47.16$.
step: Price target: $0.5 \times 38.14 + 0.5 \times 47.16 = \$42.65$.
step: Expected total return: $(42.65 + 0.60)/48.20 - 1 = -10.3\%$, which is just below the Underweight line.
step: Say so in the report: the rating rests on a result 0.3 points from the threshold, and the gap between the two methods (\$38 against \$47) means that the multiple's assumption of 18 times is doing a lot of work. That is the honest conclusion, and it points to the risks and sensitivities of week 8.
answer: Price target \$42.65; expected return -10.3%; Underweight on this framework, but close to the threshold.

@mistakes
- Discounting FCFF at the cost of equity (or equity cash flows at WACC), or using book-value weights in WACC.
- A terminal growth rate at or above WACC, or above the long-run growth of the economy, which makes the value explode.
- Subtracting net debt twice or forgetting noncontrolling interests, and dividing by basic instead of diluted shares.
- Announcing a price target without showing the method, the weights or the rating framework.

model:
```python
def dcf_value_per_share(fcffs, wacc, g, net_debt, shares):
    """Equity value per share from explicit FCFF and a Gordon terminal value."""
    if wacc <= g:
        raise ValueError("wacc must exceed g")
    pv = sum(cf / (1 + wacc) ** t for t, cf in enumerate(fcffs, start=1))
    terminal = fcffs[-1] * (1 + g) / (wacc - g)
    pv += terminal / (1 + wacc) ** len(fcffs)
    return (pv - net_debt) / shares
```
hint: Discount each flow by `(1 + wacc) ** t` with `t` starting at 1.
hint: Add the discounted terminal value (using the last year's discount factor), subtract net debt and divide by shares.
solution: Sum the present values of the explicit flows, add the present value of the Gordon terminal value (discounted by the number of forecast years), subtract net debt to get equity value, and divide by shares. Guard against a discount rate at or below the growth rate, which makes the formula meaningless.
