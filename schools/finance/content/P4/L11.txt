@article
The best model is worthless if the portfolio manager cannot find your conclusion in the first ten seconds. A research note is a decision document: it tells a busy reader what you think, why, how sure you are, and what would change your mind.

### Anatomy of a note

| Section | What it does |
|---|---|
| Header | Ticker, rating, price, price target, expected upside, market capitalization, date |
| Thesis | Two or three sentences, then three supporting points: why the market is wrong or why the stock should move |
| Estimates | Revenue, EBITDA, EPS and free cash flow for the next 2-3 years, versus prior estimates and consensus |
| Valuation | The method, the key assumptions, the scenarios and the sensitivity |
| Catalysts | Dated events that could bring the market to your view |
| Risks | The most important ways you could be wrong, with their likely size |
| Disclosures | Conflicts, ratings distribution and the analyst certification |

Put the conclusion first. Each paragraph begins with its point, and the supporting numbers follow. A one-page version (the **summary**) holds the header, thesis, a small estimates table, valuation and risks; the longer note adds the model's detail.

### Writing that earns trust

- **Be specific.** "Gross margin expands 120 basis points to 38.6% as the new plant ramps" beats "margins should improve".
- **Separate fact, estimate and opinion.** A reported figure is a fact with a source ("Revenue was \$840.0 million, 10-K, Item 8"). Your forecast is an estimate and says so. Your judgment ("we think the market underestimates...") is opinion.
- **Quantify risk.** "A 100 basis point lower gross margin cuts our target by about \$2.40 a share" is useful; "competition is a risk" is not.
- **Show the variant view.** The reader already knows the consensus; say where you differ and what evidence supports you.
- **Check the numbers.** Units, periods and signs must agree across the text, tables and model. Build the tables from the model in code, so the note cannot drift from the model.

### Rules and standards

Research is regulated because readers rely on it. In the United States, Regulation AC requires analysts to certify in the report that the views expressed reflect their personal views and to disclose whether any compensation is tied to the specific recommendations. FINRA Rule 2241 governs conflicts of interest in research by broker-dealers, including disclosures and the separation of research from investment banking. Where relevant, price targets need a stated basis and risk discussion, and the report must not promise returns.

The CFA Institute Standards apply to members and candidates wherever they work. **Standard V(A), Diligence and Reasonable Basis**, requires a reasonable and adequate basis, supported by research, for recommendations. **Standard V(B), Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients**, requires disclosing the basic format and general principles of the process, separating fact from opinion, and including important limitations and risks. **Standard I(C), Misrepresentation**, bars misleading statements, and **Standard VI(A), Disclosure of Conflicts**, requires disclosure of conflicts such as ownership or compensation that could bias the work. Material nonpublic information must never enter a note (Standard II(A)).

### Automating the routine

```python
def summary_line(ticker, rating, price, target):
    upside = target / price - 1
    return f"{ticker} | {rating} | Price ${price:,.2f} | Upside {upside:+.1%}"

print(summary_line("ABC", "Hold", 50, 55))
```

This prints `ABC | Hold | Price $50.00 | Upside +10.0%`. The format `:+.1%` shows the sign and one decimal place of a percentage. Keep the writing for the reader and the formatting for code.

@mistakes
- Burying the recommendation after pages of industry background instead of leading with it.
- Writing "the stock will double" or "no downside": research must not promise returns, and every claim needs a reasonable basis.
- Typing numbers into the note by hand, so the text, table and model disagree.
- Leaving out disclosures of conflicts, or presenting opinion as fact.

def summary_line(ticker, rating, price, target):
    """One-line header for a research note."""
    upside = target / price - 1

model:
```python
def missing_sections(note, required):
    """Required section headings that the note lacks."""
    headings = {line.strip().lower() for line in note.splitlines()}
    return [name for name in required if "## " + name.lower() not in headings]
```
hint: Build a set of the cleaned, lower-case lines first.
hint: A section is missing when `"## " + name` in lower case is not in that set.
solution: Normalize every line with `strip()` and `lower()`, collect them in a set, and return the required names whose heading line is absent, keeping the original order. The line "Valuation is later." does not count because a heading must be the whole line and start with `## `.

model:
```python
def format_estimates(years, revenues, eps):
    """Plain-text table of revenue and EPS estimates."""
    lines = ["Year  Revenue    EPS"]
    for year, revenue, earnings in zip(years, revenues, eps):
        lines.append(f"{year}  {revenue:>9,.1f}  {earnings:>5.2f}")
    return "\n".join(lines)
```
hint: Start a list with the header, then add one formatted line per year.
hint: Join the lines with a newline so there is no trailing newline.
solution: Build the header, loop over the three lists with `zip`, append each formatted line, and join with `"\n"`. Generating tables from model output with a function like this keeps the note consistent with the model.
