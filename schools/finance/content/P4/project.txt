### Week by week

1. **The job and the process.** Sell-side versus buy-side; idea, industry, company, forecast, valuation, communication, monitoring. Upside is $P_T/P_0 - 1$; total return adds dividends: $(P_T + D)/P_0 - 1$. Separate inputs from calculations and test.
2. **Industry analysis.** Market size and CAGR, concentration ratio and HHI ($\sum s_i^2$ with shares in percentage points), Porter's five forces, life cycle.
3. **Filings.** 10-K (audited), 10-Q, 8-K, proxy; Items 1, 1A, 7, 8. Discrete quarters from year-to-date data; LTM $= \text{FY} + \text{YTD}_{\text{current}} - \text{YTD}_{\text{prior}}$; read non-GAAP reconciliations.
4. **Revenue drivers.** Units times price; subscribers: ending $=$ beginning $\times (1-\text{churn}) +$ additions, revenue on the average; segments; sanity-check against market share.
5. **Margins.** Fixed plus variable costs; EBITDA, EBIT; $\text{DOL} = \%\Delta\text{EBIT}/\%\Delta\text{Revenue}$.
6. **Schedules.** DSO, DIO, DPO and the cash conversion cycle; an increase in NWC uses cash; PP&E roll-forward and depreciation waterfall.
7. **Debt.** Mandatory and optional repayment, minimum cash, cash sweep, interest on beginning or average balance and the circularity.
8. **Integration.** Net income to retained earnings; D&A add-back; cash from the cash flow statement; balance check with a tolerance.
9. **Valuation.** $\text{FCFF} = \text{EBIT}(1-t) + \text{D\&A} - \text{Capex} - \Delta\text{NWC}$; $\text{TV} = \text{FCFF}_N(1+g)/(\text{WACC}-g)$; EV to equity bridge; multiples.
10. **Targets.** Target multiple times forward EPS; $V_0(1+k_e) - D$; probability-weighted value; sensitivity tables.
11. **The note.** Conclusion first; fact, estimate, opinion; Regulation AC, FINRA Rule 2241 and CFA Standards V(A), V(B), I(C), VI(A).
12. **The pitch.** Five-minute structure; risk-reward $=(\text{Bull}-P)/(P-\text{Bear})$; break-even probability; reverse DCF growth $g = (EV \cdot \text{WACC} - \text{FCFF})/(EV + \text{FCFF})$.

### Common errors

Using ending subscribers for revenue; treating a working capital increase as a source of cash; letting depreciation run past an asset's life; sweeping more than the debt balance; subtracting interest from FCFF; discounting the terminal value the wrong number of years; adding debt instead of subtracting it in the bridge; comparing floats with `==`; plugging cash to force a balance; promising returns in the note.

model:
```python
def value_per_share(revenue, growth, ebit_margin, tax_rate, da_pct,
                    capex_pct, nwc_pct, wacc, g, net_debt, shares):
    """DCF value per share from a driver-based forecast."""
    if g >= wacc:
        raise ValueError("terminal growth must be below the WACC")
    previous = revenue
    flows = []
    for rate in growth:
        current = previous * (1 + rate)
        nopat = ebit_margin * current * (1 - tax_rate)
        delta_nwc = nwc_pct * (current - previous)
        flows.append(nopat + da_pct * current - capex_pct * current - delta_nwc)
        previous = current
    pv = sum(f / (1 + wacc) ** t for t, f in enumerate(flows, start=1))
    terminal = flows[-1] * (1 + g) / (wacc - g)
    enterprise = pv + terminal / (1 + wacc) ** len(flows)
    return (enterprise - net_debt) / shares
```
hint: Build the list of FCFF first, one per forecast year, carrying the previous year's revenue along.
hint: The working capital change uses the revenue increase, not revenue itself. Then discount, add the terminal value discounted by the last year's factor, subtract net debt and divide by shares.
solution: For each year compute revenue, NOPAT, D&A, capex and the NWC increase to get FCFF; discount the flows, add the discounted Gordon terminal value, subtract net debt for equity value and divide by the share count. The second test is a steady state: FCFF of 70 forever at 10% with no growth is worth 700, or 7 per share.

That pattern (an imbalance of twice one working capital change) points straight at the sign of that line.
