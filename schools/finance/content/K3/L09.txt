@article
We measure how long, how heavy and how much liquid with **units**. The same amount can be written in different units, like a friend with two names.

### Metric units

The metric system uses tens, so it is easy.

- Length: $10$ millimeters (mm) $= 1$ centimeter (cm). $100$ cm $= 1$ meter (m). $1{,}000$ m $= 1$ kilometer (km).
- Capacity (liquid): $1{,}000$ milliliters (mL) $= 1$ liter (L).
- Mass: $1{,}000$ grams (g) $= 1$ kilogram (kg).

### Customary units

These are used in many kitchens and shops in the United States.

- Length: $12$ inches (in) $= 1$ foot (ft). $3$ ft $= 1$ yard (yd).
- Weight: $16$ ounces (oz) $= 1$ pound (lb).
- Capacity: $2$ cups $= 1$ pint. $2$ pints $= 1$ quart. $4$ quarts $= 1$ gallon.

### Multiply or divide?

Ask yourself: "Will I get more pieces or fewer?"

- Changing a **big** unit to a **small** unit gives **more** pieces. **Multiply.** $4$ ft to inches: $4 \times 12 = 48$ in.
- Changing a **small** unit to a **big** unit gives **fewer** pieces. **Divide.** $3{,}500$ mL to liters: $3{,}500 \div 1{,}000 = 3.5$ L.

### Check it

Does it make sense? A meter stick is longer than a centimeter, so a length in centimeters must be a bigger number than the same length in meters. If $3$ m turned into $3$ cm, something went wrong.

### Recipes

Cooks change units all the time. A recipe that says $4$ cups of milk is the same as $2$ pints or $1$ quart. Knowing the unit table makes it easy to switch.

Try this with your grown-up: find a measuring cup and a bottle with a label. Look for the mL or fl oz markings and talk about which is bigger.

@example
title: Convert feet to inches and milliliters to liters
problem: Change $4$ ft to inches. Then change $3{,}500$ mL to liters.
step: A foot is a big unit and an inch is small, so we will get more pieces. There are $12$ in in $1$ ft.
step: Multiply: $4 \times 12 = 48$. So $4$ ft is $48$ in.
step: Now mL to L goes from small to big, so we get fewer pieces. Divide by $1{,}000$: $3{,}500 \div 1{,}000 = 3.5$. So $3{,}500$ mL is $3.5$ L.

@mistakes
- Multiplying when you should divide, for example making $3$ m into $0.03$ m or turning $48$ in into $576$ ft.
- Using the wrong number, such as $100$ for a kilometer when there are $1{,}000$ meters in one.
- Mixing metric and customary units in one conversion.
