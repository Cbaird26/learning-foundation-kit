@article
Last week's NPV rule is only as good as its inputs. The hard part of capital budgeting is not the arithmetic but deciding which cash flows to count. The principle is simple: count the cash flows that exist **with the project and not without it**. These are the **incremental cash flows**.

### What to include and what to leave out

**Include:**
- **Opportunity costs.** If a project uses a warehouse the firm could otherwise sell or rent, the forgone cash is a cost of the project.
- **Side effects.** **Cannibalization** (the project takes sales from the firm's existing products) is a negative side effect; **synergies** are positive ones.
- **Net working capital.** A project often needs cash tied up in inventory and receivables, net of payables. The increase in net working capital is a cash outflow when it happens, and it is usually recovered at the end of the project's life.
- **Taxes.** All cash flows are measured after tax.

**Exclude:**
- **Sunk costs**, which have been paid already and do not change with the decision (money spent on a feasibility study last year).
- **Financing costs** (interest and principal on debt used for the project): they are accounted for in the discount rate. Counting them in the cash flows would count them twice.
- **Non-cash allocations** such as overhead that does not change with the project. Depreciation is a non-cash expense, but it matters through taxes.

### Operating cash flow

With revenue $R$, cash operating costs $C$, depreciation $D$ and tax rate $T$, EBIT is $R - C - D$. Operating cash flow (OCF) can be found three equivalent ways:

$$\text{OCF} = \text{EBIT} + D - T\times\text{EBIT} \quad\text{(top-down)} \qquad \text{OCF} = \text{NI} + D \quad\text{(bottom-up, no interest)}$$

$$\text{OCF} = (R - C)(1 - T) + T\times D \quad\text{(tax shield approach)}.$$

The last form shows that depreciation matters only through the **depreciation tax shield** $T \times D$: the tax it saves. The conventions here are **straight-line depreciation** over the project's life to a book value of zero, unless stated. (Actual US tax depreciation follows MACRS schedules, with special first-year deductions whose rules change; check current IRS rules.)

### Capital spending, working capital and salvage

A project's total cash flow in year $t$ is

$$CF_t = \text{OCF}_t - \text{capital spending}_t - \Delta\text{NWC}_t.$$

At the end of the life, an asset sold for $S$ with book value $BV$ produces an after-tax amount

$$S - T\,(S - BV),$$

because a gain is taxed and a loss saves tax. Net working capital is added back when it is released.

### Consistency with inflation and discounting

Forecast nominal cash flows and discount at a nominal rate, or real cash flows at a real rate (week 4). Cash flows are assumed at the end of each year; the investment and working capital at time 0.

@example
title: Fernhill Packaging's new line
problem: Fernhill Packaging is considering a new packaging line. The equipment costs \$600,000 installed, is depreciated straight-line to zero over 5 years, and can be sold for \$80,000 at the end of year 5. Sales add \$520,000 a year and cash costs add \$210,000 a year. Net working capital of \$40,000 is needed at time 0 and recovered at the end of year 5. The tax rate is 25% and the discount rate is 9%. Find the cash flows and the NPV.
step: Depreciation is $600{,}000/5 = 120{,}000$ a year. EBIT $= 520{,}000 - 210{,}000 - 120{,}000 = 190{,}000$. Tax $= 47{,}500$. OCF $= 190{,}000 + 120{,}000 - 47{,}500 = \$262{,}500$, the same as $(310{,}000)(0.75) + 0.25(120{,}000) = 232{,}500 + 30{,}000$.
step: Time 0: $-600{,}000 - 40{,}000 = -\$640{,}000$. Years 1 to 4: \$262,500. Year 5: OCF 262,500 + recovery of working capital 40,000 + after-tax salvage $80{,}000 - 0.25(80{,}000 - 0) = 60{,}000$ is \$362,500.
step: $NPV = -640{,}000 + 262{,}500 \times 3.889651 + 100{,}000/1.09^5 = -640{,}000 + 1{,}021{,}033.39 + 64{,}993.14 \approx \$446{,}027$ (to the nearest dollar, using unrounded factors).
step: The NPV is positive, so the line adds about \$446,000 of value. The sale and the working capital recovery are small compared with the operating flows.
answer: Cash flows of $-\$640{,}000$, \$262,500 (years 1 to 4) and \$362,500 (year 5); NPV about \$446,027.

@example
title: Salvage below and above book value
problem: A machine with a book value of \$80,000 is sold at the end of a project. The tax rate is 25%. Find the after-tax cash from the sale if the price is \$50,000, and if it is \$100,000.
step: At \$50,000 there is a loss of $80{,}000 - 50{,}000 = 30{,}000$. It saves tax of $0.25 \times 30{,}000 = 7{,}500$, so the firm receives $50{,}000 + 7{,}500 = \$57{,}500$.
step: At \$100,000 there is a gain of \$20,000 taxed at 25%, which costs \$5,000, so the firm keeps $100{,}000 - 5{,}000 = \$95{,}000$.
answer: \$57,500 and \$95,000.

@mistakes
- Including a sunk cost, such as a consulting fee already paid, in the project's cash flows.
- Subtracting interest on project debt from the cash flows and also discounting at a rate that includes the cost of debt.
- Forgetting the working capital investment at time 0, or forgetting to recover it at the end.
- Taxing the whole sale price of an asset instead of only the gain over book value (or forgetting the tax saving on a loss).

model:
```python
def project_cash_flows(capex, life, revenue, cash_costs, tax_rate,
                       salvage=0.0, nwc=0.0):
    """Incremental after-tax cash flows, years 0 to life."""
    depreciation = capex / life
    ocf = (revenue - cash_costs) * (1 - tax_rate) + tax_rate * depreciation
    flows = [-(capex + nwc)] + [ocf] * life
    flows[-1] += nwc + salvage * (1 - tax_rate)
    return flows
```
hint: Depreciation is `capex / life`; compute the annual OCF once with the tax shield formula.
hint: Start the list with `-(capex + nwc)`, repeat the OCF, and add the working capital and after-tax salvage to the last element.
solution: The annual OCF is $(R - C)(1 - T) + T D$ with $D = \text{capex}/\text{life}$. Year 0 is the capital spending plus working capital; each later year is the OCF; the last year also gets back the working capital and the salvage value after tax (book value is zero, so the whole price is a gain). With no revenue the OCF is only the depreciation tax shield, which the last test checks.
