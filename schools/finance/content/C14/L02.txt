@article
The forward price is not a forecast. It is the delivery price that makes the contract worth zero at initiation, and it is pinned down by **no-arbitrage**: if two strategies give the same payoff in every state of the world, they must cost the same today, otherwise a trader could buy the cheap one, sell the expensive one and pocket a riskless profit.

**Conventions in this lesson.** Interest rates are annual and **continuously compounded** unless the problem says otherwise, so \$1 grows to $e^{rT}$ in $T$ years and the discount factor is $e^{-rT}$. Borrowing and lending are at the same rate $r$, there are no transaction costs, and the underlying can be sold short.

### The cost-of-carry argument

Take a stock that pays no dividends, with spot price $S_0$. To own it at time $T$ you can (a) enter a long forward at delivery price $F_0$ and pay $F_0$ at $T$, or (b) buy it today for $S_0$ with borrowed money, owing $S_0 e^{rT}$ at $T$. Both strategies deliver one share at $T$, so the forward price must be

$$F_0 = S_0\,e^{rT}.$$

If the market quotes $F_0 > S_0 e^{rT}$, do a **cash-and-carry arbitrage**: borrow $S_0$, buy the asset, and sell the forward. At $T$ deliver the asset, receive $F_0$, repay $S_0 e^{rT}$, and keep $F_0 - S_0e^{rT} > 0$. If $F_0 < S_0e^{rT}$, do the **reverse cash-and-carry**: short the asset, lend the proceeds, and buy the forward; at $T$ collect $S_0 e^{rT}$, pay $F_0$ to receive the asset, and close the short. The profit is $S_0e^{rT} - F_0$.

With **discrete annual compounding** the same argument gives $F_0 = S_0(1+r)^T$.

### Income, yields, storage and foreign interest

Holding the asset can produce benefits or costs that a forward holder does not get:

| Underlying | Forward price | Symbols |
|---|---|---|
| Known cash income (dividends) | $(S_0 - I)\,e^{rT}$ | $I$ = present value of income over the life, discounted at $r$ |
| Continuous yield (stock index) | $S_0\,e^{(r-q)T}$ | $q$ = dividend yield, continuously compounded |
| Storage cost (commodity) | $S_0\,e^{(r+u)T}$ | $u$ = storage cost as a continuously compounded fraction of price |
| Currency (price in domestic per foreign unit) | $S_0\,e^{(r_d-r_f)T}$ | $r_d$, $r_f$ = domestic and foreign rates |

The currency formula is **covered interest parity**: holding foreign currency earns the foreign rate $r_f$, which offsets part of the funding cost. For commodities that are consumed, the market forward can lie below the cost-of-carry price, which is summarized as a **convenience yield** $y$: $F_0 = S_0 e^{(r+u-y)T}$.

### Valuing a forward after initiation

A forward starts at zero value. At time $t$ with $T-t$ remaining, the spot price is $S_t$ and the contract's delivery price is still $F_0$. For a non-income asset the long's value is

$$V_t = S_t - F_0\,e^{-r(T-t)}.$$

Receiving the asset worth $S_t$ and paying the fixed $F_0$ later costs the present value of $F_0$. (With income, replace $S_t$ by $S_t - I_t$.) The short's value is $-V_t$.

### What the forward price is not

$F_0$ is not an expectation of the future spot price; it is the price consistent with no arbitrage. Expected spot prices depend on risk premiums, while the forward price depends only on the spot price, interest rates and the carry.

@example
title: A cash-and-carry arbitrage
problem: A stock trades at \$40 and pays no dividends. A 9-month forward is quoted at \$42.00. The continuously compounded rate is 5%. Is there an arbitrage? What is the profit per share at delivery?
step: The no-arbitrage price is $F_0 = 40\,e^{0.05 \times 0.75} = 40 \times 1.038212 = \$41.5285$.
step: The quote of \$42.00 is higher than \$41.5285, so the forward is overpriced: sell it, and replicate the long side cheaply.
step: Today: borrow \$40, buy the stock, and sell (go short) the forward at \$42. The net cash flow today is zero.
step: In 9 months: deliver the stock and receive \$42.00; repay the loan $40e^{0.0375} = \$41.5285$.
step: Profit $= 42.00 - 41.5285 = \$0.4715$ per share, with no risk.
answer: Yes; sell the forward and buy the stock with borrowed money, for a riskless profit of about \$0.47 per share at delivery.

@example
title: A storable commodity
problem: Spot gold is \$2,000 per ounce. Storage and insurance cost 1% of the price per year, the interest rate is 5%, both continuously compounded. What is the 1-year forward price?
step: With storage costs treated as a continuous proportional cost, $F_0 = S_0 e^{(r+u)T}$.
step: $F_0 = 2000\,e^{(0.05+0.01)\times 1} = 2000 \times 1.061837 = \$2{,}123.67$.
step: Check the logic: a buyer of the forward avoids paying for storage for a year, so it must pay for that saving in the price.
answer: \$2,123.67 per ounce.

@mistakes
- Subtracting the dividend at its future value, or ignoring its timing: income is subtracted at its present value $I = D\,e^{-rt}$, then the result is compounded to $T$.
- Using the same formula for a stock index and for the underlying without the dividend yield, or adding $q$ where it should be subtracted.
- Treating the forward price as the expected future spot price.
- Mixing compounding conventions: using the continuous rate in $(1+r)^T$, or the annual effective rate in $e^{rT}$.

prompt: A stock trades at \$75 and pays no dividends. The 6-month forward price is quoted at \$76.00 while the cost-of-carry price, using a 4% continuously compounded rate, is \$76.52. Which strategy locks in an arbitrage profit?
choice*: Buy the forward, short the stock and lend the proceeds of \$75; at maturity take delivery, close the short and collect the loan.
choice: Sell the forward, borrow \$75 and buy the stock today; deliver it at maturity and repay the loan.

model:
```python
import math


def forward_price(spot, r, T, q=0.0, pv_income=0.0):
    """No-arbitrage forward price with continuous compounding."""
    return (spot - pv_income) * math.exp((r - q) * T)
```
hint: The carry factor is `math.exp((r - q) * T)`.
hint: Subtract the present value of income from the spot price before applying the carry factor.
solution: Net the present value of any known income against the spot price, then multiply by $e^{(r-q)T}$. When $r=0$ and there is no income the forward equals spot, and when $T=0$ the forward equals spot as well.

tol: 0.0001
