@article
A bank advertises a savings account at "6% a year, compounded monthly." Another offers "6.1% a year, compounded annually." Which pays more? You cannot tell from the headline numbers, because they are **stated rates**: each one says how the bank computes interest, not how much your money actually grows in a year.

### Stated rates and periodic rates

A stated annual rate $r_s$ compounded $m$ times a year means the bank pays the **periodic rate** $r_s/m$ every period, and each period's interest earns interest in the next. At 6% compounded monthly, the account pays $0.06/12 = 0.5\%$ a month. In the United States, a loan's quoted rate is its **APR** (annual percentage rate). The APR folds in certain fees, but it is still a periodic rate times the number of periods, so it ignores compounding within the year.

### The effective annual rate

The **effective annual rate** is the rate that, compounded once a year, gives the same growth:

$$\text{EAR} = \left(1 + \frac{r_s}{m}\right)^{m} - 1.$$

For 6% compounded monthly, $\text{EAR} = 1.005^{12} - 1 = 6.1678\%$. So \$1,000 grows to \$1,061.68 in a year, not \$1,060. More frequent compounding raises the EAR, but by less and less:

| Compounding of 6% | Periods $m$ | EAR |
|---|---|---|
| Annual | 1 | 6.0000% |
| Semiannual | 2 | 6.0900% |
| Quarterly | 4 | 6.1364% |
| Monthly | 12 | 6.1678% |
| Daily | 365 | 6.1831% |
| Continuous | — | 6.1837% |

The limit as $m \to \infty$ is **continuous compounding**, where $\text{EAR} = e^{r_s} - 1$ and a deposit $PV$ grows to $PV\,e^{r_s t}$ after $t$ years.

### Going back from an EAR

To find the stated rate with $m$ periods that delivers a given EAR, solve the formula for $r_s$:

$$r_s = m\left[(1 + \text{EAR})^{1/m} - 1\right].$$

### Comparing offers

Put every offer on the same basis, the EAR, before comparing. A saver wants the highest EAR and a borrower the lowest. Banks in the United States must also quote a savings account's **APY** (annual percentage yield), which is its EAR, so savers can compare accounts directly. Back to the opening question: 6% compounded monthly has an EAR of 6.1678%, which beats 6.1% compounded annually.

@example
title: Two loan offers
problem: Offer A charges 7.2% compounded monthly. Offer B charges 7.3% compounded semiannually. Which loan is cheaper?
step: Find each periodic rate. A: $0.072/12 = 0.006$ a month. B: $0.073/2 = 0.0365$ every six months.
step: Compound each for a year. A: $1.006^{12} = 1.074424$. B: $1.0365^{2} = 1.074332$.
step: Subtract 1 to get the EARs: A is $7.4424\%$ and B is $7.4332\%$.
step: A borrower wants the lower EAR, so Offer B is cheaper, even though its stated rate is higher.
answer: Offer B (EAR 7.4332% against 7.4424%).

@example
title: Growing a deposit
problem: You deposit \$5,000 for 3 years at 4.8% a year. How much do you have if interest is compounded quarterly, and if it is compounded continuously?
step: Quarterly: the periodic rate is $0.048/4 = 0.012$ and there are $3 \times 4 = 12$ quarters, so $FV = 5000 \times 1.012^{12} = 5000 \times 1.153895 = \$5{,}769.47$.
step: Continuously: $FV = 5000\,e^{0.048 \times 3} = 5000\,e^{0.144} = 5000 \times 1.154884 = \$5{,}774.42$.
step: Continuous compounding adds only about \$4.95 over three years: going beyond monthly or daily compounding changes little.
answer: \$5,769.47 quarterly; \$5,774.42 continuously.

@mistakes
- Comparing stated rates with different compounding directly instead of converting each to an EAR first.
- Using the annual rate as the periodic rate (multiplying by $1.06^{12}$ instead of $1.005^{12}$ for 6% compounded monthly).
- Forgetting to subtract 1, and reporting a growth factor such as 1.0617 as the rate.
- Choosing the highest EAR as the best loan: a borrower wants the lowest.

@practice num
prompt: A lender wants to quote a rate compounded monthly that gives an effective annual rate of exactly 8%. What stated annual rate should it quote? Give it as a percent to two decimal places.
answer: 7.72
unit: %
tol: 0.01
check: 12 * (1.08 ** (1 / 12) - 1) * 100
hint: Solve $\left(1 + r_s/12\right)^{12} = 1.08$ for $r_s$.
hint: Take the 12th root: $1 + r_s/12 = 1.08^{1/12}$.
solution: $r_s = 12\left(1.08^{1/12} - 1\right) = 12 \times 0.0064340 = 7.72\%$. Compounding monthly at 7.72% gives an EAR of 8%.
