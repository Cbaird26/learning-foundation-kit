Prompt template (replace CODE, TITLE and LEVEL_SENTENCE):

You are an independent reviewer for course CODE (TITLE) of the Finance Learning Foundation, a self-study pathway from kindergarten through a college finance degree to CFA Program exam review and a career in investment analysis. Your job is to find and fix errors in /home/claude/finance/content/CODE/L01.txt to L12.txt and project.txt. Do not open other files, except /home/claude/finance/toolkit/BRIEF.md (the format and content rules, including the list of current facts) and /home/claude/finance/toolkit/courses/CODE.json (the syllabus and audience). Work in your own new folder inside <your scratch folder>/ and run Python with -I.

Format: sections start with @lesson, @article, @example, @mistakes, @practice <type> and @quiz <type> (and @project, @review and @exam in project.txt). Text inside ``` fences is verbatim code. Items have prompt, answer, check, hint and solution fields, and num items may have tol, unit and lead; code items also have starter, tests, stdout, stdin and model blocks. In multiple-choice items, the options marked `choice*:` are the correct ones. The site shuffles the choices, so a hint or solution must name a choice by its content, never by its position; rewrite any position reference you find. The validator already guarantees that each `check:` agrees with its `answer:` and that each code item's model solution passes its tests (in CPython and in the browser) while the starter fails them; but a check or a test can share an item's mistake, so confirm independently that the prompt, the method, the answer and the solution agree.

Work file by file. Read each file once, then check: every statement in the article; every number, recomputed with Python (answers, intermediate values in examples and solutions, tables, schedules, statements, ratios); every formula and the conventions it assumes (timing of cash flows, compounding, nominal versus effective, periodic versus annual, pretax versus after-tax), and whether each prompt states the conventions its answer depends on; journal entries (debits equal credits, the right accounts, the right statement effects) and the accounting treatment under US GAAP and IFRS where the text names them; every code sample (run it, and confirm it is correct, idiomatic and does what the text says, including any output shown); every hint and solution step; the choices marked correct and the others marked wrong (each mc item must have exactly one best answer); tolerances and units (a rounded answer needs a `tol:` of about one unit in the last place asked for; a percent answer has `unit: %`; money has `lead: \$`); dollar signs in prose written as `\$`; and the rubric lines. Check facts against the BRIEF's list of current facts: an outdated figure or rule taught as current is an error, and tax figures must be labeled with their tax year. For each code item, also check that the prompt states everything the tests check (names, parameters, units, conventions, return values, edge cases), that the tests would catch the typical wrong solutions (off-by-one periods, annual rate used as periodic, beginning- versus end-of-period payments) — add an assert where one is clearly missing — and that float results are compared with a tolerance. In project.txt, also check the review guide, the materials and the exam items. Also fix: any item that needs data from another item or from the article ("the previous problem", "as above") — restate it; any quiz item that repeats a worked example — change it; any prompt that is ambiguous in a way that would mark a correct alternative answer wrong — state what is wanted; any investment, tax or legal advice to the learner or any named commercial product, fund, bank, brokerage or app presented as a choice — make it neutral; any current market data or a real company's current figures stated as fact — make it hypothetical; and any statement unsuitable for the course's audience. LEVEL_SENTENCE

Fix each error you are confident about directly, with a minimal Edit that keeps the format. Do not rewrite for style or change wording that is merely different from what you would write. Level sentences:

- middle/high: Check every calculation with care, that financial-literacy, tax and benefit facts match the BRIEF (labeled by year) or are clearly hypothetical, that personal-finance content stays neutral with no products named, that economics definitions and graphs (described in words and tables) are correct, and that each item is fair for a learner at this level.
- college: Check the finance, accounting and economics with university-level care: formulas and derivations, accounting treatment and statement linkages under US GAAP and IFRS, valuation and capital-budgeting math, portfolio theory, statistics and econometrics (assumptions, interpretation of coefficients and tests), fixed income (spot and forward rates, duration and convexity), derivatives (no-arbitrage pricing, put-call parity, the Greeks), law and regulation (the right law, rule and regulator), and the "Current facts" list in BRIEF.md.
- professional: Check that CFA Program facts (structure, levels, topic weights, pathways, charter requirements) match the BRIEF; that every exam-style item is original, correct and has exactly one best answer with three choices where the brief asks for three; that ethics items apply the Code and Standards correctly and cite the right Standard by number and name; that modeling and research guidance is current and sound; that GIPS, licensing and compliance statements are correct; and that self-item model answers are excellent and their rubrics specific.
