@article
**Perimeter** is the distance around a figure: add the lengths of all its sides. For a circle it is called the **circumference**, $C = 2\pi r$. **Area** is the amount of flat surface inside a figure, measured in **square units**: square centimeters, square meters, and so on.

### Basic area formulas

- **Rectangle:** $A = bh$ (base times height), where the height is perpendicular to the base. A square of side $s$ has $A = s^2$.
- **Parallelogram:** $A = bh$. Here $h$ is the **perpendicular height**, not the slanted side. (Cut a right triangle off one end and slide it to the other end; this makes a rectangle with the same base and height.)
- **Triangle:** $A = \tfrac{1}{2}bh$. Two copies of the same triangle form a parallelogram.
- **Trapezoid** with parallel bases $b_1, b_2$ and height $h$: $A = \tfrac{1}{2}(b_1 + b_2)h$, the average base times the height.
- **Circle:** $A = \pi r^2$. A sector of central angle $\theta$ has area $\dfrac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2$.
- **Regular polygon:** $A = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot a \cdot P$, where $a$ is the **apothem** (distance from the center to a side) and $P$ is the perimeter.

### The height must be perpendicular

Many errors come from using a slanted side as the height. For a triangle, the height is the perpendicular distance from a vertex to the line containing the opposite side, and it may lie outside the triangle for an obtuse triangle. Always check that the height makes a right angle with the base.

### Composite figures

A composite figure is made of simple shapes. There are two strategies:

1. **Add:** split it into rectangles, triangles and so on, find each area, and add.
2. **Subtract:** find the area of a larger simple shape and subtract the holes or cut-outs.

Imagine an L-shaped floor: a $10$ by $8$ rectangle with a $4$ by $3$ rectangular corner removed. The area is $80 - 12 = 68$ square units. Splitting it into two rectangles instead gives the same answer, which is a good way to check.

### Units and scale

If all lengths are multiplied by $k$, perimeters are multiplied by $k$ and areas by $k^2$. To convert area units, square the length conversion: $1\ \text{m}^2 = 100^2 = 10{,}000\ \text{cm}^2$, because a square meter is a $100$ cm by $100$ cm square. Keep the same unit for every length in a problem before you multiply.

### Perimeter versus area

A figure with a larger perimeter does not necessarily have a larger area. A $1 \times 11$ rectangle and a $6 \times 6$ square both have perimeter $24$, but the areas are $11$ and $36$. Among rectangles of a fixed perimeter, the square has the greatest area.

@example
title: A composite floor
problem: A floor is a $12$ m by $7$ m rectangle with a triangular nook attached along one of the $12$ m sides. The triangle has base $12$ m and height $5$ m, measured perpendicular to the base. Find the total floor area.
step: Split the figure into the rectangle and the triangle.
step: Rectangle: $12 \times 7 = 84\ \text{m}^2$.
step: Triangle: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 5 = 30\ \text{m}^2$.
step: Add: $84 + 30 = 114\ \text{m}^2$.

@mistakes
- Using the slanted side of a parallelogram or trapezoid as the height instead of the perpendicular height.
- Forgetting the factor $\tfrac{1}{2}$ for a triangle.
- Reporting area in plain units instead of square units, or mixing units (meters and centimeters) in one formula.
- Adding a cut-out's area instead of subtracting it.

@practice num
prompt: A trapezoid has parallel bases $8$ cm and $14$ cm and a height of $5$ cm. Find its area in $\text{cm}^2$.
answer: 55
check: (8 + 14) / 2 * 5
hint: Average the two bases, then multiply by the height.
solution: $\tfrac{1}{2}(8 + 14)(5) = 11 \times 5 = 55\ \text{cm}^2$.

@practice num
prompt: A rectangle has perimeter $36$ and its length is twice its width. Find its area.
answer: 72
check: (36 / 6) * (2 * (36 / 6))
hint: Let the width be $w$, so the length is $2w$ and the perimeter is $2(w + 2w)$.
hint: $6w = 36$ gives $w = 6$.
solution: $w = 6$ and $\ell = 12$, so $A = 6 \times 12 = 72$.

@quiz num
prompt: A parallelogram has a base of $9$ cm, a slanted side of $7$ cm, and a perpendicular height of $6$ cm. Find its area in $\text{cm}^2$.
answer: 54
check: 9 * 6
hint: The area uses the perpendicular height, not the slanted side.
solution: $A = bh = 9 \times 6 = 54\ \text{cm}^2$. The $7$ cm side is not needed.
