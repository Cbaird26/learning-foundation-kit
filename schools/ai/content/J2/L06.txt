@article
A two-step equation needs two inverse operations. In $px + q = r$ the variable was multiplied by $p$, then $q$ was added. Undo them in **reverse order**: first subtract $q$, then divide by $p$.

### Solving an equation

Solve $2x + 7 = 19$. Subtract $7$ from both sides to get $2x = 12$, then divide both sides by $2$ to get $x = 6$. Check: $2(6) + 7 = 19$.

Whatever you do to one side you must do to the other, so the equation stays balanced.

### Word problems

1. Name the unknown, such as $n$ for the number of months.
2. Find the fixed amount (a starting value or flat fee) and the amount that changes with the unknown.
3. Write an equation: fixed amount plus rate times unknown equals total.
4. Solve, then check the answer in the story.

A gym that charges a \$15 sign-up fee and \$12 a month gives the equation $15 + 12n = \text{total}$.

### Inequalities

An **inequality** compares with $<$, $>$, $\leq$ or $\geq$. It has many solutions, not just one. You solve it with the same steps as an equation, with one new rule:

> When you multiply or divide both sides by a **negative** number, **reverse** the inequality sign.

For example, $-3x + 4 < 13$ becomes $-3x < 9$, and dividing by $-3$ flips the sign: $x > -3$. The flip happens because multiplying by a negative reverses order: $2 < 5$ but $-2 > -5$.

Adding or subtracting any number never flips the sign.

### Graphing a solution

Draw a number line, mark the boundary number, and shade the side where the inequality is true.

- Use an **open circle** for $<$ or $>$ (the boundary is not a solution).
- Use a **closed circle** for $\leq$ or $\geq$ (the boundary is a solution).

The solution $x \leq 4$ is a closed circle at $4$ with shading to the left. The solution $x > -3$ is an open circle at $-3$ with shading to the right.

### Check with a test value

Pick a number from the shaded region and substitute it in the original inequality. If it makes the statement true, the graph is probably right.

@example
title: A word problem with an inequality
problem: A taxi charges \$3 plus \$2 per mile. You have \$21. Write and solve an inequality for the number of miles $m$ you can ride, and describe the graph.
step: The cost of $m$ miles is $3 + 2m$ dollars, and it must not exceed $21$: $2m + 3 \leq 21$.
step: Subtract $3$ from both sides: $2m \leq 18$.
step: Divide both sides by $2$ (a positive number, so the sign stays): $m \leq 9$.
step: The graph is a closed circle at $9$ with shading to the left (and, in this story, only $m \geq 0$ makes sense). Test $m = 5$: $3 + 10 = 13 \leq 21$. True.

@mistakes
- Forgetting to reverse the inequality sign after dividing by a negative number.
- Dividing only one term by the coefficient, for example turning $2x + 7 = 19$ into $x + 7 = 9.5$.
- Using an open circle for $\leq$ or $\geq$, or a closed circle for $<$ or $>$.

check: (87 - 15) / 12
hint: Write $15 + 12n = 87$.
hint: Subtract $15$ from both sides, then divide.
solution: $12n = 72$, so $n = 6$ months. Check: $15 + 72 = 87$.

answer: 7
check: root(lambda x: -2*x + 9 + 5, -100, 100)

solution: $-2x = -14$, so $x = 7$. Check: $-14 + 9 = -5$.

@quiz num
prompt: Maya has \$50. She spends \$8 on lunch and then buys books that cost \$6.50 each. What is the greatest whole number of books she can buy?
answer: 6
check: floor((50 - 8) / 6.5)
hint: Write $8 + 6.5n \leq 50$.
hint: Solve $6.5n \leq 42$ and then round down to a whole number.
solution: $6.5n \leq 42$ gives $n \leq 6.46\ldots$. Only whole books count, so the greatest number is $6$.
