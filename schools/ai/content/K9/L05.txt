@article
A **need** is something people must have to live, like food, water, clothes and a home. A **want** is something that is nice to have but you can live without it, like a toy or a game.

### Goods and services

A **good** is a thing you can touch, like an apple or a shirt. A **service** is a job someone does for you, like a haircut or a bus ride.

A **producer** makes goods or does services. A baker is a producer of bread. A **consumer** buys and uses goods and services. You are a consumer when you eat the bread.

### Money and jobs

Many grown-ups go to work at a job and earn **money**. They use money to buy what the family needs and wants. They may also **save** money by keeping some for later.

### Choices and budgets

We cannot have everything, so we must choose. When you choose one thing, you give up another. That is called a **trade-off**.

A **budget** is a plan for how to use money. First, write how much you have. Then list what you want to buy and the price. Pay for needs first. Then see what is left for wants.

### Example budget

Class party money: \$10. Juice costs \$4. Cups cost \$2. Then there is \$4 left for other things.

@example
title: Need or want?
problem: Is a bicycle a need or a want?
step: Ask, "Can I live without it?"
step: A person can live without a bicycle.
step: So a bicycle is usually a want. But if it is the only way someone gets to work, it might feel like a need for them.

@example
title: A party budget
problem: The class has \$10 for a party. Fruit costs \$6. Napkins cost \$1. How much money is left?
step: Add what we spend: 6 + 1 = 7.
step: Subtract from the money we have: 10 - 7 = 3.
step: There is \$3 left.

@mistakes
- Saying every want is bad. Wants are fine; budgets just help us pay for needs first.
- Mixing up goods and services. A good is a thing; a service is a job someone does.
- Forgetting to subtract everything we buy from the total money.
