@article
By the 1760s, Great Britain ruled 13 colonies in North America. Britain had fought a costly war and wanted the colonies to help pay for it. It passed taxes, such as the Stamp Act of 1765. Colonists had no members in the British Parliament, the group that made the laws. Many said, "No taxation without representation." This means people should have a voice in the leaders who tax them.

### Growing anger

In 1773, some colonists dumped chests of British tea into Boston Harbor to protest a tea tax. This is the **Boston Tea Party**. Britain answered with harsh new rules. In April 1775, fighting began at Lexington and Concord in Massachusetts.

### Declaring independence

On **July 4, 1776**, leaders approved the **Declaration of Independence**. It said the colonies were free from Britain. It also said that "all men are created equal" with rights such as "Life, Liberty and the pursuit of Happiness." Those words did not yet apply to everyone. Enslaved people, Native peoples and women did not have equal rights.

### Different people, different choices

- **Patriots** wanted independence.
- **Loyalists** wanted to stay with Britain.
- Many people stayed neutral.
- Some enslaved people joined the British or the Americans hoping for freedom. Some won it. Many did not.
- Native nations chose different sides to protect their lands. Many were drawn into a war they did not start.
- Women ran farms and shops, raised money and supplies, and some served as nurses or helpers.

### The war ends

The war lasted until 1783, when the **Treaty of Paris** was signed. Britain recognized the United States as an independent country.

step: They thought they should have a voice if they were taxed.

@mistakes
- Thinking all colonists wanted independence. Loyalists and neutral people also lived in the colonies.
- Leaving out enslaved people, Native peoples and women from the story.
- Mixing up the Declaration of Independence (1776) with the Constitution (1787).
