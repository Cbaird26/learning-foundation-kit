@article
Sometimes writers say one thing and mean another. This is **figurative language**. It makes pictures in your mind.

### Simile

A **simile** compares two different things using the word **like** or **as**.

- "The snow was *like* a soft white blanket."
- "Her smile was *as* bright *as* the sun."

Snow is not really a blanket. The simile tells us the snow covered the ground, and it looked soft.

### Metaphor

A **metaphor** also compares two things, but it does not use "like" or "as." It says one thing *is* the other.

- "The classroom was a zoo."
- "Time is a thief."

The classroom is not really a zoo. The metaphor tells us it was loud and busy.

### How to explain one

1. Name the two things being compared.
2. Think about how they are alike.
3. Say what the writer means in plain words.

For "The wind was a roaring lion," the two things are wind and a lion. Both are loud and strong. The writer means the wind was very loud and strong.

### Idioms

An **idiom** is a saying that does not mean its words. "It's raining cats and dogs" means it is raining very hard. No animals fall from the sky!

### Exaggeration

An **exaggeration** (or hyperbole) makes something bigger than it is. "I'm so hungry I could eat a horse" means "I'm very hungry."

### Quick check

Look for "like" or "as" to spot a simile. If the sentence just says one thing *is* another thing, it may be a metaphor.

@example
title: Explain a simile and a metaphor
problem: Tell which is a simile and which is a metaphor, and explain one. (1) "The baby was as quiet as a mouse." (2) "The baby was a little angel."
step: Look for "like" or "as." Sentence 1 has "as quiet as," so it is a simile. Sentence 2 has no "like" or "as," so it is a metaphor.
step: Explain sentence 1. The baby is compared to a mouse. Both are quiet.
step: Say it plainly: "The baby was very quiet."

@mistakes
- Thinking every comparison needs "like" or "as." A metaphor does not use them.
- Taking an idiom word for word, like picturing animals falling from the sky.
- Naming the figure of speech but not saying what it means.
