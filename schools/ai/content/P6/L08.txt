@article
The methods half of a qualifying exam asks you to recall a result, name its conditions, and use it. Formats and syllabi differ between programs, so use your own department's reading list as the authority. For each topic below: the statement, then a number.

### Maximum likelihood

For independent examples $x_1,\dots,x_n$ from $p(x\mid\boldsymbol{\theta}_0)$, the **maximum likelihood estimator** is $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\arg\max_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\sum_i\log p(x_i\mid\boldsymbol{\theta})$. Under regularity conditions (the model is correctly specified and identifiable, $\boldsymbol{\theta}_0$ is an interior point of the parameter space, the log-likelihood is smooth in $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ with support that does not depend on $\boldsymbol{\theta}$, and the Fisher information $\mathbf{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_0)=\mathbb{E}[\nabla\log p\,\nabla\log p^\top]$ is positive definite), $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ is consistent and
$$\sqrt{n}\,(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_0)\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}\big(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_0)^{-1}\big).$$
For a Bernoulli parameter $p$, $\hat p=k/n$ and $I(p)=1/(p(1-p))$, so the standard error is about $\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n}$; with $k=60$ successes in $n=200$ trials, $\hat p=0.3$ and the standard error is about $0.0324$. MLE is not always unbiased: the MLE of a Gaussian variance divides by $n$ and has expectation $\frac{n-1}{n}\sigma^2$. The asymptotic result is a statement about large $n$ and says nothing about small samples or misspecified models.

### The evidence lower bound

For a latent-variable model $p(x,z)$ and any distribution $q(z)$, $\log p(x)=\mathrm{ELBO}(q)+\mathrm{KL}\big(q(z)\,\Vert\,p(z\mid x)\big)$, where
$$\mathrm{ELBO}(q)=\mathbb{E}_q[\log p(x\mid z)]-\mathrm{KL}\big(q(z)\,\Vert\,p(z)\big).$$
Because KL divergence is nonnegative, the ELBO lower-bounds $\log p(x)$, with equality exactly when $q$ equals the posterior $p(z\mid x)$. The EM algorithm (Dempster, Laird and Rubin, 1977) alternates setting $q$ to the posterior (E-step) and maximizing the bound over $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ (M-step); variational autoencoders (Kingma and Welling, 2013) maximize the ELBO over both encoder and decoder. For diagonal Gaussians, $\mathrm{KL}(\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)\Vert\mathcal{N}(0,1))=\tfrac12(\mu^2+\sigma^2-1-\ln\sigma^2)$ per dimension.

### Backpropagation

Backpropagation (popularized by Rumelhart, Hinton and Williams, 1986) computes the exact gradient of a loss through a composition of differentiable functions by applying the chain rule from the output backward, reusing intermediate values from the forward pass. For a scalar loss, the backward pass costs a small constant multiple of the forward pass. For $\hat y=w_2\tanh(w_1x)$ and $L=\tfrac12(\hat y-y)^2$ with $h=\tanh(w_1x)$ and $e=\hat y-y$: $\partial L/\partial w_2=e\,h$ and $\partial L/\partial w_1=e\,w_2(1-h^2)\,x$.

### Attention

Scaled dot-product attention (Vaswani and colleagues, 2017) is $\operatorname{Attention}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V})=\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top/\sqrt{d_k})\,\mathbf{V}$, with softmax applied to each row. The scaling has a reason: if the components of a query and a key are independent with mean 0 and variance 1, their dot product has mean 0 and variance $d_k$, so dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ keeps the scores at unit scale and keeps the softmax from saturating. Each output is a weighted average of the value vectors, with weights summing to 1.

### Bellman equations and Q-learning

For a discounted MDP with $\gamma\in[0,1)$, the value of a policy satisfies $v^\pi(s)=\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)\sum_{s',r}p(s',r\mid s,a)\,[r+\gamma v^\pi(s')]$, and the optimal action-values satisfy $q^\ast(s,a)=\sum_{s',r}p(s',r\mid s,a)\,[r+\gamma\max_{a'}q^\ast(s',a')]$. The Bellman optimality operator is a $\gamma$-contraction in the sup norm, so it has a unique fixed point and value iteration converges to it geometrically. **Q-learning** (Watkins, 1989) updates $Q(s,a)\leftarrow Q(s,a)+\alpha\,[r+\gamma\max_{a'}Q(s',a')-Q(s,a)]$. In the tabular case it converges to $q^\ast$ with probability one if the state and action sets are finite, rewards are bounded, $\gamma<1$, every state-action pair is updated infinitely often, and the step sizes for each pair satisfy $\sum_t\alpha_t=\infty$ and $\sum_t\alpha_t^2<\infty$. The behavior policy may be anything that visits every pair infinitely often (it is off-policy). With function approximation, no such general guarantee holds.

### Identification: the backdoor criterion

In a causal DAG, a set $\mathbf{Z}$ satisfies the **backdoor criterion** relative to $(X,Y)$ if no node in $\mathbf{Z}$ is a descendant of $X$, and $\mathbf{Z}$ blocks every path between $X$ and $Y$ that contains an arrow into $X$. Then $P(y\mid do(x))=\sum_{\mathbf{z}}P(y\mid x,\mathbf{z})\,P(\mathbf{z})$ (Pearl). The result assumes the DAG is correct; no data can verify that.

@example
title: One query of scaled dot-product attention
problem: A query $\mathbf{q}=(1,0,1,0)$ attends to three keys $(1,0,1,0)$, $(0,1,0,1)$ and $(1,1,0,0)$ with values $(2,0)$, $(0,4)$ and $(1,1)$. Here $d_k=4$. Compute the attention weights and the output.
step: The dot products are $2$, $0$ and $1$. Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}=2$ gives scores $1$, $0$ and $0.5$.
step: The exponentials are $e^{1}=2.718$, $e^{0}=1$ and $e^{0.5}=1.649$, with sum $5.367$. The weights are $0.5065$, $0.1863$ and $0.3072$, and they sum to 1.
step: The output is the weighted average of the values: first coordinate $0.5065\cdot2+0.1863\cdot0+0.3072\cdot1=1.3202$; second coordinate $0.5065\cdot0+0.1863\cdot4+0.3072\cdot1=1.0525$.
answer: Weights $(0.5065,0.1863,0.3072)$; output $(1.320,1.052)$.

@example
title: A backdoor adjustment
problem: In a DAG with edges $Z\to X$, $Z\to Y$ and $X\to Y$, with $Z$ binary, $P(Z=1)=0.4$, $P(Y=1\mid X=1,Z=1)=0.8$ and $P(Y=1\mid X=1,Z=0)=0.5$, compute $P(Y=1\mid do(X=1))$ and check that $\{Z\}$ satisfies the backdoor criterion.
step: The only path into $X$ is $X\leftarrow Z\to Y$, which $\{Z\}$ blocks, and $Z$ is not a descendant of $X$. So $\{Z\}$ satisfies the criterion.
step: Adjust: $P(Y=1\mid do(X=1))=\sum_zP(Y=1\mid X=1,z)P(z)=0.8\cdot0.4+0.5\cdot0.6=0.32+0.30$.
step: The result is $0.62$. The observed $P(Y=1\mid X=1)$ would weight the strata by $P(z\mid X=1)$ instead of $P(z)$, and it differs whenever $Z$ affects $X$.
answer: $P(Y=1\mid do(X=1))=0.62$.

@mistakes
- Stating Q-learning convergence without the visiting and step-size conditions, or claiming it for function approximation.
- Forgetting the $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling, or applying the softmax over the wrong axis (each query's weights must sum to 1 over keys).
- Adjusting for a descendant of $X$ (a mediator or a collider's child), which can block part of the effect or open a bias.
- Treating the asymptotic normality of the MLE as an exact small-sample result, or applying it to a misspecified model without saying so.

prompt: Which set of conditions guarantees that tabular Q-learning converges to $q^\ast$ with probability one?
choice*: Finite state and action sets, bounded rewards, $\gamma<1$, every state-action pair updated infinitely often, and step sizes with $\sum\alpha_t=\infty$ and $\sum\alpha_t^2<\infty$ for each pair.
choice: A constant step size of 1 and a greedy behavior policy that never explores.

choice: The behavior policy must equal the optimal policy from the start.

@practice self
prompt: Timed (10 minutes). Derive the evidence lower bound $\mathrm{ELBO}(q)=\mathbb{E}_q[\log p(x\mid z)]-\mathrm{KL}(q(z)\Vert p(z))$ and show that $\log p(x)-\mathrm{ELBO}(q)=\mathrm{KL}(q(z)\Vert p(z\mid x))$. State when the bound is tight.
hint: Start from $\log p(x)=\mathbb{E}_q[\log p(x)]$ and write $p(x)=p(x,z)/p(z\mid x)$.
solution: For any $q(z)$, $\log p(x)=\mathbb{E}_q[\log p(x)]$ because $p(x)$ does not depend on $z$. Write $p(x)=p(x,z)/p(z\mid x)$ and insert $q$: $\log p(x)=\mathbb{E}_q\big[\log\tfrac{p(x,z)}{q(z)}\big]+\mathbb{E}_q\big[\log\tfrac{q(z)}{p(z\mid x)}\big]$. The first term is the ELBO, and the second is $\mathrm{KL}(q(z)\Vert p(z\mid x))\ge0$. Hence $\mathrm{ELBO}\le\log p(x)$ with gap exactly that KL. Expanding $\log p(x,z)=\log p(x\mid z)+\log p(z)$ in the first term gives $\mathbb{E}_q[\log p(x\mid z)]-\mathrm{KL}(q(z)\Vert p(z))$. The bound is tight exactly when $q(z)=p(z\mid x)$ (almost everywhere), which is the E-step of EM; the derivation needs $q$ to be absolutely continuous with respect to the posterior so the KL terms are defined.

@quiz ms
prompt: A DAG has the edges $Z_1\to X$, $Z_1\to Y$, $Z_2\to X$, $X\to M$, $M\to Y$, $X\to C$ and $Y\to C$. Which sets satisfy the backdoor criterion relative to $(X,Y)$? Select all that apply.
choice*: $\{Z_1\}$
choice*: $\{Z_1,Z_2\}$
choice: $\{Z_2\}$
choice: $\{Z_1,C\}$
choice: $\{M\}$
hint: Find the path from $X$ into $Y$ that starts with an arrow into $X$, and exclude descendants of $X$.
solution: The only backdoor path is $X\leftarrow Z_1\to Y$, which any valid set must block, so $Z_1$ must be included. Adding $Z_2$ is harmless: it is not a descendant of $X$ and opens no path. $\{Z_2\}$ leaves the backdoor path open. $C$ and $M$ are descendants of $X$, so $\{Z_1,C\}$ and $\{M\}$ violate the first condition.

@quiz self
prompt: Timed (12 minutes). Compare value iteration and tabular Q-learning for a finite discounted MDP: what each needs, what each guarantees and when each is preferable. State the convergence conditions exactly.
hint: One method needs the model $p(s',r\mid s,a)$ and one learns from samples; list the conditions for each guarantee.
solution: Value iteration repeatedly applies the Bellman optimality operator, $v_{k+1}(s)=\max_a\sum_{s',r}p(s',r\mid s,a)[r+\gamma v_k(s')]$, so it needs the transition and reward model. Because the operator is a $\gamma$-contraction in the sup norm for $\gamma<1$, it converges to the unique fixed point $v^\ast$ from any start, with error shrinking at least like $\gamma^k$; the guarantee needs a finite (or suitably bounded) MDP and exact backups. Tabular Q-learning needs no model, only sampled transitions $(s,a,r,s')$, and updates one entry at a time with $Q\leftarrow Q+\alpha[r+\gamma\max Q'-Q]$. It converges to $q^\ast$ with probability one if the MDP is finite, rewards bounded, $\gamma<1$, every state-action pair is updated infinitely often and step sizes satisfy $\sum\alpha=\infty$, $\sum\alpha^2<\infty$ for each pair; the behavior policy can be any that explores enough. Value iteration is preferable when the model is known and the state space is small enough to sweep; Q-learning when only simulation or interaction is available, at the price of slower, stochastic convergence and the need for exploration. Neither guarantee extends to function approximation without further conditions.
rubric: States the model requirement of value iteration and the contraction argument with $\gamma<1$.
rubric: States the full Q-learning conditions: finiteness, bounded rewards, discounting, infinite visits, step-size sums.
rubric: Compares when each is preferable (known model versus samples) and notes the limits with function approximation.
