@review
## Review guide for Research apprenticeship and qualifying exams

**Weeks 1 and 2: choosing and planning.** A good target has a numeric claim, modest compute, usable code and data, and clear permissions. Score candidates with weights fixed in advance, and keep a fallback. A plan fixes the claim, scope, budget, seeds, tolerance and stopping rules before the main runs. Device-hours per run equal FLOPs divided by (peak throughput times utilization), divided by 3600. Absolute difference is $|\hat m - m|$; relative difference divides it by $|m|$.

**Weeks 3 and 4: running and debugging.** Pin the environment, log everything, guard the test set. Summarize seeds with the mean, the sample standard deviation (divide by $n-1$), the standard error $s/\sqrt n$ and a $t$-based interval. When results differ, check the noise first, then data, leakage, implementation, protocol and the authors' appendix, changing one thing at a time. Report partial failures plainly and without blame.

**Weeks 5 and 6: extending and writing.** An ablation changes one factor, with equal tuning, enough seeds and a hypothesis stated first. Use paired differences when seeds control shared sources; remember multiple comparisons and report null results with intervals. Read the call for papers for page limits, anonymity and prior-publication rules. Write the abstract last, with a caveat, and list real limitations.

**Weeks 7 to 9: exam results.** Gradient descent with step $1/L$ on a convex $L$-smooth function: $f(\mathbf{x}_k)-f^\ast\le L\lVert\mathbf{x}_0-\mathbf{x}^\ast\rVert^2/(2k)$; with $\mu$-strong convexity the gap shrinks by $(1-\mu/L)^k$. Finite class: deviation at most $\sqrt{(\ln|\mathcal H|+\ln(2/\delta))/(2m)}$. Bias-variance: noise plus squared bias plus variance. ELBO: $\log p(x)$ minus the KL to the posterior. Q-learning needs finite sets, bounded rewards, $\gamma<1$, infinite visits and the step-size sums. The backdoor criterion needs no descendants of $X$ and blocked backdoor paths. With unequal base rates, equal PPV, FPR and FNR cannot all hold for an imperfect classifier. Laplace scale is $\Delta/\varepsilon$. Laws change: check the current text.

**Weeks 10 to 12: orals and paths.** Answer with claim, setup, example, limits. Prepare the follow-up ladder, and do not bluff. Choose a direction as a question with a portfolio, and advisors by evidence of fit. Compare routes (PhD, research master's, industry, government, nonprofit, independent) on the same dimensions, and read publication and IP terms.

**Self-check.** Can you (1) compute a budget and a tolerance check by hand? (2) compute a mean, standard error and interval? (3) state each exam result with its conditions? (4) explain a paper aloud in five minutes? (5) write a limitations paragraph that a skeptic would respect?

**Common errors.** Choosing the tolerance after the result; reporting the best seed; confusing the standard deviation and the standard error; changing several things while debugging; dropping a condition from a theorem; applying a bound to a class chosen after seeing the data; quoting laws from memory; ranking advisors or labs by reputation.

@exam num
prompt: A reproduction gives five seeds of test accuracy (percent): 84.2, 83.5, 85.1, 82.9 and 84.6. The paper reports 86.0. Using the sample standard deviation and $t^\ast=2.776$, what is the upper end of the 95% interval for the mean, to two decimal places? (The reported value lies outside this interval.)
answer: 85.14
tol: 0.01
check: np.mean([84.2, 83.5, 85.1, 82.9, 84.6]) + 2.776 * np.std([84.2, 83.5, 85.1, 82.9, 84.6], ddof=1) / sqrt(5)
hint: Compute the mean, the sample standard deviation and the standard error, then add $t^\ast$ times the standard error to the mean.
solution: The mean is 84.06, the sample standard deviation is about 0.8735, and the standard error is $0.8735/\sqrt5\approx0.3906$. The half-width is $2.776\times0.3906\approx1.084$, so the upper end is $84.06+1.084=85.14$. Since $86.0>85.14$, the reported value lies outside the interval.

@exam ms
prompt: Which of these statements are correct? Select all that apply.
choice*: Gradient descent with step size $1/L$ on a convex, $L$-smooth function with a minimizer satisfies $f(\mathbf{x}_k)-f^\ast\le L\lVert\mathbf{x}_0-\mathbf{x}^\ast\rVert^2/(2k)$.
choice*: The Laplace mechanism with scale $\Delta/\varepsilon$, where $\Delta$ is the L1 sensitivity, is $\varepsilon$-differentially private.
choice*: The bias-variance decomposition into noise, squared bias and variance is exact for squared loss under independent zero-mean noise.
choice: The ELBO equals $\log p(x)$ for every choice of the approximating distribution $q$.
choice: With unequal base rates, an imperfect classifier can have equal PPV, equal false positive rates and equal false negative rates in two groups.
hint: Check each statement's conditions: what the theorem assumes and what it concludes.
solution: The first three statements are the standard results with their conditions. The ELBO equals $\log p(x)$ only when $q$ is the posterior, since the gap is the KL from $q$ to the posterior. The last statement contradicts the identity linking FPR to the base rate, PPV and TPR, which forces unequal error rates unless prediction is perfect.

@exam self
prompt: You reproduced a paper's central result. The paper reports 86.0%; your five-seed mean is 84.06% with a 95% interval of 82.98 to 85.14, and you matched the data split, but the paper leaves its learning-rate schedule unspecified. Your advisor suggests writing "we confirm the paper's result." (a) Write the paragraph you would put in the paper (100 to 150 words). (b) Write a short, respectful reply to your advisor (4 to 6 sentences) explaining your wording, with the next experiments you propose. (c) State what a qualifying-exam answer on this situation should say about the standard error and the interval, in two sentences.
hint: Say what you found, what you assumed, and what you cannot conclude; then propose one-factor-at-a-time checks.
solution: (a) We attempted to reproduce the paper's reported test accuracy of 86.0% using the paper's data split and hyperparameters. The paper does not state its learning-rate schedule, so we used a cosine schedule chosen on validation data. Over five seeds our mean test accuracy was 84.06% (sample standard deviation 0.87; standard error 0.39; 95% interval 82.98 to 85.14), so the reported value lies about five standard errors above our mean and outside the interval. We therefore did not reproduce the reported number under our protocol. We cannot say whether the unreported schedule or another detail explains the difference, and we did not test other data sets. Code and logs are released. (b) Thank you for the suggestion. I would like to avoid the word "confirm" because our interval excludes the reported value, and a reader checking our tables would see that. I would rather write that we did not reproduce the number under our protocol and describe what we tried, which is still a useful result. The most likely cause is the unreported schedule, so I propose three runs each of two alternative schedules, one change at a time, and a polite message to the authors asking for the schedule. If the gap closes, we will say so and report all runs. (c) The standard error $s/\sqrt n$ measures the uncertainty of the mean, so the interval $\bar x\pm t^\ast\mathrm{SE}$ with $t^\ast=2.776$ for four degrees of freedom tells us the reported value is inconsistent with the data at that confidence level; it does not by itself tell us why, and the gap needs the pipeline checks.
rubric: The paragraph states the target, protocol, unreported detail, numbers with labeled uncertainty and a conclusion limited to the evidence, with no blame.
rubric: The reply is respectful and factual, explains why "confirm" is not supported, and proposes one-factor-at-a-time checks and contacting the authors.
rubric: The exam statement defines the standard error and the interval correctly and says what the interval does and does not show.
