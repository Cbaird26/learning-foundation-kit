@project
title: Which phone plan is better, and for whom?
overview: You will model two phone plans as linear relationships using a table, a graph and an equation, then decide which plan is better for different kinds of users. Plan A costs \$20 per month plus \$5 for each gigabyte (GB) of data used. Plan B costs \$35 per month plus \$2 for each GB used.
goal: Write a linear equation for the monthly cost of each plan and explain the slope and intercept in context.
goal: Find where the plans cost the same by graphing, substitution and elimination, and check that the methods agree.
goal: Use your model to give a clear, reasoned recommendation for at least two different users.
step: Let $g$ be the number of GB used in a month and $C$ the monthly cost in dollars. Write $C_A$ and $C_B$ as equations in $g$, and say what the slope and the $y$-intercept of each mean.
step: Make a table of cost for $g = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10$ for both plans. Check that the cost rises by a constant amount per GB.
step: Graph both lines on the same axes, with $g$ on the horizontal axis and cost on the vertical axis. Label the lines and the point where they cross.
step: Find the crossing point by substitution (set $C_A = C_B$) and by elimination (write both as $C - 5g = 20$ and $C - 2g = 35$ and subtract). Compare with the graph.
step: Decide which plan is cheaper when $g$ is below the crossing value and when it is above. Describe the result in words and with an inequality.
step: Test your recommendation on three users: a light user (about $2$ GB), a medium user (about $5$ GB) and a heavy user (about $12$ GB). Compute each user's cost on both plans.
step: Add a third plan of your own design (for example, \$50 per month with unlimited data) and say whom it is best for. Note any limits of the model, such as extra fees or data caps.
deliverable: A one-page write-up with the equations, the table, the graph and the crossing point found by all three methods.
deliverable: A short recommendation (4 to 6 sentences) that tells each of the three users which plan to choose and why, with the costs.
rubric: The equations $C_A = 5g + 20$ and $C_B = 2g + 35$ are correct, with slope and intercept interpreted with units.
rubric: The table and graph agree with the equations and the graph is labeled with axes, units and the intersection.
rubric: The crossing point $(5, 45)$ is found by substitution and elimination, and checked in both equations.
rubric: The recommendation uses the model correctly for each user and states the cost comparison.
rubric: The write-up is clear and mentions at least one limitation of the model.
tutor: Can you check whether my equation for Plan B has the right slope and intercept, without giving me the answer?
tutor: Which method, substitution or elimination, is easier for this system, and why?
tutor: What assumptions am I making when I use a straight-line model for a phone bill?

@review
### Week by week

- **Week 1, exponents and scientific notation.** Product rule $a^m a^n = a^{m+n}$, quotient rule $a^m / a^n = a^{m-n}$, power rule $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$, $a^0 = 1$, $a^{-n} = 1/a^n$. Scientific notation is $a \times 10^n$ with $1 \le a < 10$.
- **Week 2, roots and irrational numbers.** $x^2 = p$ has two solutions $\pm\sqrt{p}$; $\sqrt[3]{p}$ has one. Non-perfect-square roots are irrational. Estimate by bracketing between perfect squares.
- **Week 3, multistep equations.** Distribute, combine like terms, collect variables, divide, check. A false result means no solution; a true result means infinitely many.
- **Week 4, slope.** $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$; the slope is a rate of change with units.
- **Week 5, linear functions.** $y = mx + b$; find $b$ from a point; two points give $m$ first.
- **Week 6, systems.** Solve by graphing, substitution or elimination; the solution is an ordered pair checked in both equations. Parallel lines give no solution.
- **Week 7, functions.** One output per input; use $f(x)$ notation; compare functions from tables, graphs and rules.
- **Week 8, transformations.** Translation, reflection, rotation and dilation; the first three are rigid motions, a dilation produces a similar figure.
- **Week 9, angles and similarity.** Triangle sum $180^\circ$, exterior angle theorem, parallel-line angle pairs, AA similarity and proportions.
- **Week 10, Pythagorean theorem.** $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, the converse, and the distance formula.
- **Week 11, volume.** Cylinder $\pi r^2 h$, cone $\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$, sphere $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$.
- **Week 12, scatter plots.** Direction, form, strength, outliers; fit a line, predict, interpret.

### Self-check

- I can multiply and divide in scientific notation without a calculator.
- I can solve an equation with variables on both sides and check it.
- I can find a slope from two points and say what it means.
- I can solve one system three ways.
- I can say whether a relation is a function.
- I can use similarity and the Pythagorean theorem together.

### Common errors

- Multiplying exponents in the product rule instead of adding them.
- Dropping a negative sign when distributing.
- Forgetting the second solution of $x^2 = p$.
- Giving a slope without units, or an $x$ without the $y$ of a system.
- Using the diameter as the radius in a volume formula.
- Extrapolating a trend line far beyond the data.

@exam num
prompt: Plan A costs \$30 per month plus \$6 per gigabyte. Plan B costs \$54 per month plus \$2 per gigabyte. For how many gigabytes in a month do the two plans cost the same?
answer: 6
check: (54 - 30) / (6 - 2)
unit: GB
hint: Write each monthly cost as an equation in the number of gigabytes.
hint: Set them equal: $30 + 6g = 54 + 2g$.
solution: $30 + 6g = 54 + 2g$ gives $4g = 24$, so $g = 6$. Both plans cost \$66 at $6$ GB.

@exam num
prompt: A right triangle has legs $5$ and $12$. It is spun about its leg of length $12$ to make a cone. Find the volume of the cone, in terms of $\pi$.
answer: 100pi
check: pi * 5**2 * 12 / 3
hint: Spinning about the leg of length $12$ gives a cone of height $12$; the other leg is the radius.
hint: Use $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$ with $r = 5$ and $h = 12$.
solution: The radius is $5$ and the height is $12$, so $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi (25)(12) = 100\pi$. The slant height is $\sqrt{25 + 144} = 13$, but it is not needed.

@exam expr
prompt: The line $y = 2x + 3$ is reflected over the $x$-axis. Write the equation of the image line as an expression for $y$.
vars: x
lead: $y =$
answer: -2x - 3
check: lambda x: -(2*x + 3)
hint: A reflection over the $x$-axis sends $(x, y)$ to $(x, -y)$.
hint: Every $y$ on the line changes sign.
solution: The image has $y$-values equal to the negatives of the original, so $y = -(2x + 3) = -2x - 3$.
