@article
A **square root** undoes squaring. The number $\sqrt{49}$ is the nonnegative number whose square is $49$, namely $7$. The equation $x^2 = 49$ has **two** solutions, $x = 7$ and $x = -7$, because $(-7)^2 = 49$ too. The symbol $\sqrt{\ }$ itself always means the nonnegative root.

A **cube root** undoes cubing: $\sqrt[3]{125} = 5$ because $5^3 = 125$. Unlike squares, cubes can be negative, so $\sqrt[3]{-8} = -2$, and $x^3 = p$ has exactly one real solution.

### Perfect squares and estimating

The **perfect squares** are $1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, \dots$ To estimate $\sqrt{30}$, notice that $30$ lies between $25$ and $36$, so $\sqrt{30}$ lies between $5$ and $6$. Since $5.5^2 = 30.25$ is just above $30$, the root is slightly less than $5.5$, about $5.48$.

### Rational and irrational numbers

A **rational number** can be written as a fraction $\frac{a}{b}$ of integers with $b \neq 0$. Its decimal form either stops ($\frac{3}{8} = 0.375$) or repeats forever ($\frac{1}{3} = 0.333\ldots$). Every integer is rational, and so is every perfect-square root such as $\sqrt{16} = 4$.

An **irrational number** cannot be written as a fraction of integers. Its decimal never stops and never repeats. The square root of any positive integer that is not a perfect square is irrational: $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{20}$ and $\sqrt{75}$ are irrational, and so is $\pi$. Together the rational and irrational numbers make up the **real numbers**.

Remember that $\frac{22}{7}$ is only a rational approximation of $\pi$, and $3.14$ is rounded; neither is equal to $\pi$.

### Sizes in scientific notation

Scientific notation and roots work together when comparing sizes. A red blood cell is about $7 \times 10^{-6}$ m across and a human hair about $7 \times 10^{-5}$ m. Dividing gives $\frac{7 \times 10^{-5}}{7 \times 10^{-6}} = 10$, so the hair is about ten cell-widths thick. To compare two sizes, divide them and subtract the exponents of ten.

@example
title: Locating a square root
problem: Between which two consecutive integers does $\sqrt{58}$ lie, and what is $\sqrt{58}$ to the nearest tenth?
step: List nearby perfect squares: $7^2 = 49$ and $8^2 = 64$. Since $49 < 58 < 64$, we have $7 < \sqrt{58} < 8$.
step: Try the midpoint: $7.5^2 = 56.25$, which is less than $58$, so the root is above $7.5$.
step: Try $7.6^2 = 57.76$ (still below $58$) and $7.7^2 = 59.29$ (above $58$). The root is between $7.6$ and $7.7$, and $58$ is much closer to $57.76$.
step: So $\sqrt{58} \approx 7.6$ to the nearest tenth.

@mistakes
- Giving only the positive solution of $x^2 = p$; there are two, $\pm\sqrt{p}$.
- Thinking a long decimal such as $0.3333$ is irrational; repeating or ending decimals are rational.
- Writing $\sqrt{a + b} = \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$; for example $\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$, not $3 + 4$.
- Treating $\frac{22}{7}$ or $3.14$ as exactly equal to $\pi$.

@practice num
prompt: Evaluate $\sqrt[3]{125}$.
answer: 5
check: round(125 ** (1/3))
hint: Ask which number multiplied by itself three times gives $125$.
hint: Try $4^3$ and $5^3$.
solution: $5^3 = 5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5 = 125$, so $\sqrt[3]{125} = 5$.

answer: 7.1
check: math.sqrt(50)
tol: 0.05
hint: $50$ is just above the perfect square $49$.
hint: $7^2 = 49$ and $7.1^2 = 50.41$.
solution: $7^2 = 49 < 50 < 50.41 = 7.1^2$, so $\sqrt{50}$ is between $7$ and $7.1$. Since $7.07^2 \approx 49.98$, the root is about $7.07$, which rounds to $7.1$.

unit: m

hint: You need a number whose square is $169$.
solution: Side $= \sqrt{169} = 13$ m, since $13^2 = 169$.
