@article
When two quantitative variables are measured on the same individuals, a **scatterplot** shows the relationship. Put the **explanatory variable** $x$ (the one used to predict) on the horizontal axis and the **response variable** $y$ on the vertical axis. Describe the direction (positive or negative), form (linear or curved), strength and any outliers.

### Correlation

The **correlation coefficient** $r$ measures the strength and direction of a *linear* relationship:

$$r = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum \left(\frac{x_i - \bar{x}}{s_x}\right)\left(\frac{y_i - \bar{y}}{s_y}\right).$$

It always satisfies $-1 \le r \le 1$. The sign gives the direction, and values near $\pm 1$ indicate a strong linear pattern. It has no units, does not change if you swap $x$ and $y$ or change units, and is not resistant: one outlier can change it a lot. A value of $r$ near $0$ means *no linear* relationship, but a strong curved pattern is still possible, so always look at the scatterplot.

### The least-squares line

The **least-squares regression line** $\hat{y} = a + bx$ is the line that minimizes the sum of squared vertical distances from the points to the line. Its slope and intercept are

$$b = r\,\frac{s_y}{s_x}, \qquad a = \bar{y} - b\,\bar{x}.$$

The line always passes through $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$. Interpretation matters:

- **Slope $b$:** the predicted change in $y$ for each one-unit increase in $x$.
- **Intercept $a$:** the predicted $y$ when $x = 0$ (meaningful only if $x = 0$ makes sense in context).

### Residuals and $r^2$

A **residual** is the observed value minus the predicted value, $y - \hat{y}$. Residuals are positive for points above the line. A residual plot with no pattern supports a linear model; a curved pattern says the line is not appropriate.

The **coefficient of determination** $r^2$ is the fraction of the variation in $y$ explained by the linear relationship with $x$. If $r = -0.6$ then $r^2 = 0.36$: $36\%$ of the variation in $y$ is accounted for by the line.

### Cautions

**Extrapolation**, predicting outside the range of the observed $x$ values, is risky because the pattern may not continue. **Outliers** and *influential points* (points far from the others in the $x$ direction) can pull the line. Most important, **correlation is not causation**. A strong association can come from a **lurking (confounding) variable** that affects both: ice cream sales and drowning deaths are correlated because both rise in hot weather. Only a well-designed randomized experiment (week 5) can support a causal claim.

@example
title: Fitting a line to a small data set
problem: In five patients, the number of weeks on a walking program is $x = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ and the drop in systolic blood pressure (mm Hg) is $y = 2, 4, 5, 4, 5$. Find the least-squares line and predict the drop at $x = 3.5$ weeks.
step: The means are $\bar{x} = 3$ and $\bar{y} = 4$. The deviation products $(x - \bar{x})(y - \bar{y})$ are $4, 0, 0, 0, 2$ with sum $S_{xy} = 6$, and $\sum (x - \bar{x})^2 = 10$.
step: The slope is $b = S_{xy}/S_{xx} = 6/10 = 0.6$ mm Hg per week.
step: The intercept is $a = \bar{y} - b\bar{x} = 4 - 0.6(3) = 2.2$, so $\hat{y} = 2.2 + 0.6x$.
step: At $x = 3.5$: $\hat{y} = 2.2 + 0.6(3.5) = 4.3$ mm Hg. This is within the observed range of $x$ (1 to 5), so it is interpolation, not extrapolation.

@example
title: Residual and interpretation
problem: For the line $\hat{y} = 2.2 + 0.6x$ above, a patient with $x = 5$ had an observed drop of $5$. Find the residual and interpret the slope.
step: The predicted value is $\hat{y} = 2.2 + 0.6(5) = 5.2$.
step: The residual is $y - \hat{y} = 5 - 5.2 = -0.2$. The point lies slightly below the line.
step: The slope $0.6$ means that each additional week on the program is associated with an average predicted $0.6$ mm Hg additional drop in blood pressure. We say "associated with", not "causes", since this is only an observed pattern.

@mistakes
- Saying a correlation of $r = 0.8$ means "$80\%$ of the data fit the line"; it is $r^2 = 0.64$ that measures explained variation.
- Swapping $x$ and $y$ when computing the slope, which gives a different line (the slope is $r\,s_y/s_x$ for predicting $y$ from $x$).
- Interpreting the intercept literally when $x = 0$ is outside the data or meaningless.
- Concluding that $x$ causes $y$ from an association alone.

@practice num
prompt: A regression has correlation $r = 0.8$, $s_x = 5$ and $s_y = 10$. Find the slope of the least-squares line for predicting $y$ from $x$.
answer: 1.6
check: 0.8 * 10 / 5
tol: 0.001
hint: The slope is $b = r\,s_y/s_x$.
solution: $b = 0.8 \cdot 10/5 = 1.6$.

@practice num
prompt: In a sample, $\bar{x} = 40$, $\bar{y} = 120$, and the slope of the least-squares line is $b = 0.5$. Find the intercept $a$.
answer: 100
check: 120 - 0.5 * 40
tol: 0.001
hint: The line passes through the point $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$.
solution: $a = \bar{y} - b\bar{x} = 120 - 0.5(40) = 100$.

@practice num
prompt: A fitted line is $\hat{y} = 3.2 + 1.5x$. A subject with $x = 10$ has an observed value $y = 20$. Find the residual (observed minus predicted).
answer: 1.8
check: 20 - (3.2 + 1.5 * 10)
tol: 0.01
hint: First compute the predicted value at $x = 10$.
solution: $\hat{y} = 3.2 + 15 = 18.2$. The residual is $20 - 18.2 = 1.8$.

@practice num
prompt: Four paired measurements are $(x, y) = (1, 1), (2, 3), (3, 2), (4, 4)$. Find the correlation coefficient $r$.
answer: 0.8
check: np.corrcoef([1, 2, 3, 4], [1, 3, 2, 4])[0, 1]
tol: 0.01
hint: Both means are $2.5$. Compute the sum of the products of deviations $S_{xy}$, and $S_{xx}$ and $S_{yy}$.
hint: $S_{xy} = 4$, $S_{xx} = 5$, $S_{yy} = 5$, and $r = S_{xy}/\sqrt{S_{xx}S_{yy}}$.
solution: Deviations of $x$: $-1.5, -0.5, 0.5, 1.5$. Deviations of $y$: $-1.5, 0.5, -0.5, 1.5$. Products: $2.25, -0.25, -0.25, 2.25$ with sum $4$. Then $S_{xx} = S_{yy} = 5$ and $r = 4/5 = 0.8$.

@quiz num
prompt: For a data set, the correlation is $r = -0.5$, $s_x = 4$ and $s_y = 12$. Find the slope of the least-squares line for predicting $y$ from $x$.
answer: -1.5
check: -0.5 * 12 / 4
tol: 0.001
hint: Use $b = r\,s_y/s_x$ and keep the sign of $r$.
solution: $b = -0.5(12)/4 = -1.5$: the predicted $y$ falls by $1.5$ for each unit increase in $x$.

@quiz mc
prompt: An observational study finds that people who carry lighters have a higher rate of lung cancer. What is the best explanation?
choice*: Smoking is a lurking variable: smokers are more likely both to carry lighters and to develop lung cancer.
choice: Carrying a lighter causes lung cancer.
choice: Lung cancer causes people to carry lighters.
choice: The correlation must be an arithmetic error.
hint: Look for a third variable that could affect both of the measured ones.
solution: Smoking is associated with both carrying a lighter and lung cancer, so it confounds the association. The lighter itself is not a plausible cause, and the association alone cannot establish causation.
