@article
Computers and the internet give us enormous benefits, and they also create risks. Knowing both helps you use them well.

### Phishing and scams

**Phishing** is a trick in which a message pretends to come from a bank, a game, a school or a friend to get you to give away a password, click a harmful link or send money. Warning signs include:

- **urgency or threats** ("your account will be closed today");
- a request for a **password, code or payment**, especially by gift card;
- a **strange sender address** or a link that does not match the real site;
- spelling mistakes, or an offer that is too good to be true.

If you are unsure, do not click. Go to the real site by typing its address yourself, or ask a trusted adult. Real companies do not ask for your password in a message.

### Reading a link

The part of a link that matters is the **domain**, which comes right after `https://` and ends at the next `/`. Only the **end** of the host name tells you who owns it. The host `login.example.com` belongs to `example.com`, but `example.com.verify-login.net` belongs to `verify-login.net`, which merely starts with familiar words.

```python
from urllib.parse import urlparse

def belongs_to(url, domain):
    host = urlparse(url).hostname
    return host == domain or host.endswith("." + domain)

print(belongs_to("https://login.example.com/", "example.com"))        # True
print(belongs_to("https://example.com.verify-login.net/", "example.com"))  # False
```

### Privacy and your digital footprint

Everything you post, search and share leaves a **digital footprint**, a trail that is hard to erase and that others can copy. Protect it: share little personal information (full name, address, school, birthday), use privacy settings, think before you post (could a stranger, a future employer or a family member see it?), and ask before posting photos of other people. Apps and websites collect data, often to show advertising; read what permissions an app asks for and turn off what it does not need.

### Staying safe

Install **software updates**, which fix security holes. Use strong, unique passwords and two-step verification. Be careful with unknown attachments and downloads. If something online upsets you or someone you do not know contacts you, **tell a trusted adult**; you are never in trouble for asking for help.

### Computing in society

Computing brings benefits, such as communication, medicine and education, and raises debates. **Bias**: a program learns from data, so if the data is unfair the results can be too. **Access**: people without devices or a good internet connection are at a disadvantage, called the **digital divide**. **Accessibility**: good design lets people with different abilities use technology. **Environment**: data centres and devices use energy and materials. Reasonable people disagree about how to balance these, which is why computer scientists need to think about people as well as code.

problem: Does `https://shop.example.com.pay-now.org/cart` belong to `example.com`?
step: Take the host: `shop.example.com.pay-now.org`.

@mistakes
- Judging a link by its first words. The owner is shown by the end of the host name, before the first `/`.
- Trusting a message because it knows your name. Scammers can find names easily.
- Thinking you are safe because you posted only to friends. Friends can copy or share a post.
- Hiding a problem out of embarrassment instead of telling a trusted adult.
