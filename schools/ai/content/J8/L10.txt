### A new government

George Washington took office as the first president in 1789. The new government faced disagreements that grew into the first political parties. **Federalists** favored a strong national government and a national bank. **Democratic-Republicans** feared concentrated power and favored states' rights and an economy based on small farms. In *Marbury v. Madison* (1803) the Supreme Court established **judicial review**, the power to decide whether a law violates the Constitution.

### The Louisiana Purchase and exploration

In 1803 the United States bought the Louisiana Territory from France for about \$15 million. It covered roughly 828,000 square miles and about doubled the size of the country. The land was home to many Native nations, who had not been part of the sale. The Corps of Discovery explored the territory from 1804 to 1806 and relied on Native guides, trade and knowledge.

### Growth and change

The **War of 1812** against Britain strengthened national feeling. The **Erie Canal** (1825) and new steam transport and canals lowered shipping costs. The **cotton gin** (1793) made growing cotton more profitable and increased demand for enslaved labor. Voting expanded to more white men during the 1820s and 1830s, while women, free Black men in many states, and Native Americans were generally excluded.

### Native nations and removal

As settlers moved west, the government pressed Native nations to give up land. The **Indian Removal Act** (1830) authorized negotiating the exchange of Native lands in the East for land west of the Mississippi. Many nations, including the Cherokee, the Choctaw, the Muscogee (Creek), the Chickasaw and the Seminole, were forced to move. The Cherokee had challenged removal in court; the journey of about 1838 to 1839 is known as the **Trail of Tears**, and thousands died. Supporters of removal said it would open land for settlers; Native nations said that the land was theirs by right and by treaty.

### Manifest Destiny and the Mexican-American War

In the 1840s many Americans embraced **Manifest Destiny**, the belief that the United States was meant to spread across the continent. Texas, which had separated from Mexico, was annexed in 1845. The **Mexican-American War** (1846 to 1848) followed a dispute over the border, and Mexico and the United States disagreed about who was responsible. In the **Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo** (1848) Mexico ceded land that includes present-day California, Nevada, Utah and most of Arizona and New Mexico. Mexicans living there could become U.S. citizens, but many lost land and rights over time.

### Expansion and slavery

Each new territory raised the question: would slavery be allowed? The **Missouri Compromise** (1820) admitted Missouri as a slave state and Maine as a free state and banned slavery in most of the rest of the Louisiana Territory north of 36°30′ latitude. The debate would grow after 1848 and lead toward the Civil War.

title: Cost per square mile
problem: The Louisiana Purchase cost about \$15 million for about 828,000 square miles. About how many dollars per square mile is that?
step: Write the cost in dollars: \$15 million is $15{,}000{,}000$.
step: Divide by the area: $15000000 \div 828000$.
step: The result is about $18.1$ dollars per square mile.
step: Remember: this measures what the United States paid France; it does not say anything about what Native nations living there received or wanted.

@mistakes
- Thinking the United States bought the land from the people living on it; it bought France's claim.
- Calling the Trail of Tears one single event involving one nation only; several nations were removed.
- Saying the Mexican-American War had only one point of view; Americans and Mexicans described its causes differently.
- Leaving out slavery when explaining why westward expansion became a national conflict.
