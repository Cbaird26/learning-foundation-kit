### The Renaissance

The **Renaissance** ("rebirth") began in the city-states of Italy in the 1300s and spread across Europe through the 1500s. Wealthy trading cities such as Florence and Venice paid for art and learning. A central idea was **humanism**: an interest in human potential and achievement, and in the literature and ideas of ancient Greece and Rome. Artists developed realistic perspective, and scholars studied classical texts. Around 1450 a European printer developed **movable-type printing** (printing with reusable metal letters), which made books cheaper and helped ideas travel fast. Printing with movable type had been developed earlier in East Asia, and the techniques developed separately.

### The Reformation

In 1517 criticism of Church practices, such as the selling of **indulgences** (documents reducing punishment for sins), led a German monk to publish the **Ninety-five Theses**. Printing helped spread the debate. The movement became the **Protestant Reformation**. Protestants stressed the Bible as the main authority, and many taught that people are saved by faith. Leaders and rulers in different regions formed Lutheran, Calvinist and Anglican churches. Many Catholics also pressed for reform: the **Council of Trent** (1545 to 1563) clarified Catholic teaching and reformed Church practice, a movement often called the Catholic Reformation or Counter-Reformation. Europe saw long conflicts, including wars between Catholic and Protestant states, and the Peace of Augsburg (1555) let German rulers choose between Catholic and Lutheran faiths for their territories.

### European exploration

In the 1400s and 1500s, European rulers sponsored voyages. Motives included wealth (especially direct access to Asian spices and silk), religious aims, rivalry among kingdoms, and curiosity. Improved technology helped: the **caravel** ship, the magnetic **compass**, and the **astrolabe**, which measured the angle of the sun or stars. Portugal explored down the coast of Africa and sailed round its southern tip in 1488; a voyage reached India in 1498. In 1492 a Spanish-sponsored voyage crossed the Atlantic and reached islands in the Caribbean, beginning lasting contact between Europe and the Americas. A Spanish-sponsored expedition completed the first voyage around the world in 1519 to 1522.

### Perspectives

The Americas were already home to millions of people with complex societies, including the **Aztec** (Mexica) and **Inca** empires and many other nations. For Europeans, the voyages brought new wealth and knowledge. For Indigenous peoples, European arrival brought conquest, forced labor, and epidemic disease. Historians weigh both the achievements of navigation and the harm of conquest and enslavement.

@example
title: Cause and effect with the printing press
problem: Explain how movable-type printing helped the Reformation spread.
step: Start with the cause: before printing, books were copied by hand, so they were slow to make and expensive.
step: Printing with movable type produced many copies quickly and cheaply, so pamphlets and Bibles could reach many more readers.
step: Connect to the Reformation: arguments against Church practices, and Bibles in local languages, circulated widely and quickly.
step: State the effect: "Because printing made texts cheap and fast to copy, Reformation ideas spread across Europe much faster than earlier reform movements."

@mistakes
- Saying Columbus (or anyone from Europe) "discovered" an empty land; millions of people already lived there.
- Treating the Reformation as only a religious event; politics and rulers' choices also mattered.
- Claiming printing was invented in Europe alone; movable type was also developed in East Asia earlier.
- Describing Catholics as simply opposed to all reform; the Catholic Reformation addressed many criticisms.
