@article
A linear function adds the same amount at each step. An **exponential** function multiplies by the same factor at each step. That is the signature of growth: bacteria, savings with interest, spreading news.

### Start with a story

A savings account holds 1000 dollars and pays 5% interest each year. After one year it holds $1000 \times 1.05 = 1050$ dollars. After two years it holds $1000 \times 1.05^2 = 1102.50$ dollars. After $t$ years:

$$A(t) = 1000\,(1.05)^t.$$

Compare a linear plan that adds 50 dollars a year: 1050, 1100, 1150, and so on. The exponential account pulls ahead because interest earns interest.

### Exponential functions

An exponential function has the form $f(x) = a\,b^x$ with $a \neq 0$, $b > 0$ and $b \neq 1$. The number $a$ is the starting value, since $f(0) = a$, and $b$ is the **growth factor** per step. If $b > 1$ the function grows; if $0 < b < 1$ it decays. A growth rate $r$ per step gives $b = 1 + r$, and a decay rate $r$ gives $b = 1 - r$. For $a > 0$ the domain is all real numbers and the range is $y > 0$: the outputs never reach zero or go negative.

### Logarithms: the inverse operation

How long until the account reaches 2000 dollars? We need $1.05^t = 2$, and the unknown is in the exponent. Ordinary algebra cannot reach it, so we define a new inverse operation, the **logarithm**:

$$\log_b y = x \iff b^x = y \qquad (b > 0,\; b \neq 1,\; y > 0).$$

In words, $\log_b y$ is the exponent you must put on $b$ to get $y$. So $\log_2 8 = 3$ because $2^3 = 8$, and $\log_{10} 1000 = 3$. Always $\log_b 1 = 0$ and $\log_b b = 1$. Since $b^x$ is always positive, logarithms of zero and negative numbers do not exist. As with any inverse, domain and range swap: $\log_b$ has domain $y > 0$ and range all real numbers. On a calculator, $\log$ means base $10$ and $\ln$ means base $e \approx 2.718$.

### The laws of logarithms

$$\log_b(MN) = \log_b M + \log_b N, \qquad \log_b\!\frac{M}{N} = \log_b M - \log_b N, \qquad \log_b(M^k) = k\,\log_b M.$$

The power law is the key to solving equations with the unknown in an exponent. Take a logarithm of both sides, then bring the exponent down:

$$1.05^t = 2 \;\Rightarrow\; t\,\ln 1.05 = \ln 2 \;\Rightarrow\; t = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln 1.05} \approx 14.2 \text{ years.}$$

### Check your work

Substitute back: $1.05^{14.2} \approx 2.0$. And remember that $\log(M + N)$ is **not** $\log M + \log N$. There is no law for the logarithm of a sum.

@example
title: Doubling without a calculator
problem: A population of 500 bacteria doubles every 3 hours. When will there be 8000 bacteria?
step: Doubling every 3 hours gives the model $P(t) = 500 \cdot 2^{t/3}$ with $t$ in hours. Check: $P(3) = 1000$.
step: Set $P(t) = 8000$: $500 \cdot 2^{t/3} = 8000$.
step: Isolate the exponential by dividing both sides by $500$: $2^{t/3} = 16$.
step: Take the logarithm base $2$ of both sides: $\frac{t}{3} = \log_2 16 = 4$, because $2^4 = 16$.
step: So $t = 12$ hours. Check: $500 \cdot 2^4 = 8000$.

@example
title: Solving for the time with natural logarithms
problem: How long does 1000 dollars take to grow to 1500 dollars at 6% interest per year?
step: The growth factor is $1 + 0.06 = 1.06$, so $A(t) = 1000\,(1.06)^t$.
step: Set $A(t) = 1500$ and divide both sides by $1000$: $1.06^t = 1.5$.
step: Take the natural logarithm of both sides: $\ln\!\left(1.06^t\right) = \ln 1.5$.
step: Use the power law to bring the exponent down: $t \ln 1.06 = \ln 1.5$, so $t = \dfrac{\ln 1.5}{\ln 1.06}$.
step: Evaluate: $t \approx \dfrac{0.4055}{0.0583} \approx 6.96$ years. Check: $1.06^{6.96} \approx 1.50$.

@mistakes
- Treating $\log(M + N)$ as $\log M + \log N$, or $\log\frac{M}{N}$ as $\frac{\log M}{\log N}$.
- Taking the logarithm before isolating the exponential. From $500 \cdot 2^{t/3} = 8000$, divide by $500$ first.
- Using the rate instead of the factor: a 5% rise multiplies by $1.05$, not by $0.05$.
- Taking the logarithm of zero or of a negative number, which is undefined.

@practice num
prompt: Solve $5^x = 40$. Give $x$ rounded to 3 decimal places.
answer: 2.292
tol: 0.0005
check: root(lambda x: 5**x - 40, 0, 10)
hint: Take the logarithm of both sides, then use the power law to bring $x$ down from the exponent.
hint: You get $x \ln 5 = \ln 40$. Divide by $\ln 5$ and use a calculator.
solution: $x = \dfrac{\ln 40}{\ln 5} \approx \dfrac{3.6889}{1.6094} \approx 2.292$. Check: $5^{2.292} \approx 40$.

@practice self
prompt: A savings account starts with 800 dollars and earns 4% interest per year, which stays in the account. Treat $t$ (years) as a continuous variable. (a) Write a formula for the balance $A(t)$. (b) State the domain and range of $A$ in this context. (c) Use a logarithm to find, to the nearest tenth of a year, when the balance reaches 1200 dollars. (d) Explain in a sentence or two why the logarithm is the right tool, using the idea of inverse operations.
hint: Growth of 4% per year means multiplying by 1.04 each year, starting from 800.
hint: For (c), divide both sides by 800 first, then take the logarithm of both sides.
solution: (a) $A(t) = 800\,(1.04)^t$. (b) In context the domain is $t \ge 0$ (time since the account opened) and the range is $A \ge 800$, because the balance never falls below the starting amount. As a bare function the domain is all real numbers and the range is all positive numbers. (c) $800\,(1.04)^t = 1200$ gives $1.04^t = 1.5$, so $t = \dfrac{\ln 1.5}{\ln 1.04} \approx \dfrac{0.4055}{0.03922} \approx 10.3$ years. (d) The unknown $t$ sits in an exponent. Exponentiation is undone by taking a logarithm, so the logarithm is the inverse operation that brings $t$ down, just as subtraction undoes addition.
rubric: The model uses the growth factor $1.04$ (not $0.04$) and the starting value $800$.
rubric: The domain $t \ge 0$ and range $A \ge 800$ are stated in context, with a reason.
rubric: The equation $1.04^t = 1.5$ is solved with logarithms to give about $10.3$ years.
rubric: The explanation says that a logarithm undoes exponentiation, isolating the exponent.

answer: 3.8

hint: Take logarithms of both sides: $t \ln 1.2 = \ln 2$.

@quiz num
prompt: Solve $2\log_5 x = \log_5 (x + 12)$. Remember that logarithms need positive inputs.
answer: 4
check: root(lambda x: 2*log(x)/log(5) - log(x + 12)/log(5), 0.01, 100)
hint: Use the power law on the left side: $2\log_5 x = \log_5 x^2$.
hint: If $\log_5 A = \log_5 B$ then $A = B$. Solve $x^2 = x + 12$, then check each answer against the domain $x > 0$.
solution: $\log_5 x^2 = \log_5 (x + 12)$ gives $x^2 = x + 12$, so $x^2 - x - 12 = (x - 4)(x + 3) = 0$. The solution $x = -3$ is not allowed, since $\log_5(-3)$ is undefined, so $x = 4$. Check: $2\log_5 4 = \log_5 16$ and $\log_5 (4 + 12) = \log_5 16$.
