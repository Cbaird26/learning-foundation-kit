@article
Every living thing is made of the same small set of elements: mostly **carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, phosphorus and sulfur**. Biology is chemistry organized into cells, so we begin with the molecules that make cells work.

### Water: the solvent of life

A water molecule is **polar**: oxygen pulls shared electrons harder than hydrogen does, so oxygen carries a slight negative charge and each hydrogen a slight positive charge. The positive hydrogen of one molecule is attracted to the negative oxygen of a neighbor. This attraction is a **hydrogen bond**. Individually weak, hydrogen bonds are numerous, and they explain water's key properties:

- **Cohesion and surface tension:** water molecules stick to each other, so water climbs plant xylem and forms droplets.
- **Excellent solvent:** polar and ionic substances (salts, sugars) dissolve because water surrounds them. Such substances are **hydrophilic**; oils are **hydrophobic**.
- **High specific heat:** breaking hydrogen bonds absorbs energy, so water resists temperature change and stabilizes bodies and oceans.
- **Ice floats:** in ice, hydrogen bonds hold molecules in an open lattice, making ice less dense than liquid water.

### pH

Water slightly ionizes into $H^+$ and $OH^-$. The **pH** is
$$\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[H^+],$$
with $[H^+]$ in mol/L. Because the scale is logarithmic, **each pH unit is a tenfold change** in $[H^+]$. Pure water at 25 °C is pH 7 (neutral); below 7 is acidic, above 7 is basic. A **buffer**, such as the bicarbonate system in blood, resists pH change; blood is held near pH 7.4.

### The four macromolecules

Most biological macromolecules are **polymers** built from **monomers**. Cells join monomers by **dehydration synthesis** (a water molecule is removed for each bond formed) and break polymers by **hydrolysis** (water is added).

| Class | Elements | Monomer | Bond | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | C, H, O (about 1:2:1) | monosaccharide | glycosidic | glucose, starch, glycogen, cellulose |
| Lipids | C, H, O (few O) | not true polymers; glycerol + fatty acids | ester | fats, phospholipids, steroids |
| Proteins | C, H, O, N, usually S | amino acid | peptide | enzymes, hemoglobin, collagen |
| Nucleic acids | C, H, O, N, P | nucleotide | phosphodiester | DNA, RNA |

A chain of $n$ amino acids has $n - 1$ peptide bonds. Protein shape has four levels: **primary** (sequence), **secondary** (alpha helices and beta sheets held by hydrogen bonds), **tertiary** (overall 3D fold) and **quaternary** (multiple chains). Shape determines function, so changing the sequence can change the protein.

Lipids are mostly hydrophobic. **Phospholipids** have a hydrophilic phosphate head and two hydrophobic fatty-acid tails, which is why they form cell membranes. **Saturated** fatty acids have no carbon-carbon double bonds; **unsaturated** ones have at least one, which puts kinks in the tail.

@example
title: Comparing acidity on the pH scale
problem: Gastric juice has pH 2 and a sample of black coffee has pH 5. How many times greater is the hydrogen-ion concentration of gastric juice?
step: Convert each pH to $[H^+]$: for pH 2, $[H^+] = 10^{-2}$ mol/L; for pH 5, $[H^+] = 10^{-5}$ mol/L.
step: Divide the larger concentration by the smaller: $10^{-2} / 10^{-5} = 10^{3}$.
step: The pH differs by 3 units, and each unit is a factor of 10, so gastric juice has $10^3 = 1000$ times more $H^+$.

@mistakes
- Thinking a hydrogen bond is a bond inside a water molecule; it is an attraction between molecules (the covalent O-H bonds are inside).
- Treating pH as linear: pH 3 is 100 times, not 2 times, more acidic than pH 5.
- Saying lipids are polymers of one repeating monomer, or that all lipids are fats; steroids and phospholipids are lipids too.
- Confusing hydrolysis (adds water, breaks bonds) with dehydration synthesis (removes water, forms bonds).
