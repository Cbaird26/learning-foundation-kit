objective: Say what a mixture and a solution are.
objective: Show that the total mass stays the same after mixing and dissolving.

@article
A **mixture** is two or more things put together that keep their own properties. Sand and pebbles in a bucket are a mixture. You can pick them apart again.

### Separating mixtures
Different tools work for different mixtures:
- A **magnet** pulls iron pieces out of a mixture.
- A **sieve** (strainer) holds back big pieces and lets small ones through.
- A **filter** (coffee filter) holds back sand but lets water pass.

### Solutions
A **solution** is a mixture where one thing **dissolves** in another so you cannot see the pieces. Salt dissolves in water. The water looks clear, but the salt is still there. Never taste science experiments. Sand does not dissolve in water. It sinks, so it is not a solution.

To get the salt back, let the water **evaporate**. Pour a little salt water on a plate and leave it in a warm, sunny spot for a few days. The water becomes a gas and the salt crystals stay behind.

### Mass stays the same
Weigh a cup of water and a spoon of salt before mixing. Then stir the salt in and weigh again. The total mass is the same. Nothing is lost. The salt is still there, just too small to see. If you separate them, you get the same salt back.

### Physical and chemical changes
In a **physical change**, the matter is the same kind of stuff after the change. Melting ice, tearing paper and dissolving salt are physical changes.

In a **chemical change**, new matter is made. Signs are bubbles of gas, a new smell or a color change that cannot go back. Mixing baking soda and vinegar makes bubbles of a new gas. A rusting nail and a baked cake are chemical changes too.

@example
title: Total mass
problem: A cup has 100 g of water. You stir in 10 g of salt until it dissolves. What is the mass of the salt water?
step: Mass is not lost when something dissolves.
step: Add: $100 + 10 = 110$. The salt water has a mass of 110 g.

@example
title: Which tool?
problem: You have a mixture of paper clips and rice. How can you separate it?
step: Paper clips are steel, and steel is pulled by a magnet.
step: Hold a magnet over the mixture and the paper clips jump up. The rice stays.

@mistakes
- Thinking the salt disappears when it dissolves. It is still there, and the mass stays the same.
- Thinking that stirring a mixture always makes a solution. Sand stirred in water is not a solution.
- Thinking that all changes can be undone. Cooking an egg is a chemical change you cannot undo.
