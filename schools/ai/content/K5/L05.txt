@article
A story has **characters**. These are the people or animals in the story. It also has a **setting**, which is where and when the story happens.

### The plot

The **plot** is what happens in a story, in order. Most plots have these parts:

- **Beginning:** we meet the characters and the setting.
- **Problem:** something goes wrong or a character wants something.
- **Events:** the character tries to fix it. Things happen.
- **Solution:** the problem is solved, or the character changes.

### Characters

We learn about a character from what they **say**, what they **do** and how they **feel**. If a girl shares her toys, we learn she is kind. If a boy keeps trying after he falls, we learn he is brave.

### A tiny story

"Tom's kite was stuck in a tree. He felt sad. He asked his big sister for help. She held the ladder and he climbed up. Tom got the kite and smiled."

- Characters: Tom and his sister.
- Setting: outside, near a tree.
- Problem: the kite is stuck.
- Solution: Tom climbs the ladder and gets it.
- Feeling: Tom feels sad, then happy.

When you read a story, stop now and then and ask: Who is it about? Where are they? What is the problem? How is it solved?

@example
title: Finding the problem and solution
problem: Read: "Ana's puppy ran away. Ana looked in the yard. She called his name. He came out from under the porch, wagging his tail." What are the problem and the solution?
step: The problem is that Ana's puppy ran away.
step: Ana looked in the yard and called his name. These are the events.
step: The solution is that the puppy came out from under the porch.

@mistakes
- Mixing up the setting (where and when) with the characters (who).
- Saying the problem is "the story" instead of naming exactly what went wrong.
- Telling how a character feels without a clue from the story.

choice: Rosa has a bike.
choice: It is a sunny day.
choice: Rosa is a girl.
hint: A problem is the thing that gets in the way.
solution: Rosa wants to ride, but the flat tire stops her. That is the problem.

choice: The fish was little.
choice: There was a big wave.
choice: The fish was scared.
hint: The solution is what makes the character feel better or fixes the problem.
solution: The mother stayed close and sang, and the fish felt calm. That solved the problem.

@practice self
prompt: Tell your grown-up a short story out loud, or draw it in four boxes. It needs a character, a setting, a problem and a solution. Then say each part: "The character is ... The setting is ..."
solution: Example: Pip the duck (character) is at the pond (setting). Pip loses her red hat in the water (problem). A frog hops over and pushes it back to her (solution).
rubric: The story has at least one character.
rubric: You said where the story happens.
rubric: The problem is something that really goes wrong.
rubric: The solution fixes the problem.
hint: Start with an animal you like, put it somewhere, then think of one thing that goes wrong for it.
