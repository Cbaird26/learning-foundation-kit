@article
Last lesson's MAP estimate returns one number. A Bayesian keeps the whole **posterior distribution** $p(\theta \mid \mathcal{D})$, which says how plausible every value of the parameter is after the data. For most models the posterior has no closed form, but for a few prior and likelihood pairs it does. These are the **conjugate** pairs.

### Conjugacy

A family of priors is **conjugate** to a likelihood if the posterior lies in the same family. Updating then reduces to changing the prior's parameters, which behave like counts.

### Beta-Bernoulli

The $\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha, \beta)$ density on $[0, 1]$ is $p(\theta) = \theta^{\alpha-1}(1-\theta)^{\beta-1}/B(\alpha, \beta)$ with $B(\alpha, \beta) = \Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)/\Gamma(\alpha + \beta)$. Its mean is $\alpha/(\alpha + \beta)$, and its variance is $\dfrac{\alpha\beta}{(\alpha + \beta)^2(\alpha + \beta + 1)}$; $\operatorname{Beta}(1, 1)$ is uniform. After $k$ successes in $n$ Bernoulli trials,

$$p(\theta \mid \mathcal{D}) \propto \underbrace{\theta^k(1-\theta)^{n-k}}_{\text{likelihood}}\ \underbrace{\theta^{\alpha-1}(1-\theta)^{\beta-1}}_{\text{prior}} = \theta^{\alpha + k - 1}(1-\theta)^{\beta + n - k - 1}.$$

This is the kernel of a Beta density, so the posterior is $\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha + k,\ \beta + n - k)$. The normalizing constant is whatever makes it integrate to 1, so we never compute it. **Rule: add the successes to $\alpha$ and the failures to $\beta$.**

With the prior $\operatorname{Beta}(2, 2)$ and 7 heads in 10 flips, the posterior is $\operatorname{Beta}(9, 5)$. Its mean is $9/14 \approx 0.643$, its mode is $8/12 \approx 0.667$ (the MAP estimate from last lesson), and its variance is $\frac{9 \cdot 5}{14^2 \cdot 15} \approx 0.0153$, a standard deviation of about $0.124$. A central 95% **credible interval** (the interval between the 2.5% and 97.5% posterior quantiles) is about $[0.386, 0.861]$. Unlike a point estimate, the posterior shows that 10 flips leave a lot of uncertainty.

The posterior mean is a weighted average:

$$\mathbb{E}[\theta \mid \mathcal{D}] = \frac{\alpha + k}{\alpha + \beta + n} = \frac{\alpha + \beta}{\alpha + \beta + n}\cdot\frac{\alpha}{\alpha + \beta} + \frac{n}{\alpha + \beta + n}\cdot\frac{k}{n}.$$

The first factor is the prior mean with weight $\alpha + \beta$ (the prior's effective sample size), the second is the MLE with weight $n$. As $n$ grows the data dominate. Because the update only adds counts, it can be done **sequentially**: the posterior after the first batch is the prior for the next, and the result equals the batch update.

### Gamma-Poisson

For counts $x_i \sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)$ use the prior $\lambda \sim \operatorname{Gamma}(a, b)$ with shape $a$ and rate $b$, density $\propto \lambda^{a-1}e^{-b\lambda}$, mean $a/b$ and variance $a/b^2$. The likelihood is $\propto \lambda^{\sum x_i}e^{-n\lambda}$, so

$$\lambda \mid \mathcal{D} \sim \operatorname{Gamma}\Big(a + \sum_i x_i,\ b + n\Big).$$

The shape accumulates the total count and the rate accumulates the number of observations.

### Gaussian mean with known variance

For $x_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with $\sigma^2$ known and prior $\mu \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \tau_0^2)$, multiply the Gaussian likelihood and prior and complete the square in $\mu$. The posterior is Gaussian, $\mathcal{N}(\mu_n, \tau_n^2)$, with

$$\frac{1}{\tau_n^2} = \frac{1}{\tau_0^2} + \frac{n}{\sigma^2}, \qquad \mu_n = \tau_n^2\Big(\frac{\mu_0}{\tau_0^2} + \frac{n\bar{x}}{\sigma^2}\Big).$$

**Precisions add**, so the posterior is always narrower than the prior, and the posterior mean is the precision-weighted average that last lesson found as the MAP estimate (for a Gaussian posterior the mean and mode coincide).

### What a posterior is not

A credible interval is a probability statement about the parameter given the data and the prior: $P(\theta \in [l, u] \mid \mathcal{D}) = 0.95$. A frequentist confidence interval (Lesson 11) makes a statement about the procedure over repeated samples. Posteriors depend on the prior, which for small $n$ matters; checking sensitivity to the prior is part of honest practice.

### In code

```python
def beta_update(alpha, beta, k, n):
    return alpha + k, beta + n - k

a, b = beta_update(2, 2, 7, 10)
mean = a / (a + b)
var = a * b / ((a + b) ** 2 * (a + b + 1))
print(a, b, round(mean, 4), round(var, 4))
```

@example
title: Updating a Beta prior
problem: A prior belief that a new feature converts about half of users is encoded as $\operatorname{Beta}(5, 5)$. In a trial, 2 of 12 users convert. Find the posterior, its mean and mode, and compare with the MLE.
step: Successes $k = 2$, failures $n - k = 10$. The posterior is $\operatorname{Beta}(5 + 2,\ 5 + 10) = \operatorname{Beta}(7, 15)$.
step: Posterior mean: $7/(7 + 15) = 7/22 \approx 0.318$.
step: Posterior mode: $(7 - 1)/(7 + 15 - 2) = 6/20 = 0.3$.
step: The MLE is $2/12 \approx 0.167$; the prior (effective sample size $5 + 5 = 10$) pulls the estimate up to $0.318$, a weighted average: $\tfrac{10}{22}(0.5) + \tfrac{12}{22}(0.167) \approx 0.318$.
answer: Posterior $\operatorname{Beta}(7, 15)$ with mean $\approx 0.318$ and mode $0.3$.

@example
title: Updating a Gamma prior for counts
problem: The number of server errors per hour is modeled as $\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)$ with prior $\lambda \sim \operatorname{Gamma}(2, 1)$ (shape 2, rate 1). Three hours show 4, 2 and 3 errors. Find the posterior, its mean and its variance.
step: The total count is $4 + 2 + 3 = 9$ and the number of observations is $n = 3$.
step: Update: shape $2 + 9 = 11$, rate $1 + 3 = 4$, so $\lambda \mid \mathcal{D} \sim \operatorname{Gamma}(11, 4)$.
step: Mean: $a/b = 11/4 = 2.75$ errors per hour (the prior mean was 2 and the sample mean $3$).
step: Variance: $a/b^2 = 11/16 = 0.6875$, a standard deviation of about $0.83$.
answer: $\operatorname{Gamma}(11, 4)$, mean $2.75$, variance $0.6875$.

@mistakes
- Confusing the posterior mean with the posterior mode: for $\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha', \beta')$ the mean is $\alpha'/(\alpha' + \beta')$, while the mode is $(\alpha' - 1)/(\alpha' + \beta' - 2)$; the MAP estimate is the mode.
- Mixing shape-rate and shape-scale Gamma conventions: with a rate $b$ the mean is $a/b$; with a scale $\theta$ it is $a\theta$.
- Averaging standard deviations instead of precisions in the Gaussian update: the posterior precision is the sum of the precisions $1/\tau_0^2$ and $n/\sigma^2$.
- Treating a credible interval as a confidence interval: the credible interval is a statement about the parameter given the data and the prior.

@quiz self
prompt: Let $x_1, \dots, x_n \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with $\sigma^2$ known and prior $\mu \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \tau_0^2)$. Show by completing the square that the posterior is Gaussian with precision $1/\tau_0^2 + n/\sigma^2$ and mean equal to the precision-weighted average of $\mu_0$ and $\bar x$.
hint: Write the log-posterior as a quadratic in $\mu$, $-\frac12 A\mu^2 + B\mu + \text{const}$, and recall that a Gaussian log-density has this form with precision $A$ and mean $B/A$.
solution: Up to terms independent of $\mu$, the log-posterior is $-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_i (x_i - \mu)^2 - \frac{1}{2\tau_0^2}(\mu - \mu_0)^2$. Expanding gives $-\frac12\big(\frac{n}{\sigma^2} + \frac{1}{\tau_0^2}\big)\mu^2 + \big(\frac{n\bar x}{\sigma^2} + \frac{\mu_0}{\tau_0^2}\big)\mu + \text{const}$, using $\sum_i x_i = n\bar x$. This has the form $-\frac12 A\mu^2 + B\mu$ with $A = n/\sigma^2 + 1/\tau_0^2$ and $B = n\bar x/\sigma^2 + \mu_0/\tau_0^2$. A Gaussian with precision $A$ and mean $m$ has log-density $-\frac12 A\mu^2 + Am\mu + \text{const}$, so the posterior is Gaussian with precision $A$ and mean $m = B/A$, which is $\frac{(1/\tau_0^2)\mu_0 + (n/\sigma^2)\bar x}{1/\tau_0^2 + n/\sigma^2}$, the precision-weighted average.
