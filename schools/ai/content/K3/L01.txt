@lesson
title: Multi-digit multiplication
summary: Multiply bigger numbers by breaking them into tens and ones, and check the answer with an estimate.
objective: Multiply a two-digit or three-digit number by a one-digit number.
objective: Multiply two two-digit numbers by multiplying in parts and adding.
objective: Estimate a product by rounding to check that an answer makes sense.
reading: Khan Academy: 5th grade math, look up "Multi-digit multiplication" (multiplying by one-digit and two-digit numbers).

@article
Multiplying means making equal groups. $4 \times 3$ is four groups of three. When the numbers get bigger, we break them into friendly parts and multiply each part.

### Break a number into tens and ones

Let us do $23 \times 4$. Think of $23$ as $20$ and $3$.

- $20 \times 4 = 80$
- $3 \times 4 = 12$
- Add the parts: $80 + 12 = 92$

So $23 \times 4 = 92$. Each small step is a fact you already know.

### Two-digit times two-digit

Now try $23 \times 14$. Break $14$ into $10$ and $4$. Multiply $23$ by each part, then add.

- $23 \times 10 = 230$
- $23 \times 4 = 92$
- $230 + 92 = 322$

You can draw this as a rectangle cut into two smaller rectangles. The area of the big rectangle is the sum of the two small ones.

### Multiply by tens

To multiply by $10$, move each digit one place to the left. That puts a zero on the end. So $23 \times 10 = 230$. To multiply by $30$, first multiply by $3$, then put a zero on the end: $23 \times 30 = 690$.

### Estimate to check

An **estimate** is a close guess made with easy numbers. Round each number, then multiply.

$23 \times 14$ is close to $20 \times 10 = 200$. Our answer, $322$, is a bit bigger than $200$ because we rounded both numbers down. That feels right. If you had got $3{,}220$ or $32$, the estimate would tell you to look again.

Say it out loud to your grown-up: "Break apart, multiply the parts, add, then estimate."

@example
title: Multiply 36 by 12
problem: What is $36 \times 12$? Then check with an estimate.
step: Break $12$ into $10$ and $2$.
step: Multiply by each part: $36 \times 10 = 360$ and $36 \times 2 = 72$.
step: Add the parts: $360 + 72 = 432$.
step: Estimate: $36$ is about $40$ and $12$ is about $10$. $40 \times 10 = 400$. Our answer $432$ is close to $400$, so it makes sense.

@mistakes
- Forgetting the zero when multiplying by ten, so $23 \times 10$ becomes $23$ or $203$.
- Multiplying only one part, for example finding $23 \times 10$ but forgetting $23 \times 4$.
- Skipping the estimate, so an answer that is far too big or too small is never noticed.

@practice num
prompt: What is $12 \times 4$?
answer: 48
check: 12 * 4
hint: Break $12$ into $10$ and $2$.
hint: Find $10 \times 4$ and $2 \times 4$, then add them.
solution: $10 \times 4 = 40$ and $2 \times 4 = 8$. Add: $40 + 8 = 48$.
