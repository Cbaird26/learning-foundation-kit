@lesson
title: Multiplying fractions
summary: Multiply fractions by drawing models and by multiplying the tops and the bottoms.
objective: Find a fraction of a whole number.
objective: Multiply two fractions by multiplying the numerators and the denominators.
objective: Draw a model to show a product of fractions.
reading: Khan Academy: 5th grade math, look up "Multiply fractions" (fractions of fractions and area models).

@article
The word **of** often means multiply. "Half of a pizza" is $\frac{1}{2} \times$ pizza.

### A fraction of a whole number

What is $\frac{2}{3}$ of $9$? Split $9$ into $3$ equal groups: that is $3$ in each group. Take $2$ groups: $2 \times 3 = 6$.

$$\frac{2}{3} \times 9 = 6$$

First divide by the denominator, then multiply by the numerator.

### A fraction of a fraction

Take a bar and shade $\frac{1}{2}$ of it. Now cut the shaded half into $3$ equal parts and keep $1$ of them. You have $\frac{1}{3}$ of $\frac{1}{2}$.

Cut the whole bar the same way and you will see $6$ equal pieces in all. Your part is $1$ piece, so

$$\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{6}.$$

### The rule

Multiply the numerators. Multiply the denominators.

$$\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{2 \times 3}{3 \times 4} = \frac{6}{12} = \frac{1}{2}$$

Always look to simplify at the end. $\frac{6}{12}$ divides by $6$ to give $\frac{1}{2}$.

### Draw an area model

Draw a square. Cut it into $3$ equal columns and $4$ equal rows. That makes $12$ small boxes. Shade $2$ columns, then shade $3$ rows. The boxes shaded both ways are the product. Here that is $6$ boxes out of $12$.

### Does it make sense?

When you multiply by a fraction smaller than $1$, the answer is smaller than what you started with. Half of a number is less than the number. If your product is bigger, check again.

Tell your grown-up one thing: "To multiply fractions, tops times tops and bottoms times bottoms."

@example
title: Multiply two fractions
problem: What is $\frac{2}{5} \times \frac{3}{4}$?
step: Multiply the numerators: $2 \times 3 = 6$.
step: Multiply the denominators: $5 \times 4 = 20$. So the product is $\frac{6}{20}$.
step: Simplify. Both $6$ and $20$ divide by $2$, so $\frac{6}{20} = \frac{3}{10}$.
step: Check: we took a part of $\frac{2}{5}$, so the answer should be smaller than $\frac{2}{5}$. Since $\frac{3}{10}$ is less than $\frac{4}{10}$, it is.

@mistakes
- Finding a common denominator before multiplying. You do not need one for multiplication.
- Multiplying the numerators but adding the denominators.
- Forgetting to simplify the answer.

@practice num
prompt: What is $\frac{2}{3}$ of $9$?
answer: 6
check: 2/3 * 9
hint: First split $9$ into $3$ equal groups.
hint: Each group has $3$. Now take $2$ groups.
solution: $9 \div 3 = 3$ and $2 \times 3 = 6$. So $\frac{2}{3}$ of $9$ is $6$.

@practice num
prompt: What is $\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{5}$? Simplify and enter a fraction.
answer: 3/10
check: 3/4 * 2/5
hint: Multiply the tops, then the bottoms.
hint: You get $\frac{6}{20}$. Divide the top and bottom by $2$.
solution: $\frac{3 \times 2}{4 \times 5} = \frac{6}{20} = \frac{3}{10}$.

@practice self
prompt: Draw a rectangle. Show $\frac{1}{2}$ of $\frac{1}{4}$ by cutting the rectangle into $4$ equal parts and splitting one of them in half. Say the answer out loud.
hint: Cut the rectangle into $4$ equal parts and look at just one of them.
solution: Cut the rectangle into $4$ equal parts. One part is $\frac{1}{4}$. Split that part into $2$ equal halves. One of those pieces is $\frac{1}{8}$ of the whole rectangle, so $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{8}$.
rubric: The rectangle is cut into $4$ equal parts.
rubric: One quarter is split in half.
rubric: The answer $\frac{1}{8}$ is named and matches the picture.
