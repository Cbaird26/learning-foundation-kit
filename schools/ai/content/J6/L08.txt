@article
**Energy** is the ability to cause change or do work. It is measured in **joules (J)**. Energy comes in many forms, including motion, height, heat, light, sound, chemical and electrical energy.

### Kinetic energy

**Kinetic energy** is energy of motion. It depends on mass and speed:

$$KE = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$$

with mass $m$ in kg and speed $v$ in m/s. A $2\ \mathrm{kg}$ ball moving at $3\ \mathrm{m/s}$ has $KE = \tfrac12(2)(3)^2 = 9\ \mathrm{J}$. Because speed is squared, **doubling the speed quadruples the kinetic energy**. This is why high-speed crashes are so much more dangerous than low-speed ones.

### Gravitational potential energy

**Potential energy** is stored energy. **Gravitational potential energy** depends on weight and height above a reference level:

$$PE = mgh$$

where $g = 9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $h$ is height in meters. A $0.5\ \mathrm{kg}$ book held $4\ \mathrm{m}$ above the floor has $PE = 0.5 \times 9.8 \times 4 = 19.6\ \mathrm{J}$.

### Conservation of energy

Energy is never created or destroyed. It only changes form. This is the **law of conservation of energy**. When an object falls, potential energy turns into kinetic energy. If we ignore air resistance, the total stays the same:

$$mgh = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v = \sqrt{2gh}.$$

The mass cancels, so all objects dropped from the same height (without air resistance) reach the ground at the same speed. In real life, some energy turns into heat and sound because of friction and collisions.

### Explaining a crash test

A crash-test car moving at speed $v$ has kinetic energy. When it hits a barrier the energy must go somewhere:

- **Newton's first law:** the car stops, but a dummy that is not restrained keeps moving forward because of its inertia.
- **Newton's second law:** the force on the dummy depends on how quickly it slows down. Airbags and crumple zones make the stopping time longer, so the acceleration and force are smaller.
- **Newton's third law:** the car pushes on the barrier and the barrier pushes back on the car with equal force.
- **Energy:** the kinetic energy is turned into work that bends metal, plus heat and sound.

@example
title: Potential to kinetic energy
problem: A $3.0\ \mathrm{kg}$ toy is held $5.0\ \mathrm{m}$ above the ground. What is its potential energy? How fast is it moving just before it hits the ground? Ignore air resistance and use $g = 9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.
step: $PE = mgh = 3.0 \times 9.8 \times 5.0 = 147\ \mathrm{J}$.
step: At the bottom, all the potential energy has become kinetic energy: $\tfrac12 m v^2 = 147$.
step: $v^2 = 2 \times 147 \div 3.0 = 98$, so $v = \sqrt{98} \approx 9.9\ \mathrm{m/s}$.
step: Check with $v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.8 \times 5.0} = \sqrt{98}$. The mass cancels.

@mistakes
- Forgetting to square the speed in $KE = \tfrac12 mv^2$.
- Using grams instead of kilograms. Convert first.
- Saying that energy is "used up". It changes form, often into heat.
- Thinking a heavier object always falls faster. Without air resistance, all fall at the same rate.

@practice self
prompt: A crash-test car hits a solid barrier while a crash-test dummy sits inside. Use Newton's three laws to explain what happens to the car and the dummy, and explain how seat belts and airbags reduce injury.
solution: First law: the car is stopped by the barrier, but the dummy has inertia and keeps moving forward until a force acts on it. Second law: the force on the dummy equals its mass times its acceleration, and acceleration is the change in velocity divided by the stopping time. Seat belts and airbags make the dummy's stopping time longer, so the acceleration and the force are smaller. Third law: the car pushes on the barrier and the barrier pushes back on the car with a force of equal size and opposite direction; the dummy and the belt also exert equal and opposite forces on each other. The car's kinetic energy is converted into work that crumples the car, plus heat and sound.
hint: Use one law at a time: what keeps the dummy moving, what controls the force, and which forces come in pairs.
rubric: Uses the first law (inertia) to explain why the dummy keeps moving.
rubric: Uses $F = ma$ and stopping time to explain how airbags and belts reduce force.
rubric: Identifies a third-law pair of equal and opposite forces on different objects.
rubric: Mentions that kinetic energy changes into other forms such as deformation, heat or sound.
