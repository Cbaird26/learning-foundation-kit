@article
Two classifiers separate the same training data perfectly. One passes within a hair of a training point, the other keeps a wide gap on both sides. Which would you trust? Margin theory says the second, and makes the intuition quantitative: the generalization guarantee depends on the margin and on the size of the data, **not on the dimension**. That is why linear separators in spaces of enormous dimension, including kernel feature spaces, can generalize.

### Margins

For a hyperplane $\{\mathbf{x} : \langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x} \rangle + b = 0\}$ and a labeled point $(\mathbf{x}, y)$, $y \in \{-1, +1\}$, the **geometric margin** of the point is $y(\langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x} \rangle + b)/\lVert \mathbf{w} \rVert$: the signed distance from the hyperplane, positive when the point is correctly classified. The margin of the hyperplane on a sample is the minimum over points. For example, with $\mathbf{w} = (3, 4)$, $b = -5$ and the point $(4, 3)$ labeled $+1$, the margin is $(12 + 12 - 5)/5 = 3.8$. Rescaling $(\mathbf{w}, b)$ does not change it.

### The perceptron

The perceptron (Rosenblatt, 1958) keeps $\mathbf{w}$, starts at $\mathbf{0}$, and cycles through the data: whenever a point has $y\langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x} \rangle \le 0$ it makes a mistake and $\mathbf{w} \leftarrow \mathbf{w} + y\mathbf{x}$.

**Theorem (Novikoff, 1962).** Suppose $\lVert \mathbf{x}_i \rVert \le R$ for all $i$ and there is a unit vector $\mathbf{w}^\star$ with $y_i\langle \mathbf{w}^\star, \mathbf{x}_i \rangle \ge \gamma > 0$ for all $i$. Then the perceptron makes at most $(R/\gamma)^2$ mistakes in total, however many passes it makes.

*Proof.* Let $\mathbf{w}_t$ be the weight vector after $t$ mistakes, so $\mathbf{w}_0 = \mathbf{0}$. A mistake on $(\mathbf{x}, y)$ gives $\langle \mathbf{w}_{t+1}, \mathbf{w}^\star \rangle = \langle \mathbf{w}_t, \mathbf{w}^\star \rangle + y\langle \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{w}^\star \rangle \ge \langle \mathbf{w}_t, \mathbf{w}^\star \rangle + \gamma$, and $\lVert \mathbf{w}_{t+1} \rVert^2 = \lVert \mathbf{w}_t \rVert^2 + 2y\langle \mathbf{w}_t, \mathbf{x} \rangle + \lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert^2 \le \lVert \mathbf{w}_t \rVert^2 + R^2$, because $y\langle \mathbf{w}_t, \mathbf{x} \rangle \le 0$ on a mistake. By induction, $t\gamma \le \langle \mathbf{w}_t, \mathbf{w}^\star \rangle \le \lVert \mathbf{w}_t \rVert \le R\sqrt{t}$ (Cauchy–Schwarz and $\lVert \mathbf{w}^\star \rVert = 1$). Hence $\sqrt{t} \le R/\gamma$. $\square$

The mistake bound is itself dimension-free and depends on the data only through $R/\gamma$. It is a statement about the training sequence; the online-to-batch argument of week 10 turns mistake bounds into generalization guarantees.

### Support vector machines

Among all separating hyperplanes the **hard-margin SVM** (the support vector machine of Cortes and Vapnik, 1995, in its separable form) chooses the one with the largest margin. Rescale so that $\min_i y_i(\langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x}_i \rangle + b) = 1$; then the margin is $1/\lVert \mathbf{w} \rVert$, and maximizing it is
$$\min_{\mathbf{w}, b}\ \tfrac{1}{2}\lVert \mathbf{w} \rVert^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad y_i(\langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x}_i \rangle + b) \ge 1,\ i = 1, \dots, m.$$
This is a convex quadratic program; the points where a constraint holds with equality are the **support vectors**, and the solution is a combination of them. The soft-margin version, which allows violations at a price, is regularized loss minimization with the hinge loss, exactly the setting of week 7.

### A margin generalization bound

For $\gamma > 0$, the **ramp loss** $\Phi_\gamma(t)$ equals $1$ for $t \le 0$, $1 - t/\gamma$ for $0 < t < \gamma$, and $0$ for $t \ge \gamma$; it is $(1/\gamma)$-Lipschitz and satisfies $\mathbb{1}[t \le 0] \le \Phi_\gamma(t) \le \mathbb{1}[t < \gamma]$. Talagrand's contraction lemma states that for an $\ell$-Lipschitz $\Phi$ and any class $\mathcal{F}$ of real functions, $R_S(\Phi \circ \mathcal{F}) \le \ell\, R_S(\mathcal{F})$ in the convention of week 5; we use it without proof.

**Theorem (margin bound).** Let $\mathcal{H} = \{\mathbf{x} \mapsto \langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x} \rangle : \lVert \mathbf{w} \rVert_2 \le B\}$, let $\mathcal{D}$ be a distribution on $\{\mathbf{x} : \lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert \le \rho\} \times \{-1, +1\}$, and fix $\gamma > 0$ and $\delta \in (0, 1)$. With probability at least $1 - \delta$ over $S \sim \mathcal{D}^m$, for all $h \in \mathcal{H}$,
$$P_{(\mathbf{x}, y)}[y h(\mathbf{x}) \le 0] \le \frac{1}{m}\big|\{i : y_i h(\mathbf{x}_i) < \gamma\}\big| + \frac{2B\rho}{\gamma\sqrt{m}} + \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta)}{2m}} .$$
The right side contains the fraction of training points with margin below $\gamma$ (the empirical margin loss), the Rademacher term and the confidence term. The bound holds for a $\gamma$ and $B$ fixed in advance; a union bound over a grid of values makes it hold for all of them at a small extra cost, and bounds of this kind are due to Bartlett (1998), Schapire, Freund, Bartlett and Lee (1998) and Koltchinskii and Panchenko (2002). With $B = 1$ and unnormalized margins it says: large margin relative to $\rho$ means small complexity.

What this does and does not explain: it explains why a large-margin separator in a very high-dimensional space has a controlled gap, since $d$ does not appear. It is a worst-case bound over distributions with the given norm bounds, its constants are visible (a margin ratio $\rho/\gamma$ of 5 and $m = 10^4$ give a complexity term of $0.1$), and it does not say that maximizing the margin is the only way to generalize.

@example
title: A hard-margin SVM by hand
problem: The data are $\mathbf{x}_1 = (3, 1)$ with $y_1 = +1$ and $\mathbf{x}_2 = (1, -1)$ with $y_2 = -1$. Find the hard-margin SVM solution $(\mathbf{w}, b)$ and its geometric margin.
step: Add the two constraints $\langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x}_1 \rangle + b \ge 1$ and $-(\langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x}_2 \rangle + b) \ge 1$: this gives $\langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_2 \rangle \ge 2$. With $\mathbf{d} = \mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_2 = (2, 2)$ and Cauchy–Schwarz, $2 \le \langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{d} \rangle \le \lVert \mathbf{w} \rVert\lVert \mathbf{d} \rVert$, so $\lVert \mathbf{w} \rVert \ge 2/\lVert \mathbf{d} \rVert = 2/\sqrt{8}$.
step: Equality holds when $\mathbf{w}$ is parallel to $\mathbf{d}$ with $\langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{d} \rangle = 2$, that is $\mathbf{w} = 2\mathbf{d}/\lVert \mathbf{d} \rVert^2 = (0.5, 0.5)$.
step: Choose $b$ so that both constraints hold with equality: $\langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x}_1 \rangle + b = 2 + b = 1$ gives $b = -1$, and then $-(\langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x}_2 \rangle + b) = -(0 - 1) = 1$ as required. So $\mathbf{w} = (0.5, 0.5)$, $b = -1$, and the hyperplane is $x_1 + x_2 = 2$, the perpendicular bisector of the two points.
step: The geometric margin is $1/\lVert \mathbf{w} \rVert = 1/\sqrt{0.5} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$, which equals half the distance $\lVert \mathbf{d} \rVert/2 = \sqrt{8}/2$ between the points.
answer: $\mathbf{w} = (0.5, 0.5)$, $b = -1$, margin $\sqrt{2}$.

@example
title: Proof of the margin bound
problem: Prove the margin generalization bound for $\mathcal{H} = \{\mathbf{x} \mapsto \langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x} \rangle : \lVert \mathbf{w} \rVert \le B\}$.
step: Let $\mathcal{G} = \{(\mathbf{x}, y) \mapsto \Phi_\gamma(y h(\mathbf{x})) : h \in \mathcal{H}\}$, a class of functions with values in $[0, 1]$. Theorem 1 of week 5 gives, with probability at least $1 - \delta$, for all $g \in \mathcal{G}$: $\mathbb{E}g \le \hat{\mathbb{E}}_S g + 2R_m(\mathcal{G}) + \sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)/(2m)}$.
step: Sandwich the ramp loss. Since $\mathbb{1}[y h(\mathbf{x}) \le 0] \le \Phi_\gamma(y h(\mathbf{x}))$, the left side of the theorem is at most $\mathbb{E}g$; since $\Phi_\gamma(t) \le \mathbb{1}[t < \gamma]$, the empirical term $\hat{\mathbb{E}}_S g$ is at most the fraction of points with $y_i h(\mathbf{x}_i) < \gamma$.
step: Bound the complexity. The contraction lemma with the $(1/\gamma)$-Lipschitz function $\Phi_\gamma$ gives $R_S(\mathcal{G}) \le \frac{1}{\gamma}R_S(y\mathcal{H})$, where $y\mathcal{H}$ is the class $(\mathbf{x}, y) \mapsto y h(\mathbf{x})$. Since $(\sigma_i y_i)_i$ has the same distribution as $(\sigma_i)_i$ for fixed labels, $R_S(y\mathcal{H}) = R_{S_{\mathcal{X}}}(\mathcal{H})$.
step: Theorem 2 of week 5 gives $R_{S_{\mathcal{X}}}(\mathcal{H}) \le B\rho/\sqrt{m}$ for every sample. Taking expectations, $R_m(\mathcal{G}) \le B\rho/(\gamma\sqrt{m})$, so the term $2R_m(\mathcal{G})$ is at most $2B\rho/(\gamma\sqrt{m})$, which completes the proof.
answer: $P[y h(\mathbf{x}) \le 0] \le \hat{L}^{\gamma}_S(h) + 2B\rho/(\gamma\sqrt{m}) + \sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)/(2m)}$ for all $h \in \mathcal{H}$ with probability at least $1 - \delta$.

@mistakes
- Using the functional margin $y(\langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x} \rangle + b)$ without dividing by $\lVert \mathbf{w} \rVert$: it can be made arbitrarily large by rescaling and carries no geometric meaning.
- Reading the perceptron bound $(R/\gamma)^2$ as depending on the dimension or on the number of examples; it depends on neither, and it counts mistakes during training, not test errors.
- Applying the margin bound with a margin $\gamma$ and norm bound $B$ chosen after looking at the data: as stated they must be fixed in advance, and a union bound over a grid is needed otherwise.

@practice code
prompt: Write `perceptron(data)` that runs the perceptron on a list of `((x1, x2), y)` pairs with labels $y \in \{-1, +1\}$ and no bias term. Start from `w = [0.0, 0.0]`, cycle through the examples in the given order, and whenever `y * (w[0]*x1 + w[1]*x2) <= 0` set `w = [w[0] + y*x1, w[1] + y*x2]` and count a mistake. Stop after a complete pass with no mistake, and return the pair `(w, mistakes)`. The data are guaranteed to be linearly separable through the origin. For example `perceptron([((2, 1), 1), ((-2, 0), -1)])` returns `([2.0, 1.0], 1)`.

def perceptron(data):

model:
```python
def perceptron(data):
    """Perceptron without bias; returns the final weights and mistake count."""
    w = [0.0, 0.0]
    mistakes = 0
    changed = True
    while changed:
        changed = False
        for (x1, x2), y in data:
            if y * (w[0] * x1 + w[1] * x2) <= 0:
                w = [w[0] + y * x1, w[1] + y * x2]
                mistakes += 1
                changed = True
    return w, mistakes
```
hint: Use an outer loop that repeats while the last pass changed `w`, and an inner loop over the examples in order; count a mistake whenever the margin is at most zero, including the first step from the zero vector.
solution: The condition `<= 0` matters: at $\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{0}$ every point has $y\langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x} \rangle = 0$ and counts as a mistake, so a strict inequality would never update. By the Novikoff theorem the loop terminates on separable data, after at most $(R/\gamma)^2$ mistakes in total; for the last data set $R = 4$ and the best margin is $\gamma \approx 0.447$ (with $\mathbf{w}^\star = (1, -2)/\sqrt{5}$), giving the bound $80$, far above the $5$ mistakes actually made.

@practice self
prompt: Let $\mathcal{H} = \{\mathbf{x} \mapsto \langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{x} \rangle : \lVert \mathbf{w} \rVert_1 \le B\}$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$ and let $\mathcal{D}$ be supported on $\{\lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert_\infty \le \rho\} \times \{-1, +1\}$. Derive a margin bound of the form: with probability at least $1 - \delta$, for all $h \in \mathcal{H}$, $P[y h(\mathbf{x}) \le 0] \le \frac{1}{m}|\{i : y_i h(\mathbf{x}_i) < \gamma\}| + (\text{complexity term}) + \sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)/(2m)}$, and give the complexity term. Use the ramp loss, the contraction lemma and the Rademacher bound $R_S \le B\rho\sqrt{2\ln(2d)/m}$ for this class.
hint: Follow the proof of the margin bound for the $\ell_2$ class and replace only the bound on $R_S(\mathcal{H})$.
solution: Let $\mathcal{G} = \{(\mathbf{x}, y) \mapsto \Phi_\gamma(y h(\mathbf{x}))\}$ with values in $[0,1]$. By Theorem 1 of week 5, with probability at least $1 - \delta$, for all $h$: $\mathbb{E}\Phi_\gamma(y h(\mathbf{x})) \le \hat{\mathbb{E}}_S\Phi_\gamma(y h(\mathbf{x})) + 2R_m(\mathcal{G}) + \sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)/(2m)}$. Since $\mathbb{1}[t \le 0] \le \Phi_\gamma(t) \le \mathbb{1}[t < \gamma]$, the left side upper bounds $P[y h(\mathbf{x}) \le 0]$ and the empirical term is at most the fraction of points with margin below $\gamma$. By the contraction lemma applied to the $(1/\gamma)$-Lipschitz function $\Phi_\gamma$, $R_S(\mathcal{G}) \le \frac{1}{\gamma}R_S(y\mathcal{H})$, and $R_S(y\mathcal{H}) = R_{S_{\mathcal{X}}}(\mathcal{H})$ because $(\sigma_i y_i)$ has the law of $(\sigma_i)$. The Rademacher bound for the $\ell_1$ class gives $R_{S_{\mathcal{X}}}(\mathcal{H}) \le B\rho\sqrt{2\ln(2d)/m}$, so $2R_m(\mathcal{G}) \le \frac{2B\rho}{\gamma}\sqrt{\frac{2\ln(2d)}{m}}$. The complexity term is therefore $\frac{2B\rho}{\gamma}\sqrt{2\ln(2d)/m}$: the dimension enters only through $\sqrt{\ln(2d)}$ instead of not at all, in exchange for an $\ell_1$ constraint that favors sparse weights.
