## Week-by-week review

**Week 1, the workflow and tables.** Ask, acquire, wrangle, explore, model, communicate. A table has rows (observations) and columns (variables); the shape is (rows, columns). Tidy data: one variable per column, one observation per row. Always state the unit of observation.

**Week 2, reading files.** CSV is text with quoting rules; every cell is a string until you convert it. JSON nests; flatten with dotted keys. Keep identifiers such as ZIP codes as strings. Report shape and column types after every load.

**Week 3, cleaning.** Map missing markers to `None`, strip and normalize labels, remove thousands separators, drop duplicates by a deliberately chosen key. Mean imputation keeps the mean but shrinks the variance. Clean with functions that you can test and rerun.

**Week 4, filtering, sorting, grouping.** Predicate filters; stable multi-key sorts (least important key first); split-apply-combine. The overall mean is the size-weighted mean of the group means.

**Week 5, joins and reshaping.** Inner and left joins; a key with $a$ and $b$ copies gives $a \times b$ rows; check row counts and key types. Melt wide tables to long; pivot long to wide with an aggregation.

**Week 6, summaries.** Mean, median, mode; sample variance divides by $n - 1$; quantiles by interpolation, $h = (n-1)q$; IQR; histograms with half-open bins; Tukey fences at 1.5 IQR beyond the quartiles.

**Week 7, exploration.** Checklist from shape to anomalies. Pearson $r$ measures linear association only. Cross-tabulations; Simpson's paradox arises from different group mixes.

**Week 8, charts.** Match the chart to the variable types; bars start at zero; lie factor = effect in graphic / effect in data; read a chart with a number.

**Week 9, features.** Standardize $z = (x - \bar{x})/\sigma$; min-max scaling; one-hot for nominal categories; logs, ratios, bins, missing indicators. Fit on training data only.

**Week 10, bias and leakage.** Selection, nonresponse and survivorship bias; inverse probability weights; target leakage, duplicate contamination, preprocessing leakage, temporal leakage; random, group and time splits.

**Week 11, documentation.** Data dictionary (name, type, unit, allowed values, missing code); profile and validate; datasheet sections (motivation, composition, collection, preprocessing, uses, distribution, maintenance); missing rate by group.

**Week 12, privacy.** Direct identifiers, quasi-identifiers, sensitive attributes; pseudonymization is not anonymization; k-anonymity by generalization and suppression; Laplace scale $b = \Delta/\varepsilon$; minimization and purpose limitation.

### Common errors

- Splitting on commas without handling quotes; converting identifiers to numbers.
- Unweighted averages of group means; a join that silently changes the row count.
- Dividing by $n$ instead of $n - 1$ for a sample variance, or the reverse for the scaler convention you chose.
- Fitting scalers on all the data; features that know the answer; the same entity on both sides of a split.
- Calling pseudonymized data anonymous.

    {"t": "Urgent ", "v": "1,000"}, {"t": "urgent", "v": "500"},

model:
```python
MISSING = {"", "na", "n/a", "?"}


def group_means(rows, group, col):
    """Per-group count and mean of messy string values."""
    sums, counts = {}, {}
    for row in rows:
        g = row[group].strip().lower()
        sums.setdefault(g, 0.0)
        counts.setdefault(g, 0)
        text = row[col].strip()
        if text.lower() in MISSING:
            continue
        sums[g] += float(text.replace(",", ""))
        counts[g] += 1
    return {g: (counts[g], sums[g] / counts[g] if counts[g] else None) for g in sums}
```
hint: Normalize the group label first, skip missing markers, remove commas before `float`, and divide by the number of rows that had a value.
solution: This combines weeks 3, 4 and 6: cleaning the label and the number, splitting by group, and averaging only the present values. The group `x` has no valid values, so its mean is `None` rather than a division by zero, and the mean divides by the count of valid rows, not by the group's total number of rows.

answer: 2.236
tol: 0.001
check: (50 - 25) / (sum((x - 25) ** 2 for x in [10, 20, 30, 40]) / 4) ** 0.5
hint: The training mean is 25, and the squared deviations from it are 225, 25, 25 and 225.
solution: The training mean is 25 and the population variance is $500/4 = 125$, so $\sigma = \sqrt{125} \approx 11.180$. The z-score of 50 is $(50 - 25)/11.180 \approx 2.236$. Using the test value's own statistics would be meaningless for a single value, and using all five values would leak the test value into the scaling.

prompt: A company wants a model to predict, when a support ticket is opened, whether it will be escalated. It has a table of 50,000 past tickets from the last three years, with columns `ticket_id`, `customer_id`, `opened_at`, `channel`, `message_length`, `first_response_minutes`, `resolution_notes` and `escalated`. Customers often open several tickets, 15% of `message_length` values are missing, and the company also wants to share the table with a university group. Describe how you would prepare the data and evaluate a model: name at least three problems (leakage, bias, contamination or privacy) and how you would handle each, and describe the split.
hint: Ask which columns exist at the time the ticket is opened, whether customers repeat, how time matters, who is missing, and what is personal.
solution: Leakage: `resolution_notes` is written after the ticket is handled, and `first_response_minutes` is known only after the response, so both must be excluded; the features are limited to what exists when the ticket is opened (`channel`, `message_length`, the time of day from `opened_at`). Contamination: the same customer opens several tickets, so I would split by `customer_id` and count the customers that appear on both sides, requiring zero; I would also use a time split (train on the first two years, test on the last year) because the model will predict future tickets and the process may change. Missing data: 15% of `message_length` is missing, so I would report the missing rate by channel, impute with the training median, add a missing indicator and check that dropping rows would not remove one channel disproportionately. All scalers, imputation values and category lists are fitted on the training rows only. Privacy: before sharing, remove `customer_id` or replace it by a random code kept apart from the data, coarsen `opened_at` to the day or the week, and check that the quasi-identifiers do not leave small groups, suppressing rows in groups smaller than a chosen $k$; I would also use the data only for the stated purpose and write a datasheet that records these choices. The test set is used once, at the end.
