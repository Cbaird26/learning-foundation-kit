@review
**Weeks 1–2: derivatives.** The gradient $\nabla f$ stacks partial derivatives and points uphill; the Jacobian of $\mathbf{g}:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m$ is $m\times n$; the Hessian is the symmetric matrix of second derivatives. Taylor: $f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{d}) \approx f + \nabla f^\top\mathbf{d} + \tfrac12\mathbf{d}^\top\mathbf{H}\mathbf{d}$. Chain rule: $\mathbf{J}_{h\circ g} = \mathbf{J}_h\mathbf{J}_g$, and for a scalar loss $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{J}_g^\top\nabla h$. Loss gradients: squared $(z-y)\mathbf{x}$; logistic $-y\sigma(-yz)\mathbf{x}$; softmax cross-entropy $\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{e}_k$; L2 $\lambda\mathbf{w}$.

**Weeks 3–4: convexity and optimality.** Convex: chord above graph; first order $f(\mathbf{y}) \ge f(\mathbf{x}) + \nabla f^\top(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x})$; second order $\mathbf{H}\succeq 0$ everywhere. Stationary: $\nabla f = \mathbf{0}$. Second-order sufficient: $\mathbf{H}\succ 0$. For convex $f$, stationary means global minimum. Quadratic $\tfrac12\mathbf{x}^\top\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}^\top\mathbf{x}$ has minimizer $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$.

**Weeks 5–6: gradient descent.** $\mathbf{x}\leftarrow\mathbf{x}-\eta\nabla f$. On a quadratic each eigen-direction shrinks by $1-\eta\lambda$, so convergence needs $\eta<2/\lambda_{\max}$; the best step $2/(L+\mu)$ gives the factor $(\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1)$. With $\eta=1/L$: convex and $L$-smooth gives $f_k-f^\star\le L\lVert\mathbf{x}_0-\mathbf{x}^\star\rVert_2^2/(2k)$; $\mu$-strongly convex adds the linear rate $(1-\mu/L)^k$. Backtracking uses the Armijo condition.

**Weeks 7–8: stochastic and momentum.** SGD uses unbiased noisy gradients; mini-batch variance is $\sigma^2/B$; constant steps stall at a noise floor $\propto\eta\sigma^2/B$; Robbins–Monro: $\sum\eta_k=\infty$, $\sum\eta_k^2<\infty$. Heavy ball adds $\beta(\mathbf{x}_k-\mathbf{x}_{k-1})$; stable iff $\eta\lambda<2(1+\beta)$; tuned, it needs about $\sqrt\kappa$ rather than $\kappa$ steps. Nesterov evaluates the gradient at a look-ahead point and gets $O(1/k^2)$ on convex smooth functions.

**Weeks 9–10: adaptive and second-order.** AdaGrad divides by $\sqrt{\sum g^2}$; RMSProp uses a moving average; Adam combines momentum and RMS scaling with bias correction $1-\beta^k$. Newton: $\mathbf{x}\leftarrow\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{H}^{-1}\nabla f$, exact on quadratics, locally quadratically convergent when $\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{x}^\star)\succ0$ and $\mathbf{H}$ is Lipschitz. BFGS satisfies the secant equation $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{s}=\mathbf{y}$; L-BFGS stores few pairs.

**Weeks 11–12: constraints, duality, non-convexity.** Lagrangian $f+\sum\mu_ig_i+\sum\lambda_jh_j$. KKT: stationarity, primal feasibility, $\mu\ge0$, complementary slackness $\mu_ig_i=0$; necessary under a constraint qualification, sufficient for convex problems. Dual $d=\inf_{\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{L}$ is concave; weak duality $d^\star\le p^\star$; strong duality for convex problems with Slater's condition. Strict saddles have a negative Hessian eigenvalue; gradient descent with $\eta<1/L$ avoids them with probability one.

**Self-check.** Can you (1) derive a gradient and verify it by finite differences; (2) prove or refute convexity of a function; (3) state the step-size limit of gradient descent on a given quadratic; (4) compute a rate and a step count from $L$ and $\mu$; (5) run Adam and Newton steps by hand; (6) solve a small KKT system by cases; (7) form a dual function; (8) explain what a small gradient does and does not tell you about a non-convex loss?

**Common errors.** Using $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{w}$ instead of $(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A}^\top)\mathbf{w}$; forgetting the transpose in the chain rule; claiming stationary means minimum without convexity; stepping above $2/L$; mixing up $O(1/k)$ and linear rates; skipping bias correction in Adam; forgetting $\mu_i\ge0$; treating weak duality as strong duality.

model:
```python
import math

def logistic_batch_grad(w, b, data, batch):
    """Mean logistic-loss gradient over the examples listed in batch."""
    gw = [0.0, 0.0]
    gb = 0.0
    for i in batch:
        (x1, x2), y = data[i]
        z = w[0] * x1 + w[1] * x2 + b
        p = 1.0 / (1.0 + math.exp(-z))
        r = p - y
        gw[0] += r * x1
        gw[1] += r * x2
        gb += r
    n = len(batch)
    return [gw[0] / n, gw[1] / n], gb / n
```
hint: For each index compute the score, the predicted probability $p$, and the residual $p - y$; accumulate $r\,x_1$, $r\,x_2$ and $r$, then divide by the batch size.
solution: The gradient of the cross-entropy with respect to the score is $p - y$, so the weight gradient is $(p - y)\mathbf{x}$ and the bias gradient is $p - y$, averaged over the batch with the batch length (counting repeated indices each time) as the divisor. The last tests catch a version that divides by the data set size instead of the batch size, or forgets the bias.

@exam self
prompt: You must minimize a smooth training loss $f(\mathbf{w}) = \frac1n\sum_i f_i(\mathbf{w})$ over a million examples and two million parameters. Gradient descent with a fixed step crawls and zigzags. Using the ideas of the course, explain (a) what is probably wrong, in terms of the Hessian, (b) how momentum or Adam each address it, with the relevant rate or mechanism, (c) why Newton's method is not the answer here, and what a quasi-Newton method changes. Finish with the experiment you would run to decide between your candidates.
hint: Think of the condition number $\kappa$, the $\sqrt\kappa$ speedup of tuned momentum, per-coordinate scaling, and the memory cost of the Hessian.
solution: (a) The Hessian is probably ill-conditioned: the loss curves sharply in some directions and gently in others, so the step size is limited by $2/L$ (the sharpest curvature) while progress in the flat directions is slow, with a rate near $(\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1)$; the zigzag is the oscillation across the steep directions. (b) Momentum averages consecutive gradients, cancelling the alternating components and accumulating the consistent ones; on quadratics, tuned heavy ball or Nesterov reduces the iteration count from order $\kappa$ to order $\sqrt\kappa$. Adam and RMSProp rescale each coordinate by a running root-mean-square of its gradient, which helps when the badly scaled directions align with coordinates, and Adam adds momentum with bias correction; neither is guaranteed to converge on all convex problems. (c) Newton's method would need to form and solve with a $2\times10^6$ by $2\times10^6$ Hessian, $4\times10^{12}$ entries and $O(n^3)$ work, which is impossible, and in a non-convex loss the Hessian is indefinite, so the Newton step may be uphill. A quasi-Newton method such as L-BFGS builds a curvature estimate from the last $m$ gradient differences with $O(mn)$ memory, but it assumes accurate gradients and so is awkward with noisy mini-batches. Experiment: compare SGD with momentum and Adam, each with step sizes on a grid spaced by factors of 3, three seeds each, on training loss against gradient evaluations, plus the final validation loss; use a decaying schedule for SGD; and check conditioning by estimating the top Hessian eigenvalue with power iteration on Hessian-vector products.
