@article
After World War II the United States and the Soviet Union emerged as the two superpowers. They had been allies against Germany, but they disagreed about Eastern Europe, the future of Germany and political and economic systems. The U.S. system combined democracy and capitalism; the Soviet system was a one-party communist state with a planned economy. The **Cold War** was a long rivalry without direct large-scale war between the two powers.

### Containment

U.S. policy was **containment**: preventing the spread of communism without a direct war. Its main tools were these.

- The **Truman Doctrine** (1947) offered aid to Greece and Turkey.
- The **Marshall Plan** (1948) gave billions of dollars to rebuild Western Europe.
- The **Berlin Airlift** (1948 to 1949) supplied West Berlin by air during a Soviet blockade.
- **NATO** (1949) was a mutual defense alliance; the Soviet bloc formed the Warsaw Pact in 1955.

The Soviet Union tested an atomic bomb in 1949, and a nuclear arms race followed. Communists won in China in 1949.

### Wars in Asia

In the **Korean War** (1950 to 1953), North Korea invaded South Korea; the United States led a United Nations force, and China later entered the war. An armistice left Korea divided. In Vietnam, the United States supported South Vietnam against the communist North. After the Gulf of Tonkin incident in 1964, Congress passed the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution, and U.S. troop numbers grew into the hundreds of thousands. The war became deeply controversial. Supporters argued that it was needed to resist communism; opponents argued that it was a civil war that the United States could not win, or that it was immoral. Protest and public opinion shaped politics. The 1973 Paris Peace Accords ended direct U.S. involvement, Congress passed the War Powers Resolution (1973) to limit presidential war-making, and South Vietnam fell in 1975. About 58,000 Americans died, along with millions of Vietnamese.

### Crises

The Soviet launch of *Sputnik* in 1957 spurred U.S. investment in science education and space exploration. The Berlin Wall was built in 1961. In the **Cuban Missile Crisis** (October 1962) the United States and the Soviet Union came close to nuclear war; the crisis ended with a public Soviet agreement to remove missiles and a private U.S. commitment not to invade Cuba and to remove missiles from Turkey. Arms control agreements followed in the 1970s.

### The postwar order at home

The postwar economy boomed. The GI Bill, the baby boom, new highways and mortgage programs fueled suburban growth. Discriminatory practices such as redlining and restrictive covenants kept many Black families out of suburban housing and wealth-building. The labor movement grew, and the Taft-Hartley Act (1947) limited some union practices. The National Security Act (1947) created the Department of Defense, the CIA and the National Security Council.

Fear of communism produced a Second Red Scare. Congressional committees and loyalty programs investigated alleged communists, and some Americans lost jobs. Supporters said that the United States faced real Soviet espionage; critics said many investigations violated civil liberties and punished beliefs and associations.

### The end of the Cold War

In the 1980s the U.S. military buildup, Soviet economic problems and reforms in the Soviet Union, along with popular movements in Eastern Europe, changed the situation. The Berlin Wall fell in 1989, and the Soviet Union dissolved in December 1991. Historians debate the relative weight of these causes.

@example
title: Matching policy to crisis
problem: Match each action to the problem it addressed: the Marshall Plan, the Berlin Airlift, the War Powers Resolution.
step: The Marshall Plan answered the fear that poverty in postwar Europe would help communist parties. It gave economic aid to rebuild Western European economies.
step: The Berlin Airlift answered the Soviet blockade of West Berlin by flying in food and fuel for about a year.
step: The War Powers Resolution (1973) responded to the debate over presidential war-making in Vietnam. It required the president to notify Congress and limited deployments without approval.
step: Each shows containment or its domestic consequences, but each used different tools: aid, logistics, and law.

@mistakes
- Saying the Cold War was a direct war between the United States and the Soviet Union; it was mostly rivalry, proxy wars and arms races.
- Describing the Vietnam debate as having only one position; Americans disagreed widely.
- Ignoring that postwar prosperity was not equally shared.
