title: Survey project: ask, collect, display and summarize
overview: You will ask a statistical question, collect 20 responses, display them in two ways and write a summary of the center and spread. The project uses statistical questions, dot plots, histograms, box plots, the mean and the median.
goal: Write a statistical question that has varying numerical answers.
goal: Collect 20 data values and display them as a dot plot and a box plot (or a histogram).
goal: Compute the mean, median, range and interquartile range and describe what they show.
step: Choose a question with numerical answers, such as "How many hours of sleep did you get last night?" or "How many minutes does your trip to school take?" Check that the answers will vary.
step: Decide who to ask, such as family, classmates or neighbors, and ask all of them the same question in the same way. Write each answer in a table; you need 20 values. If you cannot survey 20 people, use this sample: $7, 8, 8, 9, 9, 9, 7, 6, 10, 8, 9, 8, 7, 9, 10, 6, 8, 9, 5, 12$ (hours of sleep).
step: Order the values from least to greatest and find the minimum, maximum and range.
step: Find the median, then $Q_1$ and $Q_3$ (the medians of the lower and upper halves), then the IQR. Compute the mean by adding the values and dividing by $20$.
step: Make a dot plot of the data on a number line, with one dot for each value.
step: Make a second display, a box plot from the five-number summary or a histogram with equal bins.
step: Write a summary of at least five sentences that gives the question, the center, the spread, the shape and any outliers. Say whether the mean or the median is the better center and why.
deliverable: A data table with your 20 values and a clear statement of the question and who you asked.
deliverable: A dot plot and a second display, each labeled with a title and units.
deliverable: A written summary that reports the mean, median, range and IQR and describes what the data show.

rubric: The mean, median, range and IQR are computed correctly from the data.

@review
### Week by week

1. **Ratios and rates.** Equivalent ratios come from multiplying both parts by the same number. A unit rate has $1$ in the denominator, so divide. Use unit prices to compare deals.
2. **Percents.** part = percent $\times$ whole. A sale price is $(100\% - \text{discount})$ of the price.
3. **Dividing fractions.** Multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor, and check by multiplying the quotient by the divisor.
4. **Decimals.** Line up decimal points to add and subtract, count places to multiply, and move the decimal point in both numbers to divide. Estimate first.
5. **Negative numbers.** Numbers increase to the right. The opposite changes the sign. Absolute value is distance from zero.
6. **The coordinate plane.** $(x, y)$ means right or left, then up or down. Reflect across the x-axis by changing the sign of $y$. Use absolute value for distances.
7. **Expressions.** Parentheses, exponents, multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. $a(b + c) = ab + ac$.
8. **Equations and inequalities.** Undo with the inverse operation on both sides. Check by substituting.
9. **Area.** Parallelogram $bh$, triangle $\frac{1}{2}bh$. Split or subtract to handle composite shapes.
10. **Surface area and volume.** $V = lwh$ in cubic units. $SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)$ in square units.
11. **Data displays.** Statistical questions have varying answers. Dot plots show values and histograms show counts in bins.
12. **Mean, median, spread.** The median resists outliers. The IQR is $Q_3 - Q_1$.

### Self-check

- I can make a ratio table and find a unit rate.
- I can find a discount, a percent, or a whole.
- I can divide fractions and check the answer.
- I can plot points in all four quadrants.
- I can write and solve an equation from a story.
- I can find the area of a composite shape and the surface area of a box.
- I can make a dot plot and a box plot and describe center and spread.

### Common errors

- Adding instead of multiplying to make an equivalent ratio.
- Flipping the wrong fraction when dividing.
- Treating $-9$ as greater than $-2$.
- Using the slanted side as a height.
- Mixing up square units (area) and cubic units (volume).
- Using the mean when an outlier is present.

@exam num
prompt: A store sells 5 pens for \$3.50. This week all pens are 20% off. How much do 12 pens cost this week, in dollars?
answer: 6.72
check: 3.50 / 5 * 12 * 0.8
tol: 0.005
hint: Find the price of one pen first.
hint: Twelve pens cost $12$ times the unit price. Then you pay $80\%$ of that.
solution: One pen costs $3.50 \div 5 = 0.70$ dollars. Twelve pens cost $12 \times 0.70 = 8.40$. With $20\%$ off, you pay $0.80 \times 8.40 = 6.72$ dollars.

prompt: Five friends' scores in a game are $6$, $9$, $x$, $10$ and $5$. The mean of the five scores is $8$. Find $x$.
answer: 10
check: 5 * 8 - (6 + 9 + 10 + 5)
lead: $x =$
hint: The mean times the number of scores equals the total.
hint: Write an equation: $6 + 9 + x + 10 + 5 = 5 \times 8$.
solution: The total is $5 \times 8 = 40$. The known scores sum to $6 + 9 + 10 + 5 = 30$, so $30 + x = 40$ and $x = 10$.

@exam num
prompt: A gift box is $8$ cm long, $5$ cm wide and $3$ cm tall. Wrapping paper costs \$0.02 per square centimeter. What is the cost of paper needed to cover the whole box exactly, in dollars?
answer: 3.16
check: 2 * (8 * 5 + 8 * 3 + 5 * 3) * 0.02
tol: 0.005
hint: Covering the box means the total area of all six faces.
hint: The faces are $8 \times 5 = 40$, $8 \times 3 = 24$ and $5 \times 3 = 15$, each twice.
solution: The surface area is $2(40 + 24 + 15) = 158\ \text{cm}^2$. The cost is $158 \times 0.02 = 3.16$ dollars.
