@article
An **equation** states that two expressions are equal, such as $x + 7 = 15$. A **solution** is a value of the variable that makes the equation true. Here $x = 8$ works, since $8 + 7 = 15$.

### Keep the balance

Imagine a balanced scale. If you do the same thing to both sides, it stays balanced. To solve for $x$, **undo** the operation with its **inverse**:

- Addition is undone by subtraction: $x + 7 = 15$ gives $x = 15 - 7 = 8$.
- Multiplication is undone by division: $4x = 36$ gives $x = 36 \div 4 = 9$.
- Division is undone by multiplication: $\frac{x}{5} = 3$ gives $x = 3 \times 5 = 15$.

Always check by substituting the answer back into the original equation.

### Writing equations from words

1. Decide what the unknown is and give it a letter.
2. Find the sentence that says two things are equal.
3. Write it with the letter, then solve.

If Sam buys $4$ tickets for a total of \$36, let $t$ be the price of one ticket. Then $4t = 36$, so $t = 9$: each ticket costs \$9.

### Inequalities

An **inequality** compares with $<$, $>$, $\le$ or $\ge$. A solution is any value that makes it true, and there are usually many. The inequality $x - 3 > 5$ is solved the same way as an equation: add $3$ to get $x > 8$. Every number greater than $8$ is a solution, but $8$ itself is not, since $8 - 3 = 5$ is not greater than $5$.

On a number line, show $x > 8$ with an **open circle** at $8$ and an arrow to the right. For $\ge$ or $\le$ use a **closed circle**, meaning the endpoint is included.

### Testing a value

To check an inequality, test one number from your solution set and one outside it. For $x > 8$, test $10$ (true) and $6$ (false). If both give the expected result, your solution is likely right.

@example
title: Writing and solving an equation
problem: Sam buys 4 tickets for a total of \$36. All tickets cost the same. How much does one ticket cost?
step: Let $t$ be the cost of one ticket in dollars. Four tickets cost $4t$, and the total is $36$, so $4t = 36$.
step: Undo the multiplication by dividing both sides by $4$: $t = 36 \div 4 = 9$.
step: Check: $4 \times 9 = 36$. One ticket costs \$9.

@mistakes
- Using the wrong inverse, such as adding $7$ to both sides of $x + 7 = 15$.
- Doing an operation to only one side of the equation.
- Treating an inequality like $x > 8$ as if $8$ were a solution.

@practice self
prompt: Tickets to a school play cost \$6 each. The class paid \$54 in total. Write an equation for the number of tickets $n$, solve it, and check your answer.
hint: Let $n$ be the number of tickets; the cost of $n$ tickets is $6$ times $n$.
solution: Each ticket costs \$6, so $n$ tickets cost $6n$ dollars. The total is \$54, so $6n = 54$. Divide both sides by $6$: $n = 9$. Check: $6 \times 9 = 54$. The class bought $9$ tickets.
rubric: The variable is defined (the number of tickets).
rubric: The equation is $6n = 54$ (or an equivalent form).
rubric: The solution $n = 9$ is found by dividing both sides by $6$.
rubric: The answer is checked by substituting back into the original equation.
