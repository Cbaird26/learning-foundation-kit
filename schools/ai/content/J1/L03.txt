summary: Divide fractions and mixed numbers with models and the reciprocal method, and check each quotient by multiplying.
objective: Interpret a fraction division problem as "how many groups fit".
objective: Divide by a fraction by multiplying by its reciprocal.
objective: Check a quotient by multiplying it by the divisor.

@article
Division asks "how many groups fit?" The problem $6 \div 2$ asks how many $2$s fit in $6$. In the same way, $\frac{1}{2} \div \frac{1}{4}$ asks how many quarters fit in one half. Picture a half of a pizza cut into quarter slices: two slices fit, so

$$\frac{1}{2} \div \frac{1}{4} = 2.$$

### A model first

Draw a bar split into eighths. To find $\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{1}{8}$, shade $\frac{3}{4}$ of the bar, which is $\frac{6}{8}$. Count how many $\frac{1}{8}$ pieces are shaded: $6$. So $\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{1}{8} = 6$.

### The standard method

Models are great for understanding, but the standard method is faster. The **reciprocal** of a fraction swaps its numerator and denominator: the reciprocal of $\frac{5}{7}$ is $\frac{7}{5}$. A number times its reciprocal is $1$.

**To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.**

$$\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c}.$$

For example, $\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{9}{8}$.

### Whole numbers and mixed numbers

Write a whole number as a fraction over $1$: $2 = \frac{2}{1}$, so $2 \div \frac{3}{5} = \frac{2}{1} \times \frac{5}{3} = \frac{10}{3}$. Change a mixed number to an improper fraction first: $2\frac{1}{4} = \frac{9}{4}$.

### Check with multiplication

Division and multiplication undo each other. If $\text{dividend} \div \text{divisor} = \text{quotient}$, then

$$\text{quotient} \times \text{divisor} = \text{dividend}.$$

This gives you a check on every answer. Dividing by a number less than $1$ makes the result **bigger**, which is another quick sanity test.

@example
title: Divide and check
problem: Find $\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{4}{5}$ and check your answer by multiplying.
step: Write the division as multiplication by the reciprocal of $\frac{4}{5}$, which is $\frac{5}{4}$: $\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{5}{4}$.
step: Multiply the numerators and the denominators: $\frac{10}{12}$, which simplifies to $\frac{5}{6}$.
step: Check by multiplying the quotient by the divisor: $\frac{5}{6} \times \frac{4}{5} = \frac{20}{30} = \frac{2}{3}$. This matches the dividend, so the answer is $\frac{5}{6}$.

@mistakes
- Flipping the first fraction instead of the divisor (the second one).
- Flipping the fraction but leaving the operation as division.
- Forgetting to change a mixed number to an improper fraction before dividing.

@practice num
prompt: How many quarters fit in one half? That is, compute $\frac{1}{2} \div \frac{1}{4}$.
answer: 2
check: (1/2) / (1/4)
hint: Picture half a pizza cut into quarter slices.
hint: The reciprocal of $\frac{1}{4}$ is $4$.
solution: $\frac{1}{2} \times 4 = 2$.

@practice num
prompt: Compute $\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{2}{3}$. Give an exact fraction.
answer: 9/8
check: (3/4) / (2/3)
hint: Multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor.
hint: The reciprocal of $\frac{2}{3}$ is $\frac{3}{2}$.
solution: $\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{9}{8}$. Check: $\frac{9}{8} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{18}{24} = \frac{3}{4}$.

solution: $\frac{2}{1} \times \frac{5}{3} = \frac{10}{3}$. Check: $\frac{10}{3} \times \frac{3}{5} = 2$.

@practice num
prompt: Compute $2\frac{1}{4} \div \frac{3}{4}$.
answer: 3
check: (9/4) / (3/4)
hint: Change $2\frac{1}{4}$ to an improper fraction.
hint: $2\frac{1}{4} = \frac{9}{4}$. Now multiply by $\frac{4}{3}$.
solution: $\frac{9}{4} \times \frac{4}{3} = \frac{36}{12} = 3$. Check: $3 \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{9}{4} = 2\frac{1}{4}$.

@quiz num
prompt: A ribbon is $\frac{5}{6}$ meter long. It is cut into pieces that are each $\frac{1}{12}$ meter long. How many pieces are there?
answer: 10
check: (5/6) / (1/12)
hint: The question asks how many pieces of length $\frac{1}{12}$ fit in $\frac{5}{6}$.
hint: Compute $\frac{5}{6} \times 12$.
solution: $\frac{5}{6} \div \frac{1}{12} = \frac{5}{6} \times 12 = 10$ pieces. Check: $10 \times \frac{1}{12} = \frac{5}{6}$.

@quiz num

answer: 2/3

hint: Flip the divisor and multiply.
hint: $\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{10}{9}$, then simplify.
solution: $\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{10}{9} = \frac{30}{45} = \frac{2}{3}$. Check: $\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{9}{10} = \frac{18}{30} = \frac{3}{5}$.
