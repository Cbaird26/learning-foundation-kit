@article
How much of one vector points along another? A box dragged along the floor by a rope at an angle is moved only by the part of the pull that lies along the floor. The **dot product** measures exactly this kind of alignment.

### Two formulas, one number

For $\mathbf{u} = \langle u_1, u_2\rangle$ and $\mathbf{v} = \langle v_1, v_2\rangle$, the dot product is the **scalar**

$$\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = u_1v_1 + u_2v_2.$$

(In three dimensions add $u_3v_3$.) Geometrically, if $\theta \in [0, \pi]$ is the angle between the vectors drawn tail to tail,

$$\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = |\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|\cos\theta.$$

Why are these the same? Apply the law of cosines to the triangle with sides $\mathbf{u}$, $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}$:

$$|\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}|^2 = |\mathbf{u}|^2 + |\mathbf{v}|^2 - 2|\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|\cos\theta.$$

Expanding the left side in components gives $|\mathbf{u}|^2 + |\mathbf{v}|^2 - 2(u_1v_1 + u_2v_2)$. Comparing the two expressions identifies $u_1v_1 + u_2v_2$ with $|\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|\cos\theta$. Notice also that $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{u} = |\mathbf{u}|^2$.

### Angles and orthogonality

Solving for the angle gives

$$\theta = \arccos\left(\frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|}\right).$$

The sign of $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}$ describes the angle: positive means acute, negative means obtuse, and **zero means a right angle**. Two non-zero vectors with $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 0$ are **orthogonal** (perpendicular).

Here is the geometric reason. Expand $|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}|^2 = |\mathbf{u}|^2 + 2\,\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} + |\mathbf{v}|^2$. The Pythagorean relation $|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}|^2 = |\mathbf{u}|^2 + |\mathbf{v}|^2$ therefore holds exactly when $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 0$. In other words, the two sides of the triangle meet at a right angle precisely when the Pythagorean theorem applies to them.

### Projections

The **projection** of $\mathbf{u}$ onto $\mathbf{v}$ is the "shadow" of $\mathbf{u}$ on the line through $\mathbf{v}$. Its signed length, the **scalar projection**, is $|\mathbf{u}|\cos\theta$, and the shadow itself is a multiple of $\mathbf{v}$:

$$\text{comp}_{\mathbf{v}}\,\mathbf{u} = \frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{v}|}, \qquad \text{proj}_{\mathbf{v}}\,\mathbf{u} = \frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}}\,\mathbf{v}.$$

What is left over, $\mathbf{u} - \text{proj}_{\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{u}$, is orthogonal to $\mathbf{v}$. So every vector splits into a part parallel to $\mathbf{v}$ plus a part perpendicular to it. In physics, the **work** done by a constant force $\mathbf{F}$ over a displacement $\mathbf{d}$ is $W = \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{d}$.

```python
import numpy as np
u, v = np.array([3, 1]), np.array([1, 2])
c = u @ v / (np.linalg.norm(u) * np.linalg.norm(v))
print(round(float(np.degrees(np.arccos(c))), 2))   # 45.0
```

@example
title: The angle between two vectors
problem: Find the angle between $\mathbf{u} = \langle 3, 1\rangle$ and $\mathbf{v} = \langle 1, 2\rangle$.
step: Compute the dot product: $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 3(1) + 1(2) = 5$.
step: Compute the magnitudes: $|\mathbf{u}| = \sqrt{10}$ and $|\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{5}$, so $|\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$.
step: Then $\cos\theta = \dfrac{5}{5\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
step: Therefore $\theta = \arccos\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, or $45^\circ$. A positive dot product always gives an acute angle, which agrees.

@example
title: Projecting and splitting a vector
problem: Project $\mathbf{u} = \langle 5, 5\rangle$ onto $\mathbf{v} = \langle 3, 4\rangle$, and verify that the leftover part is orthogonal to $\mathbf{v}$.
step: Compute $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 15 + 20 = 35$ and $\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 9 + 16 = 25$.
step: The vector projection is $\dfrac{35}{25}\langle 3, 4\rangle = \dfrac{7}{5}\langle 3, 4\rangle = \langle 4.2,\ 5.6\rangle$. The scalar projection is $\dfrac{35}{|\mathbf{v}|} = \dfrac{35}{5} = 7$, the length of the shadow.
step: The leftover part is $\mathbf{u} - \text{proj}_{\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{u} = \langle 5 - 4.2,\ 5 - 5.6\rangle = \langle 0.8, -0.6\rangle$.
step: Check orthogonality: $\langle 0.8, -0.6\rangle\cdot\langle 3, 4\rangle = 2.4 - 2.4 = 0$.

@mistakes
- Thinking the dot product is a vector. It is a single number (a scalar).
- Concluding from $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 0$ that one of the vectors is zero. For non-zero vectors it means they are perpendicular.
- Dividing by $|\mathbf{v}|$ instead of $\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v} = |\mathbf{v}|^2$ in the vector projection formula.
- Mixing up $\text{proj}_{\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{u}$ with $\text{proj}_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{v}$. The projection onto $\mathbf{v}$ is always a multiple of $\mathbf{v}$.

@practice tuple
prompt: Find the vector projection of $\mathbf{u} = \langle 4, 3\rangle$ onto $\mathbf{v} = \langle 1, 2\rangle$. Enter its components.
answer: (2, 4)
check: tuple(np.dot([4, 3], [1, 2])/np.dot([1, 2], [1, 2])*np.array([1, 2]))
hint: The projection onto $\mathbf{v}$ is a scalar multiple of $\mathbf{v}$, with the scalar $\dfrac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}}$.
hint: Compute $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}$, divide, then multiply $\mathbf{v}$ by the result.
solution: $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 4 + 6 = 10$ and $\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 1 + 4 = 5$, so $\text{proj}_{\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{u} = 2\langle 1, 2\rangle = \langle 2, 4\rangle$. The leftover $\langle 2, -1\rangle$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf{v}$.

@practice self
prompt: Let $\mathbf{u} = \langle 3, 4\rangle$, $\mathbf{v} = \langle 4, -3\rangle$ and $\mathbf{w} = \langle 6, 2\rangle$. (a) Compute $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}$ and explain geometrically, using the Pythagorean theorem, what the result says about the angle between $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$. (b) Compute $\text{proj}_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{w}$ and the leftover vector $\mathbf{w} - \text{proj}_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{w}$, and verify that the leftover is orthogonal to $\mathbf{u}$. (c) Explain in a sentence or two, with a sketch, why the leftover must be perpendicular to $\mathbf{u}$.
hint: For part (a), compare $|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}|^2$ with $|\mathbf{u}|^2 + |\mathbf{v}|^2$.
hint: For part (b), the projection onto $\mathbf{u}$ is a multiple of $\mathbf{u}$. Compute $\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{u}$ first.
solution: (a) $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 12 - 12 = 0$. Also $\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v} = \langle 7, 1\rangle$ has $|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}|^2 = 50 = 25 + 25 = |\mathbf{u}|^2 + |\mathbf{v}|^2$, so the triangle with sides $\mathbf{u}$, $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}$ satisfies the Pythagorean theorem, and its angle between $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ is a right angle. (b) $\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{u} = 18 + 8 = 26$ and $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{u} = 25$, so $\text{proj}_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{w} = \frac{26}{25}\langle 3, 4\rangle = \langle \frac{78}{25}, \frac{104}{25}\rangle$. The leftover is $\langle 6 - \frac{78}{25}, 2 - \frac{104}{25}\rangle = \langle \frac{72}{25}, -\frac{54}{25}\rangle$, and its dot product with $\mathbf{u}$ is $\frac{216}{25} - \frac{216}{25} = 0$. (c) The projection is the foot of the perpendicular dropped from the tip of $\mathbf{w}$ onto the line through $\mathbf{u}$. The leftover vector is the segment from that foot to the tip of $\mathbf{w}$, which is the perpendicular itself.
rubric: Part (a) finds $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 0$ and links it to the Pythagorean relation $|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}|^2 = |\mathbf{u}|^2 + |\mathbf{v}|^2$, concluding a right angle.
rubric: Part (b) gives $\text{proj}_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{w} = \frac{26}{25}\langle 3, 4\rangle$ and a leftover vector whose dot product with $\mathbf{u}$ is shown to be $0$.
rubric: Part (c) says the projection is the foot of the perpendicular from the tip of $\mathbf{w}$ to the line of $\mathbf{u}$, so the leftover lies along that perpendicular.

@quiz mc
prompt: Two non-zero vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ satisfy $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 0$. Which statement must be true?
choice*: $|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}|^2 = |\mathbf{u}|^2 + |\mathbf{v}|^2$
choice: $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ are parallel.
choice: $\mathbf{u} = -\mathbf{v}$.
choice: $|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}| = |\mathbf{u}| + |\mathbf{v}|$
hint: Zero dot product means $\cos\theta = 0$. What is the angle?
hint: Expand $|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}|^2 = (\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v})\cdot(\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v})$.
solution: Expanding gives $|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}|^2 = |\mathbf{u}|^2 + 2\,\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} + |\mathbf{v}|^2$, and the middle term vanishes. The vectors are perpendicular, not parallel. The statement $|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}| = |\mathbf{u}| + |\mathbf{v}|$ would need them to point in the same direction, and $\mathbf{u} = -\mathbf{v}$ would give $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = -|\mathbf{u}|^2 \ne 0$.
