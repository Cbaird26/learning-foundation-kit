@lesson
title: Sorting and searching games
summary: Put cards in order by a rule, and find a card by looking one at a time or by guessing the middle.
objective: Sort things from smallest to biggest.
objective: Find a card by looking at the cards one at a time.
objective: Say why guessing the middle is a fast way to search sorted cards.
reading: CS Unplugged, look up the activities on sorting algorithms and on searching algorithms.

@article
**Sorting** means putting things in order by a rule. You can sort numbers from smallest to biggest. You can sort toys by size, or names by the first letter.

Try this with ten cards that have the numbers 1 to 10. Mix them up. Now put them in order, smallest on the left. You have sorted them!

The rule matters. You can sort by a number, by color, or by how many letters are in a word. You must say the rule, so that anyone can check your work.

### Searching

**Searching** means looking for one thing. Say you want to find the card with 7 on it.

**Way 1: look one at a time.** Turn over the cards from the left, one by one, until you find 7. This works on any cards, sorted or not. But it may take many looks.

**Way 2: guess the middle.** If the cards are **sorted**, you can be quicker. Look at the card in the middle. Is it too small or too big? If it is too small, forget all the cards to its left. If it is too big, forget all the cards to its right. Then look at the middle of what is left.

Each look throws away about half of the cards. That is very fast, even for a big pile.

### The higher or lower game

A friend thinks of a number from 1 to 10. You guess 5. Your friend says "higher". You now know the number is 6, 7, 8, 9 or 10. You guess the middle again. That is the same idea!

Grown-up helper: with ten number cards, let your child sort them, say the rule, and then play the higher or lower game with you.

@example
title: Looking one at a time
problem: The cards are 6, 2, 8, 4. You look for the card with 8, starting at the left. How many cards do you turn over?
step: Turn over the first card. It is 6. Not 8.
step: Turn over the next card. It is 2. Not 8.
step: Turn over the next card. It is 8. You found it!
answer: 3

@mistakes
- Using the "guess the middle" way on cards that are not sorted. It only works when the cards are in order.
- Forgetting to say the rule when you sort. Anyone should be able to check your order.
- Mixing up smallest to biggest with biggest to smallest.
