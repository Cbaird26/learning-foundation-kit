@article
A **fraction** $\frac{a}{b}$ means $a$ pieces, each of size $\frac{1}{b}$ of a whole. It also means $a \div b$, so $\frac{3}{4} = 0.75$. The top number is the **numerator**, the bottom number is the **denominator**, and the denominator can never be $0$.

### Equivalent fractions

Cut every slice of a pizza in half: you have twice as many slices, each half the size, but the same amount of pizza. So $\frac{3}{4} = \frac{6}{8}$. In general, multiplying or dividing the top and the bottom by the same nonzero number gives an **equivalent fraction**:

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{a \cdot k}{b \cdot k} \qquad (k \neq 0).$$

To **simplify**, divide the top and bottom by their greatest common factor: $\frac{18}{24} = \frac{18 \div 6}{24 \div 6} = \frac{3}{4}$. A minus sign can sit in any one of three places: $-\frac{2}{5} = \frac{-2}{5} = \frac{2}{-5}$.

### The four operations

| operation | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| multiply | tops times tops, bottoms times bottoms | $\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{9}{10} = \frac{18}{30} = \frac{3}{5}$ |
| divide | multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction | $\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{5}{8} = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{8}{5} = \frac{6}{5}$ |
| add or subtract | rewrite over a common denominator, then combine the tops | $\frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{10}{12} - \frac{3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$ |

Adding needs a **common denominator** because you can only count pieces of the same size: a third of a cake plus a quarter of a cake is not "two sevenths" of a cake. Use the **least common denominator** (LCD), the smallest number that every denominator divides into, to keep the numbers small.

Division asks "how many of these fit into that?" Half a cup fits into $3$ cups six times, so $3 \div \frac{1}{2} = 3 \cdot 2 = 6$. That is why dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal.

### Mixed numbers

A **mixed number** such as $2\frac{1}{3}$ means $2 + \frac{1}{3}$. Convert it to an **improper fraction** before multiplying or dividing: $2\frac{1}{3} = \frac{6}{3} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{7}{3}$. When you type an answer on this site, enter an improper fraction such as `7/3` rather than a mixed number.

### Ratios and proportions

A **ratio** $a : b$ compares two quantities: "$a$ for every $b$". It behaves like the fraction $\frac{a}{b}$, so it simplifies the same way: $12 : 8 = 3 : 2$. A **proportion** says that two ratios are equal. To scale a ratio up or down, multiply both parts by the same number: a recipe with $3$ cups of flour for $2$ cups of milk, doubled, uses $6$ cups of flour for $4$ cups of milk. When the scale factor is awkward, use cross-multiplication: for nonzero $b$ and $d$,

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \quad\text{exactly when}\quad ad = bc.$$

### Estimate first

Before computing, round each number to something easy. $\frac{7}{8}$ of $23$ must be a little less than $23$. If your exact answer comes out as $2.9$ or $184$, something went wrong. The exact value is $\frac{7 \cdot 23}{8} = \frac{161}{8} = 20.125$, close to the estimate. A ten-second estimate catches most slips with fractions.

@example
title: Adding mixed numbers
problem: Compute $2\frac{1}{3} + 1\frac{3}{4}$. Estimate first.
step: Estimate: $2\frac{1}{3}$ is a little more than $2$ and $1\frac{3}{4}$ is almost $2$, so the sum should be about $4$.
step: Convert to improper fractions: $2\frac{1}{3} = \frac{7}{3}$ and $1\frac{3}{4} = \frac{7}{4}$.
step: The LCD of $3$ and $4$ is $12$: $\frac{7}{3} = \frac{28}{12}$ and $\frac{7}{4} = \frac{21}{12}$.
step: Add the tops and keep the denominator: $\frac{28}{12} + \frac{21}{12} = \frac{49}{12}$.
step: Since $4 \cdot 12 = 48$, $\frac{49}{12} = 4\frac{1}{12}$. This matches the estimate of about $4$.

@example
title: Scaling a recipe
problem: A recipe uses $3$ cups of flour for every $2$ cups of milk. How much milk goes with $7$ cups of flour?
step: Write both ratios in the same order, milk to flour: $\frac{2}{3} = \frac{m}{7}$, where $m$ is the cups of milk.
step: Cross-multiply: $3m = 2 \cdot 7 = 14$.
step: Divide both sides by $3$: $m = \frac{14}{3} = 4\frac{2}{3}$ cups.
step: Check: $7$ cups of flour is a bit more than double $3$ cups, and $4\frac{2}{3}$ cups of milk is a bit more than double $2$ cups. The answer is sensible.

@mistakes
- Adding tops and bottoms: $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3}$ is not $\frac{2}{5}$. Over the LCD it is $\frac{3}{6} + \frac{2}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$.
- Flipping the first fraction when dividing. Only the second fraction, the divisor, is replaced by its reciprocal.
- Cancelling a number that is added rather than multiplied: $\frac{2 + 5}{2}$ is $\frac{7}{2}$, not $5$. You may cancel only common factors.
- Reversing a ratio. "3 cups of flour for every 2 cups of milk" is flour : milk $= 3 : 2$, so keep the same order on both sides of a proportion.

@practice num
prompt: Find the missing numerator: $\frac{42}{56} = \frac{\square}{4}$.
answer: 3
check: 42/56*4
hint: The denominator went from $56$ to $4$. What was it divided by?
hint: It was divided by $14$. Divide the numerator by the same number.
solution: $56 \div 14 = 4$, so divide the top by $14$ as well: $42 \div 14 = 3$. So $\frac{42}{56} = \frac{3}{4}$.

@practice num
prompt: Compute $\frac{5}{6} - \frac{3}{8}$. Give an exact answer as a fraction.
answer: 11/24
check: Fraction(5, 6) - Fraction(3, 8)
hint: The denominators differ, so find the LCD of $6$ and $8$.
hint: The LCD is $24$. Rewrite each fraction with denominator $24$, then subtract the tops.
solution: $\frac{5}{6} = \frac{20}{24}$ and $\frac{3}{8} = \frac{9}{24}$, so the difference is $\frac{20 - 9}{24} = \frac{11}{24}$. It cannot be simplified, since $11$ is prime and does not divide $24$.

@practice num
prompt: Compute $-\frac{4}{15} \div \frac{8}{9}$. Give an exact answer.
answer: -3/10
check: Fraction(-4, 15) / Fraction(8, 9)
hint: Dividing by $\frac{8}{9}$ is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal, $\frac{9}{8}$.
hint: Cancel common factors before multiplying: $4$ and $8$ share the factor $4$, and $9$ and $15$ share the factor $3$.
solution: $-\frac{4}{15} \cdot \frac{9}{8} = -\frac{4 \cdot 9}{15 \cdot 8} = -\frac{1 \cdot 3}{5 \cdot 2} = -\frac{3}{10}$. A negative divided by a positive is negative.

@practice num
prompt: A recipe needs $\frac{3}{4}$ cup of sugar for $6$ muffins. How many cups of sugar are needed for $10$ muffins? Give an improper fraction or an exact decimal.
unit: cups
answer: 5/4
check: Fraction(3, 4) / 6 * 10
hint: Find the sugar for one muffin first: divide $\frac{3}{4}$ by $6$.
hint: One muffin needs $\frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{8}$ cup. Now scale up to $10$ muffins.
solution: One muffin needs $\frac{3}{4} \div 6 = \frac{1}{8}$ cup, so $10$ muffins need $\frac{10}{8} = \frac{5}{4} = 1\frac{1}{4}$ cups. Estimate check: $10$ muffins is a bit less than double $6$, and $1\frac{1}{4}$ cups is a bit less than double $\frac{3}{4}$ cup.

@quiz num
prompt: Simplify $\dfrac{\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3}}{\frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{4}}$. Give an exact answer.
answer: 10/7
check: (Fraction(1, 2) + Fraction(1, 3)) / (Fraction(5, 6) - Fraction(1, 4))
hint: The main fraction bar acts like brackets: simplify the top and the bottom separately first.
hint: The top is $\frac{5}{6}$ and the bottom is $\frac{7}{12}$. Now divide the top by the bottom.
solution: Top: $\frac{3}{6} + \frac{2}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$. Bottom: $\frac{10}{12} - \frac{3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$. Then $\frac{5}{6} \div \frac{7}{12} = \frac{5}{6} \cdot \frac{12}{7} = \frac{60}{42} = \frac{10}{7}$.

@quiz num
prompt: A gardener divides $84$ kg of compost between two beds in the ratio $3 : 4$. How many kilograms go to the bed that gets the larger share?
unit: kg
answer: 48
check: 84 * 4 / (3 + 4)
hint: A ratio of $3 : 4$ splits the whole into $3 + 4 = 7$ equal parts.
hint: Find the size of one part, then take the number of parts in the larger share.
solution: One part is $84 \div 7 = 12$ kg, so the larger share is $4 \cdot 12 = 48$ kg. Check: the smaller share is $3 \cdot 12 = 36$ kg, $36 + 48 = 84$, and $36 : 48 = 3 : 4$.
