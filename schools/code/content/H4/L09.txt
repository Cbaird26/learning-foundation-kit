@article
A **sequence** is a function whose inputs are the positive integers. We list its outputs as $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots$. The subscript $n$ is the **index** and $a_n$ is the $n$-th **term**. Everything you know about functions still applies; the only new feature is that the input is discrete.

### Two ways to define a sequence

- An **explicit formula** gives $a_n$ directly from $n$. For $a_n = n^2$ the terms are $1, 4, 9, 16, \dots$ and you can jump to any term, such as $a_{100} = 10000$.
- A **recursive formula** gives a starting value and a rule that builds each term from the one before. With $a_1 = 2$ and $a_{n+1} = 3a_n - 1$ the terms are $2, 5, 14, 41, \dots$. To reach $a_{100}$ you would normally have to compute all the earlier terms.

### Arithmetic sequences

An **arithmetic sequence** has a constant **difference** $d$ between consecutive terms:

$$a_{n+1} = a_n + d, \qquad a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d.$$

The explicit formula is a linear function of $n$ with slope $d$. It is the discrete cousin of a straight line.

### Geometric sequences

A **geometric sequence** has a constant **ratio** $r$ between consecutive terms:

$$a_{n+1} = r\,a_n, \qquad a_n = a_1\, r^{\,n-1}.$$

The explicit formula is an exponential function of $n$ with base $r$. In both formulas the factor is applied $n - 1$ times, not $n$ times, because $a_1$ is the starting point. Off-by-one errors are the most common mistake in this topic.

### Recognize the type, then solve

To classify a sequence, compare **every** pair of consecutive terms: equal differences mean arithmetic, equal ratios mean geometric. If the terms are given only at two positions $m$ and $k$, then

$$d = \frac{a_k - a_m}{k - m} \quad\text{or}\quad r^{\,k-m} = \frac{a_k}{a_m}.$$

Find $d$ or $r$ first, then $a_1$, then the term you want.

### Where is the sequence heading?

As $n$ grows, a sequence may settle toward a value ($\frac1n \to 0$), grow without bound ($n^2$ or $2^n$), or oscillate ($(-1)^n$). For a geometric sequence with $a_1 \neq 0$:

- $|r| < 1$: the terms shrink to $0$.
- $|r| > 1$: the terms grow in size without bound.
- $r = 1$: constant. $r = -1$: alternates between two values.

This question, "what is the sequence approaching?", is a first taste of limits, which you will meet again in Week 12 and which sit at the heart of calculus.

@example
title: Arithmetic sequence from two terms
problem: An arithmetic sequence has $a_4 = 5$ and $a_9 = -10$. Find a formula for $a_n$ and compute $a_{20}$.
step: The difference is $d = \dfrac{a_9 - a_4}{9 - 4} = \dfrac{-10 - 5}{5} = -3$.
step: Go back from term $4$ to term $1$: $a_1 = a_4 - 3d = 5 - 3(-3) = 14$.
step: The formula is $a_n = 14 + (n-1)(-3) = 17 - 3n$.
step: Check: $a_9 = 17 - 27 = -10$, as required. Then $a_{20} = 17 - 60 = -43$.

@example
title: Geometric sequence from two terms
problem: A geometric sequence has $a_3 = 12$ and $a_6 = 96$. Find $a_1$, $r$ and $a_{10}$.
step: The terms are $3$ steps apart, so $r^3 = \dfrac{96}{12} = 8$, giving $r = 2$ (the only real cube root of $8$).
step: Go back two steps from term $3$ to term $1$: $a_1 = \dfrac{a_3}{r^2} = \dfrac{12}{4} = 3$.
step: The formula is $a_n = 3 \cdot 2^{n-1}$. Check: $a_6 = 3 \cdot 32 = 96$.
step: Then $a_{10} = 3 \cdot 2^9 = 1536$.

@mistakes
- Off-by-one errors: writing $a_1 r^n$ or $a_1 + nd$ for the $n$-th term. Both formulas use $n - 1$.
- Concluding that a sequence is geometric (or arithmetic) after checking only the first two terms.
- Mixing up the explicit and recursive forms, for example starting a recursion from the wrong initial value.
- Assuming every pattern is arithmetic or geometric. The squares $1, 4, 9, 16, \dots$ are neither.

@practice num
prompt: The sequence $7, 11, 15, \dots$ is arithmetic. Find $a_{25}$.
answer: 103
check: [7 + 4*i for i in range(25)][-1]
hint: First find the common difference.
hint: Use $a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d$ with $d = 4$ and $n = 25$.
solution: $d = 11 - 7 = 4$, so $a_{25} = 7 + 24 \cdot 4 = 103$.

@practice num
prompt: A geometric sequence has $a_1 = 2$ and $a_4 = 54$. Find $a_6$.
answer: 486
check: (lambda r: 2*r**5)(root(lambda r: 2*r**3 - 54, 0, 100))
hint: From $a_4 = a_1 r^3$, find $r$.
hint: $r^3 = 27$. Then $a_6 = a_1 r^5$.
solution: $2r^3 = 54$ gives $r^3 = 27$, so $r = 3$. Then $a_6 = 2 \cdot 3^5 = 2 \cdot 243 = 486$.

@practice mc
prompt: A sequence is defined by $a_1 = 5$ and $a_{n+1} = a_n - 3$. Which explicit formula describes it?
choice*: $a_n = 8 - 3n$
choice: $a_n = 5 - 3n$
choice: $a_n = 5(-3)^{n-1}$
choice: $a_n = 5 + 3(n-1)$
hint: Write out the first few terms: $5, 2, -1, \dots$
hint: A correct formula must give $5$ when $n = 1$ and must decrease by $3$ at every step.
solution: The recursion subtracts $3$ each time, so the sequence is arithmetic with $a_1 = 5$ and $d = -3$: $a_n = 5 - 3(n-1) = 8 - 3n$. The formula $5 - 3n$ gives $2$ at $n = 1$; $5(-3)^{n-1}$ is geometric; $5 + 3(n-1)$ increases.

@practice num
prompt: How many terms of the arithmetic sequence $3, 7, 11, \dots$ are less than $100$?
answer: 25
check: len([x for x in [3 + 4*i for i in range(1000)] if x < 100])
hint: Write the $n$-th term as a formula in $n$.
hint: Solve the inequality $a_n < 100$ and count the whole numbers $n \ge 1$ that satisfy it.
solution: $a_n = 3 + 4(n-1) = 4n - 1$. Then $4n - 1 < 100$ gives $n < 25.25$, so $n = 1, 2, \dots, 25$. There are $25$ terms (the last is $99$).

@practice num
prompt: A sequence is defined by $a_1 = 2$ and $a_{n+1} = 3a_n - 1$. Find $a_6$.
answer: 365
check: (lambda a: [a.append(3*a[-1] - 1) for _ in range(5)] and a[-1])([2])
hint: This sequence is neither arithmetic nor geometric. Use the rule step by step.
hint: The terms start $2, 5, 14, \dots$. Keep applying $a_{n+1} = 3a_n - 1$.
solution: $a_2 = 3(2) - 1 = 5$, $a_3 = 14$, $a_4 = 41$, $a_5 = 122$, $a_6 = 3(122) - 1 = 365$.

@quiz num
prompt: An arithmetic sequence has $a_5 = 17$ and $a_{12} = 45$. Find $a_{30}$.
answer: 117
check: (lambda d: 17 + (30 - 5)*d)((45 - 17)/(12 - 5))
hint: The terms are $7$ steps apart. Find the common difference.
hint: $d = \frac{45 - 17}{7} = 4$. Count how many steps it is from $a_5$ to $a_{30}$.
solution: $d = 28/7 = 4$. From term $5$ to term $30$ is $25$ steps, so $a_{30} = 17 + 25 \cdot 4 = 117$.

@quiz ms
prompt: Select every sequence that is geometric.
choice*: $2,\ 6,\ 18,\ 54,\ \dots$
choice*: $1,\ -2,\ 4,\ -8,\ \dots$
choice*: $100,\ 50,\ 25,\ 12.5,\ \dots$
choice: $1,\ 4,\ 9,\ 16,\ \dots$
choice: $2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ \dots$
hint: Divide each term by the one before it, for every consecutive pair.
hint: A geometric sequence needs the same ratio every time, and the ratio may be negative or a fraction.
solution: The ratios are $3$ for $2, 6, 18, \dots$, $-2$ for $1, -2, 4, \dots$ and $\frac12$ for $100, 50, 25, \dots$, so they are geometric. The squares have ratios $4, \frac94, \frac{16}{9}, \dots$, which are not constant; $2, 4, 6, 8$ has constant differences, so it is arithmetic.
