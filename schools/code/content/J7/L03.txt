@article
Look at a granite countertop and you will see tiny sparkling grains of different colors. Each grain is a **mineral**, and the whole thing is a **rock**. A rock is a solid mixture of one or more minerals (or other material).

### Minerals

A **mineral** is a naturally occurring, inorganic, solid substance with a definite chemical composition and an orderly crystal structure. Quartz, calcite, feldspar and mica are minerals; ice in a glacier is technically a mineral too, but water in a glass is not. Minerals are identified by properties such as color, streak (the color of the powder), luster, cleavage and **hardness**. The **Mohs scale** ranks hardness from 1 (talc) to 10 (diamond), with gypsum 2, calcite 3, fluorite 4, apatite 5, orthoclase 6, quartz 7, topaz 8 and corundum 9. A harder mineral scratches a softer one. A fingernail is about 2.5, and a steel nail about 5.5.

**Density** is mass per unit volume:
$$\rho = \frac{m}{V}.$$
Each mineral has a typical density (quartz is about 2.65 g/cm$^3$), so density helps identify it. You can find the volume of an irregular rock by how much water it displaces: 1 mL equals 1 cm$^3$.

### Three rock types

- **Igneous rock** forms when molten rock cools and hardens. Slow cooling underground gives large crystals (**intrusive**, such as granite). Fast cooling at the surface gives small crystals or glass (**extrusive**, such as basalt and obsidian).
- **Sedimentary rock** forms when sediment (pieces of rock, shell or minerals) is buried, squeezed (**compaction**) and glued together by minerals (**cementation**). Sandstone, shale and limestone are examples. It often has layers and may hold fossils.
- **Metamorphic rock** forms when existing rock is changed by heat and pressure without melting. Limestone becomes marble, shale becomes slate, and granite becomes gneiss. It often shows bands or flattened grains.

### The rock cycle

Any rock can become any other. Weathering and erosion break rock into sediment; burial turns sediment into sedimentary rock; heat and pressure make metamorphic rock; melting makes magma, which cools into igneous rock. There is no start and no end, and the cycle takes millions of years. It is powered by Earth's internal heat (which drives melting and plate motion) and by the Sun (which drives weathering and erosion).

@example
title: Finding a mineral's density
problem: A mineral sample has a mass of 78 g. It is lowered into a graduated cylinder holding 40 mL of water, and the water level rises to 70 mL. What is the sample's density?
step: The volume of the sample equals the volume of water it displaces: $70 - 40 = 30$ mL, which is $30$ cm$^3$.
step: Use $\rho = \frac{m}{V}$: $\rho = \frac{78}{30}$.
step: Divide: $\rho = 2.6$ g/cm$^3$, close to the density of quartz.
step: Check the units: grams divided by cubic centimeters gives g/cm$^3$.

@mistakes
- Believing that metamorphic rock melts. If it melts, it becomes magma and the new rock is igneous.
- Thinking hard means unbreakable. Hardness is resistance to scratching; a diamond can still shatter.
- Believing the rock cycle runs in one fixed order. Rocks can take many paths, and some skip steps.
- Using the total volume of the water and rock together as the rock's volume, instead of the volume of water displaced.

@practice mc
prompt: Which rock forms when magma cools slowly deep underground, producing large visible crystals?
choice: Sandstone
choice: Marble
choice*: Granite
choice: Obsidian
hint: Slow cooling means large crystals, and underground cooling is called intrusive.
solution: Granite is an intrusive igneous rock. Sandstone is sedimentary, marble is metamorphic, and obsidian is volcanic glass that cooled too fast to form crystals.

prompt: A quartz crystal has a volume of 12 cm$^3$. The density of quartz is 2.65 g/cm$^3$. What is the mass of the crystal in grams?
answer: 31.8
check: 2.65 * 12
tol: 1%
unit: g
hint: Rearrange $\rho = \frac{m}{V}$ to $m = \rho V$.
solution: $m = \rho V = 2.65 \times 12 = 31.8$ g.

@practice ms

hint: Look for processes that change rock from one type to another. Fossils are not made from rock.
solution: Weathering and erosion, compaction and cementation, metamorphism by heat and pressure, and melting then cooling are all steps of the rock cycle. Fossils are the remains of organisms preserved in sedimentary rock; metamorphic rock does not turn into fossils.
