@article
Two figures are **similar** if one is an enlargement or reduction of the other, possibly turned or flipped. Corresponding angles are equal and corresponding sides are **proportional**: every side of the second is the same multiple $k$ of the matching side of the first. The number $k$ is the **scale factor**. We write $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$, again with the vertices in matching order.

Congruent figures are the special case $k = 1$.

### Similarity criteria for triangles

You do not need to check all angles and sides.

- **AA (angle-angle):** two pairs of equal angles. The third pair is then equal too, since angles sum to $180^\circ$.
- **SAS similarity:** two pairs of sides are in the same ratio and the **included** angles are equal.
- **SSS similarity:** all three pairs of sides are in the same ratio.

AA is the one you will use most. Note the contrast with congruence: AAA is enough for similarity but not for congruence.

### Setting up proportions

If $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$, then
$$\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} = \frac{AC}{DF} = k.$$
Always match **corresponding** sides: the shortest with the shortest, the longest with the longest. Write the ratio in a consistent order (small triangle over large, or the reverse) on both sides of the equation, then cross-multiply.

### Triangle proportionality theorem

Draw a triangle $ABC$ and a segment $DE$ with $D$ on $AB$ and $E$ on $AC$, parallel to $BC$. Then the small triangle $ADE$ is similar to $ABC$ (the parallel lines give equal corresponding angles, plus the shared angle at $A$), and
$$\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}.$$
A parallel line cuts the two sides it meets in the same ratio. The converse holds too: if a segment divides two sides in the same ratio, it is parallel to the third side.

### Perimeter and area

For similar figures with scale factor $k$:

- lengths (sides, perimeters, heights) are multiplied by $k$,
- **areas** are multiplied by $k^2$.

Doubling every side of a square makes its area four times as large, not twice. This is the single most common similarity error.

### Indirect measurement

Similar triangles let you measure what you cannot reach: sunlight makes a tall object and a short one cast shadows at the same angle, giving two similar right triangles (AA), so height divided by shadow length is the same for both.

@example
title: Height of a tree from its shadow
problem: A vertical $1.5$ m stick casts a shadow $2$ m long on level ground. At the same moment a tree casts a shadow $12$ m long. How tall is the tree?
step: The sun's rays are parallel, so the stick and the tree both make the same angle with the ground. Each with its shadow forms a right triangle, so the triangles are similar by AA.
step: Match corresponding sides, height to height and shadow to shadow: $\dfrac{h}{12} = \dfrac{1.5}{2}$.
step: Multiply both sides by $12$: $h = \dfrac{1.5 \times 12}{2} = 9$.
step: The tree is $9$ m tall. Check the scale factor: $12 / 2 = 6$ and $1.5 \times 6 = 9$.

@mistakes
- Matching sides that are not corresponding, for example setting short side over long side on one side of the equation and long over short on the other.
- Multiplying areas by $k$ instead of $k^2$.
- Assuming similar means congruent, or using SSA as a similarity criterion.
- Using equal ratios of two sides without checking that the angle between them is equal (SAS similarity needs the included angle).

@practice num
prompt: A triangle has sides $6$, $8$ and $10$. A similar triangle has a shortest side of $9$. Find the length of its longest side.
answer: 15
check: 10 * 9 / 6
hint: Find the scale factor from the shortest sides.
solution: $k = 9/6 = 1.5$, so the longest side is $10 \times 1.5 = 15$.

@practice num
prompt: In $\triangle ABC$, point $D$ is on $\overline{AB}$ and point $E$ is on $\overline{AC}$, with $\overline{DE} \parallel \overline{BC}$. $AD = 4$, $DB = 6$ and $AE = 6$. Find $EC$.
answer: 9
check: 6 * 6 / 4
hint: A segment parallel to one side divides the other two sides proportionally.
hint: $\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$.
solution: $\dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{6}{EC}$, so $EC = \dfrac{6 \times 6}{4} = 9$.

choice*: The triangles are similar by AA.

@practice num
prompt: Two similar triangles have corresponding sides in the ratio $3 : 5$. The smaller triangle has area $18$. Find the area of the larger triangle.
answer: 50
check: 18 * 25 / 9
hint: Areas scale by the square of the ratio of lengths.
hint: The area ratio is $\dfrac{25}{9}$.
solution: $\text{Area} = 18 \times \left(\dfrac{5}{3}\right)^2 = 18 \times \dfrac{25}{9} = 50$.

@practice num
prompt: $\triangle ABC$ has $AB = 12$ and $AC = 15$. $\triangle DEF$ has $DE = 8$ and $DF = 10$, and $m\angle A = m\angle D$. Then $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ by SAS similarity. If $BC = 18$, find $EF$.
answer: 12
check: 18 * 8 / 12
hint: The scale factor from $\triangle ABC$ to $\triangle DEF$ is $8/12$.
solution: $k = \dfrac{8}{12} = \dfrac{2}{3}$, and $\dfrac{10}{15} = \dfrac{2}{3}$ confirms it. So $EF = 18 \times \dfrac{2}{3} = 12$.

@quiz num
prompt: A person $1.8$ m tall casts a shadow $2.4$ m long. At the same moment a building casts a shadow $40$ m long. How tall is the building, in meters?
answer: 30
check: 1.8 * 40 / 2.4
tol: 0.05
hint: Set up equal ratios of height to shadow length.
solution: $\dfrac{h}{40} = \dfrac{1.8}{2.4}$, so $h = 40 \times 0.75 = 30$ m.

@quiz mc
prompt: Two similar polygons have corresponding sides in the ratio $2 : 5$. What is the ratio of their areas?
choice: $2 : 5$
choice: $4 : 10$
choice*: $4 : 25$
choice: $8 : 125$
hint: Lengths scale by $k$; areas scale by a different power of $k$.
solution: Areas scale by $k^2$, so the ratio is $2^2 : 5^2 = 4 : 25$. The ratio $8 : 125$ would be for volumes.
