@lesson
title: Long division
summary: Divide bigger numbers into equal groups, handle remainders, and check each answer by multiplying and by estimating.
objective: Divide a two-digit or three-digit number by a one-digit number.
objective: Say what a remainder is and find it.
objective: Check a division answer by multiplying back and by estimating.
reading: Khan Academy: 5th grade math, look up "Multi-digit division" (dividing by one-digit and two-digit numbers).

@article
Division means sharing into equal groups. $12 \div 4 = 3$ means that $12$ things shared into $4$ equal groups gives $3$ in each group.

### Share the tens, then the ones

Let us share $84$ stickers among $4$ friends. Think of $84$ as $8$ tens and $4$ ones.

- Share the $8$ tens: each friend gets $2$ tens. That is $20$ each.
- Share the $4$ ones: each friend gets $1$ one.
- Each friend has $20 + 1 = 21$.

So $84 \div 4 = 21$.

### Remainders

Sometimes things do not share out evenly. Share $53$ crackers among $4$ children. Each child gets $13$ crackers. That uses $4 \times 13 = 52$ crackers. One cracker is left over. The leftover is the **remainder**. We write $53 \div 4 = 13$ remainder $1$.

The remainder is always smaller than the number you divide by. If it were bigger, you could give everyone one more.

### Multiplication checks division

Division and multiplication are partners. To check $84 \div 4 = 21$, multiply back: $21 \times 4 = 84$. It matches. With a remainder, multiply and then add the remainder: $13 \times 4 + 1 = 53$.

### Estimate to check

Round to numbers that divide easily. For $612 \div 3$, think $600 \div 3 = 200$. So the answer should be a bit more than $200$. If you got $20$ or $2{,}000$, you know to look again.

### Long division in steps

Long division is the sharing idea written down. Work from the left. Divide the biggest place first, multiply, subtract, and bring down the next digit. Say it as a song: **divide, multiply, subtract, bring down**.

@example
title: Divide 96 by 4 and check
problem: What is $96 \div 4$? Check by multiplying and by estimating.
step: Share the tens. $9$ tens shared among $4$ gives $2$ tens each, using $8$ tens. One ten is left. Trade it for $10$ ones.
step: Now there are $10 + 6 = 16$ ones. Share $16$ among $4$: $4$ each. So each group gets $2$ tens and $4$ ones, which is $24$.
step: Check by multiplying: $24 \times 4 = 96$. It matches.
step: Estimate: $100 \div 4 = 25$. Our answer, $24$, is close to $25$.

@mistakes
- Writing a remainder that is as big as, or bigger than, the number you divide by.
- Forgetting to bring down the next digit.
- Never checking, so a slip such as $96 \div 4 = 204$ is missed. An estimate shows that it is far too big.

@practice num
prompt: What is $84 \div 4$? Hint: share the tens first.
answer: 21
check: 84 / 4
hint: Split $84$ into $80$ and $4$.
hint: What is $80 \div 4$? What is $4 \div 4$? Add the two answers.
solution: $80 \div 4 = 20$ and $4 \div 4 = 1$. So $84 \div 4 = 21$. Check: $21 \times 4 = 84$.

@practice num
prompt: What is $156 \div 6$?
answer: 26
check: 156 / 6
hint: Try splitting $156$ into $120$ and $36$. Both are in the $6$ times table.
hint: $120 \div 6 = 20$. What is $36 \div 6$? Then add.
solution: $120 \div 6 = 20$ and $36 \div 6 = 6$. So $156 \div 6 = 26$. Check: $26 \times 6 = 156$.

@practice num
prompt: What is the remainder when $53$ is divided by $6$?
answer: 5
check: 53 % 6
hint: Find the biggest number in the $6$ times table that is not more than $53$.
hint: $6 \times 8 = 48$. How far is $48$ from $53$?
solution: $6 \times 8 = 48$, and $53 - 48 = 5$. The remainder is $5$, which is smaller than $6$.

@quiz num
prompt: What is $372 \div 6$?
answer: 62
check: 372 / 6
hint: Split $372$ into $360$ and $12$.
hint: $360 \div 6 = 60$. Now divide $12$ by $6$ and add.
solution: $360 \div 6 = 60$ and $12 \div 6 = 2$. So $372 \div 6 = 62$. Check: $62 \times 6 = 372$.
