@article
A **chemical reaction** rearranges atoms: bonds in the **reactants** break and new bonds form in the **products**. No atoms are created or destroyed, a statement known as the **law of conservation of mass**. A **balanced equation** shows this by having the same number of atoms of each element on both sides.

### Reading an equation

$$2\,\text{H}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g)\rightarrow 2\,\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$

The numbers in front are **coefficients** and give the ratio of moles (or molecules) that react. State symbols show the phase: $(s)$ solid, $(l)$ liquid, $(g)$ gas, $(aq)$ dissolved in water.

### Balancing

Change only coefficients, never subscripts; changing a subscript changes the substance. A reliable method:

1. Write correct formulas for all reactants and products.
2. Balance elements that appear in only one substance on each side first.
3. Balance elements that appear in several substances (often oxygen and hydrogen) near the end; if a coefficient is a fraction, multiply the whole equation to clear it.
4. Treat polyatomic ions that appear unchanged on both sides as units.
5. Recount every element, and confirm the coefficients have no common factor.

For hydrocarbon **combustion**, balance carbon (one $\text{CO}_2$ per carbon), then hydrogen (one $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ per two hydrogen), then count oxygen atoms on the product side and halve for $\text{O}_2$.

### Types of reaction

| Type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| combination (synthesis) | $\text{A}+\text{B}\rightarrow\text{AB}$ | $2\text{Na}+\text{Cl}_2\rightarrow2\text{NaCl}$ |
| decomposition | $\text{AB}\rightarrow\text{A}+\text{B}$ | $2\text{H}_2\text{O}_2\rightarrow2\text{H}_2\text{O}+\text{O}_2$ |
| single replacement | $\text{A}+\text{BC}\rightarrow\text{AC}+\text{B}$ | $\text{Zn}+2\text{HCl}\rightarrow\text{ZnCl}_2+\text{H}_2$ |
| double replacement | $\text{AB}+\text{CD}\rightarrow\text{AD}+\text{CB}$ | $\text{AgNO}_3+\text{NaCl}\rightarrow\text{AgCl}+\text{NaNO}_3$ |
| combustion | fuel $+\ \text{O}_2\rightarrow$ oxides | $\text{CH}_4+2\text{O}_2\rightarrow\text{CO}_2+2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ |

### Precipitation and net ionic equations

When two aqueous ionic solutions are mixed, a **precipitate** forms if a combination of ions is insoluble. A short version of the solubility rules: compounds of Group 1 ions, ammonium and nitrate are soluble; most chlorides, bromides and iodides are soluble except those of $\text{Ag}^+$, $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ and $\text{Hg}_2^{2+}$; most sulfates are soluble except those of $\text{Ba}^{2+}$, $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ and $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ (slightly); most carbonates, phosphates, hydroxides and sulfides are insoluble except those of Group 1 and ammonium.

To write a **net ionic equation**, write the **complete ionic equation** with soluble ionic compounds split into ions, then cancel the **spectator ions** that appear unchanged on both sides. Charge must balance, as well as atoms.

### Acid–base, redox and gas-forming reactions

Many other reactions fit these types. An acid and a base give a salt and water; a carbonate and an acid give a salt, water and carbon dioxide gas. In **redox** reactions electrons are transferred, and you can often see it as a change in oxidation number. Balancing procedures are the same: conserve atoms and charge.

@example
title: Balancing a combustion reaction
problem: Balance $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6+\text{O}_2\rightarrow\text{CO}_2+\text{H}_2\text{O}$ using the smallest whole numbers.
step: Balance carbon: each ethane gives two $\text{CO}_2$, so write $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6+\text{O}_2\rightarrow2\,\text{CO}_2+\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
step: Balance hydrogen: six H atoms give three $\text{H}_2\text{O}$: $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6+\text{O}_2\rightarrow2\,\text{CO}_2+3\,\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
step: Count oxygen on the right: $2(2)+3(1)=7$ atoms, which would need $\tfrac{7}{2}\ \text{O}_2$. Clear the fraction by doubling everything.
step: Result: $2\,\text{C}_2\text{H}_6+7\,\text{O}_2\rightarrow4\,\text{CO}_2+6\,\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Check: C $4=4$, H $12=12$, O $14=8+6$.

@example
title: A net ionic equation
problem: Write the net ionic equation for the reaction of aqueous $\text{BaCl}_2$ with aqueous $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$.
step: Predict the products by exchanging partners: $\text{BaSO}_4$ and $\text{NaCl}$. By the solubility rules, $\text{BaSO}_4$ is insoluble and $\text{NaCl}$ is soluble.
step: Complete ionic equation: $\text{Ba}^{2+}+2\text{Cl}^{-}+2\text{Na}^{+}+\text{SO}_4^{2-}\rightarrow\text{BaSO}_4(s)+2\text{Na}^{+}+2\text{Cl}^{-}$.
step: Cancel the spectator ions $\text{Na}^{+}$ and $\text{Cl}^{-}$.
step: Net ionic equation: $\text{Ba}^{2+}(aq)+\text{SO}_4^{2-}(aq)\rightarrow\text{BaSO}_4(s)$. Charge is $0$ on both sides.

@mistakes
- Changing subscripts to balance (turning $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ into $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ is a different compound).
- Leaving a fractional coefficient or a set of coefficients with a common factor.
- Splitting solids, liquids, gases and weak electrolytes into ions in a net ionic equation.
- Forgetting to balance charge as well as atoms in an ionic equation.

answer: 5
check: (3 * 2 + 4 * 1) / 2
hint: Balance C to get three $\text{CO}_2$ and H to get four $\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

@practice num
prompt: Balance $\text{Fe}+\text{O}_2\rightarrow\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ using the smallest whole-number coefficients. What is the sum of all the coefficients?
answer: 9
check: 4 + 3 + 2
hint: Oxygen is easiest with six atoms on each side: three $\text{O}_2$ and two $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$.
hint: Two $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ contain four iron atoms.
solution: $4\,\text{Fe}+3\,\text{O}_2\rightarrow2\,\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ has $4$ Fe and $6$ O on each side. The coefficients sum to $4+3+2=9$.

@quiz num
prompt: Balance $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}+\text{O}_2\rightarrow\text{CO}_2+\text{H}_2\text{O}$ with the smallest whole-number coefficients. What is the coefficient of $\text{O}_2$?
answer: 13
check: (8 * 2 + 10) / 2
hint: Start with two butane molecules so that hydrogen balances without fractions.
hint: $2\,\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}$ gives $8\,\text{CO}_2$ and $10\,\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Count oxygen atoms on the right.
solution: $2\,\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}+13\,\text{O}_2\rightarrow8\,\text{CO}_2+10\,\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Oxygen on the right is $8(2)+10=26$ atoms, so $13\,\text{O}_2$.
