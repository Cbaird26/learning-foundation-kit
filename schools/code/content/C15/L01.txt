@article
A **vector** in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is an ordered list of $n$ real numbers, written $v = (v_1, v_2, \dots, v_n)$ or as a column. Geometrically it is an arrow from the origin to a point; computationally it is just a list. In Python, a vector is a list of numbers, and everything in this course can be built from that.

### The two basic operations

You add vectors **componentwise** and scale them by multiplying every component:

$$u + v = (u_1 + v_1, \dots, u_n + v_n), \qquad c\,v = (c\,v_1, \dots, c\,v_n).$$

Addition needs vectors of the same length. A **linear combination** of $v_1, \dots, v_k$ is any vector $c_1 v_1 + \dots + c_k v_k$. Linear combinations are the central idea of the whole subject: solving a linear system will turn out to mean asking whether a vector is a linear combination of given vectors.

```python
def add(u, v):
    return [a + b for a, b in zip(u, v)]

def scale(c, v):
    return [c * a for a in v]

print(add([1, 2, 3], scale(2, [1, 0, -1])))  # [3, 2, 1]
```

### The dot product

The **dot product** of $u, v \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the *number*

$$u \cdot v = u_1 v_1 + u_2 v_2 + \dots + u_n v_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} u_i v_i.$$

It is symmetric ($u \cdot v = v \cdot u$), linear in each argument ($(cu + w) \cdot v = c\,(u \cdot v) + w \cdot v$), and $v \cdot v \ge 0$ with equality only for $v = 0$. In code it is `sum(a * b for a, b in zip(u, v))`.

### Length and unit vectors

The **norm** (length) of $v$ is $\|v\| = \sqrt{v \cdot v} = \sqrt{v_1^2 + \dots + v_n^2}$, which is the Pythagorean theorem in $n$ dimensions. A **unit vector** has norm $1$. Any nonzero $v$ can be **normalized**: $v / \|v\|$ points the same way and has length $1$.

### Angles and orthogonality

For nonzero $u$ and $v$ with angle $\theta$ between them,

$$u \cdot v = \|u\|\,\|v\|\cos\theta, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \cos\theta = \frac{u \cdot v}{\|u\|\,\|v\|}.$$

The right-hand side always lies in $[-1, 1]$; this is the **Cauchy–Schwarz inequality**, $|u \cdot v| \le \|u\|\,\|v\|$. It also gives the **triangle inequality** $\|u + v\| \le \|u\| + \|v\|$. Two vectors are **orthogonal** (perpendicular) exactly when $u \cdot v = 0$, because then $\cos\theta = 0$. The zero vector is orthogonal to everything.

A useful identity, from expanding $(u+v)\cdot(u+v)$:

$$\|u + v\|^2 = \|u\|^2 + 2\,u \cdot v + \|v\|^2.$$

When $u \cdot v = 0$ this is the Pythagorean theorem. The quantity $\cos\theta$ is called **cosine similarity** in data science, where documents or users are vectors of counts or ratings and a value near $1$ means they point in nearly the same direction.

@example
title: Angle between two vectors
problem: Find the angle between $u = (1, 1, 0)$ and $v = (0, 1, 1)$.
step: Compute the dot product: $u \cdot v = 1\cdot 0 + 1 \cdot 1 + 0 \cdot 1 = 1$.
step: Compute the norms: $\|u\| = \sqrt{1 + 1 + 0} = \sqrt{2}$ and $\|v\| = \sqrt{0 + 1 + 1} = \sqrt{2}$.
step: Substitute into the formula: $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt2 \cdot \sqrt2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
step: The angle in $[0, \pi]$ with cosine $\tfrac12$ is $\theta = \pi/3$, which is $60^\circ$.
answer: $\theta = \pi/3$

@example
title: Normalizing a vector and checking orthogonality
problem: Normalize $w = (2, -1, 2)$, then decide whether $w$ is orthogonal to $p = (1, 4, 1)$.
step: The norm is $\|w\| = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3$.
step: Divide every component by $3$: $w / \|w\| = (2/3, -1/3, 2/3)$.
step: Check the result has length $1$: $\tfrac49 + \tfrac19 + \tfrac49 = 1$.
step: Orthogonality test: $w \cdot p = 2\cdot 1 + (-1)\cdot 4 + 2 \cdot 1 = 0$, so $w$ and $p$ are orthogonal.
answer: $(2/3, -1/3, 2/3)$; yes, they are orthogonal.

@mistakes
- Treating $u \cdot v$ as a vector. The dot product of two vectors is a single number.
- Forgetting the square root: $\|v\|$ is $\sqrt{v \cdot v}$, not $v \cdot v$.
- Believing $\|u + v\| = \|u\| + \|v\|$. Equality holds only when the vectors point the same way; in general $\|u+v\| \le \|u\| + \|v\|$.
- Concluding $u = 0$ or $v = 0$ from $u \cdot v = 0$. Nonzero vectors can be orthogonal, such as $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$.

def dot(u, v):

assert dot([1, 2, 3], [4, 5, 6]) == 32

model:
```python
def dot(u, v):
    if len(u) != len(v):
        raise ValueError("vectors must have the same length")
    return sum(a * b for a, b in zip(u, v))
```
hint: `zip(u, v)` pairs up matching components.
hint: Check the lengths first, because `zip` silently stops at the shorter list.
solution: Compare the lengths and raise `ValueError` if they differ, then sum the products of paired components. `sum` of an empty generator is `0`, which handles the empty case.

@practice mc
prompt: Which statement about vectors $u, v \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is true for all choices?
choice*: $u \cdot v = v \cdot u$
choice: $\|u + v\| = \|u\| + \|v\|$
choice: If $u \cdot v = 0$ then $u = 0$ or $v = 0$.
choice: $u \cdot v$ is a vector with $n$ components.
hint: Test each statement with $u = (1, 0)$ and $v = (0, 1)$.
solution: The dot product is symmetric because each product $u_i v_i$ equals $v_i u_i$. The others fail for $u = (1,0)$, $v = (0,1)$: the dot product is $0$ though neither is zero, and $\|u+v\| = \sqrt2 \ne 2$. The dot product is a number, not a vector.

model:
```python
import math

def cosine_similarity(u, v):
    d = sum(a * b for a, b in zip(u, v))
    nu = math.sqrt(sum(a * a for a in u))
    nv = math.sqrt(sum(b * b for b in v))
    return d / (nu * nv)
```
hint: You need the dot product and the two norms.
hint: A norm is the square root of the vector's dot product with itself.
solution: Compute `d = u . v`, then the two norms with `math.sqrt` of the sums of squares, and return `d / (nu * nv)`. For `[3, 4]` and `[4, 3]` this is `24 / 25 = 0.96`.

@quiz num
prompt: Vectors $u$ and $v$ satisfy $\|u\| = 3$, $\|v\| = 4$ and $u \cdot v = 6$. Find $\|u - v\|$. Give an exact value.
answer: sqrt(13)
check: sqrt(3**2 + 4**2 - 2 * 6)
hint: Expand $(u - v)\cdot(u - v)$ using linearity of the dot product.
solution: $\|u - v\|^2 = \|u\|^2 - 2\,u\cdot v + \|v\|^2 = 9 - 12 + 16 = 13$, so $\|u - v\| = \sqrt{13}$.
