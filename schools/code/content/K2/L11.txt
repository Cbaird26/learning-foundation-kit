@article
Two words help us talk about flat shapes: **area** and **perimeter**.

### Area: the space inside

**Area** is how much flat space is inside a shape. We measure it by counting **square units**, which are small squares that are all the same size.

Picture a rectangle on grid paper that is 5 squares wide and 3 squares tall. There are 3 rows with 5 squares in each row. That is an array! So the area is

$$3 \times 5 = 15 \text{ square units.}$$

### Perimeter: the way around

**Perimeter** is the distance around the outside of a shape. Imagine walking along the edge of a garden. To find the perimeter, add the lengths of all the sides.

The rectangle that is 5 wide and 3 tall has sides 5, 3, 5 and 3. So the perimeter is

$$5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16 \text{ units.}$$

### A square

A square has 4 sides that are all the same length. A square with sides of 4 has a perimeter of $4 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 16$ units. Its area is $4 \times 4 = 16$ square units. The numbers match here, but area and perimeter are different things.

### Do not mix them up

- Area counts squares **inside**. It is measured in **square** units.
- Perimeter measures the **edge**. It is measured in plain units.

A trick: **p**erimeter goes around the **p**ath on the outside.

@example
title: Area and perimeter of a rectangle
problem: A rectangle on grid paper is 5 squares wide and 3 squares tall. Find its area and its perimeter.
step: Area: there are 3 rows with 5 squares in each row. $3 \times 5 = 15$ square units.
step: Perimeter: add the four sides. $5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16$ units.
step: The area is 15 square units and the perimeter is 16 units.

@mistakes
- Adding only two sides of the rectangle and forgetting the other two.
- Using the area numbers when the question asks about perimeter, or the reverse.
- Forgetting that area is counted in square units.
