@review
### Week by week

1. **Theme and character**: a theme is a full sentence about life; trace change with early and late evidence.
2. **Central idea**: the author's main point about the topic; separate key from minor details; summaries are objective.
3. **Structure and purpose**: description, sequence, compare/contrast, cause/effect, problem/solution; purposes persuade, inform, entertain, explain.
4. **Point of view and tone**: first person, third limited, third omniscient; tone comes from diction, details and rhythm.
5. **Arguments and evidence**: claim, reasons, evidence; test for relevance, sufficiency and credibility; know common fallacies.
6. **Figurative language**: simile, metaphor, personification, hyperbole, idiom; explain the effect; denotation versus connotation.
7. **Grammar**: phrases versus clauses; simple, compound, complex, compound-complex sentences; fix fragments, run-ons and comma splices.
8. **Vocabulary**: prefixes, roots, suffixes and context clues; check meaning by substitution.
9. **Narrative**: plot structure, show don't tell, dialogue punctuation, an earned resolution.
10. **Informative**: thesis, topic sentences, details, transitions, formal tone.
11. **Argument**: arguable claim, evidence plus reasoning, counterclaim and rebuttal.
12. **Research**: credible sources, paraphrase versus quotation, citation, a three-minute presentation of about 390 words.

### Self-check

- I can state a theme as a sentence and prove it with two moments.
- I can write a central idea and an objective summary.
- I can name a structure and explain how it serves the author's purpose.
- I can judge evidence as relevant, sufficient and credible.
- I can classify a sentence and fix a fragment, run-on or comma splice.
- I can cite a source and avoid plagiarism.

### Common errors

- Writing a topic or a command instead of a theme.
- Naming a figure of speech but not its effect.
- Confusing tone with mood, or point of view with opinion.
- Giving evidence without reasoning, or ignoring the counterclaim.
- Copying a source and changing only a few words.

@exam self
prompt: Read: "The lake was a mirror at dawn. I, Ana, stood at the edge, certain I would never swim again. Then my brother handed me his goggles and said, 'I'll stay right here.' I stepped into the cold water." Name the point of view, trace how Ana changes, state a theme, and identify one figure of speech with its effect.
hint: Answer in four parts: point of view, change, theme sentence, figure of speech and effect.
solution: Point of view: first person (the narrator uses "I"). Change: Ana moves from certain she will never swim again to stepping into the water after her brother's support. Theme: Fear can be faced when someone offers steady support. Figure of speech: the metaphor "The lake was a mirror" shows how calm and still the water is, which sets a quiet, hopeful tone.
rubric: Correctly names first person and gives evidence.
rubric: Describes an early and a late state for Ana with evidence.
rubric: States a theme as a full sentence, and explains the effect of the metaphor.
