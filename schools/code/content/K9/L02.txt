@article
A **rule** tells us what to do or not to do. Families and schools have rules. A **law** is a rule for a whole community, state or country. Laws are made by leaders the people choose.

### Why do we have rules?

Rules help keep people safe. They help things be fair. A stop sign is a rule: cars must stop. If there were no rule, people could get hurt. A rule at school such as "take turns on the slide" helps everyone have a turn.

### Rights and responsibilities

A **right** is something people are free to do or have. A **responsibility** is a job you should do. A child has a right to go to school. A child has a responsibility to listen and be kind.

### Citizens

A **citizen** is a member of a country. A person can be a citizen by being born in the country, or by learning about it and taking a special oath. Good citizens follow laws, help others, tell the truth and take care of shared places. Grown-up citizens may vote to choose leaders.

### Community helpers

**Community helpers** do jobs that help everyone.

- A firefighter puts out fires.
- A police officer helps keep people safe.
- A doctor or nurse helps sick people get well.
- A teacher helps you learn.
- A mail carrier brings letters and packages.
- A garbage collector keeps streets clean.

### Try this

Walk around your block with a grown-up. Draw a map of what you see and mark where helpers work, like a school or a fire station.

@example
title: Why is this a rule?
problem: A pool has a rule: "No running." Why?
step: Think about a wet floor. It is slippery.
step: Running on a slippery floor can make people fall and get hurt.
step: The rule helps keep people safe.

@example
title: Who helps?
problem: A kitten is stuck in a tree and a ladder is needed. Which helper might come?
step: Think about who uses ladders and trucks to help.
step: A firefighter has a ladder and helps in many kinds of emergencies.
step: So a firefighter might come.

@mistakes
- Thinking rules are only to be bossy. Rules help keep people safe and treat people fairly.
- Mixing up rights and responsibilities. A right is what you may do; a responsibility is a job you should do.
- Thinking only grown-ups can be good citizens. Kids can help, be kind and take care of places too.
