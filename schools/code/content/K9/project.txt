@project
title: Community history project
overview: With your grown-up, learn how your town or neighborhood has changed. You will interview a family member or neighbor, make a map of the town, and put what you learn on a timeline poster.
goal: Ask good questions and listen to someone's story about the community.
goal: Make a map with a title, key and compass rose.
goal: Put events in order on a timeline.
step: Pick a person to interview, such as a grandparent, a neighbor or an older friend. Ask your grown-up to set a time.
step: Write 5 questions, such as "What was our town like when you were a child?" and "What places are new? What places are gone?"
step: Do the interview. Your grown-up can help you write or record the answers.
step: Draw a map of your town or neighborhood. Add a title, a map key and a compass rose, and mark 3 places and 2 helpers.
step: Pick 4 to 6 events from the interview or from what you learned, and write the year for each.
step: Draw a timeline poster. Put the events in order from the earliest year to the latest, with a small picture for each.
step: Show your poster to your family. Tell one thing that changed and one thing that stayed the same.
deliverable: A map of your community with a title, key and compass rose.
deliverable: A timeline poster with at least 4 dated events in order.
deliverable: A short talk or page about what you learned from your interview.
rubric: The interview has at least 3 questions and answers.
rubric: The map has a title, key and compass rose.
rubric: The timeline events are in the right order.
rubric: The learner tells what changed and what stayed the same.
safety: Do the interview with a grown-up present, and only with people your grown-up knows. Do not share addresses or private information on the poster.
tutor: How can I ask a question that helps someone tell a longer story?
tutor: How do I decide which events to put on my timeline?
tutor: How do I show north on my map?

@review
### Week by week

1. **Families and communities:** Families come in many shapes. A community is people and shared places.
2. **Rules and helpers:** Rules keep us safe and fair. Citizens have rights and responsibilities. Helpers do jobs for us.
3. **Maps and globes:** Maps use a title, key and compass rose. A globe is round like the Earth.
4. **Landforms:** There are 7 continents and 5 oceans.
5. **Needs and wants:** Needs come first. A budget is a plan for money.
6. **Government:** Mayors and councils work locally. Governors and legislatures work for states.
7. **U.S. geography:** 50 states and five regions.
8. **Native nations:** Many nations with different ways of life, still here today.
9. **Colonies:** Jamestown 1607 and Plymouth 1620. Native, colonist and enslaved people saw events differently.
10. **Revolution:** Declaration of Independence in 1776, war ended in 1783.
11. **Constitution:** Three branches and the Bill of Rights.
12. **Growing nation:** The Civil War lasted 1861 to 1865 and the civil rights movement worked for fairness.

### Self-check

- Can I name the 7 continents and 5 oceans?
- Can I tell a need from a want?
- Can I tell who makes local rules?
- Can I say one thing about two Native nations?
- Can I put events in order on a timeline?

### Common errors

Mixing up a mayor and a governor, leaving out Native peoples or enslaved people from the story, and mixing up the order of years.
