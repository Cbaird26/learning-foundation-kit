@article
**Research** means finding facts to answer a question. Here is how to do it.

### Step 1: Ask a question

A good research question needs more than a yes or no. It often starts with *How, Why* or *What.* Example: "How does the heart pump blood?"

### Step 2: Find sources

A **source** is where you get facts, such as a book, an encyclopedia or a trusted website. Use **two sources** so you can check that the facts match.

Good sources are made for learning, have an author or a group named, and agree with other sources. Ask your grown-up or librarian to help you pick.

### Step 3: Take notes

**Notes** are short. You do not copy sentences. You write the key words or facts **in your own words**.

Say you read: "The heart is a muscle about the size of your fist. It pumps blood all around the body."

Notes:
- heart = muscle
- size of fist
- pumps blood

### Keep track

Write the source name next to each note. Then you know where each fact came from.

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| heart is a muscle | Book 1 |
| size of a fist | Book 1 |
| pumps blood | Website 2 |

### Why own words?

Copying words from a source and saying they are yours is wrong. It is called plagiarism. Writing notes in your own words helps you understand the facts, too.

### Check

Do both sources agree? If not, look at a third source or ask your grown-up.

@example
title: Take notes
problem: You read: "Your lungs fill with air when you breathe in. They are like two soft pink sponges." Write two short notes.
step: Find the key facts. Lungs fill with air when you breathe in. Lungs are soft and pink.
step: Write short notes in your own words: "lungs hold air" and "soft, pink."
step: Add the source next to each note, such as Book 1.

@mistakes
- Copying whole sentences instead of writing short notes.
- Forgetting to write which source a fact came from.
- Asking a question that can be answered with only yes or no.
