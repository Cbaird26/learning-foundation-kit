@article
A **story** has characters, a problem and an ending. When you finish a story, two good questions are: "What happened?" and "What did the story want me to learn?"

### Summary

A **summary** tells the most important parts of a story in your own words. It is short. It leaves out small details, like the color of a hat or what someone ate for lunch.

A good summary has three parts:

- **Beginning:** Who is in the story, and what is the problem?
- **Middle:** What does the character do to try to fix it?
- **End:** How does it turn out?

Here is a tiny story. "Mia's kite got stuck in a tree. She jumped, but she could not reach it. Then she asked her big brother to help. He lifted her up, and she got the kite back."

A summary: "Mia's kite got stuck in a tree. She could not reach it alone, so her brother helped her. She got the kite back."

### Theme

The **theme** is the big message of a story. It is a lesson that could be true for people in real life. A theme is not one word like "kites." It is a whole idea, like "Asking for help can solve a problem."

To find the theme, ask:

1. What was the problem?
2. What did the character learn or change?
3. What lesson could I use in my own life?

### Topic or theme?

The **topic** is what the story is about, such as friends or kites. The theme is what the story says about it, such as "Good friends share."

### Remember

Use your own words. Keep it short. Tell the big events in order.

@example
title: Find the summary and the theme
problem: Read: "Ben was scared to swim in the deep end. Each day he practiced in the shallow end. His sister cheered for him. At last he swam across the deep end. He smiled." What is the summary and the theme?
step: Find the problem. Ben was scared of the deep end.
step: Find what he did. He practiced a little each day, and his sister cheered.
step: Write the summary: "Ben was scared of the deep end, but he practiced every day. In the end he swam across."
step: Find the lesson: "If you keep practicing, you can do things that scare you." That is the theme.

@mistakes
- Putting in too many small details, so the summary is as long as the story.
- Writing only a topic, like "swimming," when the question asks for the theme.
- Telling the events out of order, with the end before the beginning.
