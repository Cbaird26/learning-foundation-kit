@article
Many curves are not graphs of functions $y=f(x)$: a circle fails the vertical line test, and a path traced over time may revisit points. Two systems fix this.

### Parametric curves

A **parametric curve** gives both coordinates as functions of a parameter $t$ (often time): $x=x(t)$, $y=y(t)$, for $t$ in an interval. The circle of radius $r$ is $x=r\cos t$, $y=r\sin t$ for $0\le t\le2\pi$. The parametrization also records the *direction* and speed of travel.

**Slope.** By the chain rule, $\frac{dy}{dt}=\frac{dy}{dx}\frac{dx}{dt}$, so where $\frac{dx}{dt}\ne0$,

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}.$$

The tangent is horizontal where $\frac{dy}{dt}=0$ and $\frac{dx}{dt}\ne0$, and vertical where $\frac{dx}{dt}=0$ and $\frac{dy}{dt}\ne0$. If both vanish, the point needs more analysis. For the second derivative differentiate $\frac{dy}{dx}$ with respect to $t$ and divide again by $\frac{dx}{dt}$:

$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\frac{\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)}{dx/dt}.$$

**Arc length.** A short piece of the curve has length $\sqrt{(dx)^2+(dy)^2}$, so

$$L=\int_\alpha^\beta\sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2+\left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}\,dt,$$

provided the curve is traced exactly once. (The integral of the speed counts a retraced part twice.) This generalizes the formula of Week 5, which is the case $x=t$.

### Polar coordinates

A point is located by its distance $r$ from the origin and the angle $\theta$ from the positive $x$-axis:

$$x=r\cos\theta,\quad y=r\sin\theta,\qquad r^2=x^2+y^2,\quad\tan\theta=\frac yx\ (x\ne0).$$

A point has many polar representations, since $(r,\theta)$ and $(r,\theta+2\pi)$ coincide, as do $(r,\theta)$ and $(-r,\theta+\pi)$. When converting from Cartesian coordinates, choose the quadrant of $\theta$ from the signs of $x$ and $y$ (use `atan2` in programs), because $\tan\theta=y/x$ alone does not determine it.

Useful curves: the circle $r=a$; the circle $r=2a\cos\theta$ through the origin with center $(a,0)$; the **cardioid** $r=a(1+\cos\theta)$; the **rose** $r=a\cos n\theta$ or $r=a\sin n\theta$, which has $n$ petals for odd $n$ and $2n$ petals for even $n$; the spiral $r=\theta$.

**Area.** A thin sector of angle $d\theta$ and radius $r$ has area $\frac12r^2\,d\theta$, so the region swept by the curve $r=r(\theta)$ for $\alpha\le\theta\le\beta$ (with $\beta-\alpha\le2\pi$ and $r$ not retracing) has area

$$A=\frac12\int_\alpha^\beta r(\theta)^2\,d\theta.$$

Be careful with limits: the region inside $r=2\cos\theta$ is covered once for $-\frac\pi2\le\theta\le\frac\pi2$; integrating over $[0,2\pi]$ would count it twice.

**Arc length.** With $x=r\cos\theta$ and $y=r\sin\theta$ as parametric functions of $\theta$, the formula becomes $L=\int\sqrt{r^2+\left(\frac{dr}{d\theta}\right)^2}\,d\theta$.

### Computing areas numerically

The midpoint rule for $\frac12\int r^2\,d\theta$ adds the areas of $n$ thin sectors, each with radius $r(m_i)$ at the middle angle $m_i$. For smooth periodic integrands over a full period (such as a cardioid) this rule converges especially fast.

@example
title: Slope and concavity of a parametric curve
problem: For $x=t^2$, $y=t^3$ find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ at $t=1$.
step: $\frac{dx}{dt}=2t$ and $\frac{dy}{dt}=3t^2$, so $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{3t^2}{2t}=\frac{3t}{2}$ for $t\ne0$.
step: At $t=1$ the slope is $\frac32$.
step: Differentiate the slope with respect to $t$: $\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{3t}2\right)=\frac32$.
step: Divide by $\frac{dx}{dt}=2t$: $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\frac{3/2}{2t}=\frac3{4t}$, which equals $\frac34$ at $t=1$.
step: The positive second derivative means the curve is concave up there.
answer: slope $\frac32$ and $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\frac34$

@example
title: Area inside a polar circle
problem: Find the area enclosed by $r=2\cos\theta$.
step: The curve is traced once for $-\frac\pi2\le\theta\le\frac\pi2$ (where $r\ge0$).
step: $A=\frac12\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}4\cos^2\theta\,d\theta=2\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^2\theta\,d\theta$.
step: Use $\cos^2\theta=\frac{1+\cos2\theta}2$: the integral is $\left[\frac\theta2+\frac{\sin2\theta}4\right]_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}=\frac\pi2$.
step: So $A=2\cdot\frac\pi2=\pi$, which matches the circle of radius $1$ (its Cartesian form is $(x-1)^2+y^2=1$).
answer: $\pi$

@mistakes
- Writing $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dx/dt}{dy/dt}$ (inverted).
- Computing $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ as $\frac{d^2y/dt^2}{d^2x/dt^2}$; instead differentiate $\frac{dy}{dx}$ with respect to $t$ and divide by $\frac{dx}{dt}$.
- Forgetting the $\frac12$ in the polar area formula, or squaring incorrectly: it is $r^2$, not $(r\,d\theta)^2$.
- Integrating over too many angles, so that a region is counted twice (a rose or cardioid traced more than once).

answer: -2/3

hint: Compute $\frac{dx}{dt}$ and $\frac{dy}{dt}$ and divide.
hint: $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{2\cos t}{-3\sin t}$, and $\cos\frac\pi4=\sin\frac\pi4$.

@practice num
prompt: Find the length of one arch of the cycloid $x=t-\sin t$, $y=1-\cos t$ for $0\le t\le2\pi$.
answer: 8
check: quad(lambda t: math.sqrt((1 - math.cos(t))**2 + math.sin(t)**2), 0, 2*pi)
hint: The integrand is $\sqrt{(1-\cos t)^2+\sin^2t}=\sqrt{2-2\cos t}$.
hint: Use $1-\cos t=2\sin^2\frac t2$ so that $\sqrt{2-2\cos t}=2\sin\frac t2$ for $0\le t\le2\pi$.
solution: $L=\int_0^{2\pi}2\sin\frac t2\,dt=\left[-4\cos\frac t2\right]_0^{2\pi}=4+4=8$.

prompt: Write `polar_area(r, a, b, n)` that approximates the area $\frac12\int_a^b r(\theta)^2\,d\theta$ of the region swept by the polar curve `r(theta)` for `a <= theta <= b` using the midpoint rule with `n` equal subintervals of width `h = (b - a) / n`: the sum of `0.5 * r(m) ** 2 * h` over the midpoints `m`.
starter:
```python
def polar_area(r, a, b, n):
    pass
```
tests:
```python
import math
assert abs(polar_area(lambda t: 2, 0, 2 * math.pi, 1) - 4 * math.pi) < 1e-9
assert abs(polar_area(lambda t: t, 0, 2, 1) - 1.0) < 1e-12
assert abs(polar_area(lambda t: 1 + math.cos(t), 0, 2 * math.pi, 200)
           - 1.5 * math.pi) < 1e-6
assert abs(polar_area(lambda t: 2 * math.sin(2 * t), 0, 2 * math.pi, 200)
           - 2 * math.pi) < 1e-6
assert abs(polar_area(lambda t: 3, 1.0, 1.0, 5)) < 1e-12
```
model:
```python
def polar_area(r, a, b, n):
    h = (b - a) / n
    total = 0.0
    for i in range(n):
        m = a + (i + 0.5) * h
        total += 0.5 * r(m) ** 2 * h
    return total
```
hint: The midpoint of the $i$th subinterval (from $i=0$) is `a + (i + 0.5) * h`.
hint: Add `0.5 * r(m) ** 2 * h` for each subinterval and return the sum.
solution: Each sector has area `0.5 * r(m)**2 * h`. With `r = t` on `[0, 2]` and one subinterval, the midpoint is `1`, so the area estimate is `0.5 * 1 * 2 = 1` (the exact value is 4/3). For a full period of a smooth periodic function the midpoint rule is far more accurate, which the cardioid and rose tests show.

@quiz num
prompt: Find the total area enclosed by the four-petal rose $r=2\sin2\theta$, $0\le\theta\le2\pi$. Give an exact value.
answer: 2pi
check: quad(lambda t: 0.5 * (2 * math.sin(2 * t))**2, 0, 2*pi)
hint: $A=\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}4\sin^22\theta\,d\theta$; use the half-angle identity.
