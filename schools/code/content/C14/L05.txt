@article
Every application in this lesson uses one idea: **slice, approximate each slice by something simple, add, and take a limit**. The limit of a Riemann sum is a definite integral, so the integral is the exact total. Your job is to choose the slice and write its size.

### Area between curves

If $f(x)\ge g(x)$ on $[a,b]$, the area between the graphs is

$$A=\int_a^b\big(f(x)-g(x)\big)\,dx.$$

Find the limits by solving $f(x)=g(x)$. If the curves cross, split the interval at each crossing so that the integrand is always "top minus bottom", or integrate $|f-g|$. Sometimes slicing horizontally, with $A=\int_c^d(\text{right}-\text{left})\,dy$, is simpler.

### Volumes by slicing

If a solid has cross-sectional area $A(x)$ at position $x$, its volume is $V=\int_a^bA(x)\,dx$. For a solid of revolution about the $x$-axis the slices are circular:

- **Disks:** rotating the region under $y=f(x)$ on $[a,b]$ gives $V=\pi\int_a^b f(x)^2\,dx$.
- **Washers:** rotating the region between $y=f(x)\ge g(x)\ge0$ gives $V=\pi\int_a^b\big(f(x)^2-g(x)^2\big)\,dx$. Note that you subtract the *squares*, not the square of the difference.

### Cylindrical shells

Rotating the region under $y=f(x)$, $a\le x\le b$ (with $a\ge0$) about the **$y$-axis**, you can use thin cylindrical shells of radius $x$, height $f(x)$ and thickness $dx$:

$$V=2\pi\int_a^b x\,f(x)\,dx.$$

Shells are convenient when slicing perpendicular to the axis would give washers whose radii require solving for $x$ in terms of $y$.

### Arc length

For a function $f$ with continuous derivative on $[a,b]$, the length of its graph is

$$L=\int_a^b\sqrt{1+\big(f'(x)\big)^2}\,dx.$$

It comes from approximating the curve by short segments of length $\sqrt{(\Delta x)^2+(\Delta y)^2}=\sqrt{1+(\Delta y/\Delta x)^2}\,\Delta x$. The integrand usually has no elementary antiderivative; the exercises in this course are chosen so that $1+(f')^2$ is a perfect square, or numerical integration is used.

### A numerical view

Each formula above is an integral of a known function, so it can be approximated with the rules of Week 3. For example, the midpoint rule applied to $\pi f(x)^2$ approximates a volume of revolution by $n$ thin cylinders: the volume of each cylinder is $\pi\,f(m_i)^2\,h$, where $m_i$ is the midpoint of the $i$th subinterval.

@example
title: Volume of a solid of revolution
problem: The region under $y=\sqrt x$ for $0\le x\le4$ is rotated about the $x$-axis. Find the volume.
step: The cross-sections are disks with radius $\sqrt x$, so the area at $x$ is $\pi(\sqrt x)^2=\pi x$.
step: $V=\pi\displaystyle\int_0^4x\,dx$.
step: $\displaystyle\int_0^4x\,dx=\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^4=8$.
step: So $V=8\pi$.
answer: $8\pi$

@example
title: An arc length with a perfect square
problem: Find the length of $y=\tfrac23x^{3/2}$ for $0\le x\le3$.
step: $f'(x)=x^{1/2}$, so $1+(f')^2=1+x$.
step: $L=\displaystyle\int_0^3\sqrt{1+x}\,dx$.
step: Substitute $u=1+x$ (limits $1$ to $4$): $\displaystyle\int_1^4u^{1/2}du=\left[\tfrac23u^{3/2}\right]_1^4$.
step: $\tfrac23(8-1)=\dfrac{14}{3}$.
answer: $\frac{14}{3}$

@mistakes
- Writing the washer integrand as $\pi(f-g)^2$ instead of $\pi(f^2-g^2)$.
- Forgetting the factor $\pi$ (disks, washers) or $2\pi$ (shells).
- Using the shell formula with a radius of $f(x)$ and height $x$ (swapped), or using $dx$ when slicing in $y$.
- Putting the curves in the wrong order, giving a negative area; the integrand must be top minus bottom.

@practice num
prompt: Find the length of the curve $y=\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{\ln x}{4}$ for $1\le x\le2$. Give an exact value.
answer: 3/2 + ln(2)/4
check: quad(lambda x: math.sqrt(1 + (x - 1/(4*x))**2), 1, 2)
hint: Compute $f'(x)=x-\frac{1}{4x}$ and expand $1+(f')^2$.

solution: $L=\int_1^2\left(x+\frac1{4x}\right)dx=\left[\frac{x^2}2+\frac{\ln x}{4}\right]_1^2=\left(2-\frac12\right)+\frac{\ln2}{4}=\frac32+\frac{\ln2}4$.

import math
assert abs(volume_disk(lambda x: 2, 0, 5, 1) - 20 * math.pi) < 1e-9
assert abs(volume_disk(lambda x: x, 0, 3, 3) - 8.75 * math.pi) < 1e-9

model:
```python
import math


def volume_disk(f, a, b, n):
    h = (b - a) / n
    total = 0.0
    for i in range(n):
        m = a + (i + 0.5) * h
        total += math.pi * f(m) ** 2 * h
    return total
```
hint: The midpoint of the $i$th subinterval (counting from $0$) is `a + (i + 0.5) * h`.
hint: Add `pi * f(m) ** 2 * h` for each subinterval and return the total.
solution: Each subinterval contributes a thin cylinder of volume `pi * f(m)**2 * h`. For `f(x) = x` on `[0, 3]` with three pieces the midpoints are 0.5, 1.5, 2.5, so the total is 8.75 pi, close to the exact 9 pi.

@quiz num
prompt: Find the area between $y=\cos x$ and $y=\sin x$ for $0\le x\le\frac\pi4$. Give an exact value.
answer: sqrt(2) - 1
check: quad(lambda x: math.cos(x) - math.sin(x), 0, pi/4)
hint: On $[0,\pi/4]$, $\cos x\ge\sin x$.
hint: An antiderivative of $\cos x-\sin x$ is $\sin x+\cos x$.
