@article
If you build an interface, some of your users will be blind or have low vision, some will be deaf or hard of hearing, some will have limited use of their hands, some will be dyslexic or easily overwhelmed by motion, and almost everyone will at some point be using a phone in bright sunlight with one hand. Accessibility makes software usable for all of them, and it is often a legal requirement for public bodies and businesses in many places. People and products here are **fictional**.

### WCAG 2.2 in practice

The **Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2**, a W3C recommendation published in 2023, organizes accessibility into four principles, often remembered as **POUR**:

- **Perceivable**: information can be perceived (text alternatives for images, captions, sufficient contrast).
- **Operable**: everything can be operated (keyboard access, enough time, no seizure-inducing flashes, clear focus).
- **Understandable**: content and behavior are predictable and errors are explained.
- **Robust**: it works with assistive technology, which depends on correct semantics.

Success criteria have conformance levels **A**, **AA** and **AAA**; most laws and policies target **AA**. Habits that cover most of the ground:

- Use **semantic HTML** (`button`, `a`, `nav`, `main`, `label`, headings in order) before reaching for ARIA attributes; native elements bring keyboard and screen-reader behavior for free.
- Give every informative image a meaningful text alternative; mark decorative images so they are skipped.
- Make everything usable by **keyboard alone**, in a logical order, with a clearly visible **focus indicator**.
- Label every form field, and put error messages next to the field in text, not only color.
- **Do not rely on color alone**; add text, icons or patterns.
- **Contrast**: normal text needs a contrast ratio of at least **4.5:1** (AA) and large text at least **3:1**; interface components and graphics need 3:1.
- Provide **captions** for video and transcripts for audio; respect the user's reduced-motion setting.

WCAG 2.2 added criteria such as: focus not obscured by sticky headers or banners (2.4.11), dragging alternatives (2.5.7), a **minimum target size of 24 by 24 CSS pixels** for pointer targets with some exceptions (2.5.8), consistent placement of help (3.2.6), not asking users to re-enter information they already gave (3.3.7), and **accessible authentication** that does not depend on a cognitive test such as remembering or transcribing a password (3.3.8). Of these, 3.2.6 and 3.3.7 are level A and the others are level AA. WCAG 2.2 also marks the old parsing criterion (4.1.1) as obsolete and removed.

The contrast ratio compares relative luminance. For sRGB colors, convert each channel $c$ (0 to 1) to linear light with $c/12.92$ when $c \le 0.04045$, otherwise $((c+0.055)/1.055)^{2.4}$; then luminance is $L = 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B$ and the ratio is $(L_1 + 0.05)/(L_2 + 0.05)$ with $L_1$ the lighter color. Black on white is 21:1, white on white is 1:1.

### Testing accessibility

Automated checkers find only a portion of problems, so combine them with manual checks: tab through the page with only the keyboard, zoom to 200 percent, try a screen reader, check contrast, and test with people who use assistive technology when you can. Build accessibility into design and code review, since fixing it late is expensive.

### Privacy fundamentals

Privacy is about people's control over information about them. Practical engineering principles:

- **Data minimization**: collect only what you need. Data you never collect cannot leak.
- **Purpose limitation**: use data only for the purposes you told people about.
- **Retention limits**: delete data when it is no longer needed.
- **Security**: encrypt, restrict access, log access (the security courses cover how).
- **Transparency and control**: plain-language notices and meaningful choices; avoid consent screens designed to confuse.
- **Privacy by design**: raise privacy questions in the design document, not after launch.

Laws vary by place and change over time; the following is **general information, not legal advice.** The EU's **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)**, applicable since May 2018, covers organizations established in the EU and, even if the company is elsewhere, processing of personal data of people in the EU when it relates to offering them goods or services or monitoring their behavior in the EU. It requires a **lawful basis** for processing (consent is one of several), follows principles including lawfulness, fairness and transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity and confidentiality, and accountability, and gives individuals rights such as access, correction, erasure in some circumstances, restriction, portability and objection. A personal-data breach must be reported to the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to people. Maximum fines are large for serious violations. Other laws, such as US state privacy laws and sector rules for health or children's data, add their own requirements; ask your privacy or legal team.

"Anonymous" is a high bar. Data stripped of names can often be re-identified when combined with other data (location traces are famously hard to anonymize). Treat de-identified data carefully and describe it honestly.

### Inclusive design

Inclusive design means considering the full range of people from the start: different abilities, languages and scripts, ages, devices and network speeds, cultures and time zones. Avoid assumptions in forms (a single "first name, last name" model, a required gender, strict phone formats), support text resizing and translation, and test on low-end devices. Involve people who differ from you, and treat their feedback as requirements.

@example
title: Checking a color pair
problem: A fictional app shows gray text `#777777` on a white background at normal size. Does it meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast?
step: Compute relative luminance: `#777777` has each channel $119/255 \approx 0.467$, which converts to about $0.184$ in linear light, so $L \approx 0.184$. White has $L = 1$.
step: Ratio: $(1 + 0.05)/(0.184 + 0.05) \approx 4.48$.
step: AA for normal text requires 4.5, and $4.48 < 4.5$, so it fails by a hair. A slightly darker gray such as `#767676` gives about 4.54 and passes.
answer: It fails AA (about 4.48:1); use a slightly darker color.

@example
title: Minimizing data collection
problem: A fictional recipe app wants to add "share your location" so it can show nearby grocery stores. It also plans to keep a log of every location forever. What do you suggest?
step: Purpose: show nearby stores right now. That needs approximate location only while the user asks.
step: Minimize: request location permission only when the user taps "find stores," use coarse (city-level) location if it is enough, and do not store the raw coordinates.
step: Retention: if some data must be kept (for example a saved home store), keep that, not the history, and give a way to delete it.
step: Write these decisions in the design document and the privacy notice so users can see what happens.
answer: Collect coarse location only on demand, do not store history, and document and disclose the decision.

@mistakes
- Adding ARIA attributes everywhere instead of using the native element (a `button`) that already has the correct role and keyboard behavior.
- Conveying information only by color (red and green status dots) or only on hover.
- Calling data "anonymous" because names were removed, when it can still be linked back to individuals.
- Treating accessibility or privacy as a final-week checklist rather than a design requirement.

model:
```python
def _luminance(color):
    color = color.lstrip("#")
    channels = []
    for i in (0, 2, 4):
        c = int(color[i:i + 2], 16) / 255
        if c <= 0.04045:
            channels.append(c / 12.92)
        else:
            channels.append(((c + 0.055) / 1.055) ** 2.4)
    r, g, b = channels
    return 0.2126 * r + 0.7152 * g + 0.0722 * b


def contrast_ratio(fg, bg):
    first, second = _luminance(fg), _luminance(bg)
    lighter, darker = max(first, second), min(first, second)
    return round((lighter + 0.05) / (darker + 0.05), 2)
```
hint: Write a helper that turns one hex color into its relative luminance.
hint: Use `int(hex_pair, 16)` to parse each channel and take `max` and `min` of the two luminances.
solution: Parse the three hex pairs, convert each to linear light, combine with the standard weights to get luminance, then divide the lighter plus 0.05 by the darker plus 0.05. Taking `max` and `min` makes the order irrelevant. Round only for display; when deciding pass or fail, compare the unrounded value.
