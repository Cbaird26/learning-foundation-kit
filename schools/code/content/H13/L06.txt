@article
Between about 1500 and 1830, European and Atlantic societies changed how they thought about nature and about government. The two changes are connected: both stressed evidence and reason over inherited authority.

### The Scientific Revolution

In 1543 two books changed their fields. Copernicus's *On the Revolutions of the Heavenly Spheres* proposed that the Earth orbits the Sun, and Vesalius's *De humani corporis fabrica* corrected old anatomy with dissection. In 1628 Harvey's work on the circulation of the blood used measurement and experiment to show that the heart pumps blood in a circuit. In 1687 Newton's *Principia* described motion and gravitation with mathematical laws. Telescopes and microscopes, scientific societies and journals spread results. The **scientific method** emphasized observation, hypothesis, experiment and public criticism. Science also drew on knowledge from the Islamic world, China and India, and on many practitioners who were not recognized at the time.

### The Enlightenment

Enlightenment writers of the 1600s and 1700s argued that reason could improve society. Locke argued for natural rights to life, liberty and property and for government by consent; Montesquieu proposed **separation of powers** among branches; Rousseau wrote about the **general will** and popular sovereignty. Others criticized censorship, torture and religious intolerance. Enlightenment thinkers disagreed with each other, and many accepted slavery or excluded women from political rights; critics in their own time and later pointed this out.

### Revolutions

**American Revolution (1775 to 1783).** Colonists objected to taxes and decisions made by a Parliament in which they had no representatives. The Declaration of Independence (1776) stated natural-rights principles. Loyalists, enslaved people, Native nations and women experienced the war and its outcome differently.

**French Revolution (1789 onward).** Financial crisis, social inequality and Enlightenment ideas led to the National Assembly, the abolition of feudal privileges and the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen (1789). The revolution then radicalized, with a republic, the execution of the king, the Terror (1793 to 1794) and finally Napoleon's rise. Historians differ on whether social class, ideas, financial crisis or political conflict was most important.

**Haitian Revolution (1791 to 1804).** Enslaved people in the French colony of Saint-Domingue rose up, defeated French, Spanish and British forces, and founded Haiti in 1804, the first state created by a successful revolt of enslaved people. Its example alarmed slaveholding societies and inspired others.

**Latin American independence (1808 to 1825).** Napoleon's invasion of Spain weakened colonial rule, and local elites and others led independence movements across Spanish America.

### Comparing revolutions

| Revolution | Main aim | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| American | Self-government, no taxation without representation | Republic with a written constitution; slavery continued |
| French | End privilege, create a nation of citizens | Republic, terror, then empire; lasting legal reforms |
| Haitian | End slavery and colonial rule | Independent state; heavy debt and isolation afterward |

### Medicine and revolution

Dissection and measurement shaped modern anatomy and physiology. In 1796 Edward Jenner showed that inoculation with cowpox protected against smallpox, a procedure called **vaccination** (from Latin *vacca*, cow). Revolutionary governments also reorganized hospitals and medical training, especially in France, where bedside observation and autopsy became central to the Paris clinical school.

@mistakes
- Saying that Copernicus "proved" heliocentrism in 1543; the evidence developed over the next century.
- Treating the Enlightenment as a unified movement whose thinkers agreed.
- Describing the French Revolution as simply good or simply bad; historians weigh reforms against violence.
- Omitting the Haitian Revolution or treating it only as a side effect of the French one.
