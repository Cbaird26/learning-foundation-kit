@project
title: Robot maze
overview: Draw a maze on grid paper, hide a treasure in it, and write step-by-step instructions that guide a "robot" partner to the treasure. Use loops and if-then choices, test the instructions, fix any bugs, and present your final program.
goal: Write exact instructions with loops and if-then choices.
goal: Test the instructions like a computer, and find and fix bugs.
goal: Explain your program out loud to your grown-up.
step: Draw a grid on paper that is 4 squares wide and 4 squares tall. Write the letters A, B, C and D under the columns and the numbers 1 to 4 up the side.
step: Put the robot on A1. Choose a square for the treasure. Color a few squares gray for walls. The robot cannot go through walls, and the treasure must not be in a wall.
step: Plan a path from the robot to the treasure. Say the path out loud, square by square.
step: Write your instructions with arrows. Use at least one loop (Repeat ___ times) and at least one if-then choice (for example: if the square ahead is a wall, then go a different way).
step: Test your instructions. Give them to your partner, who must follow them exactly, one at a time, like a computer.
step: Look for bugs. Where did the robot go wrong? Fix the step, then test again. Do this until the robot reaches the treasure.
step: Present your final program. Show the maze and the instructions, and say what your loop and your choice do.
deliverable: A drawing of your maze on grid paper, with the robot, the treasure and the walls.
deliverable: Your final instructions, written with arrows, with at least one loop and one if-then choice.
deliverable: A short talk, out loud, about one bug you found and how you fixed it.
rubric: The maze has a robot, a treasure and walls, and the path is possible.
rubric: The instructions are exact, so a partner can follow them without guessing.
rubric: The program has at least one loop and one if-then choice.
rubric: The child tested the program and fixed at least one bug.
rubric: The child explained the program out loud in their own words.
safety: Do this project on paper, away from the computer. If you want to share your maze with anyone outside your family, ask your grown-up first and share only the maze, never your name or address.
tutor: How can I use a loop to make my robot instructions shorter?
tutor: What is a good if-then choice for a robot in a maze?
tutor: How can I test my instructions like a computer would?
material: Robot card. The robot starts on A1 and understands four arrows: ↑ (up one square), ↓ (down one square), → (right one square) and ← (left one square). It does one arrow at a time and never skips an arrow. It cannot go through a gray wall, and it stays on the grid.
material: A sample maze. The robot is R, the treasure is T and walls are #.

```text
4 . . T .
3 . # # .
2 . . . .
1 R . . .
  A B C D
```

One way to reach the treasure is: Repeat 3 times: ↑. Then Repeat 2 times: →. The robot goes up to A4, then right to B4 and C4, which is the treasure. Make your own maze, with different walls and a different treasure square.

@review
Here are the key ideas, week by week.

**Weeks 1 and 2.** A sequence is steps in order. A computer does them exactly. A pattern is something that repeats, so you can say what comes next.

**Weeks 3 and 4.** A loop repeats steps. Count the times around, and the steps in the loop. An if-then choice does one thing if the "if" is true, and "otherwise" something else.

**Weeks 5 and 6.** A bug is a mistake in instructions. Run the steps one at a time, find the step that goes wrong, fix it, and run again. Break big jobs into small steps, and give a group of steps a name.

**Weeks 7 and 8.** Sorting puts things in order by a rule. Searching finds a thing. Guessing the middle of sorted cards is fast. A code changes a message by a rule, and you undo the rule to decode.

**Weeks 9 and 10.** Binary uses 1 and 0, or face-up and face-down cards of 16, 8, 4, 2 and 1. A picture is a grid of pixels, and each pixel is a number.

**Weeks 11 and 12.** A computer follows its program exactly. Input goes in, and output comes out. Keep private things private, tell a grown-up if something feels wrong, and be kind online.

**Self-check.** Can you say what each of these words means: sequence, pattern, loop, bug, binary, pixel, input, output?

**Common mistakes.** Skipping a step when you follow instructions. Counting a loop wrongly. Adding face-down cards. Shifting a code the wrong way.

@exam text
prompt: The robot starts on A1 of a 4 by 4 grid. It follows this program. Which square does it end on? Type the square name.

```text
Repeat 2 times:
    ↑
    →
↑
```
answer: C4
accept: c4
check: run("d={'R':(1,0),'L':(-1,0),'U':(0,1),'D':(0,-1)}\nx,y=0,1\nfor m in 'UR' * 2 + 'U':\n    x+=d[m][0]\n    y+=d[m][1]\nprint('ABCD'[x]+str(y))\n")
hint: Do the loop two times first. Each time the robot goes up one and right one. Then do the last ↑.
solution: The loop takes the robot to B2 and then to C3. The last ↑ takes it up to C4.

@exam num
prompt: The five binary cards from left to right are 16, 8, 4, 2 and 1. A row of cards is 0 1 1 0 1, where 1 means face up and 0 means face down. What number do the cards show? Type a number.
answer: 13
check: int("01101", 2)
hint: Find the cards that are face up. Then add up their dots.
solution: The face-up cards are 8, 4 and 1. 8 plus 4 plus 1 is 13.

@exam self
prompt: Your grown-up gives you this long set of steps for a robot that starts on A1: → ↑ → ↑ → ↑. Shorten it with a loop. Then change the last step so that the robot ends on D3 instead of D4, and test it by following each arrow.
hint: Look for the part that repeats. How many times does it repeat?
solution: The loop is: Repeat 3 times: →, then ↑. It takes the robot to B2, C3 and D4. To end on D3, the last ↑ must go. Use Repeat 2 times: → ↑, and then →. The robot goes to B2, C3 and D3.
rubric: The child wrote a loop that repeats → and ↑ the right number of times.
rubric: The child fixed the bug by taking away the last ↑, so the robot stops on D3.
rubric: The child tested the new instructions by following each arrow, one at a time.
