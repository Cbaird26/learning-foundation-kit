title: Instructions in order: sequences
summary: Follow a list of steps in the right order and say where a robot ends up on a grid.
objective: Say what a sequence of instructions is.
objective: Put steps in the right order.
objective: Follow arrow instructions on a grid and find where the robot stops.

@article
A **sequence** is a list of steps done one after another, in order.

You follow sequences every day. To brush your teeth, you do step 1, then step 2, then step 3. If you mix up the order, it goes wrong. Imagine putting your shoes on before your socks!

A computer works the same way. A computer cannot guess. It does exactly the steps you give it, in the order you give them. A list of steps for a computer is called a **program**.

### The robot game

Let us make a pretend robot. It lives on a grid of squares. Each square has a name, like a spot on a treasure map. The letters go along the bottom. The numbers go up the side.

```text
4 . . . .
3 . . . .
2 . . . .
1 R . . .
  A B C D
```

The robot `R` starts on square **A1**. The robot understands four arrows:

- ↑ means go up one square.
- ↓ means go down one square.
- → means go right one square.
- ← means go left one square.

The robot does one arrow at a time. It does not skip any. It does not decide anything by itself.

To find where the robot ends up, put your finger on A1. Then follow each arrow, one by one, and say the square out loud.

Grown-up helper: let your child move a small toy or a finger on the picture. Moving a real thing makes the idea stick.

@example
title: Where does the robot stop?
problem: The robot starts on A1 and follows these steps: ↑ → ↑ →. Which square does it end on?
step: Start on A1. The first arrow ↑ goes up one square, to A2.
step: The next arrow → goes right one square, to B2.
step: Then ↑ goes up to B3, and → goes right to C3.
step: The robot ends on C3.
answer: C3

@mistakes
- Doing the steps in the wrong order. The order is part of the instructions.
- Skipping an arrow, or doing one twice. Touch each arrow once as you go.
- Mixing up left and right, or up and down. Check with the picture.

@practice mc
prompt: Which list of steps for brushing teeth is in the right order?
choice*: Put paste on the brush, brush your teeth, rinse your mouth.
choice: Brush your teeth, put paste on the brush, rinse your mouth.
choice: Rinse your mouth, brush your teeth, put paste on the brush.
hint: Think about what you must have on the brush before you can brush with it.
solution: The paste goes on the brush first. Then you brush. Then you rinse.

@practice text
prompt: The robot starts on A1 of this grid. It follows → → ↑ ↑ ↑. Which square does it end on? Type the square name, like B2.

```text
4 . . . .
3 . . . .
2 . . . .
1 R . . .
  A B C D
```
answer: C4
accept: c4
check: run("d={'R':(1,0),'L':(-1,0),'U':(0,1),'D':(0,-1)}\nx,y=0,1\nfor m in 'RRUUU':\n    x+=d[m][0]\n    y+=d[m][1]\nprint('ABCD'[x]+str(y))\n")
hint: Start on A1. Move right two squares. Then move up three squares.
solution: Two → arrows take the robot from A1 to C1. Three ↑ arrows take it up to C2, C3 and then C4.

@practice num
prompt: The robot starts on A1 and follows ↑ ↑ → → → ↑. Which row number is it on at the end? Type a number.
answer: 4
check: int(run("y=1\nfor m in 'UURRRU':\n    if m == 'U':\n        y += 1\nprint(y)\n"))
hint: Only the ↑ arrows change the row. Count them.
solution: There are three ↑ arrows. The robot starts in row 1, so it ends in row 1 + 3 = 4.

@practice mc
prompt: The robot starts on A1. Which steps make it end on B3?
choice*: → ↑ ↑
choice: → → ↑
choice: ↑ ↑ ↑ →
choice: → ↑
hint: B3 is one square right of A and two squares up from row 1.
solution: One → goes from column A to column B. Two ↑ go from row 1 to row 3. That makes B3.

@practice self
prompt: Pretend you are the computer. Ask your grown-up to write down five steps for making a bowl of cereal. Follow the steps one at a time, exactly as written. Then try the steps in a mixed-up order. What goes wrong?
hint: Take it one step at a time, and ask your grown-up to check each part with you.
solution: A good list is: get a bowl, put cereal in the bowl, pour milk on the cereal, get a spoon, eat. In a mixed-up order, you might pour milk into an empty bowl, or eat before you have a spoon. Order matters.
rubric: The list has five steps in an order that works.
rubric: The child followed the steps exactly, one at a time.
rubric: The child can say one thing that went wrong when the order was mixed up.

@quiz mc
prompt: Which list of steps for planting a seed is in the right order?
choice*: Dig a hole, put the seed in, cover it with soil, water it.
choice: Cover it with soil, put the seed in, dig a hole, water it.
choice: Water it, cover it with soil, dig a hole, put the seed in.
choice: Put the seed in, dig a hole, water it, cover it with soil.
hint: You cannot put a seed in a hole before the hole is there.
solution: First dig the hole. Then the seed goes in. Then you cover it with soil. Last, water it.
