@article
**Cell theory** states that all living things are made of cells, the cell is the basic unit of life, and new cells come only from existing cells. All cells have a plasma membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes and DNA. The big divide is between two cell types.

### Prokaryotes and eukaryotes

**Prokaryotic cells** (bacteria and archaea) have no membrane-bound nucleus. Their DNA is usually a single circular chromosome in a region called the **nucleoid**, and they have no membrane-bound organelles. They are typically 1 to 5 micrometers ($\mu$m) long. Most have a **cell wall** (bacterial walls contain peptidoglycan), and many have flagella, pili or plasmids (small circular DNA).

**Eukaryotic cells** (animals, plants, fungi, protists) have a **nucleus** enclosed by a double membrane, linear chromosomes, and membrane-bound organelles. They are typically 10 to 100 $\mu$m. Both types have **ribosomes**, but eukaryotic ribosomes (80S) are larger than prokaryotic ones (70S), a difference some antibiotics exploit.

### Eukaryotic organelles

| Organelle | Function |
|---|---|
| Nucleus | stores DNA, site of transcription; nucleolus makes ribosomal subunits |
| Ribosomes | build proteins by translation |
| Rough ER | ribosome-studded; makes proteins for secretion and membranes |
| Smooth ER | lipid synthesis, detoxification, calcium storage |
| Golgi apparatus | modifies, sorts and packages proteins into vesicles |
| Mitochondria | most ATP production by aerobic respiration; own DNA |
| Lysosomes | digest macromolecules and worn-out parts using hydrolytic enzymes |
| Peroxisomes | break down fatty acids and hydrogen peroxide |
| Chloroplasts (plants, algae) | photosynthesis; own DNA |
| Central vacuole (plants) | stores water and ions, maintains turgor |
| Cell wall (plants: cellulose) | support and protection |
| Cytoskeleton | microfilaments, intermediate filaments and microtubules give shape, movement and transport tracks |

Animal cells lack chloroplasts, a cell wall and a large central vacuole; plant cells (unlike animal cells) have a cell wall, chloroplasts and a large central vacuole. The **endosymbiotic theory** proposes that mitochondria and chloroplasts descend from engulfed prokaryotes, supported by their own circular DNA, 70S ribosomes and double membranes.

A protein made for export travels: rough ER, then vesicle, then Golgi, then vesicle, then plasma membrane (**secretory pathway**).

### Why are cells small?

As a cell grows, volume increases faster than surface area. For a cube of side $s$: surface area $= 6s^2$, volume $= s^3$, so
$$\frac{\text{SA}}{V} = \frac{6}{s}.$$
Materials enter and wastes leave through the surface, but must serve the whole volume. A low SA/V ratio limits exchange, so cells stay small or become folded (like microvilli).

### Microscopy

$$\text{magnification} = \frac{\text{image size}}{\text{actual size}}$$
with both in the same units. Light microscopes show cells; electron microscopes reveal organelle detail.

@example
title: Surface-area-to-volume ratio
problem: A cube-shaped cell has sides of 5 $\mu$m. What is its surface-area-to-volume ratio, and what happens to the ratio if the side doubles?
step: Surface area is $6s^2 = 6(25) = 150\ \mu\text{m}^2$.
step: Volume is $s^3 = 125\ \mu\text{m}^3$. The ratio is $150/125 = 1.2\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$.
step: For $s = 10\ \mu$m the ratio is $6/10 = 0.6\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$, half as large. Doubling the size halves the ratio.

@mistakes
- Saying prokaryotes have no DNA or no ribosomes; they have both, just no nucleus or membrane-bound organelles.
- Forgetting that plant cells also have mitochondria, not only chloroplasts.
- Mixing up rough ER (protein synthesis for export) with smooth ER (lipids and detox).
- Mixing units in magnification: convert millimeters to micrometers before dividing.
