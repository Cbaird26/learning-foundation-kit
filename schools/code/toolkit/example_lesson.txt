@lesson
title: Linear equations
summary: Solve one-variable linear equations, including ones with fractions, and check every solution by substitution.
objective: Isolate a variable by undoing operations in reverse order.
objective: Clear fractions by multiplying every term by the least common denominator.
objective: Check a solution by substituting it into the original equation.
reading: OpenStax College Algebra 2e, Section 2.2 "Linear Equations in One Variable" (Precalculus 2e assumes this skill).

@article
An **equation** says two expressions are equal. A **linear equation** in one variable can be written as $ax + b = c$ with $a \neq 0$: the variable appears only to the first power. Solving it means finding the value of $x$ that makes the statement true.

### Keep the balance

Think of an equation as a balanced scale. You may do anything to one side as long as you do exactly the same thing to the other side. The goal is to get $x$ alone, so you **undo** what has been done to $x$, in reverse order.

In $3x + 5 = 20$, the variable was multiplied by $3$, then $5$ was added. Undo in reverse: subtract $5$, then divide by $3$:

$$3x + 5 = 20 \;\Longrightarrow\; 3x = 15 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 5.$$

### Variables on both sides

When $x$ appears on both sides, first collect the $x$-terms on one side and the constants on the other. In $7x - 4 = 2x + 11$, subtract $2x$ from both sides to get $5x - 4 = 11$, then finish as before: $x = 3$.

### Clearing fractions

Fractions make arithmetic error-prone. Multiply **every term** on both sides by the least common denominator (LCD) and the fractions disappear. The equation $\frac{x}{4} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{4}$ has LCD $4$, and multiplying through gives $x + 2 = 3$.

### Always check

Substitute your answer into the **original** equation. If both sides agree, you are done. Checking catches sign slips that are otherwise invisible, and it is a habit every later course relies on.

@example
title: An equation with fractions on both sides
problem: Solve $\frac{x}{3} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{6}$.
step: The denominators are $3$, $2$ and $6$, so the LCD is $6$. Multiply every term by $6$: $2x + 3 = 5$.
step: Subtract $3$ from both sides: $2x = 2$.
step: Divide both sides by $2$: $x = 1$.
step: Check: $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{6} + \frac{3}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$. Both sides agree.

@mistakes
- Multiplying only the fraction terms by the LCD and forgetting the whole-number terms.
- Distributing a negative sign to the first term in brackets but not the second.
- Skipping the check, so a sign error goes unnoticed.

@practice num
prompt: Solve for $x$: $4x - 7 = 21$.
answer: 7
check: root(lambda x: 4*x - 7 - 21, -100, 100)
hint: Undo the subtraction first: add $7$ to both sides.
hint: You now have $4x = 28$. What undoes multiplying by $4$?
solution: Add $7$: $4x = 28$. Divide by $4$: $x = 7$. Check: $4(7) - 7 = 21$.

@practice num
prompt: Solve for $x$: $7x - 4 = 2x + 11$.
answer: 3
check: root(lambda x: (7*x - 4) - (2*x + 11), -100, 100)
hint: Collect the $x$-terms on one side: subtract $2x$ from both sides.
hint: $5x - 4 = 11$. Now add $4$, then divide by $5$.
solution: $5x - 4 = 11$, so $5x = 15$ and $x = 3$. Check: $7(3) - 4 = 17$ and $2(3) + 11 = 17$.

@practice num
prompt: Solve for $x$: $\frac{x}{4} - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{6}$. Give an exact answer.
answer: 6
check: root(lambda x: x/4 - 2/3 - 5/6, -100, 100)
hint: The LCD of $4$, $3$ and $6$ is $12$. Multiply every term by $12$.
hint: You should get $3x - 8 = 10$.
solution: Multiplying by $12$ gives $3x - 8 = 10$, so $3x = 18$ and $x = 6$. Check: $\frac{6}{4} - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{18}{12} - \frac{8}{12} = \frac{10}{12} = \frac{5}{6}$.

@practice num
prompt: Solve for $x$: $3(x - 2) - (x + 4) = 0$.
answer: 5
check: root(lambda x: 3*(x - 2) - (x + 4), -100, 100)
hint: Distribute first. The minus sign in front of $(x + 4)$ applies to both terms.
hint: $3x - 6 - x - 4 = 0$ simplifies to $2x - 10 = 0$.
solution: $3x - 6 - x - 4 = 0$ gives $2x - 10 = 0$, so $x = 5$. Check: $3(3) - 9 = 0$.

choice*: They divided only the $2x$ term by $2$ instead of every term.

@quiz num
prompt: Solve for $x$: $\frac{2x + 1}{5} = \frac{x - 1}{2}$.
answer: 7
check: root(lambda x: (2*x + 1)/5 - (x - 1)/2, -100, 100)
hint: Multiply both sides by $10$.
hint: $2(2x + 1) = 5(x - 1)$.
solution: $4x + 2 = 5x - 5$, so $x = 7$. Check: $\frac{15}{5} = 3$ and $\frac{6}{2} = 3$.

@quiz expr
prompt: Solve $ax + b = c$ for $x$, assuming $a \neq 0$.
vars: a, b, c
lead: $x =$
answer: (c - b)/a
check: lambda a, b, c: root(lambda x: a*x + b - c, -100, 100)
domain: a: 1..3; b: 0..2; c: 2..5
hint: Treat $a$, $b$ and $c$ as fixed numbers and undo the operations on $x$ in reverse order.
hint: Subtract $b$, then divide by $a$.
solution: $ax = c - b$, so $x = \frac{c - b}{a}$.
