@article
Two letters can team up to make **one sound**. A team like this is called a **digraph**. Today you meet four: sh, ch, th and wh.

### Note for the grown-up

- sh says /sh/, the quiet "shhh" sound, as in ship, fish, shop.
- ch says /ch/, like a train, as in chip, chin, lunch.
- th says /th/ with the tongue between the teeth. It can be soft, as in thin and bath, or buzzing, as in this and that.
- wh says /w/ in most speech, as in whale, wheel, when. (Some people say a puff of air first.)

### Two letters, one sound

The word "ship" has 4 letters, but only 3 sounds: /sh/ /i/ /p/. The letters s and h stay together. Do not read them as /s/ then /h/.

### Read it

- sh: shop, shut, dish, fish
- ch: chip, chat, much, rich
- th: thin, this, with, math
- wh: when, whip, whiz

### Try it with your grown-up

Make a "team" card for each of the four teams. Then look for the teams in a picture book. Point to each one and say its sound.

@example
title: Read a word with a team
problem: Read the word "chin" and count its sounds.
step: Find the team. The first two letters, ch, stay together and say /ch/.
step: Add the other sounds: /ch/ /i/ /n/. Blend them: chin.
step: There are 4 letters but only 3 sounds.

@mistakes
- Splitting the team, like reading "ship" as /s/ /h/ /i/ /p/.
- Mixing up sh and ch. Sh is quiet like "shhh". Ch is like a chugging train.
- Forgetting that th has a soft sound and a buzzing sound. Both are spelled th.

hint: Look at the first two letters of each word.
solution: Ship and shop start with sh. That makes 2.
