@project
title: Plan a garden
overview: You and your grown-up will plan a small garden on paper. You will find the area of the plot, give each plant a fraction of the space, work out what the seeds cost, and draw a map on a grid.
goal: Use multiplication and area to describe the garden plot.
goal: Use fractions to share the plot between plants.
goal: Add and multiply decimals to find the cost.
goal: Plot each plant on a coordinate grid map.
step: The plot is $8$ m long and $5$ m wide. Draw it on grid paper with each square equal to $1$ m. Put the corners at $(0, 0)$, $(8, 0)$, $(8, 5)$ and $(0, 5)$. Find the area by multiplying $8 \times 5$ and check it with an estimate.
step: Share the plot. Tomatoes get $\frac{1}{2}$ of the plot, beans get $\frac{1}{4}$ and carrots get $\frac{1}{8}$. Find how many square meters each plant gets. Then add the three fractions to see how much of the plot is used. What fraction is left for a path?
step: Work out the cost. Tomato plants cost \$1.25 each and you want $4$. A packet of bean seeds costs \$2.50. A packet of carrot seeds costs \$1.75. Add everything to find the total. Estimate first.
step: Pay with \$20. Subtract the total to find your change.
step: Make your map. Pick a point for each plant, such as tomatoes at $(2, 2)$, beans at $(5, 4)$ and carrots at $(7, 4)$. Mark each on your grid and write the ordered pair beside it. Find how far apart two of them are when they share a row or a column.
step: Add a water tap. It is on the edge at $(0, 3)$. Say how many steps right and up you go from the origin, and how far the tap is from the corner $(0, 0)$.
step: Tell your grown-up one big idea from this course in your own words, such as "equivalent fractions", "decimals as money" or "ordered pairs".
deliverable: A grid-paper map of the garden with the plot, plants and tap labeled with ordered pairs.
deliverable: A sheet showing the area, the fraction of the plot for each plant, and the cost with change.
deliverable: One or two sentences, said or written, about a big idea from the course.
rubric: The area is $8 \times 5 = 40$ square meters, with the units named.
rubric: The fractions of the plot are multiplied and added correctly, and the path fraction is found.
rubric: The decimal total and change are lined up by decimal point and checked with an estimate.
rubric: Every plant on the map is plotted right first, then up, and labeled with its ordered pair.
safety: Do any digging, tools or watering with a grown-up. Wash your hands after touching soil or seeds.
tutor: Can you show me how to line up the decimal points when I add money?
tutor: How can I check that my fraction of the plot makes sense?
tutor: How do I remember which number to go along and which to go up on the grid?

@review
### Week by week
- **Weeks 1 and 2:** Multiply by breaking numbers into tens and ones. Divide by sharing the tens, then the ones. A remainder is smaller than the number you divide by. Check with an estimate.
- **Weeks 3 and 4:** A factor goes in evenly. A multiple is what skip counting gives. A prime has exactly two factors. Equivalent fractions come from multiplying or dividing the top and bottom by the same number.
- **Weeks 5 and 6:** To add or subtract fractions, make the bottom numbers match. To multiply, multiply tops and bottoms.
- **Weeks 7 and 8:** Tenths and hundredths are places after the decimal point. Line up the points to add and subtract. Money and meters use hundredths.
- **Weeks 9 and 10:** Big unit to small unit: multiply. Small to big: divide. Volume is length times width times height, in cubic units.
- **Weeks 11 and 12:** Triangle angles add to $180^\circ$. An ordered pair is right first, then up.

### Self-check
- Can I multiply $23 \times 14$ and estimate to check?
- Can I list the factor pairs of $36$?
- Can I add $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{6}$?
- Can I add $2.5 + 1.75$?
- Can I find the volume of a box that is $5$ by $3$ by $2$?

### Common errors
- Forgetting the zero when multiplying by ten.
- Adding the denominators of fractions.
- Not lining up the decimal points.
- Going up before right on the grid.

@exam num
prompt: A garden plot is $12$ m long and $5$ m wide. Beans take up $\frac{1}{4}$ of the plot. How many square meters are for beans?
answer: 15
check: 12 * 5 / 4
unit: m²
hint: First find the area of the whole plot by multiplying length by width.
hint: The area is $60$ square meters. Now find $\frac{1}{4}$ of it.
solution: The area is $12 \times 5 = 60$ square meters. One quarter of $60$ is $60 \div 4 = 15$ square meters.

@exam num
prompt: A gardener buys $3$ tomato plants at \$1.25 each and $2$ packets of carrot seeds at \$1.75 each. How many dollars is the total?
answer: 7.25
check: 3 * 1.25 + 2 * 1.75
tol: 0.001
unit: dollars
hint: Find the cost of the tomato plants and the cost of the seed packets separately.
hint: The plants cost $3 \times 1.25$ and the seeds cost $2 \times 1.75$. Then add the two amounts.
solution: Plants: $3 \times 1.25 = 3.75$. Seeds: $2 \times 1.75 = 3.50$. Total: $3.75 + 3.50 = 7.25$, so \$7.25.

@exam mc
prompt: On a garden map, the tap is at $(0, 3)$ and a flower is $5$ steps right of the tap on the same row. Which ordered pair names the flower?
choice*: $(5, 3)$
choice: $(3, 5)$
choice: $(5, 0)$
choice: $(0, 8)$
hint: Moving right changes the first number only.
solution: The row stays the same, so $y$ stays $3$. Moving $5$ right makes $x = 0 + 5 = 5$. The flower is at $(5, 3)$.
