@lesson
title: Adding and subtracting fractions
summary: Add and subtract fractions with the same bottom number, and with different bottom numbers by making them match first.
objective: Add and subtract fractions that have the same denominator.
objective: Find a common denominator for fractions with different denominators.
objective: Add and subtract unlike fractions using equivalent fractions.
reading: Khan Academy: 5th grade math, look up "Add and subtract fractions" (fractions with unlike denominators).

@article
A fraction has two numbers. The **denominator** (bottom) tells how many equal pieces make one whole. The **numerator** (top) tells how many pieces we have.

### Same denominator

Picture a pizza cut into $8$ equal slices. You eat $3$ slices and your friend eats $2$ slices. Together that is $5$ slices out of $8$.

$$\frac{3}{8} + \frac{2}{8} = \frac{5}{8}$$

The slices are the same size, so we just add the numerators. The denominator stays the same, because the pieces did not change size. Subtracting works the same way: $\frac{5}{8} - \frac{2}{8} = \frac{3}{8}$.

### Different denominators

What is $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4}$? The pieces are different sizes, so we cannot just add the tops. First we make the pieces the same size using equivalent fractions.

Half a pizza is the same as $2$ slices out of $4$. So $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4}$. Now add:

$$\frac{2}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}$$

### Finding a common denominator

A **common denominator** is a number that both denominators go into. An easy way is to multiply the two denominators. For $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4}$ use $12$:

$$\frac{1}{3} = \frac{4}{12}, \qquad \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{12}, \qquad \frac{4}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$$

### Draw a model

You can draw two bars of the same length. Cut one into thirds and one into fourths, then cut both into twelfths. Shade each fraction and count the twelfths. Models make the numbers easy to trust.

### Check and simplify

At the end, look at your answer. If the top and bottom share a factor, simplify. For example, $\frac{2}{8}$ can become $\frac{1}{4}$.

@example
title: Add one half and one third
problem: What is $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3}$?
step: The denominators are $2$ and $3$. A number that both go into is $6$.
step: Change each fraction to sixths: $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{6}$ and $\frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{6}$.
step: Add the numerators and keep the denominator: $\frac{3}{6} + \frac{2}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$.
step: Check with a picture. Half a bar plus a third of a bar is a bit less than one whole bar. $\frac{5}{6}$ is a bit less than $1$, so it makes sense.

@mistakes
- Adding the denominators too, so $\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4}$ becomes $\frac{2}{8}$. The pieces stay the same size, so the answer is $\frac{2}{4}$.
- Adding the tops and bottoms of unlike fractions without making a common denominator first.
- Forgetting to simplify at the end.

hint: You get $\frac{2}{8}$. Both $2$ and $8$ are in the $2$ times table.
solution: $\frac{5}{8} - \frac{3}{8} = \frac{2}{8}$. Divide the top and bottom by $2$ to get $\frac{1}{4}$.
