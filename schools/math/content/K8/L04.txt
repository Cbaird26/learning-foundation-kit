@lesson
title: Adaptations and survival
summary: Explain how an animal's or plant's body parts and behaviors help it survive where it lives.
objective: Say what an adaptation is.
objective: Match features such as thick fur, webbed feet and camouflage to how they help.
objective: Explain how one animal's features help it survive in a food web.
reading: NASA Space Place: look up the topic "adaptations" or animals in extreme places for how living things fit where they live.

@article
An **adaptation** is a body part or a behavior that helps a living thing survive in the place where it lives.

### Body parts
- A **polar bear** has thick fur and a layer of fat. These keep it warm in the cold Arctic.
- A **duck** has webbed feet. They push water like paddles when it swims.
- A **giraffe** has a long neck. It can reach leaves high in the trees.
- A **cactus** has thick stems that store water and sharp spines. The spines protect it from animals that want to eat it.
- A **bird's beak** fits its food. A hummingbird has a long thin beak for flower nectar. An eagle has a hooked beak for tearing meat.

### Camouflage
**Camouflage** means colors or patterns that help an animal blend in. A green insect on a green leaf is hard to see. A hidden animal is less likely to be eaten, and a hidden hunter can sneak up on its food.

### Behaviors
A behavior is something an animal does. Birds **migrate**, which means they fly to warmer places for winter. Bears **hibernate**, which means they sleep through the cold season when food is hard to find. A skunk sprays to scare enemies away.

### Why adaptations matter
Animals with helpful features live longer and have more young. When the place changes a lot, living things that do not fit may not survive.

### In a food web
A rabbit has big ears to hear a fox coming and strong back legs to run away. A fox has sharp eyes and sharp teeth to catch rabbits. Each has features that help it eat or avoid being eaten.

@example
title: Match the feature to the job
problem: A frog has long, strong back legs. How could this help it survive?
step: Ask what the feature lets the animal do. Long back legs let the frog jump far.
step: Jumping helps it escape from animals that want to eat it and catch insects to eat.

@example
title: Count the migrators
problem: 9 geese start flying south. 4 more join them. How many geese are flying?
step: Write the numbers: $9$ and $4$.
step: Add them: $9 + 4 = 13$.

@mistakes
- Thinking that an animal chooses to grow a feature. Adaptations are features living things already have, and they build up over many, many generations.
- Mixing up a body-part adaptation (thick fur) with a behavior (hibernating).
- Thinking one adaptation works in every place. Thick fur helps in the cold but is not helpful in a hot desert.
