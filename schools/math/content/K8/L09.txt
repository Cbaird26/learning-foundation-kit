@lesson
title: Rocks, weathering and erosion
summary: Name the three kinds of rock and explain how weathering and erosion change Earth's surface.
objective: Name igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic rocks and say how each forms.
objective: Tell weathering (breaking) from erosion (moving) and deposition (dropping).
objective: Tell how water, wind and ice change the land.
reading: NASA Space Place: look up the topics "rocks" or "Earth's surface" and "erosion" in the kids' science pages.

@article
Rocks are made of **minerals**. Rocks look different because they form in different ways.

### Three kinds of rock
- **Igneous** rock forms when hot melted rock cools and hardens. Lava that cools becomes igneous rock, such as basalt.
- **Sedimentary** rock forms when bits of sand, mud and shells settle in layers and press together over a very long time. Sandstone is an example. Fossils are found in sedimentary rock.
- **Metamorphic** rock forms when heat and pressure change rock that was already there. Limestone can change into marble.

### Weathering
**Weathering** breaks rock into smaller pieces. Water seeps into a crack, freezes and expands, and splits the rock. Plant roots can grow in cracks and push them wider. Wind and rain can wear rock down too. Weathering does not move the pieces. It only breaks them.

### Erosion
**Erosion** moves the broken pieces. Rivers carry sand and mud. Wind blows dust and sand. Glaciers (moving ice) drag rocks. Where the moving water or wind slows down, the pieces drop. This is **deposition**. A river drops mud at its mouth and builds new land called a delta.

### Slow and fast
Most changes are slow. The Grand Canyon was cut by a river over millions of years. A heavy storm or a landslide can change land fast.

### Soil
Weathered rock bits mix with dead plants to make **soil**. Plants grow in soil.

### Model erosion
Pile damp sand on a tray to make a hill. Pour water slowly down the hill. The water carries sand down in little channels and drops it at the bottom. This is a model of erosion and deposition.

@example
title: Weathering or erosion?
problem: A river carries sand to the sea. Is this weathering or erosion?
step: Weathering breaks rock. Erosion moves the pieces.
step: The river is moving sand, so this is erosion.

@example
title: Count the layers
problem: A cliff has 4 layers of sandstone and 3 layers of shale. How many layers does it have?
step: Write the numbers: $4$ and $3$.
step: Add them: $4 + 3 = 7$ layers.

@mistakes
- Mixing up weathering (breaking) with erosion (moving).
- Thinking that all rocks form the same way.
- Thinking that landforms change only fast. Most changes take a very long time.
