@article
Drive a car along a road at constant speed and watch the steering wheel. How far you must turn it measures how sharply the road bends; how much the road lifts out of the plane of that bend is a second quantity. These are the curvature and the torsion. Together they will turn out to determine the curve completely (next week).

### The Frenet frame

Let $\beta\colon I \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be a smooth unit-speed curve. The **unit tangent** is $T = \beta'$. Because $T \cdot T = 1$, differentiating gives $2\,T' \cdot T = 0$, so $T'$ is perpendicular to $T$. The **curvature** is $\kappa(s) = |T'(s)| \ge 0$. At points where $\kappa > 0$ we define the **principal normal** $N = T'/\kappa$ and the **binormal** $B = T \times N$. Then $T, N, B$ is a positively oriented orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$ at each point, called the **Frenet frame**. A straight line has $\kappa = 0$ and no Frenet frame.

Since $B$ is a unit vector, $B' \perp B$. We will show that $B'$ is also perpendicular to $T$, so it is a multiple of $N$; the **torsion** $\tau$ is defined by $B' = -\tau N$.

**Theorem 1 (Frenet–Serret equations).** For a smooth unit-speed curve with $\kappa > 0$ everywhere,
$$T' = \kappa N,\qquad N' = -\kappa T + \tau B,\qquad B' = -\tau N .$$

*Proof.* The first equation is the definition of $N$. For $B$, differentiate $B = T \times N$: $B' = T' \times N + T \times N' = \kappa\, N \times N + T \times N' = T \times N'$. A cross product is perpendicular to both factors, so $B' \perp T$. Together with $B' \perp B$ this puts $B'$ along $N$, so $B' = -\tau N$ for a unique smooth function $\tau$. For $N'$, expand in the orthonormal basis: $N' = (N' \cdot T)T + (N' \cdot N)N + (N' \cdot B)B$. Differentiating $N \cdot T = 0$ gives $N' \cdot T = -N \cdot T' = -\kappa$; differentiating $N \cdot N = 1$ gives $N' \cdot N = 0$; differentiating $N \cdot B = 0$ gives $N' \cdot B = -N \cdot B' = \tau$. $\blacksquare$

### Arbitrary parametrizations

Let $\alpha$ be regular with speed $v = |\alpha'|$ and arc length $s$, so $ds/dt = v$. Then $\alpha' = vT$, and by the Frenet–Serret equations $\frac{d}{dt}T = v\kappa N$. Hence
$$\alpha'' = v'T + \kappa v^2 N,\qquad \alpha' \times \alpha'' = \kappa v^3\, B .$$
Taking norms gives the curvature formula below. Differentiating once more, the only term of $\alpha'''$ with a $B$ component comes from $\kappa v^2 \frac{dN}{dt} = \kappa v^3(-\kappa T + \tau B)$, so $\alpha''' \cdot B = \kappa v^3 \tau$.

**Theorem 2.** For a regular curve $\alpha$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with $\alpha' \times \alpha'' \ne 0$,
$$\kappa = \frac{|\alpha' \times \alpha''|}{|\alpha'|^3},\qquad \tau = \frac{(\alpha' \times \alpha'') \cdot \alpha'''}{|\alpha' \times \alpha''|^2}.$$

*Proof.* The curvature formula follows from $|\alpha' \times \alpha''| = \kappa v^3$. For the torsion, $(\alpha' \times \alpha'') \cdot \alpha''' = \kappa v^3\, B \cdot \alpha''' = \kappa v^3 \cdot \kappa v^3 \tau = \kappa^2 v^6 \tau$, and $|\alpha' \times \alpha''|^2 = \kappa^2 v^6$. Divide. $\blacksquare$

The condition $\alpha' \times \alpha'' \ne 0$ is exactly $\kappa \ne 0$: without it the frame, and the torsion, are undefined. For a plane curve $(x(t), y(t))$ the same formula reads $\kappa = |x'y'' - y'x''|/(x'^2 + y'^2)^{3/2}$.

**The helix.** For $\alpha(t) = (a\cos t,\ a\sin t,\ bt)$ with $a > 0$ and $b \in \mathbb{R}$ we get $\alpha' \times \alpha'' = (ab\sin t,\ -ab\cos t,\ a^2)$ and $\alpha''' = (a\sin t,\ -a\cos t,\ 0)$, so
$$\kappa = \frac{a}{a^2 + b^2},\qquad \tau = \frac{b}{a^2 + b^2}.$$
Both are constant. Reflecting the helix in a mirror (replace $b$ by $-b$) flips the sign of $\tau$ and leaves $\kappa$ alone: torsion records handedness.

### Finite differences

When a curve is given only by a program, replace derivatives by central differences with step $h$: $\alpha' \approx \frac{\alpha(t+h) - \alpha(t-h)}{2h}$ and $\alpha'' \approx \frac{\alpha(t+h) - 2\alpha(t) + \alpha(t-h)}{h^2}$. The truncation error is of order $h^2$, while rounding error grows like $\varepsilon/h^2$ for the second difference.

```python
import math


def cross(u, w):
    return (u[1] * w[2] - u[2] * w[1],
            u[2] * w[0] - u[0] * w[2],
            u[0] * w[1] - u[1] * w[0])


def curvature(f, t, h=1e-3):
    """Curvature of the space curve f at t by central differences."""
    p, c, m = f(t + h), f(t), f(t - h)
    d1 = [(x - y) / (2 * h) for x, y in zip(p, m)]
    d2 = [(x - 2 * z + y) / h**2 for x, y, z in zip(p, m, c)]
    return math.hypot(*cross(d1, d2)) / math.hypot(*d1) ** 3


f = lambda t: (2 * math.cos(t), 2 * math.sin(t), t)
print(round(curvature(f, 0.7), 4))
```

This prints `0.4`, matching $a/(a^2 + b^2) = 2/5$ for $a = 2$, $b = 1$. A match to four digits is a check of the formula and the code, not a proof.

@example
title: Curvature and torsion of a twisted cubic
problem: Find $\kappa$, $\tau$ and the binormal of $\alpha(t) = (t, t^2, t^3)$ at $t = 1$.
step: Differentiate: $\alpha' = (1, 2t, 3t^2)$, $\alpha'' = (0, 2, 6t)$, $\alpha''' = (0, 0, 6)$. At $t = 1$: $\alpha' = (1, 2, 3)$, $\alpha'' = (0, 2, 6)$, $\alpha''' = (0, 0, 6)$.
step: Cross product: $\alpha' \times \alpha'' = (2\cdot 6 - 3\cdot 2,\ 3\cdot 0 - 1\cdot 6,\ 1\cdot 2 - 2\cdot 0) = (6, -6, 2)$, with norm $\sqrt{36 + 36 + 4} = \sqrt{76}$.
step: Speed: $|\alpha'| = \sqrt{14}$, so $\kappa = \sqrt{76}/14^{3/2} \approx 0.1664$.
step: Torsion: $(\alpha' \times \alpha'') \cdot \alpha''' = 2 \cdot 6 = 12$, so $\tau = 12/76 = 3/19 \approx 0.1579$.
step: Binormal: $B = (6, -6, 2)/\sqrt{76} = (3, -3, 1)/\sqrt{19}$.
answer: $\kappa = \sqrt{76}/14^{3/2} \approx 0.1664$, $\tau = 3/19$, $B = (3,-3,1)/\sqrt{19}$.

@example
title: Curvature of a parabola
problem: Find the curvature of the plane curve $\alpha(t) = (t, t^2)$ and where it is largest.
step: Differentiate: $\alpha' = (1, 2t)$ and $\alpha'' = (0, 2)$.
step: Use the plane formula: $x'y'' - y'x'' = 1 \cdot 2 - 2t \cdot 0 = 2$, and $(x'^2 + y'^2)^{3/2} = (1 + 4t^2)^{3/2}$.
step: So $\kappa(t) = \dfrac{2}{(1 + 4t^2)^{3/2}}$.
step: The denominator is smallest at $t = 0$, where $\kappa = 2$; the osculating circle there has radius $1/\kappa = 1/2$. As $|t| \to \infty$ the curvature tends to $0$.
answer: $\kappa(t) = 2(1 + 4t^2)^{-3/2}$, with maximum $2$ at the vertex $t = 0$.

@mistakes
- Using the unit-speed formula $\kappa = |\alpha''|$ for a parametrization with $v \ne 1$: the correct formula is $|\alpha' \times \alpha''|/|\alpha'|^3$, and $|\alpha''|$ also contains the tangential part $v'$.
- Computing the torsion where $\kappa = 0$: the principal normal and binormal do not exist there, and the formula divides by $|\alpha' \times \alpha''|^2 = 0$.
- Forgetting the sign of torsion: $\kappa \ge 0$ always, but $\tau$ can be negative, and it changes sign under a reflection.
- Dividing by $|\alpha'|$ instead of $|\alpha'|^3$, or by $|\alpha' \times \alpha''|$ instead of its square in the torsion.

model:
```python
def cross(u, w):
    return (u[1] * w[2] - u[2] * w[1],
            u[2] * w[0] - u[0] * w[2],
            u[0] * w[1] - u[1] * w[0])


def torsion(f, t, h):
    """Torsion of the space curve f at t by central differences."""
    p1, p2, m1, m2, c = f(t + h), f(t + 2 * h), f(t - h), f(t - 2 * h), f(t)
    d1 = [(a - b) / (2 * h) for a, b in zip(p1, m1)]
    d2 = [(a - 2 * z + b) / h**2 for a, b, z in zip(p1, m1, c)]
    d3 = [(a - 2 * b + 2 * c1 - d) / (2 * h**3)
          for a, b, c1, d in zip(p2, p1, m1, m2)]
    w = cross(d1, d2)
    return sum(x * y for x, y in zip(w, d3)) / sum(x * x for x in w)
```
hint: Evaluate the curve at $t - 2h, t - h, t, t + h, t + 2h$ once, then build the three derivative vectors componentwise.
hint: The numerator is a dot product of the cross product $\alpha' \times \alpha''$ with $\alpha'''$; the denominator is the squared length of the cross product.
solution: Build $\alpha'$, $\alpha''$ and $\alpha'''$ componentwise from the five sample points, take the cross product of the first two, dot it with the third and divide by its squared norm. The sign matters: a left-handed helix must give a negative torsion, which a formula using $|\alpha' \times \alpha''|$ or an absolute value would miss.
