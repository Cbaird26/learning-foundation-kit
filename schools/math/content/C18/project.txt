## Review guide for Differential geometry of curves and surfaces

**Week 1: curves and arc length.** A regular curve has $\alpha' \ne 0$; length is $\int|\alpha'|$ and is invariant under reparametrization. Every regular curve has a unit-speed reparametrization (arc length $s' = |\alpha'|$, invertible with smooth inverse).

**Week 2: curvature and torsion.** For unit speed, $T' = \kappa N$, $N' = -\kappa T + \tau B$, $B' = -\tau N$. In any parametrization, $\kappa = |\alpha'\times\alpha''|/|\alpha'|^3$ and $\tau = (\alpha'\times\alpha'')\cdot\alpha'''/|\alpha'\times\alpha''|^2$. The helix $(a\cos t, a\sin t, bt)$ has $\kappa = a/(a^2 + b^2)$ and $\tau = b/(a^2 + b^2)$.

**Week 3: fundamental theorem.** Smooth $\kappa > 0$ and $\tau$ determine the curve up to a rigid motion; uniqueness by the invariant $\sum\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ of two frames, existence by solving the linear Frenet system. $\tau\equiv 0$ means planar; constant $\kappa, \tau$ means a circular helix.

**Week 4: plane curves.** Signed curvature $\kappa_s = \theta'$, rotation index $\frac1{2\pi}\int\kappa_s \in \mathbb{Z}$, Umlaufsatz ($\pm1$ for simple closed curves), four vertices for strictly convex simple closed curves.

**Week 5: regular surfaces.** Charts with $x_u\times x_v \ne 0$ and homeomorphism onto the image; graphs and regular level sets; $T_pS = \operatorname{span}\{x_u, x_v\} = \nabla f^\perp$.

**Week 6: first fundamental form.** $E, F, G$; length $\int\sqrt{Eu'^2 + 2Fu'v' + Gv'^2}$; area $\iint\sqrt{EG - F^2}$.

**Week 7: Gauss map and second fundamental form.** $dN_p$ is self-adjoint on $T_pS$; $\mathrm{II}(w) = -\langle dN(w), w\rangle$, $e = \langle N, x_{uu}\rangle$ etc.; normal curvature $= \mathrm{II}(w)$.

**Week 8: principal, Gaussian and mean curvature.** $K = (eg - f^2)/(EG - F^2)$, $H = (Eg - 2Ff + Ge)/(2(EG - F^2))$, Euler's formula; for a unit-speed profile of revolution $K = -\rho''/\rho$.

**Week 9: Theorema Egregium.** $K$ is computed from $E, F, G$; for $du^2 + G\,dv^2$, $K = -(\sqrt G)_{uu}/\sqrt G$.

**Week 10: isometries.** Isometries preserve lengths, angles, areas and $K$; the sphere is not locally isometric to the plane; cylinders and cones are.

**Week 11: geodesics.** $\alpha''\parallel N$; constant speed; Christoffel equations; great circles; Clairaut's relation $\rho\cos\psi = $ constant.

**Week 12: Gauss–Bonnet.** Local form with $k_g$ and exterior angles; geodesic triangle excess $= \iint K$; global form $\iint_SK\,dA = 2\pi\chi(S)$.

**Self-check.** Can you prove the Frenet–Serret equations, both parts of the fundamental theorem, $|x_u\times x_v|^2 = EG - F^2$, the self-adjointness of $dN$, the Gauss formula, invariance of $K$ under isometry and Clairaut's relation? Can you compute $K$ of a torus, a catenoid and a graph in three different ways?

**Common errors.** Using $|\alpha''|$ instead of the cross-product formula; dropping the cube on $|\alpha'|$; using $\sqrt{EG}$ when $F \ne 0$; forgetting that $H$ and $\mathrm{II}$ change sign with $N$ while $K$ does not; applying $K = -(\sqrt G)_{uu}/\sqrt G$ when $E \ne 1$; claiming a surface with $K = 0$ is a plane; forgetting the exterior angles in Gauss–Bonnet.

prompt: Let $\beta$ be a smooth unit-speed space curve with $\kappa > 0$ and $\tau/\kappa \equiv c$ constant. Prove that the tangent $T$ makes a constant angle with some fixed nonzero vector $w$.
hint: Look for a combination $w = cT + B$ and differentiate it with the Frenet–Serret equations.
solution: Put $w = cT + B$. By the Frenet–Serret equations, $w' = c\kappa N - \tau N = (c\kappa - \tau)N = 0$ because $\tau = c\kappa$. So $w$ is a constant vector, nonzero since $|w|^2 = c^2 + 1$ ($T\perp B$ and both are unit vectors). Then $\langle T, w\rangle = c\langle T, T\rangle + \langle T, B\rangle = c$ is constant, so the angle $\arccos(c/\sqrt{c^2 + 1})$ between $T$ and $w$ is constant. $\blacksquare$
