@article
The map $z \mapsto z^2$ wraps the circle twice around itself. Every small arc has two preimages, each mapped homeomorphically. A loop in the target may lift to a closed loop or to an open path, depending on how many times it winds. This bookkeeping, carried out for all covers, is what links covering spaces to the fundamental group.

### Covering spaces

A **covering space** of $X$ is a continuous surjection $p\colon \tilde X \to X$ such that each point of $X$ has an open neighborhood $U$ that is **evenly covered**: $p^{-1}(U)$ is a disjoint union of open sets $\tilde U_i$ (the **sheets**), each mapped homeomorphically onto $U$ by $p$. The fiber $p^{-1}(x)$ has the same cardinality on each component of $X$, the number of sheets.

Examples: $\mathbb{R} \to S^1$, $s \mapsto e^{2\pi i s}$; $S^1 \to S^1$, $z \mapsto z^n$; $\mathbb{R}^2 \to T^2$; $S^n \to \mathbb{RP}^n$ with two sheets; $\mathbb{C}^* \to \mathbb{C}^*$, $z \mapsto z^n$. **Non-example:** restrict $s \mapsto e^{2\pi i s}$ to $(0, 3)$. It is a surjective local homeomorphism, but a small arc around $1$ has preimage $(0, \epsilon) \cup (1 - \epsilon, 1 + \epsilon) \cup (2 - \epsilon, 2 + \epsilon) \cup (3 - \epsilon, 3)$, and the two end pieces map onto only half of the arc, so the arc is not evenly covered. Being a local homeomorphism is not enough.

### Lifting properties

For a map $f\colon Y \to X$, a **lift** is $\tilde f\colon Y \to \tilde X$ with $p\tilde f = f$.

**Theorem 1 (unique lifting).** If $Y$ is connected and two lifts $\tilde f_1, \tilde f_2$ of $f$ agree at one point, they agree everywhere. (The set where they agree is open and closed, by evenly covered neighborhoods.)

**Theorem 2 (homotopy lifting).** Let $p\colon \tilde X \to X$ be a covering, $F\colon Y \times I \to X$ a homotopy, and $\tilde F_0\colon Y \times \{0\} \to \tilde X$ a lift of $F|_{Y \times \{0\}}$. Then there is a unique lift $\tilde F\colon Y \times I \to \tilde X$ of $F$ extending $\tilde F_0$. (Stated here without proof; the proof cuts $I$ into short intervals using a Lebesgue number and lifts one strip at a time. For $Y$ a point it is the path lifting property.)

**Proposition 3.** Let $p\colon (\tilde X, \tilde x_0) \to (X, x_0)$ be a covering. Then $p_*\colon \pi_1(\tilde X, \tilde x_0) \to \pi_1(X, x_0)$ is injective, and its image $H$ consists of the classes of loops at $x_0$ whose lifts starting at $\tilde x_0$ are loops. If $\tilde X$ is path-connected, the number of sheets equals the index $[\pi_1(X, x_0) : H]$.

*Proof of the index statement.* For a loop $g$ at $x_0$ let $\tilde g$ be its lift from $\tilde x_0$; send the coset $Hg$ to $\tilde g(1) \in p^{-1}(x_0)$. This is well defined: if $h \in H$, the lift of $h \cdot g$ is a loop at $\tilde x_0$ followed by $\tilde g$, so it ends at $\tilde g(1)$; and path-homotopic loops lift to paths with the same endpoint by Theorem 2. It is surjective because $\tilde X$ is path-connected: a path from $\tilde x_0$ to a point of the fiber projects to a loop. It is injective: if $g_1, g_2$ give the same endpoint, then $g_1 \cdot \bar g_2$ lifts to a loop, so $[g_1][g_2]^{-1} \in H$ and $Hg_1 = Hg_2$. $\blacksquare$

### Monodromy

The group $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ acts on the right on the fiber $p^{-1}(x_0)$ by $\tilde x \cdot [g] = \tilde g_{\tilde x}(1)$, where $\tilde g_{\tilde x}$ is the lift of $g$ starting at $\tilde x$. A finite cover of a wedge of two circles with loops $a, b$ with sheets $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ is therefore given by two permutations: where lifting $a$ and lifting $b$ sends each sheet. A word is read by following letters in order, and $x^{-1}$ by the inverse permutation.

```python
def follow(perms, word, start):
    """Sheet reached by lifting the loop `word` from sheet `start`.

    perms[k][i] is the sheet reached by lifting x_k from sheet i; a letter
    -k means x_k traversed backward.
    """
    sheet = start
    for letter in word:
        perm = perms[abs(letter)]
        sheet = perm[sheet] if letter > 0 else perm.index(sheet)
    return sheet


perms = {1: [1, 2, 0], 2: [0, 2, 1]}
print(follow(perms, [1, 2], 0), follow(perms, [1, 1, 1], 0))
```

This prints `2 0`: the word $ab$ lifts from sheet $0$ to an open path ending at sheet $2$, while $a^3$ lifts to a closed loop on every sheet. Here $a^3$ is in the image of $p_*$ at every basepoint.

### The lifting criterion

**Theorem 4 (lifting criterion).** Let $p\colon (\tilde X, \tilde x_0) \to (X, x_0)$ be a covering and $f\colon (Y, y_0) \to (X, x_0)$ a map with $Y$ **path-connected and locally path-connected**. Then a lift $\tilde f\colon (Y, y_0) \to (\tilde X, \tilde x_0)$ exists if and only if $f_*\pi_1(Y, y_0) \subseteq p_*\pi_1(\tilde X, \tilde x_0)$.

*Proof.* If $\tilde f$ exists, $f_* = p_* \tilde f_*$ has image inside $p_*\pi_1(\tilde X)$. Conversely, for $y \in Y$ choose a path $\gamma$ from $y_0$ to $y$, let $\widetilde{f\gamma}$ be the lift of $f\gamma$ from $\tilde x_0$, and put $\tilde f(y) = \widetilde{f\gamma}(1)$. This is well defined: if $\gamma'$ is another path, then $f(\gamma' \cdot \bar\gamma)$ is a loop whose class lies in $f_*\pi_1(Y) \subseteq H$, so by Proposition 3 its lift from $\tilde x_0$ is a loop, so the lifts of $f\gamma'$ and $f\gamma$ end at the same point. For continuity at $y$, choose an evenly covered neighborhood $U$ of $f(y)$ with the sheet $\tilde U$ containing $\tilde f(y)$, and a path-connected neighborhood $V$ of $y$ with $f(V) \subseteq U$ (here local path-connectedness is used). For $y' \in V$ use a path to $y$ followed by a path in $V$; its image under $f$ lifts inside $\tilde U$, so $\tilde f|_V = (p|_{\tilde U})^{-1} f|_V$, which is continuous. $\blacksquare$

**The hypotheses matter.** (i) Without the inclusion of images, there is no lift: $f = \mathrm{id}\colon S^1 \to S^1$ does not lift through $z \mapsto z^2$ since $\mathbb{Z} \not\subseteq 2\mathbb{Z}$. (ii) Local path-connectedness cannot be dropped: the Warsaw circle is path-connected with trivial $\pi_1$, but it has a map to $S^1$ that does not lift to $\mathbb{R}$ (a standard example, which we state without proof). (iii) The basepoints matter: changing $\tilde x_0$ within the fiber replaces $p_*\pi_1(\tilde X, \tilde x_0)$ by a conjugate subgroup, so the criterion can hold for one choice of $\tilde x_0$ and fail for another.

@example
title: Does a power map lift through another?
problem: Let $p\colon S^1 \to S^1$, $p(z) = z^3$, and let $f\colon S^1 \to S^1$, $f(z) = z^{9}$. Decide whether there is a continuous $g\colon S^1 \to S^1$ with $g(1) = 1$ and $g(z)^3 = z^9$.
step: Identify $\pi_1(S^1, 1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ by winding number (Lesson 2). Then $p_*$ is multiplication by $3$, so $p_*\pi_1 = 3\mathbb{Z}$, and $f_*$ is multiplication by $9$ with image $9\mathbb{Z}$.
step: The space $Y = S^1$ is path-connected and locally path-connected, and $p$ is a covering, so Theorem 4 applies.
step: $9\mathbb{Z} \subseteq 3\mathbb{Z}$, so a lift $g$ exists with $g(1) = 1$ ($p(1) = 1 = f(1)$).
step: In fact $g(z) = z^3$ works, and by Theorem 1 it is the only continuous lift with $g(1) = 1$.
answer: Yes, $g(z) = z^3$, and it is the unique such lift.

@example
title: Index and sheets for a cover of the torus
problem: A connected covering $p\colon \tilde X \to T^2$ has $p_*\pi_1(\tilde X) = \langle (1, 1), (0, 4) \rangle \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^2 = \pi_1(T^2)$. How many sheets does it have?
step: Proposition 3: the number of sheets is the index of the subgroup $H = p_*\pi_1(\tilde X)$.
step: The subgroup $H \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^2$ is a lattice with basis $(1, 1)$ and $(0, 4)$, since these are independent.
step: The index of the lattice with basis the rows of a matrix is the absolute value of its determinant: $\left| \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix} \right| = 4$.
answer: The covering has $4$ sheets.

@mistakes
- Treating a surjective local homeomorphism as a covering: the restriction of $\mathbb{R} \to S^1$ to a bounded open interval is not a covering.
- Forgetting the basepoint: the lifting criterion is stated for pointed maps. Changing $\tilde x_0$ within the fiber changes $p_*\pi_1(\tilde X)$ to a conjugate subgroup.
- Applying the lifting criterion to a space $Y$ that is not locally path-connected, or concluding that a lift exists from "$f_*$ is trivial" when $Y$ is not path-connected.
- Reading $x^{-1}$ in monodromy as applying the same permutation instead of its inverse, which changes which loops close up.

model:
```python
from math import gcd


def word_order(perms, word):
    """Order of the permutation of the sheets induced by the word."""
    n = len(perms[1])

    def step(sheet):
        for letter in word:
            perm = perms[abs(letter)]
            sheet = perm[sheet] if letter > 0 else perm.index(sheet)
        return sheet

    image = [step(s) for s in range(n)]
    order = 1
    seen = [False] * n
    for s in range(n):
        if not seen[s]:
            length, t = 0, s
            while not seen[t]:
                seen[t] = True
                t = image[t]
                length += 1
            order = order * length // gcd(order, length)
    return order
```
hint: First compute where the word sends each sheet, giving a permutation of the sheets.
hint: The order of a permutation is the least common multiple of its cycle lengths, not the largest cycle length.
solution: Lifting the word from every sheet defines a permutation of the sheets (the monodromy of the word). Repeating the word $m$ times lifts to closed loops on all sheets exactly when this permutation raised to the power $m$ is the identity, so the answer is the order of the permutation: the least common multiple of its cycle lengths. In the five-sheet test the word $x_1$ has cycles of length $2$ and $3$, and the answer is $6$, not $3$.

prompt: Let $p\colon (\tilde X, \tilde x_0) \to (X, x_0)$ be a covering. Prove that $p_*\colon \pi_1(\tilde X, \tilde x_0) \to \pi_1(X, x_0)$ is injective, using the homotopy lifting property (Theorem 2) and the unique lifting property (Theorem 1).
hint: Take a loop $\tilde f$ with $p\tilde f$ null-homotopic and lift the null-homotopy.
hint: Look at the four edges of the square after lifting, and use uniqueness of lifts of constant paths.
solution: Let $[\tilde f] \in \ker p_*$, so there is a path homotopy $F\colon I \times I \to X$ from $p\tilde f$ to the constant path $c_{x_0}$, with $F(s, 0) = p\tilde f(s)$, $F(s, 1) = x_0$, and $F(0, t) = F(1, t) = x_0$. By Theorem 2 (with $Y = I$) there is a lift $\tilde F$ of $F$ with $\tilde F(s, 0) = \tilde f(s)$. The edges $t \mapsto \tilde F(0, t)$ and $t \mapsto \tilde F(1, t)$ lift constant paths, and so do $s \mapsto \tilde F(s, 1)$; a lift of a constant path is constant since the fiber is discrete, or by Theorem 1. Since $\tilde F(0, 0) = \tilde f(0) = \tilde x_0$ and $\tilde F(1, 0) = \tilde f(1) = \tilde x_0$, the left and right edges are constant at $\tilde x_0$, and the top edge is constant at $\tilde F(0, 1) = \tilde x_0$. So $\tilde F$ is a path homotopy from $\tilde f$ to the constant loop at $\tilde x_0$, and $[\tilde f] = 1$. $\blacksquare$
rubric: Sets up the null-homotopy of $p\tilde f$ as a map from the square and lifts it by the homotopy lifting property with initial lift $\tilde f$.
rubric: Argues that the three other edges lift constant paths and are therefore constant, using uniqueness of lifts.
rubric: Concludes that $\tilde F$ is a path homotopy in $\tilde X$ from $\tilde f$ to the constant loop.
