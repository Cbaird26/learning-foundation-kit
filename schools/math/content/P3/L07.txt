@article
A graph has a loop exactly when it has more edges than a tree on its vertices would allow. Counting edges against vertices detects one-dimensional holes. Simplicial homology extends this counting to all dimensions by turning the count into linear algebra: a $k$-dimensional hole is a $k$-dimensional cycle (a collection of $k$-simplices with no boundary) that is not itself the boundary of a collection of $(k+1)$-simplices.

### Simplicial complexes and chains

A (finite, abstract) **simplicial complex** $K$ is a finite set of nonempty finite subsets (**simplices**) of a vertex set, closed under taking nonempty subsets. A simplex with $n + 1$ vertices has dimension $n$. A complex is determined by its **facets**, the maximal simplices. Fix a total order on the vertices and write each $n$-simplex as $[v_0, \dots, v_n]$ with $v_0 < \dots < v_n$. The chain group $C_n(K)$ is the free abelian group on the $n$-simplices, and the **boundary** is
$$\partial_n [v_0, \dots, v_n] = \sum_{i=0}^{n} (-1)^i\,[v_0, \dots, \widehat{v_i}, \dots, v_n], \qquad \partial_0 = 0,$$
where the hat means that the vertex is omitted.

**Lemma 1.** $\partial_{n-1}\partial_n = 0$.

*Proof.* In $\partial_{n-1}\partial_n[v_0, \dots, v_n]$ every $(n-2)$-face is obtained by omitting two vertices $v_j, v_i$ with $j < i$. Omitting $v_i$ first and then $v_j$ gives the sign $(-1)^i(-1)^j$. Omitting $v_j$ first moves $v_i$ to position $i - 1$, and then omitting it gives $(-1)^j(-1)^{i-1}$. These two terms cancel, and each face arises exactly twice. $\blacksquare$

So $\operatorname{im}\partial_{n+1} \subseteq \ker\partial_n$, and the **homology** is $H_n(K) = \ker\partial_n / \operatorname{im}\partial_{n+1}$. Its elements are classes of cycles modulo boundaries. The group $H_0(K)$ is free abelian with one generator for each connected component. The structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups gives $H_n(K) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{b_n} \oplus T_n$ with $T_n$ finite; $b_n$ is the **Betti number**. By the rank-nullity theorem over $\mathbb{Q}$,
$$b_n = f_n - \operatorname{rank}\partial_n - \operatorname{rank}\partial_{n+1},$$
where $f_n$ is the number of $n$-simplices and ranks are taken over $\mathbb{Q}$ (the rank over $\mathbb{Q}$ of an integer matrix is the free rank of its image).

**Theorem 2 (Euler–Poincaré).** For a finite simplicial complex of dimension $d$, $\sum_{n=0}^d (-1)^n f_n = \sum_{n=0}^d (-1)^n b_n$.

*Proof.* Put $r_n = \operatorname{rank}\partial_n$ with $r_0 = r_{d+1} = 0$. Then $b_n = f_n - r_n - r_{n+1}$, so $\sum (-1)^n b_n = \sum (-1)^n f_n - \sum (-1)^n r_n - \sum (-1)^n r_{n+1}$, and the last two sums cancel termwise. $\blacksquare$

This number is the **Euler characteristic** $\chi(K)$. The theorem shows that it depends only on the homology. Homology depends only on the homotopy type of the geometric realization $|K|$; that is proved with singular homology in Lessons 8 and 9, and we use it freely from here on.

### Computing by machine

```python
from fractions import Fraction
from itertools import combinations


def faces_of(facets):
    """All nonempty faces of the given facets, as sorted tuples."""
    out = set()
    for f in facets:
        f = tuple(sorted(f))
        for k in range(1, len(f) + 1):
            out.update(combinations(f, k))
    return out


def boundary_matrix(high, low):
    """Matrix of the boundary from the simplices `high` to the simplices `low`."""
    index = {s: i for i, s in enumerate(low)}
    matrix = [[0] * len(high) for _ in low]
    for j, s in enumerate(high):
        for i in range(len(s)):
            matrix[index[s[:i] + s[i + 1:]]][j] = (-1) ** i
    return matrix


def rank(matrix):
    """Rank over the rationals by Gaussian elimination."""
    rows = [[Fraction(x) for x in row] for row in matrix]
    r = 0
    for c in range(len(rows[0]) if rows else 0):
        pivot = next((i for i in range(r, len(rows)) if rows[i][c] != 0), None)
        if pivot is None:
            continue
        rows[r], rows[pivot] = rows[pivot], rows[r]
        for i in range(len(rows)):
            if i != r and rows[i][c] != 0:
                f = rows[i][c] / rows[r][c]
                rows[i] = [a - f * b for a, b in zip(rows[i], rows[r])]
        r += 1
    return r


triangles = [(i, (i + 1) % 7, (i + 3) % 7) for i in range(7)]
triangles += [(i, (i + 2) % 7, (i + 3) % 7) for i in range(7)]
K = faces_of(triangles)
by_dim = [sorted(s for s in K if len(s) == k + 1) for k in range(3)]
print([len(x) for x in by_dim])
print(rank(boundary_matrix(by_dim[1], by_dim[0])),
      rank(boundary_matrix(by_dim[2], by_dim[1])))
```

The fourteen triangles $\{i, i+1, i+3\}$ and $\{i, i+2, i+3\}$ (indices mod $7$) triangulate the torus with $7$ vertices. The program prints `[7, 21, 14]` and then `6 13`. So $b_0 = 7 - 6 = 1$, $b_1 = 21 - 6 - 13 = 2$, $b_2 = 14 - 13 = 1$, and $\chi = 0$, as expected for $T^2$. A computation on one triangulation is evidence, not a proof of what $H_*(T^2)$ is; the theory proves independence of the triangulation.

**What rationals cannot see.** The six-vertex triangulation of $\mathbb{RP}^2$ with ten triangles $123, 134, 145, 156, 162, 235, 346, 452, 563, 624$ has $\chi = 6 - 15 + 10 = 1$, and its Betti numbers over $\mathbb{Q}$ are $(1, 0, 0)$. But $H_1(\mathbb{RP}^2; \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ (Lesson 4), so $T_1 \ne 0$: the torsion is invisible to ranks over $\mathbb{Q}$ and shows up when you compute ranks over the field with two elements, where the Betti numbers become $(1, 1, 1)$.

@example
title: A cycle that is not a boundary
problem: Let $K$ be the boundary of a triangle: vertices $0, 1, 2$ and edges $[0,1], [0,2], [1,2]$, with no $2$-simplex. Compute $H_0(K)$ and $H_1(K)$.
step: The chain groups are $C_0 = \mathbb{Z}^3$, $C_1 = \mathbb{Z}^3$ with basis $[0,1], [0,2], [1,2]$, and $C_2 = 0$.
step: $\partial_1[i,j] = [j] - [i]$, so in the bases the matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ with rows indexed by $[0], [1], [2]$. Its rank is $2$ (the rows sum to zero and any two are independent).
step: $\ker\partial_1$ has rank $3 - 2 = 1$. The chain $z = [0,1] + [1,2] - [0,2]$ satisfies $\partial z = ([1]-[0]) + ([2]-[1]) - ([2]-[0]) = 0$ and generates the kernel.
step: Since $C_2 = 0$ the group $\operatorname{im}\partial_2$ is zero, so $H_1(K) = \ker\partial_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by $z$. Also $H_0 = C_0/\operatorname{im}\partial_1$ is $\mathbb{Z}^3$ modulo the span of $[1]-[0]$ and $[2]-[0]$, which is free of rank $1$ (generated by the class of $[0]$).
answer: $H_0 \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $H_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the cycle going once around the triangle.

@example
title: Filling in the triangle
problem: Add the $2$-simplex $[0,1,2]$ to the complex of the previous example. What are the homology groups?
step: Now $C_2 = \mathbb{Z}$, and $\partial_2[0,1,2] = [1,2] - [0,2] + [0,1] = z$.
step: So $\operatorname{im}\partial_2 = \mathbb{Z}z = \ker\partial_1$, and $H_1 = 0$.
step: $\ker\partial_2 = 0$ because $\partial_2$ sends the generator to $z \ne 0$, so $H_2 = 0$.
step: $H_0 \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is unchanged. The filled triangle has the homology of a point, as a contractible space should.
answer: $H_0 \cong \mathbb{Z}$, $H_1 = H_2 = 0$.

@mistakes
- Forgetting the sign $(-1)^i$ in the boundary formula: without it $\partial\partial \ne 0$ and the homology is not defined.
- Computing the rank of a boundary matrix with floating-point numbers or modulo a prime and reading it as the rank over $\mathbb{Q}$: torsion in $H_n$ changes the rank mod $p$ for primes dividing the torsion.
- Assuming that $b_n = f_n - \operatorname{rank}\partial_{n+1}$: you must also subtract the rank of $\partial_n$, the dimension of the boundaries to be killed from the cycles.
- Taking the Euler characteristic of a triangulation to prove two spaces are homeomorphic: equal $\chi$ is necessary for homotopy equivalence, never sufficient ($S^1$ and $S^3$ both have $\chi = 0$).

model:
```python
from itertools import combinations


def betti_mod2(facets):
    """Betti numbers over F_2 of the complex generated by the facets."""
    faces = set()
    for f in facets:
        f = tuple(sorted(f))
        for k in range(1, len(f) + 1):
            faces.update(combinations(f, k))
    top = max(len(s) for s in faces) - 1
    by_dim = [sorted(s for s in faces if len(s) == k + 1)
              for k in range(top + 1)]

    def rank_mod2(high, low):
        index = {s: i for i, s in enumerate(low)}
        rows = []
        for s in high:
            mask = 0
            for i in range(len(s)):
                mask ^= 1 << index[s[:i] + s[i + 1:]]
            rows.append(mask)
        rank = 0
        for bit in range(len(low)):
            pivot = next((r for r in range(rank, len(rows))
                          if rows[r] >> bit & 1), None)
            if pivot is None:
                continue
            rows[rank], rows[pivot] = rows[pivot], rows[rank]
            for r in range(len(rows)):
                if r != rank and rows[r] >> bit & 1:
                    rows[r] ^= rows[rank]
            rank += 1
        return rank

    ranks = [0] + [rank_mod2(by_dim[k], by_dim[k - 1])
                   for k in range(1, top + 1)] + [0]
    return [len(by_dim[k]) - ranks[k] - ranks[k + 1] for k in range(top + 1)]
```
hint: List the simplices of each dimension, then treat each column of the boundary matrix as a bit mask and eliminate with XOR.
hint: Mod $2$ the boundary of a simplex is just the set of its facets, with no signs.
solution: Over $\mathbb{F}_2$ every sign is $+1$, so the boundary of a simplex is the sum of its codimension-one faces. The rank is found by Gaussian elimination, where adding rows is XOR of bit masks. The Betti formula is the same as over $\mathbb{Q}$. The nine-vertex Klein bottle gives $(1, 2, 1)$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$, whereas over $\mathbb{Q}$ it gives $(1, 1, 0)$: the difference is the $2$-torsion in $H_1$, so a solution that works over the rationals fails this test.

@practice self
prompt: Let $\Delta^n$ be the simplicial complex of all nonempty subsets of $\{0, 1, \dots, n\}$, $n \ge 1$. Prove that $\tilde H_k(\Delta^n) = 0$ for all $k$, that is, $H_0 \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $H_k = 0$ for $k \ge 1$. Deduce that the boundary $\partial\Delta^n$ (all proper subsets) has $H_{n-1} \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $H_k = 0$ for $0 < k < n - 1$ when $n \ge 2$.
hint: Define $h\colon C_k \to C_{k+1}$ by $h[v_0, \dots, v_k] = [0, v_0, \dots, v_k]$ if $v_0 \ne 0$ and $h = 0$ otherwise, and compute $\partial h + h\partial$.
hint: For the second part, compare cycles of $\partial\Delta^n$ in degree $k < n - 1$ with those of $\Delta^n$, which has the same $k$- and $(k+1)$-simplices when $k + 1 < n$.
solution: Write simplices with increasing vertices. Define $h$ as in the hint (a cone from the vertex $0$). For a simplex $\sigma = [v_0, \dots, v_k]$ with $v_0 \ne 0$ and $k \ge 1$, $\partial h\sigma = \partial[0, v_0, \dots, v_k] = \sigma - [0, \partial\sigma] = \sigma - h\partial\sigma$, so $(\partial h + h\partial)\sigma = \sigma$. For $\sigma = [0, w_1, \dots, w_k]$ with $k \ge 1$: $h\sigma = 0$, and $\partial\sigma = [w_1, \dots, w_k] + (\text{terms containing } 0)$, so $h\partial\sigma = [0, w_1, \dots, w_k] = \sigma$ because $h$ kills the terms containing $0$; again $(\partial h + h\partial)\sigma = \sigma$. Hence for $k \ge 1$ and a cycle $z \in C_k$, $z = \partial h z + h\partial z = \partial(hz)$, so $z$ is a boundary and $H_k = 0$. For $k = 0$ and a vertex $[v]$, $v \ne 0$: $\partial h[v] = [v] - [0]$, so every $0$-chain $\sum n_v [v]$ is homologous to $(\sum n_v)[0]$, and the augmentation $\sum n_v$ vanishes on boundaries; thus $H_0 \cong \mathbb{Z}$. For the boundary complex: $\partial\Delta^n$ contains all simplices of $\Delta^n$ of dimension at most $n - 1$. So for $1 \le k < n - 1$ its $k$-cycles and $(k+1)$-chains (dimension $k + 1 \le n - 1$) agree with those of $\Delta^n$, giving $H_k(\partial\Delta^n) = 0$; $H_0 \cong \mathbb{Z}$ similarly when $n \ge 2$. In degree $n - 1$ there is no $n$-simplex, so $H_{n-1}(\partial\Delta^n) = \ker\partial_{n-1}$; this is the group of $(n-1)$-cycles of $\Delta^n$, which equals the group of boundaries $\partial(C_n(\Delta^n)) = \partial(\mathbb{Z}[0, \dots, n]) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ since $H_{n-1}(\Delta^n) = 0$, and $\partial_n$ is injective on $C_n(\Delta^n) = \mathbb{Z}$ because $H_n(\Delta^n) = 0$ forces $\ker\partial_n = 0$. So $H_{n-1}(\partial\Delta^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. $\blacksquare$

@quiz ms
prompt: Which of these statements about a finite simplicial complex $K$ are true?

choice*: $H_0(K)$ is free abelian of rank equal to the number of connected components.

A reordering of the vertices changes signs of generators of the chain groups but gives an isomorphic chain complex, so the homology groups are isomorphic.
