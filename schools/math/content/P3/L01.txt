@article
A rubber band lies flat on a table with a thumbtack standing in the middle of the loop. You cannot shrink the band to a point without lifting it over the tack. A band on a table with no tack shrinks easily. Algebraic topology makes this precise by attaching to a space $X$ and a point $x_0$ a group $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ that records which loops can be shrunk. This course is built on invariants of this kind, and every one of them must be shown to be invariant under the right notion of equivalence. That is the work of this lesson.

### Homotopy

Let $I = [0, 1]$. A **homotopy** between maps $f, g\colon X \to Y$ is a continuous $F\colon X \times I \to Y$ with $F(x, 0) = f(x)$ and $F(x, 1) = g(x)$; write $f \simeq g$. A **path homotopy** between paths $f, g\colon I \to X$ with the same endpoints is a homotopy that fixes both endpoints for all $t$; write $f \simeq_p g$. A map $f\colon X \to Y$ is a **homotopy equivalence** if there is $g\colon Y \to X$ with $gf \simeq \mathrm{id}_X$ and $fg \simeq \mathrm{id}_Y$; then $X$ and $Y$ have the same **homotopy type**. A space is **contractible** if it is homotopy equivalent to a point. A subspace $A \subseteq X$ is a **deformation retract** if there is a homotopy $r_t\colon X \to X$ with $r_0 = \mathrm{id}$, $r_1(X) \subseteq A$ and $r_t|_A = \mathrm{id}_A$ for all $t$. The inclusion of a deformation retract is a homotopy equivalence, with $r_1$ as inverse.

For paths $f, g$ with $f(1) = g(0)$ the **product** $f \cdot g$ runs $f$ at double speed on $[0, \tfrac12]$ and then $g$ on $[\tfrac12, 1]$. The **inverse** is $\bar f(s) = f(1 - s)$, and $c_x$ is the constant path at $x$.

### The fundamental group

**Definition.** $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is the set of classes $[f]$ of loops at $x_0$ under $\simeq_p$, with $[f][g] = [f \cdot g]$.

**Theorem 1.** For paths $f, g, h$ that can be composed: (a) $f \simeq_p f'$ and $g \simeq_p g'$ imply $f \cdot g \simeq_p f' \cdot g'$; (b) $(f \cdot g) \cdot h \simeq_p f \cdot (g \cdot h)$; (c) $c_{f(0)} \cdot f \simeq_p f \simeq_p f \cdot c_{f(1)}$; (d) $f \cdot \bar f \simeq_p c_{f(0)}$ and $\bar f \cdot f \simeq_p c_{f(1)}$. Hence $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is a group.

*Proof.* (a) If $F$ and $G$ are path homotopies, then $H(s,t) = F(2s,t)$ for $s \le \tfrac12$ and $G(2s-1,t)$ for $s \ge \tfrac12$ is a path homotopy, continuous because $F(1,t) = f(1) = g(0) = G(0,t)$. For (b) and (c) use reparametrization: if $\varphi\colon I \to I$ is continuous with $\varphi(0) = 0$ and $\varphi(1) = 1$, then $f\varphi \simeq_p f$ via $f((1-t)\varphi(s) + ts)$. Both $(f \cdot g) \cdot h$ and $f \cdot (g \cdot h)$ are reparametrizations of one another by a piecewise linear $\varphi$ with $\varphi(0) = 0$, $\varphi(1) = 1$, and likewise $c \cdot f = f\varphi$ with $\varphi(s) = \max(0, 2s - 1)$. For (d) take $H(s,t) = f(2s(1-t))$ for $s \le \tfrac12$ and $f((2 - 2s)(1-t))$ for $s \ge \tfrac12$; at $t = 0$ it is $f \cdot \bar f$, at $t = 1$ it is constant, and the endpoints stay at $f(0)$. The second statement in (d) follows by applying the first to $\bar f$. $\blacksquare$

**Change of basepoint.** If $h$ is a path from $x_0$ to $x_1$, then $\beta_h[f] = [h \cdot f \cdot \bar h]$ defines an isomorphism $\pi_1(X, x_1) \to \pi_1(X, x_0)$ with inverse $\beta_{\bar h}$ (Theorem 1). So for path-connected $X$ the isomorphism class of $\pi_1$ does not depend on the basepoint, but the isomorphisms depend on $h$ by conjugation.

**Induced maps.** A map $f\colon (X, x_0) \to (Y, y_0)$ induces $f_*[\gamma] = [f\gamma]$, a homomorphism with $(gf)_* = g_* f_*$ and $(\mathrm{id})_* = \mathrm{id}$. So homeomorphisms induce isomorphisms.

### Homotopy invariance

**Theorem 2.** Let $F$ be a homotopy from $f$ to $g\colon X \to Y$, let $x_0 \in X$, and let $h(t) = F(x_0, t)$, a path from $f(x_0)$ to $g(x_0)$. Then $f_* = \beta_h \circ g_*\colon \pi_1(X, x_0) \to \pi_1(Y, f(x_0))$.

*Proof.* Let $\gamma$ be a loop at $x_0$ and put $G(s,t) = F(\gamma(s), t)$ on the square $I \times I$. Its bottom edge is $f\gamma$, its top edge is $g\gamma$, and both vertical edges are $h$ because $\gamma(0) = \gamma(1) = x_0$. In the convex square, the path bottom-then-right and the path left-then-top have the same endpoints, so they are homotopic rel endpoints by a straight-line homotopy. Composing with $G$ gives $f\gamma \cdot h \simeq_p h \cdot g\gamma$, so $f\gamma \simeq_p h \cdot g\gamma \cdot \bar h$. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 3.** If $f\colon X \to Y$ is a homotopy equivalence, then $f_*\colon \pi_1(X, x_0) \to \pi_1(Y, f(x_0))$ is an isomorphism for every $x_0 \in X$.

*Proof.* Let $g$ be a homotopy inverse. Theorem 2 applied to $gf \simeq \mathrm{id}$ shows that $g_* f_*$ is $\beta_h$ for a suitable path $h$, an isomorphism, so $g_*\colon \pi_1(Y, f(x_0)) \to \pi_1(X, gf(x_0))$ is surjective. Applied to $fg \simeq \mathrm{id}$, it shows $f_* g_*$ is an isomorphism, so this $g_*$ is injective. Thus $g_*$ is bijective, and $f_* = g_*^{-1}(g_* f_*)$ is a bijective homomorphism. $\blacksquare$

Hence a contractible space has trivial $\pi_1$, and a space that deformation retracts onto $A$ has the same $\pi_1$ as $A$. Homotopy equivalence is much weaker than homeomorphism: a closed disk, an open disk and a point all have the same homotopy type. **Warning:** a homotopy that moves the basepoint changes $f_*$ by conjugation. The loops $a$ and $bab^{-1}$ in a figure eight are freely homotopic, yet their images are different elements of $\pi_1$ (Lesson 3 shows the group is free).

### Retractions

A **retraction** is a map $r\colon X \to A$ with $r|_A = \mathrm{id}_A$, where $A \subseteq X$. Then $ri = \mathrm{id}_A$, so $r_* i_* = \mathrm{id}$: the inclusion-induced map $i_*$ is injective and $r_*$ is surjective. This one-line argument will prove the Brouwer fixed point theorem in dimension two (Lesson 2) and in every dimension (Lesson 12).

@example
title: The punctured plane has the homotopy type of a circle
problem: Show that $S^1 = \{|x| = 1\}$ is a deformation retract of $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$, and conclude that $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}, 1) \cong \pi_1(S^1, 1)$.
step: Define $r_t(x) = (1-t)\,x + t\,x/|x|$ for $x \neq 0$ and $t \in I$. It is continuous on $(\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}) \times I$.
step: It never takes the value $0$: $r_t(x) = \big((1-t) + t/|x|\big)\,x$ is a positive multiple of $x$, since $1 - t \ge 0$, $t/|x| \ge 0$, and the two cannot both vanish. So $r_t$ maps into $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$.
step: Check the three conditions: $r_0 = \mathrm{id}$; $r_1(x) = x/|x| \in S^1$; and for $|x| = 1$ we get $r_t(x) = x$ for all $t$.
step: So $S^1$ is a deformation retract, the inclusion is a homotopy equivalence, and by Corollary 3 the inclusion induces an isomorphism on $\pi_1$ at the basepoint $1 \in S^1$.
answer: $S^1$ is a deformation retract of $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$, so the two have isomorphic fundamental groups.

@example
title: When does the choice of path in a basepoint change not matter?
problem: Let $X$ be path-connected with $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ abelian, and let $h, h'$ be two paths from $x_0$ to a point $x_1$. Show that $\beta_h = \beta_{h'}$ as maps $\pi_1(X, x_1) \to \pi_1(X, x_0)$.
step: State what is to be shown: $[h \cdot f \cdot \bar h] = [h' \cdot f \cdot \bar h']$ for every loop $f$ at $x_1$.
step: Put $\ell = h \cdot \bar h'$, a loop at $x_0$. Using Theorem 1, $h \simeq_p \ell \cdot h'$, because $\ell \cdot h' = (h \cdot \bar h') \cdot h' \simeq_p h \cdot (\bar h' \cdot h') \simeq_p h \cdot c_{x_1} \simeq_p h$.
step: Therefore $h \cdot f \cdot \bar h \simeq_p \ell \cdot (h' \cdot f \cdot \bar h') \cdot \bar\ell$, that is, $\beta_h[f] = [\ell]\,\beta_{h'}[f]\,[\ell]^{-1}$.
step: The group $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is abelian, so conjugation by $[\ell]$ is the identity, and $\beta_h[f] = \beta_{h'}[f]$.
answer: In a path-connected space with abelian $\pi_1$, the isomorphisms between fundamental groups at different points are canonical.

@mistakes
- Treating "simply connected" as "contractible": $S^2$ is simply connected (Lesson 2) but not contractible (Lesson 8 shows $H_2(S^2) \neq 0$). Trivial $\pi_1$ is necessary for contractibility, not sufficient.
- Claiming that homotopic maps induce the same homomorphism with no condition on the basepoint: $f_* = \beta_h g_*$, which is conjugation by the class of the track loop $h$ when $f(x_0) = g(x_0)$.
- Writing $\pi_1(X)$ for a space that is not path-connected: the group depends on the path component of the basepoint.
- Using a retraction in the wrong direction: for a retraction $r\colon X \to A$ the inclusion-induced map $i_*$ is injective and $r_*$ is surjective, not the other way around.

@practice mc
prompt: Let $X$ be path-connected. Which statement is equivalent to $\pi_1(X, x_0) = 1$?
choice*: Any two paths in $X$ with the same endpoints are homotopic rel endpoints.
choice: $X$ is contractible.
choice: $X$ is homeomorphic to a convex subset of some $\mathbb{R}^n$.
choice: Every continuous map $X \to X$ is homotopic to the identity.
hint: If $f$ and $g$ are paths from $p$ to $q$, consider the loop $f \cdot \bar g$.
solution: If $\pi_1 = 1$ and $f, g$ are paths from $p$ to $q$, then $\pi_1(X, p) = 1$ too, because $X$ is path-connected and the groups at different points are isomorphic. So the loop $f \cdot \bar g$ at $p$ is null-homotopic, and $f \simeq_p f \cdot \bar g \cdot g \simeq_p g$. Conversely, taking $g$ constant shows that every loop is null-homotopic. The sphere $S^2$ is simply connected but not contractible and not convex, so the other statements are strictly stronger.

@practice self
prompt: Let $A \subseteq X$ and let $r\colon X \to A$ be a retraction, so $r|_A = \mathrm{id}_A$. Let $a \in A$ and let $i\colon A \to X$ be the inclusion. Prove that $i_*\colon \pi_1(A, a) \to \pi_1(X, a)$ is injective and $r_*\colon \pi_1(X, a) \to \pi_1(A, a)$ is surjective. Then show that if $A$ is a point of $X$ and $r$ is the constant map, nothing is learned, but if $X = \mathbb{R}^2$ and $A$ is a circle $S^1 \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$, there is no retraction $\mathbb{R}^2 \to S^1$, given that $\pi_1(S^1) \neq 1$.
hint: Compute $r \circ i$.
hint: Functoriality: $(r \circ i)_* = r_* \circ i_*$.
solution: Since $r|_A = \mathrm{id}_A$ we have $r \circ i = \mathrm{id}_A$ as maps of pointed spaces $(A, a) \to (A, a)$. By functoriality, $r_* \circ i_* = (ri)_* = \mathrm{id}$ on $\pi_1(A, a)$. A map with a left inverse is injective, so $i_*$ is injective; a map with a right inverse is surjective, so $r_*$ is surjective. If $A = S^1 \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ were a retract of $\mathbb{R}^2$, then $i_*\colon \pi_1(S^1) \to \pi_1(\mathbb{R}^2) = 1$ would be injective, which forces $\pi_1(S^1) = 1$, contradicting $\pi_1(S^1) \ne 1$. For a point $A = \{a\}$ the group $\pi_1(A) = 1$ injects into anything, so the argument gives no information.
rubric: Identifies $r \circ i = \mathrm{id}_A$ and applies functoriality to get $r_* i_* = \mathrm{id}$.
rubric: Deduces injectivity of $i_*$ and surjectivity of $r_*$ from the one-sided inverse, not both from the same statement.
rubric: Uses that $\mathbb{R}^2$ is contractible, so $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^2) = 1$, to reach the contradiction.

@practice self
prompt: Let $G$ be a topological group (a group with a topology in which multiplication $G \times G \to G$ and inversion are continuous) with identity $e$. Prove that $\pi_1(G, e)$ is abelian.
hint: For loops $f, g$ at $e$ let $f * g$ be the pointwise product $s \mapsto f(s) g(s)$. Compare $f \cdot g$ with $f * g$.
hint: Write $f \cdot g$ as the pointwise product of the two loops $f \cdot c_e$ and $c_e \cdot g$.
solution: Let $f, g$ be loops at $e$ and let $c$ be the constant loop at $e$. The pointwise product $u * v$ of loops at $e$ is a loop at $e$, and if $u \simeq_p u'$ and $v \simeq_p v'$ then $u * v \simeq_p u' * v'$ by multiplying the two path homotopies pointwise, which is continuous. Now compute pointwise: $(f \cdot c) * (c \cdot g)$ equals $f(2s) \cdot e$ for $s \le \tfrac12$ and $e \cdot g(2s-1)$ for $s \ge \tfrac12$, which is exactly $f \cdot g$. Since $f \cdot c \simeq_p f$ and $c \cdot g \simeq_p g$ by Theorem 1, we get $f \cdot g \simeq_p f * g$. Similarly $(c \cdot f) * (g \cdot c)$ equals $e \cdot g(2s)$ for $s \le \tfrac12$ and $f(2s-1) \cdot e$ for $s \ge \tfrac12$, which is $g \cdot f$; and $c \cdot f \simeq_p f$, $g \cdot c \simeq_p g$ give $g \cdot f \simeq_p f * g$. Therefore $f \cdot g \simeq_p g \cdot f$, that is, $[f][g] = [g][f]$. $\blacksquare$
rubric: Defines the pointwise product of loops and justifies that it respects path homotopy using continuity of multiplication.
rubric: Shows both $f \cdot g$ and $g \cdot f$ are path homotopic to $f * g$ by writing each as a pointwise product of reparametrized loops.
rubric: Concludes commutativity of classes, not just equality of paths.
