@review
**Week 1: homotopy and $\pi_1$.** Homotopy, path homotopy, homotopy equivalence, deformation retraction; $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is a group (reparametrization), change of basepoint $\beta_h$, induced maps, and $f_* = \beta_h g_*$ for a homotopy with track $h$. A homotopy equivalence induces isomorphisms. A retraction gives $i_*$ injective.

**Week 2: circle and spheres.** Lifting through $\mathbb{R} \to S^1$ gives $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ by winding number. A union of two open, path-connected, simply connected sets with path-connected intersection is simply connected, so $\pi_1(S^n) = 1$ for $n \ge 2$. Applications: fundamental theorem of algebra, no retraction $D^2 \to S^1$, $\mathbb{R}^2 \not\cong \mathbb{R}^n$.

**Weeks 3 and 4: van Kampen and cell complexes.** Two open path-connected sets, path-connected intersection: $\pi_1(X) = (\pi_1 U * \pi_1 V)/N$. Attaching a $2$-cell kills the normal closure of the attaching loop; cells of dimension $\ge 3$ change nothing. Surface groups $\langle a_i, b_i \mid \prod [a_i, b_i] \rangle$ and $\langle a_i \mid \prod a_i^2 \rangle$; abelianization by exponent sums.

**Weeks 5 and 6: covers.** Evenly covered neighborhoods; unique and homotopy lifting; $p_*$ injective; sheets $=$ index. Lifting criterion needs $Y$ path-connected and locally path-connected. Classification needs $X$ path-connected, locally path-connected, semilocally simply connected: subgroups $\leftrightarrow$ covers, conjugacy classes $\leftrightarrow$ covers without basepoint. $\mathrm{Deck} \cong N(H)/H$.

**Weeks 7 and 8: simplicial and singular homology.** $\partial\partial = 0$, $H_n = \ker\partial/\operatorname{im}\partial$, $b_n = f_n - \operatorname{rank}\partial_n - \operatorname{rank}\partial_{n+1}$, Euler–Poincaré. Singular homology: chain homotopy and the prism operator give homotopy invariance; $H_1 = \pi_1^{ab}$.

**Weeks 9 and 10: exact sequences, excision, Mayer–Vietoris.** Long exact sequence of a short exact sequence of chain complexes; long exact sequence of a pair; excision needs $\bar Z \subseteq \operatorname{int} A$; good pairs give $\tilde H_n(X/A)$. $\tilde H_k(S^n) = \mathbb{Z}$ for $k = n$. Mayer–Vietoris needs interiors covering $X$.

**Weeks 11 and 12: cellular homology and degree.** $H_n = \ker d_n/\operatorname{im} d_{n+1}$; $\chi = \sum (-1)^n c_n = \sum (-1)^n b_n$; covers multiply $\chi$. Degree of sphere maps: reflection $-1$, antipodal $(-1)^{n+1}$, orthogonal $\det A$; Brouwer; hairy ball iff $n$ odd.

**Key formulas.** $\chi(\Sigma_g) = 2 - 2g$, $\chi(N_h) = 2 - h$; $H_1(N_h) = \mathbb{Z}^{h-1} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2$; $H_*(\mathbb{RP}^n)$ from $d_k = 1 + (-1)^k$; $\chi(\tilde X) = n\chi(X)$.

**Self-check.** Can you (1) prove that $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is a group, (2) state van Kampen with hypotheses, (3) name the hypotheses of the classification theorem, (4) derive the long exact sequence of a pair, (5) compute the homology of a surface from its word, (6) prove Brouwer from $\tilde H_{n-1}(S^{n-1}) \ne 0$? Can you give the counterexample for each dropped hypothesis?

**Common errors.** Computing $H_1$ as $\pi_1$ without abelianizing; forgetting the normal closure; dropping the connectedness of the intersection; confusing normal subgroup with normal cover; applying excision with $\bar Z$ only inside $A$; reporting torsion-free answers from rank computations over $\mathbb{Q}$; treating Euler characteristic as a complete invariant.

@exam self
prompt: Let $X$ be the space obtained from a wedge of two circles $S^1 \vee S^1$ with loops $a, b$ by attaching one $2$-cell along the loop $a^2b^3$. (a) Give a presentation of $\pi_1(X)$ and compute its abelianization. (b) Compute $H_n(X; \mathbb{Z})$ for all $n$. (c) Prove that $X$ is not homotopy equivalent to $S^1$, although it has the same integral homology as $S^1$. (d) Prove that $X$ has exactly one connected $2$-sheeted covering space up to isomorphism, and find the Euler characteristic of its total space.
hint: Use the attaching theorem for $2$-cells for (a), and the cellular chain complex with $d_2 = (2, 3)$ for (b).
hint: For (c) map $\pi_1(X)$ onto $S_3$ by sending $a$ to a transposition and $b$ to a $3$-cycle. For (d) count homomorphisms onto $\mathbb{Z}/2$.
solution: (a) By the attaching theorem, $\pi_1(X) = \langle a, b \mid a^2b^3 \rangle$. The exponent-sum row is $(2, 3)$; since $\gcd(2, 3) = 1$ the vector is primitive, so the abelianization is $\mathbb{Z}^2/\mathbb{Z}(2, 3) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. (b) The cellular chain complex has $C_0 = \mathbb{Z}$, $C_1 = \mathbb{Z}^2$, $C_2 = \mathbb{Z}$, with $d_1 = 0$ (one vertex) and $d_2(e) = 2a + 3b$ by the cellular boundary formula. So $H_0 = \mathbb{Z}$, $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}^2/\mathbb{Z}(2,3) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, $H_2 = \ker d_2 = 0$ because $d_2$ is injective, and $H_n = 0$ for $n \ge 3$: the homology of $S^1$. (c) Define $\varphi\colon F(a, b) \to S_3$ by $a \mapsto (1\ 2)$, $b \mapsto (1\ 2\ 3)$. Then $\varphi(a^2b^3) = 1 \cdot 1 = 1$, so $\varphi$ induces a homomorphism $\pi_1(X) \to S_3$. Its image contains $(1\ 2)$ and $(1\ 2\ 3)$, which do not commute ($(1\ 2)(1\ 2\ 3) = (2\ 3)$ while $(1\ 2\ 3)(1\ 2) = (1\ 3)$ composing right to left), so the image is nonabelian. Hence $\pi_1(X)$ is nonabelian and not isomorphic to $\pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$; a homotopy equivalence would induce an isomorphism of fundamental groups (Lesson 1), so $X \not\simeq S^1$. (d) A connected $2$-sheeted covering corresponds to a subgroup of index $2$ of $\pi_1(X)$ (classification theorem: $X$ is a CW complex, hence locally path-connected and semilocally simply connected, and path-connected), and conjugacy is trivial since index-$2$ subgroups are normal. Such a subgroup is the kernel of a surjection $\pi_1(X) \to \mathbb{Z}/2$, that is a pair $(\alpha, \beta) \in (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$, not both $0$, with $2\alpha + 3\beta = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}/2$. This says $\beta = 0$, so the only choice is $(\alpha, \beta) = (1, 0)$. So there is exactly one subgroup of index $2$, hence one covering. A $2$-sheeted covering of a finite CW complex has $\chi(\tilde X) = 2\chi(X)$ and $\chi(X) = 1 - 2 + 1 = 0$, so the total space has Euler characteristic $0$. $\blacksquare$
