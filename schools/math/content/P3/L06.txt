@article
The circle has one connected cover for each positive integer $n$ (the map $z \mapsto z^n$) and one infinite cover, the line. These correspond exactly to the subgroups $n\mathbb{Z}$ and $0$ of $\pi_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$. This is not a coincidence: for reasonable spaces, subgroups of $\pi_1$ and connected covers are the same thing. The classification theorem reduces a topological problem, listing covers, to an algebraic one, listing subgroups.

### The classification

A space $X$ is **semilocally simply connected** if every point has a neighborhood $U$ such that every loop in $U$ is null-homotopic in $X$ (not necessarily in $U$). The Hawaiian earring, the union of the circles in $\mathbb{R}^2$ of radius $1/n$ centered at $(1/n, 0)$, is not semilocally simply connected at the origin.

**Theorem 1 (classification).** Let $X$ be path-connected, locally path-connected and semilocally simply connected, with basepoint $x_0$. For every subgroup $H \le \pi_1(X, x_0)$ there is a path-connected covering $p\colon (X_H, \tilde x_0) \to (X, x_0)$ with $p_*\pi_1(X_H, \tilde x_0) = H$. Two path-connected coverings with basepoints are isomorphic (by a homeomorphism commuting with the projections and preserving the basepoints) if and only if they give the same subgroup. Without basepoints, isomorphism classes of path-connected coverings correspond to conjugacy classes of subgroups of $\pi_1(X, x_0)$.

*Existence (outline).* Let $X_H$ be the set of equivalence classes of paths $\gamma$ from $x_0$, with $\gamma \sim \gamma'$ if $\gamma(1) = \gamma'(1)$ and $[\gamma \cdot \bar\gamma'] \in H$. The topology is generated by sets $U_{[\gamma]}$ of classes $[\gamma \cdot \eta]$ for $\eta$ a path inside a fixed path-connected neighborhood $U$ of $\gamma(1)$ on which loops are null-homotopic in $X$. Semilocal simple connectivity is what makes these sets give an evenly covered cover. The details are in Hatcher.

*Uniqueness (complete proof).* Let $p_i\colon (\tilde X_i, \tilde x_i) \to (X, x_0)$ be two path-connected coverings with the same image subgroup $H$. The space $\tilde X_1$ is path-connected, and locally path-connected because $X$ is and $p_1$ is a local homeomorphism. By the lifting criterion (Lesson 5) applied to $p_1$ and the covering $p_2$, there is $\varphi\colon (\tilde X_1, \tilde x_1) \to (\tilde X_2, \tilde x_2)$ with $p_2\varphi = p_1$; symmetrically there is $\psi\colon \tilde X_2 \to \tilde X_1$ with $p_1\psi = p_2$. The composite $\psi\varphi$ is a lift of $p_1$ through $p_1$ fixing $\tilde x_1$, as is the identity, so $\psi\varphi = \mathrm{id}$ by unique lifting; likewise $\varphi\psi = \mathrm{id}$. Conversely an isomorphism preserves the image subgroup. For the version without basepoints: changing the basepoint $\tilde x_0$ to another point of the fiber replaces $H$ by a conjugate, and every conjugate arises. $\blacksquare$

The case $H = 1$ is the **universal cover**: a simply connected covering. It covers every path-connected cover (lifting criterion). Examples: $\mathbb{R} \to S^1$, $\mathbb{R}^2 \to T^2$, $S^n \to \mathbb{RP}^n$ ($n \ge 2$), and for the wedge of two circles an infinite tree in which each vertex has four edges.

### Deck transformations

A **deck transformation** of $p\colon \tilde X \to X$ is a homeomorphism $\tau\colon \tilde X \to \tilde X$ with $p\tau = p$. They form a group $G(\tilde X)$. If $\tilde X$ is connected and $\tau \ne \mathrm{id}$, then $\tau$ has no fixed point: $\tau$ and $\mathrm{id}$ are two lifts of $p$ through $p$, and they would agree everywhere if they agreed at one point (unique lifting). The cover is **normal** (or regular) if $G(\tilde X)$ acts transitively on each fiber.

**Theorem 2.** Let $X$ be path-connected and locally path-connected, $p\colon (\tilde X, \tilde x_0) \to (X, x_0)$ a path-connected covering, and $H = p_*\pi_1(\tilde X, \tilde x_0)$. Then (a) the cover is normal if and only if $H$ is normal in $\pi_1(X, x_0)$; (b) $G(\tilde X) \cong N(H)/H$, where $N(H)$ is the normalizer of $H$. In particular, for the universal cover $G(\tilde X) \cong \pi_1(X, x_0)$, and for a normal cover $G(\tilde X) \cong \pi_1(X, x_0)/H$.

The proof of (b) is the practice problem below: a deck transformation $\tau$ sends $\tilde x_0$ to some $\tilde x_1$; choose a path $\tilde\gamma$ from $\tilde x_0$ to $\tilde x_1$; then $\tau \mapsto H[p\tilde\gamma]$ is the isomorphism.

### Covers of a wedge of two circles

Let $X = S^1 \vee S^1$ with $\pi_1 = F_2 = F(a, b)$. A connected $n$-sheeted cover is a connected graph with $n$ vertices, $2n$ edges (each vertex has one outgoing and one incoming $a$-edge and the same for $b$), and its $\pi_1$ is free of rank $2n - n + 1 = n + 1$. It is given by a pair of permutations $(\sigma, \tau)$ of $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$ (lifting $a$ and $b$) generating a transitive subgroup of $S_n$. Isomorphism classes are such pairs up to simultaneous conjugation. Since the subgroup attached to sheet $0$ is the stabilizer, the number of index-$n$ subgroups is the number of transitive pairs divided by $(n-1)!$ (the choices of relabeling the other sheets).

```python
from itertools import permutations
from math import factorial


def transitive(perms, n):
    """True if the permutations of range(n) generate a transitive group."""
    seen, stack = {0}, [0]
    while stack:
        x = stack.pop()
        for perm in perms:
            if perm[x] not in seen:
                seen.add(perm[x])
                stack.append(perm[x])
    return len(seen) == n


def count_subgroups(n):
    """Number of subgroups of index n in the free group on two generators."""
    perms = list(permutations(range(n)))
    pairs = sum(transitive((s, t), n) for s in perms for t in perms)
    return pairs // factorial(n - 1)


print([count_subgroups(n) for n in range(1, 5)])
```

This prints `[1, 3, 13, 71]`. Up to isomorphism (conjugacy of subgroups) the counts are `1, 3, 7, 26` for $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$: for $n = 3$, four of the $13$ subgroups are normal (kernels of the $4$ surjections $F_2 \to \mathbb{Z}/3$ up to automorphism) and the other nine fall into three conjugacy classes of three, giving $4 + 3 = 7$. Computer enumeration counts examples, and the correspondence is the theorem.

@example
title: The two-sheeted covers of the figure eight
problem: List the connected $2$-sheeted covers of $S^1 \vee S^1$ up to isomorphism, give each one's fundamental group, and name the deck group.
step: A connected $2$-sheeted cover corresponds to an index-$2$ subgroup $H \le F(a, b)$. Such a subgroup is the kernel of a surjection $F_2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2$ (it has index $2$, hence is normal).
step: Surjections $F_2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2$ are given by the images of $a, b$, not both $0$: $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$, $(1, 1)$. So there are exactly $3$ subgroups, all normal, so $3$ isomorphism classes.
step: For $(1, 0)$ the permutation for $a$ swaps the two sheets and $b$ fixes both: a graph with two vertices joined by two $a$-edges, with a $b$-loop at each vertex. Similarly for $(0, 1)$ with the roles exchanged, and for $(1, 1)$ both $a$ and $b$ swap the sheets: two vertices joined by two $a$-edges and two $b$-edges.
step: Each graph has $2$ vertices and $4$ edges, so $\pi_1$ is free of rank $4 - 2 + 1 = 3$, the same as $H$ by the Schreier formula $n(r-1)+1$ with $n = 2$, $r = 2$. The deck group is $F_2/H \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, swapping the sheets.
answer: There are three covers, each with $\pi_1 \cong F_3$ and deck group $\mathbb{Z}/2$.

@example
title: The deck group of a nonnormal cover
problem: The connected $3$-sheeted cover of $S^1 \vee S^1$ with $a \mapsto (0\ 1)$ and $b \mapsto (1\ 2)$ (permutations of the sheets $\{0, 1, 2\}$). Find its deck group.
step: The deck group of a connected cover is the group of permutations of the sheets that commute with the permutations of $a$ and $b$ (they must carry each lifted edge to a lifted edge).
step: The monodromy group generated by $(0\ 1)$ and $(1\ 2)$ is all of $S_3$, which has trivial center.
step: A permutation that commutes with all of $S_3$ is in the center of $S_3$, which is trivial, so only the identity qualifies.
answer: The deck group is trivial, so the cover is not normal: $N(H) = H$ for the corresponding index-$3$ subgroup.

@mistakes
- Dropping a hypothesis of the classification theorem: locally path-connected and semilocally simply connected are both needed for existence; the Hawaiian earring has no simply connected cover.
- Confusing subgroups with conjugacy classes: covers up to isomorphism correspond to conjugacy classes, so a nonnormal subgroup of index $n$ with $m$ conjugates gives one cover, not $m$.
- Saying every cover has deck group $\pi_1(X)/H$: that holds only for normal $H$; in general it is $N(H)/H$ and can be trivial.
- Assuming a deck transformation can have a fixed point: a nonidentity deck transformation of a connected cover is fixed-point free.

```python
from itertools import permutations


def count_covers(n):
    """Isomorphism classes of connected n-sheeted covers of S^1 v S^1."""
    perms = list(permutations(range(n)))

    def transitive(s, t):
        seen, stack = {0}, [0]
        while stack:
            x = stack.pop()
            for p in (s, t):
                if p[x] not in seen:
                    seen.add(p[x])
                    stack.append(p[x])
        return len(seen) == n

    classes = set()
    for s in perms:
        for t in perms:
            if not transitive(s, t):
                continue
            best = None
            for g in perms:
                inv = [0] * n
                for i, x in enumerate(g):
                    inv[x] = i
                cs = tuple(g[s[inv[i]]] for i in range(n))
                ct = tuple(g[t[inv[i]]] for i in range(n))
                if best is None or (cs, ct) < best:
                    best = (cs, ct)
            classes.add(best)
    return len(classes)
```

prompt: Prove that the connected $n$-sheeted covers of the torus $T^2$, up to isomorphism, are in bijection with the subgroups of index $n$ of $\mathbb{Z}^2$, and that there are $\sum_{d \mid n} d$ of them. Compute the number for $n = 6$.

hint: Show that every subgroup of index $n$ has a unique basis $(a, b), (0, d)$ with $ad = n$ and $0 \le b < d$.
solution: The torus is a manifold, hence locally path-connected, and it is semilocally simply connected, being locally contractible. By Theorem 1, connected covers up to isomorphism correspond to conjugacy classes of subgroups of $\pi_1(T^2) = \mathbb{Z}^2$, and in an abelian group conjugacy classes are single subgroups. The number of sheets is the index (Lesson 5). Now let $L \le \mathbb{Z}^2$ have index $n$. The set $\{y : (0, y) \in L\}$ is a subgroup $d\mathbb{Z}$ with $d \ge 1$ (it is nonzero since $L$ has finite index), and the projection of $L$ to the first coordinate is $a\mathbb{Z}$ with $a \ge 1$. Choose $(a, b) \in L$ over $a$; changing the choice changes $b$ by a multiple of $d$, so $b$ is unique modulo $d$ and we take $0 \le b < d$. Then $(a, b), (0, d)$ generate $L$: for $(x, y) \in L$ we have $x = ka$, and $(x, y) - k(a, b) \in L$ lies on the second axis, so is a multiple of $(0, d)$. They are independent, so $[\mathbb{Z}^2 : L] = |\det| = ad$, and thus $ad = n$. Conversely every triple $(a, d, b)$ with $ad = n$, $a, d \ge 1$ and $0 \le b < d$ gives a subgroup of index $n$ with this invariant, and distinct triples give distinct subgroups by the uniqueness just proved. For fixed divisor $d$ of $n$ there are $d$ choices of $b$. The count is $\sum_{d \mid n} d$. For $n = 6$ the divisors are $1, 2, 3, 6$ with sum $12$. $\blacksquare$

prompt: Let $X$ be path-connected and locally path-connected, $p\colon (\tilde X, \tilde x_0) \to (X, x_0)$ a path-connected covering and $H = p_*\pi_1(\tilde X, \tilde x_0)$. Prove that the deck group $G(\tilde X)$ is isomorphic to $N(H)/H$.
hint: To $\tau \in G$ associate the class of the loop $p\tilde\gamma$, where $\tilde\gamma$ is a path from $\tilde x_0$ to $\tau(\tilde x_0)$.
hint: Surjectivity: use the lifting criterion to build $\tau$ from an element of $N(H)$, and check the inverse using uniqueness of lifts.
solution: Write $G = G(\tilde X)$. Recall the facts of Lesson 5: for $\tilde x$ in the fiber over $x_0$ and a path $\tilde\gamma$ from $\tilde x_0$ to $\tilde x$, with $\gamma = p\tilde\gamma$, we have $p_*\pi_1(\tilde X, \tilde x) = [\gamma]^{-1} H [\gamma]$; and a loop at $x_0$ lifts to a loop at $\tilde x_0$ exactly when its class is in $H$. The space $\tilde X$ is path-connected and locally path-connected. *Definition of $\theta$.* For $\tau \in G$ let $\tilde\gamma$ be a path from $\tilde x_0$ to $\tau(\tilde x_0)$ and $\gamma = p\tilde\gamma$, a loop at $x_0$. Since $p\tau = p$, $p_*\pi_1(\tilde X, \tau(\tilde x_0)) = p_*\tau_*\pi_1(\tilde X, \tilde x_0) = H$, so $[\gamma]^{-1}H[\gamma] = H$ and $[\gamma] \in N(H)$. Another choice $\tilde\gamma'$ satisfies $\tilde\gamma' \simeq_p \ell \cdot \tilde\gamma$ for a loop $\ell$ at $\tilde x_0$ (project to see $[\gamma'] = [p\ell][\gamma]$ with $[p\ell] \in H$), so the coset $H[\gamma]$ is well defined; set $\theta(\tau) = H[\gamma] \in N(H)/H$. *Homomorphism.* If $\tilde\gamma_1, \tilde\gamma_2$ are paths for $\tau_1, \tau_2$, then $\tilde\gamma_1 \cdot \tau_1\tilde\gamma_2$ goes from $\tilde x_0$ to $\tau_1\tau_2(\tilde x_0)$, and $p(\tilde\gamma_1 \cdot \tau_1\tilde\gamma_2) = \gamma_1 \cdot \gamma_2$, so $\theta(\tau_1\tau_2) = H[\gamma_1][\gamma_2] = \theta(\tau_1)\theta(\tau_2)$. *Injective.* If $\theta(\tau) = H$, then $[\gamma] \in H$, so $\gamma$ lifts to a loop at $\tilde x_0$; this lift is $\tilde\gamma$ by uniqueness, so $\tau(\tilde x_0) = \tilde x_0$. Then $\tau$ and $\mathrm{id}$ are lifts of $p$ agreeing at one point, so $\tau = \mathrm{id}$. *Surjective.* Let $[\gamma] \in N(H)$, lift $\gamma$ to $\tilde\gamma$ from $\tilde x_0$ ending at $\tilde x_1$. Then $p_*\pi_1(\tilde X, \tilde x_1) = [\gamma]^{-1}H[\gamma] = H = p_*\pi_1(\tilde X, \tilde x_0)$. By the lifting criterion there are lifts $\tau\colon (\tilde X, \tilde x_0) \to (\tilde X, \tilde x_1)$ and $\tau'\colon (\tilde X, \tilde x_1) \to (\tilde X, \tilde x_0)$ of $p$. Then $\tau'\tau$ and $\tau\tau'$ are lifts of $p$ fixing a point, hence identities by unique lifting, so $\tau \in G$ with $\theta(\tau) = H[\gamma]$. $\blacksquare$
