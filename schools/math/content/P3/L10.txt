@article
Van Kampen's theorem computes $\pi_1$ of a union from the pieces. The homology analogue is the Mayer–Vietoris sequence: it relates $H_*(A \cup B)$ to $H_*(A)$, $H_*(B)$ and $H_*(A \cap B)$. The price of working with abelian groups is that the answer is a long exact sequence, not a pushout, and the connecting homomorphism $\partial$ carries geometric information: it turns a cycle in the union into the cycle along which the two pieces are glued.

### The sequence

**Theorem 1 (Mayer–Vietoris).** Let $X$ be a space and $A, B \subseteq X$ subspaces whose interiors cover $X$. Then there is an exact sequence
$$\cdots \to H_n(A \cap B) \xrightarrow{\Phi} H_n(A) \oplus H_n(B) \xrightarrow{\Psi} H_n(X) \xrightarrow{\partial} H_{n-1}(A \cap B) \to \cdots \to H_0(X) \to 0,$$
with $\Phi(x) = (x, -x)$ and $\Psi(a, b) = a + b$, the maps induced by inclusions. If $A \cap B \ne \emptyset$, the same sequence holds with reduced homology.

*Proof.* Let $C_n(A + B) = C_n(A) + C_n(B) \subseteq C_n(X)$ be the chains that are sums of simplices each lying in $A$ or in $B$. Since $C_n(X)$ is free on singular simplices, the sequence $0 \to C_n(A \cap B) \xrightarrow{\ \Phi\ } C_n(A) \oplus C_n(B) \xrightarrow{\ \Psi\ } C_n(A + B) \to 0$ is exact: $\Phi$ is injective, $\Psi$ is onto by definition, and $\Psi(a, b) = 0$ iff $a = -b$ is a chain supported in $A \cap B$. These are chain maps, so Lemma 1 of Lesson 9 gives a long exact sequence in homology of the three complexes. The inclusion $C(A + B) \to C(X)$ induces an isomorphism on homology when the interiors of $A, B$ cover $X$: every chain is homologous to a sum of small simplices obtained by barycentric subdivision (stated without proof, the same subdivision argument as in the proof of excision). Replacing $H_n(C(A+B))$ by $H_n(X)$ gives the theorem. $\blacksquare$

The connecting map has a concrete description: if $z$ is a cycle in $X$, write $z = x + y$ with $x$ a chain in $A$ and $y$ a chain in $B$ (after subdivision). Then $\partial x = -\partial y$ is a chain in $A \cap B$, a cycle, and $\partial[z] = [\partial x]$.

**Euler characteristic.** If all groups are finitely generated, exactness gives an exact sequence of finite-dimensional vector spaces over $\mathbb{Q}$ with alternating sum of dimensions zero, so $\chi(A) + \chi(B) = \chi(A \cup B) + \chi(A \cap B)$ with $\chi = \sum (-1)^n b_n$ (practice problem).

### Spheres again

For $n \ge 1$ let $A = S^n \setminus \{S\}$ and $B = S^n \setminus \{N\}$, open and contractible, with $A \cap B \simeq S^{n-1}$. Reduced Mayer–Vietoris has $\tilde H_k(A) \oplus \tilde H_k(B) = 0$, so $\partial\colon \tilde H_k(S^n) \to \tilde H_{k-1}(S^{n-1})$ is an isomorphism for all $k$. This recovers $\tilde H_k(S^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ for $k = n$ and $0$ otherwise from $S^0$, without the pair $(D^n, S^{n-1})$.

### Closed surfaces

**Proposition 2.** Let $\Sigma$ be a closed surface with a cell structure having one vertex, edges $x_1, \dots, x_k$, and a single $2$-cell attached along a word whose exponent-sum vector is $w \in \mathbb{Z}^k$. Then $H_1(\Sigma) \cong \mathbb{Z}^k / \mathbb{Z}w$, $H_2(\Sigma) \cong \ker(\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}^k,\ 1 \mapsto w)$, and $H_n(\Sigma) = 0$ for $n \ge 3$.

*Proof.* Let $B$ be the open $2$-cell and $A = \Sigma \setminus \{\text{center of the cell}\}$. Both are open, $A$ deformation retracts onto the $1$-skeleton, a wedge of $k$ circles with $H_1(A) \cong \mathbb{Z}^k$ (Lesson 8), $H_n(A) = 0$ for $n \ge 2$, and $B$ is contractible. The intersection is a punctured disk $\simeq S^1$ whose generator maps to the attaching loop; by Hurewicz its class in $H_1(A) = \mathbb{Z}^k$ is the exponent-sum vector $w$. The reduced sequence is
$$0 \to H_2(\Sigma) \xrightarrow{\partial} H_1(A \cap B) \cong \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\Phi} H_1(A) \oplus H_1(B) \cong \mathbb{Z}^k \to H_1(\Sigma) \to \tilde H_0(A \cap B) = 0,$$
where $\Phi(1) = (w, 0)$ up to sign, and $\partial$ is injective because $H_2(A) \oplus H_2(B) = 0$. Exactness gives $H_2(\Sigma) \cong \ker\Phi$ and $H_1(\Sigma) \cong \operatorname{coker}\Phi$. For $n \ge 3$ all neighbors vanish. $\blacksquare$

Applications: for the orientable surface $\Sigma_g$ the word is a product of commutators, so $w = 0$: $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}^{2g}$ and $H_2 = \mathbb{Z}$. For the nonorientable surface $N_h$ the word is $a_1^2 \cdots a_h^2$, so $w = (2, \dots, 2)$ is injective: $H_2 = 0$ and $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}^h / \mathbb{Z}(2, \dots, 2) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{h-1} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2$. For $h = 1$ this is $H_*(\mathbb{RP}^2) = (\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2, 0)$.

**Hypotheses.** The interiors of $A$ and $B$ must cover $X$ for the proof: take open sets or thickenings of subcomplexes. The surface proposition applies the theorem to open sets. If $A \cap B$ is empty you may use only the unreduced sequence.

@example
title: The torus by Mayer–Vietoris
problem: Compute $H_*(T^2)$ using $A = T^2 \setminus \{\text{pt}\}$ and $B$ an open disk around the point.
step: $A$ deformation retracts onto $S^1 \vee S^1$ (Lesson 3), so $H_1(A) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ with basis $a, b$, and $H_n(A) = 0$ for $n \ge 2$. $B$ is contractible. $A \cap B$ is a punctured disk $\simeq S^1$ with $H_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
step: The generator of $H_1(A \cap B)$ is homotopic in $A$ to the loop $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$, whose class in $H_1(A) = \mathbb{Z}^2$ is $a + b - a - b = 0$. So $\Phi = 0$ on $H_1$.
step: The reduced sequence reads $0 \to H_2(T^2) \xrightarrow{\partial} \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{0} \mathbb{Z}^2 \to H_1(T^2) \to 0$. Exactness at $H_1(A \cap B)$ gives $\operatorname{im}\partial = \ker\Phi = \mathbb{Z}$, so $\partial$ is an isomorphism and $H_2(T^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
step: Exactness at $\mathbb{Z}^2$: $\Psi$ is injective (its kernel $\operatorname{im}\Phi = 0$) and onto (the next term is zero), so $H_1(T^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$. Also $H_0 = \mathbb{Z}$ and $H_n = 0$ for $n \ge 3$.
answer: $H_*(T^2) = (\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}^2, \mathbb{Z})$.

@example
title: The projective plane by Mayer–Vietoris
problem: Compute $H_*(\mathbb{RP}^2)$ from Proposition 2.
step: $\mathbb{RP}^2$ has one vertex, one edge $a$ and one $2$-cell attached along $a^2$, so $k = 1$ and $w = (2)$.
step: $\Phi\colon \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$ is multiplication by $2$: injective with cokernel $\mathbb{Z}/2$.
step: Hence $H_2 = \ker\Phi = 0$ and $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}/2$; $H_0 = \mathbb{Z}$.
answer: $H_*(\mathbb{RP}^2) = (\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/2, 0)$. The torsion explains why $b_1 = 0$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ but $H_1 \ne 0$.

@mistakes
- Forgetting the sign in $\Phi(x) = (x, -x)$: with the wrong sign the composite $\Psi\Phi$ is not zero and the sequence is not a complex.
- Choosing $A, B$ closed with interiors that do not cover $X$ (for instance two closed hemispheres of $S^2$): thicken them to open sets deformation retracting to the closed ones.
- Reading $\partial\colon H_n(X) \to H_{n-1}(A \cap B)$ as an inclusion-induced map: it is the connecting homomorphism and drops the degree by one.
- Computing $H_1(X)$ as $H_1(A) \oplus H_1(B)$ without taking the cokernel of $\Phi$ and the kernel of $\tilde H_0(A \cap B) \to \tilde H_0(A) \oplus \tilde H_0(B)$.

@practice code
prompt: Complexes are given by lists of facets (tuples of integer vertices). The faces of a complex are all nonempty subsets of its facets. Write `mv_euler(facets_a, facets_b)` that returns $\chi(A) + \chi(B) - \chi(A \cap B)$, where $\chi = \sum_n (-1)^n f_n$ counts faces by dimension $n$ (one less than the number of vertices) and $A \cap B$ is the complex of faces that are faces of both $A$ and $B$ (not the intersection of the facet lists). model:
```python
from itertools import combinations


def mv_euler(facets_a, facets_b):
    """chi(A) + chi(B) - chi(A and B), the latter built from common faces."""

    def faces(facets):
        out = set()
        for f in facets:
            f = tuple(sorted(f))
            for k in range(1, len(f) + 1):
                out.update(combinations(f, k))
        return out

    def chi(face_set):
        return sum((-1) ** (len(s) - 1) for s in face_set)

    fa, fb = faces(facets_a), faces(facets_b)
    return chi(fa) + chi(fb) - chi(fa & fb)
```
hint: Build the full set of faces of each complex first, then intersect the two sets of faces.
hint: Each face contributes $(-1)^{\dim}$, where $\dim$ is the number of vertices minus one.
solution: The intersection of two simplicial complexes consists of the simplices that belong to both, which is the intersection of the face sets; comparing facet lists would miss shared edges and vertices, since the shared simplex is a face of facets that differ. Two triangles sharing an edge have $\chi = 1 + 1 - 1 = 1$, equal to the Euler characteristic of their union (a square), and the cycle of length six built from two paths has $\chi = 1 + 1 - 2 = 0$ as a circle should.

@practice self
prompt: Compute the integral homology of the Klein bottle $K$ by the Mayer–Vietoris sequence, using the cell structure with one vertex, edges $a, b$, and one $2$-cell attached along $abab^{-1}$.
hint: Take $A = K \setminus \{\text{center of the cell}\}$, a wedge of two circles up to homotopy, and $B$ the open cell.
hint: The image of the generator of $H_1(A \cap B)$ in $H_1(A) = \mathbb{Z}^2$ is the exponent-sum vector of the attaching word.
solution: Let $A = K \setminus \{\text{center}\}$ and $B$ the open $2$-cell. Both are open and $A \cup B = K$. $A$ deformation retracts onto the $1$-skeleton $S^1 \vee S^1$ (radial deformation of the punctured cell), so $H_1(A) = \mathbb{Z}^2$ with basis $a, b$ and $H_n(A) = 0$ for $n \ge 2$; $B$ is contractible; $A \cap B \simeq S^1$. The generator of $H_1(A \cap B)$ is homotopic in $A$ to the attaching loop $abab^{-1}$, whose class in $H_1(A)$ is $a + b + a - b = 2a$, that is $(2, 0)$. The reduced Mayer–Vietoris sequence is $0 \to H_2(K) \xrightarrow{\partial} \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\Phi} \mathbb{Z}^2 \to H_1(K) \to 0$, with $\Phi(1) = (2, 0)$ (up to sign; the second component from $H_1(B) = 0$). The map $\partial$ is injective as $H_2(A) \oplus H_2(B) = 0$, and $\Phi$ is injective, so $\operatorname{im}\partial = \ker\Phi = 0$ and $H_2(K) = 0$. Then $H_1(K) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2 / \mathbb{Z}(2, 0) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}$. Finally $H_0 = \mathbb{Z}$ and $H_n(K) = 0$ for $n \ge 3$. So $H_*(K) = (\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2, 0)$. $\blacksquare$

@practice self
prompt: Let $X = A \cup B$ with the interiors of $A$ and $B$ covering $X$, and suppose all the homology groups of $A$, $B$, $A \cap B$ are finitely generated and vanish in high degrees. Prove that $\chi(X) = \chi(A) + \chi(B) - \chi(A \cap B)$, where $\chi(Y) = \sum_n (-1)^n \operatorname{rank} H_n(Y)$.
hint: Tensor the Mayer–Vietoris sequence with $\mathbb{Q}$ to get an exact sequence of finite-dimensional vector spaces.
hint: In a bounded exact sequence of finite-dimensional vector spaces the alternating sum of the dimensions is zero (rank-nullity at each map).
solution: Tensoring with $\mathbb{Q}$ preserves exactness, so Mayer–Vietoris gives an exact sequence of finite-dimensional rational vector spaces, which is bounded because the groups vanish in high degrees and the sequence ends with $H_0(X) \to 0$. Number its terms from the right, putting $H_n(X)_\mathbb{Q}$ in position $3n$, $(H_n(A) \oplus H_n(B))_\mathbb{Q}$ in position $3n + 1$ and $H_n(A \cap B)_\mathbb{Q}$ in position $3n + 2$; the connecting map goes from position $3n$ to position $3n - 1$, which is $H_{n-1}(A \cap B)_\mathbb{Q}$. In a bounded exact sequence of finite-dimensional vector spaces with maps $f_i\colon W_i \to W_{i-1}$, rank-nullity gives $\dim W_i = \dim\operatorname{im} f_{i+1} + \dim\operatorname{im} f_i$, because $\ker f_i = \operatorname{im} f_{i+1}$, so $\sum_i (-1)^i \dim W_i$ telescopes to $0$. Here the dimensions are $b_n(X)$, $b_n(A) + b_n(B)$ and $b_n(A \cap B)$ and the signs are $(-1)^{3n} = (-1)^n$, $(-1)^{3n+1} = -(-1)^n$ and $(-1)^{3n+2} = (-1)^n$. So $0 = \sum_n (-1)^n \big[b_n(X) - b_n(A) - b_n(B) + b_n(A \cap B)\big] = \chi(X) - \chi(A) - \chi(B) + \chi(A \cap B)$. $\blacksquare$
