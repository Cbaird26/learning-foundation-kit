@article
Homotopy invariance computes the homology of contractible spaces, which is not very interesting. To compute anything else you need a way to relate the homology of a space to the homology of its pieces. That tool is the long exact sequence, and its engine is the excision theorem. This lesson uses both to compute the homology of every sphere.

### Exact sequences

A sequence of homomorphisms $\cdots \to A_{n+1} \xrightarrow{\alpha_{n+1}} A_n \xrightarrow{\alpha_n} A_{n-1} \to \cdots$ is **exact** if $\operatorname{im}\alpha_{n+1} = \ker\alpha_n$ at every spot. A **short exact sequence** $0 \to A \xrightarrow{i} B \xrightarrow{j} C \to 0$ means $i$ is injective, $j$ is surjective and $\operatorname{im} i = \ker j$, so $C \cong B/A$. Not every such sequence splits: $0 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/4 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$ has $B = \mathbb{Z}/4 \not\cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2$.

**Five lemma** (stated here without proof; it is a diagram chase). In a commutative diagram with exact rows $A \to B \to C \to D \to E$ and $A' \to B' \to C' \to D' \to E'$ and vertical maps $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta, \varepsilon$, if $\alpha, \beta, \delta, \varepsilon$ are isomorphisms then so is $\gamma$.

**Lemma 1.** Let $0 \to A_\bullet \xrightarrow{i} B_\bullet \xrightarrow{j} C_\bullet \to 0$ be a short exact sequence of chain complexes (chain maps $i, j$ that are exact in each degree). Then there are homomorphisms $\delta\colon H_n(C) \to H_{n-1}(A)$ so that
$$\cdots \to H_n(A) \xrightarrow{i_*} H_n(B) \xrightarrow{j_*} H_n(C) \xrightarrow{\delta} H_{n-1}(A) \xrightarrow{i_*} H_{n-1}(B) \to \cdots$$
is exact.

*Proof.* Definition of $\delta$: given a cycle $c \in C_n$, choose $b \in B_n$ with $j(b) = c$. Then $j(\partial b) = \partial c = 0$, so $\partial b = i(a)$ for a unique $a \in A_{n-1}$, and $i(\partial a) = \partial\partial b = 0$ so $\partial a = 0$ by injectivity. Set $\delta[c] = [a]$. This is independent of the choices: another lift is $b + i(a')$, which changes $a$ by $\partial a'$; and replacing $c$ by $c + \partial c'$ with $c' = j(b')$ allows the lift $b + \partial b' + i(a')$, whose boundary is $\partial b + i(\partial a')$, so $a$ changes by the boundary $\partial a'$. Exactness at $H_n(C)$: if $\delta[c] = 0$ then $a = \partial a''$ and $b - i(a'')$ is a cycle with $j(b - i(a'')) = c$, so $[c] = j_*[b - i a'']$; conversely $\delta j_* = 0$ because a cycle $c = j(b)$ with $b$ a cycle has $\partial b = 0$. Exactness at $H_n(B)$ and at $H_{n-1}(A)$ is the same kind of chase: if $j_*[b] = 0$ then $j(b) = \partial c'$, lift $c' = j(b')$, and $b - \partial b' = i(a)$ with $a$ a cycle, so $[b] = i_*[a]$; and $j_*i_* = 0$ since $ji = 0$. At $H_{n-1}(A)$: $i_*\delta[c] = [\partial b] = 0$, and conversely if $i_*[a] = 0$ with $a$ a cycle then $i(a) = \partial b$ for some $b$, so $j(b)$ is a cycle ($\partial j(b) = j(i(a)) = 0$) and $\delta[j(b)] = [a]$. $\blacksquare$

### Relative homology and the long exact sequence of a pair

For a subspace $A \subseteq X$ put $C_n(X, A) = C_n(X)/C_n(A)$ and let $H_n(X, A)$ be its homology. A **relative cycle** is a chain of $X$ whose boundary lies in $A$. The sequence $0 \to C(A) \to C(X) \to C(X, A) \to 0$ is short exact, so Lemma 1 gives:

**Theorem 2 (long exact sequence of a pair).** For $A \subseteq X$,
$$\cdots \to H_n(A) \to H_n(X) \to H_n(X, A) \xrightarrow{\partial} H_{n-1}(A) \to \cdots \to H_0(X, A) \to 0$$
is exact. If $A \ne \emptyset$ the same holds with reduced homology $\tilde H$ throughout, ending in $\tilde H_0(X, A) = H_0(X, A)$.

### Excision and good pairs

**Theorem 3 (excision).** Let $Z \subseteq A \subseteq X$ with the closure of $Z$ contained in the interior of $A$. Then the inclusion $(X \setminus Z, A \setminus Z) \to (X, A)$ induces isomorphisms $H_n(X \setminus Z, A \setminus Z) \to H_n(X, A)$ for all $n$. (Stated without proof. The proof subdivides simplices barycentrically until each lies in $A$ or in $X \setminus Z$.)

**The hypothesis is needed.** Take $X = [0, 1]$, $A = \{0, 1\}$ and $Z = \{0\}$. Then $\bar Z = \{0\}$ is not in the interior of $A$, which is empty. The long exact sequence gives $H_1(X, A) \cong \tilde H_0(A) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, while $(X \setminus Z, A \setminus Z) = ((0, 1], \{1\})$ has $H_n = 0$ for all $n$. So the excision isomorphism fails.

A pair $(X, A)$ is **good** if $A$ is nonempty and closed and is a deformation retract of some neighborhood in $X$. Excision applied to a thickening of $A$ gives, for good pairs, an isomorphism $H_n(X, A) \cong \tilde H_n(X/A)$ for all $n$ (stated without proof; see the subsection on exact sequences and excision in the reading). For example, $(D^n, S^{n-1})$ is good, and $D^n / S^{n-1} \cong S^n$.

### Homology of spheres

**Theorem 4.** For $n \ge 0$, $\tilde H_k(S^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ if $k = n$ and $0$ otherwise.

*Proof.* For $n = 0$, $S^0$ is two points, so $H_0 = \mathbb{Z}^2$, $\tilde H_0 = \mathbb{Z}$ and $H_k = 0$ for $k > 0$. Let $n \ge 1$ and assume the result for $S^{n-1}$. The disk $D^n$ is contractible, so $\tilde H_k(D^n) = 0$ for all $k$, and the reduced long exact sequence of $(D^n, S^{n-1})$ gives isomorphisms $H_k(D^n, S^{n-1}) \cong \tilde H_{k-1}(S^{n-1})$ for all $k$. Because $(D^n, S^{n-1})$ is a good pair with quotient $S^n$, also $H_k(D^n, S^{n-1}) \cong \tilde H_k(S^n)$. Thus $\tilde H_k(S^n) \cong \tilde H_{k-1}(S^{n-1})$, which is $\mathbb{Z}$ exactly when $k - 1 = n - 1$. $\blacksquare$

**Local homology** is a useful corollary. For $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $H_k(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{x\}) \cong \tilde H_{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{x\}) \cong \tilde H_{k-1}(S^{n-1})$, since $\mathbb{R}^n$ is contractible and the punctured space deformation retracts onto a sphere. This is nonzero only for $k = n$, and the dimension $n$ is therefore a topological invariant of $\mathbb{R}^n$ (practice problem).

@example
title: Relative homology of a circle in a disk
problem: Compute $H_k(D^2, S^1)$ for all $k$ from the long exact sequence, and compare with $\tilde H_k(S^2)$.
step: The reduced long exact sequence of the pair is $\tilde H_k(S^1) \to \tilde H_k(D^2) \to H_k(D^2, S^1) \to \tilde H_{k-1}(S^1) \to \tilde H_{k-1}(D^2)$.
step: $D^2$ is contractible, so $\tilde H_k(D^2) = 0$ for every $k$, and the sequence collapses to $H_k(D^2, S^1) \cong \tilde H_{k-1}(S^1)$.
step: By Theorem 4 with $n = 1$, $\tilde H_{k-1}(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}$ for $k = 2$ and $0$ otherwise. So $H_2(D^2, S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and the other relative groups vanish.
step: The pair is good and $D^2/S^1 \cong S^2$, so these agree with $\tilde H_k(S^2)$: $\mathbb{Z}$ in degree $2$ and zero otherwise.
answer: $H_2(D^2, S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $H_k(D^2, S^1) = 0$ for $k \ne 2$.

@example
title: An extension problem
problem: Find all abelian groups $B$ (up to isomorphism) with a short exact sequence $0 \to \mathbb{Z}/3 \to B \to \mathbb{Z}/3 \to 0$.
step: $|B| = 9$, so $B$ is $\mathbb{Z}/9$ or $\mathbb{Z}/3 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3$ (the abelian groups of order $9$).
step: For $\mathbb{Z}/3 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3$: include the first factor and project to the second; this is exact.
step: For $\mathbb{Z}/9$: take $i(1) = 3$ and $j$ reduction mod $3$. Then $\operatorname{im} i = \{0, 3, 6\} = \ker j$, and $j$ is onto, so it is exact.
answer: Both $\mathbb{Z}/9$ and $\mathbb{Z}/3 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3$ occur, so $B$ is not determined by $A$ and $C$.

@mistakes
- Applying excision with $\bar Z \subseteq A$ only: the closure of $Z$ must lie in the interior of $A$, as the pair $([0,1], \{0,1\})$ shows.
- Writing $H_n(X, A) \cong H_n(X/A)$ without the tilde, or for a pair that is not good: use $\tilde H_n(X/A)$, and check that $A$ is closed and a deformation retract of a neighborhood.
- Assuming exactness gives a direct sum: an extension $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ need not split, so $B$ is not always $A \oplus C$.
- Using the reduced long exact sequence when $A$ is empty: reduced groups require $A \ne \emptyset$ so that the augmentation is defined on both spaces.

@practice self
prompt: Prove that if $\mathbb{R}^m$ and $\mathbb{R}^n$ are homeomorphic with $m, n \ge 1$, then $m = n$. Use the computation of $\tilde H_*(S^k)$ and the long exact sequence of a pair.
hint: A homeomorphism $h\colon \mathbb{R}^m \to \mathbb{R}^n$ gives a homeomorphism of pairs $(\mathbb{R}^m, \mathbb{R}^m \setminus \{0\}) \to (\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{h(0)\})$.
hint: Compute $H_k(\mathbb{R}^m, \mathbb{R}^m \setminus \{0\})$ from the long exact sequence of the pair, using contractibility of $\mathbb{R}^m$ and a deformation retraction of the punctured space onto a sphere.
solution: Let $h\colon \mathbb{R}^m \to \mathbb{R}^n$ be a homeomorphism and $y = h(0)$. Then $h$ restricts to a homeomorphism of pairs $(\mathbb{R}^m, \mathbb{R}^m \setminus \{0\}) \to (\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{y\})$, so $h_*$ is an isomorphism on all relative homology groups. For any $N \ge 1$ and $x \in \mathbb{R}^N$, the reduced long exact sequence of $(\mathbb{R}^N, \mathbb{R}^N \setminus \{x\})$ has $\tilde H_k(\mathbb{R}^N) = 0$ (contractible), so $H_k(\mathbb{R}^N, \mathbb{R}^N \setminus \{x\}) \cong \tilde H_{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^N \setminus \{x\})$. The punctured space deformation retracts onto the sphere $S^{N-1}$ around $x$, so this equals $\tilde H_{k-1}(S^{N-1})$, which is $\mathbb{Z}$ if $k = N$ and $0$ otherwise (Theorem 4). Therefore $H_k(\mathbb{R}^m, \mathbb{R}^m \setminus \{0\})$ is nonzero only for $k = m$ and $H_k(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{y\})$ only for $k = n$. Since the two are isomorphic in each degree, $m = n$. $\blacksquare$

@practice self
prompt: Let $X$ and $Y$ be path-connected spaces with points $x_0 \in X$, $y_0 \in Y$ such that $\{x_0\}$ and $\{y_0\}$ are deformation retracts of open neighborhoods in $X$ and $Y$ respectively. Prove that $\tilde H_n(X \vee Y) \cong \tilde H_n(X) \oplus \tilde H_n(Y)$ for all $n$, where $X \vee Y = (X \sqcup Y)/(x_0 \sim y_0)$.
hint: Consider the pair $(X \vee Y, X)$ and identify its quotient.
hint: The map collapsing $Y$ to the base point is a retraction of $X \vee Y$ onto $X$; use it to split the long exact sequence.
solution: Regard $X$ as a closed subspace of $X \vee Y$. The pair $(X \vee Y, X)$ is good: $X$ is closed and nonempty, and it is a deformation retract of the neighborhood $X \cup U$ where $U \subseteq Y$ is an open neighborhood of $y_0$ deformation retracting to $y_0$ (use the given deformation on $U$ and the identity on $X$; this is continuous because the deformation fixes $y_0$ throughout). The quotient $(X \vee Y)/X \cong Y$. Hence $H_n(X \vee Y, X) \cong \tilde H_n(Y)$. The reduced long exact sequence of the pair is $\cdots \to \tilde H_n(X) \xrightarrow{i_*} \tilde H_n(X \vee Y) \to \tilde H_n(Y) \xrightarrow{\partial} \tilde H_{n-1}(X) \to \cdots$. The map $r\colon X \vee Y \to X$ that is the identity on $X$ and sends $Y$ to $x_0$ is a retraction (continuous by the quotient topology), so $r_* i_* = \mathrm{id}$ and each $i_*$ is injective. Hence every connecting map $\partial\colon \tilde H_n(Y) \to \tilde H_{n-1}(X)$ is zero: by exactness its image is the kernel of the injective map $i_*$ on $\tilde H_{n-1}(X)$. We get short exact sequences $0 \to \tilde H_n(X) \to \tilde H_n(X \vee Y) \to \tilde H_n(Y) \to 0$ split by $r_*$, so $\tilde H_n(X \vee Y) \cong \tilde H_n(X) \oplus \tilde H_n(Y)$. $\blacksquare$
