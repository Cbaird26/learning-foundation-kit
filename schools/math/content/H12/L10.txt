@article
Recent history is harder to write than older history. Events are still unfolding, sources are incomplete, and people disagree about their meaning. Good historians describe what happened, identify who held which views and avoid treating a current opinion as settled fact.

### Politics and the economy in the 1980s and 1990s

The 1980 election began an era in which many voters and leaders favored lower taxes, less regulation and a stronger military; supporters said this encouraged growth, while critics argued it increased deficits and inequality. The Cold War ended between 1989 and 1991. The Immigration Reform and Control Act (1986) offered legal status to some long-term residents while penalizing the hiring of unauthorized workers. The Americans with Disabilities Act (1990) prohibited discrimination against people with disabilities. In the Gulf War (1991), a coalition led by the United States removed Iraqi forces from Kuwait.

The 1990s saw the spread of the internet and personal computers and a long economic expansion. The **North American Free Trade Agreement** (1994) reduced trade barriers among the United States, Canada and Mexico. Supporters said it expanded trade and lowered prices; critics said it cost some manufacturing jobs. Welfare law was reformed in 1996. The House impeached a president in 1998, and the Senate voted not to remove him in 1999. Political polarization became a major subject of study.

### After September 11, 2001

On September 11, 2001, terrorists hijacked four airplanes and attacked New York and Washington, killing nearly 3,000 people. The United States invaded Afghanistan to remove the Taliban government that sheltered the attackers. Congress created the Department of Homeland Security (2002) and passed the USA PATRIOT Act (2001), which expanded surveillance powers. Supporters said these laws were needed for security; civil liberties advocates said they threatened privacy. In 2003 the United States and allies invaded Iraq; the war's justification and results remain debated. U.S. forces left Afghanistan in 2021, 20 years after the first invasion.

### The financial crisis and the economy

A collapse in housing prices and risky financial practices led to the 2008 financial crisis and the deepest recession since the Depression. The government rescued banks and automakers through programs such as the Troubled Asset Relief Program; critics argued it rewarded risky behavior, while supporters said it prevented a worse collapse. The Dodd-Frank Act (2010) increased financial regulation. The Affordable Care Act (2010) expanded health insurance coverage through subsidies and Medicaid expansion and was the subject of lasting political and legal debate, including a Supreme Court decision in 2012 upholding its central requirement as a tax.

### Society and technology

The population has become more diverse; the Latino population has grown quickly. Smartphones and social media changed communication, news and campaigns. Disagreements about campaign finance, voting rules, immigration, policing, the economy and the role of the courts have been prominent. In 2020 the COVID-19 pandemic caused millions of deaths worldwide, major disruptions in education and work, and debates over public health measures and the role of government.

### Reading about recent events

When you read about recent events, ask: Who is the author? What evidence is cited? Are different perspectives presented? Is a claim a fact (which can be checked), an interpretation (reasoned judgment) or a value (a belief about what ought to be)? These habits prepare you for the document-based project in week 13.

@example
title: Separating fact, interpretation and value
problem: Classify each statement: (a) "NAFTA took effect in 1994." (b) "NAFTA caused most manufacturing job losses." (c) "Free trade is good for the country."
step: Statement (a) is a fact that can be checked in treaty and legal records.
step: Statement (b) is an interpretation or causal claim. It can be tested with evidence, but economists weigh trade, technology and other causes differently, so it needs careful support.
step: Statement (c) is a value judgment because "good" depends on whose interests and which goals one considers.
step: A fair account presents the facts, shows evidence for competing interpretations, and identifies values openly.

@mistakes
- Treating one's own political view as a historical fact.
- Assuming that events that are close in time are clearly understood; evidence and perspective change over time.
- Describing a party, group or region as if all its members think alike.
