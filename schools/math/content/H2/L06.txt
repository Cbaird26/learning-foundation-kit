@article
In a **right triangle** the side opposite the right angle is the **hypotenuse**, the longest side; the other two are the **legs**. The **Pythagorean theorem** says that with legs $a$, $b$ and hypotenuse $c$,
$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2.$$
Its converse is also true: if three side lengths satisfy $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, the triangle has a right angle opposite $c$. For example $5, 12, 13$ works since $25 + 144 = 169$.

### Two special triangles

- **45-45-90:** an isosceles right triangle. If each leg is $s$, the hypotenuse is $s\sqrt{2}$. Picture a square of side $s$ cut along a diagonal.
- **30-60-90:** half of an equilateral triangle. Cut an equilateral triangle of side $2s$ in half along its altitude: the short leg (opposite $30^\circ$) is $s$, the hypotenuse is $2s$, and the long leg (opposite $60^\circ$) is $s\sqrt{3}$.

### Trigonometric ratios

Because all right triangles with the same acute angle $\theta$ are similar (AA), the ratios of their sides depend only on $\theta$. Label the sides **relative to $\theta$**: the **opposite** side is across from $\theta$, the **adjacent** side touches $\theta$ (and is not the hypotenuse). Then

$$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}},\quad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}},\quad \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}.$$

The memory aid is **SOH-CAH-TOA**. To find a side, choose the ratio that contains the one side you know and the one you want, write the equation, and solve. To find an **angle**, use the inverse function: if $\tan\theta = 0.75$, then $\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.75) \approx 36.9^\circ$. Set your calculator to **degrees**.

A useful fact: for the two acute angles of a right triangle, $\sin\theta = \cos(90^\circ - \theta)$.

### Angles of elevation

The **angle of elevation** is the angle above the horizontal when you look up at something; the **angle of depression** is the angle below the horizontal when you look down. Picture yourself standing on level ground a distance $d$ from the base of a tall pole. The horizontal line from your eye, the pole and your line of sight form a right triangle, with the angle at your eye equal to the angle of elevation. The pole's height above your eye level is the **opposite** side and $d$ is the **adjacent** side, so

$$\text{height above eye level} = d \tan\theta,$$

and you add your eye height to get the full height. This lets you find the height of a building, tree or cliff without climbing it.

@example
title: Height of a tree
problem: You stand $20$ m from the base of a tree on level ground. The angle of elevation to the top of the tree is $28^\circ$, and your eyes are $1.6$ m above the ground. How tall is the tree, to the nearest tenth of a meter?
step: Sketch a right triangle at eye level: the adjacent side is $20$ m, and the opposite side is the height of the tree above your eyes.
step: Opposite and adjacent suggest tangent: $\tan 28^\circ = \dfrac{h}{20}$, so $h = 20 \tan 28^\circ$.
step: With the calculator in degrees, $\tan 28^\circ \approx 0.5317$, so $h \approx 10.63$ m.
step: Add your eye height: $10.63 + 1.6 \approx 12.2$ m. The tree is about $12.2$ m tall.

@mistakes
- Using the Pythagorean theorem with the hypotenuse as one of the legs, or adding squares for a non-right triangle.
- Labeling opposite and adjacent relative to the wrong angle.
- Working with the calculator in radians mode when the angle is in degrees.
- Forgetting to add the observer's eye height to the height found from the angle of elevation.

@quiz num
prompt: A $6$ m ladder leans against a vertical wall and makes an angle of $70^\circ$ with the level ground. How high up the wall does it reach, in meters, rounded to the nearest hundredth?
answer: 5.64
check: 6 * math.sin(math.radians(70))
tol: 0.01
hint: The ladder is the hypotenuse and the height is opposite the $70^\circ$ angle.
solution: $\sin 70^\circ = h/6$, so $h = 6 \sin 70^\circ \approx 6(0.9397) \approx 5.64$ m.
