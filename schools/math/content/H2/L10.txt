@article
A **solid** is a three-dimensional figure. **Volume** measures the space inside, in cubic units. **Surface area** is the total area of all its faces, in square units.

### Prisms and cylinders

A **prism** has two parallel, congruent bases joined by flat faces; a **cylinder** has two circular bases. For both, the volume is the area of the base times the height, where the height is the perpendicular distance between the bases:

$$V = B\,h.$$

For a rectangular box $V = \ell w h$, and for a cylinder $V = \pi r^2 h$. Picture stacking identical layers: each layer has area $B$, and there are $h$ units of layers.

### Pyramids and cones

A **pyramid** or **cone** that has the same base and height as a prism or cylinder has exactly one-third of its volume:

$$V = \tfrac{1}{3}\,B\,h.$$

For a cone, $V = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$. The **slant height** $\ell$ of a cone satisfies $\ell^2 = r^2 + h^2$ (a right triangle with legs $r$ and $h$), and its lateral surface area is $\pi r \ell$.

### Spheres

$$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3,\qquad \text{surface area} = 4\pi r^2.$$

### Surface area

Imagine unfolding a solid into a **net**, a flat pattern of its faces. A rectangular box with dimensions $\ell, w, h$ has surface area $2(\ell w + \ell h + wh)$. A cylinder's net is two circles and one rectangle whose width equals the circumference $2\pi r$, so

$$SA_{\text{cylinder}} = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h.$$

### Scaling

If every length of a solid is multiplied by $k$, surface area is multiplied by $k^2$ and volume by $k^3$. Doubling all dimensions of a box makes its volume eight times as large.

### Units and capacity

Check units: $1\ \text{m}^3 = 1000\ \text{L}$, and $1\ \text{L} = 1000\ \text{cm}^3$. Convert every dimension to one unit before multiplying.

### Putting it together

Real design problems combine both topics: area tells you how much flooring or paint you need, and volume tells you how much a container holds. Break each problem into simple figures, state which formula you use and why, and keep the units visible in every line.

@example
title: Surface area of a cylinder
problem: A closed cylindrical can has radius $2$ cm and height $5$ cm. Find its total surface area in terms of $\pi$.
step: A closed cylinder has two circular ends and a curved side.
step: Two ends: $2 \times \pi r^2 = 2 \times \pi (2)^2 = 8\pi$.
step: Curved side (a rectangle of width $2\pi r$ and height $h$): $2\pi r h = 2\pi (2)(5) = 20\pi$.
step: Total: $8\pi + 20\pi = 28\pi\ \text{cm}^2$.

@mistakes
- Forgetting the factor $\tfrac{1}{3}$ for pyramids and cones.
- Using the slant height instead of the perpendicular height in a volume formula.
- Mixing units (centimeters with meters), or giving volume in square units.
- Multiplying volume by $k^2$ instead of $k^3$ when a solid is scaled.

@quiz num
prompt: A pyramid has a square base of side $6$ and a perpendicular height of $7$. Find its volume.
answer: 84
check: 6 * 6 * 7 / 3
hint: The base area is $6^2$, and a pyramid is one-third of the matching prism.
solution: $V = \tfrac{1}{3}(36)(7) = 84$.
