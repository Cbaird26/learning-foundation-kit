@article
The coordinate plane lets you describe geometric figures with numbers. A point is an ordered pair $(x, y)$: $x$ is the horizontal position and $y$ the vertical position, measured from the origin $(0, 0)$.

### Distance

Take two points $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$. Draw a right triangle whose hypotenuse is $AB$, with a horizontal leg of length $|x_2 - x_1|$ and a vertical leg of length $|y_2 - y_1|$. The Pythagorean theorem gives the **distance formula**:

$$AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}.$$

### Midpoint

The midpoint is the point halfway between: average the coordinates.

$$M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\ \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right).$$

To find the point that divides $\overline{AB}$ in the ratio $m : n$ (measured from $A$), move the fraction $\dfrac{m}{m+n}$ of the way:

$$\left(x_1 + \tfrac{m}{m+n}(x_2 - x_1),\ y_1 + \tfrac{m}{m+n}(y_2 - y_1)\right).$$

### Slope

The **slope** of the line through $A$ and $B$ is "rise over run":

$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}.$$

A horizontal line has slope $0$, and a vertical line has undefined slope. Two non-vertical lines are:

- **parallel** if and only if their slopes are equal,
- **perpendicular** if and only if the product of their slopes is $-1$ (the slopes are **negative reciprocals**, such as $\tfrac{3}{4}$ and $-\tfrac{4}{3}$). A horizontal line is perpendicular to a vertical line.

### Equation of a circle

A circle with center $(h, k)$ and radius $r$ is the set of points at distance $r$ from the center. By the distance formula,

$$(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2.$$

For example $(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 49$ is a circle of radius $7$ centered at $(3, -2)$. Notice the sign flip: $y + 2$ means $k = -2$.

### Classifying figures

To classify a figure from its vertices, compute **slopes** (to test for parallel and perpendicular sides) and **distances** (to test for equal sides). Then use the definitions: a triangle with two equal sides is isosceles; one with a pair of perpendicular sides is a right triangle; a quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel is a parallelogram; and so on. Always give the numerical evidence for each property that you claim.

### Why coordinates help

Coordinate proofs turn geometric claims into algebra. For example, to show that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, you can compute the midpoints of both diagonals and see that they coincide.

@example
title: Classifying a triangle
problem: Classify the triangle with vertices $A(1, 1)$, $B(5, 3)$ and $C(0, 3)$ by its angles, using slopes, and confirm with distances.
step: Slope of $AB$: $\dfrac{3 - 1}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Slope of $AC$: $\dfrac{3 - 1}{0 - 1} = -2$.
step: The product of the slopes is $\tfrac{1}{2} \times (-2) = -1$, so $AB \perp AC$ and the angle at $A$ is a right angle.
step: Distances: $AB = \sqrt{4^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{20}$, $AC = \sqrt{1^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{5}$, $BC = \sqrt{5^2 + 0^2} = 5$.
step: Check: $AB^2 + AC^2 = 20 + 5 = 25 = BC^2$, consistent with a right angle at $A$. The triangle is a right triangle, and it is not isosceles because the three sides are different.

@mistakes
- Subtracting the coordinates in different orders in the numerator and denominator of the slope formula.
- Forgetting the square root in the distance formula, or squaring a negative difference incorrectly.
- Using the reciprocal without the sign change (or the opposite without the reciprocal) for perpendicular slopes.
- Reading the center of a circle with the wrong signs from $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$, and giving $r^2$ as the radius.

@practice tuple
prompt: Find the midpoint of the segment with endpoints $(-2, 5)$ and $(8, 1)$. Enter it as an ordered pair.
answer: (3, 3)
check: ((-2 + 8) / 2, (5 + 1) / 2)
hint: Average the $x$-coordinates and average the $y$-coordinates.
solution: $\left(\dfrac{-2 + 8}{2}, \dfrac{5 + 1}{2}\right) = (3, 3)$.

@practice num
prompt: Find the slope of the line through $(2, -1)$ and $(6, 7)$.
answer: 2
check: (7 - (-1)) / (6 - 2)
hint: Slope is the change in $y$ divided by the change in $x$.
solution: $\dfrac{7 - (-1)}{6 - 2} = \dfrac{8}{4} = 2$.

@practice num
prompt: A line has slope $\dfrac{3}{4}$. Find the slope of any line perpendicular to it. Give an exact fraction.
answer: -4/3
check: -1 / (3 / 4)
hint: Perpendicular slopes multiply to $-1$.
solution: The negative reciprocal of $\tfrac{3}{4}$ is $-\tfrac{4}{3}$. Check: $\tfrac{3}{4} \times (-\tfrac{4}{3}) = -1$.

@quiz num
prompt: Find the distance between $(-3, 4)$ and $(2, -8)$.
answer: 13
check: math.sqrt((2 - (-3))**2 + (-8 - 4)**2)
hint: The differences are $5$ and $-12$.
solution: $\sqrt{5^2 + (-12)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13$.

@quiz tuple
prompt: Point $P$ lies on segment $\overline{AB}$ with $A(0, 0)$ and $B(10, 5)$, and $AP : PB = 2 : 3$. Find the coordinates of $P$.
answer: (4, 2)
check: (0 + 2 / 5 * (10 - 0), 0 + 2 / 5 * (5 - 0))
hint: $P$ is $\dfrac{2}{2 + 3}$ of the way from $A$ to $B$.
solution: $P = \left(\tfrac{2}{5}(10), \tfrac{2}{5}(5)\right) = (4, 2)$.
