@article
Authors do not tell you everything. They leave clues, and you figure out the rest. That is called an **inference**.

### What is an inference?

An **inference** is a smart guess made from clues in the text plus what you already know. It is not a wild guess. You must be able to point to clues.

You make inferences every day. If you see wet boots by the door and a puddle on the floor, you infer that it rained or snowed. Nobody told you. The clues told you.

### The inference recipe

**Clue from the text + what I know = my inference**

Try it. Text: "Ana put on her coat, her hat and her mittens before she opened the door."

- Clues: coat, hat, mittens.
- What I know: People dress like that when it is cold.
- Inference: It is cold outside.

### Quotations

A **quotation** is the exact words from the text, put inside quotation marks. When you make an inference, prove it with quotations. A strong answer uses two.

Example: "I think it is cold outside. The text says Ana put on 'her coat, her hat and her mittens.'"

Use words like "The text says" or "The author wrote" before the quotation.

### Check your inference

Ask: "Can I point to the words that made me think this?" If you cannot, you may be guessing. Look for the clues again.

### Feelings clues

Authors often show feelings with actions. "Jo's hands shook and her voice was small" shows that Jo feels nervous. The word "nervous" is never written, but the clues show it.

@example
title: Make an inference with two quotations
problem: Read: "Dev looked at the clock again. He tapped his foot. 'Is it time yet?' he asked, for the third time." How does Dev feel? Prove it with two quotations.
step: Find clues. He looks at the clock again, taps his foot, and asks the same question three times.
step: Think about what you know. People who keep checking the clock and tapping their foot are waiting and cannot sit still.
step: Make the inference: Dev feels impatient. He wants the time to come quickly.
step: Prove it with two quotations: The text says he "tapped his foot" and he asked "Is it time yet?" for "the third time."

@mistakes
- Making a guess with no clue in the text to point to.
- Copying the clue but not saying what you think it shows.
- Using quotations without quotation marks, or changing the author's words inside them.

choice: Kai is hungry.
choice*: Kai is sleepy.
choice: Kai is angry.
choice: Kai is playing outside.
hint: Think about when people yawn and close their eyes under a blanket.
solution: The yawning, closing eyes and blanket are clues that Kai is sleepy.
