summary: Name equal parts of a whole as halves, thirds, fourths and other fractions.
objective: Tell when a whole is cut into equal parts.
objective: Name a fraction using a top number and a bottom number.

@article
A **fraction** names part of a whole. The whole can be a pizza, a sandwich, a ribbon or a square.

### Equal parts

Cut a sandwich into 2 pieces of the **same size**. Each piece is one **half**. If the pieces are not the same size, they are not halves.

Other names:

- 3 equal parts are **thirds**.
- 4 equal parts are **fourths** (also called quarters).
- 6 equal parts are **sixths**.
- 8 equal parts are **eighths**.

### Two numbers

A fraction is written with two numbers, one over the other.

$$\frac{3}{4}$$

The **bottom number** (the denominator) tells how many equal parts the whole is cut into. The **top number** (the numerator) tells how many of those parts we are talking about.

So $\frac{3}{4}$ means the whole was cut into 4 equal parts and we have 3 of them.

### One part

$\frac{1}{2}$ is one of 2 equal parts. $\frac{1}{3}$ is one of 3 equal parts. $\frac{1}{4}$ is one of 4 equal parts.

### A whole

When you have all the parts, you have the whole thing. If a bar is cut into 4 equal parts and you have all 4, that is $\frac{4}{4}$, which is 1 whole.

### Bigger bottom number, smaller piece

Cut a pizza for 2 people and each slice is big. Cut it for 8 people and each slice is small. So $\frac{1}{8}$ is smaller than $\frac{1}{2}$.

@example
title: Name the fraction
problem: A chocolate bar is cut into 4 equal pieces. You eat 3 pieces. What fraction of the bar did you eat?
step: The bar is cut into 4 equal pieces, so the bottom number is 4.
step: You ate 3 pieces, so the top number is 3.
step: You ate $\frac{3}{4}$ of the bar.

@mistakes
- Counting parts that are not equal in size.
- Writing the numbers upside down. The bottom number is how many parts in all.
- Thinking a bigger bottom number means a bigger piece. It means a smaller piece.
