@article
Inside a computer, everything is stored with just **two choices**: on or off. We write them as **1** (on) and **0** (off). This way of writing numbers is called **binary**.

### The binary cards

Make five cards. The first has 1 dot. The next has 2 dots. Then 4 dots, 8 dots and 16 dots. Lay them in a row with the biggest on the left:

```text
16   8   4   2   1
```

Each card is either **face up** (you see the dots) or **face down** (you do not). To make a number, **add up the dots on the face-up cards**.

### Making a number

Say the 4 card and the 1 card are face up. The other cards are face down. Add 4 plus 1. The number is **5**.

We write the cards as 1s and 0s. A 1 is a face-up card. A 0 is a face-down card.

```text
16  8  4  2  1
 0  0  1  0  1
```

So 5 in binary is **00101**.

### Counting from 0 to 31

With all cards face down, the number is 0. With only the 1 card up, it is 1. With only the 2 card up, it is 2. With the 2 card and the 1 card up, it is 3. Keep going! With all five cards face up, it is 16 + 8 + 4 + 2 + 1, which is 31. Five cards can make every number from 0 to 31.

Grown-up helper: say a number, such as 11, and let your child flip the cards to show it. Then flip some cards and let your child add up the dots.

@example
title: Showing a number with cards
problem: Which cards are face up to show the number 10?
step: Start with the biggest card that is not too big. 16 is more than 10, so it stays face down. 8 is not too big, so turn it up. We have used 8, and 2 is left to make.
step: The 4 card is too big for 2, so it stays down. The 2 card makes exactly 2, so turn it up.
step: The 1 card stays down. Check: 8 plus 2 is 10.
answer: The 8 card and the 2 card.

@mistakes
- Adding the face-down cards. Only the face-up cards count.
- Putting the cards in the wrong order. Always keep the biggest on the left.
- Forgetting that a card can be used only once. Each card is either up or down.
