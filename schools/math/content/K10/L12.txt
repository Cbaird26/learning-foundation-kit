@article
The internet is a big place, with many people and many computers. It can be fun and useful. Like any big place, it needs some rules to stay safe.

### Keep private things private

Some things are **private**. Do not share them online without asking a grown-up you trust:

- your full name,
- your home address,
- your school,
- your phone number,
- your passwords.

A **password** is a secret word that keeps your account safe. Do not tell it to friends. You may tell a grown-up who looks after you.

### Strangers and surprises

People online are not always who they say they are. If someone you do not know sends you a message, do not reply. Tell a grown-up.

If something online makes you feel scared, sad or confused, stop and **tell a grown-up** right away. You are not in trouble. Telling is always the right thing to do.

Be careful with pop-ups and messages that say "You won a prize! Click here!" Do not click. Ask a grown-up first.

### Be kind

Behind every screen is a real person with feelings. Be kind online, just as you are in person:

- Say please and thank you.
- Do not say mean things.
- Ask before you share a picture of a friend.
- If someone is unkind to you or a friend, tell a grown-up.

### Three rules

Making three safety rules into a poster is a good way to remember them. Keep it short, with big words and pictures.

Grown-up helper: look at a game or website that your child uses. Talk about which parts are private, and agree on what to do if something feels wrong.

@example
title: What would you do?
problem: A person you do not know sends you a message online and asks for your address. What do you do?
step: Do not answer the question. Your address is private.
step: Stop and show the message to a grown-up you trust.
step: Your grown-up can help you decide what to do next.
answer: Do not reply. Tell a grown-up.

@mistakes
- Sharing private things, such as a home address or a password, because someone asked nicely.
- Keeping quiet when something feels wrong. Always tell a grown-up.
- Thinking that being unkind online does not matter because the other person cannot see you.

@practice mc
prompt: Which of these is private, so you should not share it online without a grown-up's help?
choice*: Your home address
choice: Your favorite color
choice: Your favorite animal
choice: Your favorite cartoon
hint: Which one would help a stranger find where you live?
solution: Your home address tells people where to find you, so keep it private. Favorite colors and animals are not private in that way.

@practice self
prompt: Make a poster of three rules for staying safe online. Use big words and a picture for each rule. Show your poster to your grown-up and explain why each rule helps.
hint: Take it one step at a time, and ask your grown-up to check each part with you.
solution: Three good rules are: 1. Keep private things private (like your address and password). 2. Do not talk to or click on things from strangers. 3. Tell a grown-up if something feels wrong. You could also pick a rule about being kind.
rubric: The poster has three clear rules about staying safe or kind online.
rubric: Each rule is short, and has big words and a picture.
rubric: The child explained why each rule helps.
