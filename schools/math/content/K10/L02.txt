@article
A **pattern** is something that repeats in a way you can predict.

Look at these beads:

```text
red blue red blue red blue
```

The part **red blue** repeats again and again. We can say what comes next: red! Spotting the part that repeats is how we find a pattern.

Patterns can be made of sounds, colors, shapes or numbers. Here is a clap pattern: clap, clap, stomp, clap, clap, stomp. The part that repeats is **clap, clap, stomp**.

### Number patterns

Numbers can make a pattern too. Count by twos: 2, 4, 6, 8. Each number is 2 more than the one before. The next number is 10.

### Why patterns help

Computers are good at doing the same thing again and again. If we see a pattern, we can write the instructions in a short way. You will do that in the next lesson.

### Instructions for a partner

Now try this with a partner. Choose a shape, such as a square. Write steps that a partner can follow to draw it. The partner cannot see your shape! Use exact words, such as "draw a line down, 3 boxes long".

Then your partner follows only your words. Look at the drawing. Does it match your shape? If not, change your steps and try again.

Grown-up helper: be the partner. Follow the words exactly, even if you can guess what the shape is. Do not fill in what is missing.

@example
title: What comes next?
problem: Here is a pattern of shapes: circle, square, square, circle, square, square, circle. What comes next?
step: Look for the part that repeats. It is circle, square, square.
step: The pattern has just shown circle. So the next shapes are square, then square.
step: The next shape is a square.
answer: square

@mistakes
- Picking the part that repeats too short or too long. Check that it fits all the way along.
- Forgetting where you are in the pattern. Say the repeating part out loud as you go.
- Writing steps for a partner that leave out details, like how long a line is or which way to go.

@practice self
prompt: Pick a square. Write steps for your grown-up to draw it. Use words like "draw a line to the right, 3 boxes long". Your grown-up must follow only your words and may not look at your paper. Does the drawing match?
hint: Take it one step at a time, and ask your grown-up to check each part with you.
solution: A good set is: 1. Put your pencil on a dot. 2. Draw a line to the right, 3 boxes long. 3. Draw a line down, 3 boxes long. 4. Draw a line to the left, 3 boxes long. 5. Draw a line up, 3 boxes long. The pencil ends where it started and the shape is a square.
rubric: The steps are numbered and in an order that works.
rubric: The steps say which way to go and how far.
rubric: The grown-up followed only the words, and the child fixed any step that went wrong.
