@lesson
title: Loops: doing it again
summary: Use "repeat" to shorten steps that happen again and again, and work out what a loop does.
objective: Say what a loop is.
objective: Count how many times a loop does a step.
objective: Follow a loop on the robot grid.
reading: CS Unplugged, look up an activity about repeating instructions, such as drawing a picture with repeat steps.

@article
A **loop** means doing the same steps again and again.

Say you want to clap four times. You could write:

```text
clap
clap
clap
clap
```

That is long. We can write it in a short way:

```text
Repeat 4 times:
    clap
```

The steps that get repeated are tucked under the word "Repeat". We push them in a little from the left so we can see which steps are in the loop.

### More than one step in a loop

A loop can have more than one step. Look:

```text
Repeat 3 times:
    jump
    spin
```

Each time around, you do **jump** and then **spin**. You do that three times. So you jump 3 times and spin 3 times. That is 6 moves in all.

### Loops for the robot

Let us use the robot from before. It starts on A1. Here is a program:

```text
Repeat 2 times:
    →
    →
```

The robot goes right, right, right, right. Careful! The grid has only four columns, so the robot would fall off the edge. In our games the robot always stays on the grid.

Loops are one of the most useful ideas in computers. They make long lists of steps short.

@example
title: Counting a loop
problem: Look at this program. How many times do you clap, and how many times do you stomp?

```text
Repeat 4 times:
    clap
    stomp
```
step: The steps inside the loop are clap and stomp.
step: The loop goes around 4 times. Each time around, you clap once and stomp once.
step: So you clap 4 times and you stomp 4 times.
answer: 4 claps and 4 stomps

@mistakes
- Counting the steps inside the loop only once. Multiply: the number of times around, times the steps in the loop.
- Forgetting that every step under "Repeat" is repeated, not just the first one.
- Repeating the wrong number of times. Say "one, two, three" out loud as you go around.

@practice num

```text
Repeat 3 times:
    step forward
    step forward
```
answer: 6
check: 3 * 2
hint: How many steps forward are there each time around the loop?
solution: Each time around, you take 2 steps forward. You go around 3 times, so 3 times 2 is 6 steps.

@practice text
prompt: The robot starts on A1. It follows this program. Which square does it end on? Type the square name.

```text
Repeat 3 times:
    →
    ↑
```

```text
4 . . . .
3 . . . .
2 . . . .
1 R . . .
  A B C D
```
answer: D4
accept: d4
check: run("d={'R':(1,0),'L':(-1,0),'U':(0,1),'D':(0,-1)}\nx,y=0,1\nfor m in 'RU' * 3:\n    x+=d[m][0]\n    y+=d[m][1]\nprint('ABCD'[x]+str(y))\n")
hint: Each time around the loop the robot goes right one and up one. Go around three times, saying the square each time.
solution: First time around: B2. Second time: C3. Third time: D4. The robot ends on D4.

@practice mc

choice: Repeat 2 times: →
