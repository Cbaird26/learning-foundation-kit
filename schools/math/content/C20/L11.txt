@article
Suppose you must solve $-u''=f$ with $u(0)=u(L)=0$ for many different loads $f$; physically, $u$ is the sag of a string under a load $f$. A load is a sum of many tiny point loads, and by linearity the response to a sum is the sum of the responses. So it is enough to know the response to one unit point load at position $s$. That response is the **Green function** $G(x,s)$.

### Definition by four properties

For fixed $s\in(0,L)$, $G(\cdot,s)$ solves
$$-\frac{\partial^2G}{\partial x^2}=\delta(x-s),\qquad G(0,s)=G(L,s)=0,$$
where $\delta(x-s)$ is the idealized unit point load at $x=s$ (week 12 makes this precise). Integrating the equation across $x=s$ shows what it means in ordinary terms:

1. $G$ is linear in $x$ on each side of $s$, because $-G_{xx}=0$ for $x\ne s$;
2. $G(0,s)=G(L,s)=0$;
3. $G$ is continuous at $x=s$;
4. $G_x$ jumps by $-1$ there: $G_x(s^+,s)-G_x(s^-,s)=-1$.

Use these four properties as the working test for any proposed $G$.

### The formula and the solution operator

$$G(x,s)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{x(L-s)}{L},&0\le x\le s,\\[2mm]\dfrac{s(L-x)}{L},&s\le x\le L,\end{cases}\qquad u(x)=\int_0^LG(x,s)\,f(s)\,ds.$$
The slopes are $\frac{L-s}{L}$ to the left of $s$ and $-\frac sL$ to the right, so the jump is $-\frac sL-\frac{L-s}{L}=-1$. The integral says the solution is a superposition of point responses weighted by the load. Also $G(x,s)=G(s,x)$ (**reciprocity**) and $G\ge0$.

### Other expressions

**Eigenfunction expansion.** With $\phi_n=\sqrt{2/L}\,\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}$ and $\lambda_n=\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2$, the Green function is $\sum_n\frac{\phi_n(x)\phi_n(s)}{\lambda_n}$:
$$G(x,s)=\frac{2L}{\pi^2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\sin\frac{n\pi s}{L}}{n^2}.$$
For $L=1$ and $x=s=\frac12$ this gives $\frac{2}{\pi^2}\sum_{n\text{ odd}}\frac1{n^2}=\frac{2}{\pi^2}\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{8}=\frac14$, in agreement with $\frac{x(L-s)}{L}$.

**Free space and images.** On the whole line, $-G_0''=\delta(x-s)$ is solved by $G_0=-\frac12|x-s|$ (plus any linear function). On the half line $x>0$ with $G(0,s)=0$, an image source of opposite sign at $-s$ gives $G_0(x-s)-G_0(x+s)=\min(x,s)$.

**Heat and higher dimensions.** The heat kernel $\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi kt}}e^{-x^2/(4kt)}$ of week 6 is the response to a point pulse of heat: the Green function of the heat equation. In three dimensions the Green function of $-\Delta u=\delta$ is $\frac{1}{4\pi|x-y|}$, the electrostatic potential of a unit point charge.

@example
title: Constructing the Green function
problem: Construct $G(x,s)$ for $-u''=f$ on $(0,L)$ with $u(0)=u(L)=0$.
step: For $x\ne s$ we need $G_{xx}=0$, so $G$ is linear on each side. The end conditions give $G=a\,x$ for $x<s$ and $G=b\,(L-x)$ for $x>s$.
step: Continuity at $x=s$: $a\,s=b\,(L-s)$.
step: Jump of the derivative: $G_x(s^+)-G_x(s^-)=-b-a=-1$, so $a+b=1$.
step: Solve the two equations: $a=\frac{L-s}{L}$ and $b=\frac sL$. Hence $G=\frac{x(L-s)}{L}$ for $x\le s$ and $\frac{s(L-x)}{L}$ for $x\ge s$.

@example
title: Solving with the Green function
problem: Use the Green function with $L=1$ to solve $-u''=1$, $u(0)=u(1)=0$.
step: For a fixed $x$, split at $s=x$. For $s<x$ we use the branch $x\ge s$: $G=s(1-x)$; for $s>x$ we use $G=x(1-s)$.
step: $u(x)=\int_0^xs(1-x)\,ds+\int_x^1x(1-s)\,ds=(1-x)\frac{x^2}{2}+x\frac{(1-x)^2}{2}$.
step: Factor: $u(x)=\frac{x(1-x)}{2}\left[x+(1-x)\right]=\frac{x(1-x)}{2}$.
step: Check: $-u''=1$ and $u(0)=u(1)=0$. The sag is largest at the midpoint, $u(\frac12)=\frac18$.

@mistakes
- Using a continuous $G_x$ or a jump in $G$ itself; the Green function is continuous and its slope jumps by $-1$ for the equation $-G''=\delta$.
- Getting the sign of the jump wrong: for $G''=\delta$ (no minus sign) the jump is $+1$.
- Swapping the two branches: the formula $\frac{x(L-s)}{L}$ holds where $x\le s$, and $\frac{s(L-x)}{L}$ where $x\ge s$.
- Imposing the end conditions on only one of the two pieces.

@practice self

solution: For $x<s$, $G=a\,x$. For $x>s$, $G_x(L)=0$ forces $G$ to be constant, $G=c$. Continuity: $as=c$. Jump: $0-a=-1$, so $a=1$ and $c=s$. Hence $G(x,s)=\min(x,s)$. hint: Since $\sin\pi x$ is an eigenfunction with $\lambda=\pi^2$, the solution is a multiple of it. Find the multiple by substituting into $-u''=\sin\pi x$.

solution: $u=A\sin\pi x$ satisfies the end conditions and $-u''=A\pi^2\sin\pi x$, so $A=\frac1{\pi^2}$ and $u\left(\frac12\right)=\frac1{\pi^2}\approx0.1013$. @quiz ms
prompt: Let $G(x,s)=\frac{\min(x,s)\,(L-\max(x,s))}{L}$ be the Green function of $-u''=f$ with $u(0)=u(L)=0$. Select all true statements.
choice*: $G(x,s)=G(s,x)$ for all $x,s$ in $[0,L]$.
choice*: $G(x,s)\ge0$ everywhere.
choice: $G$ is differentiable in $x$ at $x=s$.
choice: $G$ satisfies $-G_{xx}=1$ for every $x\ne s$.
hint: Look at the formula under the swap $x\leftrightarrow s$, and recall that $G$ is linear on each side of $s$.
solution: Swapping $x$ and $s$ swaps min and max in the formula and gives the same value, and both factors are nonnegative. The slope jumps by $-1$ at $x=s$, so $G$ is not differentiable there, and $G$ is linear (so $G_{xx}=0$) on each side.
