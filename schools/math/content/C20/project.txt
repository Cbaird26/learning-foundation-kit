step: Fix the problem: $u_t=u_{xx}$ on $0<x<\pi$, $u(0,t)=u(\pi,t)=0$, $u(x,0)=f(x)=\min(x,\pi-x)$ (a tent with a kink at $\pi/2$). Derive $b_n=\frac{4}{\pi n^2}\sin\frac{n\pi}{2}$ and the solution $u=\sum b_ne^{-n^2t}\sin nx$. For the wave variant use $u_{tt}=u_{xx}$ with $g=0$, so $u=\sum b_n\cos(nt)\sin(nx)$.
step: Build a reference by summing 4000 modes on a fine grid. Verify it three ways: the initial condition at $t=0$, the boundary values, and Parseval's identity $\frac\pi2\sum b_n^2=\int_0^\pi f^2\,dx$. Check the PDE with centered differences at a time $t\ge0.05$.

Plot on a log-log scale for $t=0$ and a semilog scale for $t>0$. step: Explain the stability limit: insert a single Fourier mode $\sin(jk\Delta x)$ into the scheme, compute its amplification factor $1-4r\sin^2\frac{k\Delta x}{2}$, and show that it leaves $[-1,1]$ exactly when $r>\frac12$.
step: Cost comparison. Plot the error at $t=0.1$ against the number of unknowns for both methods ($N$ modes versus $M$ grid points) and describe where each method wins and why.
step: Rough data and the wave variant. Repeat the truncation study with $f=1$ (a jump after odd extension) and measure the overshoot of the partial sums near the ends at $t=0$ and at $t=0.01$. Then run the wave equation with the tent profile and a leapfrog scheme at $\Delta t=0.5\Delta x$, and show that the truncation error does not improve as $t$ grows.
step: Write the report and rehearse a three-minute spoken explanation, without notes, of one result (for example why the heat error decays exponentially in $N$ for $t>0$ but only like $1/N$ at $t=0$).

@review
This course solved the three model equations with one toolkit: expand in orthogonal functions, let the equation act on each mode, and reassemble.

### Week by week

1. **Fourier series.** On $[-L,L]$, $a_n=\frac1L\int f\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}$ and $b_n=\frac1L\int f\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}$, with constant term $\frac{a_0}{2}$. Even gives cosines, odd gives sines. The series converges to $\frac12[f(x^-)+f(x^+)]$.
2. **Orthogonality.** Squared norms $L$ for each sine and cosine, $2L$ for $1$; Parseval's identity; Gibbs overshoot of about $9\%$ of the jump; at $x=\pm L$ the series averages $f(L^-)$ and $f(-L^+)$.
3. **Fourier transform.** $\hat f(\xi)=\int fe^{-i\xi x}dx$; shift, scaling, $\widehat{f'}=i\xi\hat f$; Plancherel; box, $e^{-a|x|}$ and Gaussian pairs.
4. **Convolution.** $\widehat{f*g}=\hat f\hat g$; smoothing and low-pass filtering; modulation moves the spectrum.
5. **Separation of variables.** $u=XT$, then $X''+\lambda X=0$ with the end conditions, superposition, matching the initial data.
6. **Heat equation.** Modes decay like $e^{-k(n\pi/L)^2t}$; subtract the steady state for nonzero ends; heat kernel; maximum principle.
7. **Wave equation.** D'Alembert's formula; fixed ends with $\omega_n=\frac{n\pi c}{L}$, $A_n$ from $f$ and $B_n=\frac1{\omega_n}\times$ (sine coefficient of $g$); finite speed.
8. **Energy estimates.** Wave energy is conserved, heat energy $\frac12\int u^2$ decreases; uniqueness; $E=\frac L4\sum\omega_n^2(A_n^2+B_n^2)$.
9. **Laplace equation.** Mean value and maximum principles; rectangle with $\sinh$ factors; disk with $r^n$.
10. **Boundary conditions.** Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin; uniqueness from $\int|\nabla w|^2=\oint ww_n$; Neumann needs $\oint g\,ds=0$; the $n=0$ mode.
11. **Green functions.** Four properties (linear pieces, end conditions, continuity, slope jump $-1$) and $u=\int G f\,ds$.
12. **Distributions.** $\langle\delta_a,\varphi\rangle=\varphi(a)$, $\langle T',\varphi\rangle=-\langle T,\varphi'\rangle$, $H'=\delta$, the jump rule, $|x|''=2\delta$.

### Self-check

- I can derive the coefficient formulas from orthogonality without notes.
- For a piecewise function I can state the series value at each jump and at $\pm L$ before computing any coefficient.
- I can transform a box, exponential or Gaussian and use the shift, scaling and derivative rules.
- For each pair of end conditions I can list the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions.
- I can solve heat, wave and Laplace problems and check each answer against the PDE, boundary and initial data.
- I can prove uniqueness by an energy or Green's identity argument and verify a Green function from its four properties.

### Common errors

- Forgetting the $\frac12$ on $a_0$, or using $\cos nx$ when the period is $2L\ne2\pi$.
- Sign slips: $X''+\lambda X=0$ with $T'+k\lambda T=0$; $\sinh$ (not oscillation) in the Laplace variable; the jump $-1$ of $G_x$.
- Using zero-end formulas for nonzero ends without subtracting the steady state.
- Dropping $\frac{1}{\omega_n}$ in $B_n$, or taking $c^2$ as the wave speed.
- Treating $\delta$ as an ordinary function with a value at $0$.

Take the Fourier transform in $x$ (convention $\hat f(\xi)=\int f e^{-i\xi x}dx$) to get $\hat u(\xi,t)=\hat f(\xi)\,e^{-\xi^2t}$, then use the Gaussian transform pair and the convolution theorem to find $u\!\left(1,\tfrac12\right)$. Give an exact value.
answer: e^(-1/4)/sqrt(2)
check: quad(lambda y: exp(-(1 - y)**2/(4*0.5))/sqrt(4*pi*0.5)*exp(-y**2/2), -15, 15)
hint: The factor $e^{-\xi^2t}$ is the transform of the heat kernel $\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}e^{-x^2/(4t)}$, so $u=G(\cdot,t)*f$.
hint: Convolving two Gaussians adds their variances: $f\propto e^{-x^2/2}$ has variance $1$ and $G$ has variance $2t$.
hint: The result is $u(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+2t}}\,e^{-x^2/(2(1+2t))}$.

Its sine coefficients are $A_n=\frac{4}{\pi n^2}\sin\frac{n\pi}{2}$. What fraction of the total energy $E=\frac12\int_0^\pi\left(u_t^2+u_x^2\right)dx$ is carried by the fundamental mode $n=1$? Give an exact value.
answer: 8/pi^2
check: (lambda E1, E: E1/E)(pi/4*(2/pi*(quad(lambda x: x*sin(x), 0, pi/2) + quad(lambda x: (pi - x)*sin(x), pi/2, pi)))**2, 0.5*(quad(lambda x: nderiv(lambda s: s, x)**2, 0, pi/2) + quad(lambda x: nderiv(lambda s: pi - s, x)**2, pi/2, pi)))
hint: At $t=0$ the string is at rest, so $E=\frac12\int_0^\pi f'(x)^2\,dx$, and $f'=\pm1$ except at the kink.
hint: The energy of mode $n$ is $E_n=\frac L4\omega_n^2\left(A_n^2+B_n^2\right)$ with $L=\pi$, $\omega_n=n$ and $B_n=0$.
hint: Compute $A_1=\frac4\pi$, then divide $E_1$ by the total energy.
solution: Total energy: $E=\frac12\int_0^\pi1\,dx=\frac\pi2$. Fundamental: $E_1=\frac\pi4\cdot1\cdot A_1^2=\frac\pi4\cdot\frac{16}{\pi^2}=\frac4\pi$. @exam self
prompt: Consider the pure Neumann problem $-u''=f$ on $0<x<1$ with $u'(0)=u'(1)=0$. (a) Show that a solution can exist only if $\int_0^1f\,dx=0$, and that two solutions differ by a constant. (b) Test the weak form $-\int_0^1G\,\varphi''\,dx=\varphi(s)$ with $\varphi\equiv1$ to show that no Green function $G$ with $G_x(0,s)=G_x(1,s)=0$ can satisfy $-G_{xx}=\delta_s$. (c) Explain how adding a uniform compensating load, $-G_{xx}=\delta_s-1$, repairs the problem, and check this with the same test function.
hint: For (a) integrate the equation over $(0,1)$ and use the end conditions; for the constant, look at $w=u_1-u_2$.
hint: The test function $\varphi\equiv1$ has $\varphi'=0$ at both ends, so no boundary terms appear when you integrate by parts against $G$ with $G_x=0$ at the ends.
solution: (a) $\int_0^1f\,dx=-\int_0^1u''\,dx=-\left[u'(1)-u'(0)\right]=0$. If $u_1,u_2$ both solve the problem, $w=u_1-u_2$ has $w''=0$ and $w'(0)=0$, so $w'\equiv0$ and $w$ is constant. (b) With $\varphi\equiv1$ we have $\varphi''=0$, so the left side of the weak form is $0$, while the right side is $\varphi(s)=1$. This contradiction shows that no such $G$ exists: free ends cannot balance a single point load. (c) For $-G_{xx}=\delta_s-1$ the weak form reads $-\int G\varphi''\,dx=\varphi(s)-\int_0^1\varphi\,dx$, and for $\varphi\equiv1$ both sides are $0$, so the compatibility condition holds. This modified Green function is a point load balanced by a uniform load of opposite sign, and it is unique only up to an additive constant.
rubric: Obtains the compatibility condition $\int_0^1f\,dx=0$ by integrating the equation and using $u'(0)=u'(1)=0$, and shows that solutions differ by a constant.
rubric: Uses the test function $\varphi\equiv1$ correctly to derive the contradiction $0=1$, and explains physically why free ends cannot support a point load.
rubric: Shows that the modified equation $-G_{xx}=\delta_s-1$ passes the test ($0=1-1$) and notes that the modified Green function is unique only up to a constant.
