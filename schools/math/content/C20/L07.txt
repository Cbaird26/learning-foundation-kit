@article
A taut string, a sound wave in a pipe, a ripple along a cable: for small disturbances each obeys the **wave equation**
$$u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx},$$
where $c>0$ is the wave speed. For a string, $c=\sqrt{T/\rho}$ with tension $T$ (newtons) and linear density $\rho$ (kg/m), so $c$ is in m/s. The equation is second order in time, so it needs two initial conditions: the shape $u(x,0)=f(x)$ and the velocity $u_t(x,0)=g(x)$.

### Traveling waves and d'Alembert's formula

For any twice-differentiable $F$, the function $F(x-ct)$ solves the equation: it is a fixed shape moving right at speed $c$. Likewise $H(x+ct)$ moves left. On the whole line every solution is $u=F(x-ct)+H(x+ct)$, and matching the initial data gives **d'Alembert's formula**:
$$u(x,t)=\frac{f(x-ct)+f(x+ct)}{2}+\frac1{2c}\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}g(s)\,ds.$$
Each datum plays a different role. The displacement splits into two half-size copies that run apart. The velocity is **integrated** over the window $[x-ct,x+ct]$.

### Finite speed

The value at $(x,t)$ depends only on the data on $[x-ct,x+ct]$, its **domain of dependence**; a disturbance starting at $x_0$ cannot be felt outside $|x-x_0|\le ct$. The heat kernel, in contrast, is positive everywhere for every $t>0$. Waves also do not smooth: a jump in $f$ travels as a jump.

### The string with fixed ends

For $0<x<L$ with $u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0$, separation of variables gives the eigenfunctions $\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}$ of week 5. The time equation is now $T''+c^2\lambda_nT=0$, so time dependence oscillates instead of decaying:
$$u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(A_n\cos\omega_nt+B_n\sin\omega_nt\right)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L},\qquad\omega_n=\frac{n\pi c}{L}.$$
Setting $t=0$ and using orthogonality,
$$A_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx,\qquad B_n=\frac{2}{n\pi c}\int_0^Lg(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx.$$
The factor $\frac{1}{n\pi c}$ appears because $u_t(x,0)=\sum\omega_nB_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}$, so $\omega_nB_n$ is the sine coefficient of $g$.

### Standing waves and music

Each term is a **standing wave**: a fixed spatial pattern oscillating in place at frequency $\frac{\omega_n}{2\pi}=\frac{nc}{2L}$ hertz. The first mode is the fundamental, and the overtones have whole-number multiples of its frequency, which is why a string sounds musical. A standing wave is also a pair of traveling waves, since
$$\sin kx\cos kct=\tfrac12\sin k(x-ct)+\tfrac12\sin k(x+ct).$$
Without damping the modes never decay; compare the heat equation, where mode $n$ decays at rate $k(n\pi/L)^2$.

@example
title: D'Alembert with a velocity-only start
problem: Solve $u_{tt}=u_{xx}$ on $\mathbb R$ with $u(x,0)=0$ and $u_t(x,0)=\cos x$.
step: Here $c=1$ and $f=0$, so $u(x,t)=\frac12\int_{x-t}^{x+t}\cos s\,ds$.
step: Integrate: $u=\frac12\left[\sin(x+t)-\sin(x-t)\right]$.
step: Use $\sin(x+t)-\sin(x-t)=2\cos x\sin t$ to get $u(x,t)=\cos x\sin t$.
step: Check: $u_{tt}=-\cos x\sin t=u_{xx}$, $u(x,0)=0$ and $u_t(x,0)=\cos x\cos0=\cos x$.

@example
title: A string with fixed ends
problem: Solve $u_{tt}=u_{xx}$ on $0<x<\pi$ with $u(0,t)=u(\pi,t)=0$, $u(x,0)=\sin x+2\sin 3x$ and $u_t(x,0)=\sin 2x$.
step: With $c=1$ and $L=\pi$ the frequencies are $\omega_n=n$.
step: The displacement is already a sine series: $A_1=1$, $A_3=2$ and all other $A_n=0$.
step: The velocity $\sin2x$ means $\omega_2B_2=1$, so $B_2=\frac12$, and all other $B_n=0$.
step: Hence $u(x,t)=\cos t\sin x+2\cos3t\sin3x+\frac12\sin2t\sin2x$.
step: Check: $u(x,0)=\sin x+2\sin3x$ and $u_t(x,0)=\frac12\cdot2\cos0\sin2x=\sin2x$.

@mistakes
- Using $c^2$ as the speed: the equation contains $c^2$, but waves travel at speed $c$.
- Forgetting the factor $\frac{1}{2c}$ in d'Alembert's formula, or averaging $g$ instead of integrating it over $[x-ct,x+ct]$.
- Dropping the factor $\frac{1}{\omega_n}$ when finding $B_n$ from the velocity $g$.
- Expecting the vibrating string to settle down; without damping its modes oscillate forever.

hint: Compute $u_{tt}$ and $u_{xx}$ directly; then use $\sin A\cos B=\frac12[\sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B)]$.
hint: A function of $x-ct$ moves right at speed $c$; a function of $x+ct$ moves left.
solution: (a) $u_{tt}=-k^2c^2\sin kx\cos kct$ and $u_{xx}=-k^2\sin kx\cos kct$, so $u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}$. (c) Nodes are where $\sin kx=0$, namely $x=\frac{m\pi}{k}$, $m\in\mathbb Z$. At a fixed $x$ the motion is $\cos(kct)$, which repeats when $kct$ increases by $2\pi$, so the period is $\frac{2\pi}{kc}$ and the frequency is $\frac{kc}{2\pi}$.
