@article
A PDE alone does not single out a solution; the **boundary conditions** do, and they also decide the eigenfunctions in separation of variables. Let $u_n$ be the derivative in the direction of the outward normal. The classical types are:

| type | condition | heat-flow meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Dirichlet | $u=g$ | temperature prescribed |
| Neumann | $u_n=g$ | heat flux prescribed ($g=0$: insulated) |
| Robin | $\alpha u+\beta u_n=g$ | heat exchanged with the surroundings |

A condition is **homogeneous** if $g=0$; separation of variables needs homogeneous conditions on the sides that carry the eigenfunctions. **Periodic** conditions ($u(0)=u(L)$, $u'(0)=u'(L)$) are a fourth type and produce both sines and cosines.

### Robin conditions in practice

The cooling law says an end at $x=L$ loses heat at a rate proportional to its excess temperature over the surroundings: $-k\,u_x(L)=h\,[u(L)-u_{\mathrm{env}}]$. With $u_{\mathrm{env}}=0$ this reads $u_x+\kappa u=0$ with $\kappa=h/k>0$. The eigenvalue problem $X''+\lambda X=0$, $X(0)=0$, $X'(L)+\kappa X(L)=0$ has $X=\sin\mu x$ and
$$\mu\cos\mu L+\kappa\sin\mu L=0,\qquad\text{i.e. }\tan\mu L=-\frac{\mu}{\kappa}.$$
There is no closed form, so the roots are found numerically. They lie between the mixed values $\mu L=\left(n-\frac12\right)\pi$ (for $\kappa\to0$, insulated) and the Dirichlet values $\mu L=n\pi$ (for $\kappa\to\infty$). The eigenfunctions are still orthogonal, so the coefficient formulas work with $\int_0^LX_n^2\,dx$ in the denominator.

### Uniqueness by Green's identity

Let $w=u_1-u_2$ be the difference of two solutions of $\Delta u=0$ in a bounded region $\Omega$. Since $\Delta w=0$,
$$\int_\Omega|\nabla w|^2\,dA=\oint_{\partial\Omega}w\,w_n\,ds.$$
- **Dirichlet:** $w=0$ on $\partial\Omega$, so $\nabla w=0$, $w$ is constant, hence $w\equiv0$. The solution is unique.
- **Neumann:** $w_n=0$, so $\nabla w=0$ and $w$ is constant: unique **up to an additive constant**. A solution can exist only if $\oint_{\partial\Omega}g\,ds=0$, because the divergence theorem gives $\oint u_n\,ds=\int_\Omega\Delta u\,dA=0$.
- **Mixed** (Dirichlet on part, Neumann elsewhere): $\nabla w=0$ and $w$ vanishes somewhere, so $w\equiv0$.
- **Robin** with $\alpha/\beta>0$ (heat loss): $\oint ww_n=-\frac\alpha\beta\oint w^2\le0$ forces $w\equiv0$.

### A rectangle with insulated sides

Solve $\Delta u=0$ on $0<x<a$, $0<y<b$ with $u_x(0,y)=u_x(a,y)=0$, $u(x,0)=0$ and $u(x,b)=f(x)$. Insulated sides give cosines in $x$, including $n=0$. For $n=0$ the equation $Y''=0$ with $Y(0)=0$ gives the linear function $Y=y$, not a $\sinh$:
$$u(x,y)=A_0\frac yb+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n\frac{\sinh(n\pi y/a)}{\sinh(n\pi b/a)}\cos\frac{n\pi x}{a},\quad A_0=\frac1a\int_0^af\,dx,\ \ A_n=\frac2a\int_0^af\cos\frac{n\pi x}{a}\,dx.$$

@example
title: A Robin eigenvalue
problem: Find the smallest eigenvalue of $X''+\lambda X=0$ with $X(0)=0$ and $X'(1)+X(1)=0$.
step: For $\lambda\le0$ there is no nontrivial solution: $X=x$ gives $1+1\ne0$, and $X=\sinh\mu x$ gives $\mu\cosh\mu+\sinh\mu>0$. So $\lambda=\mu^2>0$ and $X=\sin\mu x$.
step: The condition at $x=1$ reads $\mu\cos\mu+\sin\mu=0$. Let $g(\mu)=\sin\mu+\mu\cos\mu$.
step: $g\!\left(\frac\pi2\right)=1>0$ and $g(\pi)=-\pi<0$, so a root lies in $\left(\frac\pi2,\pi\right)$, between the insulated and Dirichlet values as predicted.
step: Bisection or a tangent-line iteration: $g(2)\approx0.077>0$ and $g(2.1)\approx-0.197<0$, and refining gives $\mu_1\approx2.0288$.
step: Hence $\lambda_1=\mu_1^2\approx4.116$, between $\left(\frac\pi2\right)^2\approx2.47$ and $\pi^2\approx9.87$.

@example
title: Insulated sides on a rectangle
problem: Solve $\Delta u=0$ on $0<x<\pi$, $0<y<1$ with $u_x(0,y)=u_x(\pi,y)=0$, $u(x,0)=0$ and $u(x,1)=2+\cos2x$.
step: Insulated sides give $X_n=\cos nx$ for $n=0,1,2,\dots$ The data $2+\cos2x$ is already a cosine series with $A_0=2$ and $A_2=1$.
step: The $n=0$ mode has $Y''=0$ and $Y(0)=0$, so $Y=y$ and the term is $2y$. The $n=2$ mode has $Y=\frac{\sinh2y}{\sinh2}$.
step: Therefore $u(x,y)=2y+\frac{\sinh2y}{\sinh2}\cos2x$.
step: Check: $u_{xx}=-4\frac{\sinh2y}{\sinh2}\cos2x$ and $u_{yy}=+4\frac{\sinh2y}{\sinh2}\cos2x$ cancel; $u_x\propto\sin2x$ vanishes at $x=0,\pi$; $u(x,0)=0$ and $u(x,1)=2+\cos2x$.
step: Compatibility check: the net flux is $\int_0^\pi u_y(x,1)\,dx-\int_0^\pi u_y(x,0)\,dx=2\pi-2\pi=0$ (the cosine terms integrate to zero), as it must be for insulated sides.

@mistakes
- Using $\sinh\frac{n\pi y}{a}$ for the $n=0$ mode with insulated sides; the $n=0$ mode is the linear function $y$.
- Applying the outward normal incorrectly: at the left end of an interval $u_n=-u_x$, at the right end $u_n=+u_x$, which matters for the sign in Robin conditions.
- Claiming that a pure Neumann problem always has a solution or has exactly one; it needs $\oint g\,ds=0$ and is unique only up to a constant.
- Assuming Robin eigenvalues are $\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2$; they come from the transcendental equation $\tan\mu L=-\mu/\kappa$.

@practice self
prompt: Consider $\Delta u=0$ on $0<x<\pi$, $0<y<1$ with $u(0,y)=u(\pi,y)=0$ on the sides, an insulated bottom $u_y(x,0)=0$, and $u(x,1)=\sin x+\frac12\sin3x$ on top. (a) Solve the problem. (b) Prove that the solution is unique. (c) Does uniqueness survive if the top condition is replaced by $u_y(x,1)=g(x)$? Is a compatibility condition needed?
hint: Sine modes in $x$; in $y$ solve $Y''=n^2Y$ with $Y'(0)=0$, which selects $\cosh$.
hint: For (b), use $\int|\nabla w|^2=\oint ww_n$ and check, side by side, whether $w$ or $w_n$ vanishes.
solution: (a) $X_n=\sin nx$, $Y''=n^2Y$ and $Y'(0)=0$ give $Y_n=\cosh ny$. Matching the top: $u=\frac{\cosh y}{\cosh1}\sin x+\frac{\cosh3y}{2\cosh3}\sin3x$. (b) For two solutions, $w$ is harmonic with $w=0$ on the sides and top and $w_n=-w_y=0$ on the bottom. Then $\int|\nabla w|^2=\oint ww_n=0$, so $\nabla w=0$, $w$ is constant, and it is $0$ because it vanishes on the sides. (c) Uniqueness still holds, since $w=0$ on the two sides makes the constant zero. No compatibility condition is needed: the Dirichlet sides can carry whatever net flux balances the top and bottom, so the flux prescribed on the top and bottom is not forced to be zero.
rubric: Obtains $\sin nx$ in $x$ and $\cosh ny$ in $y$ (from $Y'(0)=0$), with the correct matching constants $\frac{1}{\cosh n}$ for $n=1,3$.
rubric: Proves uniqueness by Green's identity, checking on each piece of the boundary that $w=0$ or $w_n=0$, and concludes that $w$ is a constant equal to $0$.
rubric: Explains that with Dirichlet sides the top Neumann data keeps uniqueness and needs no compatibility condition, unlike the pure Neumann problem.

@quiz num
prompt: A rod of length $0.4\ \text{m}$ has conductivity $k=2\ \text{W/(m K)}$. Its left end is held at $400\ \text{K}$. Its right end loses heat to air at $300\ \text{K}$ according to $-k\,u_x(L)=h\,[u(L)-300]$ with $h=5\ \text{W/(m}^2\text{K)}$. In the steady state $u_{xx}=0$. Find the temperature at the right end.
answer: 350
check: (lambda c: c[0] + c[1]*0.4)(lin([[1, 0], [5, 2 + 5*0.4]], [400, 5*300]))
unit: K
tol: 1%
hint: In steady state $u=c_0+c_1x$ with $c_0=400$.
hint: The Robin condition at $x=L$ gives $-kc_1=h\,(c_0+c_1L-300)$; solve for $c_1$.
solution: $-2c_1=5(400+0.4c_1-300)$ gives $-2c_1=500+2c_1$, so $c_1=-125\ \text{K/m}$. Then $u(L)=400-125\cdot0.4=350\ \text{K}$.

@quiz ms
prompt: Select all true statements about uniqueness for $\Delta u=0$ in a bounded region.
choice*: The Dirichlet problem has at most one solution.
choice*: Solutions of the Neumann problem are unique only up to an additive constant.
choice: The Neumann problem has a solution for every choice of the boundary function $g$.
choice: A Robin condition $\alpha u+\beta u_n=0$ guarantees uniqueness whatever the signs of $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
hint: Think about what Green's identity gives when $w=0$, and when only $w_n=0$.
hint: The divergence theorem limits which Neumann data $g$ are possible; test the signs in the Robin case with $\oint ww_n=-\frac\alpha\beta\oint w^2$.
solution: The statements that the Dirichlet problem has at most one solution and that Neumann solutions are unique only up to a constant follow from $\int|\nabla w|^2=\oint ww_n$. A Neumann solution exists only if $\oint g\,ds=0$. For Robin conditions uniqueness needs $\frac{\alpha}{\beta}>0$; with the opposite sign the boundary term has the wrong sign and nonzero solutions can exist.
