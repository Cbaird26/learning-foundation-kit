@lesson
title: The second and third isomorphism theorems
summary: State and prove the second and third isomorphism theorems and the correspondence theorem, and use them to compute orders and identify quotients.
objective: Prove that $HN$ is a subgroup when $N$ is normal, and prove the second isomorphism theorem $HN/N \cong H/(H \cap N)$.
objective: Prove the third isomorphism theorem $(G/N)/(K/N) \cong G/K$.
objective: State and prove the correspondence theorem between subgroups of $G/N$ and subgroups of $G$ containing $N$.
objective: Apply the theorems to subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ and to the product formula $|HN| = |H||N|/|H \cap N|$.
reading: Look up the second and third isomorphism theorems and the correspondence theorem in Judson, Abstract Algebra: Theory and Applications, in chapter 11, Homomorphisms (the isomorphism theorems).
@article
In $\mathbb{Z}$ take $H = 4\mathbb{Z}$ and $N = 6\mathbb{Z}$. The sum $H + N = 2\mathbb{Z}$ is a bigger subgroup, and the intersection $H \cap N = 12\mathbb{Z}$ is a smaller one. Count cosets: $2\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ has $3$ elements ($0, 2, 4$ modulo $6$) and $4\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ has $3$ elements ($0, 4, 8$ modulo $12$). These two quotients are isomorphic, and this is no accident.

### The second isomorphism theorem

For subsets $H, N$ of a group write $HN = \{hn : h \in H, n \in N\}$.

**Theorem 1.** Let $N \trianglelefteq G$ and $H \le G$. Then $HN$ is a subgroup of $G$ containing $N$ as a normal subgroup, $H \cap N$ is a normal subgroup of $H$, and
$$HN/N \cong H/(H \cap N).$$

*Proof.* $HN$ is a subgroup: it contains $e = ee$. For $h, h' \in H$ and $n, n' \in N$, $(hn)(h'n') = hh' \cdot (h'^{-1} n h') n'$, and $h'^{-1}nh' \in N$ by normality, so the product lies in $HN$. Also $(hn)^{-1} = n^{-1}h^{-1} = h^{-1}(hn^{-1}h^{-1}) \in HN$. $N$ is normal in $HN$ because it is normal in $G$. Define $\varphi\colon H \to HN/N$ by $\varphi(h) = hN$. It is a homomorphism, being the restriction of the quotient map $G \to G/N$ to $H$. It is onto: a coset of $HN/N$ has the form $hnN = hN = \varphi(h)$. Its kernel is $\{h \in H : hN = N\} = H \cap N$, which is therefore normal in $H$. The first isomorphism theorem gives $H/(H \cap N) \cong HN/N$. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 2 (product formula).** If $H$ and $N$ are finite subgroups of a group (neither need be normal), then $|HN| = |H||N|/|H \cap N|$, counting $HN$ as a set.

*Proof.* Consider the map $H \times N \to HN$, $(h, n) \mapsto hn$, which is onto. The fiber over $x = h_0 n_0$ consists of the pairs $(h, n)$ with $hn = h_0 n_0$, that is, with $h^{-1}h_0 = n n_0^{-1} \in D = H \cap N$. These pairs are $(h_0 d^{-1}, d n_0)$ for $d \in D$, and distinct $d$ give distinct pairs. So every fiber has $|D|$ elements and $|H||N| = |HN||D|$. $\blacksquare$

### The third isomorphism theorem

**Theorem 3.** Let $N$ and $K$ be normal subgroups of $G$ with $N \le K$. Then $K/N$ is a normal subgroup of $G/N$ and
$$(G/N)/(K/N) \cong G/K.$$

*Proof.* Define $\psi\colon G/N \to G/K$ by $\psi(gN) = gK$. It is well defined: if $gN = g'N$ then $g^{-1}g' \in N \le K$, so $gK = g'K$. It is a homomorphism, since $\psi(gN \cdot g'N) = gg'K = gK \cdot g'K$, and it is onto. Its kernel is $\{gN : g \in K\} = K/N$, which is therefore normal in $G/N$. By the first isomorphism theorem, $(G/N)/(K/N) \cong G/K$. $\blacksquare$

In words: dividing by $N$ and then by $K/N$ is the same as dividing by $K$ at once.

### The correspondence theorem

**Theorem 4.** Let $N \trianglelefteq G$ and $\pi\colon G \to G/N$ the quotient map. The map $H \mapsto H/N = \pi(H)$ is a bijection from the set of subgroups $H$ with $N \le H \le G$ onto the set of subgroups of $G/N$, with inverse $\bar{H} \mapsto \pi^{-1}(\bar{H})$. It preserves inclusion, and $H/N$ is normal in $G/N$ if and only if $H$ is normal in $G$.

*Proof.* The image of a subgroup under a homomorphism is a subgroup. The preimage $\pi^{-1}(\bar{H})$ of a subgroup is a subgroup (closed under products and inverses because $\pi$ is a homomorphism) and contains $\ker\pi = N$. Since $\pi$ is onto, $\pi(\pi^{-1}(\bar H)) = \bar H$. If $N \le H$ then $\pi^{-1}(\pi(H)) = HN = H$, because $gN \in \pi(H)$ means $g \in hN \subseteq H$ for some $h \in H$. So the two maps are inverse to each other. Both maps preserve inclusion, since images and preimages do. For normality: if $H \trianglelefteq G$, then for $g \in G$ and $h \in H$ we get $(gN)(hN)(gN)^{-1} = ghg^{-1}N \in H/N$, so $H/N$ is normal in $G/N$. Conversely, if $H/N$ is normal in $G/N$, then for $g \in G$ and $h \in H$ we have $ghg^{-1}N = (gN)(hN)(gN)^{-1} \in H/N$, so $ghg^{-1} \in \pi^{-1}(H/N) = H$. $\blacksquare$

For $G = \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ and $N = \langle 4 \rangle$, the subgroups of $G$ containing $N$ are $\langle 4 \rangle, \langle 2 \rangle, \langle 1 \rangle$, so $G/N$ (cyclic of order $4$) has exactly three subgroups, of orders $1, 2, 4$.

@example
title: The second isomorphism theorem in $\mathbb{Z}/60\mathbb{Z}$
problem: In $G = \mathbb{Z}/60\mathbb{Z}$ let $H = \langle 10 \rangle$ and $N = \langle 15 \rangle$. Find $HN$ and $H \cap N$ and verify that $|HN/N| = |H/(H \cap N)|$.
step: $|H| = 60/10 = 6$ and $|N| = 60/15 = 4$. Since $G$ is abelian, $N$ is normal and Theorem 1 applies.
step: $HN = H + N$ is generated by $10$ and $15$, so $HN = \langle \gcd(10, 15) \rangle = \langle 5 \rangle$, of order $60/5 = 12$.
step: $H \cap N$ consists of the elements that are multiples of both $10$ and $15$ modulo $60$, that is, the multiples of $\operatorname{lcm}(10, 15) = 30$: $H \cap N = \langle 30 \rangle = \{0, 30\}$, of order $2$.
step: Check the product formula: $|H||N|/|H \cap N| = 6 \cdot 4/2 = 12 = |HN|$. Then $|HN/N| = 12/4 = 3$ and $|H/(H \cap N)| = 6/2 = 3$, in agreement with the theorem.
answer: $HN = \langle 5 \rangle$ has order $12$, $H \cap N = \{0, 30\}$, and both quotients have order $3$ (both are cyclic of order $3$).

@example
title: The third isomorphism theorem in $\mathbb{Z}/60\mathbb{Z}$
problem: In $G = \mathbb{Z}/60\mathbb{Z}$ let $N = \langle 12 \rangle \le K = \langle 6 \rangle$. Identify $(G/N)/(K/N)$.
step: $G$ is abelian, so $N$ and $K$ are normal, and $N \le K$ because $12 = 2 \cdot 6$.
step: By Theorem 3, $(G/N)/(K/N) \cong G/K$.
step: $K = \langle 6 \rangle$ has order $10$, so $G/K$ has order $6$; it is cyclic, generated by $1 + K$ (since $k \in K$ exactly when $6 \mid k$).
step: So $(G/N)/(K/N) \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$. Check by orders: $|G/N| = 60/5 = 12$, $|K/N| = 10/5 = 2$, and $12/2 = 6$.
answer: $(G/N)/(K/N) \cong G/K \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$.

@mistakes
- Assuming $HN$ is always a subgroup. In $S_3$, with $H = \{e, (1\,2)\}$ and $N = \{e, (1\,3)\}$, the set $HN$ has $4$ elements, which does not divide $6$. The product formula counts the set, but $HN$ is guaranteed to be a subgroup only under extra hypotheses, for example when one of $H, N$ is normal.
- Mixing up the roles of $H$ and $N$ in the second isomorphism theorem. The subgroup that gets divided out must be the normal one: $HN/N \cong H/(H \cap N)$ needs $N$ normal, while $HN/H \cong N/(H \cap N)$ would need $H$ normal.
- Believing that $(G/N)/(K/N)$ is $G/N$ with $K$ removed. It is a quotient of $G/N$ and is isomorphic to $G/K$; both $N$ and $K$ must be normal in $G$, with $N \le K$.
- Applying the correspondence theorem to subgroups that do not contain $N$. Only subgroups of $G$ containing $N$ correspond to subgroups of $G/N$.

answer: 12
check: len({(14 * a + 21 * b) % 84 for a in range(84) for b in range(84)})
hint: Find $|H|$, $|N|$ and $|H \cap N|$, then use the product formula.
hint: $H \cap N$ consists of the common multiples of $14$ and $21$, that is, the multiples of $\operatorname{lcm}(14, 21) = 42$.
solution: $|H| = 84/14 = 6$, $|N| = 84/21 = 4$, and $H \cap N = \langle 42 \rangle = \{0, 42\}$ has order $2$. So $|HN| = 6 \cdot 4/2 = 12$ (indeed $HN = \langle \gcd(14, 21) \rangle = \langle 7 \rangle$).

@practice num

answer: 8

@practice mc
prompt: Let $N \trianglelefteq G$ and $H \le G$. Which statement is the second isomorphism theorem?
choice*: $HN/N \cong H/(H \cap N)$
choice: $HN/H \cong N/(H \cap N)$ for any subgroups $H$ and $N$
choice: $G/(H \cap N) \cong G/H \times G/N$
choice: $HN \cong H \times N$
hint: The map $h \mapsto hN$ from $H$ to $HN/N$ is onto with kernel $H \cap N$.
solution: Mapping $H$ onto $HN/N$ by $h \mapsto hN$ has kernel $H \cap N$, so the first isomorphism theorem gives $H/(H \cap N) \cong HN/N$. The statement $HN/H \cong N/(H \cap N)$ would need $H$ normal, and the statements about $G/(H \cap N)$ and $HN \cong H \times N$ are false in general (take $H = N$, so that $HN = H$ is not $H \times H$).

@practice self
prompt: Let $H$ and $N$ be finite subgroups of a group $G$ and put $D = H \cap N$. Prove that $|HN| = |H||N|/|D|$, where $HN = \{hn : h \in H, n \in N\}$ is counted as a set.
hint: Count the pairs $(h, n)$ mapping to a fixed element $x = h_0 n_0$ of $HN$ under $(h, n) \mapsto hn$.
hint: If $hn = h_0 n_0$ then $h^{-1}h_0 = n n_0^{-1}$ lies in both $H$ and $N$.
solution: Consider $f\colon H \times N \to HN$, $f(h, n) = hn$, which is onto. Fix $x = h_0 n_0 \in HN$. If $hn = h_0 n_0$ then $h^{-1}h_0 = n n_0^{-1}$; the left side lies in $H$ and the right side in $N$, so this element is some $d \in D$. Then $h = h_0 d^{-1}$ and $n = d n_0$. Conversely each $d \in D$ gives the pair $(h_0 d^{-1}, d n_0) \in H \times N$ with product $h_0 d^{-1} d n_0 = x$. Distinct $d$ give distinct pairs (the first coordinates $h_0 d^{-1}$ differ). So every fiber of $f$ has exactly $|D|$ elements, and $|H||N| = |H \times N| = |D||HN|$. $\blacksquare$
rubric: Describes the fiber of $(h, n) \mapsto hn$ over $x = h_0 n_0$ as the pairs $(h_0 d^{-1}, d n_0)$ with $d \in H \cap N$.
rubric: Shows that every fiber has exactly $|H \cap N|$ elements (both containments and distinctness).
rubric: Concludes $|H||N| = |HN||H \cap N|$ by counting pairs.

@quiz tuple
prompt: In $G = \mathbb{Z}/72\mathbb{Z}$ let $H = \langle 12 \rangle$ and $N = \langle 18 \rangle$. Give the ordered pair $(|HN|, |H \cap N|)$.
answer: (12, 2)
check: (len({(12 * a + 18 * b) % 72 for a in range(72) for b in range(72)}), len({(12 * a) % 72 for a in range(72)} & {(18 * b) % 72 for b in range(72)}))
hint: $|H| = 6$ and $|N| = 4$. The intersection is the set of common multiples of $12$ and $18$ modulo $72$.
solution: $\operatorname{lcm}(12, 18) = 36$, so $H \cap N = \langle 36 \rangle = \{0, 36\}$ has order $2$. By the product formula $|HN| = 6 \cdot 4/2 = 12$; indeed $HN = \langle \gcd(12, 18) \rangle = \langle 6 \rangle$ has order $72/6 = 12$.

@quiz num
prompt: Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/36\mathbb{Z}$ and $N = \langle 12 \rangle$. How many subgroups of $G$ contain $N$?
answer: 6
check: sum(1 for d in range(1, 37) if 36 % d == 0 and 12 % d == 0)
hint: By the correspondence theorem these correspond to the subgroups of $G/N$, which is cyclic of order $12$.
solution: $G/N$ is cyclic of order $12$, and a cyclic group of order $12$ has exactly one subgroup for each divisor of $12$: the divisors are $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12$, so there are $6$ subgroups. They correspond to the subgroups $\langle d \rangle$ of $G$ with $d \mid 12$.
