## Week-by-week key ideas

**Weeks 1–2: metric spaces.** A metric satisfies positivity, symmetry and the triangle inequality. Open sets are unions of open balls; finite intersections and arbitrary unions of open sets are open. A set is closed iff it contains the limits of its convergent sequences. A space is *complete* if every Cauchy sequence converges: $\mathbb{R}^n$ and $C[0,1]$ with the sup metric are, $\mathbb{Q}$ and $(0,1)$ are not, and a subset of a complete space is complete iff closed.

**Weeks 3–4: compactness and continuity.** Compact means every sequence has a convergent subsequence, equivalently every open cover has a finite subcover. In $\mathbb{R}^n$ it means closed and bounded (Heine–Borel); elsewhere closed and bounded is not enough. Continuous images of compact sets are compact, so continuous functions on compact sets attain extremes and are uniformly continuous. Continuous images of connected sets are connected, which gives the intermediate value theorem.

**Weeks 5–6: uniform convergence.** $f_n \to f$ uniformly if $\sup|f_n - f| \to 0$. Uniform limits of continuous functions are continuous; the M-test gives uniform convergence of series; uniform convergence lets you interchange limits and integrals; for derivatives you need uniform convergence of $f_n'$ plus convergence at a point.

**Weeks 7–8: power series, approximation, compactness in $C(K)$.** A power series converges uniformly on $[-r,r]$ for every $r$ below its radius and can be differentiated termwise. Bernstein polynomials prove the Weierstrass approximation theorem. Arzelà–Ascoli: a uniformly bounded, equicontinuous sequence on a compact space has a uniformly convergent subsequence.

**Weeks 9–10: contractions and derivatives.** A contraction on a complete space has a unique fixed point, reached by iteration with the error bounds $k^n/(1-k)\,d(x_1,x_0)$ and $k/(1-k)\,d(x_n,x_{n-1})$. The derivative of $f\colon\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m$ is the linear map $Df(a)$ with remainder $o(\|h\|)$; continuous partials imply differentiability; the chain rule is $Dg(f(a))Df(a)$; the mean value inequality bounds Lipschitz constants.

**Weeks 11–12: inverse and implicit functions, integrals.** If $f$ is $C^1$ and $Df(a)$ is invertible then $f$ is locally invertible with $C^1$ inverse and $D(f^{-1}) = (Df)^{-1}$. The implicit function theorem gives $Dg = -(D_yF)^{-1}D_xF$. Fubini turns double integrals into iterated ones for continuous functions; the change of variables formula multiplies by $|\det D\Phi|$ (polar coordinates: $r$).

## Key formulas

- Contraction a priori bound: $d(x_n, x^*) \le \dfrac{k^n}{1-k}\,d(x_1,x_0)$.
- Bernstein polynomial: $B_nf(x) = \sum_k f(k/n)\binom nk x^k(1-x)^{n-k}$.
- Implicit derivative: $g'(x) = -F_x/F_y$ (one equation).

## Common errors

Confusing pointwise with uniform convergence; using Heine–Borel outside $\mathbb{R}^n$; checking $d(Tx,Ty) < d(x,y)$ instead of a uniform $k < 1$; forgetting the absolute value of the Jacobian; applying the implicit function theorem with the wrong block of the Jacobian.

prompt: (Dini's theorem) Let $K$ be a compact metric space and let $f_n, f\colon K \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuous with $f_1 \le f_2 \le \cdots \le f$ pointwise and $f_n(x) \to f(x)$ for every $x \in K$. Prove that $f_n \to f$ uniformly on $K$.
hint: Set $g_n = f - f_n \ge 0$ and fix $\varepsilon > 0$. Look at the sets $K_n = \{x : g_n(x) \ge \varepsilon\}$: they are closed, nested, and have empty intersection.
hint: A decreasing sequence of nonempty closed subsets of a compact space has a nonempty intersection: take $x_n \in K_n$ and a convergent subsequence.
solution: Put $g_n = f - f_n$, continuous and, by monotonicity, $g_1 \ge g_2 \ge \cdots \ge 0$ with $g_n \to 0$ pointwise. Fix $\varepsilon > 0$ and let $K_n = \{x \in K : g_n(x) \ge \varepsilon\}$. Each $K_n$ is closed in $K$ (the preimage of the closed set $[\varepsilon, \infty)$ under a continuous map), and $K_{n+1} \subseteq K_n$ because $g_{n+1} \le g_n$. Also $\bigcap_n K_n = \emptyset$: for each $x$, $g_n(x) \to 0$, so $g_n(x) < \varepsilon$ for large $n$. Suppose every $K_n$ were nonempty. Pick $x_n \in K_n$. By compactness a subsequence $x_{n_j} \to p \in K$. For fixed $m$ and $n_j \ge m$ we have $x_{n_j} \in K_{n_j} \subseteq K_m$; since $K_m$ is closed, $p \in K_m$. So $p \in \bigcap_m K_m$, a contradiction. Therefore some $K_N = \emptyset$, so $0 \le g_n(x) < \varepsilon$ for all $x \in K$ and all $n \ge N$ (using $g_n \le g_N$). That is $\sup_K |f - f_n| \le \varepsilon$ for $n \ge N$, hence uniform convergence. $\blacksquare$
