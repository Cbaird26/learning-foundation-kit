@article
A **polynomial** is a function $p(x) = a_n x^n + a_{n-1}x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0$ with $a_n \neq 0$. The number $n$ is the **degree** and $a_n$ is the **leading coefficient**. Polynomials are smooth (no jumps, corners or gaps) and defined for every real $x$.

### End behavior comes from the leading term

For very large $|x|$ the term $a_n x^n$ dwarfs all the others, so the ends of the graph copy $a_n x^n$.

| Degree | Leading coefficient | Left end | Right end |
|---|---|---|---|
| even | positive | up | up |
| even | negative | down | down |
| odd | positive | down | up |
| odd | negative | up | down |

### Zeros and factors

A **zero** of $p$ is a number $c$ with $p(c) = 0$. The **Factor Theorem** says $(x - c)$ is a factor of $p(x)$ exactly when $p(c) = 0$. The **Remainder Theorem** says the remainder on dividing $p(x)$ by $x - c$ is $p(c)$.

To hunt for zeros of a polynomial with integer coefficients, use the **rational root test**: any rational zero $r/s$ in lowest terms has $r$ dividing the constant term $a_0$ and $s$ dividing the leading coefficient $a_n$. Test the candidates, then divide out the factor you find.

### Multiplicity: how the graph meets the axis

If $(x - c)^m$ is a factor, with no more copies of $(x-c)$, then $c$ is a zero of **multiplicity** $m$.

- **Odd** $m$: the graph **crosses** the axis at $c$. For $m \ge 3$ it flattens as it crosses.
- **Even** $m$: the graph **touches** the axis at $c$ and turns back without crossing.

A degree-$n$ polynomial has at most $n$ real zeros (counting multiplicity) and at most $n - 1$ turning points.

### A sketching recipe

1. End behavior from the leading term.
2. Zeros and their multiplicities from the factored form.
3. The $y$-intercept $p(0)$, which is the constant term $a_0$.
4. The sign of $p$ on each interval between zeros (test one point per interval).
5. Join the pieces with a smooth curve, crossing or touching as the multiplicities say.

A factored form fixes a polynomial only up to a constant multiplier: $a(x+1)^2(x-2)$ has the same zeros for every $a \neq 0$. One extra point pins down $a$.

@example
title: Analyze a polynomial from its factored form
problem: Describe the graph of $p(x) = -(x+2)(x-1)^2(x-3)$ without a graphing tool.
step: The degree is $1 + 2 + 1 = 4$ (even) and the leading coefficient is $-1$. Both ends fall.
step: Zeros: $x = -2$ (multiplicity 1, crosses), $x = 1$ (multiplicity 2, touches and turns), $x = 3$ (multiplicity 1, crosses).
step: The $y$-intercept is $p(0) = -(2)(1)(-3) = 6$.
step: Test signs: $p(-3) = -96 < 0$, $p(0) = 6 > 0$, $p(2) = 4 > 0$, $p(4) = -54 < 0$.
step: Sketch: start low on the left, cross up through $-2$, stay positive, touch $0$ at $x = 1$ and bounce back up, cross down through $3$, and fall. That is $3$ turning points, which equals $n - 1$.

@example
title: Build a polynomial from its description
problem: A cubic has a double zero at $x = -1$, a simple zero at $x = 2$, and $y$-intercept $4$. Find $p(x)$.
step: Write the factored form with an unknown constant: $p(x) = a(x+1)^2(x-2)$.
step: Use the $y$-intercept: $p(0) = a(1)^2(-2) = -2a = 4$, so $a = -2$.
step: Therefore $p(x) = -2(x+1)^2(x-2)$, which expands to $-2x^3 + 6x + 4$.
step: Sanity check: odd degree with a negative leading coefficient rises on the left and falls on the right, which fits touching at $-1$ and crossing at $2$.

@mistakes
- Assuming every zero is a crossing. An even-multiplicity zero touches the axis and turns back.
- Reading end behavior from the constant term or from the factor that looks biggest, instead of the leading term of the whole product.
- Writing the factored form without the constant $a$ and then being unable to match the $y$-intercept.
- Saying a degree-$n$ polynomial has $n$ turning points. The most it can have is $n - 1$.

choice*: Rises on the left, falls on the right

choice: Falls on both ends
choice: Falls on the left, rises on the right
hint: Only the leading term $-3x^5$ matters for large $|x|$.
hint: Odd power: the two ends go in opposite directions. The coefficient is negative, which flips the usual rising-to-the-right shape.
solution: The degree $5$ is odd and the leading coefficient $-3$ is negative. As $x \to +\infty$, $-3x^5 \to -\infty$. As $x \to -\infty$, $x^5$ is large and negative, so $-3x^5 \to +\infty$.

@practice num
prompt: Find the value of $k$ for which $x - 2$ is a factor of $x^3 + kx^2 - 4x + 4$.
answer: -1
check: root(lambda k: 2**3 + k*2**2 - 4*2 + 4, -100, 100)
hint: By the Factor Theorem, $x - 2$ is a factor exactly when the polynomial is $0$ at $x = 2$.
hint: Substitute $x = 2$ and set the result equal to $0$: $8 + 4k - 8 + 4 = 0$.
solution: $p(2) = 8 + 4k - 8 + 4 = 4k + 4 = 0$, so $k = -1$. Check: $x^3 - x^2 - 4x + 4 = (x-1)(x-2)(x+2)$.

@practice num
prompt: How many of the zeros of $p(x) = (x-3)^2(x+1)^3(x-2)$ are points where the graph actually crosses the $x$-axis (rather than only touching it)?
answer: 2
check: (lambda p: sum(1 for r in [-1, 2, 3] if p(r - 0.01) * p(r + 0.01) < 0))(lambda x: (x - 3)**2 * (x + 1)**3 * (x - 2))
hint: Odd multiplicity means crossing; even multiplicity means touching.
hint: List the multiplicities: $3$ has multiplicity $2$, $-1$ has multiplicity $3$, and $2$ has multiplicity $1$.
solution: The zero at $3$ has even multiplicity $2$, so the graph only touches there. The zeros at $-1$ (multiplicity $3$) and $2$ (multiplicity $1$) are odd, so the graph crosses at both. Answer: $2$.

@practice num
prompt: A quartic has the form $p(x) = a(x-1)^2(x+2)(x-3)$ and its graph passes through $(0, 12)$. Find $a$.
answer: -2
check: root(lambda a: a*(0 - 1)**2*(0 + 2)*(0 - 3) - 12, -100, 100)
hint: The point $(0, 12)$ means $p(0) = 12$.
hint: Evaluate the three factors at $x = 0$ and multiply them: you get $-6a = 12$.
solution: $p(0) = a(-1)^2(2)(-3) = -6a = 12$, so $a = -2$. The graph then falls on both ends (even degree, negative leading coefficient).

@practice list
prompt: Find all real zeros of $p(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 11x + 6$. Enter them separated by commas.
answer: -2, 1/2, 3
check: allroots(lambda x: 2*x**3 - 3*x**2 - 11*x + 6, -10, 10)
hint: Rational root test: a zero $r/s$ has $r$ dividing $6$ and $s$ dividing $2$. Try small integers first.
hint: Test $x = 3$. If it is a zero, divide the cubic by $x - 3$ to leave a quadratic you can factor.
solution: $p(3) = 54 - 27 - 33 + 6 = 0$, so $x - 3$ is a factor and $p(x) = (x-3)(2x^2 + 3x - 2) = (x-3)(2x-1)(x+2)$. The zeros are $3$, $\tfrac12$ and $-2$.

@quiz num
prompt: Find the remainder when $x^4 - 3x^2 + 2x - 5$ is divided by $x - 2$.
answer: 3
check: np.polydiv([1, 0, -3, 2, -5], [1, -2])[1][-1]
hint: You do not need long division. The Remainder Theorem gives the remainder directly.
hint: The remainder is the value of the polynomial at $x = 2$.
solution: By the Remainder Theorem the remainder is $p(2) = 16 - 12 + 4 - 5 = 3$.

@quiz ms
prompt: Let $p(x) = -(x-1)^3(x+2)^2$. Select every true statement.
choice*: The graph touches the $x$-axis at $x = -2$ without crossing it.
choice*: The graph crosses the $x$-axis at $x = 1$ and flattens there.
choice*: The $y$-intercept is $4$.
choice: The graph falls on the far left.
choice: The degree of $p$ is $3$.
hint: Expand only the leading term: $-(x)^3(x)^2$. What are its degree and sign?
hint: Compare the multiplicities $3$ and $2$, and evaluate $p(0)$ directly.
solution: The degree is $3 + 2 = 5$ and the leading term is $-x^5$, so the graph rises on the far left and falls on the far right. The zero $-2$ has even multiplicity (touch) and $1$ has odd multiplicity $3$ (crosses, flattened). $p(0) = -(-1)^3(2)^2 = 4$.
