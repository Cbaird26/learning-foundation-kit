@project
title: Function portfolio: one rational and one trigonometric function
overview: You will analyze one rational function and one trigonometric function completely by hand, then check your work with a graphing tool and compare the two against the other function families on your comparison sheet. The portfolio shows that you can choose and combine algebraic, graphical and trigonometric representations before starting calculus.
goal: Analyze a rational function by hand: domain, holes, intercepts, vertical, horizontal or slant asymptotes, sign chart and sketch.
goal: Analyze a transformed sine or cosine by hand: amplitude, period, phase shift, midline, key points and the sequence of transformations from the parent graph.
goal: Compare both functions with the other families using secant slopes and explain in words what a limiting process means.
step: Choose your functions. The rational function needs numerator and denominator of degree at least 2, one hole and at least one vertical asymptote. The trigonometric function needs at least three transformations of the parent graph, such as a stretch, a period change, a shift and a vertical shift. If you want a starting pair, use $r(x) = \dfrac{2x^2 - 8}{x^2 - x - 6}$ and $g(x) = 2\sin\!\left(3x - \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) - 1$.
step: Rational function by hand. Factor everything. Write the domain, the location and height of each hole, the intercepts, each vertical asymptote with the sign of $r(x)$ on both sides, and the horizontal or slant asymptote. Test whether the graph crosses its horizontal asymptote. Build a sign chart and sketch the graph on paper.
step: Trigonometric function by hand. Factor the coefficient of $x$ out of the argument to get $A\sin(B(x - C)) + D$. List the transformations in the order they are applied to $y = \sin x$, find the five key points of one period, and find the $x$-intercepts on one period. Sketch it.
step: Check with technology. Graph both functions and compare with your sketches. For every disagreement decide whether you made an error or the tool hid something (many tools do not show a hole). Record what you changed and why.
step: Secant slopes. For each function choose an interval and compute the average rate of change with units. For one function, compute secant slopes from a fixed point for $h = 1,\ 0.1,\ 0.01$ and simplify the difference quotient. Explain what number the slopes approach and what that number would mean.
step: Finish the comparison sheet. Add rows for your two functions beside linear, quadratic, polynomial, exponential, logarithmic and radical. For each row give formula, domain, range, intercepts, end behavior and the situation it models best. Add an inverse-pairs entry.
step: Write and rehearse. Write a one-page summary in plain language of what each function does and why. Then explain one major result aloud, without notes, to your tutor or to a recording.
deliverable: Hand-worked analysis pages for both functions with every step of algebra shown, sign chart and sketches.
deliverable: The completed comparison sheet with at least eight families and an inverse-pairs entry.
deliverable: A one-page write-up that includes the technology check, the secant-slope table and the explanation of the limiting process.
deliverable: A spoken explanation of one major result, given without notes.
rubric: The rational analysis is correct and complete: factored form, domain, holes versus vertical asymptotes, intercepts, end behavior and a sign chart that matches the sketch.
rubric: The trigonometric analysis is correct: amplitude, period, phase shift (from the factored form), midline and key points agree with the graph.
rubric: Transformations are listed in a valid order and checked with at least one point of the final graph.
rubric: Every disagreement between hand and technology is identified and explained, not just erased.
rubric: Average rates and secant slopes are computed correctly with units, and the limiting process is explained in words without setting $h = 0$ too early.

@review
Use this guide to plan your review, then try the self-check list without notes.

### Weeks 1-2: families, composition, inverses
- Fingerprints: constant first differences (linear), constant second differences (quadratic), constant ratios (exponential). Domains exclude zero denominators, negative numbers under even roots and non-positive logarithm inputs.
- $(f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x))$: the inner function acts first, and the domain needs $x$ in the domain of $g$ and $g(x)$ in the domain of $f$.
- Inverses: swap $x$ and $y$ and solve; domain and range trade places; the graph reflects in $y = x$; restrict the domain if $f$ is not one-to-one.

### Weeks 3-4: polynomials and rational functions
- The leading term sets end behavior. Odd multiplicity crosses the axis, even multiplicity touches it. At most $n$ zeros and $n - 1$ turning points. Tools: Factor Theorem, Remainder Theorem, rational root test.
- Rational functions: factor first. A canceled factor gives a hole; a surviving denominator factor gives a vertical asymptote. Compare degrees for horizontal or slant asymptotes. A graph may cross its horizontal asymptote.

### Weeks 5-6: exponentials and logarithms
- $f(x) = ab^x$ with $b = 1 + r$; half-life; $P(1 + r/n)^{nt}$ and $Pe^{rt}$; two-point fit $b = (y_2/y_1)^{1/(x_2 - x_1)}$.
- $\log_b x = y \iff b^y = x$; the product, quotient, power and change-of-base rules; $t = \ln(c/a)/\ln b$ solves $ab^t = c$; fit data with a line through $(x, \ln y)$ and check units and plausible ranges.

### Weeks 7-8: trigonometry and conics
- Radians, the unit circle, reference angles, quadrant signs; $y = A\sin(B(x - C)) + D$; ranges of the inverse trig functions; list every solution in the interval.
- Complete the square to reach standard form. Ellipse $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$; hyperbola $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$ with asymptote slopes $\pm\frac{b}{a}$; a parabola's vertex is halfway between focus and directrix.

### Weeks 9-10: sequences and series
- $a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d$ and $a_n = a_1 r^{n-1}$; explicit versus recursive forms.
- $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(a_1 + a_n)$, $S_n = a\frac{1 - r^n}{1 - r}$, and $\frac{a}{1 - r}$ when $|r| < 1$; telescoping; test every formula at $n = 1, 2, 3$.

### Weeks 11-12: rates and limits
- The average rate $\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$ is a secant slope, with units. Cancel $h$ in difference quotients.
- A limit describes behavior near a point: check both sides, treat $\frac00$ as "simplify first", and remember that the derivative is the limit of secant slopes.

### Self-check
- Classify a table as linear, quadratic or exponential and write its formula.
- Find the domain of a composite and the inverse of a rational function.
- Sketch a factored polynomial and a rational function with no graphing tool.
- Turn a percent rate into a factor and solve for the time to reach a target.
- Read the four parameters of a sine graph, and identify a conic by completing the square.
- Sum arithmetic and geometric series, and explain a limit in your own words.

### Most common errors
- Off-by-one errors with $n - 1$ and with counting terms of a sum.
- Canceling terms instead of factors; calling a hole an asymptote.
- Using the rate instead of the factor $1 + r$; mixing time units.
- Reading a phase shift before factoring out $B$; dropping the multiplier when completing the square.
- Setting $h = 0$ before simplifying a difference quotient.

prompt: Let $f(x) = 5e^{0.3x}$ and $g(x) = 2x - 1$. Solve $(f \circ g)(x) = 40$. Give $x$ to three decimal places.
answer: 3.966
check: root(lambda x: 5*exp(0.3*(2*x - 1)) - 40, -10, 10)
tol: 0.001
hint: Write $(f \circ g)(x) = f(2x - 1) = 5e^{0.3(2x - 1)}$.
hint: Divide both sides by $5$ to isolate the exponential, then take the natural logarithm of both sides.
solution: $5e^{0.3(2x-1)} = 40$ gives $e^{0.6x - 0.3} = 8$. Taking logs, $0.6x - 0.3 = \ln 8 \approx 2.0794$, so $x = \dfrac{2.0794 + 0.3}{0.6} \approx 3.966$. Check: $2(3.966) - 1 = 6.932$ and $5e^{2.0796} \approx 40$.

@exam tuple
prompt: Let $r(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 - x - 6}$. Find, in order: (a) the $x$-value of the vertical asymptote; (b) the $y$-coordinate of the hole; (c) the height of the horizontal asymptote; (d) the average rate of change of $r$ on $[0, 1]$. Enter $(a, b, c, d)$ as exact values.
answer: (-2, 6/5, 1, -1/6)
check: (lambda r: (root(lambda x: x**2 - x - 6, -2.5, -1.5), lim(r, 3), r(1e9), (r(1) - r(0))/(1 - 0)))(lambda x: (x**2 - 9)/(x**2 - x - 6))
tol: 0.001
hint: Factor the numerator and the denominator completely and cancel the common factor.
hint: The numerator is $(x-3)(x+3)$ and the denominator is $(x-3)(x+2)$. For (d), evaluate the original function at $0$ and $1$.
solution: $r(x) = \dfrac{(x-3)(x+3)}{(x-3)(x+2)}$. The factor $x - 3$ cancels, so there is a hole at $x = 3$ with height $\dfrac{3 + 3}{3 + 2} = \dfrac65$. The surviving factor $x + 2$ gives the vertical asymptote $x = -2$. Degrees are equal with leading coefficients $1$ and $1$, so the horizontal asymptote is $y = 1$. Finally $r(0) = \dfrac{-9}{-6} = \dfrac32$ and $r(1) = \dfrac{-8}{-6} = \dfrac43$, so the average rate is $\dfrac{4/3 - 3/2}{1} = -\dfrac16$.

@exam self
prompt: Savings plan (assumed numbers). You deposit \$100 at the end of every month for 5 years into an account paying 6% per year, compounded monthly. (a) Show that the final balance is a finite geometric series and identify its first term, ratio and number of terms. (b) Use the geometric sum formula to find the final balance to the nearest cent. (c) The balance after 48 months is \$5409.78. Find the average rate of change of the balance over the last year, with units, and explain why it is more than \$100 per month. (d) Which function family models the value of one \$100 deposit as a function of the time it has spent in the account, and what is its growth factor per month?
hint: The monthly rate is $0.06/12 = 0.005$. A deposit made at the end of month $m$ earns interest for $60 - m$ months.
hint: List the deposit values in reverse order of age: $100,\ 100(1.005),\ 100(1.005)^2,\ \dots$
solution: (a) The deposit made at the end of month $m$ ($m = 1, \dots, 60$) grows for $60 - m$ months and is worth $100(1.005)^{60-m}$. Adding all of them gives $100 + 100(1.005) + \dots + 100(1.005)^{59}$, a geometric series with first term $100$, ratio $1.005$ and $60$ terms. (b) $S = 100\cdot\dfrac{1.005^{60} - 1}{1.005 - 1} = 100\cdot\dfrac{0.34885}{0.005} \approx 6977.00$ dollars. (c) Average rate $= \dfrac{6977.00 - 5409.78}{12} \approx 130.60$ dollars per month. It is more than $100$ because each month the balance gains the new deposit plus interest on the existing balance, which is about $0.5\%$ of $5400$ to $7000$, or roughly $27$ to $35$ dollars. (d) One deposit grows exponentially: $V(t) = 100(1.005)^t$ with $t$ in months, so the growth factor is $1.005$ per month, a $0.5\%$ monthly increase.
rubric: Identifies a geometric series with first term $100$, ratio $1.005$ and $60$ terms, and explains why the deposits have different ages.
rubric: Applies $S_n = a\frac{1 - r^n}{1 - r}$ correctly and obtains about $6977.00$ dollars.
rubric: Computes the average rate of about $130.60$ dollars per month with units and explains the role of interest.
rubric: Names the exponential family and the monthly growth factor $1.005$.
