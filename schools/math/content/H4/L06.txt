@article
The **logarithm** answers the question "what exponent do I need?" For $b > 0$, $b \neq 1$ and $x > 0$,

$$\log_b x = y \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b^{\,y} = x.$$

So $\log_2 32 = 5$ because $2^5 = 32$. Convention in this course: $\log x$ means base $10$ and $\ln x$ means base $e$.

### The inverse relationship

Logarithms and exponentials undo each other: $\log_b(b^x) = x$ for every real $x$, and $b^{\log_b x} = x$ for $x > 0$. So the graph of $y = \log_b x$ is the reflection of $y = b^x$ in the line $y = x$. It has domain $(0, \infty)$, range all reals, a vertical asymptote at $x = 0$, and passes through $(1, 0)$.

### Rules

For positive $M$ and $N$:

$$\log_b(MN) = \log_b M + \log_b N, \qquad \log_b\frac{M}{N} = \log_b M - \log_b N,$$

$$\log_b(M^p) = p\,\log_b M, \qquad \log_b x = \frac{\ln x}{\ln b}.$$

The last one (change of base) is how a calculator evaluates $\log_3 20$. Note what is missing: there is **no** rule for $\log(M + N)$.

### Solving exponential equations

Isolate the exponential, take a logarithm of both sides, and use the power rule to bring the exponent down. From $a\,b^t = c$:

$$b^t = \frac{c}{a} \;\Longrightarrow\; t = \frac{\ln(c/a)}{\ln b}.$$

Setting $c/a = 2$ gives the **doubling time** $T_d = \dfrac{\ln 2}{\ln b}$.

### Estimating parameters: take logs of the data

If $y = ab^x$, then $\ln y = \ln a + (\ln b)\,x$. This is a straight line in the variables $(x, \ln y)$ with slope $\ln b$ and intercept $\ln a$. So:

1. Compute $\ln y$ for every data point.
2. Check that the slopes between neighboring points are nearly equal. If not, the data are not exponential.
3. Read off the slope $k = \ln b$ and the intercept $\ln a$ (or use two far-apart points).
4. Write $y = a\,e^{kx}$ or $y = ab^x$ with $b = e^k$.

### Check units and plausible ranges

A fitted model is only useful if it makes physical sense. Before trusting one, ask:

- **Units:** is $k$ in "per hour" while your time is in minutes?
- **Initial value:** does the model reproduce the measured starting value?
- **Range:** what does it predict far outside the data? A model that predicts a billion people in a small town, or a negative mass, is valid only on a limited range.

Logarithmic scales also compress huge ranges. On a base-10 scale such as pH, one unit is a factor of $10$.

@example
title: Solve an exponential equation
problem: How many years does it take an investment growing at $8\%$ per year to quadruple? Solve $1.08^t = 4$.
step: The unknown is in the exponent, so take the natural logarithm of both sides: $\ln(1.08^t) = \ln 4$.
step: Apply the power rule: $t \ln 1.08 = \ln 4$.
step: Divide: $t = \dfrac{\ln 4}{\ln 1.08} \approx \dfrac{1.38629}{0.07696}$.
step: So $t \approx 18.01$ years. Check: $1.08^{18} \approx 3.996$, which is very close to $4$.

@example
title: Estimate parameters and test plausibility
problem: Simulated data for a town's population (in thousands): at $t = 0, 2, 4, 6$ years the values are $3.0, 4.3, 6.2, 9.0$. Fit $N(t) = ab^t$ and comment on the model.
step: Take logs: $\ln N \approx 1.099,\ 1.459,\ 1.825,\ 2.197$.
step: Slopes per $2$ years, divided by $2$: $0.180,\ 0.183,\ 0.186$ per year. They are nearly constant, so an exponential model is reasonable.
step: Use the end points for the slope: $k = \dfrac{2.197 - 1.099}{6} \approx 0.183$ per year, so $b = e^{0.183} \approx 1.20$. The intercept gives $a = 3.0$ thousand.
step: The model is $N(t) = 3.0\,(1.20)^t$ thousand people, with $t$ in years. The doubling time is $\ln 2 / 0.183 \approx 3.8$ years.
step: Plausibility: the model matches the data (it gives $6.2$ at $t = 4$). But it predicts $3.0 \cdot 1.2^{40} \approx 4400$ thousand, which is $4.4$ million people after $40$ years. That is implausible for a town, so use the model only for the observed range and short extrapolations.

@mistakes
- Writing $\log(a + b) = \log a + \log b$. The product rule is for $\log(ab)$, and there is no rule for a sum.
- Taking the logarithm of a quantity that is zero or negative, or keeping a candidate solution that makes a logarithm's input non-positive.
- Mis-bracketing $\dfrac{\ln x}{\ln b}$: it is not $\ln\dfrac{x}{b}$.
- Reporting a fitted rate without units, or using a rate per month with a time measured in years.

@practice num
prompt: Evaluate $\log_2 32 + \log_5 \dfrac{1}{25}$.
answer: 3
check: math.log(32, 2) + math.log(1/25, 5)
hint: Ask for each term: "what power of the base gives this number?"
hint: $2^5 = 32$, and $5^{-2} = \frac{1}{25}$.
solution: $\log_2 32 = 5$ and $\log_5 \frac{1}{25} = -2$, so the sum is $5 + (-2) = 3$.

@practice num
prompt: Suppose $\ln a = 2$ and $\ln b = 3$. Find $\ln\!\left(\dfrac{a^3}{\sqrt{b}}\right)$.
answer: 9/2
check: math.log((math.e**2)**3 / math.sqrt(math.e**3))
hint: Use the quotient rule, then the power rule on each part. Remember $\sqrt{b} = b^{1/2}$.
hint: $\ln\frac{a^3}{b^{1/2}} = 3\ln a - \frac12 \ln b$.
solution: $\ln\frac{a^3}{\sqrt b} = 3\ln a - \tfrac12\ln b = 3(2) - \tfrac12(3) = 6 - 1.5 = 4.5 = \tfrac92$.

@practice num
prompt: Solve $3^{2x-1} = 20$ for $x$. Give your answer to three decimal places.
answer: 1.863
check: root(lambda x: 3**(2*x - 1) - 20, -10, 10)
tol: 0.001
hint: Take the natural log of both sides and bring the exponent down.
hint: $(2x - 1)\ln 3 = \ln 20$. Solve for $2x - 1$ first, then for $x$.
solution: $2x - 1 = \frac{\ln 20}{\ln 3} \approx 2.7268$, so $2x \approx 3.7268$ and $x \approx 1.863$. Check: $3^{2.7268} \approx 20$.

@practice mc
prompt: For all positive numbers $a$ and $b$, which statement is true?
choice*: $\ln(ab) = \ln a + \ln b$
choice: $\ln(a + b) = \ln a + \ln b$
choice: $\dfrac{\ln a}{\ln b} = \ln(a - b)$
choice: $(\ln a)^2 = 2\ln a$
hint: Test each statement with simple numbers such as $a = b = e$ or $a = 1,\ b = e$.
hint: Which rule turns a product inside the log into a sum outside it?
solution: The product rule gives $\ln(ab) = \ln a + \ln b$. With $a = b = e$: $\ln(2e) \neq 2$, so the statement $\ln(a + b) = \ln a + \ln b$ fails; $\frac{\ln a}{\ln b}$ is not a log of a difference; and $(\ln a)^2 \neq 2\ln a$ in general, since the power rule applies to an exponent inside the log, not to a power of the log.

@practice self
prompt: Deliverable practice (simulated data). A detector records counts per minute from a decaying sample: at $t = 0, 10, 20, 30$ minutes the counts are $800, 490, 300, 185$. (a) Take logs and decide whether an exponential model fits. (b) Estimate $C(t) = C_0 e^{-kt}$, giving $C_0$ and $k$ with units. (c) Find the half-life. (d) Predict the count at $t = 60$ min and say whether the prediction is plausible and over what range you would trust the model.
hint: Compute $\ln C$ at each time and look at the slope between neighboring points. Divide by the $10$-minute step.
hint: Use the first and last points for the slope $-k$. Then the half-life is $\ln 2 / k$.
solution: (a) $\ln C \approx 6.685,\ 6.194,\ 5.704,\ 5.220$. The slopes per minute are $-0.0490,\ -0.0491,\ -0.0483$, nearly constant, so an exponential model fits. (b) Using the end points, $k = \dfrac{6.685 - 5.220}{30} \approx 0.0488$ per minute, and $C_0 = 800$ counts per minute. So $C(t) = 800\,e^{-0.0488t}$ with $t$ in minutes. (c) Half-life $= \ln 2 / 0.0488 \approx 14.2$ minutes. (d) $C(60) = 800 e^{-2.93} \approx 43$ counts per minute. This is plausible as a prediction within a few half-lives, but a real detector has a constant background count, so the model overestimates the decay at late times and should be trusted only over the measured range and a short extension.

rubric: Gives $k \approx 0.049$ per minute and $C_0 = 800$ counts per minute, with units.
rubric: Computes the half-life as $\ln 2 / k \approx 14$ minutes.

@quiz num
prompt: A quantity grows by $7\%$ per year. How many years does it take to double? Round to two decimal places.
answer: 10.24
check: root(lambda t: 1.07**t - 2, 0, 100)
tol: 0.01
unit: years
hint: Solve $1.07^t = 2$.
hint: Take the logarithm of both sides: $t \ln 1.07 = \ln 2$.
solution: $t = \dfrac{\ln 2}{\ln 1.07} \approx \dfrac{0.69315}{0.06766} \approx 10.24$ years.
