@article
Offspring resemble their parents because parents pass on **genes**, segments of DNA that code for traits. Different versions of a gene are **alleles**. The combination of alleles is the **genotype**; the observable trait is the **phenotype**. An organism with two identical alleles is **homozygous**; with two different alleles it is **heterozygous**. A **dominant** allele shows its phenotype when present in one copy; a **recessive** allele shows only when homozygous.

### Mendel's laws

1. **Law of segregation:** each parent has two alleles for a gene, and they separate in gamete formation so each gamete carries one (this is what homologs do in meiosis I).
2. **Law of independent assortment:** alleles of genes on different chromosomes (or far apart on one) assort independently.

### Monohybrid crosses

For pea height with $T$ (tall, dominant) and $t$ (short): $Tt \times Tt$ gives $\tfrac14 TT$, $\tfrac12 Tt$, $\tfrac14 tt$: a **3 : 1** phenotype ratio and a 1 : 2 : 1 genotype ratio. A **test cross** crosses an unknown dominant-phenotype individual with a homozygous recessive ($tt$). If all offspring show the dominant trait, the unknown is likely $TT$; a 1 : 1 ratio shows it is $Tt$.

### Probability rules

- **Product rule:** the probability of independent events both happening is the product. A dihybrid cross $AaBb \times AaBb$ gives $P(A\_) = 3/4$, $P(B\_) = 3/4$, so $P(A\_ B\_) = 9/16$. The full phenotype ratio is **9 : 3 : 3 : 1**.
- **Sum rule:** the probability of either of two mutually exclusive events is the sum.
- **Binomial:** probability of exactly $k$ of $n$ children showing a trait with probability $p$ each is $\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$.

Expected counts equal probability times sample size; real data deviate by chance, which a **chi-square test** can evaluate.

### Beyond simple dominance

- **Incomplete dominance:** heterozygote is intermediate (red $\times$ white snapdragon gives pink; 1 : 2 : 1 phenotype ratio).
- **Codominance:** both alleles are expressed (human AB blood type: $I^A$ and $I^B$).
- **Multiple alleles:** more than two alleles in the population (ABO has $I^A$, $I^B$, $i$).
- **Polygenic traits** (height, skin color) are controlled by many genes and show continuous variation.
- **Linked genes** lie close together on the same chromosome and tend to be inherited together unless crossing over separates them.
- **Sex-linked traits:** genes on the X chromosome. Males ($XY$) have one X, so a recessive X-linked allele (red-green color blindness, hemophilia A) is expressed in males more often. A carrier mother ($X^AX^a$) and unaffected father ($X^AY$) have a 50% chance that each son is affected and each daughter is a carrier or unaffected, never affected.

@example
title: A dihybrid cross using the product rule
problem: In pea plants, round seeds ($R$) are dominant to wrinkled ($r$) and yellow ($Y$) to green ($y$). In a cross $RrYy \times RrYy$, what fraction of offspring are round and yellow?
step: Treat the genes separately. For $Rr \times Rr$, $P(\text{round}) = 3/4$.
step: For $Yy \times Yy$, $P(\text{yellow}) = 3/4$.
step: The genes assort independently, so multiply: $3/4 \times 3/4 = 9/16$.
step: This matches the 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratio, in which 9 of 16 are round and yellow.

@mistakes
- Treating the 3 : 1 ratio as a guarantee; each child has the same independent chance regardless of earlier children.
- Calling a trait dominant because it is common; dominance describes expression, not frequency.
- Adding probabilities for events that must both occur; use the product rule for "and".
- Thinking X-linked recessive traits can pass from father to son; sons get their father's Y.

@practice mc
prompt: A heterozygous tall pea plant ($Tt$) is crossed with a short plant ($tt$). What phenotype ratio is expected?
choice: All tall
choice: 3 tall : 1 short
choice*: 1 tall : 1 short
choice: 1 tall : 3 short
hint: List the gametes from each parent.
solution: $Tt$ gives $T$ or $t$ equally; $tt$ gives only $t$. Offspring are half $Tt$ (tall) and half $tt$ (short).

@practice num
prompt: Two heterozygous carriers of a recessive condition ($Aa \times Aa$) have a child. What is the probability that the child is affected ($aa$)? Give a fraction.
answer: 1/4
check: 0.5 * 0.5
hint: Each parent passes $a$ with probability 1/2.
solution: $\tfrac12 \times \tfrac12 = \tfrac14$.

@practice num
prompt: In a cross $AaBb \times AaBb$ (independent assortment, complete dominance), what is the probability of an offspring with the dominant phenotype for $A$ and the recessive phenotype for $b$? Give a fraction.
answer: 3/16
check: (3/4) * (1/4)
hint: Treat each gene separately and multiply.
solution: $P(A\_) = 3/4$ and $P(bb) = 1/4$, so $3/16$.

@practice num

answer: 120
check: 160 * 3 / 4

solution: $\tfrac34 \times 160 = 120$.

@practice num
prompt: Two carriers of a recessive allele ($Aa \times Aa$) have 4 children. What is the probability that exactly 1 child is affected ($aa$)? Give a fraction.
answer: 27/64
check: comb(4, 1) * (1/4) * (3/4)**3
hint: Use the binomial formula with $p = 1/4$, $n = 4$ and $k = 1$.
hint: There are 4 ways to choose which child is affected.
solution: $\binom41 (1/4)(3/4)^3 = 4 \times 27/256 = 27/64$.

@quiz num
prompt: In a cross $AaBbCc \times AaBbCc$ with independent assortment, what is the probability of offspring that are $aabbcc$? Give a fraction.
answer: 1/64
check: (1/4) ** 3
hint: Find the probability for one gene, then multiply across three genes.
solution: Each gene gives $aa$ with probability 1/4, so $(1/4)^3 = 1/64$.

@quiz mc
prompt: A woman who is a carrier of X-linked recessive hemophilia A has children with an unaffected man. Which outcome is expected?
choice: All sons are affected
choice: All daughters are affected
choice*: Each son has a 50% chance of being affected and no daughter is affected
choice: No children can be affected or carriers
hint: Write the mother's genotype as $X^AX^a$ and the father's as $X^AY$.
solution: Sons receive the mother's $X^A$ or $X^a$ with equal probability, so 50% are affected. Daughters always receive the father's $X^A$, so none is affected (half are carriers).
