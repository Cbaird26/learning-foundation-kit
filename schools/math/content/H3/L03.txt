@article
Here is the unit circle in motion. As the angle $x$ grows from $0$, the point $(\cos x, \sin x)$ runs around the circle, and its height is $\sin x$. Plot that height against the angle and you have the graph of sine: a smooth wave that repeats every full turn.

### The two basic waves

Reading the coordinates at the key angles gives one period of each function:

| $x$ | $0$ | $\pi/2$ | $\pi$ | $3\pi/2$ | $2\pi$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\sin x$ | $0$ | $1$ | $0$ | $-1$ | $0$ |
| $\cos x$ | $1$ | $0$ | $-1$ | $0$ | $1$ |

Both functions have **domain** all real numbers, **range** $[-1, 1]$ and **period** $2\pi$, meaning $f(x + 2\pi) = f(x)$. The cosine graph is the sine graph slid left by $\frac{\pi}{2}$, because $\cos x = \sin\left(x + \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$.

### Transformations: amplitude, period, shift

Almost every oscillation you will meet is a **sinusoid**:

$$y = A\sin\bigl(B(x - C)\bigr) + D \qquad (B > 0).$$

Each constant does one visible thing:

- **Amplitude** $|A|$: the distance from the midline to a peak. A negative $A$ also flips the wave upside down.
- **Period** $\dfrac{2\pi}{B}$: the length of one cycle. A larger $B$ squeezes more cycles into $2\pi$.
- **Phase shift** $C$: the whole wave slides right by $C$ (left if $C < 0$).
- **Midline** $y = D$: the wave oscillates between $D - |A|$ and $D + |A|$.

The same description works for cosine. **Always factor $B$ out first.** The wave $y = \sin(2x - \pi)$ equals $\sin\bigl(2(x - \frac{\pi}{2})\bigr)$, so its shift is $\frac{\pi}{2}$, not $\pi$.

### Sketching in four moves

1. Draw the midline and the lines for the maximum and the minimum.
2. Compute the period $2\pi/B$ and mark the start of a cycle at $x = C$.
3. Divide the period into four equal quarters. These five $x$-values are where the wave is on the midline, at a peak or at a trough.
4. Plot the five points and join them with a smooth curve.

### Tangent

Since $\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$, the tangent graph has a **vertical asymptote** wherever $\cos x = 0$, that is at $x = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. Its period is only $\pi$, its range is all real numbers, and it climbs from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$ between consecutive asymptotes, passing through $(0, 0)$. Its domain is every real number except those asymptote positions.

@example
title: Reading and sketching a transformed sine wave
problem: Describe $y = 3\sin\left(2x - \frac{\pi}{2}\right) + 1$: find its amplitude, period, phase shift, midline, maximum and minimum, and the five key points of one cycle.
step: Factor out $B$: $2x - \frac{\pi}{2} = 2\left(x - \frac{\pi}{4}\right)$. So $A = 3$, $B = 2$, $C = \frac{\pi}{4}$ and $D = 1$.
step: The amplitude is $3$, the period is $\frac{2\pi}{2} = \pi$, the phase shift is $\frac{\pi}{4}$ to the right, and the midline is $y = 1$.
step: The maximum is $D + |A| = 4$ and the minimum is $D - |A| = -2$.
step: One cycle starts at $x = \frac{\pi}{4}$ on the midline. A quarter period is $\frac{\pi}{4}$, so the key $x$-values are $\frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{4}, \pi, \frac{5\pi}{4}$, with $y = 1, 4, 1, -2, 1$.
step: Check one point. At $x = \pi$ the angle is $2\pi - \frac{\pi}{2} = \frac{3\pi}{2}$, so $y = 3\sin\frac{3\pi}{2} + 1 = -2$, which matches the table.

@example
title: Writing an equation from a description
problem: A sinusoid has maximum value $7$ and minimum value $1$. It has a maximum at $x = 0$ and its next maximum at $x = 8$. Write an equation for it.
step: The midline is halfway between the extremes: $D = \frac{7 + 1}{2} = 4$.
step: The amplitude is half the distance between them: $A = \frac{7 - 1}{2} = 3$.
step: The period is $8$, so $B = \frac{2\pi}{8} = \frac{\pi}{4}$.
step: Cosine starts at its maximum, so with a maximum at $x = 0$ no shift is needed: $y = 3\cos\left(\frac{\pi x}{4}\right) + 4$. Check: half a period later, at $x = 4$, the wave should be at its minimum, and $3\cos\pi + 4 = 1$.

@mistakes
- Reading the phase shift of $\sin(2x - \pi)$ as $\pi$. Factor out the coefficient of $x$ first; the shift is $\frac{\pi}{2}$.
- Using $B$ as the period. The period is $\frac{2\pi}{B}$; $B$ counts how many cycles fit in $2\pi$.
- Reporting a negative amplitude. Amplitude is a distance, $|A|$; the sign of $A$ means a reflection.
- Drawing the tangent graph as one continuous curve that ignores its vertical asymptotes.

answer: 2
check: 2*cos(2*pi/3 - pi/3) + 1
hint: Substitute $x = \frac{2\pi}{3}$ and simplify the angle inside the cosine first.
hint: The angle becomes $\frac{\pi}{3}$. Evaluate the cosine there, then multiply and add.
solution: The angle is $\frac{2\pi}{3} - \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\pi}{3}$, so $y = 2\cdot\frac{1}{2} + 1 = 2$.

answer: 3.5

unit: m
hint: The cosine oscillates between two values. Which one makes $d$ smallest?
hint: The midline is $6$ and the amplitude is $2.5$. The minimum is the midline minus the amplitude.
solution: The midline is $6$ and the amplitude is $2.5$, so the depth ranges from $6 - 2.5 = 3.5$ m to $8.5$ m. The shallowest depth is $3.5$ m, reached when $\cos\frac{\pi t}{6} = -1$, at $t = 6$ hours.

vars: x
lead: $y =$
answer: 2 sin(2(x - pi/4)) - 1
check: lambda x: -2*cos(2*x) - 1

solution: $B = \frac{2\pi}{\pi} = 2$ and the upward midline crossing is the start of a sine cycle, so $C = \frac{\pi}{4}$: $y = 2\sin\left(2\left(x - \frac{\pi}{4}\right)\right) - 1$. Equivalent form: $y = -2\cos(2x) - 1$.

@quiz ms
prompt: Select every true statement about $y = \tan x$.
choice*: Its period is $\pi$.
choice*: It has a vertical asymptote at $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$.
choice: Its range is $[-1, 1]$.
choice: It is undefined wherever $\sin x = 0$.
hint: Write $\tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x}$. When is a quotient undefined?
hint: Check $\tan(x + \pi)$ using the signs of sine and cosine after a half turn.
solution: A quotient is undefined when its denominator is zero, so $\tan x$ is undefined where $\cos x = 0$, such as $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$; there are vertical asymptotes. Adding $\pi$ negates both sine and cosine, so $\tan(x + \pi) = \tan x$ and the period is $\pi$. The range is all real numbers, and $\tan x = 0$ (not undefined) where $\sin x = 0$.

@quiz num
prompt: The height above the ground of a rider on a Ferris wheel is $h(t) = 12 - 10\cos\left(\frac{\pi t}{20}\right)$ meters, where $t$ is in seconds. Find the height at $t = \frac{40}{3}$ s.
answer: 17
check: 12 - 10*cos(pi*(40/3)/20)
unit: m
hint: Substitute $t = \frac{40}{3}$ and simplify the angle inside the cosine.
hint: The angle is a standard angle in Quadrant II; look up its cosine, then watch the minus sign in front of the $10$.
solution: $\frac{\pi}{20}\cdot\frac{40}{3} = \frac{2\pi}{3}$ and $\cos\frac{2\pi}{3} = -\frac{1}{2}$, so $h = 12 - 10\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = 17$ m.
