@article
Most statements about $L^p$ functions are easy for continuous functions and true in general; **density** is the bridge. A subset $D \subseteq L^p$ is dense if every $f \in L^p$ is a norm limit of elements of $D$. Throughout, $1 \le p < \infty$ and the measure is Lebesgue measure $\lambda$ on $\mathbb{R}$ unless stated.

**Theorem 1 (simple functions).** In any measure space, the simple functions $s = \sum a_i \mathbf{1}_{A_i}$ with $\mu(A_i) < \infty$ whenever $a_i \ne 0$ are dense in $L^p(\mu)$.

*Proof.* For $f \ge 0$ in $L^p$ take the simple functions $s_n \uparrow f$ of Lesson 3. Then $0 \le f - s_n \le f$, so $|f - s_n|^p \le f^p \in L^1$ and $|f - s_n|^p \to 0$ pointwise; dominated convergence gives $\|f - s_n\|_p \to 0$. Also $\mu(\{s_n \ge a\}) \le a^{-p} \int f^p < \infty$ for $a > 0$, so the nonzero values sit on sets of finite measure. For general $f$ approximate $f^+$ and $f^-$. $\blacksquare$

**Theorem 2 (continuous functions).** $C_c(\mathbb{R})$, the continuous functions of compact support, is dense in $L^p(\mathbb{R})$ for $1 \le p < \infty$.

*Proof.* By Theorem 1 and the triangle inequality it suffices to approximate $\mathbf{1}_E$ with $\lambda(E) < \infty$. Let $\varepsilon > 0$. By regularity there are a compact $K$ and an open $G$ with $K \subseteq E \subseteq G$ and $\lambda(G \setminus K) < \varepsilon$. Let $\delta = \operatorname{dist}(K, \mathbb{R} \setminus G) > 0$ (any $\delta$ if $G = \mathbb{R}$) and $\varphi(x) = \max\left(0, 1 - \frac{2 \operatorname{dist}(x, K)}{\delta}\right)$. This is continuous, equals $1$ on $K$, vanishes unless $\operatorname{dist}(x, K) < \delta/2$, so has compact support inside $G$, and $0 \le \varphi \le 1$. The difference $\mathbf{1}_E - \varphi$ vanishes on $K$ and outside $G$, and is bounded by $1$, so $|\mathbf{1}_E - \varphi| \le \mathbf{1}_{G \setminus K}$ and $\|\mathbf{1}_E - \varphi\|_p \le \varepsilon^{1/p}$. $\blacksquare$

The theorem fails for $p = \infty$: a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, and $\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}$ has distance at least $1/2$ from $C(\mathbb{R})$.

**Corollary 3 (separability).** $L^p(\mathbb{R})$ is separable for $1 \le p < \infty$, with countable dense set the rational combinations of indicators of intervals with rational endpoints. $L^\infty$ is not separable: the indicators $\mathbf{1}_{(0,t)}$, $0 < t < 1$, are uncountably many points at mutual distance $1$.

**Theorem 4 (continuity of translation).** For $f \in L^p(\mathbb{R})$, $1 \le p < \infty$, and $\tau_h f(x) = f(x + h)$, $\|\tau_h f - f\|_p \to 0$ as $h \to 0$.

*Proof.* Given $\varepsilon > 0$ pick $\varphi \in C_c$ with $\|f - \varphi\|_p < \varepsilon/3$, supported in $[-R, R]$. Lebesgue measure is translation invariant, so $\|\tau_h f - \tau_h \varphi\|_p = \|f - \varphi\|_p$. For $|h| \le 1$, $\tau_h \varphi - \varphi$ is supported in $[-R-1, R+1]$ and bounded by the modulus of continuity $\omega(h) = \sup_{|s| \le |h|} \sup_x |\varphi(x + s) - \varphi(x)|$, which tends to $0$ as $\varphi$ is uniformly continuous. So $\|\tau_h \varphi - \varphi\|_p \le \omega(h)(2R + 2)^{1/p} < \varepsilon/3$ for small $|h|$. By the triangle inequality $\|\tau_h f - f\|_p < \varepsilon$. $\blacksquare$

This fails for $p = \infty$ ($\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}$ translated by any $h \ne 0$ stays distance $1$ away).

**Theorem 5 (Lusin, statement).** If $f\colon [a,b] \to \mathbb{R}$ is measurable and $\varepsilon > 0$, there is a compact $K \subseteq [a,b]$ with $\lambda([a,b] \setminus K) < \varepsilon$ such that $f|_K$ is continuous. It says that a measurable function is nearly continuous, in the same spirit as Egorov's theorem (a convergent sequence is nearly uniformly convergent).

### Completeness of $L^1$ by series

A normed space is complete if and only if every absolutely convergent series $\sum \|x_n\| < \infty$ converges. For $L^1$: if $\sum_n \|f_n\|_1 < \infty$, then $G = \sum |f_n|$ has $\int G = \sum \int |f_n| < \infty$ by monotone convergence, so $G < \infty$ a.e., the series $f = \sum f_n$ converges absolutely a.e., and the partial sums $S_N$ satisfy $|S_N| \le G$, so by dominated convergence $\|f - S_N\|_1 \to 0$.

@example
title: The Riemann–Lebesgue lemma by translation
problem: For $f \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$ let $\hat{f}(\xi) = \int f(x) e^{-i x \xi} \, dx$. Prove $\hat{f}(\xi) \to 0$ as $|\xi| \to \infty$.
step: Fix $\xi \ne 0$ and substitute $x \mapsto x + \pi/\xi$. Since $e^{-i(x + \pi/\xi)\xi} = -e^{-ix\xi}$, we get $\hat{f}(\xi) = -\int f(x + \pi/\xi) e^{-ix\xi} \, dx$ by translation invariance.
step: Average the two expressions: $\hat{f}(\xi) = \frac{1}{2} \int \left(f(x) - f(x + \pi/\xi)\right) e^{-ix\xi} \, dx$.
step: Bound by the modulus: $|\hat{f}(\xi)| \le \frac{1}{2} \|f - \tau_{\pi/\xi} f\|_1$.
step: By continuity of translation in $L^1$ (Theorem 4) the right side tends to $0$ as $\pi/\xi \to 0$, i.e. as $|\xi| \to \infty$.
answer: $\hat{f}(\xi) \to 0$; no rate holds in general, since the decay depends on $f$ only through its modulus of translation continuity.

@example
title: Translation distance for an indicator
problem: For $f = \mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}$ and $0 < |h| \le 1$ compute $\|\tau_h f - f\|_p$ and check Theorem 4.
step: $\tau_h f = \mathbf{1}_{[-h, 1-h]}$. For $h > 0$ the two indicators differ exactly on $[-h, 0) \cup (1 - h, 1]$.
step: This set has measure $2h$ (for $|h| \le 1$, by symmetry $2|h|$), and $|\tau_h f - f| = 1$ there.
step: Hence $\|\tau_h f - f\|_p^p = 2|h|$ and $\|\tau_h f - f\|_p = (2|h|)^{1/p} \to 0$ as $h \to 0$, as Theorem 4 predicts for each $p < \infty$.
answer: $\|\tau_h f - f\|_p = (2|h|)^{1/p}$; for $p = \infty$ the norm equals $1$ for all $h \ne 0$.

@mistakes
- Claiming $C_c$ is dense in $L^\infty$: uniform limits of continuous functions are continuous.
- Assuming the approximating continuous functions converge pointwise a.e. to $f$: density gives norm convergence; a subsequence converges a.e. (Lesson 7).
- Using continuous bounded functions or polynomials as the dense class: neither lies in $L^p(\mathbb{R})$ in general, and polynomials are only dense in the sense of Weierstrass on compact intervals.
- Proving a statement only for continuous functions and stopping: the extension to $L^p$ needs both density and continuity (boundedness) of the operation in the $L^p$ norm.

@practice self
prompt: Prove that $L^1(\mu)$ is complete, using the following criterion (which you should also prove): a normed space $V$ is complete if every absolutely convergent series $\sum \|x_n\| < \infty$ in $V$ converges in $V$.
hint: For the criterion, extract from a Cauchy sequence a subsequence with $\|x_{n_{k+1}} - x_{n_k}\| < 2^{-k}$.
hint: For $L^1$, show $G = \sum |f_n|$ is integrable by monotone convergence, then apply dominated convergence to the partial sums.
solution: *Criterion.* Let $(x_n)$ be Cauchy in $V$. Choose $n_1 < n_2 < \cdots$ with $\|x_{n_{k+1}} - x_{n_k}\| < 2^{-k}$. Then $\sum \|x_{n_{k+1}} - x_{n_k}\| < \infty$, so the series $x_{n_1} + \sum_k (x_{n_{k+1}} - x_{n_k})$ converges in $V$ to some $x$; its partial sums are $x_{n_{k}}$, so $x_{n_k} \to x$. A Cauchy sequence with a convergent subsequence converges: given $\varepsilon$, choose $N$ with $\|x_n - x_m\| < \varepsilon/2$ for $n, m \ge N$ and $k$ with $n_k \ge N$ and $\|x_{n_k} - x\| < \varepsilon/2$; then $\|x_n - x\| < \varepsilon$ for $n \ge N$. *$L^1$.* Let $\sum \|f_n\|_1 < \infty$ and $G = \sum_n |f_n| \colon X \to [0, \infty]$. By the monotone convergence theorem $\int G = \sum \int |f_n| < \infty$, so $G < \infty$ a.e. On this set $\sum f_n$ converges absolutely to a measurable $f$ (set $f = 0$ elsewhere). The partial sums satisfy $|S_N| \le G$ and $S_N \to f$ a.e., so $|f - S_N| \le 2G$ and $|f - S_N| \to 0$ a.e.; dominated convergence gives $\|f - S_N\|_1 \to 0$. Thus every absolutely convergent series converges, and $L^1$ is complete. $\blacksquare$
rubric: Proves the series criterion by passing from a Cauchy sequence to a rapidly Cauchy subsequence and back.
rubric: Uses the monotone convergence theorem to show $\int \sum |f_n| < \infty$ and deduces finiteness a.e.
rubric: Applies dominated convergence with dominating function $2G$ (or equivalent) to get $\|f - S_N\|_1 \to 0$.

Step 3: by regularity of Lebesgue measure choose compact $K \subseteq E \subseteq G$ open with $\lambda(G \setminus K) < \varepsilon$. For $|h| \le 1$, $\|\tau_h \varphi - \varphi\|_1 \le (2R + 2) \sup_x |\varphi(x + h) - \varphi(x)| \to 0$ by uniform continuity of $\varphi$. @quiz self
prompt: Prove that $L^p(\mathbb{R})$ is separable for $1 \le p < \infty$ and that $L^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ is not. You may use that $C_c(\mathbb{R})$ is dense in $L^p$ for $p < \infty$.
hint: Approximate a function of $C_c$ uniformly by a step function with rational values on rational intervals; compare norms on a bounded interval.
hint: For $L^\infty$ look for an uncountable family at mutual distance $1$.
solution: Let $D$ be the countable set of finite rational combinations of indicators of bounded intervals with rational endpoints. Given $f \in L^p$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ choose $\varphi \in C_c$ with $\|f - \varphi\|_p < \varepsilon/2$, supported in $[-R, R]$. By uniform continuity there is a step function $s \in D$ supported in $[-R, R]$ with $\sup |\varphi - s| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} (2R)^{-1/p}$ (use a fine rational partition and rational approximations of the values of $\varphi$ on each cell). Then $\|\varphi - s\|_p \le \sup|\varphi - s| (2R)^{1/p} < \varepsilon/2$ and $\|f - s\|_p < \varepsilon$. So $D$ is dense and countable. For $L^\infty$, the indicators $g_t = \mathbf{1}_{(0,t)}$, $0 < t < 1$, satisfy $\|g_s - g_t\|_\infty = 1$ for $s \ne t$. The open balls $B(g_t, 1/2)$ are pairwise disjoint, and a dense set must meet every nonempty open ball, so it would be uncountable. $\blacksquare$
