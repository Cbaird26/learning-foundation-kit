@article
To describe one quantitative variable, always address four features: **shape**, **center**, **spread** and **outliers** (unusual values), and say them in the context of the data.

### Shape

A distribution is **symmetric** if the left and right sides mirror each other, **right-skewed** if it has a long tail toward large values, and **left-skewed** if the tail points toward small values. A peak is a **mode**; one peak is **unimodal**, two is **bimodal**. Length of hospital stay and serum triglycerides are classic right-skewed variables: most values are modest, and a few are very large.

### Center

The **mean** $\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum x_i$ is the balance point. The **median** is the middle value of the sorted data (the average of the two middle values when $n$ is even). In a right-skewed distribution the mean is pulled toward the tail and exceeds the median; in a left-skewed one it is smaller.

### Spread

The **range** is maximum minus minimum. The **quartiles** split the sorted data into quarters, and the **interquartile range** is $\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$, the spread of the middle half of the data.

**Convention for quartiles.** Several methods exist and software can disagree slightly. This course uses the median-of-halves method: split the sorted data at the median, leaving out the median itself when $n$ is odd, and take $Q_1$ as the median of the lower half and $Q_3$ as the median of the upper half.

The **standard deviation** measures typical distance from the mean. Two versions exist:

$$s = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i - \mu)^2}{N}}.$$

Use $s$ (divide by $n-1$) when the data are a **sample** meant to represent a larger population, which is nearly always the case in health research. Use $\sigma$ (divide by $N$) only when you really have the whole population. Calculators label them $s_x$ (or $\sigma_{n-1}$) and $\sigma_x$ (or $\sigma_n$). Unless a problem says otherwise, this course's "standard deviation" of data means $s$.

### Resistant measures and outliers

A statistic is **resistant** if extreme values barely move it. The median and IQR are resistant; the mean, range and standard deviation are not. For skewed data or data with outliers, report the median and IQR.

**The 1.5 times IQR rule.** Compute the fences
$$Q_1 - 1.5\,\text{IQR} \quad\text{and}\quad Q_3 + 1.5\,\text{IQR}.$$
A value beyond a fence is flagged as a suspected outlier. A flag is a reason to investigate (a data-entry error? a patient from a different population?), not permission to delete the value.

@example
title: Stay lengths with a possible outlier
problem: Lengths of stay (days) for 8 patients after a procedure are $2, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 21$. Find the mean, median, $Q_1$, $Q_3$ and IQR, and decide whether $21$ is an outlier by the $1.5\,\text{IQR}$ rule.
step: The sum is $52$, so the mean is $52/8 = 6.5$ days.
step: The data are sorted and $n = 8$, so the median is the average of the 4th and 5th values: $(4+5)/2 = 4.5$ days. The mean exceeds the median, which fits a right-skewed shape.
step: The lower half is $2, 3, 3, 4$ with median $Q_1 = (3+3)/2 = 3$. The upper half is $5, 6, 8, 21$ with median $Q_3 = (6+8)/2 = 7$. So $\text{IQR} = 7 - 3 = 4$.
step: The upper fence is $7 + 1.5(4) = 13$ and the lower fence is $3 - 6 = -3$. Since $21 > 13$, it is a suspected outlier.

@example
title: Sample versus population standard deviation
problem: The values $2, 4, 4, 6$ are a sample. Find $s$, and compare with the value you would get by dividing by $n$.
step: The mean is $16/4 = 4$. The deviations are $-2, 0, 0, 2$ and their squares are $4, 0, 0, 4$, with sum $8$.
step: With $n - 1 = 3$: $s^2 = 8/3 \approx 2.667$, so $s \approx 1.633$.
step: Dividing by $n = 4$ would give $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$. The sample version is larger, which corrects for the fact that deviations measured from $\bar{x}$ (rather than the unknown $\mu$) are too small on average.

@mistakes
- Using the mean and standard deviation to summarize a strongly skewed distribution, where the median and IQR describe it better.
- Dividing by $n$ instead of $n - 1$ when the data are a sample.
- Including the median in both halves when finding quartiles of an odd-sized data set (under this course's convention it is left out).
- Deleting a flagged outlier automatically instead of checking whether it is an error or a real, informative observation.

@practice num
prompt: Five resting pulse readings (beats per minute) are $72, 68, 80, 76, 74$. Find the mean.
answer: 74
check: (72 + 68 + 80 + 76 + 74) / 5
tol: 0.01
unit: bpm
hint: Add the values and divide by how many there are.
solution: The sum is $370$ and $370/5 = 74$ beats per minute.

@practice num
prompt: The number of clinic visits last year for six patients was $12, 7, 9, 15, 11, 30$. Find the median number of visits.
answer: 11.5
check: np.median([12, 7, 9, 15, 11, 30])
tol: 0.01
hint: Sort the values first.
hint: With six values the median is the average of the 3rd and 4th sorted values.
solution: Sorted: $7, 9, 11, 12, 15, 30$. The median is $(11 + 12)/2 = 11.5$.

@practice num
prompt: Seven systolic blood pressure changes (mm Hg) are $3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 12, 14$. Using the median-of-halves method (leave out the median when $n$ is odd), find the IQR.
answer: 7
check: np.median([9, 12, 14]) - np.median([3, 5, 6])
tol: 0.01
hint: The median is the 4th value, $8$; exclude it and split the data into two halves of three values.
hint: $Q_1$ is the median of the lower half and $Q_3$ is the median of the upper half.
solution: Lower half $3, 5, 6$ gives $Q_1 = 5$. Upper half $9, 12, 14$ gives $Q_3 = 12$. So $\text{IQR} = 12 - 5 = 7$.

@practice num
prompt: A sample of four doses has values $3, 5, 7, 9$ (mg). Find the sample standard deviation $s$ (dividing by $n - 1$), rounded to two decimal places.
answer: 2.58
check: np.std([3, 5, 7, 9], ddof=1)
tol: 0.01
unit: mg
hint: The mean is $6$. Find each deviation from the mean and square it.
hint: The squared deviations sum to $20$. Divide by $n - 1 = 3$, then take the square root.
solution: Deviations $-3, -1, 1, 3$ have squares $9, 1, 1, 9$ with sum $20$. $s^2 = 20/3 \approx 6.667$, so $s \approx 2.58$.

@practice mc
prompt: A distribution of triglyceride levels is strongly right-skewed. Which statement about its mean and median is most likely true?
choice*: The mean is greater than the median, because the long right tail pulls the mean upward.
choice: The mean is less than the median, because the tail pulls the median upward.
choice: The mean and median are equal in every skewed distribution.
choice: The median cannot be computed for skewed data.
hint: Which of the two is resistant to extreme values?
solution: The mean follows extreme values and is pulled toward the tail, so it exceeds the median in a right-skewed distribution. The median is resistant and stays near the bulk of the data.

@quiz num
prompt: For a sample of hospital stays, $Q_1 = 62$ and $Q_3 = 78$ hours. What is the upper fence of the $1.5\,\text{IQR}$ rule for outliers?
answer: 102
check: 78 + 1.5 * (78 - 62)
tol: 0.01
unit: hours
hint: First find the IQR as $Q_3 - Q_1$.
solution: $\text{IQR} = 78 - 62 = 16$. The upper fence is $78 + 1.5(16) = 78 + 24 = 102$ hours.

@quiz ms
prompt: Which of these summary statistics are resistant to extreme outliers?
choice*: Median
choice: Mean
choice*: Interquartile range
choice: Standard deviation
choice: Range
hint: Ask which statistics would change a lot if the single largest value were replaced by a value ten times bigger.
solution: The median and IQR depend only on the middle of the sorted data, so a single extreme value does not move them. The mean, standard deviation and range all use the extreme values directly.
