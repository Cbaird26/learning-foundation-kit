### Week by week

**Weeks 1 and 2: flows on the line.** A fixed point of $\dot{x} = f(x)$ is stable if $f'(x^*) < 0$ and unstable if $f'(x^*) > 0$; at $f' = 0$ look at the sign of $f$. Bounded solutions are monotone and converge to a fixed point; there are no periodic solutions on the line. Saddle-node ($\dot{x} = r + x^2$), transcritical ($rx - x^2$) and pitchfork ($rx \mp x^3$) are the local bifurcations. Euler's method for $\dot{x} = -\lambda x$ needs $h < 2/\lambda$.

**Weeks 3 and 4: the plane.** For $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = A\mathbf{x}$ classify by $\tau$, $\Delta$ and $D = \tau^2 - 4\Delta$: saddle ($\Delta < 0$), node, spiral, center. Asymptotic stability if and only if $\tau < 0$ and $\Delta > 0$. For nonlinear systems linearize with the Jacobian; Hartman–Grobman applies at hyperbolic fixed points only.

**Weeks 5 and 6: energy and cycles.** $E = \tfrac12 v^2 + V(x)$ is conserved; conservative systems have no attractors; $V'' > 0$ at a critical point gives a center. Poincaré–Bendixson: a trapping region with no fixed point contains a closed orbit; Bendixson's criterion rules cycles out when the divergence has one sign on a simply connected region. Runge–Kutta is accurate to $O(h^4)$.

**Weeks 7 and 8: Hopf and maps.** A Hopf bifurcation has $\tau = 0$, $\Delta > 0$, transversality, $\ell_1 \ne 0$; the normal form $\dot{r} = \mu r \mp r^3$ gives amplitude $\sqrt{|\mu|}$. For $x_{n+1} = f(x_n)$ a fixed point is attracting if $|f'(p)| < 1$; the logistic map has $p = 1 - 1/r$ with multiplier $2 - r$.

**Weeks 9 and 10: period doubling and Lyapunov exponents.** The $2$-cycle has multiplier $4 + 2r - r^2$, equal to $-1$ at $r = 1 + \sqrt{6}$; Feigenbaum's $\delta \approx 4.669$ is universal. At $r = 4$, $x_n = \sin^2(2^n\pi\theta_0)$. The exponent is $\lambda = \lim \frac1n\sum\log|f'(x_i)|$, equal to $\frac1k\log|m|$ on an attracting $k$-cycle and to $\log 2$ typically at $r = 4$.

**Weeks 11 and 12: Lorenz and fractals.** The Lorenz system has divergence $-(\sigma + 1 + b)$, fixed points $C_\pm$ stable for $r < r_H = \sigma(\sigma + b + 3)/(\sigma - b - 1)$, and a trapping ellipsoid. Box dimension is the slope of $\log N$ against $\log(1/\varepsilon)$; for self-similar sets with the open set condition solve $\sum r_i^s = 1$.

### Common errors

Using $f'(x^*) = 0$ as a stability test; trusting linearization at non-hyperbolic points; testing a trapping condition at one angle only; confusing superstable ($0$) with period-doubling ($-1$) parameters; omitting the absolute value in $\log|f'|$; calling every positive exponent chaos; comparing floats with `==`; using `int()` for box indices; trusting the digits of a long chaotic orbit.

model:
```python
import math


def positive_exponent_params(rs):
    """Parameters of the logistic map with a positive Lyapunov exponent."""
    out = []
    for r in rs:
        x = 0.3
        for _ in range(500):
            x = r * x * (1 - x)
        total = 0.0
        for _ in range(2000):
            slope = abs(r * (1 - 2 * x))
            if slope == 0:
                total = float('-inf')
                break
            total += math.log(slope)
            x = r * x * (1 - x)
        if total / 2000 > 0:
            out.append(r)
    return out
```

@exam self
prompt: Consider the system $\dot{x} = \mu x - y - x(x^2 + y^2)$, $\dot{y} = x + \mu y - y(x^2 + y^2)$. (a) Show that the origin is stable for $\mu < 0$ and unstable for $\mu > 0$, and that the eigenvalues of the Jacobian at the origin cross the imaginary axis at $\mu = 0$ with nonzero speed. (b) Convert to polar coordinates and show that for $\mu > 0$ there is a closed orbit $r = \sqrt{\mu}$. (c) Using a trapping annulus and the Poincaré–Bendixson theorem, show that every orbit with $r(0) > 0$ is attracted to this closed orbit, and say what this tells you about the type of the Hopf bifurcation.
hint: At the origin $J = \begin{pmatrix} \mu & -1 \\ 1 & \mu \end{pmatrix}$. In polar form $r\dot{r} = x\dot{x} + y\dot{y}$ and $r^2\dot{\theta} = x\dot{y} - y\dot{x}$.
solution: (a) $J = \begin{pmatrix} \mu & -1 \\ 1 & \mu \end{pmatrix}$ has $\tau = 2\mu$ and $\Delta = \mu^2 + 1 > 0$, so the eigenvalues are $\mu \pm i$. The origin is asymptotically stable if $\tau < 0$, that is $\mu < 0$, and unstable for $\mu > 0$ (trace–determinant criterion). The real part $\mu$ has derivative $1 \ne 0$, and $\omega_0 = 1$ at $\mu = 0$: a Hopf point. (b) $r\dot{r} = x\dot{x} + y\dot{y} = \mu r^2 - r^4$ and $r^2\dot{\theta} = x\dot{y} - y\dot{x} = r^2$, so $\dot{r} = r(\mu - r^2)$ and $\dot{\theta} = 1$. For $\mu > 0$ the circle $r = \sqrt{\mu}$ has $\dot{r} = 0$ and $\dot{\theta} = 1$, so it is a closed orbit of period $2\pi$. (c) Let $0 < r_1 < \sqrt{\mu} < r_2$. On $r = r_1$, $\dot{r} = r_1(\mu - r_1^2) > 0$; on $r = r_2$, $\dot{r} < 0$. The annulus $r_1 \le r \le r_2$ is closed, bounded, forward invariant and has no fixed point ($\dot{\theta} = 1$). Since $\dot{r}$ depends only on $r$ and has the sign of $\sqrt{\mu} - r$, in fact $r(t) \to \sqrt{\mu}$ for every orbit with $r(0) > 0$ (the radial equation is one-dimensional and every nonconstant solution is monotone and converges to a fixed point by Week 1). The cycle is stable and exists only after the origin loses stability: the bifurcation is supercritical.
