@article
A fluid layer heated from below is described by partial differential equations. Lorenz kept three modes of the solution and obtained, in 1963, the system
$$\dot{x} = \sigma(y - x), \qquad \dot{y} = x(r - z) - y, \qquad \dot{z} = xy - bz,$$
with parameters $\sigma, r, b > 0$ (classically $\sigma = 10$, $b = 8/3$ and $r$ varied). The right side contains only two quadratic terms. Yet for $r = 28$ solutions never settle down, never repeat, and are exquisitely sensitive to the starting point. Nothing in the plane could do that: the Poincaré–Bendixson theorem of Week 6 says planar flows have no such orbits. In three dimensions there is room.

### Fixed points and symmetry

The system is unchanged by $(x, y, z) \mapsto (-x, -y, z)$. Fixed points: $\dot{x} = 0$ gives $y = x$; $\dot{z} = 0$ gives $z = x^2/b$; $\dot{y} = 0$ then gives $x(r - z) - x = x(r - 1 - z) = 0$. So $x = 0$ (the origin) or $z = r - 1$ and $x^2 = b(r - 1)$. For $r > 1$ there are two more fixed points $C_\pm = \left(\pm\sqrt{b(r-1)}, \pm\sqrt{b(r-1)}, r - 1\right)$, and for $r \le 1$ only the origin.

At the origin the Jacobian is $\begin{pmatrix} -\sigma & \sigma & 0 \\ r & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -b \end{pmatrix}$. One eigenvalue is $-b$, and the other two are those of the upper block, with $\tau = -(\sigma + 1) < 0$ and $\Delta = \sigma(1 - r)$. By the corollary of Week 3 the origin is asymptotically stable if $r < 1$; if $r > 1$ then $\Delta < 0$ and it is a saddle in the $xy$-block, hence unstable.

At $C_\pm$ the characteristic polynomial of the Jacobian is $\lambda^3 + (\sigma + b + 1)\lambda^2 + b(\sigma + r)\lambda + 2\sigma b(r - 1)$. The **Routh–Hurwitz criterion for a cubic** $\lambda^3 + a\lambda^2 + \beta\lambda + c$ with real coefficients says that all roots have negative real part if and only if $a, \beta, c > 0$ and $a\beta > c$. *(Stated without proof.)* Here $a, \beta, c > 0$ for $r > 1$, and $a\beta > c$ reads $(\sigma + b + 1)(\sigma + r) > 2\sigma(r - 1)$, that is, $r(\sigma - b - 1) < \sigma(\sigma + b + 3)$. If $\sigma > b + 1$ this is $r < r_H = \frac{\sigma(\sigma + b + 3)}{\sigma - b - 1}$. For $\sigma = 10$ and $b = 8/3$:
$$r_H = \frac{10\cdot(47/3)}{19/3} = \frac{470}{19} \approx 24.737 .$$
At $r = r_H$ there is a pair of purely imaginary eigenvalues (a Hopf bifurcation, which is subcritical for these parameters). So for $1 < r < 470/19$ the points $C_\pm$ are stable, and for $r = 28$ all three fixed points are unstable. If $\sigma \le b + 1$ the inequality holds for all $r > 1$.

### Dissipation and a trapping region

The divergence of the vector field is $\partial_x\dot{x} + \partial_y\dot{y} + \partial_z\dot{z} = -\sigma - 1 - b$, a negative constant. By Liouville's formula (stated without proof), the volume of a small blob carried by the flow satisfies $V(t) = V(0)e^{-(\sigma + 1 + b)t}$: for $\sigma = 10$ and $b = 8/3$ the volume shrinks by the factor $e^{-41/3} \approx 1.2\times 10^{-6}$ per unit of time. Any attractor therefore has zero volume.

**Theorem (trapping ellipsoid).** Let $L(x, y, z) = rx^2 + \sigma y^2 + \sigma(z - 2r)^2$. Along solutions
$$\frac{dL}{dt} = -2\sigma\bigl(rx^2 + y^2 + b(z - r)^2 - br^2\bigr).$$
Consequently every orbit enters, after finite time, the bounded region $\{L \le c\}$ for any sufficiently large $c$, and stays there.

*Proof.* Compute $\frac12\dot{L} = rx\dot{x} + \sigma y\dot{y} + \sigma(z - 2r)\dot{z}$. The three terms are $r\sigma xy - r\sigma x^2$, then $\sigma rxy - \sigma y^2 - \sigma xyz$, then $\sigma xyz - \sigma bz^2 - 2r\sigma xy + 2r\sigma bz$. The $xy$ terms add to $r\sigma + r\sigma - 2r\sigma = 0$ and the $xyz$ terms cancel, leaving $-\sigma(rx^2 + y^2 + bz^2 - 2rbz) = -\sigma(rx^2 + y^2 + b(z - r)^2 - br^2)$. With $Q = rx^2 + y^2 + b(z-r)^2$ this is the stated formula, and $\dot{L} < 0$ whenever $Q > br^2$. Because $L$ and $Q$ are positive definite quadratics up to a shift, there are constants $m > 0$ and $M$ with $Q - br^2 \ge mL - M$, so $\dot{L} \le -2\sigma(mL - M)$: where $L > M/m$, the quantity $L$ decreases at least exponentially toward $M/m$. Hence every orbit enters $\{L \le c\}$ for any $c > M/m$ and cannot leave it. $\blacksquare$

So for $r = 28$ all orbits are bounded, the fixed points are unstable, and the flow contracts volumes: the long-term set is a bounded set of zero volume that is not a point or a cycle, a **strange attractor**. Its structure is fractal; Week 12 measures its dimension (about $2.06$ for the classical parameters, a value found by numerical studies). Lorenz also noticed that the successive maxima of $z(t)$ follow, to good approximation, a one-dimensional unimodal map, which is why the period-doubling and Lyapunov ideas of Weeks 8 to 10 carry over.

### Simulating

```python
def lorenz(s, sigma, r, b):
    x, y, z = s
    return [sigma * (y - x), x * (r - z) - y, x * y - b * z]


def rk4(s, h, sigma, r, b):
    k1 = lorenz(s, sigma, r, b)
    k2 = lorenz([a + h / 2 * k for a, k in zip(s, k1)], sigma, r, b)
    k3 = lorenz([a + h / 2 * k for a, k in zip(s, k2)], sigma, r, b)
    k4 = lorenz([a + h * k for a, k in zip(s, k3)], sigma, r, b)
    return [a + h / 6 * (p + 2 * q + 2 * u + v)
            for a, p, q, u, v in zip(s, k1, k2, k3, k4)]


s = [1.0, 1.0, 1.0]
for _ in range(1500):
    s = rk4(s, 0.02, 10.0, 10.0, 8 / 3)
print([round(v, 3) for v in s])
```

For $r = 10 < r_H$ the orbit converges to $C_+$ and the program prints `[4.899, 4.899, 9.0]`, since $\sqrt{b(r-1)} = \sqrt{24} \approx 4.899$. For $r = 28$ the same loop produces the butterfly-shaped attractor, but the exact numbers after $t \approx 20$ depend on rounding and on the step size: only statistics (the shape, the exponent, how long two orbits stay together) are reliable.

@example
title: Volume contraction over one unit of time
problem: By what factor does the volume of a small blob of initial conditions shrink in one unit of time under the classical Lorenz flow?
step: The divergence is $-(\sigma + 1 + b)$ with $\sigma = 10$ and $b = 8/3$.
step: $\sigma + 1 + b = 10 + 1 + 8/3 = 41/3 \approx 13.667$.
step: The volume is multiplied by $e^{-41/3}$ per unit time, since the divergence is constant.
step: $e^{-13.667} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-6}$: after one unit of time a blob occupies about one millionth of its initial volume.
answer: By the factor $e^{-41/3} \approx 1.2\times 10^{-6}$.

@mistakes
- Concluding from the contraction of volumes that all orbits converge to a point: volumes shrink to zero, but the attractor can be a fractal set of zero volume that is much bigger than a point.
- Testing the Hopf value with floating point: at $r = r_H$ exactly the inequality $a\beta > c$ is an equality, so a rounding error flips the answer; use `Fraction`.
- Assuming that the stability condition $r < r_H$ holds for every $\sigma$ and $b$: the formula is meaningful only when $\sigma > b + 1$; otherwise the nonzero fixed points are stable for every $r > 1$.
- Believing a long simulation reproduces the true orbit. Beyond a few Lyapunov times the computed orbit is a different orbit; compare statistics, not individual values.

```python
def divergence(F, p, h):
    """Central-difference divergence of F at the point p."""
    total = 0.0
    for i in range(len(p)):
        up, dn = list(p), list(p)
        up[i] += h
        dn[i] -= h
        total += (F(up)[i] - F(dn)[i]) / (2 * h)
    return total
```
hint: Perturb only coordinate `i`, and read only component `i` of the result.
solution: Only the diagonal entries of the Jacobian enter the divergence, so each coordinate is perturbed on its own. The central-difference error is $O(h^2)$ by the Week 4 bound, and the Lorenz field is quadratic, so its differences are exact up to rounding. The zero for the rotation field $(y, -x, 0)$ is the signature of a volume-preserving flow, and the value $-41/3$ for Lorenz is independent of the point.

def nonzero_fixed_stable(sigma, b, r):
    """Routh-Hurwitz test for the fixed points C+- of the Lorenz system."""
    a = sigma + b + 1
    beta = b * (sigma + r)
    c = 2 * sigma * b * (r - 1)
    return bool(a > 0 and beta > 0 and c > 0 and a * beta > c)
```

Substituting into $\dot{y} = 0$: $x(r - x^2/b) - x = x(r - 1 - x^2/b) = 0$, so $x = 0$ (then $y = z = 0$) or $x^2 = b(r - 1)$ (possible only for $r \ge 1$), with $z = x^2/b = r - 1$ and $y = x$. These are the stated points. At the origin the Jacobian block for $(x, y)$ is $\begin{pmatrix} -\sigma & \sigma \\ r & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ with determinant $\sigma(1 - r) < 0$ for $r > 1$, so it has one positive and one negative eigenvalue; with the eigenvalue $-b$ in the $z$-direction the origin is an unstable saddle. ```python
import math


def separation_time(r, d0, thr, h, nmax):
    """Time for two Lorenz orbits started d0 apart to be thr apart."""
    sigma, b = 10.0, 8 / 3

    def field(s):
        x, y, z = s
        return [sigma * (y - x), x * (r - z) - y, x * y - b * z]

    def step(s):
        k1 = field(s)
        k2 = field([a + h / 2 * k for a, k in zip(s, k1)])
        k3 = field([a + h / 2 * k for a, k in zip(s, k2)])
        k4 = field([a + h * k for a, k in zip(s, k3)])
        return [a + h / 6 * (p + 2 * q + 2 * u + v)
                for a, p, q, u, v in zip(s, k1, k2, k3, k4)]

    s1 = [1.0, 1.0, 1.0]
    s2 = [1.0 + d0, 1.0, 1.0]
    for k in range(nmax + 1):
        if math.dist(s1, s2) > thr:
            return k * h
        s1, s2 = step(s1), step(s2)
    return None
```
hint: Check the distance before advancing, so that an initial separation above `thr` returns `0.0`.
solution: On the attractor the separation grows like $d_0e^{\lambda t}$ with $\lambda \approx 0.9$, so reducing $d_0$ by a factor $1000$ adds about $\ln 1000/0.9 \approx 7.7$ time units, which the test brackets between $3$ and $12$. The ranges are wide because the growth rate fluctuates along the orbit and the first time units are transient; the exact value depends on rounding and step size, as the article warns.
