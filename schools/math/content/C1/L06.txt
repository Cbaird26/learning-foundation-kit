@article
Calculus turns many geometry problems into one recipe: **slice the object, approximate each slice by something simple, add up, and take a limit.** The sum becomes an integral, and your integration techniques do the rest.

### Area between curves

If $f(x)\ge g(x)$ on $[a,b]$, the area between the graphs is

$$A=\int_a^b\big(f(x)-g(x)\big)\,dx,$$

because each thin vertical strip has height "top minus bottom".

### Volumes of revolution

Rotate a region about an axis and slice the solid.

- **Disks.** The region under $y=f(x)$ on $[a,b]$, rotated about the $x$-axis, has circular slices of radius $f(x)$, so $V=\pi\int_a^b f(x)^2\,dx$.
- **Washers.** If the region lies between an outer curve $R(x)$ and an inner curve $r(x)$, each slice is a washer with area $\pi\big(R^2-r^2\big)$, so $V=\pi\int_a^b\big(R(x)^2-r(x)^2\big)\,dx$.
- **Shells.** Rotate the region under $y=f(x)$, with $0\le a\le x\le b$, about the $y$-axis and cut it into thin vertical strips. Each strip sweeps out a cylindrical shell of radius $x$, height $f(x)$ and thickness $dx$, so $V=2\pi\int_a^b x\,f(x)\,dx$.

Slices perpendicular to the axis of rotation give disks or washers; slices parallel to it give shells. Pick whichever leads to the easier integral.

### Arc length

Approximate a curve by short straight pieces. Each piece has length $\sqrt{dx^2+dy^2}=\sqrt{1+f'(x)^2}\,dx$, so

$$L=\int_a^b\sqrt{1+f'(x)^2}\,dx.$$

The square root is usually hard to integrate, so worked examples are chosen with $1+f'(x)^2$ a perfect square. The same idea gives the surface area of revolution about the $x$-axis, $S=2\pi\int_a^b f(x)\sqrt{1+f'(x)^2}\,dx$.

### Decide convergence first

If the region stretches to infinity, the volume or length is an improper integral, and last week's habit applies: test convergence (p-integrals, comparison) **before** evaluating. Rotating the region under $y=\frac1x$, $x\ge1$, about the $x$-axis gives $V=\pi\int_1^\infty\frac{dx}{x^2}$, which is finite because $p=2>1$. The surface area integrand, however, is at least $\frac{2\pi}{x}$, and that integral diverges. A finite volume with an infinite surface area is not a contradiction: the two integrals measure different things.

@example
title: Washers for a region between two curves
problem: The region between $y=x$ and $y=x^2$ is rotated about the $x$-axis. Find the volume.
step: Find where the curves meet: $x=x^2$ gives $x=0$ and $x=1$. On $[0,1]$ we have $x\ge x^2$, so the outer radius is $R=x$ and the inner radius is $r=x^2$.
step: Each slice perpendicular to the $x$-axis is a washer with area $\pi\big(R^2-r^2\big)=\pi\big(x^2-x^4\big)$.
step: Add the slices: $V=\pi\int_0^1\big(x^2-x^4\big)\,dx$.
step: Integrate: $V=\pi\left[\frac{x^3}{3}-\frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^1=\pi\left(\frac13-\frac15\right)=\frac{2\pi}{15}$.

@example
title: An arc length with a perfect square
problem: Find the length of the curve $y=\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{\ln x}{4}$ for $1\le x\le2$.
step: Differentiate: $y'=x-\frac{1}{4x}$.
step: Square and add $1$: $1+(y')^2=1+x^2-\frac12+\frac{1}{16x^2}=x^2+\frac12+\frac{1}{16x^2}=\left(x+\frac{1}{4x}\right)^2$.
step: The root is $x+\frac{1}{4x}$, which is positive on $[1,2]$, so $L=\int_1^2\left(x+\frac{1}{4x}\right)dx$.
step: Integrate: $L=\left[\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{\ln x}{4}\right]_1^2=\left(2+\frac{\ln 2}{4}\right)-\frac12=\frac32+\frac{\ln 2}{4}\approx1.673$.

@mistakes
- Squaring the difference of the radii, $(R-r)^2$, instead of taking the difference of the squares, $R^2-r^2$, in the washer formula.
- Dropping the radius factor $x$ in the shell formula, or using the height of the wrong curve.
- Writing the arc length integrand as $\sqrt{1+f'(x)}$ instead of $\sqrt{1+\big(f'(x)\big)^2}$.
- Evaluating a volume or length over an infinite region without first testing whether the improper integral converges.

@practice num
prompt: Find the area of the region between $y=e^x$ and $y=x+1$ for $0\le x\le1$. Give an exact answer.
answer: e - 5/2
check: quad(lambda x: abs(exp(x) - (x + 1)), 0, 1)
hint: On $[0,1]$ the curve $y=e^x$ lies above the line (the line is tangent to it at $x=0$). The height of a strip is top minus bottom.
hint: Integrate $e^x-x-1$ from $0$ to $1$.
solution: $\int_0^1(e^x-x-1)\,dx=\left[e^x-\frac{x^2}{2}-x\right]_0^1=\left(e-\frac12-1\right)-1=e-\frac52$.

@practice num
prompt: The region under $y=e^{-x}$ for $0\le x\le1$ is rotated about the $x$-axis. Find the volume of the solid. Give an exact answer.
answer: pi*(1 - e^(-2))/2
check: pi*quad(lambda x: exp(-x)**2, 0, 1)
hint: Slices perpendicular to the $x$-axis are disks of radius $e^{-x}$, each with area $\pi e^{-2x}$.
hint: $V=\pi\int_0^1 e^{-2x}\,dx$. An antiderivative of $e^{-2x}$ is $-\frac12e^{-2x}$.
solution: $V=\pi\left[-\frac12e^{-2x}\right]_0^1=\frac{\pi}{2}\left(1-e^{-2}\right)$.

@practice num
prompt: The region under $y=e^{-x^2}$ for $0\le x\le1$ is rotated about the $y$-axis. Use cylindrical shells to find the volume. Give an exact answer.
answer: pi*(1 - e^(-1))
check: 2*pi*quad(lambda x: x*exp(-x**2), 0, 1)
hint: A vertical strip at position $x$ sweeps out a shell of radius $x$, height $e^{-x^2}$ and thickness $dx$.
hint: $V=2\pi\int_0^1 xe^{-x^2}\,dx$. Substitute $u=x^2$, so $du=2x\,dx$.
solution: $V=2\pi\int_0^1xe^{-x^2}\,dx=2\pi\left[-\frac12e^{-x^2}\right]_0^1=\pi\left(1-e^{-1}\right)$.

@practice mc
prompt: The region between $y=x$ and $y=x^2$ for $0\le x\le1$ is rotated about the $y$-axis. Which integral gives the volume?
choice*: $2\pi\int_0^1 x\,(x-x^2)\,dx$
choice: $\pi\int_0^1 \left(x^2-x^4\right)dx$
choice: $2\pi\int_0^1 (x-x^2)\,dx$
choice: $\pi\int_0^1 (x-x^2)^2\,dx$
hint: Rotating about the $y$-axis with vertical strips suggests shells. What are the radius and the height of the shell at position $x$?
hint: The radius is the distance from the axis of rotation, and the height is top minus bottom.
solution: The shell at $x$ has radius $x$ and height $x-x^2$, so $V=2\pi\int_0^1x(x-x^2)\,dx=2\pi\left(\frac13-\frac14\right)=\frac{\pi}{6}$. The integral $\pi\int_0^1 \left(x^2-x^4\right)dx$ is the washer integral for rotation about the $x$-axis, $2\pi\int_0^1 (x-x^2)\,dx$ forgets the radius factor $x$, and $\pi\int_0^1 (x-x^2)^2\,dx$ squares the height as if it were a disk radius.

@practice self
prompt: The region under $y=\frac1x$ for $x\ge1$ is rotated about the $x$-axis. (a) Before computing anything, write the improper integral for the volume and decide, using a p-integral, whether the volume is finite; if it is, compute it. (b) The surface area is $S=2\pi\int_1^\infty\frac1x\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{x^4}}\,dx$. Decide by comparison, without evaluating, whether $S$ is finite.
hint: For (a) the slices are disks of radius $\frac1x$, so the integrand is $\pi\cdot\frac{1}{x^2}$.
hint: For (b) compare $\frac1x\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{x^4}}$ with $\frac1x$. Which one is larger, and what does the p-integral with $p=1$ do?
solution: (a) $V=\pi\int_1^\infty x^{-2}\,dx$. Since $p=2>1$ it converges, and $V=\pi\left[-\frac1x\right]_1^\infty=\pi$. (b) For $x\ge1$, $\frac1x\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{x^4}}\ge\frac1x>0$, so $S\ge2\pi\int_1^\infty\frac{dx}{x}$. That integral diverges ($p=1$), so $S$ diverges by comparison. The volume is finite while the surface area is infinite.
rubric: Writes $V=\pi\int_1^\infty x^{-2}\,dx$ and justifies convergence with $p=2>1$ before evaluating.
rubric: Evaluates the volume correctly as $\pi$.
rubric: Compares the surface-area integrand with $\frac{2\pi}{x}$ in the correct direction (the given integrand is larger).
rubric: Concludes that $S$ diverges because $\int_1^\infty\frac{dx}{x}$ diverges.

@quiz num
prompt: Find the arc length of $y=\frac{x^3}{6}+\frac{1}{2x}$ for $1\le x\le3$. Give an exact answer.
answer: 14/3
check: quad(lambda x: sqrt(1 + nderiv(lambda t: t**3/6 + 1/(2*t), x)**2), 1, 3)
hint: Differentiate: $y'=\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{1}{2x^2}$. Then simplify $1+(y')^2$.
hint: $1+(y')^2=\left(\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{1}{2x^2}\right)^2$, so the square root is $\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{1}{2x^2}$.
solution: $L=\int_1^3\left(\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{1}{2x^2}\right)dx=\left[\frac{x^3}{6}-\frac{1}{2x}\right]_1^3=\left(\frac{27}{6}-\frac16\right)-\left(\frac16-\frac12\right)=\frac{13}{3}+\frac13=\frac{14}{3}$.

@quiz num
prompt: The region bounded by $y=\sqrt{x}$, the $x$-axis and the line $x=4$ is rotated about the $y$-axis. Find the volume. Give an exact answer.
answer: 128*pi/5
check: pi*quad(lambda y: 16 - y**4, 0, 2)
hint: Shells are natural here: a vertical strip at position $x$ has radius $x$ and height $\sqrt{x}$.
hint: $V=2\pi\int_0^4 x^{3/2}\,dx$.
solution: $V=2\pi\int_0^4x^{3/2}\,dx=2\pi\cdot\frac25\left[x^{5/2}\right]_0^4=2\pi\cdot\frac25\cdot32=\frac{128\pi}{5}$. Washers in $y$ agree: $\pi\int_0^2\left(16-y^4\right)dy=\pi\left(32-\frac{32}{5}\right)=\frac{128\pi}{5}$.
