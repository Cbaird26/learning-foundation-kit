objective: Understand that subtracting means taking some away.
objective: Read and write a subtracting sentence such as $5 - 2 = 3$.
objective: Subtract small numbers from a number up to 10.

@article
**Subtracting** means taking some away. It tells you how many are left.

### Take some away

You have 5 grapes. You eat 2 of them. How many grapes are left? Count the ones that are left: 1, 2, 3. There are 3 grapes left.

We write this as a number sentence:

$$5 - 2 = 3$$

The **-** sign says "minus" or "take away". The answer is how many are left.

### Count back

Start at the big number and count back. For $7 - 2$, start at 7 and say 6, 5. You get 5. So $7 - 2 = 5$.

### Adding and subtracting are friends

If $3 + 2 = 5$, then $5 - 2 = 3$ and $5 - 3 = 2$. Adding puts things together. Subtracting takes them apart.

### Taking away nothing, taking away everything

If you take away 0, nothing changes: $4 - 0 = 4$. If you take away all of them, nothing is left: $4 - 4 = 0$.

### Use objects

Use blocks, coins or fingers. Put them in a group, slide some away, and count what is left. Act it out with a grown-up.

@example
title: Taking away cookies
problem: There are 8 cookies. You eat 3 of them. How many cookies are left?
step: Write the subtracting sentence: $8 - 3$.
step: Start at 8 and count back 3: 7, 6, 5.
step: $8 - 3 = 5$. There are 5 cookies left.

@mistakes
- Counting the starting number as one of the steps back, and getting an answer that is one too big.
- Adding when the story says take away.
- Forgetting that $5 - 5 = 0$, not 5.
