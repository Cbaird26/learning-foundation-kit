@article
The **coordinate plane** lets you draw an equation. Two number lines cross at right angles: the horizontal one is the **$x$-axis**, the vertical one is the **$y$-axis**, and they meet at the **origin** $(0, 0)$. Every point has an **ordered pair** $(x, y)$: go $x$ units right (left if negative), then $y$ units up (down if negative).

### Start with a story

Go back to the taxi from the linear-equations lesson. The fare $y$ dollars for a ride of $x$ kilometres is $y = 2x + 4$. A 1 km ride costs 6 dollars and a 5 km ride costs 14 dollars, giving the points $(1, 6)$ and $(5, 14)$. Work out more fares and plot them: they all fall on one straight line. That is why such equations are called **linear**.

### Slope: how steep, and which way

The **slope** of a line tells you how much $y$ changes when $x$ increases by $1$. From two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$:

$$m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}.$$

For the taxi, $m = \frac{14 - 6}{5 - 1} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$: the fare rises 2 dollars for every extra kilometre. Slope is a **rate of change**, measured in (units of $y$) per (unit of $x$).

- $m > 0$: the line rises from left to right. $m < 0$: it falls.
- $m = 0$: a horizontal line $y = \text{constant}$.
- A vertical line $x = \text{constant}$ has **undefined** slope, because the run is $0$ and we cannot divide by $0$.

Subtract in the **same order** on the top and the bottom.

### Slope-intercept form

A non-vertical line can be written

$$y = mx + b,$$

where $m$ is the slope and $b$ is the **$y$-intercept**: the value of $y$ when $x = 0$, where the line crosses the $y$-axis. For the taxi, $m = 2$ and $b = 4$, the flat fee. To graph it, plot $(0, b)$, then use the slope as "up $2$, right $1$" to find the next point.

### Intercepts of any equation

For the **$y$-intercept** set $x = 0$; for the **$x$-intercept** set $y = 0$. In $3x - 4y = 12$, putting $y = 0$ gives $x = 4$, and putting $x = 0$ gives $y = -3$. To read the slope, solve for $y$ first: $y = \frac{3}{4}x - 3$, so $m = \frac{3}{4}$.

### Building an equation

If a line has slope $m$ and passes through $(x_1, y_1)$, then

$$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$

(**point-slope form**). Expand and tidy it to reach $y = mx + b$.

### Parallel and perpendicular

Parallel lines have **equal** slopes. Perpendicular lines have slopes that multiply to $-1$, so one slope is the **negative reciprocal** of the other: $m$ and $-\frac{1}{m}$.

@example
title: The line through two points
problem: Find the equation of the line through $(2, 1)$ and $(6, 9)$.
step: Find the slope: $m = \frac{9 - 1}{6 - 2} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$.
step: Write $y = 2x + b$ and substitute one point, $(2, 1)$: $1 = 2(2) + b$.
step: Solve for $b$: $b = 1 - 4 = -3$.
step: The equation is $y = 2x - 3$.
step: Check with the other point, $(6, 9)$: $2(6) - 3 = 9$. It lies on the line.

@example
title: A perpendicular line through a point
problem: Find the equation of the line through $(3, 5)$ that is perpendicular to $y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 4$.
step: The given line has slope $-\frac{1}{2}$. A perpendicular line has the negative reciprocal slope, $m = 2$.
step: Use point-slope form with $(3, 5)$: $y - 5 = 2(x - 3)$.
step: Expand the bracket: $y - 5 = 2x - 6$.
step: Add $5$ to both sides: $y = 2x - 1$.
step: Check: at $x = 3$, $y = 2(3) - 1 = 5$, and the slopes multiply to $2 \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = -1$.

@mistakes
- Subtracting in opposite orders, as in $\frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_1 - x_2}$, which gives the wrong sign.
- Writing run over rise instead of rise over run.
- Reading the slope off an equation that is not solved for $y$: in $3x - 4y = 12$ the slope is neither $3$ nor $-4$.
- Mixing up the intercepts. The $y$-intercept has $x = 0$; the $x$-intercept has $y = 0$.

@practice num
prompt: Find the slope of the line through $(1, 2)$ and $(4, 11)$.
answer: 3
check: lin([[1, 1], [4, 1]], [2, 11])[0]
hint: Slope is rise over run. Subtract the $y$-values and the $x$-values in the same order.
hint: The rise is $11 - 2$ and the run is $4 - 1$.
solution: $m = \frac{11 - 2}{4 - 1} = \frac{9}{3} = 3$.

@practice num
prompt: A line has slope $-2$ and passes through $(3, 1)$. What is its $y$-intercept?
answer: 7
check: root(lambda b: -2*3 + b - 1, -100, 100)
hint: Start from $y = mx + b$ with $m = -2$, and put in the $x$ and $y$ of the point.
hint: You get $1 = -2(3) + b$. Solve for $b$.
solution: $1 = -6 + b$, so $b = 7$. Check: on $y = -2x + 7$, at $x = 3$ we get $y = -6 + 7 = 1$.

@practice num
prompt: Find the $x$-intercept of the line $5x - 2y = 30$.
answer: 6
check: root(lambda x: 5*x - 2*0 - 30, -100, 100)
hint: The $x$-intercept is where the line crosses the $x$-axis. What is $y$ there?
hint: Put $y = 0$ into the equation and solve for $x$.
solution: With $y = 0$: $5x = 30$, so $x = 6$. The intercept is the point $(6, 0)$.

@practice expr
prompt: A line has slope $\frac{2}{3}$ and passes through $(3, 4)$. Write $y$ as an expression in $x$.
vars: x
lead: $y =$
answer: 2x/3 + 2
check: lambda x: 4 + (2/3)*(x - 3)
hint: Use point-slope form: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.
hint: Substitute $m = \frac{2}{3}$ and $(x_1, y_1) = (3, 4)$, expand the bracket, then add $4$ to both sides.
solution: $y - 4 = \frac{2}{3}(x - 3) = \frac{2}{3}x - 2$, so $y = \frac{2}{3}x + 2$. Check: at $x = 3$, $y = 2 + 2 = 4$.

@practice mc
prompt: Which line is perpendicular to $y = 2x + 1$ and passes through the origin?
choice*: $y = -\frac{1}{2}x$
choice: $y = \frac{1}{2}x$
choice: $y = -2x$
choice: $y = 2x$
hint: A perpendicular line has a slope whose product with $2$ is $-1$.
hint: The slope must be the negative reciprocal of $2$. Passing through the origin means $b = 0$.
solution: The negative reciprocal of $2$ is $-\frac{1}{2}$, and $b = 0$, so $y = -\frac{1}{2}x$. The choice $y = \frac{1}{2}x$ only takes the reciprocal, $y = -2x$ only changes the sign, and $y = 2x$ is parallel to the given line.

@quiz num
prompt: The points $(-2, 7)$ and $(4, -5)$ lie on a line. What is the $y$-intercept of the line?
answer: 3
check: lin([[-2, 1], [4, 1]], [7, -5])[1]
hint: Find the slope first, keeping the order of subtraction the same on top and bottom.
hint: The slope is $\frac{-5 - 7}{4 - (-2)}$. Then substitute one point into $y = mx + b$ and solve for $b$.
solution: $m = \frac{-12}{6} = -2$. Using $(-2, 7)$: $7 = -2(-2) + b = 4 + b$, so $b = 3$. Check with $(4, -5)$: $-2(4) + 3 = -5$.

@quiz num
prompt: For what value of $k$ is the line through $(1, 2)$ and $(3, k)$ parallel to the line $y = 4x - 1$?
answer: 10
check: root(lambda k: (k - 2)/(3 - 1) - 4, -100, 100)
hint: Parallel lines have equal slopes. What is the slope of $y = 4x - 1$?
hint: Set the slope through the two points, $\frac{k - 2}{3 - 1}$, equal to $4$ and solve for $k$.
solution: $\frac{k - 2}{2} = 4$, so $k - 2 = 8$ and $k = 10$. Check: the slope from $(1, 2)$ to $(3, 10)$ is $\frac{8}{2} = 4$.
