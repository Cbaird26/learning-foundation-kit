@article
Factoring is multiplication run backwards: you write an expression as a product. It is the main tool for solving equations and for simplifying fractions in the next lesson.

### Start with a puzzle

Find two numbers that multiply to $12$ and add to $7$. After a moment: $3$ and $4$. This is exactly the question behind factoring $x^2 + 7x + 12$, because

$$(x + 3)(x + 4) = x^2 + 7x + 12.$$

The constant term $12$ is the **product** $3 \cdot 4$ and the middle coefficient $7$ is the **sum** $3 + 4$.

### Step 1: take out the common factor

Always look first for the greatest common factor (GCF): $6x^2 + 9x = 3x(2x + 3)$. It makes everything after it simpler.

### Step 2: trinomials

For $x^2 + bx + c$, find two numbers with product $c$ and sum $b$. If $c > 0$ the two numbers have the same sign (the sign of $b$); if $c < 0$ they have opposite signs. So $x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3)$ and $x^2 + x - 12 = (x + 4)(x - 3)$.

When the leading coefficient is not $1$, use the **ac method**: find two numbers with product $ac$ and sum $b$, split the middle term with them, then factor by grouping. For $2x^2 + 7x + 3$ we have $ac = 6$, and the numbers are $1$ and $6$:

$$2x^2 + x + 6x + 3 = x(2x + 1) + 3(2x + 1) = (2x + 1)(x + 3).$$

### Special forms

$$a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b), \qquad a^2 \pm 2ab + b^2 = (a \pm b)^2.$$

So $x^2 - 49 = (x - 7)(x + 7)$ and $x^2 + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)^2$. A **sum** of squares such as $x^2 + 49$ does not factor over the real numbers.

### Solving a quadratic equation

A **quadratic equation** has the form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ with $a \neq 0$.

**Method 1: factor.** The zero-product property says that if $AB = 0$ then $A = 0$ or $B = 0$. It only works when the product equals zero, so first move every term to one side. Then $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$ becomes $(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$, so $x = 2$ or $x = 3$.

**Method 2: the quadratic formula.** It always works:

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}.$$

The **discriminant** $D = b^2 - 4ac$ counts the real solutions: two if $D > 0$, one repeated solution if $D = 0$, and none if $D < 0$.

**Method 3: completing the square.** Rewrite $x^2 + bx$ as $\left(x + \frac{b}{2}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2$. For $x^2 + 6x + 2 = 0$ this gives $(x + 3)^2 = 7$, so $x = -3 \pm \sqrt{7}$. The quadratic formula is this method done once and for all.

Finish by substituting each solution back into the original equation.

@example
title: Factoring with the ac method
problem: Solve $6x^2 - x - 2 = 0$ by factoring.
step: Here $ac = 6 \cdot (-2) = -12$. We need two numbers with product $-12$ and sum $-1$. They are $-4$ and $3$.
step: Split the middle term: $6x^2 - 4x + 3x - 2 = 0$.
step: Factor by grouping: $2x(3x - 2) + 1(3x - 2) = (3x - 2)(2x + 1) = 0$.
step: Apply the zero-product property: $3x - 2 = 0$ gives $x = \frac{2}{3}$, and $2x + 1 = 0$ gives $x = -\frac{1}{2}$.
step: Check $x = \frac{2}{3}$: $6 \cdot \frac{4}{9} - \frac{2}{3} - 2 = \frac{8}{3} - \frac{2}{3} - 2 = 0$. Check $x = -\frac{1}{2}$: $\frac{6}{4} + \frac{1}{2} - 2 = 0$.

@example
title: When factoring does not work: the formula
problem: Solve $x^2 + 4x - 3 = 0$.
step: Read off $a = 1$, $b = 4$, $c = -3$. No pair of whole numbers has product $-3$ and sum $4$, so use the formula.
step: The discriminant is $D = 4^2 - 4(1)(-3) = 16 + 12 = 28$. It is positive, so there are two real solutions.
step: Substitute: $x = \dfrac{-4 \pm \sqrt{28}}{2}$.
step: Simplify $\sqrt{28} = 2\sqrt{7}$ and divide every term of the numerator by $2$: $x = -2 \pm \sqrt{7}$.
step: Check numerically with $x \approx 0.646$: $0.417 + 2.583 - 3 \approx 0$.

@mistakes
- Applying the zero-product property when the product is not zero, such as $(x - 2)(x - 3) = 6$ leading to $x - 2 = 6$.
- Dividing both sides by $x$ and losing the solution $x = 0$. Write $x^2 = 5x$ as $x(x - 5) = 0$ instead.
- Sign slips in the formula: when $b = -3$, the quantity $-b$ is $+3$, and when $a$ and $c$ have opposite signs (for example $a > 0$ and $c < 0$), $-4ac$ is positive.
- Canceling only part of a numerator: $\dfrac{-4 \pm 2\sqrt{7}}{2}$ is $-2 \pm \sqrt{7}$, not $-4 \pm \sqrt{7}$.

@practice list
prompt: Solve $x^2 - 5x - 14 = 0$. Enter both solutions, separated by a comma.
answer: -2, 7
check: allroots(lambda x: x**2 - 5*x - 14, -100, 100)
hint: You need two numbers with product $-14$ and sum $-5$.
hint: The factor pairs of $14$ are $1, 14$ and $2, 7$. Which pair, with suitable signs, adds to $-5$?
solution: $x^2 - 5x - 14 = (x - 7)(x + 2) = 0$, so $x = 7$ or $x = -2$. Check: $49 - 35 - 14 = 0$ and $4 + 10 - 14 = 0$.

@practice expr
prompt: Factor $2x^2 - 8x - 10$ completely.
vars: x
form: factored
answer: 2(x - 5)(x + 1)
check: lambda x: 2*x**2 - 8*x - 10
hint: Look for a common factor of all three terms first.
hint: Taking out $2$ leaves $x^2 - 4x - 5$. Find two numbers with product $-5$ and sum $-4$.
solution: $2x^2 - 8x - 10 = 2(x^2 - 4x - 5) = 2(x - 5)(x + 1)$. Expanding $(x - 5)(x + 1) = x^2 - 4x - 5$ confirms it.

choice*: $(3x - 5)(3x + 5)$

choice: $(9x - 5)(x + 5)$
choice: It cannot be factored, because there is no middle term.

hint: Use $a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$ with $a = 3x$ and $b = 5$.
solution: $9x^2 - 25 = (3x)^2 - 5^2 = (3x - 5)(3x + 5)$. The middle terms $+15x$ and $-15x$ cancel when you expand. $(3x - 5)^2$ would have a middle term $-30x$.

@practice num
prompt: For what value of $k$ does $x^2 + 6x + k = 0$ have exactly one solution?
answer: 9
check: root(lambda k: 6**2 - 4*1*k, -100, 100)
hint: Exactly one solution happens when the discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$ equals zero.
hint: Here $a = 1$, $b = 6$ and $c = k$. Solve $36 - 4k = 0$.
solution: The discriminant is $36 - 4k$, which is zero when $k = 9$. Then $x^2 + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)^2 = 0$ has the single solution $x = -3$.

@quiz list
prompt: Solve $3x^2 = 5x + 2$. Enter both solutions, separated by a comma.
answer: -1/3, 2
check: allroots(lambda x: 3*x**2 - (5*x + 2), -100, 100)
hint: Get zero on one side first: subtract $5x$ and $2$ from both sides.
hint: You have $3x^2 - 5x - 2 = 0$. Use the ac method: find two numbers with product $-6$ and sum $-5$.
solution: $3x^2 - 6x + x - 2 = 3x(x - 2) + (x - 2) = (3x + 1)(x - 2) = 0$, so $x = -\frac{1}{3}$ or $x = 2$. Check $x = 2$: $3(4) = 12$ and $5(2) + 2 = 12$.

@quiz num
prompt: The height in meters of a ball $t$ seconds after it is thrown is $h = 20t - 5t^2$. At what time $t > 0$ does the ball return to the ground, where $h = 0$?
answer: 4
unit: s
check: root(lambda t: 20*t - 5*t**2, 1, 100)
hint: Set $h = 0$ and take out the greatest common factor.
hint: $20t - 5t^2 = 5t(4 - t)$. Use the zero-product property and keep the solution with $t > 0$.
solution: $5t(4 - t) = 0$ gives $t = 0$ (the moment of the throw) or $t = 4$. The ball returns to the ground after $4$ seconds.
