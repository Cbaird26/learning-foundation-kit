@article
A mass $m$ (kg) on a spring of stiffness $k$ (N/m) with friction coefficient $c$ (N s/m) obeys Newton's second law:
$$m\,x'' + c\,x' + k\,x = 0,$$
where $x(t)$ is the displacement from equilibrium in meters. This is a second order, linear, **homogeneous**, constant-coefficient equation. Divide by $m$ and it has the form $y'' + by' + cy = 0$; we solve that form first.

### The key idea

Exponentials survive differentiation, so try $y = e^{rt}$. Then $y' = re^{rt}$ and $y'' = r^2e^{rt}$, and the equation becomes
$$(r^2 + br + c)\,e^{rt} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r^2 + br + c = 0.$$
This is the **characteristic equation**. Every root $r$ gives a solution $e^{rt}$.

### Superposition and initial conditions

The equation is linear and homogeneous, so if $y_1$ and $y_2$ are solutions, so is $C_1y_1 + C_2y_2$. Two independent solutions give the **general solution**. Two constants need two conditions, so an IVP specifies the position $y(0)$ and the velocity $y'(0)$.

### Three cases

| Roots | General solution |
|---|---|
| distinct real $r_1 \ne r_2$ | $C_1e^{r_1t} + C_2e^{r_2t}$ |
| repeated real $r$ | $(C_1 + C_2t)\,e^{rt}$ |
| complex $\alpha \pm i\beta$ | $e^{\alpha t}\left(C_1\cos\beta t + C_2\sin\beta t\right)$ |

The complex case comes from Euler's formula $e^{i\beta t} = \cos\beta t + i\sin\beta t$: the real and imaginary parts of $e^{(\alpha + i\beta)t}$ are themselves solutions.

### Damping regimes

For $mx'' + cx' + kx = 0$ the roots are $r = \dfrac{-c \pm \sqrt{c^2 - 4mk}}{2m}$, and the discriminant decides the motion.

- **Overdamped**, $c^2 > 4mk$: two negative real roots. The mass creeps back without oscillating.
- **Critically damped**, $c^2 = 4mk$: a repeated negative root $-c/(2m)$. The fastest return to equilibrium with no oscillation.
- **Underdamped**, $c^2 < 4mk$: complex roots with real part $-c/(2m)$. The mass oscillates at the damped frequency
$$\omega_d = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m} - \left(\frac{c}{2m}\right)^2}$$
inside the decaying envelope $e^{-ct/(2m)}$.
- **Undamped**, $c = 0$: pure oscillation at $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$.

In every damped case the real parts of the roots are negative, so all solutions tend to $0$: the motion is a **transient**.

@example
title: An overdamped initial value problem
problem: Solve $x'' + 5x' + 6x = 0$ with $x(0) = 1$ and $x'(0) = 0$.
step: The characteristic equation is $r^2 + 5r + 6 = (r+2)(r+3) = 0$, so $r = -2$ and $r = -3$. These are distinct and real, so the motion is overdamped.
step: The general solution is $x = C_1e^{-2t} + C_2e^{-3t}$, so $x' = -2C_1e^{-2t} - 3C_2e^{-3t}$.
step: Apply the conditions at $t = 0$: $C_1 + C_2 = 1$ and $-2C_1 - 3C_2 = 0$.
step: The second equation gives $C_1 = -\frac32 C_2$. Substituting, $-\frac12 C_2 = 1$, so $C_2 = -2$ and $C_1 = 3$.
step: $x = 3e^{-2t} - 2e^{-3t}$. Check: $x(0) = 1$ and $x'(0) = -6 + 6 = 0$.

@example
title: An underdamped initial value problem
problem: Classify $x'' + 2x' + 10x = 0$ and solve it with $x(0) = 0$, $x'(0) = 3$.
step: Here $m = 1$, $c = 2$, $k = 10$, so $c^2 - 4mk = 4 - 40 < 0$: underdamped.
step: The roots are $r = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{-36}}{2} = -1 \pm 3i$, so $\alpha = -1$ and $\beta = 3$. The general solution is $x = e^{-t}\left(C_1\cos 3t + C_2\sin 3t\right)$.
step: $x(0) = C_1 = 0$, so $x = C_2e^{-t}\sin 3t$.
step: By the product rule $x' = C_2e^{-t}(-\sin 3t + 3\cos 3t)$, so $x'(0) = 3C_2 = 3$ and $C_2 = 1$.
step: $x = e^{-t}\sin 3t$: oscillation with angular frequency $3$ under the envelope $e^{-t}$.

@mistakes
- Getting the signs of the characteristic equation wrong: $y'' - 3y' + 2y = 0$ gives $r^2 - 3r + 2 = 0$.
- For a repeated root, writing $C_1e^{rt} + C_2e^{rt}$; the second solution is $te^{rt}$.
- In the complex case, forgetting the factor $e^{\alpha t}$ or using $\alpha + \beta$ inside the cosine instead of $\beta$ alone.
- Differentiating $(C_1 + C_2t)e^{rt}$ or $e^{\alpha t}\sin\beta t$ without the product rule when applying $y'(0)$.

@practice list
prompt: Find all values of $r$ for which $y = e^{rt}$ solves $y'' - y' - 6y = 0$.
answer: -2, 3
check: allroots(lambda r: r**2 - r - 6, -10, 10)
hint: Substitute $y = e^{rt}$ and divide by $e^{rt}$.
hint: You get $r^2 - r - 6 = 0$. Factor it.
solution: $r^2 - r - 6 = (r-3)(r+2) = 0$, so $r = 3$ or $r = -2$.

@practice num
prompt: Solve $y'' + 4y = 0$ with $y(0) = 0$ and $y'(0) = 2$, and give $y(\pi/4)$.
answer: 1
check: np.ravel(ivp(lambda t, y: [y[1], -4*y[0]], [0, 2], 0, pi/4))[0]
hint: The characteristic equation is $r^2 + 4 = 0$, with roots $r = \pm 2i$.
hint: $y = C_1\cos 2t + C_2\sin 2t$. The condition $y(0) = 0$ kills one term.
solution: $y = C_1\cos 2t + C_2\sin 2t$. $y(0) = C_1 = 0$, and $y'(0) = 2C_2 = 2$ gives $C_2 = 1$. So $y = \sin 2t$ and $y(\pi/4) = \sin(\pi/2) = 1$.

@practice expr
prompt: Solve $y'' + 2y' + 5y = 0$ with $y(0) = 1$ and $y'(0) = 1$.
vars: t
lead: $y(t) =$
answer: e^(-t)*(cos(2t) + sin(2t))
check: lambda t: np.ravel(ivp(lambda s, y: [y[1], -2*y[1] - 5*y[0]], [1, 1], 0, t))[0]
hint: The roots of $r^2 + 2r + 5 = 0$ are complex. Write them as $\alpha \pm i\beta$.
hint: $r = -1 \pm 2i$, so $y = e^{-t}(C_1\cos 2t + C_2\sin 2t)$. Use the product rule for $y'(0)$.
solution: $y = e^{-t}(C_1\cos 2t + C_2\sin 2t)$ and $y(0) = C_1 = 1$. Also $y'(0) = -C_1 + 2C_2 = 1$, so $C_2 = 1$. Hence $y = e^{-t}(\cos 2t + \sin 2t)$.

@practice num
prompt: A critically damped system satisfies $x'' + 6x' + 9x = 0$ with $x(0) = 0$ and $x'(0) = 1$. At what time $t > 0$ is the displacement largest? Give an exact value.
answer: 1/3
check: root(lambda t: np.ravel(ivp(lambda s, y: [y[1], -6*y[1] - 9*y[0]], [0, 1], 0, t))[1], 0.05, 2)
hint: The characteristic equation $r^2 + 6r + 9 = 0$ has the repeated root $r = -3$.
hint: $x = (C_1 + C_2t)e^{-3t}$. Find $C_1$, $C_2$, then solve $x'(t) = 0$.
solution: $x = (C_1 + C_2t)e^{-3t}$ with $x(0) = C_1 = 0$ and $x'(0) = C_2 - 3C_1 = 1$, so $x = te^{-3t}$. Then $x' = (1 - 3t)e^{-3t} = 0$ at $t = \frac13$, and the displacement there is $\frac{1}{3e}$.
