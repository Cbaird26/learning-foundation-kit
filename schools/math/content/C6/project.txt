### Key results week by week

- **Week 1, first order.** A solution satisfies both the equation and the initial condition. For $y' = f(y)$ the equilibria are the zeros of $f$, and stability follows from the sign of $f$ on each side. Uniqueness needs $f$ and $f_y$ continuous, but a solution may exist only on a short interval ($y' = y^2$ blows up).
- **Week 2, separable and linear.** Separable: $\int dy/h(y) = \int g\,dt$. Linear: $y' + py = q$ with $\mu = e^{\int p}$ and $(\mu y)' = \mu q$. For linear equations the interval of validity comes from the continuity of $p$ and $q$ around $t_0$.
- **Week 3, second order.** Solve $r^2 + br + c = 0$: distinct real, repeated ($te^{rt}$) or complex ($e^{\alpha t}\cos\beta t$, $e^{\alpha t}\sin\beta t$). The sign of $c^2 - 4mk$ gives the damping regime.
- **Week 4, forcing.** $y = y_h + y_p$, with initial conditions applied to the sum. Sinusoidal forcing needs both $\cos$ and $\sin$ in the trial. The transient decays at the slowest root's rate; the steady state remains.
- **Week 5, resonance.** If the trial duplicates a homogeneous solution, multiply by $t$. Damped amplitude: $A = 1/\sqrt{(k - \omega^2)^2 + b^2\omega^2}$, with peak at $\sqrt{k - b^2/2}$.
- **Week 6, variation of parameters.** $y_p = -y_1\int y_2f/W + y_2\int y_1f/W$, with $f$ in standard form.
- **Weeks 7 and 8, Laplace.** Derivative rules, the shift $F(s - a)$, partial fractions; $\mathcal{L}\{u(t - c)g(t - c)\} = e^{-cs}G(s)$; $\delta$ gives a jump in velocity.
- **Weeks 9 and 10, systems.** Eigenpairs build $\mathbf{x}' = A\mathbf{x}$ solutions; classify with $\tau$ and $\Delta$; stable exactly when $\tau < 0$ and $\Delta > 0$.
- **Week 11, nonlinear.** Jacobian at each equilibrium; decisive unless an eigenvalue has real part zero; Lyapunov functions otherwise.
- **Week 12, numerics.** Global error $O(h^p)$, so halving $h$ divides error by $2^p$; Euler on $y' = -\lambda y$ is stable iff $h < 2/\lambda$.

### Most common errors

- Fitting initial conditions to $y_h$ before adding $y_p$.
- Dropping the $-y(0)$ and $-y'(0)$ terms in Laplace transforms.
- Reporting an interval of validity that does not contain $t_0$.
- Calling a center asymptotically stable, or trusting linearization at zero real part.

prompt: A $1$ kg cart on a spring with $k = 13$ N/m and damping coefficient $c = 4$ N s/m rests at $x = 0$. At $t = 0$ a constant force of $26$ N is applied, so that $x'' + 4x' + 13x = 26$ with $x(0) = 0$ and $x'(0) = 0$ ($x$ in metres). The cart overshoots its final position. Find the first maximum value of $x$ as an exact value.
answer: 2*(1 + e^(-2*pi/3))
unit: m
check: (lambda g: np.ravel(ivp(g, [0, 0], 0, root(lambda t: np.ravel(ivp(g, [0, 0], 0, t))[1], 0.5, 2)))[0])(lambda t, y: [y[1], 26 - 4*y[1] - 13*y[0]])
hint: Find the constant particular solution, and the roots of $r^2 + 4r + 13 = 0$.
hint: $x = 2 + e^{-2t}(C_1\cos 3t + C_2\sin 3t)$. Apply both initial conditions to this whole expression, then find where $x' = 0$.
solution: The particular solution is $x_p = 26/13 = 2$ and the roots are $-2 \pm 3i$, so $x = 2 + e^{-2t}(C_1\cos 3t + C_2\sin 3t)$. From $x(0) = 0$: $C_1 = -2$. From $x'(0) = -2C_1 + 3C_2 = 0$: $C_2 = -\frac43$. Then $x' = \frac{26}{3}e^{-2t}\sin 3t$, which first vanishes at $t = \pi/3$. There $x = 2 - e^{-2\pi/3}(2\cos\pi) = 2\left(1 + e^{-2\pi/3}\right) \approx 2.246$ m, an overshoot of about $12\%$ above the final position $2$ m.

@exam num
prompt: A damped pendulum obeys $\theta'' + 0.5\theta' + 4\sin\theta = 0$. With $x = \theta$ and $y = \theta'$ it is the system $x' = y$, $y' = -4\sin x - 0.5y$. Linearizing at the hanging equilibrium $(0,0)$ gives $\mathbf{u}' = J\mathbf{u}$. Euler's method with step $h$ applied to this linear system is $\mathbf{u}_{n+1} = (I + hJ)\mathbf{u}_n$, and the iterates decay to $\mathbf{0}$ exactly when every eigenvalue of $I + hJ$ has modulus less than $1$. Find the boundary value $h^*$ of the stable step sizes: the iterates decay for every $0 < h < h^*$ and not for any $h > h^*$. Give an exact value.
answer: 1/8
check: root(lambda h: max(abs(np.linalg.eigvals(np.eye(2) + h*np.array([[0, 1], [nderiv(lambda x: -4*sin(x), 0), -0.5]])))) - 1, 0.01, 1)
hint: Find $J$ and its eigenvalues $\lambda$ (they are complex). The eigenvalues of $I + hJ$ are $1 + h\lambda$.
hint: For a complex pair, $|1 + h\lambda|^2 = 1 + h\,\operatorname{tr}J + h^2\det J$.
solution: $J = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1\\ -4 & -0.5\end{pmatrix}$ has trace $-0.5$ and determinant $4$, with complex eigenvalues. For $\lambda = \alpha \pm i\beta$, $|1 + h\lambda|^2 = (1 + h\alpha)^2 + h^2\beta^2 = 1 + 2h\alpha + h^2(\alpha^2 + \beta^2) = 1 + h\operatorname{tr}J + h^2\det J = 1 - 0.5h + 4h^2$. This is less than $1$ when $4h^2 < 0.5h$, i.e. $0 < h < \frac18$. At $h = \frac18$ the modulus is exactly $1$, and for $h > \frac18$ it exceeds $1$. Because the inequality is strict there is no largest stable step; the boundary value is $h^* = \frac18$.

@exam self
prompt: A unit mass on a spring with $k = 4$ and no damping, starting at rest, is pushed by a force pulse of unit size and duration $T$: $y'' + 4y = u(t) - u(t - T)$ with $y(0) = 0$ and $y'(0) = 0$. (a) Use Laplace transforms to find $y(t)$ for $t \ge T$ and simplify it to a single sinusoid. (b) What is the amplitude of the oscillation after the pulse, as a function of $T$? (c) For which pulse durations does the mass end at rest, and why?
hint: Transform both sides. You will need $\dfrac{1}{s(s^2 + 4)} = \dfrac14\left(\dfrac1s - \dfrac{s}{s^2 + 4}\right)$ and the second shift rule.
hint: For $t \ge T$ both step terms are on. Use $\cos A - \cos B = -2\sin\frac{A+B}{2}\sin\frac{A-B}{2}$.
solution: Transforming, $(s^2 + 4)Y = \dfrac{1 - e^{-Ts}}{s}$, so $Y = (1 - e^{-Ts})\cdot\frac14\left(\frac1s - \frac{s}{s^2+4}\right)$. With $g(t) = \frac14(1 - \cos 2t)$ we get $y = g(t) - u(t - T)g(t - T)$. (a) For $t \ge T$: $y = \frac14\left[\cos 2(t - T) - \cos 2t\right] = \frac12\sin T\,\sin(2t - T)$. (b) The amplitude is $\frac12|\sin T|$; for instance $T = \pi/2$ leaves an oscillation of amplitude $\frac12$. (c) The mass ends at rest when $\sin T = 0$, i.e. $T = \pi, 2\pi, \dots$, whole multiples of the natural period $2\pi/2 = \pi$. Then the pulse's end (a second step of opposite sign, delayed by whole periods) produces exactly the oscillation that cancels the one produced at its start.
rubric: Sets up the transform correctly, $Y = (1 - e^{-Ts})/(s(s^2 + 4))$, and uses the partial fractions to get $g(t) = \frac14(1 - \cos 2t)$.
rubric: Applies the second shift rule to write $y = g(t) - u(t - T)g(t - T)$ and simplifies for $t \ge T$ to $\frac12\sin T\sin(2t - T)$.
rubric: States the amplitude $\frac12|\sin T|$ and checks a special case.
rubric: Identifies $T$ equal to a whole multiple of the natural period $\pi$ as the durations that leave the mass at rest, with a physical explanation.
