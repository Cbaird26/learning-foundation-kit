@lesson
title: Line integrals
summary: Compute line integrals of scalar functions with respect to arc length and of vector fields along oriented curves, and interpret them as mass, work and circulation.

@article
A particle moves along a curve $C$ through a force field. How much work does the field do? A wire has varying density. What is its mass? Both questions are answered by a **line integral**: we add up small contributions along a curve.

### Integral with respect to arc length

For a scalar function $f$ on a curve $C$ parametrized by $\mathbf r(t)$, $a\le t\le b$,

$$\int_C f\,ds=\int_a^b f(\mathbf r(t))\,|\mathbf r'(t)|\,dt .$$

Here $ds=|\mathbf r'(t)|\,dt$ is a small piece of arc length (Week 1). With $f=1$ the integral is the length of $C$. With $f$ a density it is the mass of a wire.

### Work and circulation

For a vector field $\mathbf F$, only the part of $\mathbf F$ along the direction of motion does work. The **line integral of $\mathbf F$ along $C$** is

$$\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\int_a^b\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\,dt=\int_C P\,dx+Q\,dy+R\,dz .$$

It adds up $\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf T\,ds$, the tangential component of the field times the arc length. If $\mathbf F$ is a force, this is the **work**. If $C$ is a closed curve it is called the **circulation** and is written $\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r$.

### Procedure

1. Parametrize $C$, including its direction.
2. Substitute $x(t),y(t),z(t)$ into the integrand.
3. For a scalar integral multiply by $|\mathbf r'(t)|$; for a vector integral dot $\mathbf F$ with $\mathbf r'(t)$.
4. Integrate over the parameter interval. For a path made of several pieces, add the pieces.

### Orientation and parametrization

Any parametrization that traces the curve once in the same direction gives the same answer. Reversing the direction changes the sign of $\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r$, because $\mathbf r'$ changes sign. The scalar integral $\int_C f\,ds$ does not change, because arc length is positive.

### Does the path matter?

Unlike a one-variable integral, the answer may depend on which path joins two points, not only on the endpoints. The second example below shows this. Next week we find exactly when it does not happen.

@example
title: The mass of a helical wire
problem: A wire follows the helix $\mathbf r(t)=\langle\cos t,\ \sin t,\ t\rangle$ for $0\le t\le 2\pi$. Its density at height $z$ is $\delta=z$. Find its mass $\int_C\delta\,ds$.
step: Differentiate: $\mathbf r'(t)=\langle-\sin t,\ \cos t,\ 1\rangle$, so $|\mathbf r'(t)|=\sqrt2$ and $ds=\sqrt2\,dt$.
step: On the wire $z=t$, so the density is $\delta(\mathbf r(t))=t$.
step: The mass is $\int_0^{2\pi}t\,\sqrt2\,dt=\sqrt2\cdot\dfrac{(2\pi)^2}{2}=2\sqrt2\,\pi^2$.

@example
title: Work that depends on the path
problem: Let $\mathbf F=\langle -y,\ x\rangle$. Compute $\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r$ from $(1,0)$ to $(0,1)$ along (a) the quarter circle and (b) the straight segment.
step: (a) Take $\mathbf r(t)=\langle\cos t,\sin t\rangle$, $0\le t\le\pi/2$. Then $\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))=\langle-\sin t,\cos t\rangle$ and $\mathbf r'(t)=\langle-\sin t,\cos t\rangle$.
step: The dot product is $\sin^2t+\cos^2t=1$, so the work along the arc is $\int_0^{\pi/2}1\,dt=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
step: (b) Take $\mathbf r(t)=\langle1-t,\ t\rangle$, $0\le t\le 1$. Then $\mathbf F=\langle -t,\ 1-t\rangle$ and $\mathbf r'=\langle-1,\ 1\rangle$.
step: The dot product is $t+(1-t)=1$, so the work along the segment is $\int_0^1 1\,dt=1$.
step: The endpoints are the same, but $\pi/2\ne 1$. For this field the line integral depends on the path.

@mistakes
- Forgetting the factor $|\mathbf r'(t)|$ in a scalar line integral, or inserting it in a work integral, where $\mathbf r'(t)$ appears inside a dot product instead.
- Ignoring the direction of travel: reversing a path changes the sign of the work.
- Substituting the curve into $\mathbf F$ but forgetting to dot with $\mathbf r'(t)$, that is, forgetting $dx=x'(t)\,dt$.
- Assuming that two paths with the same endpoints give the same work. In general they do not.

@practice num
prompt: Let $\mathbf F(x,y)=\langle 2xy,\ x^2\rangle$ and let $C$ be the curve $\mathbf r(t)=\langle t,\ t^2\rangle$ for $0\le t\le 2$. Evaluate $\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r$.
answer: 16
check: quad(lambda t: dot([2*t*t**2, t**2], [nderiv(lambda s: s, t), nderiv(lambda s: s**2, t)]), 0, 2)
hint: Substitute $x=t$, $y=t^2$ into $\mathbf F$, and compute $\mathbf r'(t)$.
hint: $\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))=\langle 2t^3,\ t^2\rangle$ and $\mathbf r'(t)=\langle 1,\ 2t\rangle$.
solution: $\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=2t^3+2t^3=4t^3$, and $\int_0^2 4t^3\,dt=16$.

@practice num
prompt: Evaluate $\int_C (x+y)\,ds$, where $C$ is the straight segment from $(0,0)$ to $(3,4)$.
answer: 35/2
check: quad(lambda t: (3*t + 4*t)*norm([nderiv(lambda s: 3*s, t), nderiv(lambda s: 4*s, t)]), 0, 1)
hint: Parametrize with $\mathbf r(t)=\langle 3t,\ 4t\rangle$, $0\le t\le 1$, and find $|\mathbf r'(t)|$.
hint: $|\mathbf r'(t)|=5$ and $x+y=7t$ along the segment.
solution: $\int_0^1 7t\cdot 5\,dt=35\cdot\frac12=\frac{35}{2}$.

@practice mc
prompt: Let $-C$ be the curve $C$ traversed in the opposite direction. Which statement is correct?
choice*: $\int_{-C}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=-\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r$, while $\int_{-C}f\,ds=\int_C f\,ds$.
choice: Both $\int_{-C}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r$ and $\int_{-C}f\,ds$ change sign.
choice: Neither integral changes when the direction is reversed.
choice: $\int_{-C}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r$ is unchanged, but $\int_{-C}f\,ds$ changes sign.
hint: Reversing the direction replaces $\mathbf r'(t)$ by $-\mathbf r'(t)$ in the new parametrization. What happens to $|\mathbf r'(t)|$?
solution: Reversing direction flips the velocity, so the dot product $\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'$ changes sign and the work changes sign. The speed $|\mathbf r'|$, and therefore $ds$, stays positive, so the scalar integral is unchanged.

@practice num
prompt: Evaluate $\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r$ for $\mathbf F=\langle y,\ z,\ x\rangle$ along the helix $\mathbf r(t)=\langle\cos t,\ \sin t,\ t\rangle$, $0\le t\le 2\pi$. Give an exact answer.
answer: -pi
check: quad(lambda t: dot([sin(t), t, cos(t)], [nderiv(lambda s: cos(s), t), nderiv(lambda s: sin(s), t), nderiv(lambda s: s, t)]), 0, 2*pi)
hint: Substitute $x=\cos t$, $y=\sin t$, $z=t$ into $\mathbf F$, and dot with $\mathbf r'(t)=\langle-\sin t,\cos t,1\rangle$.
hint: The integrand is $-\sin^2 t+t\cos t+\cos t$. Integrate the three terms separately; use integration by parts for $t\cos t$.
solution: $\int_0^{2\pi}(-\sin^2t)\,dt=-\pi$. Also $\int_0^{2\pi}t\cos t\,dt=\left[t\sin t+\cos t\right]_0^{2\pi}=0$ and $\int_0^{2\pi}\cos t\,dt=0$. The total is $-\pi$.

@practice num
prompt: Find the circulation $\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r$ of $\mathbf F=\langle -y,\ x\rangle$ around the triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$, traversed counterclockwise.
answer: 1
check: (lambda seg: seg((0, 0), (1, 0)) + seg((1, 0), (0, 1)) + seg((0, 1), (0, 0)))(lambda A, B: quad(lambda t: dot([-(A[1] + t*(B[1] - A[1])), A[0] + t*(B[0] - A[0])], [B[0] - A[0], B[1] - A[1]]), 0, 1))
hint: Split $C$ into three segments and parametrize each one as $P+t(Q-P)$.
hint: On the leg along the $x$-axis $\mathbf F=\langle 0,x\rangle$ is perpendicular to the motion, and the same holds on the leg along the $y$-axis. Only the hypotenuse contributes.
solution: On the legs along the axes, $\mathbf F\perp d\mathbf r$, so those integrals are $0$. On the hypotenuse $\mathbf r=\langle 1-t,t\rangle$ we get $\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=t+(1-t)=1$, so the integral is $1$. The circulation is $1$, which is twice the area of the triangle.

@quiz num
prompt: Evaluate $\int_C xy\,ds$, where $C$ is the quarter of the circle $x^2+y^2=4$ in the first quadrant.
answer: 4
check: quad(lambda t: 2*cos(t)*2*sin(t)*norm([nderiv(lambda s: 2*cos(s), t), nderiv(lambda s: 2*sin(s), t)]), 0, pi/2)
hint: Use $\mathbf r(t)=\langle 2\cos t,\ 2\sin t\rangle$ for $0\le t\le\pi/2$.
hint: Here $ds=2\,dt$ and $xy=4\sin t\cos t$.
solution: $\int_0^{\pi/2}4\sin t\cos t\cdot 2\,dt=8\cdot\frac12=4$.

@quiz num
prompt: Find the work done by $\mathbf F=\langle x^2,\ xy\rangle$ along the parabola $y=x^2$ from $(0,0)$ to $(1,1)$. Give an exact answer.
answer: 11/15
check: quad(lambda t: dot([t**2, t*t**2], [nderiv(lambda s: s, t), nderiv(lambda s: s**2, t)]), 0, 1)
hint: Parametrize with $\mathbf r(t)=\langle t,t^2\rangle$, $0\le t\le 1$.
hint: $\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))=\langle t^2,\ t^3\rangle$ and $\mathbf r'(t)=\langle 1,2t\rangle$.
solution: $\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=t^2+2t^4$, so the work is $\frac13+\frac25=\frac{11}{15}$.
