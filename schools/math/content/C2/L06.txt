@article
A **triple integral** $\iiint_E f\,dV$ adds up $f\,\Delta V$ over small pieces of a solid $E$. With $f=1$ it gives volume. With $f$ a density it gives mass. Divided by the volume, it gives the average value of $f$.

### Cartesian iterated integrals

Describe $E$ as the set of points above a region $D$ in the $xy$-plane (its shadow), between a lower surface and an upper surface:

$$\iiint_E f\,dV=\iint_D\left(\int_{z_1(x,y)}^{z_2(x,y)}f\,dz\right)dA .$$

Then $D$ is handled exactly as in the last lesson. At every stage the outer limits are constants and each inner limit depends only on variables outside it.

### Cylindrical coordinates

Use polar coordinates in the $xy$-plane and keep $z$: $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$, $z=z$. The volume element is

$$dV=r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta .$$

### Spherical coordinates

Let $\rho$ be the distance to the origin, $\phi\in[0,\pi]$ the angle measured down from the positive $z$-axis, and $\theta$ the same angle as in cylindrical coordinates:

$$x=\rho\sin\phi\cos\theta,\quad y=\rho\sin\phi\sin\theta,\quad z=\rho\cos\phi,\qquad dV=\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta .$$

This is the convention used in this course; some books swap the names $\phi$ and $\theta$, so always check which angle is which.

### Choosing coordinates

| Shape of the solid | Good choice |
|---|---|
| Boxes, regions bounded by planes | Cartesian |
| Cylinders, paraboloids $z=x^2+y^2$, cones around the $z$-axis | Cylindrical |
| Balls, spherical shells, cones with the vertex at the origin | Spherical |

### Justifying bounds

For each variable ask three questions. Which surfaces bound it? Do those bounds depend on variables further out? Does the bound cover the whole solid, or does it only work over part of it? Sketching the shadow in the $xy$-plane answers the second and third questions. For example, the cone $z=r$ and the sphere $\rho=3$ meet at $r=3/\sqrt2$, so a cylindrical description of the region between them must stop at $r=3/\sqrt2$, not at $r=3$.

@example
title: Volume in cylindrical coordinates
problem: Find the volume of the solid between the paraboloid $z=x^2+y^2$ and the plane $z=4$.
step: The solid has circular symmetry about the $z$-axis and the paraboloid is $z=r^2$, so use cylindrical coordinates.
step: The surfaces meet where $r^2=4$, so the shadow is the disk $0\le r\le 2$, with $0\le\theta\le 2\pi$. Over each point of the shadow, $z$ runs from the paraboloid $r^2$ up to the plane $4$.
step: $V=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^2\int_{r^2}^{4}r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^2 r(4-r^2)\,dr\,d\theta$.
step: The inner integral is $\left[2r^2-\tfrac{r^4}{4}\right]_0^2=8-4=4$, so $V=2\pi\cdot 4=8\pi$.

@example
title: An integral over a half-ball in spherical coordinates
problem: Evaluate $\iiint_E z\,dV$, where $E$ is the upper half of the unit ball $x^2+y^2+z^2\le 1$, $z\ge 0$.
step: A ball centered at the origin suggests spherical coordinates. The upper half means $0\le\rho\le 1$, $0\le\phi\le\pi/2$ and $0\le\theta\le 2\pi$.
step: The integrand is $z=\rho\cos\phi$, so the integral is $\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2}\int_0^1(\rho\cos\phi)\,\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta$.
step: The integral separates into three one-variable integrals: $\int_0^1\rho^3\,d\rho=\tfrac14$, $\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos\phi\sin\phi\,d\phi=\tfrac12$ and $\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta=2\pi$.
step: The result is $2\pi\cdot\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac12=\tfrac{\pi}{4}$.

@mistakes
- Forgetting the Jacobian factor: $r$ in cylindrical coordinates, $\rho^2\sin\phi$ in spherical coordinates.
- Treating $\phi$ as the angle in the $xy$-plane, or letting it run from $0$ to $2\pi$. In this course $\phi$ is measured from the positive $z$-axis and runs from $0$ to $\pi$.
- Using the radius of a sphere as the $r$-limit of a region that only reaches part of the way, such as the region above a cone.
- Choosing Cartesian coordinates by default for a solid with axis or central symmetry.

@practice num
prompt: Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^1\int_0^2\int_0^3 (x+yz)\,dz\,dy\,dx$.
answer: 12
check: quad3(lambda x, y, z: x + y*z, 0, 1, 0, 2, 0, 3)
hint: Integrate in $z$ first, treating $x$ and $y$ as constants.
hint: The inner integral is $3x+\tfrac92y$. Then integrate in $y$, then in $x$.
solution: $\int_0^3(x+yz)\,dz=3x+\frac92y$; then $\int_0^2\left(3x+\frac92y\right)dy=6x+9$; then $\int_0^1(6x+9)\,dx=3+9=12$.

@practice num
prompt: Find the volume of the tetrahedron bounded by the three coordinate planes and the plane $x+2y+3z=6$.
answer: 6
check: quad(lambda x: quad(lambda y: (6 - x - 2*y)/3, 0, (6 - x)/2), 0, 6)
hint: The solid lies under the plane $z=(6-x-2y)/3$. Its shadow is the triangle where $z\ge 0$.
hint: The shadow has $0\le x\le 6$ and $0\le y\le (6-x)/2$.
solution: $V=\int_0^6\int_0^{(6-x)/2}\frac{6-x-2y}{3}\,dy\,dx$. The inner integral is $\frac{(6-x)^2}{12}$, and $\int_0^6\frac{(6-x)^2}{12}\,dx=\frac{216}{36}=6$. Check: perpendicular edges of lengths $6$, $3$, $2$ give $\frac{6\cdot 3\cdot 2}{6}=6$.

@practice mc
prompt: A solid lies above the cone $z=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ and inside the sphere $x^2+y^2+z^2=9$. Which description of its volume integral uses correct coordinates and bounds?
choice*: Spherical: $0\le\rho\le 3$, $0\le\phi\le\pi/4$, $0\le\theta\le 2\pi$.
choice: Spherical: $0\le\rho\le 3$, $0\le\phi\le\pi/2$, $0\le\theta\le 2\pi$.
choice: Spherical: $0\le\rho\le 3$, $\pi/4\le\phi\le\pi/2$, $0\le\theta\le 2\pi$.
choice: Cylindrical: $0\le r\le 3$, $r\le z\le\sqrt{9-r^2}$, $0\le\theta\le 2\pi$.
hint: On the cone $z=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ we have $z=r$, so the angle from the $z$-axis satisfies $\tan\phi=r/z=1$.
hint: Points above the cone are closer to the $z$-axis than the cone is, so they have a smaller $\phi$. Also check where the cone meets the sphere.
solution: The cone is $\phi=\pi/4$ and the solid above it is $0\le\phi\le\pi/4$, with $\rho$ from $0$ to the sphere at $3$. The spherical choice with $0\le\phi\le\pi/2$ is the whole upper half-ball. The spherical choice with $\pi/4\le\phi\le\pi/2$ is the region below the cone. The cylindrical choice fails because the cone meets the sphere at $r=3/\sqrt2$, and for $r$ between $3/\sqrt2$ and $3$ the lower bound $r$ exceeds the upper bound $\sqrt{9-r^2}$.

@practice num
prompt: Find the volume of the part of the ball $x^2+y^2+z^2\le 4$ that lies outside the cylinder $x^2+y^2\le 1$. Give an exact answer.
answer: 4sqrt(3)pi
check: 4/3*pi*2**3 - quad(lambda x: quad(lambda y: 2*sqrt(4 - x*x - y*y), -sqrt(1 - x*x), sqrt(1 - x*x)), -1, 1)
hint: Use cylindrical coordinates. For $1\le r\le 2$, $z$ runs from $-\sqrt{4-r^2}$ to $\sqrt{4-r^2}$.
hint: $V=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_1^2 2\sqrt{4-r^2}\;r\,dr\,d\theta$. Substitute $u=4-r^2$.
solution: $V=2\pi\int_1^2 2r\sqrt{4-r^2}\,dr=2\pi\left[-\tfrac23(4-r^2)^{3/2}\right]_1^2=2\pi\cdot\tfrac23\cdot 3\sqrt3=4\sqrt3\,\pi\approx 21.77$.

@practice self
prompt: A solid lies between the paraboloid $z=x^2+y^2$ (below) and the plane $z=2y$ (above). Choose a coordinate system, set up the volume integral, justify each bound, and evaluate it.
solution: The paraboloid is symmetric about the $z$-axis and equals $z=r^2$, so cylindrical coordinates are natural. The surfaces meet where $r^2=2r\sin\theta$, that is $r=2\sin\theta$, so the shadow is the disk $x^2+(y-1)^2\le 1$. In polar form it is $0\le r\le 2\sin\theta$, and since $r\ge 0$ we need $\sin\theta\ge 0$, so $0\le\theta\le\pi$. Over each point of the shadow, $z$ runs from $r^2$ up to $2r\sin\theta$. Hence $V=\int_0^\pi\int_0^{2\sin\theta}\int_{r^2}^{2r\sin\theta}r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta=\int_0^\pi\int_0^{2\sin\theta}(2r^2\sin\theta-r^3)\,dr\,d\theta=\int_0^\pi\tfrac43\sin^4\theta\,d\theta=\tfrac43\cdot\tfrac{3\pi}{8}=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$.
rubric: Chooses cylindrical coordinates and gives a reason (symmetry about the $z$-axis, $x^2+y^2=r^2$).
rubric: Finds the shadow by equating the two surfaces, gets $r\le 2\sin\theta$, and explains why $\theta$ only runs from $0$ to $\pi$.
rubric: Gives the $z$ bounds $r^2\le z\le 2r\sin\theta$ and includes the factor $r$ in $dV$.
rubric: Evaluates the integral correctly to $\pi/2$.
hint: Equate the two surfaces to find where they meet, then write that curve in polar form.
hint: The curve $r^2=2r\sin\theta$ simplifies to $r=2\sin\theta$. Which $\theta$ values give $r\ge 0$?

@quiz num
prompt: A ball of radius $2$ centered at the origin has density $\delta(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2+z^2$ (arbitrary units). Find its total mass. Give an exact answer.
answer: 128pi/5
check: quad(lambda x: quad(lambda y: quad(lambda z: x*x + y*y + z*z, -sqrt(4 - x*x - y*y), sqrt(4 - x*x - y*y)), -sqrt(4 - x*x), sqrt(4 - x*x)), -2, 2)
hint: Use spherical coordinates, where $x^2+y^2+z^2=\rho^2$.
hint: $M=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^\pi\int_0^2\rho^2\cdot\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta$.
solution: The three integrals separate: $\int_0^2\rho^4\,d\rho=\frac{32}{5}$, $\int_0^\pi\sin\phi\,d\phi=2$ and $\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta=2\pi$. So $M=\frac{32}{5}\cdot 2\cdot 2\pi=\frac{128\pi}{5}$.

@quiz num
prompt: Find the volume of the solid inside the sphere $\rho=2$ and inside the cone $\phi=\pi/3$, that is, the region $0\le\rho\le 2$, $0\le\phi\le\pi/3$ (with $\phi$ measured from the positive $z$-axis). Give an exact answer.
answer: 8pi/3
check: quad2(lambda x, y: sqrt(4 - x*x - y*y) - sqrt(x*x + y*y)/sqrt(3), -sqrt(3), sqrt(3), lambda x: -sqrt(3 - x*x), lambda x: sqrt(3 - x*x))
hint: In spherical coordinates the volume is $\int\!\!\int\!\!\int\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta$ with the stated bounds and $0\le\theta\le 2\pi$.
hint: The three integrals separate. The $\phi$ integral is $\int_0^{\pi/3}\sin\phi\,d\phi=1-\cos(\pi/3)$.
solution: $V=\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta\cdot\int_0^{\pi/3}\sin\phi\,d\phi\cdot\int_0^2\rho^2\,d\rho=2\pi\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac83=\frac{8\pi}{3}$.
