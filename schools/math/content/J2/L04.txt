@article
Multiplication is repeated addition, and that idea explains the sign rules. Division is the reverse of multiplication, so the same rules apply to quotients.

### Why the signs work

Look at the pattern of products as the second factor drops by one each time:

$$3 \cdot 2 = 6,\quad 3 \cdot 1 = 3,\quad 3 \cdot 0 = 0,\quad 3 \cdot (-1) = -3,\quad 3 \cdot (-2) = -6.$$

Each step lowers the product by $3$, so a positive times a negative is **negative**. Now keep going with $-3$ as the first factor:

$$-3 \cdot 2 = -6,\quad -3 \cdot 1 = -3,\quad -3 \cdot 0 = 0,\quad -3 \cdot (-1) = 3,\quad -3 \cdot (-2) = 6.$$

Here each step raises the product by $3$, so a negative times a negative is **positive**.

### The rules

- Same signs give a **positive** result.
- Different signs give a **negative** result.

The same holds for division: $\frac{-12}{4} = -3$, $\frac{-12}{-4} = 3$ and $\frac{12}{-4} = -3$. With several factors, count the negatives. An **even** number of negative factors gives a positive product; an **odd** number gives a negative one.

Zero is special: $0$ times anything is $0$, and you can never divide by $0$.

### Fractions

Multiply numerators together and denominators together, after deciding the sign. To divide by a fraction, multiply by its **reciprocal** (flip it):

$$-\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{9}{10} = -\frac{18}{30} = -\frac{3}{5}, \qquad \frac{1}{2} \div \left(-\frac{3}{4}\right) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \left(-\frac{4}{3}\right) = -\frac{2}{3}.$$

You can simplify before multiplying by canceling common factors.

### Decimals

Multiply or divide as if the signs were not there, place the decimal point, then attach the sign. For $-0.3 \cdot 0.4$, compute $3 \cdot 4 = 12$ with two decimal places, giving $0.12$, and the sign is negative: $-0.12$. For division, multiply top and bottom by a power of $10$ to remove the decimals: $\frac{-2.4}{0.6} = \frac{-24}{6} = -4$.

### Fractions as decimals

Every rational number is a terminating or repeating decimal. Dividing the numerator by the denominator converts it: $\frac{3}{8} = 0.375$ and $\frac{1}{3} = 0.\overline{3}$.

@example
title: Dividing negative fractions
problem: Compute $-\frac{5}{6} \div \left(-\frac{10}{9}\right)$.
step: Dividing means multiplying by the reciprocal: $-\frac{5}{6} \cdot \left(-\frac{9}{10}\right)$.
step: Both factors are negative, so the product is positive.
step: Multiply and cancel: $\frac{5 \cdot 9}{6 \cdot 10} = \frac{45}{60}$.
step: Simplify by dividing by $15$: $\frac{3}{4}$.

@mistakes
- Using the addition rule for signs: writing $(-4)(-5) = -20$ because "two negatives make a bigger negative."
- Flipping the wrong fraction when dividing. Only the divisor (the second fraction) is flipped.
- Forgetting that an odd number of negative factors gives a negative product.
