@article
After the Revolution, the new country had a weak plan of government. In 1787, leaders met in Philadelphia and wrote a new plan called the **Constitution**. It was approved by the states in 1788. The Constitution is the highest law of the United States. It starts with the words "We the People."

### Three branches

The Constitution divides power into three branches so no one group has all of it:

1. The **legislative** branch is **Congress**. It makes laws.
2. The **executive** branch is led by the **president**. It carries out laws.
3. The **judicial** branch is the **courts**. It explains laws and decides if they follow the Constitution.

This idea is called **checks and balances**: each branch can limit the others.

### The Bill of Rights

Some people said the Constitution needed to list rights that the government must protect. In 1791, ten changes, called **amendments**, were added. They are the **Bill of Rights**. Here are a few:

- **First Amendment:** Freedom of religion, speech, the press, to meet in groups and to ask the government to fix problems.
- **Fourth Amendment:** Protects people from unfair searches.
- **Sixth Amendment:** A person charged with a crime has the right to a fair and quick trial.

### Who had rights?

When the Constitution was written, only some people could vote. Most voters were white men who owned property. Enslaved people, Native Americans and women were left out of many rights. Over time, amendments and laws changed this.

@mistakes
- Mixing up Congress (makes laws) with the president (carries them out).
- Thinking the Bill of Rights is a separate country. It is the first ten amendments to the Constitution.
- Thinking everyone had rights from the start. Rights were widened over time.
