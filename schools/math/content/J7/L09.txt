@article
A **natural hazard** is a natural event that can harm people and property. It becomes a **disaster** when it hits where people live, and good preparation reduces the harm.

### Earthquakes

Most earthquakes occur along plate boundaries. Rock builds up stress and then suddenly slips along a **fault**, releasing energy as seismic waves. The point underground where the slip begins is the **focus**; the point on the surface directly above it is the **epicenter**. A **seismograph** records the waves. The **moment magnitude** scale measures an earthquake's size: each increase of 1 in magnitude means the recorded ground motion (amplitude) is **10 times** larger and the energy released is about **32 times** larger ($10^{1.5}$). A magnitude 7 earthquake therefore has 100 times the amplitude of a magnitude 5.

To find the distance to an earthquake, use the gap between the arrival of the fast **P wave** and the slower **S wave**. If P waves travel at 6 km/s and S waves at 3.5 km/s (typical crustal values), then $d = \dfrac{\Delta t}{\frac{1}{3.5} - \frac{1}{6}} = 8.4\,\Delta t$ kilometers, with $\Delta t$ in seconds. Distances from three stations locate the epicenter where three circles meet.

### Volcanoes

Volcanoes form at subduction zones, divergent boundaries and hot spots. Runny, low-silica magma makes gentle eruptions and wide **shield volcanoes**; thick, gas-rich magma makes explosive eruptions and steep **composite volcanoes**. Hazards include lava, ash, hot gas, and mudflows called **lahars**.

### Tsunamis

A **tsunami** is a series of long ocean waves, usually caused by an undersea earthquake that suddenly moves the seafloor, or by a landslide or volcano. In deep water it is low and fast; near shore it slows and grows tall.

### Hurricanes and tornadoes

A **hurricane** (tropical cyclone) is a huge rotating storm that forms over warm ocean water (about 26.5 °C or more). Its hazards are strong winds, heavy rain and **storm surge**, a rise of the sea pushed onto land. The Saffir–Simpson scale rates winds from category 1 (74 to 95 mph) to category 5 (157 mph or more). A **tornado** is a rapidly rotating column of air touching the ground, rated EF0 to EF5 on the Enhanced Fujita scale by the damage it causes.

### Being prepared

A family emergency plan lists the most likely hazards where you live, what to do in each (for earthquakes: **drop, cover and hold on**; for tornadoes: go to the lowest, most interior room; for hurricanes: follow evacuation orders), two meeting places, an out-of-town contact, and a kit with water, food, a flashlight, a radio, medicines and copies of important papers.

@example
title: Distance to an earthquake
problem: At a seismograph station the S wave arrives 15 seconds after the P wave. Using $d = 8.4\,\Delta t$ km, how far away is the earthquake?
step: Identify the time gap: $\Delta t = 15$ s.
step: Substitute into the formula: $d = 8.4 \times 15$.
step: Multiply: $d = 126$ km.
step: Check the reasoning: P waves arrive first, so a bigger gap means a longer distance.

@mistakes
- Thinking magnitude 7 is only a little more than magnitude 6. It has 10 times the ground motion and about 32 times the energy.
- Mixing up focus (underground) and epicenter (on the surface above it).
- Believing a tsunami is a single giant wave. It is a series of waves that can keep arriving for hours.
- Running outside during shaking, or using a doorway. Current advice is drop, cover and hold on.

choice*: Drop to the ground, take cover under a sturdy table, and hold on.
choice: Use the elevator to leave quickly.
hint: Falling objects are the main danger indoors.
solution: Drop, cover and hold on protects you from falling objects until the shaking stops. Elevators should not be used, and running out can expose you to falling debris.

@practice self

Then explain one way the plan reduces harm.
hint: Start by naming the hazard and the first action people should take when the warning or the shaking starts.
solution: A model plan names one hazard (for example, tornado). Action: when a warning sounds, go to the lowest floor in an interior room without windows and cover your head. Meeting places: the mailbox for a house fire, and a relative's house across town for an area-wide event. Contact: an aunt in another city whom everyone can call or text. Kit: water, nonperishable food, flashlight, batteries, radio, first-aid supplies, medicines, copies of important papers, phone charger. The plan reduces harm because everyone knows what to do and how to reunite, so no one has to decide under stress.

@quiz mc

solution: A sudden vertical shift of the seafloor, usually in an undersea earthquake at a subduction zone, displaces a huge volume of water and creates a tsunami. Wind-driven waves and tides are different.
