@article
Stars are enormous balls of hot gas. The Sun is the closest one. Stars shine because of **nuclear fusion** in their cores, where hydrogen nuclei combine to make helium and release huge amounts of energy.

### Birth and life of a star

Stars form in giant clouds of gas and dust called **nebulae**. Gravity pulls the cloud together into a hot, dense ball (a **protostar**). When the core is hot enough, fusion starts and the star becomes a **main-sequence star**, where it spends most of its life (the Sun will for about 10 billion years in total). Massive stars burn fuel much faster and live shorter lives.

### How stars end

- **Low-mass stars like the Sun** swell into **red giants**, blow off their outer layers as a planetary nebula, and leave a **white dwarf**.
- **Massive stars** become red supergiants and explode in **supernovae**, leaving a **neutron star** or, for the most massive, a **black hole**.

The heavy elements in our bodies and in Earth were made inside stars and spread by such explosions.

### Color, temperature and the H-R diagram

A star's color depends on its surface temperature: blue-white stars are hottest, yellow stars (like the Sun) are medium, and red stars are coolest. The **Hertzsprung–Russell (H-R) diagram** plots **luminosity** (how much total light a star gives off) against surface temperature. Most stars lie on a diagonal band, the main sequence; red giants and supergiants lie above it, and white dwarfs lie below.

### Cosmic distances

Space is so large that we use the **light-year (ly)**, the distance light travels in one year, about **$9.46 \times 10^{12}$ km**. The nearest star after the Sun, Proxima Centauri, is about 4.24 ly away. Looking at distant objects is looking into the past: light from a star 100 ly away left it 100 years ago.

### Galaxies and the universe

A **galaxy** is a huge system of stars, gas and dust held together by gravity. Our **Milky Way** is a spiral galaxy about 100,000 ly across with hundreds of billions of stars. The Sun lies in one of its spiral arms. The nearest large galaxy, Andromeda, is about 2.5 million ly away. Galaxies are moving apart, and light from distant ones is stretched to longer, redder wavelengths (**redshift**). This evidence led to the **Big Bang** theory: the universe has been expanding for about 13.8 billion years.

### Scaling space

In a model where 1 AU is 1 m, one light-year is about 63,241 m, because there are about 63,241 AU in a light-year. Distances between stars dwarf distances inside the solar system.

@example
title: Light-years to kilometers
problem: A star is 10 light-years away. How many kilometers is that? Use $1\ \text{ly} = 9.46 \times 10^{12}$ km.
step: Multiply the number of light-years by the length of one: $10 \times 9.46 \times 10^{12}$.
step: $10 \times 9.46 = 94.6$, so the distance is $94.6 \times 10^{12}$ km.
step: Write in standard form: $9.46 \times 10^{13}$ km.

@mistakes
- Treating the light-year as a unit of time. It is a unit of distance.
- Thinking that stars burn by ordinary fire. The energy comes from nuclear fusion.
- Believing that the Sun will become a black hole or a supernova. It is not massive enough; it will become a white dwarf.
- Confusing a galaxy with the solar system or the universe. A galaxy has billions of stars; the universe holds billions of galaxies.
