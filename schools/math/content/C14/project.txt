### Week-by-week

**Weeks 1 to 2.** A topology is a family of sets closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections containing $\emptyset$ and the whole set. Bases are checked with the two conditions (cover, and a smaller basis set inside each intersection around each point). Closure is the smallest closed superset; a point is in the closure iff every open neighborhood meets the set; $\operatorname{Cl} A = A \cup A'$; interior and closure are dual.

**Weeks 3 to 4.** Continuity means open sets pull back to open sets; test by closed sets, closures or a basis. A homeomorphism is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse. Subspace, product and quotient topologies are characterized by universal properties; a quotient map with a continuous section, or a continuous surjection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, is a quotient map.

**Weeks 5 to 7.** Connected subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ are the intervals; continuous images, closures, products and chains of connected sets stay connected; path connected implies connected but not conversely. Compactness: closed subsets, continuous images and finite products of compact spaces are compact; compact subsets of Hausdorff spaces are closed; the tube lemma; Heine-Borel; compact metric spaces are sequentially compact; a continuous bijection from compact to Hausdorff is a homeomorphism.

**Weeks 8 to 10.** First and second countable, separable, Lindelof; T0, T1, Hausdorff, regular, normal; metric spaces are normal; Urysohn's lemma (dyadic construction); the metrization theorem in outline; the Cantor set and its ternary description.

**Weeks 11 to 12.** Homotopy of paths, the groupoid laws, the fundamental group, induced maps, retractions; path and homotopy lifting for $\mathbb{R} \to S^1$, the degree isomorphism $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, no retraction of the disk onto the circle, Brouwer's theorem, the fundamental theorem of algebra.

### Key facts and tools

- Basis criterion: cover, and for $x \in B_1 \cap B_2$ some $B_3$ with $x \in B_3 \subseteq B_1 \cap B_2$.
- Quotient test: $g$ on $X/{\sim}$ is continuous iff $g \circ q$ is.
- Compact $\Rightarrow$ closed needs Hausdorff; closed and bounded $\Rightarrow$ compact needs $\mathbb{R}^n$.
- Urysohn function for a metric space: $d(x, A)/(d(x, A) + d(x, B))$.
- Degree of a loop in $S^1$: the endpoint of the lift starting at $0$.

### Self-check

- Can you decide whether a finite family is a topology or a basis in under a minute?
- Can you prove a space is not homeomorphic to another by a topological property (compactness, connectedness after removing a point)?
- Can you prove each of the lifting lemmas without notes?
- Can you list, for each of the twelve atlas spaces, which implications between properties it breaks?

### Common errors

- Checking only the cover condition of a basis, or demanding that intersections of basis sets be basis sets.
- Taking closed and bounded to mean compact outside $\mathbb{R}^n$.
- Using "compact implies closed" without Hausdorff, and "connected implies path connected."
- Assuming a continuous bijection is a homeomorphism.
- Forgetting that a path homotopy fixes endpoints for every $t$.

@exam self
prompt: Prove that no two of the spaces $\mathbb{R}$, $[0, 1]$ and the circle $S^1$ (each with its standard subspace topology from $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$) are homeomorphic.
hint: Compactness separates $\mathbb{R}$ from the other two. For $[0, 1]$ and $S^1$ delete one point from each and compare connectedness.
solution: The spaces $[0, 1]$ and $S^1$ are compact: $[0, 1]$ is closed and bounded, and $S^1$ is the continuous image of $[0, 1]$ under $t \mapsto e^{2\pi i t}$. The space $\mathbb{R}$ is not compact (the cover by the intervals $(-n, n)$ has no finite subcover), and compactness is preserved by homeomorphisms (continuous images of compact sets are compact), so $\mathbb{R}$ is homeomorphic to neither. Suppose $h\colon [0, 1] \to S^1$ is a homeomorphism and let $z = h(\tfrac12)$. Then $h$ restricts to a homeomorphism from $[0, 1] \setminus \{\tfrac12\}$ onto $S^1 \setminus \{z\}$. The first set equals $[0, \tfrac12) \cup (\tfrac12, 1]$, which is not an interval and hence disconnected (Week 5, Theorem 1). The second is the image of $(0, 1)$ under the continuous map $t \mapsto z e^{2\pi i t}$, hence connected. A homeomorphism preserves connectedness, a contradiction. $\blacksquare$
rubric: Shows $\mathbb{R}$ is not compact and the other two are compact, and that compactness is a topological property.
rubric: Removes a well-chosen point and shows the interval minus an interior point is disconnected.
rubric: Shows the circle minus a point is connected, using a continuous image of an interval, and concludes by comparing connectedness.

answer: 12
check: int(run("from itertools import combinations\nX = frozenset({1, 2, 3})\nP = [frozenset(c) for r in (1, 2) for c in combinations(X, r)]\nn = 0\nfor k in range(len(P) + 1):\n    for c in combinations(P, k):\n        T = set(c) | {frozenset(), X}\n        if all(a | b in T and a & b in T for a in T for b in T):\n            conn = not any(U and U != X and X - U in T for U in T)\n            t0 = all(any((x in U) != (y in U) for U in T) for x in X for y in X if x < y)\n            n += conn and t0\nprint(n)"))
hint: Enumerate the topologies (each is a subfamily of the six proper nonempty subsets together with $\emptyset$ and $X$) and test both properties.
solution: Exhaustive enumeration of the $29$ topologies gives $12$ that are connected and T0, and $7$ that are T0 but disconnected (the others are not T0). The enumeration tests every pair of points for T0 and every open set for being clopen.
