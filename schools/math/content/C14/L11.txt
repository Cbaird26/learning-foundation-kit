@article
A rubber band around a pole cannot be shrunk to a point without leaving the surface it lies on, while a rubber band on a flat table can. Open sets cannot see this, but loops can. Algebraic topology attaches a group to a space by recording the loops that cannot be deformed into each other.

Throughout $I = [0, 1]$.

### Homotopy

Two continuous maps $f, g\colon X \to Y$ are **homotopic**, written $f \simeq g$, if there is a continuous $H\colon X \times I \to Y$ with $H(x, 0) = f(x)$ and $H(x, 1) = g(x)$. For **paths** (continuous maps $I \to X$) with the same endpoints, a **path homotopy** is a homotopy $H\colon I \times I \to X$ with $H(0, t) = f(0)$ and $H(1, t) = f(1)$ for all $t$; we write $f \simeq_p g$. Both relations are equivalence relations: reflexive with $H(x, t) = f(x)$, symmetric with $H(x, 1 - t)$, and transitive by running one homotopy in the first half of $t$-time and the other in the second half (the two pieces agree at $t = \tfrac12$, so the glued map is continuous by the pasting lemma for finitely many closed sets). The class of $f$ is $[f]$.

For paths $f, g$ with $f(1) = g(0)$ define the **product** $f * g(s) = f(2s)$ for $s \le \tfrac12$ and $g(2s - 1)$ for $s \ge \tfrac12$, the **reverse** $\bar f(s) = f(1 - s)$, and for $x \in X$ the **constant path** $e_x$.

**Lemma 1 (reparametrization).** If $\varphi\colon I \to I$ is continuous with $\varphi(0) = 0$ and $\varphi(1) = 1$, then $f \circ \varphi \simeq_p f$. *Proof.* $H(s, t) = f((1 - t)\varphi(s) + t s)$ is continuous, equals $f \circ \varphi$ at $t = 0$ and $f$ at $t = 1$, and fixes the endpoints. $\blacksquare$

**Theorem 2.** Path classes satisfy: (a) $[f] * [g] := [f * g]$ is well defined; (b) $([f] * [g]) * [h] = [f] * ([g] * [h])$; (c) $[e_{f(0)}] * [f] = [f] = [f] * [e_{f(1)}]$; (d) $[f] * [\bar f] = [e_{f(0)}]$ and $[\bar f] * [f] = [e_{f(1)}]$.

*Proof.* (a) If $F\colon f \simeq_p f'$ and $G\colon g \simeq_p g'$, then $H(s, t) = F(2s, t)$ for $s \le \tfrac12$ and $G(2s - 1, t)$ for $s \ge \tfrac12$ is well defined, since $F(1, t) = f(1) = g(0) = G(0, t)$, and continuous by pasting. It is a path homotopy from $f * g$ to $f' * g'$. (b) One checks directly that $(f * g) * h = (f * (g * h)) \circ \varphi$ for the piecewise linear $\varphi$ with $\varphi(s) = 2s$ on $[0, \tfrac14]$, $s + \tfrac14$ on $[\tfrac14, \tfrac12]$ and $(s + 1)/2$ on $[\tfrac12, 1]$; apply Lemma 1. (c) $e_{f(0)} * f = f \circ \varphi$ with $\varphi(s) = \max(0, 2s - 1)$, and $f * e_{f(1)} = f \circ \psi$ with $\psi(s) = \min(1, 2s)$. (d) Let $H(s, t) = f(2s(1 - t))$ for $s \le \tfrac12$ and $f((2 - 2s)(1 - t))$ for $s \ge \tfrac12$. At $t = 0$ this is $f * \bar f$ (since $\bar f(2s - 1) = f(2 - 2s)$), at $t = 1$ it is constant at $f(0)$, both pieces equal $f(1 - t)$ at $s = \tfrac12$, and $H(0, t) = H(1, t) = f(0)$. Apply the same to $\bar f$ for the other identity. $\blacksquare$

### The fundamental group

A **loop** at $x_0$ is a path with $f(0) = f(1) = x_0$. By Theorem 2 the classes of loops at $x_0$ form a group under $*$, with identity $[e_{x_0}]$ and inverse $[\bar f]$: the **fundamental group** $\pi_1(X, x_0)$. A path-connected space with trivial $\pi_1$ is **simply connected**.

If $h$ is a path from $x_0$ to $x_1$, then $[f] \mapsto [\bar h * f * h]$ is an isomorphism $\pi_1(X, x_0) \to \pi_1(X, x_1)$ (inverse given by $\bar h$ in place of $h$), so for path-connected spaces we may write $\pi_1(X)$. Every convex subset $X$ of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is simply connected: for a loop $f$ at $x_0$, $H(s, t) = (1 - t) f(s) + t x_0$ stays in $X$ and is a path homotopy from $f$ to $e_{x_0}$.

### Induced homomorphisms

A continuous $\varphi\colon X \to Y$ with $\varphi(x_0) = y_0$ gives $\varphi_*\colon \pi_1(X, x_0) \to \pi_1(Y, y_0)$, $\varphi_*[f] = [\varphi \circ f]$. It is well defined (compose a path homotopy with $\varphi$), a homomorphism ($\varphi \circ (f * g) = (\varphi \circ f) * (\varphi \circ g)$), and satisfies $(\psi \circ \varphi)_* = \psi_* \circ \varphi_*$ and $(\mathrm{id})_* = \mathrm{id}$. Hence a homeomorphism induces an isomorphism, with inverse the map induced by the inverse homeomorphism.

@example
title: A loop in a convex set shrinks to a point
problem: Let $X = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 : y \ge x^2\}$ and let $f(s) = (\sin 2\pi s,\ 3 + \cos 2\pi s)$, a loop at $x_0 = (0, 4)$. Show that $f$ is path homotopic to the constant loop and evaluate the homotopy at $(s, t) = (\tfrac14, \tfrac12)$.
step: $f$ lies in $X$: $y = 3 + \cos 2\pi s \ge 2 \ge 1 \ge \sin^2 2\pi s = x^2$, and $f(0) = f(1) = (0, 4)$.
step: $X$ is convex: it is the set above the graph of the convex function $x^2$ (if $y_i \ge x_i^2$, then $(1 - t)y_1 + t y_2 \ge ((1-t)x_1 + t x_2)^2$ by convexity of $x \mapsto x^2$).
step: Define $H(s, t) = (1 - t) f(s) + t x_0$. It is continuous, lies in $X$ by convexity, $H(s, 0) = f(s)$, $H(s, 1) = x_0$, and $H(0, t) = H(1, t) = x_0$ for all $t$. So $f \simeq_p e_{x_0}$.
step: At $(s, t) = (\tfrac14, \tfrac12)$: $f(\tfrac14) = (1, 3)$, so $H = \tfrac12 (1, 3) + \tfrac12 (0, 4) = (\tfrac12, \tfrac72)$.
answer: $f \simeq_p e_{(0,4)}$ via $H$, and $H(\tfrac14, \tfrac12) = (\tfrac12, \tfrac72)$.

@example
title: Retractions induce injections
problem: Let $A \subseteq X$, $a \in A$, and suppose there is a continuous $r\colon X \to A$ with $r(x) = x$ for $x \in A$ (a **retraction**). Prove that the inclusion $i\colon A \to X$ induces an injective homomorphism $i_*\colon \pi_1(A, a) \to \pi_1(X, a)$, and conclude that if $X$ is simply connected then $\pi_1(A, a)$ is trivial.
step: The hypothesis says $r \circ i = \mathrm{id}_A$.
step: Apply functoriality: $r_* \circ i_* = (r \circ i)_* = (\mathrm{id}_A)_* = \mathrm{id}$ on $\pi_1(A, a)$.
step: If $i_*[f] = i_*[g]$, then applying $r_*$ gives $[f] = r_* i_*[f] = r_* i_*[g] = [g]$, so $i_*$ is injective.
step: If $\pi_1(X, a)$ is trivial, then $i_*$ is a map into the trivial group that is injective, so $\pi_1(A, a)$ is trivial.
answer: $i_*$ is injective; a retract of a simply connected space has trivial fundamental group.

@mistakes
- Treating $(f * g) * h$ and $f * (g * h)$ as equal maps: they are different parametrizations, equal only up to path homotopy.
- Forgetting that a path homotopy must fix both endpoints for all $t$: $H(s, t) = f((1 - t)s)$ is a homotopy from $f$ to a constant path, but it moves the endpoint, so it proves nothing about $\pi_1$.
- Assuming $\pi_1$ is abelian or independent of the base point in a space that is not path connected: changing the base point needs a path between the points.
- Saying a space is simply connected because it is connected: connectedness is about open sets, simple connectivity is about loops.

@practice num
prompt: Let $f(s) = s$ and $g(s) = s^3$ for $s \in [0, 1]$; both are paths in $\mathbb{R}$ from $0$ to $1$. The straight-line homotopy is $H(s, t) = (1 - t) f(s) + t g(s)$. Compute $H(\tfrac12, \tfrac14)$.
answer: 0.40625
check: (lambda s, t: (1 - t) * s + t * s ** 3)(0.5, 0.25)
hint: Substitute $s = \tfrac12$ and $t = \tfrac14$: $f(\tfrac12) = \tfrac12$ and $g(\tfrac12) = \tfrac18$.
solution: $H(\tfrac12, \tfrac14) = \tfrac34 \cdot \tfrac12 + \tfrac14 \cdot \tfrac18 = \tfrac{3}{8} + \tfrac{1}{32} = \tfrac{13}{32} = 0.40625$. Since $f$ and $g$ have the same endpoints, $H(0, t) = 0$ and $H(1, t) = 1$ for all $t$, so $H$ is a path homotopy.

@practice self
prompt: Prove that a path-connected space $X$ is simply connected if and only if any two paths in $X$ with the same initial and terminal points are path homotopic.
hint: For ($\Rightarrow$) write $[f] = [f] * [\bar g] * [g]$ and use that $f * \bar g$ is a loop. For ($\Leftarrow$) a loop and the constant loop have the same endpoints.
solution: ($\Rightarrow$) Let $f, g$ be paths from $x$ to $y$. Then $f * \bar g$ is a loop at $x$, so $[f * \bar g] = [e_x]$ because $\pi_1(X, x)$ is trivial. By Theorem 2, $[f] = [f] * [e_y] = [f] * [\bar g] * [g] = [e_x] * [g] = [g]$. ($\Leftarrow$) Let $f$ be a loop at $x_0$. The loop $f$ and the constant path $e_{x_0}$ are paths from $x_0$ to $x_0$, so $f \simeq_p e_{x_0}$. Thus $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is trivial, and $X$ is path connected by hypothesis. $\blacksquare$
rubric: Uses that $f * \bar g$ is a loop and hence trivial in $\pi_1$.
rubric: Applies the group laws of Theorem 2 (identity and inverse) to conclude $[f] = [g]$.
rubric: Proves the converse by comparing a loop with the constant loop.

@practice self
prompt: Let $\varphi\colon X \to Y$ and $\psi\colon Y \to Z$ be continuous, $\varphi(x_0) = y_0$ and $\psi(y_0) = z_0$. Prove that $\varphi_*\colon \pi_1(X, x_0) \to \pi_1(Y, y_0)$, $\varphi_*[f] = [\varphi \circ f]$, is a well-defined homomorphism, that $(\psi \circ \varphi)_* = \psi_* \circ \varphi_*$, and that a homeomorphism $\varphi$ gives an isomorphism.
hint: Compose a path homotopy $F$ with $\varphi$ to get a path homotopy of the images. For the isomorphism, use $(\varphi^{-1})_* \circ \varphi_* = (\mathrm{id})_*$.
solution: If $F\colon f \simeq_p f'$ for loops at $x_0$, then $\varphi \circ F$ is continuous, equals $\varphi \circ f$ at $t = 0$ and $\varphi \circ f'$ at $t = 1$, and keeps the endpoints fixed at $\varphi(x_0) = y_0$, so $\varphi_*$ is well defined. Since $\varphi \circ (f * g) = (\varphi \circ f) * (\varphi \circ g)$ pointwise, $\varphi_*([f] * [g]) = \varphi_*[f] * \varphi_*[g]$. Next $((\psi \circ \varphi) \circ f) = \psi \circ (\varphi \circ f)$ gives $(\psi \circ \varphi)_* = \psi_* \circ \varphi_*$, and $(\mathrm{id}_X)_*$ is the identity. If $\varphi$ is a homeomorphism, $(\varphi^{-1})_* \circ \varphi_* = (\varphi^{-1} \circ \varphi)_* = \mathrm{id}$ and $\varphi_* \circ (\varphi^{-1})_* = \mathrm{id}$, so $\varphi_*$ is a bijective homomorphism. $\blacksquare$
rubric: Shows well-definedness by composing a path homotopy with $\varphi$.
rubric: Checks the homomorphism property and functoriality pointwise.
rubric: Deduces the isomorphism from functoriality applied to $\varphi^{-1} \circ \varphi$ and $\varphi \circ \varphi^{-1}$.
