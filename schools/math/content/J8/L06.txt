@article
The **Middle Ages** or **medieval** period lasted roughly from 500 to 1500 CE. It was not a "dark" time everywhere; major cities and centers of learning thrived across the world.

### Medieval Europe

After the Western Roman Empire ended, western Europe was divided among many kingdoms. In the system called **feudalism**, a king or lord gave land (a **fief**) to a **vassal**, who promised military service and loyalty. Most people were peasants or **serfs**, who worked on a lord's **manor** and owed him labor and goods in return for protection and use of land. The Catholic Church was very influential, and monasteries preserved and copied books. Towns, universities and cathedrals grew after about 1000 CE.

### The Byzantine and Islamic worlds

The **Byzantine Empire**, the Eastern Roman Empire, centered on Constantinople and lasted until 1453. Meanwhile, Muslim-ruled lands formed a wide network of trade and scholarship. In Baghdad, scholars translated and extended works from Greek, Persian and Indian sources. Muslim scholars made advances in medicine, astronomy, optics and mathematics, including algebra; the word "algebra" comes from an Arabic term.

### Africa and Asia

In West Africa the **Mali Empire** grew rich from the gold and salt trade across the Sahara. Its emperor's pilgrimage to Mecca in 1324 became known across the Mediterranean world, and Timbuktu became a center of Islamic learning. In China, the **Tang** and **Song** dynasties saw advances in printing, gunpowder, and trade. In 1206 the **Mongol Empire** began; it grew to be the largest connected land empire in history and made the Silk Road safer for travel. Mongol conquest caused great destruction, and Mongol rule also encouraged trade and exchange across Eurasia.

### The Crusades

From 1096 to 1291, European Christian armies launched a series of wars called the **Crusades**, aiming to control Jerusalem and the Holy Land. Perspectives differ. Many Europeans saw them as religious pilgrimage and defense of Christianity. Muslims in the region saw an invasion, and they resisted and eventually regained the territory. Byzantines asked for help at first, but in 1204 Crusaders attacked and sacked Constantinople itself. Jewish communities in Europe also suffered violence during the Crusades.

### The Black Death

In the 1340s, plague, caused by bacteria and spread by fleas on rodents, moved along trade routes from Asia into Europe, the Middle East and North Africa. In Europe it peaked in 1347 to 1351. Historians estimate that it killed about one-third or more of Europe's population (estimates vary). Because so many workers died, surviving laborers could demand better pay, which weakened feudal obligations in many places.

@example
title: Population after the plague
problem: In an invented town of 6,000 people, the plague kills one-third of the population. How many people are left?
step: Find the deaths: one-third of 6,000 is $6000 \div 3 = 2000$.
step: Subtract the deaths from the starting population: $6000 - 2000 = 4000$.
step: Check: 4,000 is two-thirds of 6,000, which matches losing one-third.
step: Explain an effect: with fewer workers, surviving laborers could ask for more pay, which helped change medieval society.

@mistakes
- Calling the whole Middle Ages "dark"; learning and trade flourished in the Islamic world, China and West Africa.
- Telling the Crusades from only one side's point of view.
- Treating all of medieval Europe as one kingdom, instead of many kingdoms and lords.
- Saying the plague was caused by "bad air"; medieval people believed this, but it was a bacterial infection.
