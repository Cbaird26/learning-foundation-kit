@article
A function on a manifold has no preferred coordinates, so "smooth" must be defined by asking that its coordinate expressions be smooth, and then one must check that the answer does not depend on which charts we used. Once that is done, the real power of the Hausdorff and second countable axioms appears: smooth functions can be cut off and glued.

### Smooth maps

Let $M, N$ be smooth manifolds of dimensions $m, n$. A map $F\colon M \to N$ is **smooth** if it is continuous and for every $p \in M$ there are charts $(U,\varphi)$ at $p$ and $(V,\psi)$ at $F(p)$ with $F(U) \subseteq V$ such that the **coordinate representation** $\psi \circ F \circ \varphi^{-1}\colon \varphi(U) \to \mathbb{R}^n$ is $C^\infty$.

*Independence of charts.* If $(U',\varphi')$ and $(V',\psi')$ are other charts at $p$ and $F(p)$, then near $\varphi'(p)$, on the open set $\varphi'(U \cap U' \cap F^{-1}(V \cap V'))$,
$$\psi' \circ F \circ \varphi'^{-1} = (\psi' \circ \psi^{-1}) \circ (\psi \circ F \circ \varphi^{-1}) \circ (\varphi \circ \varphi'^{-1}),$$
a composition of smooth maps by compatibility. Continuity of $F$ is used to make $F^{-1}(V \cap V')$ open. Consequently a composition of smooth maps is smooth, and a **diffeomorphism** is a smooth bijection with smooth inverse. The set of smooth functions $M \to \mathbb{R}$ is the ring $C^\infty(M)$.

*Example.* The quotient map $\pi\colon S^n \to \mathbb{RP}^n$, $x \mapsto [x]$, is smooth: on $\{x_i \ne 0\}$ the coordinate representation in the chart $\psi_i$ of the previous lesson is $x \mapsto (x_k/x_i)_{k \ne i}$, restricted to the sphere, which is smooth.

### $\mathbb{RP}^n$ is a smooth manifold

Local Euclideanity and compatibility were shown last time. **Second countable:** $\pi$ is continuous and open (for $V \subseteq S^n$ open, $\pi^{-1}(\pi(V)) = V \cup (-V)$ is open), and the image of a countable basis of $S^n$ under an open surjection is a countable basis of $\mathbb{RP}^n$. **Hausdorff:** the set $R = \{(x,y) \in S^n \times S^n : y = x \text{ or } y = -x\}$ is closed (a union of two graphs of continuous maps). If $[x] \ne [y]$, then $(x,y) \notin R$, so there are open $V \ni x$ and $W \ni y$ in $S^n$ with $(V \times W) \cap R = \emptyset$. Then $\pi(V)$ and $\pi(W)$ are open, contain $[x]$ and $[y]$, and are disjoint: a common point $[z]$ would give $v \in V$, $w \in W$ with $w = \pm v$, so $(v,w) \in (V\times W)\cap R$. Hence $\mathbb{RP}^n$ is a smooth manifold of dimension $n$, and with $S^n$ this completes the first deliverable.

### Bump functions

The function $f(t) = e^{-1/t}$ for $t > 0$ and $f(t) = 0$ for $t \le 0$ is $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb{R}$: for $t > 0$, induction gives $f^{(k)}(t) = p_k(1/t)e^{-1/t}$ with $p_k$ a polynomial, and $s^j e^{-s} \to 0$ as $s \to \infty$ shows that every derivative tends to $0$ at $0^+$, matching the zero derivatives from the left. Then
$$\beta(x) = \frac{f(4 - |x|^2)}{f(4 - |x|^2) + f(|x|^2 - 1)}$$
is smooth on $\mathbb{R}^n$ (the denominator never vanishes: if $|x|^2 \ge 4$ the second term is positive, otherwise the first is), equals $1$ on $|x| \le 1$ and $0$ on $|x| \ge 2$. Transporting by a chart gives **bump functions** on manifolds: for $p \in U$ there is a smooth $\beta \ge 0$ with $\beta(p) > 0$ and $\operatorname{supp}\beta$ compact, inside $U$. Here $\operatorname{supp}\beta$ is the closure of $\{\beta \ne 0\}$, and extending by $0$ outside $U$ is smooth because the support is a compact, hence closed, subset of $U$.

### Partitions of unity

**Theorem (smooth partitions of unity).** Let $M$ be a smooth manifold (Hausdorff, second countable) and $\{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in A}$ an open cover. Then there are smooth functions $\rho_\alpha\colon M \to [0,1]$ with $\operatorname{supp}\rho_\alpha \subseteq U_\alpha$, such that the family of supports is **locally finite** (each point has a neighborhood meeting only finitely many) and $\sum_\alpha \rho_\alpha = 1$.

*Proof.* (1) $M$ is locally compact and has a countable basis, so the open sets $B$ of the basis with $\overline{B}$ compact and $\overline{B}\subseteq U_\alpha$ for some $\alpha$ form a countable cover $B_1, B_2, \dots$ (given $p \in U_\alpha$ pick a chart ball $W$ with $p\in W$, $\overline W$ compact in $U_\alpha$ and a basic $B$ with $p \in B \subseteq W$). (2) Build compact sets $K_1 \subseteq \operatorname{int}K_2 \subseteq K_3 \subseteq \cdots$ with union $M$: let $K_1 = \overline{B_1}$ and, given $K_j$, choose $m > j$ with $K_j \subseteq B_1 \cup \dots \cup B_m$ (compactness) and put $K_{j+1} = \overline{B_1} \cup \dots \cup \overline{B_m}$. Put $K_0 = K_{-1} = \emptyset$. (3) For each $j \ge 1$ the set $C_j = K_j \setminus \operatorname{int}K_{j-1}$ is compact and lies in the open set $W_j = \operatorname{int}K_{j+1} \setminus K_{j-2}$. For every $p \in C_j$ choose $\alpha(p)$ with $p \in U_{\alpha(p)}$ and a bump function $\beta_p \ge 0$ with $\beta_p(p) > 0$ and support compact inside $U_{\alpha(p)} \cap W_j$. Finitely many of the open sets $\{\beta_p > 0\}$ cover $C_j$; call the chosen functions $\beta_{j,1}, \dots, \beta_{j,N_j}$ with indices $\alpha(j,i)$. (4) Every point of $M$ lies in some $C_j$, so $\beta = \sum_{j,i} \beta_{j,i} > 0$ everywhere. Because $\operatorname{supp}\beta_{j,i} \subseteq W_j$ is disjoint from $K_{j-2}$, the open set $\operatorname{int}K_m$ meets only the supports with $j \le m + 1$: the family is locally finite and $\beta$ is smooth. (5) Set $\rho_\alpha = \beta^{-1}\sum_{\alpha(j,i) = \alpha}\beta_{j,i}$ (zero if there are no terms). The sum is locally finite, so $\operatorname{supp}\rho_\alpha$ is the union of the supports of its terms, which lies in $U_\alpha$, and $\sum_\alpha\rho_\alpha = 1$. $\blacksquare$

Local finiteness is what makes sums of smooth functions smooth. Typical uses: gluing local objects (Riemannian metrics, later), integration of forms, and extension of functions from closed sets (practice). In Python, a partition of unity on a line is just the formula of $\beta$ with different shifts.

title: A smooth function on a quotient
problem: Let $g\colon \mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$, $g(x,y) = x^2 + 3xy$. Is the function $G\colon S^1\to\mathbb{R}$, $G = g|_{S^1}$, smooth, and is there a function $h$ on $\mathbb{RP}^1$ with $G = h \circ \pi$ ($\pi\colon S^1\to\mathbb{RP}^1$ the quotient map)?
step: Smoothness of $G$: $g$ is a polynomial, hence smooth on $\mathbb{R}^2$, and the inclusion $S^1\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^2$ is smooth (in the stereographic chart it is $u \mapsto (2u, u^2-1)/(u^2+1)$), so $G = g \circ \iota$ is a composition of smooth maps.
step: A function $h$ with $G = h\circ\pi$ must satisfy $G(x) = G(-x)$ because $\pi(x) = \pi(-x)$. Compute $G(-x,-y) = x^2 + 3xy = G(x,y)$ because $g$ is homogeneous of degree $2$: $G$ is even.
step: Define $h([x]) = G(x)$; this is well defined because $G$ is even. Then $h$ is smooth: in the chart $\psi_0$ of $\mathbb{RP}^1$ (where $x \neq 0$ in the first slot), $\psi_0^{-1}(s) = [1:s]$ and a local section is $s \mapsto (1,s)/\sqrt{1+s^2}$, so $h\circ\psi_0^{-1}(s) = G(1,s)/(1+s^2) = (1+3s)/(1+s^2)$, which is smooth.
answer: Yes. $G$ is smooth and even, and it descends to the smooth function $h\circ\psi_0^{-1}(s) = (1+3s)/(1+s^2)$.
@example
title: A partition of unity subordinate to two sets
problem: Cover $\mathbb{R}$ by $U_1 = (-\infty, 2)$ and $U_2 = (1, \infty)$. Construct a smooth partition of unity subordinate to this cover and evaluate it at $x = 1.4$.
step: Let $f(t) = e^{-1/t}$ for $t>0$ and $f(t)=0$ otherwise. The supports must lie inside the open sets, so shrink the cover first: choose $1 < 1.2 < 1.8 < 2$ and put $\rho_1(x) = \dfrac{f(1.8-x)}{f(1.8-x)+f(x-1.2)}$ and $\rho_2 = 1-\rho_1 = \dfrac{f(x-1.2)}{f(1.8-x)+f(x-1.2)}$.
step: The denominator is positive for every $x$: if $x < 1.8$ then $f(1.8-x) > 0$, and if $x \ge 1.8$ then $x - 1.2 > 0$. So $\rho_1, \rho_2$ are smooth, take values in $[0,1]$, and sum to $1$.
step: Supports: $\rho_1(x) \ne 0$ exactly when $x < 1.8$, so $\operatorname{supp}\rho_1 = (-\infty, 1.8] \subseteq U_1$; similarly $\rho_2(x) \ne 0$ exactly when $x > 1.2$, so $\operatorname{supp}\rho_2 = [1.2, \infty) \subseteq U_2$. Had we used $f(2-x)$ and $f(x-1)$, the supports would be $(-\infty,2]$ and $[1,\infty)$, which are not contained in the open sets.
step: At $x = 1.4$: $\rho_1(1.4) = \dfrac{f(0.4)}{f(0.4)+f(0.2)} = \dfrac{1}{1 + e^{-5 + 2.5}} = \dfrac{1}{1+e^{-2.5}} \approx 0.924$.
answer: $\rho_1 = \dfrac{f(1.8-x)}{f(1.8-x)+f(x-1.2)}$, $\rho_2 = 1-\rho_1$, and $\rho_1(1.4) \approx 0.924$.
@mistakes
- Defining smoothness of $F\colon M\to N$ by asking the coordinate representation to be smooth in some chart without requiring $F$ to be continuous: continuity is needed so the domain of the representation is open.
- Using supports instead of the open sets $U_\alpha$: the support of a function is closed, so $\operatorname{supp}\rho_\alpha \subseteq U_\alpha$ is a stronger condition than $\{\rho_\alpha \ne 0\} \subseteq U_\alpha$.
- Forgetting local finiteness: an infinite sum of smooth functions, even with sum $1$ pointwise, need not be smooth, and integrals of forms need it.
- Assuming partitions of unity exist on every topological space: the proof used Hausdorff (compact sets are closed), local compactness and second countability.

import math


def _f(t):
    """The smooth function that vanishes for t <= 0."""
    return math.exp(-1 / t) if t > 0 else 0.0

    """Partition of unity on R for the cover (-inf, 1), (0, inf)."""
    a = _f(0.8 - x)
    b = _f(x - 0.2)
    return (a / (a + b), b / (a + b))

@practice self
prompt: Let $M$ be a smooth manifold and $A \subseteq M$ closed. Call $f\colon A\to\mathbb{R}$ smooth if every $p\in A$ has an open neighborhood $W_p\subseteq M$ and a smooth $\tilde f_p\colon W_p\to\mathbb{R}$ with $\tilde f_p = f$ on $A\cap W_p$. Prove that every such $f$ is the restriction to $A$ of a smooth function $F\colon M\to\mathbb{R}$.
hint: Cover $M$ by the sets $W_p$ ($p\in A$) together with $M\setminus A$, and take a subordinate partition of unity.
hint: Define $F=\sum_p \rho_p\tilde f_p$; each term has to be extended by $0$ outside the support of $\rho_p$.
solution: The sets $W_p$ ($p \in A$) and $W_* = M\setminus A$ are open (since $A$ is closed) and cover $M$. Let $\{\rho_p\}_{p\in A}\cup\{\rho_*\}$ be a smooth partition of unity subordinate to this cover, with locally finite supports. For each $p$, the function $\rho_p\tilde f_p$, extended by $0$ outside $W_p$, is smooth: it is smooth on $W_p$, and it vanishes on the open set $M \setminus \operatorname{supp}\rho_p$; these two open sets cover $M$ because $\operatorname{supp}\rho_p\subseteq W_p$. Define $F = \sum_{p\in A}\rho_p\tilde f_p$. The sum is locally finite, hence smooth. For $x\in A$ we have $\rho_*(x) = 0$ because $\operatorname{supp}\rho_*\subseteq M\setminus A$, so $\sum_{p\in A}\rho_p(x) = 1$. If $\rho_p(x)\ne0$ then $x\in W_p\cap A$, and so $\tilde f_p(x) = f(x)$. Therefore $F(x) = \sum_p\rho_p(x)f(x) = f(x)$. $\blacksquare$

@quiz self
prompt: Let $\pi\colon S^n\to\mathbb{RP}^n$ be the quotient map. Prove that a function $h\colon\mathbb{RP}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ is smooth if and only if $h\circ\pi$ is smooth on $S^n$.
hint: One direction is a composition. For the other, use the charts $\psi_i$ and a smooth local section of $\pi$ over $\psi_i^{-1}$.
solution: If $h$ is smooth, then $h\circ\pi$ is a composition of smooth maps. Conversely suppose $g = h\circ\pi$ is smooth (in particular $g$ is even). Fix $i$ and let $s_i\colon\mathbb{R}^n\to S^n$ be $s_i(u) = \iota_i(u)/\sqrt{1+|u|^2}$, where $\iota_i(u)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ has the entries of $u$ in the slots other than $i$ and a $1$ in slot $i$. Then $s_i$ is smooth into $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ with values in $S^n$, hence smooth into $S^n$ (an inclusion that is a smooth embedding; one can also check this in the stereographic charts), and $\pi(s_i(u)) = [\iota_i(u)] = \psi_i^{-1}(u)$. So $h\circ\psi_i^{-1} = h\circ\pi\circ s_i = g\circ s_i$ is smooth on $\mathbb{R}^n$. Since $h$ is continuous (because $g$ is continuous and $\pi$ is a quotient map) and every coordinate representation of $h$ in the charts $\psi_i$ is smooth, $h$ is smooth. $\blacksquare$
rubric: Proves the easy direction by composition of smooth maps.
rubric: Constructs a smooth local section $s_i$ with $\pi\circ s_i=\psi_i^{-1}$.
rubric: Concludes that $h\circ\psi_i^{-1}=g\circ s_i$ is smooth and notes that continuity of $h$ comes from the quotient topology.
