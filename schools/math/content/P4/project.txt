### Weeks 1 to 12 at a glance

**Weeks 1 and 2: manifolds and smooth maps.** A manifold is Hausdorff, second countable and locally Euclidean; a smooth structure is a maximal atlas. $S^n$ (stereographic charts) and $\mathbb{RP}^n$ (standard charts) are smooth manifolds. Smooth maps are smooth in charts. Bump functions give partitions of unity, which glue local constructions.

**Weeks 3 and 4: tangent spaces and maps.** $T_pM$ is the space of derivations, $\dim T_pM=n$, $dF_p$ is the Jacobian in charts, and the chain rule is $d(G\circ F)=dG\circ dF$. Immersions, submersions and embeddings have local normal forms; an injective immersion from a compact manifold is an embedding.

**Weeks 5 and 6: submanifolds and transversality.** A regular value gives a submanifold of dimension $m-n$ with $T=\ker dF$ (sufficient, not necessary). Sard's theorem: critical values have measure zero. Transversality $dF(T_pM)+T S=TN$ gives preimage submanifolds of the right codimension.

**Weeks 7 and 8: flows and Lie theory.** Integral curves are unique on a Hausdorff manifold; compactly supported fields are complete; $\theta_s\theta_t=\theta_{s+t}$. $[X,Y]f=XYf-YXf$ with $[X,Y]^j=X(Y^j)-Y(X^j)$, Jacobi identity, and $\mathfrak{gl}(n)$ has bracket $AB-BA$. Flows commute iff the bracket vanishes.

**Weeks 9 to 11: forms and Stokes.** $\dim\Lambda^k=\binom nk$, $\alpha\wedge\beta=(-1)^{kl}\beta\wedge\alpha$, $F^*$ commutes with $\wedge$ and $d$, $d^2=0$. Orientation is a nowhere-vanishing top form; $\mathbb{RP}^n$ is orientable iff $n$ is odd. Stokes: $\int_Md\omega=\int_{\partial M}\omega$ for compactly supported $\omega$ on an oriented manifold with boundary (outward normal first).

**Week 12: cohomology and metrics.** $H^k=\ker d/\operatorname{im}d$; $H^1(S^1)\cong\mathbb{R}\cong H^1(\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{0\})$ via $\int d\theta$; homotopy invariance. Riemannian metrics exist by partitions of unity; $G=J^\top J$ and $d\mathrm{vol}=\sqrt{\det G}\,dx$.

### Common errors

Forgetting a hypothesis (Hausdorff, compact support, constant rank); confusing critical points with critical values; the wrong sign in $[fX,Y]=f[X,Y]-(Yf)X$; the wrong order in the chain rule; orientation signs on boundary components; and taking numerical agreement for a proof.

prompt: Let $\mu=\varphi\,dx^1\wedge\dots\wedge dx^n$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ ($n\ge1$), where $\varphi\ge0$ is a smooth function with compact support and $\varphi(0)>0$. Prove that there is no smooth $(n-1)$-form $\eta$ with compact support such that $\mu=d\eta$, and explain which results from the course you use.
hint: Integrate both sides and use Stokes's theorem on $\mathbb{R}^n$ (a manifold without boundary).
solution: Suppose $\mu=d\eta$ with $\eta$ a smooth, compactly supported $(n-1)$-form on $\mathbb{R}^n$. $\mathbb{R}^n$ with the standard orientation is an oriented manifold without boundary, and $\eta$ has compact support, so Stokes's theorem gives $\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}d\eta=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^n}\eta=0$ because $\partial\mathbb{R}^n=\emptyset$. On the other hand, $\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\varphi\,dx^1\cdots dx^n$ (the integral of a top form in the standard chart, orientation positive) is the Lebesgue integral of a nonnegative continuous function that is positive on a neighborhood of $0$, hence it is strictly positive. This contradicts $\int\mu=\int d\eta=0$. So no such $\eta$ exists. Compare this with $H^n_{dR}(\mathbb{R}^n)=0$: every $n$-form is exact, but not by a compactly supported primitive. $\blacksquare$

solution: Equal dimensions and injective differential make $dF_p$ an isomorphism, so the inverse function theorem applies.
