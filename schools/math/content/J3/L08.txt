@article
A **transformation** moves or resizes a figure. The original is the **preimage** and the result is the **image**; a prime mark marks the image, so $A$ becomes $A'$. A transformation is itself a function: it takes each point to exactly one image point.

### Rigid motions

Three transformations keep every length and angle the same, so the image is **congruent** to the preimage. They are called **rigid motions**.

- **Translation** slides every point the same distance in the same direction. Translating by $3$ left and $4$ up is $(x, y) \to (x - 3, y + 4)$.
- **Reflection** flips the figure over a line. Over the $x$-axis: $(x, y) \to (x, -y)$. Over the $y$-axis: $(x, y) \to (-x, y)$.
- **Rotation** turns the figure about a point. About the origin, $90^\circ$ counterclockwise: $(x, y) \to (-y, x)$; $90^\circ$ clockwise: $(x, y) \to (y, -x)$; $180^\circ$: $(x, y) \to (-x, -y)$.

### Dilation

A **dilation** with center the origin and **scale factor** $k > 0$ sends $(x, y)$ to $(kx, ky)$. If $k > 1$ the figure enlarges; if $0 < k < 1$ it shrinks. A dilation keeps the shape and all angles, but multiplies every length by $k$, so the image is **similar** but generally not congruent.

### Which properties are preserved?

| Transformation | Lengths | Angles | Image congruent? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Translation | same | same | yes |
| Reflection | same | same | yes |
| Rotation | same | same | yes |
| Dilation ($k \neq 1$) | times $k$ | same | no, similar |

### Sequences

Transformations can be combined, and the order matters. Translating $(1, 4)$ right $5$ and then reflecting over the $y$-axis gives $(6, 4)$ and then $(-6, 4)$. Reflecting first would give $(-1, 4)$ and then $(4, 4)$, a different point.

### Transformations as functions

Describing a transformation as a rule is like describing a function: the rule gives the output point for every input point, and a table of a few points shows its pattern. Checking a handful of points against the rule is a good way to detect an error.

@example
title: Rotating a point
problem: Rotate the point $(2, 3)$ by $90^\circ$ clockwise about the origin.
step: A $90^\circ$ clockwise rotation about the origin follows the rule $(x, y) \to (y, -x)$.
step: Substitute $x = 2$ and $y = 3$: the image is $(3, -2)$.
step: Check the distance from the origin: both $(2, 3)$ and $(3, -2)$ are $\sqrt{13}$ from $(0, 0)$, as a rotation requires.

@mistakes
- Mixing up clockwise and counterclockwise rotation rules.
- Reflecting over the $x$-axis but changing the sign of $x$ instead of $y$.
- Applying a scale factor to only one coordinate in a dilation.
- Assuming a dilation produces a congruent figure.

@practice tuple
prompt: Translate the point $(2, 5)$ by $3$ units left and $4$ units up. Give the image as $(x, y)$.
answer: (-1, 9)
check: (2 - 3, 5 + 4)
hint: Left subtracts from $x$; up adds to $y$.
hint: $x$ becomes $2 - 3$.
solution: $(2 - 3, 5 + 4) = (-1, 9)$.

@practice tuple
prompt: Reflect the point $(3, -2)$ over the $x$-axis. Give the image as $(x, y)$.
answer: (3, 2)
check: (3, -(-2))
hint: A reflection over the $x$-axis keeps $x$ and changes the sign of $y$.
hint: The point stays the same distance from the axis, on the other side.
solution: $(x, y) \to (x, -y)$ gives $(3, 2)$.

@practice tuple
prompt: Dilate the point $(6, -9)$ about the origin by scale factor $\frac{1}{3}$. Give the image as $(x, y)$.
answer: (2, -3)
check: (6 / 3, -9 / 3)
hint: Multiply both coordinates by the scale factor.
hint: $6 \cdot \frac{1}{3}$ and $-9 \cdot \frac{1}{3}$.
solution: $(6 \cdot \frac{1}{3}, -9 \cdot \frac{1}{3}) = (2, -3)$.

@practice mc

@practice self
prompt: The transformation $T$ sends each point $(x, y)$ to $(x + 3, y - 2)$. Describe $T$ as a function: give a table for the three points $A(0, 0)$, $B(2, 1)$ and $C(1, 4)$, describe the graph of the triangle and its image, and say whether the image is congruent to the original.
hint: Apply the rule to the x and y of each point in turn.
hint: Compare the size and shape of the two triangles.
solution: The table of (input point, output point) is $A(0,0) \to A'(3,-2)$, $B(2,1) \to B'(5,-1)$ and $C(1,4) \to C'(4,2)$. The image triangle is the original triangle slid $3$ units right and $2$ units down, so on the graph the two triangles have the same size, shape and orientation. $T$ is a translation, a rigid motion, so every length and angle is preserved and the image is congruent. Each input point has exactly one output point, so $T$ is a function.
