@article
You started life as a single cell. Today you are made of trillions. Cells make more cells by dividing, and the body uses division to **grow**, to **repair** injuries and to **replace** worn-out cells.

### Why cells do not just keep growing

A cell takes in food and oxygen through its surface and uses them throughout its volume. As a cell gets bigger, volume grows faster than surface area. Take a cube-shaped cell with side length $s$:

$$\text{surface area} = 6s^2, \qquad \text{volume} = s^3, \qquad \frac{\text{surface area}}{\text{volume}} = \frac{6}{s}.$$

The ratio gets smaller as $s$ grows. A big cell would have too little surface to supply its inside, so it divides into smaller cells.

### The cell cycle

The **cell cycle** is the life of a dividing cell. Most of it is **interphase**, when the cell grows, does its normal jobs and copies its DNA. Then the nucleus divides in **mitosis**, and the cytoplasm splits in **cytokinesis**.

### Stages of mitosis

1. **Prophase**: the copied DNA coils into visible **chromosomes**, each made of two identical **sister chromatids**; the nuclear membrane breaks down.
2. **Metaphase**: chromosomes line up across the middle of the cell.
3. **Anaphase**: sister chromatids are pulled apart to opposite ends.
4. **Telophase**: two new nuclei form around the separated sets.

Mitosis makes two **daughter cells** that are genetically identical to the parent. A human body cell with 46 chromosomes makes two cells with 46 chromosomes each.

### Growth that goes wrong

Cells normally divide only when needed, controlled by signals. **Cancer** begins when changes in a cell's DNA let it divide without proper control, forming a mass called a tumor. Doctors treat cancer in several ways, and your own body is not something to experiment on.

### Counting cells

If a cell divides every 30 minutes, the number doubles each time:

$$N = N_0 \times 2^n,$$

where $n$ is the number of divisions. After two hours that is $n = 4$ divisions, so one cell becomes $2^4 = 16$.

@example
title: Surface area to volume
problem: A cube-shaped cell has side 2 cm. Find its surface-area-to-volume ratio, then say what happens when the side doubles to 4 cm.
step: Surface area is $6s^2 = 6 \times 4 = 24\ \text{cm}^2$.
step: Volume is $s^3 = 8\ \text{cm}^3$, so the ratio is $24 \div 8 = 3$.
step: For $s = 4$: area $= 6 \times 16 = 96$, volume $= 64$, ratio $= 1.5$.
step: Doubling the side halved the ratio, so the bigger cell has less surface for each bit of volume.

@mistakes
- Saying mitosis makes cells with half the chromosomes; that is meiosis, which makes sex cells.
- Thinking interphase is a resting stage; the cell is actively growing and copying DNA.
- Confusing the order of anaphase and metaphase; chromosomes line up first, then separate.

@practice self
prompt: A friend says, "If a cell just kept getting bigger and bigger, it would be even better at its job." Write 3 or 4 sentences explaining why a cell divides instead, using the ideas of surface area and volume.
hint: Which grows faster as a cell gets bigger, surface area or volume?
solution: As a cell grows, its volume increases faster than its surface area. Food, oxygen and wastes must pass through the cell membrane, which is the surface. A very large cell would have too little surface for its volume and could not supply the inside fast enough. Dividing makes smaller cells with a higher surface-area-to-volume ratio, so materials can reach all parts.
rubric: States that volume increases faster than surface area as a cell grows.
rubric: Explains that materials enter and leave through the surface.
rubric: Concludes that division restores a higher ratio of surface area to volume.
