@article
A **wave** is a disturbance that carries energy from one place to another without carrying matter along. When you shake one end of a rope, the pulse travels along the rope, but the rope does not move away.

### Wave properties

- **Amplitude:** the maximum displacement from rest. A larger amplitude means more energy (louder sound, brighter light).
- **Wavelength** ($\lambda$): the distance between two matching points, such as crest to crest, in meters.
- **Frequency** ($f$): the number of waves passing a point each second, in **hertz (Hz)**.
- **Period** ($T$): the time for one wave, $T = 1/f$.

The speed of a wave is

$$v = f\lambda.$$

For a given medium the speed is fixed, so a higher frequency means a shorter wavelength.

### Mechanical and electromagnetic waves

**Mechanical waves**, such as sound and water waves, need a medium (matter) to travel through. **Electromagnetic waves**, such as light, radio waves and X-rays, can travel through empty space.

### Sound

Sound is a longitudinal wave: air particles are pushed together (compressions) and pulled apart (rarefactions) along the direction of travel. Sound travels about $343\ \mathrm{m/s}$ in air at room temperature, faster in liquids and faster still in solids, and it cannot travel in a vacuum. **Pitch** depends on frequency: a high frequency gives a high pitch. **Loudness** depends on amplitude. An echo is sound reflected from a hard surface.

### Light

Light is an electromagnetic wave that travels at about $3.0 \times 10^8\ \mathrm{m/s}$ in a vacuum. Its behavior includes:

- **Reflection:** bouncing off a surface, as in a mirror (angle in equals angle out).
- **Refraction:** bending when it passes from one material to another, such as from air into water.
- **Absorption:** being taken in by a material and turned into thermal energy. Dark objects absorb more visible light than light-colored ones.

The **electromagnetic spectrum** orders waves by frequency: radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light (red to violet), ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays. All travel at the same speed in a vacuum.

### Using what you know: a fair test of insulators

Infrared radiation, conduction and convection all move heat. To compare insulators fairly, wrap identical cups of warm water in different materials, keep the amount and starting temperature of water the same, and record the temperature with a thermometer every few minutes. The insulator with the smallest temperature drop is best.

@example
title: Wave speed
problem: A sound wave has frequency $440\ \mathrm{Hz}$ and wavelength $0.78\ \mathrm{m}$. What is its speed?
step: Use $v = f\lambda$.
step: $v = 440 \times 0.78$.
step: $v = 343.2\ \mathrm{m/s}$, about the speed of sound in air.

@mistakes
- Thinking sound can travel through a vacuum. Sound needs a medium.
- Confusing pitch with loudness. Pitch depends on frequency, loudness on amplitude.
- Forgetting to divide the echo's round-trip time by two when finding the distance to a wall.
- Using the wavelength as the wave's height. Amplitude is the height; wavelength is the length of one cycle.

@practice self
prompt: Plan a fair test to compare three insulators (for example wool, aluminum foil and paper) using identical cups of warm water and a thermometer. State what you keep the same, what you change, what you measure, how often, and how you decide which insulator is best.
solution: Fill four identical cups with the same amount of warm water at the same starting temperature (for example 250 mL at about 50 degrees C, from the warm tap, with an adult). Wrap three cups in the same thickness of wool, foil and paper, and leave the fourth unwrapped as a control. Cover each cup the same way and record the temperature every 5 minutes for 30 minutes. The variable changed is the insulator. The best insulator is the one with the smallest temperature drop. Repeat the test to check that the result is consistent.
hint: Change only one thing, the insulating material, and measure the same quantity for every cup.
rubric: Keeps water amount, starting temperature, cups and timing the same.
rubric: Changes only the insulating material and includes an uninsulated control.
rubric: Measures temperature at regular times and records the data.
rubric: Chooses the insulator with the smallest temperature drop and suggests repeating the test.
