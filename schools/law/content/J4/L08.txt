@article
You will meet thousands of new words in middle school and beyond. You cannot memorize each one, but you can learn the **parts** words are built from and read the **clues** around them.

### Word parts

A word may have a **prefix** (before the root), a **root** (the core meaning) and a **suffix** (after the root, often changing the part of speech).

Common prefixes: **un-/in-/dis-** (not), **re-** (again), **pre-** (before), **sub-** (under), **inter-** (between), **mis-** (wrong), **anti-** (against).

Common roots:

| Root | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| *bio* | life | biology, biography |
| *graph* | write | autograph, paragraph |
| *spect* | look | inspect, spectator |
| *port* | carry | transport, portable |
| *dict* | say | predict, dictate |
| *scrib/script* | write | describe, manuscript |
| *aud* | hear | audible, audience |
| *rupt* | break | interrupt, erupt |

Common suffixes: **-able/-ible** (can be), **-ful** (full of), **-less** (without), **-tion/-ment** (state or act), **-ology** (study of).

Example: **transportable** = *trans-* (across) + *port* (carry) + *-able* (can be): "can be carried across," or easily moved.

### Context clues

When parts do not help, use the sentence.

- **Definition**: the meaning is stated. "A *herbivore*, an animal that eats only plants, grazed nearby."
- **Synonym**: a similar word. "She was *elated*, or overjoyed."
- **Antonym/contrast**: an opposite signals meaning. "Unlike her *frugal* brother, she spent freely." (frugal means careful with money)
- **Example**: examples show meaning. "Nocturnal animals, such as owls and bats, are active at night."
- **Inference**: use the situation as a whole.

### A safe method

1. Read the whole sentence, and the sentences nearby.
2. Break the word into parts you know.
3. Guess a meaning and **substitute** it for the word. Does the sentence still make sense?
4. Confirm with a dictionary when accuracy matters.

### Watch out

Words can have multiple meanings (*bank*, *run*) so context decides which one fits. Roots can also mislead: *butterfly* has nothing to do with butter. Treat word-part analysis as a strong clue, not proof.

@example
title: Inferring a word's meaning
problem: Use word parts and context to define *unpredictable* in: "The weather in April is unpredictable; sunny at noon, hailing by three."
step: Break the word: *un-* (not) + *pre-* (before) + *dict* (say) + *-able* (can be). Together: cannot be said before, that is, cannot be foretold.
step: Check context: the sentence gives sunny and hailing within a few hours, an example of sudden change.
step: Substitute: "The weather in April is hard to foretell." The sentence makes sense.
step: Meaning: not able to be predicted; likely to change unexpectedly.
@mistakes
- Guessing from a word that looks similar (butterfly means butter) instead of testing the meaning in the sentence.
- Ignoring the sentence and choosing a meaning the word has only in another setting.
- Memorizing a root's meaning but forgetting the prefix, which can reverse the meaning.
