@article
An **inference question** gives you a set of statements and asks what follows. Unlike assumption and strengthen questions, you do not look for a gap. You treat the statements as true and ask what **must** be true, or what is **most strongly supported**. The correct answer is almost always modest and is a restatement or combination of what the stimulus says. Anything that requires an outside fact is wrong.

### Conditional statements

Many inference stimuli are built on **conditional logic**. A conditional "If A, then B" is written $A \to B$. A is the **sufficient** condition (A guarantees B) and B is the **necessary** condition (without B, A cannot occur).

- "If A, then B": $A \to B$.
- "A only if B": $A \to B$. The word after *only if* is the necessary condition.
- "A unless B": $\text{not } B \to A$, or equivalently $\text{not } A \to B$. (*Unless* means "if not.")
- "No A is B": $A \to \text{not } B$.
- "All A are B": $A \to B$.
- "Every A requires B": $A \to B$.

### The contrapositive

The only valid inference from $A \to B$ is its **contrapositive**: $\text{not } B \to \text{not } A$. If B is absent, A cannot be present. Two tempting errors are not valid: the **converse** ($B \to A$) and the **inverse** ($\text{not } A \to \text{not } B$). Knowing that someone who graduates passed the exam does not tell you that everyone who passed graduated.

### Chains

Conditionals link when the necessary term of one is the sufficient term of the next: $A \to B$ and $B \to C$ give $A \to C$, and its contrapositive $\text{not } C \to \text{not } A$. Always write the contrapositive of the chain.

### "Some," "most" and "all"

- **Some** means at least one, and possibly all. "Some A are B" is reversible: "some B are A."
- **Most** means more than half. "Most A are B" is not reversible.
- **Overlap rule:** if most A are B and most A are C, then at least one A is both B and C, because two majorities of the same group must overlap. If most A are B and most B are C, nothing follows about A and C.
- "All A are B" and "some A are C" give "some B are C."

### Most strongly supported

On "most strongly supported" questions, the answer need not be certain; it must be the best-supported choice. Wrong answers go beyond the stimulus (extreme words like "all," "never"), reverse a conditional, or bring in outside facts. Read the stimulus, combine any statements that share a term, and predict a combination before looking at the choices.

### The method for your diagrams

For each question in the block deliverable of 15 conditional questions: (1) translate each sentence into arrow notation, (2) write the contrapositive, (3) combine chains, (4) test each choice against your diagram, and (5) record the translation in your error log if you missed it. Most misses on this question type are translation errors, not reasoning errors.

@example
title: Chaining and the contrapositive
problem: "If a trial is randomized, then it controls for selection effects. Any study that controls for selection effects is eligible for the review. This study was not eligible for the review." What must be true about the study?
step: Translate. Randomized $\to$ controls for selection effects. Controls for selection effects $\to$ eligible for the review.
step: Chain them: randomized $\to$ eligible. Contrapositive: not eligible $\to$ not randomized.
step: The study was not eligible, so it was not randomized. That is the inference.
step: The second statement also gives not eligible $\to$ does not control for selection effects. Notice what does not follow: that an eligible study is randomized, or that a study that controls for selection effects is randomized. Those would be converse errors.

@mistakes
- Reading "A only if B" as "if B then A," which reverses the arrow.
- Treating the converse or inverse as valid after seeing a conditional.
- Reasoning from "most A are B and most B are C" to a link between A and C.
- Using real-world knowledge to decide a must-be-true question instead of the statements alone.

@practice mc
prompt: "If a student is enrolled in the seminar, then she has completed the prerequisite." Which statement must be true?
choice: If a student has completed the prerequisite, she is enrolled in the seminar.
choice*: If a student has not completed the prerequisite, she is not enrolled in the seminar.
choice: If a student is not enrolled in the seminar, she has not completed the prerequisite.
choice: Some students who completed the prerequisite are enrolled in no seminar.
hint: Which choice is the contrapositive?
solution: The contrapositive of enrolled $\to$ prerequisite is not prerequisite $\to$ not enrolled. The converse and inverse are not valid, and the claim about students enrolled in no seminar is not stated.

prompt: "Tickets are refunded only if the event is cancelled." Which must be true?
choice: If the event is cancelled, tickets are refunded.
choice*: If tickets were refunded, the event was cancelled.
choice: If tickets were not refunded, the event was not cancelled.
choice: Tickets are refunded whenever fewer than half of seats are sold.

solution: Refunded $\to$ cancelled. The other choices state the converse or inverse or add facts.

@practice mc
prompt: "The project will not be approved unless the budget is cut." Which must be true?
choice: If the budget is cut, the project will be approved.
choice*: If the project is approved, the budget was cut.
choice: If the budget is not cut, the project is approved.
choice: If the project is not approved, the budget was not cut.
hint: Treat "unless" as "if not": not cut $\to$ not approved.
solution: Not cut $\to$ not approved, so its contrapositive is approved $\to$ cut. Cutting the budget is necessary, not sufficient.

@practice ms
prompt: "If the lab is open, the door is unlocked. If the door is unlocked, the alarm is off." Suppose the lab is open. Which must be true?
choice*: The door is unlocked.
choice*: The alarm is off.
choice: Someone is working in the lab.
choice: The alarm was off before the lab opened.
hint: Chain the two conditionals starting from "lab open."
solution: Open $\to$ unlocked $\to$ alarm off. Who is in the lab and when the alarm was switched off are not stated.

@practice self
prompt: Translate into arrow notation and write the contrapositive of each: (a) "Every applicant who interviews has a complete file." (b) "A grant is renewed only if the report is on time." (c) "No late report is accepted."
hint: Plan your answer against the rubric before you write, then compare.
solution: (a) interviews $\to$ complete file; contrapositive: incomplete file $\to$ no interview. (b) renewed $\to$ report on time; contrapositive: report late $\to$ not renewed. (c) late $\to$ not accepted; contrapositive: accepted $\to$ not late.
rubric: The sufficient and necessary terms are on the correct sides of each arrow.
rubric: Each contrapositive reverses and negates both terms.
rubric: "Only if" and "no" are translated correctly.

@quiz mc
prompt: "Most of the committee's members are lawyers. Most of the committee's members are parents." Which must be true?
choice: Most of the committee's members are both lawyers and parents.
choice*: At least one of the committee's members is both a lawyer and a parent.
choice: Most lawyers are parents.
choice: No committee member is neither a lawyer nor a parent.
hint: Two majorities of the same group must share at least one member.
solution: If more than half are lawyers and more than half are parents, some member is in both groups. The other choices need more than the stimulus gives.

choice*: The grant is not renewed.
