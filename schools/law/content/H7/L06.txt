@article
Photosynthesis converts light energy into the chemical energy of sugars. Its overall equation is the reverse of respiration:
$$6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \text{light} \longrightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2.$$
It occurs in **chloroplasts**. **Thylakoids** (stacked in grana) hold the pigments and electron carriers; the fluid **stroma** holds the enzymes of the Calvin cycle. **Chlorophyll** absorbs mostly red and blue light and reflects green.

### Light-dependent reactions (thylakoid membrane)

- **Photosystem II** absorbs light and splits water: $2H_2O \rightarrow 4H^+ + 4e^- + O_2$. **The oxygen released comes from water**, not from $CO_2$.
- Electrons pass down an electron transport chain, pumping protons into the thylakoid lumen.
- **Photosystem I** re-energizes the electrons, which are used to reduce **NADP$^+$ to NADPH**.
- Protons flow through **ATP synthase** into the stroma, making ATP (**photophosphorylation**, chemiosmosis again).

Products: $O_2$, ATP and NADPH.

### Calvin cycle (stroma)

The cycle does not need light directly but needs the ATP and NADPH from the light reactions.
1. **Carbon fixation:** the enzyme **rubisco** attaches $CO_2$ to the five-carbon sugar **RuBP**, producing two three-carbon molecules of 3-PGA.
2. **Reduction:** ATP and NADPH convert 3-PGA to **G3P** (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate).
3. **Regeneration:** most G3P is used to remake RuBP, using ATP.

Fixing **3 $CO_2$** produces 6 G3P, of which 1 G3P leaves the cycle, at a cost of **9 ATP and 6 NADPH**. Two G3P can combine to make one glucose, so one glucose requires 6 $CO_2$, **18 ATP and 12 NADPH**.

### Photosynthesis and respiration together

The products of one are the reactants of the other. Plants carry out both: chloroplasts make sugar; mitochondria in the same cells oxidize sugar for ATP. Energy flows one way (light to chemical energy to heat), but carbon and oxygen cycle:

| | Photosynthesis | Cellular respiration |
|---|---|---|
| Location | chloroplast | cytosol and mitochondria |
| Carbon | $CO_2$ in, sugar out | sugar in, $CO_2$ out |
| Oxygen | released (from water) | consumed (to make water) |
| Energy | light captured into sugar | sugar energy into ATP |
| Electron carrier | NADPH | NADH, FADH$_2$ |

Some plants (C4, CAM) reduce wasteful **photorespiration**, when rubisco binds $O_2$ instead of $CO_2$ on hot, dry days.

@example
title: Cost of fixing carbon
problem: How many ATP and NADPH does the Calvin cycle use to make 1 G3P from 3 $CO_2$, and how many does it need to fix 12 $CO_2$ into 4 G3P?
step: For 3 $CO_2$ (one G3P) the cycle uses 9 ATP and 6 NADPH.
step: Per $CO_2$ fixed, that is $9/3 = 3$ ATP and $6/3 = 2$ NADPH.
step: For 12 $CO_2$ the cycle runs the equivalent of four sets of 3: $4 \times 9 = 36$ ATP and $4 \times 6 = 24$ NADPH, yielding 4 G3P.

@mistakes
- Saying the oxygen released comes from $CO_2$; it comes from the splitting of water.
- Saying the Calvin cycle is "the dark reaction" that happens at night; it depends on ATP and NADPH made in the light and is usually active only in the light.
- Mixing up NADH (respiration) and NADPH (photosynthesis).
- Thinking plants only photosynthesize; they respire constantly too.

@practice self
prompt: Trace carbon and energy for a sugar molecule in a leaf cell. Describe where a carbon atom in $CO_2$ goes in photosynthesis and then where it goes when the sugar is used for respiration, and name the forms of energy at each step.
solution: A $CO_2$ molecule enters the leaf, is fixed by rubisco onto RuBP in the stroma and ends up in G3P and then glucose; light energy was captured as ATP and NADPH in the light reactions and stored as chemical energy in the sugar's bonds. In respiration, the glucose is split in glycolysis, oxidized to $CO_2$ in pyruvate oxidation and the citric acid cycle (the carbon leaves as $CO_2$), and its energy moves into NADH and FADH2 and then ATP by the electron transport chain; some energy is lost as heat. Carbon and oxygen cycle between the two processes, but energy flows one way.
hint: Start with light, water and $CO_2$ entering the chloroplast and follow the carbon to glucose.
