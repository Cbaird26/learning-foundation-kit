@article
Electric **charge** comes in positive and negative kinds; like charges repel and unlike charges attract. Charges moving through a conductor form a **current**, and circuits are the paths they follow.

### Current, voltage, resistance

- **Current** $I$ is the rate of flow of charge, $I = \Delta q/\Delta t$, in amperes ($1\ \text{A} = 1\ \text{C/s}$). By convention it points the way positive charge would move.
- **Voltage** (potential difference) $V$ is the energy per unit charge, in volts ($1\ \text{V} = 1\ \text{J/C}$). A battery maintains a voltage across a circuit.
- **Resistance** $R$ measures how much a component opposes current, in ohms ($\Omega$).

**Ohm's law** relates them for ohmic materials:

$$V = IR.$$

### Power

The power delivered to or dissipated in a component is

$$P = IV = I^2R = \frac{V^2}{R}\qquad (\text{watts}).$$

Energy used is power times time. Utilities bill in kilowatt-hours: a $1500\ \text{W}$ heater run for $2$ hours uses $3\ \text{kWh}$.

### Series circuits

Components in **series** share one path, so:

- the **current** is the same through each;
- the **voltages** add up to the source voltage;
- the equivalent resistance is $R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2 + \cdots$

If one element breaks, the current stops everywhere.

### Parallel circuits

Components in **parallel** are connected across the same two points, so:

- the **voltage** is the same across each branch;
- the **currents** in the branches add up to the total current;
- the equivalent resistance obeys $\dfrac{1}{R_{eq}} = \dfrac{1}{R_1} + \dfrac{1}{R_2} + \cdots$

The equivalent resistance is always smaller than the smallest branch. Household outlets are wired in parallel so each device gets full voltage and works independently.

### Series-parallel circuits

Reduce the circuit step by step:

1. Replace each parallel group with its equivalent resistance.
2. Add series resistances to get the total.
3. Find the total current $I = V/R_{total}$.
4. Work backward: find the voltage across the series resistor ($IR$), subtract it from the source voltage to get the voltage across the parallel group, and divide by each branch resistance to get branch currents.

Check that currents split correctly and that $\sum IV$ in all resistors equals the battery's power.

@example
title: A series-parallel circuit
problem: A $9\ \text{V}$ battery is connected to a $2\ \Omega$ resistor in series with a parallel pair of $3\ \Omega$ and $6\ \Omega$ resistors. Find the total current and the current through the $3\ \Omega$ resistor.
step: Reduce the parallel pair: $1/R_p = 1/3 + 1/6 = 1/2$, so $R_p = 2\ \Omega$.
step: Add the series resistor: $R_{total} = 2 + 2 = 4\ \Omega$. Then $I = V/R = 9/4 = 2.25\ \text{A}$.
step: Voltage across the parallel pair: $V_p = I R_p = 2.25 \times 2 = 4.5\ \text{V}$.
step: Current in the $3\ \Omega$ branch: $4.5/3 = 1.5\ \text{A}$. The $6\ \Omega$ branch carries $0.75\ \text{A}$, and $1.5 + 0.75 = 2.25\ \text{A}$, as required.

@mistakes
- Adding parallel resistances directly instead of adding reciprocals (or forgetting to invert at the end).
- Assuming current gets "used up" as it passes through a resistor; it is the same entering and leaving, and the voltage drops.
- Applying the full battery voltage to every resistor in a series-parallel circuit.
- Confusing power (W) with energy (J or kWh).

@practice num
prompt: A $12\ \text{V}$ battery is connected across a single $4\ \Omega$ resistor. What current flows, in amperes?
answer: 3
check: 12 / 4
hint: Use Ohm's law.
hint: $I = V/R$.
solution: $I = 12/4 = 3\ \text{A}$.

@practice num
prompt: Three resistors of $10\ \Omega$, $20\ \Omega$ and $30\ \Omega$ are connected in series across a $12\ \text{V}$ battery. What current flows through the circuit, in amperes?
answer: 0.2
check: 12 / (10 + 20 + 30)
tol: 0.001
hint: In series, resistances add.
hint: $R_{eq} = 60\ \Omega$, then $I = V/R_{eq}$.
solution: $R_{eq} = 60\ \Omega$ and $I = 12/60 = 0.2\ \text{A}$, the same through all three resistors.
unit: A

@practice num
prompt: A $6\ \Omega$ resistor and a $12\ \Omega$ resistor are connected in parallel. What is their equivalent resistance in ohms?
answer: 4
check: 1 / (1 / 6 + 1 / 12)
tol: 0.001
hint: Add reciprocals for parallel resistors.
hint: $1/R_{eq} = 1/6 + 1/12 = 3/12$.
solution: $1/R_{eq} = 2/12 + 1/12 = 3/12 = 1/4$, so $R_{eq} = 4\ \Omega$, smaller than either resistor.
unit: ohms

@practice mc
prompt: Three identical light bulbs are connected in parallel to a battery. One bulb is unscrewed (removed). What happens to the other two bulbs?
choice: They go out, because the circuit is broken.
choice: They become dimmer, because the current is shared by fewer bulbs.
choice*: They stay equally bright, because each is still connected across the full battery voltage.
choice: They become brighter, because the total resistance increased.
hint: In parallel, each branch is connected directly across the battery terminals.
solution: Each parallel branch has the full battery voltage, so the current and power in the remaining bulbs are unchanged. Only the total current drawn from the battery decreases.

@practice num
prompt: A $12\ \text{V}$ battery is connected to a $4\ \Omega$ resistor in series with a parallel pair of $6\ \Omega$ and $12\ \Omega$ resistors. What total current does the battery supply, in amperes?
answer: 1.5
check: 12 / (4 + 1 / (1 / 6 + 1 / 12))
tol: 0.001
hint: Reduce the parallel pair to a single equivalent resistance first.
hint: The pair is equivalent to $4\ \Omega$; add it in series to the $4\ \Omega$ resistor.
solution: $R_p = 4\ \Omega$, so $R_{total} = 4 + 4 = 8\ \Omega$ and $I = 12/8 = 1.5\ \text{A}$.
unit: A

@quiz num
prompt: A $24\ \text{V}$ battery is connected to an $8\ \Omega$ resistor in series with a parallel pair of $12\ \Omega$ and $24\ \Omega$ resistors. What current flows through the $12\ \Omega$ resistor, in amperes?
answer: 1
check: (24 / (8 + 1 / (1 / 12 + 1 / 24))) * (1 / (1 / 12 + 1 / 24)) / 12
tol: 0.001
hint: Find the total current, then the voltage across the parallel pair.
hint: $R_p = 8\ \Omega$, $R_{total} = 16\ \Omega$, $I = 1.5\ \text{A}$ and $V_p = 12\ \text{V}$.
solution: $R_p = 8\ \Omega$, so $R_{total} = 16\ \Omega$ and $I = 24/16 = 1.5\ \text{A}$. The voltage across the pair is $1.5 \times 8 = 12\ \text{V}$, so the $12\ \Omega$ resistor carries $12/12 = 1\ \text{A}$.
unit: A

@quiz num
prompt: A $1500\ \text{W}$ space heater runs for $4$ hours. How much electrical energy does it use, in kilowatt-hours?
answer: 6
check: 1.5 * 4
hint: Convert the power to kilowatts.
hint: Energy $=$ power $\times$ time.
solution: $1500\ \text{W} = 1.5\ \text{kW}$, so $E = (1.5)(4) = 6\ \text{kWh}$.
unit: kWh
