@article
Legal readers are busy and the stakes of a misreading are high. That makes two virtues essential. **Concision** means every word earns its place. **Precision** means the words can be read only one way. Editing is how you turn a first draft into prose with both.

### Concision

- **Cut throat-clearing.** "It is important to note that" and "The fact that" add nothing.
- **Cut redundancy.** *Null and void*, *each and every*, *any and all*, *free and clear*: choose one word. (Not every pair is redundant: *offer and acceptance* names two different things.)
- **Turn nominalizations back into verbs.** "The court made a determination" becomes "The court determined"; "give consideration to" becomes "consider."
- **Prefer the active voice.** "The court denied the motion" beats "The motion was denied by the court" because it is shorter and names the actor. Use the passive on purpose: when the actor is unknown or unimportant ("The contract was signed in 2019") or when you want to emphasize the recipient.
- **Shorten prepositional chains.** "The decision of the court of appeals in the case of Smith" becomes "The court of appeals' decision in *Smith*."
- **Replace legalese with plain words.** Avoid *herein*, *aforesaid*, *said* (as a determiner), *wherein*, *prior to* (use *before*).
- **Put the subject, verb and object close together** and limit sentences to one main idea. Aim for an average near 20 to 25 words, varying the rhythm.

### Precision

- **Keep terms consistent.** In literature, varying words is style; in law, a different word suggests a different meaning. If you call it a "lease" once, do not call it an "agreement" and a "contract" later. If you define a term, use it exactly as defined.
- **Fix ambiguous pronouns.** "The manager told the employee that she was liable" (who?) is repaired by repeating the noun.
- **Place modifiers carefully.** "Only" and similar words change meaning by position: "The seller only delivered the car" versus "The seller delivered only the car."
- **Handle connectors.** *And/or* is ambiguous; choose *and* or *or*, or write "A, B or both." Parallel items in a list need parallel form.
- **Use modal verbs consistently.** In drafting, *must* (or *shall*) states a duty, *may* a permission, *may not* a prohibition, and *shall* can be ambiguous (modern drafters prefer *must* for obligations).
- **Check "including."** Does the list that follows illustrate or exhaust?
- **Use quantity and time words exactly:** "30 days after receipt," not "a reasonable time soon after."

### Editing in passes

Edit in layers. First, check **structure**: is the answer first, are the issues in order, are the paragraphs unified? Second, **paragraphs**: does each have a topic sentence and one point? Third, **sentences**: cut and simplify. Fourth, **words**: precision and consistency. Fifth, **citations and quotations**: verify format and accuracy. Finally, **proofread** slowly, preferably on paper or reading aloud; take a break between drafting and editing. Cutting a draft by 10 to 25 percent often improves it.

### Applying it

Treat every edit as a rule application: identify the defect, apply the fix, check that the meaning is unchanged. Be sure that cutting words never cuts a legally significant fact or qualification.

@example
title: Tightening a sentence
problem: Edit: "It is the opinion of this writer that the court in the case of Smith v. Jones made a determination that the contract was null and void and of no effect."
step: Cut the throat-clearing: "It is the opinion of this writer that" is unnecessary in an objective memo, which states predictions directly.
step: Restore the verb: "made a determination" becomes "determined" or "held."
step: Remove redundancy: "null and void and of no effect" becomes "void."
step: Result: "The court in *Smith v. Jones* held that the contract was void." (Cite with a pinpoint if relying on it.)

@example
title: Resolving an ambiguity
problem: Edit: "The seller shall deliver the goods or notify the buyer and refund the price within 10 days."
step: Identify the ambiguity: does "within 10 days" modify all of the acts or only the refund, and does the writer mean the seller must choose between delivering and (notifying and refunding)?
step: Decide the intended meaning, here that the seller must do one of two things within 10 days.
step: Rewrite: "Within 10 days, the seller must either (1) deliver the goods or (2) notify the buyer and refund the price."

@mistakes
- Cutting qualifications or facts that matter along with the extra words.
- Varying terms for style ("agreement," "contract," "deal") so that the reader wonders whether they differ.
- Using "and/or" or an ambiguous "shall" and leaving the duty unclear.
- Editing only for sentences and never checking the structure or citations.

@practice ms

choice*: Null and void
choice*: Each and every
choice: Offer and acceptance
choice: Landlord and tenant
hint: Which pairs name two different things?

@quiz mc
prompt: A contract says "The tenant shall pay rent and/or utilities by the first of the month." The parties dispute whether the tenant must pay both. What is the best repair?
choice: Replace "and/or" with "&."
choice: Leave it, because readers know it means both.
choice*: Rewrite to state the intended obligation: "The tenant must pay rent and utilities by the first of the month," or "The tenant must pay rent, utilities or both ..." if that is what is meant.
choice: Delete "utilities."
hint: Decide what the parties intended and say it.
solution: And/or is ambiguous. The drafter should decide the intended meaning and state it. Changing the symbol, ignoring the problem or deleting a term does not resolve it.

@quiz mc
prompt: A first draft says: "The court's holding was that the employee had been unlawfully terminated by the company." The writer wants to emphasize the employer's conduct. Which edit is best?
choice: "The employee was found to have been unlawfully terminated, as the court said in its holding."
choice*: "The court held that the company unlawfully terminated the employee."
choice: "It was held by the court that the employee's termination was unlawful."
choice: "The holding of the court was one of unlawful termination."
hint: Which version puts the actor and verb up front?
solution: The active version names the actor (the company) and the action, with fewer words. The other choices use the passive, nominalizations or vague phrasing.
