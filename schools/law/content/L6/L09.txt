@article
Article VI, clause 2 makes the Constitution, federal statutes and treaties "the supreme Law of the Land." When a valid federal law conflicts with a state law, the state law yields: it is **preempted**. Preemption is a question of congressional intent ("the ultimate touchstone"), read in light of the text and structure of the federal law. Remember two entry conditions: the federal law must be a valid exercise of federal power (Lessons 3 to 7), and it must regulate private actors, not simply tell states what to do (*Murphy v. NCAA* (2018), Lesson 6).

### The three types

1. **Express preemption.** Federal law says it preempts state law. The court reads the clause's text and scope, for example whether "requirement" includes state common-law tort duties. *Riegel v. Medtronic, Inc.* (2008) held that a federal clause preempting state "requirements" different from or in addition to federal ones for premarket-approved medical devices bars state tort claims against the device's maker.
2. **Field preemption.** Congress intends to occupy a whole field exclusively, inferred from a scheme so pervasive that there is no room for states to supplement it or from a dominant federal interest. *Arizona v. United States* (2012) found alien registration to be a field occupied by federal law and held a state alien-registration crime preempted.
3. **Conflict preemption.** A state law is preempted if:
   - **Impossibility:** it is impossible to comply with both (*Florida Lime & Avocado Growers v. Paul* (1963)). *PLIVA, Inc. v. Mensing* (2011) held that state failure-to-warn claims against generic drug makers are preempted because federal law requires a generic's label to match the brand's, so the generic maker could not unilaterally add a warning.
   - **Obstacle:** the state law stands as an obstacle to the accomplishment and execution of the full purposes and objectives of Congress (*Hines v. Davidowitz* (1941); *Geier v. American Honda Motor Co.* (2000)). *Arizona* held that a state crime of unauthorized aliens seeking work was preempted as an obstacle because Congress deliberately chose to impose penalties on employers, not on workers.

### The presumption against preemption

Where Congress legislates in a field the states have traditionally occupied, such as health and safety, the courts assume that state law is not superseded unless that was the clear and manifest purpose of Congress (*Rice v. Santa Fe Elevator Corp.* (1947)). *Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr* (1996) applied it to the scope of an express clause. The presumption does not allow a court to ignore clear text, and the Court has shown less reliance on it where the federal statute contains an express preemption clause. It carries little weight where there is a long history of federal regulation, such as in foreign affairs.

### Drugs and devices

*Wyeth v. Levine* (2009) held that state failure-to-warn claims against a brand-name drug maker were not preempted, because the maker could strengthen its label unilaterally under an FDA regulation and there was no clear evidence that the FDA would have rejected a change. *Merck Sharp & Dohme Corp. v. Albrecht* (2019) held that the "clear evidence" question is for a judge to decide, not a jury, and that it requires showing the FDA would not have approved the change. *Mutual Pharmaceutical Co. v. Bartlett* (2013) extended *Mensing* to design-defect claims against a generic maker.

### Agencies and preemption

An agency may preempt state law only when it acts within its delegated authority and in a way that has the force of law. After *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo* (2024), courts decide the best reading of the statute, including whether Congress authorized preemption, and give an agency's views only *Skidmore* respect, which depends on the agency's thoroughness and the persuasiveness of its reasoning. A bare agency statement in a preamble that state suits are preempted does not bind a court (*Wyeth*).

### Policy

Preemption secures a uniform national regime, avoids conflicting obligations and protects federal programs. The presumption against it protects state police powers and recognizes that Congress rarely intends to displace state remedies silently.

### Applying the rules

(1) Is there a valid federal law that regulates private conduct? (2) Does it contain an express preemption clause, and what does the clause cover? (3) If not, is there field preemption? (4) If not, is there conflict preemption, by impossibility or obstacle? (5) Apply the presumption against preemption where relevant.

@example
title: Impossibility for a generic drug maker
problem: Federal law requires a generic drug's label to match that of the brand-name drug and gives the generic maker no way to change it unilaterally. Patient, injured by a generic drug, sues the maker under state tort law, claiming that it should have added a warning. Is the claim preempted?
step: Identify the type: conflict preemption, since no express clause is described.
step: State law would require a stronger warning, but federal law bars the maker from changing the label, so compliance with both is impossible.
step: Under *Mensing*, such claims are preempted.
step: Conclusion: preempted. A brand-name maker, who can change its label, is treated differently under *Wyeth*.

@example
title: Obstacle preemption
problem: Congress deliberately chose to impose penalties on employers who hire unauthorized workers but not on the workers. State makes it a crime for an unauthorized worker to seek work. Is the state crime preempted?
step: Identify the type: obstacle preemption.
step: Congress made a judgment about how to enforce immigration law by regulating employers, not workers.
step: A state penalty on workers would upset that balance and interfere with federal objectives.
step: Conclusion: preempted, as in *Arizona v. United States*.

@mistakes
- Treating every overlap of federal and state law as preemption; the presumption against preemption and the need for conflict or intent apply.
- Forgetting that express clauses are read for their scope: a clause may cover only some state laws.
- Confusing impossibility (cannot comply with both) with mere inconvenience.
- Saying agency views control preemption; courts decide the best reading of the statute after *Loper Bright*.
