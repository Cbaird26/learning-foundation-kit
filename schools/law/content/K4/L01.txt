@article
Words are made of **letters**. The English alphabet has **26 letters**. Every letter has a name, and we say the names in order: a, b, c, d, e, f, g, h, i, j, k, l, m, n, o, p, q, r, s, t, u, v, w, x, y, z.

### Big and small

Each letter has two shapes. The big shape is a **capital** letter: A, B, C. The small shape is a **lowercase** letter: a, b, c. Capitals start names and sentences. Most words use lowercase.

### Vowels and consonants

Five letters are special. They are the **vowels**: a, e, i, o, u. Every word needs at least one vowel sound. The other 21 letters are **consonants**.

### Look closely

Some letters look alike. Trace them with a finger to tell them apart.

- b has its belly on the right: a stick, then a ball. Think "bat and ball".
- d has its belly on the left: a ball, then a stick.
- p and q both hang below the line. p has its belly on the right of its stick; q has its belly on the left, and its stick often has a little tail.

### Try it with your grown-up

Sing the alphabet song while pointing at each letter. Then pick three letters from your own name and find the big and small shape of each.

@example
title: Find the partner
problem: The big letter is G. Which small letter is its partner, g or q?
step: Look at the big letter G. Say its name: "gee".
step: Small g has a round top and a tail that curls down on the left.
step: Small q has a tail that goes straight down on the right, and its name is "cue". So the partner of G is g.

@mistakes
- Mixing up b and d. Remember: b has the ball on the right, like "bat and ball".
- Thinking y is always a vowel. For now, the five vowels are a, e, i, o, u.
- Skipping letters in the alphabet song. Point to each letter as you say it.
