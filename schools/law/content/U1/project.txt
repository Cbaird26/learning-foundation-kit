title: Trial evidence file: three motions in limine and a ruling memo
overview: Working from a supplied criminal case file, you will write motions in limine on three evidence issues and then act as the trial judge, ruling on every objection in a supplied transcript excerpt. The project applies relevance, character evidence, impeachment, hearsay, the Confrontation Clause and expert testimony in one file.
goal: Frame an evidence issue as a motion in limine with the governing rule, the best argument for each side and a requested ruling.
goal: Rule on live objections quickly and accurately, stating the rule and the reason for each ruling.
goal: Connect hearsay analysis, Crawford and Rule 702 in a single realistic case.
step: Read the case file and the exhibit list. List each person who made an out-of-court statement and each piece of "other act" or opinion evidence.
step: Motion 1: decide whether the prosecution may offer the 2022 bar incident, and whether the prosecution may impeach the defendant with his 2019 conviction. Write the motion for the defendant, citing Rules 404(b), 403 and 609, and anticipate the prosecution's best response.
step: Motion 2: write a motion on the victim's two statements, covering hearsay and the Confrontation Clause. Separate the on-scene statement from the next-day statement and analyze each under the hearsay exceptions and Crawford and Davis.
step: Motion 3: write a motion to exclude the prosecution's bloodstain expert under Rule 702 (as amended in 2023) and Daubert, covering qualifications, sufficiency of data, method and application.
step: Read the transcript. For each numbered objection, write a ruling (sustained, overruled, or sustained in part), the rule and one or two sentences of reasoning, and say whether a limiting instruction or a rephrased question would cure a problem.
step: Check your file: every ruling cites a rule, you handled every level of any hearsay chain, and your motions state the standard the court applies (for example Rule 104(a) or 104(b)).
material: **Case file: State v. Dorn.** Cal Dorn is charged with aggravated assault. At about 11 p.m. on a Friday at Harbor Tavern, Vic Alder was struck on the head and arm with a broken bottle. Witnesses agree the attacker wore a green jacket. Eve Park, a patron, identified Cal Dorn as the man in the green jacket at a lineup the next day and will so testify. Cal says he was elsewhere. Vic gave two statements: (1) on the scene, bleeding, while the attacker was still thought to be in the tavern, to Officer Quill: "The guy in the green jacket hit me with a bottle, he's still in here"; (2) the next afternoon, in a recorded interview at the police station with a detective, naming Cal and describing the earlier argument. Vic has since moved abroad and will not testify; the defense has never questioned him. In 2022, Cal was involved in an incident at a different bar in which he threw a glass at a bartender, was not charged, and the bartender is available to testify. In 2019 Cal was convicted of misdemeanor simple assault (maximum penalty six months) and was released in 2020. The prosecution proposes to call Dr. Soto, who will testify, using a software program she wrote, that the bloodstain pattern shows the blow came from a standing person holding the bottle in the right hand. The software has never been tested by anyone else or published, and she cannot state an error rate. The tavern's own security video was recorded over before anyone asked for it.
material: **Transcript excerpt, State v. Dorn.** The prosecution is the State. Eve Park is a prosecution witness and has already identified the defendant in court as the man in the green jacket. (1) Direct of Eve. Q: What did the man in the green jacket say to the bartender just before the fight? A: He said, "I'm going to teach that guy a lesson." Defense: Objection, hearsay. (2) Direct of Eve. Q: After the fight, what did your friend Fay tell you about what she saw? A: She said she saw the whole thing and it was the man in green. Defense: Objection, hearsay. (3) Direct of Eve. Q: And you saw him swing the bottle at Vic's head, didn't you? Defense: Objection, leading. (4) Direct of Eve. Q: In your opinion, is Cal Dorn a violent person? Defense: Objection, character evidence. (5) Cross of Eve by the defense. Q: Isn't it true that in 2021 you were convicted of a misdemeanor for lying on a loan application? State: Objection, the offense was only a misdemeanor and was long ago. (6) Cross of Eve. Q: Isn't it true that last year you lied to your landlord about a broken window? A: No. Defense then calls the landlord, who says Eve did lie. State: Objection to the landlord's testimony. (7) Direct of Officer Quill, who has not been qualified as an expert. Q: Based on your training in bloodstain analysis, what does the blood on the wall show about where the attacker stood? State: no objection by the State; Defense: Objection, improper opinion. (8) Direct of Officer Quill. Q: When you arrived and found Vic bleeding, what did he tell you? A: He said, "The guy in the green jacket hit me with a bottle, he's still in here." Defense: Objection, hearsay and the Confrontation Clause. (9) Cross of Cal Dorn by the State, after Cal testifies in his own defense. Q: You were convicted of simple assault in 2019, weren't you? Defense: Objection.

### Week by week

**Weeks 1 and 2: relevance.** Rule 401 (any tendency; fact of consequence), Rule 402 (default admissibility), Rule 403 (probative value substantially outweighed by listed dangers; Old Chief), Rule 104(a) and (b), Rule 103 and offers of proof, Rule 107 illustrative aids. Policy rules: Rule 407 (subsequent remedial measures; other purposes if disputed), Rule 408 (compromise), Rule 409 (medical payments, not accompanying statements), Rule 410 (pleas and plea discussions with a prosecutor; Mezzanatto) and Rule 411 (insurance).

**Weeks 3 and 4: character and other acts.** Rule 404(a)(1) ban; criminal exceptions (defendant, victim, homicide peacefulness); Rule 405 methods; Rule 412 and Rules 413 to 415; Rule 404(b) non-propensity purposes, notice, Huddleston, Dowling; Rule 406 habit and routine practice.

**Weeks 5 and 6: impeachment and witnesses.** Rule 608; Rule 609(a)(1), (a)(2), (b); Luce; Rule 613 (including the 2024 amendment); bias (Abel); Rules 601 to 606, 611, 612, 615.

**Weeks 7 and 8: hearsay.** Rule 801(a) to (c), non-truth uses, conduct and machines, Rule 805; Rule 801(d)(1)(A) to (C) and Tome; Rule 801(d)(2)(A) to (E) and Bourjaily.

**Weeks 9 and 10: exceptions and confrontation.** Rule 803(1) to (6), (8); Rule 804(a) and (b)(1) to (3), (6); Rule 807; Crawford, Davis, Bryant, Clark, Melendez-Diaz, Bullcoming, Smith v. Arizona, Bruton, Giles.

**Weeks 11 and 12: opinion, privilege, authentication.** Rule 701; Rule 702 as amended in 2023; Daubert, Joiner, Kumho Tire, Frye; Rules 703 to 705; Rule 501 privileges (Upjohn, Trammel, Jaffee), Rule 502; Rules 901 and 902; Rules 1002 to 1004.

- Can you run the hearsay sequence (statement, purpose, Rule 801(d), exception, Confrontation Clause) without skipping a step?

### Most common errors

- Applying Rule 403 as if it excluded evidence that is merely damaging.
- Treating other-acts evidence as inadmissible whenever it is harmful, or admitting it without a non-propensity chain.
- Calling a statement hearsay without asking what it is offered to prove, or calling it admissible because it fits an exception when the Confrontation Clause bars it.
- Forgetting that each level of a hearsay chain needs its own basis.
- Saying that gaps in an expert's basis "go only to weight."

@exam mc
prompt: Dana is on trial for assault. Her friend Fern testifies for the prosecution. On cross, the defense asks Fern, "In 2023 you were convicted of felony forgery and released in 2024, weren't you?" Later Fern denies telling Officer Ross the night of the crime, "Dana hit him first." The prosecution calls Officer Ross to testify that Fern said that. Fern's statement to Ross was unsworn. Which of the following is the best statement of the law?
choice: The forgery conviction is excluded by Rule 609(b), and Fern's statement to Ross is admissible to prove Dana hit first.
choice: The forgery conviction must be balanced under Rule 403, and Fern's statement to Ross is admissible to prove Dana hit first as a prior inconsistent statement.
choice*: The forgery conviction must be admitted under Rule 609(a)(2), and Fern's statement to Ross may be used to impeach her but not as proof that Dana hit first.
choice: The forgery conviction is inadmissible because Fern is not a defendant, and Fern's statement to Ross is hearsay for every purpose.
hint: Sort the issues: conviction type and age first, then what the statement is offered to prove and whether it qualifies under Rule 801(d)(1)(A).
solution: Forgery has a false-statement element, so Rule 609(a)(2) requires admission (the conviction is within ten years, and Fern is not a defendant). Fern's unsworn statement to a police officer was not under penalty of perjury at a proceeding, so it is not within Rule 801(d)(1)(A); it may be used to impeach under Rule 613 but not for its truth.

@exam ms
prompt: Vic is stabbed at a tavern. In Cal's trial for assault, Vic does not testify. Which of the following statements, if offered by the prosecution for their truth, are barred by the Confrontation Clause? Assume each statement otherwise fits a hearsay exception. Select all that apply.
choice*: Vic's recorded interview at the police station the next day describing what Cal did.
choice*: A sworn affidavit that Vic signed for the prosecutor a week later.
choice: Vic's 911 call, made while the attacker was still in the building, saying "Cal is stabbing me, send help."
choice: Vic's statement to his friend an hour before the attack, "I'm afraid Cal will start something tonight."
hint: Apply the primary-purpose test and ask which statements were made to create evidence for prosecution.
solution: The station interview and the affidavit were made to establish past events for prosecution, so they are testimonial and Vic was never cross-examined. The 911 call during an ongoing emergency is nontestimonial under Davis, and a private statement to a friend is nontestimonial; both are governed by the hearsay rules only.

@exam self
prompt: Zed sues Acme Corp. for injuries from a defective ladder. At trial, Zed offers (1) Acme's post-injury redesign of the ladder to prove the original design was defective, (2) a letter from Acme's lawyer to Zed's lawyer before trial offering \$30,000 to settle the claim, to prove liability, and (3) a safety inspector's written report prepared at the request of Acme's counsel after the injury, offered by Acme to show the ladder met standards. Write a short ruling memo on each item, stating the rule, how the item is offered and the best ruling.
solution: (1) Excluded under Rule 407 if offered to prove a defect or negligence: a subsequent remedial measure may not be used for that purpose, and the rule applies to product-design claims. It could be admitted for a disputed purpose such as feasibility or impeachment, but Zed offers it to prove defect. (2) Excluded under Rule 408: an offer to compromise a disputed claim is not admissible to prove the validity or amount of the claim; the pretrial timing does not matter, and a permitted other purpose such as bias is not at issue. (3) The report is hearsay when Acme offers it for its truth. It is not a regularly conducted activity record under Rule 803(6), because it was prepared at counsel's request in anticipation of litigation and its circumstances indicate a lack of trustworthiness under Rule 803(6)(E). It is inadmissible unless another exception applies; the inspector may testify live.
rubric: Applies Rule 407 to the redesign with the purpose-based analysis and its other-purpose exceptions.
rubric: Applies Rule 408 to the settlement letter and the validity-or-amount purpose.
rubric: Identifies the report as hearsay not within Rule 803(6) because of the litigation purpose and untrustworthy circumstances.
hint: For each item, name what it is offered to prove and then the rule that governs that purpose.
