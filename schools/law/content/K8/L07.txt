@lesson
title: Energy and forces
summary: Name forms of energy and explain how pushes, pulls, gravity and friction change motion.
objective: Say that energy makes things move or change and can change form.
objective: Describe a force as a push or a pull and name gravity and friction.
objective: Tell how a bigger force or a heavier object changes motion and find speed from distance and time.
reading: NASA Space Place: look up the topics "energy" and "gravity" in the kids' science pages.

@article
**Energy** is what makes things move or change. You cannot hold energy in your hand, but you can see what it does.

### Forms of energy
- **Light** energy from the Sun or a lamp.
- **Heat** energy from a warm stove or the Sun.
- **Sound** energy from a drum or your voice.
- **Motion** energy in a rolling ball or a running dog.
- **Stored** energy in a stretched rubber band, a wound-up toy or food.

Energy can change form. A toaster turns electric energy into heat and light. When you eat food, your body turns the stored energy into motion and heat.

### Forces
A **force** is a push or a pull. A force can start something moving, stop it, speed it up, slow it down or turn it.

**Gravity** is a force that pulls things toward the center of Earth. It makes a dropped ball fall and keeps you on the ground.

**Friction** is a force that slows moving things when two surfaces rub. A toy car rolls farther on a smooth floor than on a thick rug. Friction between brake pads and wheels helps a bike stop. Friction also makes rubbing hands warm.

### Bigger forces and heavier things
A bigger push makes a ball go faster. A heavier object needs a bigger push to move the same way as a light one. If forces on an object are **balanced**, it does not change its motion. If they are **unbalanced**, it starts to move, stops or changes direction.

### Speed
**Speed** tells how far something goes in a certain time. If a snail crawls 6 centimeters in 2 seconds, its speed is $6 \div 2 = 3$ centimeters each second.

@example
title: Find a speed
problem: A toy car rolls 12 meters in 4 seconds. How many meters does it roll each second?
step: Speed is distance divided by time.
step: Divide: $12 \div 4 = 3$.
step: The car goes 3 meters each second.

@example
title: Which surface has more friction?
problem: A block slides 2 meters on a rug and 5 meters on a smooth floor. Which surface has more friction?
step: More friction stops the block sooner.
step: The block stopped sooner on the rug, so the rug has more friction.

@mistakes
- Thinking that gravity only works when things fall. It is pulling on you now.
- Thinking that moving things need a force to keep going. They slow down because friction pushes against them.
- Mixing up a push with a pull. A push moves away from you and a pull moves toward you.
