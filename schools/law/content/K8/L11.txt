@article
Our **solar system** is the Sun and everything that goes around it: eight planets, moons, asteroids and comets. The Sun is a **star**. It gives us light and heat.

### The planets
In order from the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. The first four are small and rocky. The next four are much bigger. Jupiter and Saturn are giant balls of gas, and Uranus and Neptune are giants that are very cold.

### Day and night
Earth **rotates**, or spins, like a top. It turns once every 24 hours. The side facing the Sun has day. The side facing away has night. The Sun only seems to move across the sky. Really, we move as Earth spins.

Try it with a lamp and a ball. Put a small sticker on the ball. Turn the ball slowly in the lamp's light. The sticker goes from light to dark and back to light.

### A year
Earth also **orbits**, or travels around, the Sun. One trip takes about 365 days, which is one year.

### The seasons
Earth's axis (the imaginary stick through its poles) is **tilted**. As Earth goes around the Sun, the tilt means the north half of Earth leans toward the Sun for part of the year, and away for the other part. Leaning toward the Sun gives more direct sunlight and longer days. That is **summer**. Leaning away gives **winter**. When the north has summer, the south has winter. The seasons are not caused by Earth being closer to the Sun.

### The Moon
The Moon orbits Earth in about a month. It does not make its own light. It shines because it reflects sunlight. We see different parts of its lit half as it goes around. These are the **phases**, from the new moon to the full moon and back.

@example
title: Day or night?
problem: A child in the part of Earth turned away from the Sun looks at the sky. Is it day or night?
step: The side of Earth facing away from the Sun is dark.
step: It is night.

@mistakes
- Thinking the Sun goes around Earth. Earth is spinning and going around the Sun.
- Thinking summer happens because Earth is closer to the Sun. The tilt causes the seasons.
- Thinking the Moon makes its own light. It reflects sunlight.
