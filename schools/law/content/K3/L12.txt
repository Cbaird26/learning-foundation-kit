@article
A **coordinate plane** is a grid made of two number lines that cross. The flat number line going left and right is the **x-axis**. The number line going up and down is the **y-axis**. They meet at the **origin**, which is the point $(0, 0)$.

### Ordered pairs

Every point has an address called an **ordered pair**, two numbers in brackets such as $(3, 2)$.

- The first number tells how far to go **right**. This is the $x$-coordinate.
- The second number tells how far to go **up**. This is the $y$-coordinate.

So $(3, 2)$ means: start at the origin, go right $3$, then up $2$. Put a dot there.

A way to remember: "walk **along** the hall, then **up** the stairs." Along first, up second.

The order matters. $(3, 2)$ and $(2, 3)$ are different points.

### Points on the axes

The point $(4, 0)$ is $4$ right and $0$ up, so it sits on the x-axis. The point $(0, 5)$ is $0$ right and $5$ up, so it sits on the y-axis.

### Drawing a picture

Plot dots from a list, then join them with straight lines in order. The points $(1, 1)$, $(1, 4)$, $(4, 4)$ and $(4, 1)$ make a square when joined.

### Distance on a grid

If two points have the same $y$-coordinate, they are on a flat line. Count the squares between them, or subtract the $x$ numbers. From $(1, 2)$ to $(6, 2)$ is $6 - 1 = 5$ steps.

If two points have the same $x$-coordinate, they are on an up-and-down line. Subtract the $y$ numbers.

### Plot a picture on a grid

You can plan a map or picture on a grid, such as a garden. Each plant gets an ordered pair. This is how your project will work.

@example
title: Plot and measure
problem: Plot the points $(3, 2)$ and $(3, 6)$. How far apart are they?
step: For $(3, 2)$, start at the origin, go right $3$, then up $2$. Mark a dot.
step: For $(3, 6)$, go right $3$, then up $6$. Mark a second dot.
step: Both points have $x = 3$, so they are on an up-and-down line. Subtract the $y$ numbers: $6 - 2 = 4$. The points are $4$ units apart.

@mistakes
- Swapping the order and going up first. Always go right first, then up.
- Counting the starting dot when finding a distance. Count the steps between dots.
- Forgetting that points on an axis have a $0$ in one place.

solution: The first number is the $x$-coordinate (right) and the second is the $y$-coordinate (up). So go right $3$, then up $5$.

answer: 7
check: 3 + 4
hint: Moving up changes the second number.
hint: Add $4$ to the $y$-coordinate, $3$.
solution: Moving up adds to $y$. The new point is $(2, 7)$, so the $y$-coordinate is $7$.

answer: 5
check: 6 - 1
hint: The $y$ numbers match, so only $x$ changes.
hint: Count from $x = 1$ to $x = 6$.
solution: Subtract the $x$-coordinates: $6 - 1 = 5$ units.
