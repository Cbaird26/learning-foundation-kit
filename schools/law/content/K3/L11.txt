@article
An **angle** is the space between two lines that meet at a point, like the opening of a door. We measure angles in **degrees**. The little circle sign is $^\circ$.

### Kinds of angles

- A **right angle** is $90^\circ$. It looks like the corner of a book.
- An **acute angle** is smaller than $90^\circ$. It is sharp and narrow.
- An **obtuse angle** is bigger than $90^\circ$ but smaller than $180^\circ$. It is wide.
- A **straight angle** is $180^\circ$. It is a straight line.

A full turn all the way around is $360^\circ$.

### Using a protractor

A protractor is a half-circle ruler with degrees on it. Put the center hole on the point of the angle. Line up one side of the angle with the zero line. Read the number where the other side crosses the scale. Use the scale that starts at zero on your side.

### Angles in a triangle

A triangle has three angles. They always add up to $180^\circ$.

$$\text{angle 1} + \text{angle 2} + \text{angle 3} = 180^\circ$$

If two angles are $60^\circ$ and $70^\circ$, the third is $180 - 60 - 70 = 50$, so $50^\circ$.

### Sorting triangles

By sides: an **equilateral** triangle has $3$ equal sides. An **isosceles** triangle has at least $2$ equal sides. A **scalene** triangle has no equal sides.

By angles: a **right** triangle has one right angle.

### Four-sided shapes

A shape with $4$ straight sides is a **quadrilateral**.

- A **rectangle** has $4$ right angles.
- A **square** has $4$ right angles and $4$ equal sides.
- A **rhombus** has $4$ equal sides.
- A **parallelogram** has two pairs of parallel sides.
- A **trapezoid** has at least one pair of parallel sides.

A square is also a rectangle and a rhombus. Shapes can belong to more than one group.

@example
title: Find the missing angle
problem: A triangle has angles of $35^\circ$ and $90^\circ$. What is the third angle? What kind of triangle is it?
step: The three angles add to $180^\circ$. Add the two you know: $35 + 90 = 125$.
step: Subtract from $180$: $180 - 125 = 55$. The third angle is $55^\circ$.
step: One angle is a right angle, so this is a right triangle.

@mistakes
- Forgetting that the three angles of a triangle must add to $180^\circ$, not $360^\circ$.
- Reading the wrong scale on the protractor. Check whether your angle is acute or obtuse first.
- Thinking a square is not a rectangle.
