@article
A **decimal** is a way to write parts of a whole. The dot is called the **decimal point**. Whole numbers go to the left of the dot. Parts of a whole go to the right.

### Tenths

Cut a bar into $10$ equal pieces. One piece is one **tenth**. We write $\frac{1}{10} = 0.1$. Three pieces are $0.3$. Ten pieces make the whole bar, which is $1.0$.

### Hundredths

Now cut each tenth into $10$ smaller pieces. The bar has $100$ pieces. One small piece is one **hundredth**: $\frac{1}{100} = 0.01$.

In $4.63$:

- the $4$ is in the ones place
- the $6$ is in the tenths place (six tenths)
- the $3$ is in the hundredths place (three hundredths)

We read $4.63$ as "four and sixty-three hundredths".

### Money is a decimal

A dollar is cut into $100$ cents. One cent is one hundredth of a dollar. A dime is $10$ cents, which is one tenth of a dollar. So \$0.30 means $3$ dimes, and \$0.35 means $3$ dimes and $5$ pennies.

### Comparing decimals

Line up the places and compare from the left. Compare $0.5$ and $0.45$. Write $0.5$ as $0.50$. Now compare $50$ hundredths and $45$ hundredths. So $0.5$ is bigger than $0.45$.

Adding zeros at the end does not change a decimal: $0.5 = 0.50$. Many people think $0.45$ is bigger because $45$ is bigger than $5$. Check by writing the same number of places.

### Fractions and decimals

Tenths and hundredths turn into decimals easily:

$$\frac{7}{10} = 0.7, \qquad \frac{3}{10} + \frac{4}{100} = 0.34$$

@example
title: Read and compare decimals
problem: Which is bigger, $0.6$ or $0.58$? Name the hundredths digit of $0.58$.
step: Write $0.6$ with two decimal places: $0.60$. That is $60$ hundredths.
step: $0.58$ is $58$ hundredths. Since $60$ is more than $58$, $0.6$ is bigger.
step: In $0.58$ the first digit after the point is the tenths digit ($5$) and the next is the hundredths digit. The hundredths digit is $8$.

@mistakes
- Thinking a decimal with more digits is bigger, for example that $0.45$ is bigger than $0.5$.
- Mixing up tenths and hundredths. The first place after the point is tenths.
- Writing $\frac{3}{100}$ as $0.3$ instead of $0.03$.
