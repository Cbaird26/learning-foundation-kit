### Pleading and Rule 12

A complaint needs "a short and plain statement of the claim showing that the pleader is entitled to relief" (FRCP 8(a)(2)). Under *Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly* (2007) and *Ashcroft v. Iqbal* (2009), the complaint must contain factual allegations that make the claim **plausible**; on a **Rule 12(b)(6)** motion the court accepts well-pleaded facts as true but not legal conclusions. Fraud and mistake must be pleaded with **particularity** (Rule 9(b)). **Rule 11** requires a signing attorney to certify a reasonable inquiry; a motion for sanctions must first be served and gives the opponent a **21-day safe harbor** to withdraw the challenged paper.

A defendant answers within **21 days** after service (60 if service was waived); a Rule 12 motion postpones the answer until 14 days after the ruling. The Rule 12(b) defenses are lack of subject-matter jurisdiction, lack of personal jurisdiction, improper venue, insufficient process, insufficient service and failure to state a claim, plus failure to join a required party under Rule 19. Under Rule 12(g)–(h), the defenses of **personal jurisdiction, venue, process and service are waived** if omitted from a defendant's first Rule 12 motion or from its answer. Failure to state a claim and failure to join may be raised later, and **subject-matter jurisdiction can never be waived**.

### Amendment

A party may amend once **as a matter of course** within **21 days after serving** the pleading or, if a responsive pleading is required, within **21 days after service of a responsive pleading or of a Rule 12(b), (e) or (f) motion**, whichever of those two is earlier. After that, amendment needs the opposing party's written consent or leave of court, which is to be freely given when justice requires. An amendment **relates back** (Rule 15(c)) if the law allows it, it arises from the same conduct, transaction or occurrence, or, for a new defendant, the defendant received notice within the Rule 4(m) period and knew or should have known the action would have been brought against it but for a mistake about identity.

### Joinder and class actions

A **compulsory counterclaim** (same transaction or occurrence, Rule 13(a)) must be asserted or it is lost. A defendant may **implead** (Rule 14) a nonparty who may be liable to the defendant for all or part of the plaintiff's claim; impleader needs leave only after 14 days following the answer. Rule 19 governs required parties, Rule 20 permissive joinder, Rule 22 interpleader and Rule 24 intervention. A **class action** needs numerosity, commonality, typicality and adequacy (Rule 23(a)) and one Rule 23(b) category, such as (b)(3): common questions **predominate** and the class action is **superior** (*Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Dukes* (2011) requires a common contention capable of classwide resolution). *Trump v. CASA, Inc.* (2025) held that universal injunctions likely exceed the equitable authority Congress gave federal courts: relief is limited to what is needed to give the plaintiffs complete relief, and the Court did not decide the availability of class actions as a way to obtain broader relief.

### Discovery

Under Rule 26(b)(1), a party may discover any **nonprivileged** matter that is **relevant to a claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the case**. **Work product** (prepared in anticipation of litigation) is discoverable only on a showing of **substantial need** and inability to obtain the equivalent without **undue hardship**, and mental impressions are almost absolutely protected (*Hickman v. Taylor* (1947)). Limits: **10 depositions** per side, **25 interrogatories** per party including discrete subparts (Rule 33), and requests for admission are **deemed admitted** unless answered or objected to within 30 days (Rule 36). **Rule 37** provides for motions to compel and sanctions. Effective **December 1, 2025**, new **Rule 16.1** addresses the early management of multidistrict litigation, and amended **Rules 16(b) and 26(f)** require the parties' discovery plan to address how they will describe withheld privileged material.

### Summary judgment, trial and post-trial

**Summary judgment** (Rule 56(a)) is proper if there is **no genuine dispute of material fact** and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. A movant without the burden of proof may point to the absence of evidence (*Celotex Corp. v. Catrett* (1986)); a dispute is genuine if a reasonable jury could find for the nonmovant (*Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.* (1986)), and the court views the evidence in the light most favorable to the nonmovant. The **Seventh Amendment** preserves jury trial in suits at law; a demand must be served within **14 days after the last pleading** directed to the issue (Rule 38), and legal issues common to legal and equitable claims go to the jury first (*Beacon Theatres, Inc. v. Westover* (1959)). *SEC v. Jarkesy* (2024) requires a jury when the SEC seeks civil penalties for securities fraud. **JMOL** (Rule 50(a)) must be made before the case goes to the jury and renewed within **28 days** after judgment; a motion for **new trial** (Rule 59) is also due within 28 days.

### Preclusion and appeals

**Claim preclusion** bars a later suit on the same claim (the same transaction) between the same parties or their privies after a valid final judgment on the merits, including claims that could have been brought. **Issue preclusion** bars relitigation of an issue that was identical, actually litigated, determined and essential to the prior judgment, against a party who had a full and fair opportunity. **Nonmutual defensive** use is allowed; **nonmutual offensive** use is discretionary (*Parklane Hosiery Co. v. Shore* (1979)). Appeal lies from a **final judgment** (28 U.S.C. § 1291); interlocutory orders are appealable if they involve injunctions (§ 1292(a)(1)), are certified (§ 1292(b)) or fall within the collateral-order doctrine. A notice of appeal is due **30 days** after entry of judgment.

@example
title: Waiver of a Rule 12 defense
problem: Dan is sued in a state far from his home. He files a Rule 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss for failure to state a claim, which is denied. He then files an answer and in it asserts lack of personal jurisdiction. Can he press the defense?
step: Rule 12(g)(2) requires a party who makes a Rule 12 motion to consolidate all Rule 12(b)(2)–(5) defenses then available to it.
step: Dan omitted the personal jurisdiction defense from his first Rule 12 motion.
step: Under Rule 12(h)(1), that defense is waived.
step: Dan cannot press it, though he could still raise failure to state a claim later and subject-matter jurisdiction at any time.

@example
title: Preclusion across suits
problem: Pam sues Zed for negligence over a car collision and loses; the court finds Pam was not injured. Pam then sues Zed over the same collision for a different injury she claims. Is she barred?
step: The first suit ended in a valid final judgment on the merits between the same parties.
step: The second suit arises from the same transaction, the collision, so it is the same claim.
step: Claim preclusion bars claims that were or could have been brought in the first suit, even new theories or injuries.
step: Pam is barred.

@mistakes
- Treating a Rule 12(b)(6) motion as allowing the court to decide disputed facts.
- Forgetting that Rule 12(h)(1) waiver reaches personal jurisdiction, venue, process and service, but not subject-matter jurisdiction.
- Confusing the 21-day Rule 15(a)(1) clock with the time to answer, or the time to move for JMOL.
- Applying issue preclusion to an issue that was not essential to the judgment or not actually litigated.
