### Week by week

- **Week 1: the exams.** The legacy UBE: MBE (200 questions, 25 unscored, 50%), MEE (six 30-minute essays, 30%), MPT (two 90-minute tasks, 20%); each jurisdiction sets passing scores. NextGen: first given July 2026 in ten jurisdictions, more in July 2027, nearly all by July 2028, with the legacy UBE through February 2028. Pace: 108 seconds per MBE question.
- **Week 2: planning.** Plan backward from the exam; retrieval, spacing, feedback; the five error types; the error log; dated tasks.
- **Weeks 3 and 4: contracts and sales.** Common law versus UCC Article 2; offer, firm offer (§ 2-205), mailbox rule; § 2-207; consideration, pre-existing duty, § 2-209, promissory estoppel; statute of frauds and merchant confirmation (§ 2-201); parol evidence; defenses; conditions, perfect tender and cure; repudiation and adequate assurance; expectation damages, cover and resale; warranties; assignment, delegation, beneficiaries.
- **Weeks 5 and 6: torts.** Negligence elements, per se, res ipsa; duty limits, premises, emotional distress; actual and proximate cause; contributory and comparative fault, joint and several liability; intentional torts and privileges; strict liability and products; defamation by plaintiff type; vicarious liability.
- **Weeks 7 and 8: real property.** Estates and future interests, the Rule Against Perpetuities, co-ownership, landlord-tenant; contracts of sale, deeds and covenants, recording acts, mortgages, easements and servitudes, adverse possession.
- **Weeks 9 and 10: civil procedure.** Subject-matter jurisdiction, removal, personal jurisdiction (*International Shoe*, *Daimler*, *Ford*, *Mallory*), venue and transfer, service, Erie; pleading and Rule 12 waiver, amendment, joinder and class actions, discovery (including the December 1, 2025 amendments), summary judgment, preclusion, appeals; *Trump v. CASA*.
- **Weeks 11 and 12: mixed practice and repair.** The six-step method, distractor patterns, checkpoints, cross-subject rules; ranking weak topics, repair sheets, delayed retests.

### Most common errors

Applying mirror-image acceptance to a goods contract; forgetting that a revocation is effective on receipt and a mailed acceptance on dispatch; treating every intervening cause as superseding; applying the wrong comparative-fault bar; confusing recording-act types; forgetting an LLC takes its members' citizenship; waiving personal jurisdiction by omitting it from the first Rule 12 motion; mistaking a reading error for a content gap.

@exam self
prompt: Dana, a delivery driver employed by Fast Parcel Inc., negligently runs a stop sign while making a delivery and injures Pia. Pia, a citizen of State A, sues Dana (a citizen of State B) and Fast Parcel (incorporated in State A, with its principal place of business in State A) in a State A state court for \$300,000 under state law. Dana wants to remove the case. Write a short analysis of (1) whether Fast Parcel is liable for Dana's negligence and (2) whether the case is removable to federal court.
hint: Is Dana within the scope of employment, and what must exist for a case to be removed?
hint: Check whether the case could have been brought in federal court, including complete diversity.
solution: (1) Respondeat superior: an employer is vicariously liable for torts an employee commits within the scope of employment. Dana was making a delivery, which is within the scope of employment, so Fast Parcel is liable along with Dana (the two are jointly and severally liable in most states for the same indivisible harm). (2) Removal: a defendant may remove only an action that could have been filed in federal court (28 U.S.C. § 1441(a)). There is no federal question. Diversity requires complete diversity, and Pia and Fast Parcel are both citizens of State A, so it is lacking. Also, the forum defendant rule would bar removal of a diversity case if a properly joined and served defendant is a citizen of the forum state, as Fast Parcel is. The case is not removable and stays in state court.
rubric: States respondeat superior and applies the scope-of-employment test.
rubric: States that removal requires original federal jurisdiction and identifies the lack of complete diversity.
rubric: Notes the forum-defendant rule as an additional bar.
rubric: Reaches clear conclusions on both questions.
