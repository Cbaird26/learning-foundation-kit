@article
### Subject-matter jurisdiction

Federal courts have limited jurisdiction. **Federal question** jurisdiction (28 U.S.C. § 1331) exists when the plaintiff's **well-pleaded complaint** shows a claim arising under federal law; a federal defense or anticipated defense (even preemption) does not count (*Louisville & Nashville R.R. v. Mottley* (1908)). A state-law claim can also arise under federal law when it necessarily raises a federal issue that is actually disputed and substantial, and a federal forum can hear it without upsetting the federal-state balance (*Grable & Sons Metal Products v. Darue Engineering* (2005)). **Diversity** jurisdiction (§ 1332(a)) needs **complete diversity** (no plaintiff shares citizenship with any defendant, *Strawbridge v. Curtiss* (1806)) and an amount in controversy that **exceeds \$75,000**, exclusive of interest and costs, claimed in good faith. A single plaintiff may **aggregate** all claims against one defendant; separate plaintiffs generally may not. An individual is a citizen of her **domicile**; a corporation is a citizen of its state of **incorporation** and of its **principal place of business**, meaning its **nerve center** where officers direct the business (*Hertz Corp. v. Friend* (2010)); an LLC or partnership takes the citizenship of **all its members**. **Supplemental jurisdiction** (§ 1367(a)) reaches claims in the same case or controversy as a claim within original jurisdiction; § 1367(b) bars certain plaintiff-asserted claims that would destroy diversity, and § 1367(c) lets a court decline.

### Removal

A defendant may remove a civil action that could have been filed in federal court (28 U.S.C. § 1441(a)). Notice of removal must be filed within **30 days** of the defendant's receipt of the complaint through formal service; **all served defendants must join or consent**. A diversity case cannot be removed if any properly joined and served defendant is a citizen of the **forum state**, and diversity removal is barred more than **one year** after commencement unless the plaintiff acted in bad faith. A case removed without subject-matter jurisdiction must be remanded at any time; other defects must be raised within 30 days.

### Personal jurisdiction

A court needs a statutory basis (usually a state **long-arm statute**, FRCP 4(k)(1)(A)) and must satisfy due process: the defendant must have **minimum contacts** such that the suit does not offend "traditional notions of fair play and substantial justice" (*International Shoe Co. v. Washington* (1945)). **General jurisdiction** exists where the defendant is "at home": an individual's domicile, a corporation's place of incorporation and principal place of business (*Daimler AG v. Bauman* (2014)). **Specific jurisdiction** requires purposeful availment, a claim that **arises out of or relates to** the forum contacts, and reasonableness; the claim need not be strictly caused by the contacts, so a manufacturer that systematically serves a market for a product may be sued there for injuries that product causes in the forum (*Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court* (2021)). **Consent**, including a statute conditioning registration to do business on consent to general jurisdiction, can support jurisdiction (*Mallory v. Norfolk Southern Railway Co.* (2023)). **Presence** when served in the forum supports "tag" jurisdiction (*Burnham v. Superior Court* (1990)). **Notice** must be reasonably calculated to inform the defendant (*Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co.* (1950)).

### Service, venue and transfer

Under FRCP 4, the plaintiff must serve the summons and complaint within **90 days** of filing the complaint (Rule 4(m)). A defendant who **waives service** under Rule 4(d) has **60 days** after the request was sent to answer (90 days if sent outside the United States). Personal service, leaving papers at the defendant's usual abode with a person of suitable age and discretion, or delivery to an authorized agent are valid.

**Venue** (§ 1391(b)) lies in a district where any defendant resides (if all reside in the same state), a district where a **substantial part of the events** occurred, or, if neither exists, any district where a defendant is subject to personal jurisdiction. A court may transfer a case under **§ 1404(a)** for convenience and in the interest of justice to a district where it might have been brought or to which the parties consent; a valid **forum-selection clause** is given controlling weight absent extraordinary circumstances (*Atlantic Marine Construction Co. v. U.S. District Court* (2013)). **Forum non conveniens** is used when the better forum is a foreign or state court.

### The Erie doctrine

In diversity cases, a federal court applies **state substantive law** (*Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins* (1938)) and federal procedure, and uses the **forum state's choice-of-law rules** (*Klaxon*). If a valid **federal statute or Federal Rule** directly covers the question, it governs (*Hanna v. Plumer* (1965)). If not, the court asks whether applying a federal practice rather than state law would cause forum shopping or inequitable administration of the laws (the "twin aims"), with *Byrd* weighing federal interests such as the jury right.

@example
title: Diversity with a corporation
problem: Pia, a citizen of Ohio, sues Acme Corp. in federal court on a state-law contract claim for \$120,000. Acme is incorporated in Delaware, and its officers direct the business from Ohio. Is there diversity jurisdiction?
step: Amount in controversy: \$120,000 exceeds \$75,000, satisfied.
step: Pia is a citizen of Ohio.
step: Acme is a citizen of Delaware (incorporation) and of Ohio (nerve center).
step: Pia and Acme are both Ohio citizens, so complete diversity is lacking and there is no diversity jurisdiction.

@example
title: Removal deadline
problem: Dev is served with a state-court complaint presenting a federal claim on Monday, March 2, 2026. Is a notice of removal filed on April 3, 2026, timely?
step: The 30-day period runs from formal service; day 1 is March 3.
step: March has 31 days, so day 29 is March 31 and day 30 is April 1.
step: The deadline is April 1, 2026.
step: A notice filed April 3 is untimely.

@mistakes
- Treating a federal defense, or a federal counterclaim, as creating federal question jurisdiction.
- Counting only a corporation's state of incorporation, forgetting its principal place of business.
- Treating the forum-defendant rule as applying to federal question removal; it applies only to diversity removal.
- Saying that a corporation is subject to general jurisdiction anywhere it does substantial business.
