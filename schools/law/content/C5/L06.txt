@article
A market is **perfectly competitive** when there are many small buyers and sellers, the product is homogeneous, there are no barriers to entry or exit, and participants have good information. Each firm is a **price taker**: it can sell as much as it likes at the market price but nothing at a higher price. The demand curve facing one firm is therefore horizontal at the market price $P$.

### Profit maximization

**Profit** is total revenue minus total economic cost. For a price taker, $TR = PQ$, so marginal revenue (the extra revenue from one more unit) is just $MR = P$. A firm in any market maximizes profit by expanding output while marginal revenue exceeds marginal cost and stopping where they are equal; for a price taker,

$$P = MR = MC,$$

on the **rising** part of the marginal cost curve (beyond that point producing more would cost more than it earns). Marginal cost is the firm's supply curve above the shutdown price, and adding up the firms' supply curves gives the market supply curve.

With $TC = F + cQ + aQ^2$, the condition $P = c + 2aQ$ gives $Q = (P - c)/(2a)$.

### The shutdown rule

In the short run fixed costs must be paid whether or not the firm produces, so they are sunk and should not affect the decision. Compare operating with shutting down:

- If the firm produces, its loss is $FC - (TR - VC)$.
- If it shuts down, its loss is $FC$.

So the firm should keep operating if $TR \ge VC$, that is, if $P \ge AVC$ at the best output. The **shutdown price** is the minimum of $AVC$. If $AVC \le P < ATC$, the firm loses money but loses less by operating, since revenue covers all variable cost and part of fixed cost. If $P < AVC$, it shuts down in the short run.

### Long-run equilibrium

When firms earn **positive economic profit**, new firms enter, market supply shifts right, and the price falls. When firms earn **losses**, firms exit, supply shifts left, and price rises. Entry and exit stop when economic profit is zero, which in the long run means

$$P = MC = ATC \text{ at the minimum of } ATC.$$

At that point the market is efficient in two senses: **productive efficiency** (each good is produced at the lowest possible average cost) and **allocative efficiency** ($P = MC$, so the marginal value of the good to buyers equals its marginal cost). Zero economic profit does not mean owners earn nothing: their opportunity cost is already counted as a cost, so they earn a normal return.

Real markets are rarely perfectly competitive, but the model is a benchmark. In antitrust, courts and economists ask how far a market departs from it, and the benchmark is used to describe what "competitive pricing" means.

@example
title: Profit maximization and the shutdown decision
problem: A price-taking firm has $TC = 100 + 10Q + Q^2$. (a) At a price of \$40, find the profit-maximizing output and the profit. (b) At a price of \$14, should it operate or shut down in the short run? (c) What price is the shutdown price?
step: $MC = 10 + 2Q$. Setting $P = MC$: $40 = 10 + 2Q$, so $Q = 15$.
step: $TR = 40 \times 15 = 600$ and $TC = 100 + 150 + 225 = 475$, so profit is $600 - 475 = 125$.
step: At $P = 14$: $14 = 10 + 2Q$ gives $Q = 2$. Revenue is $28$ and variable cost is $10(2) + 4 = 24$, so $TR > VC$. Profit is $28 - 124 = -96$.
step: Shutting down would lose the fixed cost of 100, which is worse than losing 96, so the firm should operate. (Here $AVC = 10 + Q$ is smallest at $Q = 0$, with value 10, so the shutdown price is 10; at any price above 10, $P > AVC$ at the chosen output.)

@mistakes
- Setting price equal to average total cost to find output; the output decision uses marginal cost.
- Shutting down whenever profit is negative; the firm should shut down only if revenue does not cover variable cost.
- Treating fixed costs as avoidable in the short run.
- Believing zero economic profit means the owners earn nothing; a normal return is part of cost.

hint: $Q = 3$; revenue is 30 and total cost is $200 + 12 + 9$.
solution: $Q = 3$, $TR = 30$ and $TC = 200 + 12 + 9 = 221$, so profit is $30 - 221 = -191$ dollars. The loss is smaller than the fixed cost of 200, so the firm keeps operating in the short run.

@practice num
prompt: Every firm in a competitive industry has total cost $TC = 225 + Q^2$. In long-run equilibrium, what is the market price in dollars?
answer: 30
check: min(225 / q + q for q in range(1, 200))
unit: dollars
hint: In the long run, price equals the minimum of average total cost.
hint: Minimum $ATC$ occurs where $MC = 2Q$ equals $225/Q + Q$.
solution: $Q^2 = 225$, so $Q = 15$ and $ATC = 15 + 15 = 30$. Long-run price is 30 dollars.

choice: Produce until price equals average total cost.
