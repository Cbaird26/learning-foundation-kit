objective: Cut a number line from 0 to 1 into equal parts and name each mark.

reading: Khan Academy, 3rd grade math: look up the topics "Fractions on a number line" and "Comparing fractions".

@article
A number line shows numbers in order. The space from 0 to 1 is one whole. We can cut that space into equal parts, just like a pizza.

### Marks for thirds

Cut the space from 0 to 1 into 3 equal parts. There are 2 marks in the middle. The first mark is $\frac{1}{3}$. The second mark is $\frac{2}{3}$. The end is $\frac{3}{3}$, which is 1.

To place a fraction, the bottom number tells how many equal parts between 0 and 1. The top number tells how many of the parts to count from 0.

### Comparing with strips

Take paper strips that are the same length. Fold one in halves, one in thirds, one in fourths and one in eighths. Line them up under each other. You can see which parts are bigger.

You will see that $\frac{1}{2}$ is longer than $\frac{1}{3}$, and $\frac{1}{3}$ is longer than $\frac{1}{4}$. When the top number is 1, the bigger bottom number means the smaller piece.

### Same spot, different names

Fold a strip in fourths and look at the second fold. It is at the middle, exactly where $\frac{1}{2}$ is. So $\frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$. Fractions that name the same spot are **equal fractions**. Also, $\frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}$.

### Comparing

A fraction farther to the right on the number line is greater. If two fractions have the same bottom number, look at the top numbers: $\frac{3}{4}$ is more than $\frac{1}{4}$.

@example
title: Mark a fraction on a number line
problem: A number line from 0 to 1 is cut into 3 equal parts. Where are $\frac{1}{3}$ and $\frac{2}{3}$?
step: Cut the line into 3 equal parts. There are 2 marks between 0 and 1.
step: Count one part from 0 to find $\frac{1}{3}$. Count two parts from 0 to find $\frac{2}{3}$.
step: The marks are in order $0$, $\frac{1}{3}$, $\frac{2}{3}$, $1$.

@example
title: Compare with a number line
problem: Which is greater, $\frac{1}{2}$ or $\frac{1}{3}$?
step: Cut one number line into halves and another into thirds.
step: The mark for $\frac{1}{2}$ is farther from 0 than the mark for $\frac{1}{3}$.
step: So $\frac{1}{2}$ is greater than $\frac{1}{3}$.

@mistakes
- Counting the marks instead of the spaces between them.
- Thinking $\frac{1}{8}$ is bigger than $\frac{1}{2}$ because 8 is bigger than 2.
- Making unequal parts when you fold or draw.
