title: Reading fluency
summary: Read a passage smoothly, at a steady pace, with the right pauses and a good voice.
objective: Read words correctly without guessing.
objective: Use punctuation to know when to pause or change your voice.
objective: Practice a passage several times to read it more smoothly.
reading: ReadWorks, free reading passages: pick a short passage and read it aloud three times.

@article
**Fluency** means reading smoothly, like talking. A fluent reader reads the words right, not too slow and not too fast, and makes the reading sound like a story.

### Three parts of fluency

- **Accuracy:** you read the words correctly.
- **Pace:** you read at a steady speed. Not a race! Too fast and the listener cannot follow. Too slow and the words break apart.
- **Expression:** your voice goes up, goes down and shows feelings.

### Punctuation tells you what to do

- A **comma** (,) means take a tiny pause, like a breath.
- A **period** (.) means stop and pause a little longer.
- A **question mark** (?) means your voice goes up at the end.
- An **exclamation mark** (!) means read with strong feeling.

### How to get smoother

Read the same short passage three times. The first time, sound out hard words. The second time, read it more smoothly. The third time, add your best voice. Reading with a grown-up, taking turns, also helps. They read a sentence, then you read it back.

Your reading gets better when you practice a little every day. Short practice, like five minutes, works better than one very long practice.

@example
title: Reading with punctuation
problem: How do you read this? "Look, a frog! Is it hiding?"
step: At the comma after "Look", take a tiny pause.
step: The exclamation mark after "frog" tells you to read with surprise.
step: The question mark at the end means your voice goes up on "hiding".

@mistakes
- Racing through the words as fast as possible. Fast is not the same as fluent.
- Ignoring punctuation and reading everything in one flat voice.
- Reading only once. Reading the same passage again makes it smoother.

@practice mc
prompt: What should you do when you see a period at the end of a sentence?
choice*: Stop and pause a little.
choice: Read faster.
choice: Make your voice go up like a question.
choice: Skip to the next word with no pause.
hint: A period means the sentence is finished. What do you do when something is finished?
solution: A period tells you to stop and pause before you read the next sentence.

@practice mc
prompt: Which sentence should you read with a voice that goes up at the end?
choice*: Can we go to the park?
choice: We went to the park.
choice: What a big park!
choice: The park is green.
hint: Look at the mark at the end. Which mark means asking?
solution: The question mark in "Can we go to the park?" tells you to let your voice go up at the end.

@practice num
prompt: Sam reads 40 words in 1 minute. If he keeps the same steady pace, how many words does he read in 2 minutes?
answer: 80
check: 40 * 2
hint: A steady pace means he reads the same number of words each minute.
hint: Add 40 words for the first minute and 40 words for the second minute.
solution: 40 + 40 = 80. Sam reads 80 words in 2 minutes.

@practice ms

choice*: Paying attention to punctuation

hint: Think about what a good reader does, and about what a racing reader does.
solution: Rereading and following punctuation both help. Racing and skipping hard words make reading choppy or wrong.

@practice self
prompt: Read this passage out loud to your grown-up three times. "Mia saw a red kite. It went up, up, up! Did it fly over the roof? Yes, it did." Each time, try to read it more smoothly.
solution: A good reading pauses a little at each comma and period, uses a surprised voice for the exclamation mark, and lets the voice go up at the question mark. The third reading sounds smoother than the first.
rubric: You read every word correctly, or you fixed a word you missed.
rubric: Your voice went up at the question mark.
rubric: You used surprise at the exclamation mark.
rubric: The third reading was smoother than the first, and your grown-up agrees.
hint: Mark the end of each sentence first. Notice the exclamation mark after "up" and the question mark after "roof".
