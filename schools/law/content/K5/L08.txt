@article
Sometimes you find a word you do not know. You do not always need a dictionary. The words around it, called the **context**, can tell you what it means. A **context clue** is a hint in the text.

### Where to look

- **Definition clue:** the author tells you the meaning. "A **burrow** is a hole an animal digs to live in."
- **Synonym clue:** another word with the same meaning. "The cave was **enormous**, very big."
- **Example clue:** examples show the meaning. "Fruits like apples, pears and plums are **nutritious** foods."
- **Opposite clue:** words like "but" show the opposite. "The road was **narrow**, but the highway was wide."

### Steps to follow

1. Read the sentence with the hard word.
2. Look at the sentence before it and the sentence after it.
3. Look for a clue.
4. Guess the meaning and put it in place of the hard word. Does it make sense?

### Features help too

A heading, a caption or a picture can also be a clue. If a heading says "How Bees Make Honey" and a word "hive" appears beneath it, the picture of a bee house is a clue.

Keep a word list! When you learn a new word, write it and its meaning in a notebook.

@example
title: Using a clue
problem: Read: "The kitten was timid. It hid under the bed when the doorbell rang." What does timid mean?
step: Find the hard word. It is timid.
step: Look at the next sentence. The kitten hid when the doorbell rang. That is a clue.
step: Someone who hides when they are scared is shy and easily scared. Timid means shy or scared.

@mistakes
- Picking a meaning that does not make sense when you put it back in the sentence.
- Looking only at the hard word and not the sentences around it.
- Ignoring words like "but" that point to an opposite.
