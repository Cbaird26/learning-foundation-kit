@article
Grammar is the system that lets readers follow your meaning; style is the choice among correct options.

### Clauses and sentences

A **clause** has a subject and a verb. An **independent clause** can stand alone ("The bell rang."). A **dependent clause** cannot ("Because the bell rang"). Sentence types: **simple** (one independent clause), **compound** (two joined by a coordinating conjunction or semicolon), **complex** (one independent plus a dependent), **compound-complex**.

Three common errors:

- **Fragment**: a piece that lacks a complete thought ("Although we waited."). Attach it: "Although we waited, the bus never came."
- **Run-on (fused sentence)**: two independent clauses with no punctuation ("The bus was late we walked").
- **Comma splice**: two independent clauses joined with only a comma ("The bus was late, we walked"). Fix with a period, a semicolon, or a comma plus a conjunction: "The bus was late, so we walked."

### Punctuation

- **Comma**: after an introductory element ("After lunch, we left"); between items in a series; before a coordinating conjunction joining independent clauses; around nonessential phrases ("My sister, who lives in Ohio, called").
- **Semicolon**: joins two closely related independent clauses ("She studied; he slept").
- **Colon**: introduces a list or explanation after a complete clause ("She brought three things: a map, a compass and water").
- **Apostrophe**: shows possession (*the dog's bone*, *the dogs' bones*) or a contraction (*it's* = *it is*). The possessive *its* has no apostrophe.

### Agreement and pronouns

A verb agrees with its subject, not the nearest noun: "The box of pencils **is** on the desk." Words such as *each*, *everyone* and *neither* take singular verbs in formal writing. A pronoun must have a clear **antecedent**: in "When Ana met Beth, she smiled," who smiled? Rewrite to remove ambiguity.

### Style

- **Parallel structure**: keep matching forms: "She likes hiking, swimming and reading," not "hiking, to swim and reading."
- **Active voice** usually is clearer: "The committee chose a design," not "A design was chosen by the committee." Passive voice is useful when the actor is unknown or unimportant.
- **Concision**: delete filler ("due to the fact that" becomes "because").
- **Variety**: mix short and long sentences, vary openings, and put important ideas at the end of a sentence for emphasis.

@example
title: Fixing a comma splice and a fragment
problem: Revise: "The experiment failed, we repeated it. Because the sample was contaminated."
step: Identify the errors: the first part is a comma splice (two independent clauses with only a comma); the second is a fragment (a dependent clause).
step: Fix the splice with a conjunction: "The experiment failed, so we repeated it."
step: Attach the fragment to a complete clause: "The experiment failed because the sample was contaminated, so we repeated it."
step: Check that every clause is complete and punctuated.

@mistakes
- Joining independent clauses with only a comma.
- Writing "it's" for the possessive "its".
- Starting a sentence with a pronoun that could refer to two nouns.
- Mixing forms in a list, which breaks parallel structure.

prompt: Which sentence is a comma splice?
choice: The rain stopped, so we left.
choice*: The rain stopped, we left.
choice: When the rain stopped, we left.
choice: The rain stopped; we left.
hint: Look for two complete sentences joined only by a comma.
solution: "The rain stopped, we left" joins two independent clauses with just a comma. The others are correctly joined.

choice*: The dog wagged its tail.
choice: The dog wagged its' tail.
choice: The dog wagged tails.
hint: The possessive form of "it" does not use an apostrophe.
solution: "Its" is the possessive; "it's" means "it is."

solution: Because costs had risen, the board increased the fees.
rubric: Uses active voice with the board as subject.
rubric: Replaces "due to the fact that" with a short word such as "because."
rubric: Preserves the original meaning with fewer words.

@quiz mc
prompt: Which revision correctly fixes "I wanted to call, however, I lost my phone."?
choice: I wanted to call however, I lost my phone.
choice*: I wanted to call; however, I lost my phone.
choice: I wanted to call, however I lost my phone.
choice: I wanted to call however I, lost my phone.
hint: "However" is a conjunctive adverb, not a coordinating conjunction.
solution: A semicolon before "however" and a comma after it correctly join the two independent clauses.
