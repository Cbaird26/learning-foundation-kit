@article
**Evolution** is a change in the inherited traits of a population over many generations. Individuals do not evolve during their lifetimes. Populations do.

### The idea of natural selection

**Natural selection** is the process by which individuals with traits that help them survive and reproduce leave more offspring, so those traits become more common. It works when four things are true:

1. **Variation**: individuals in a population differ in their traits.
2. **Inheritance**: some of those differences are passed to offspring through genes.
3. **Overproduction**: more offspring are born than can survive, so there is competition for food, space and mates.
4. **Differential survival and reproduction**: individuals with helpful traits are more likely to live and reproduce.

A trait that increases the chance of survival and reproduction in a particular environment is an **adaptation**. Over many generations, the proportion of individuals with adaptive traits goes up.

### An example

Imagine a population of beetles with green and brown individuals living on brown soil. Birds find green beetles more easily and eat more of them. More brown beetles survive and reproduce. If color is inherited, the next generation has a higher fraction of brown beetles. Nothing made the beetles "decide" to change. The change happened because of who survived.

### Where do new variations come from?

Variation arises from **mutations**, which are random changes in DNA, and from the mixing of genes in sexual reproduction. Most mutations are neutral or harmful, but a few help in a certain environment.

### Evidence for evolution

- **Fossils** show that past organisms differed from today's and show gradual changes, such as in whale ancestors.
- **Comparative anatomy**: the arm of a human, the wing of a bat and the flipper of a whale have similar bones, which suggests a common ancestor.
- **DNA**: closely related species have more similar DNA.
- **Observed change**: bacteria become resistant to antibiotics when a few resistant cells survive treatment and multiply.

### Common misunderstandings

Evolution does not mean organisms try to improve or that evolution has a goal. Environments change, so what counts as helpful changes too.

@example
title: Reading survival data
problem: A population has 50 green and 50 brown beetles. Birds eat 30 of the green beetles and 10 of the brown beetles. What percent of the surviving beetles are brown?
step: Survivors of each color: green $= 50 - 30 = 20$, brown $= 50 - 10 = 40$.
step: Total survivors are $20 + 40 = 60$.
step: Fraction brown is $\frac{40}{60} = \frac{2}{3}$, which is about 66.7%.
step: Brown beetles went from 50% of the population to about 66.7%, so natural selection is favoring brown, if color is inherited.

@mistakes
- Saying individuals change their traits because they need to; selection acts on variation that already exists.
- Saying an organism "evolved" during its own life; only populations change over generations.
- Thinking "survival of the fittest" means strongest; fitness means leaving more offspring that survive.

@practice mc
prompt: Which statement correctly describes natural selection?
choice: Organisms develop new traits because they need them.
choice*: Individuals with helpful inherited traits tend to leave more offspring, so those traits become more common.
choice: Individual organisms slowly change during their lives and pass the changes on.
choice: The strongest individual always survives.
hint: Selection acts on traits already present in a population.
solution: Selection favors inherited traits that help survival and reproduction, so they spread in the population. Needing a trait does not create it.

@practice num
prompt: A population has 50 dark moths and 50 light moths on a tree with dark bark. Predators leave 45 dark moths and 15 light moths alive. What percent of the surviving moths are dark?
answer: 75
check: 45 / (45 + 15) * 100
unit: %
tol: 1%
hint: Add the survivors to get the total.
solution: There are $45 + 15 = 60$ survivors, and $45 \div 60 = 0.75$, so 75%.

@practice num
prompt: A culture has 100 bacteria that are resistant to an antibiotic and no others after treatment. Each resistant bacterium divides once per hour. If none die, how many resistant bacteria are there after 5 hours?
answer: 3200
check: 100 * 2 ** 5
hint: The number doubles each hour.
solution: After 5 doublings, the number is $100 \times 2^5 = 100 \times 32 = 3200$.

@practice self

solution: In a population of grass-colored and tan grasshoppers living in a field that turns brown in a drought, the grasshoppers vary in color. Color is controlled by genes, so it is inherited. Birds see green grasshoppers more easily, so fewer green ones survive and reproduce. The tan grasshoppers leave more offspring, which inherit tan color. After many generations, most grasshoppers in the population are tan. The population evolved because the tan variation helped survival in that environment.

@quiz num
prompt: A drought leaves 200 finches, of which 120 have large beaks and 80 have small beaks. After the drought, 90% of the large-beaked finches and 30% of the small-beaked finches survive. What percent of the survivors have large beaks, to the nearest tenth of a percent?
answer: 81.8
check: (120 * 0.9) / (120 * 0.9 + 80 * 0.3) * 100
unit: %
tol: 0.2
hint: Find the number of survivors with each beak size.
hint: Large: $108$ survive. Small: $24$ survive.
solution: Large-beaked survivors $= 120 \times 0.9 = 108$ and small-beaked survivors $= 80 \times 0.3 = 24$. Total $= 132$ and $108 \div 132 \approx 0.818$, so 81.8%.
