@article
Your cells need food for energy and materials, and oxygen to release that energy. Three systems team up to supply both.

### Digestion

**Digestion** breaks food into molecules small enough to enter the blood. It has a mechanical part (chewing, churning) and a chemical part (**enzymes** that split molecules).

The path is: mouth, **esophagus**, **stomach**, **small intestine**, **large intestine**. In the mouth, saliva begins to digest starch. The stomach churns food with acid and enzymes that start digesting protein. In the small intestine, bile from the **liver** (stored in the gallbladder) helps break up fat, and enzymes from the **pancreas** finish digesting carbohydrates, proteins and fats. The inner wall has tiny folds called **villi** that greatly increase surface area, so most nutrients are **absorbed** into the blood there. The large intestine mainly absorbs water and forms solid waste.

### Breathing

Air enters through the nose or mouth, then passes down the **trachea**, into two **bronchi**, then into smaller tubes ending in tiny air sacs called **alveoli**. The **diaphragm**, a sheet of muscle below the lungs, contracts and moves down so the lungs expand and air flows in. When it relaxes, air flows out.

Each alveolus is wrapped in tiny blood vessels called capillaries. Oxygen **diffuses** from the air into the blood, and carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood into the air. Diffusion is movement from where there is more of a substance to where there is less.

Breathing volume per minute is

$$\text{minute ventilation} = \text{breathing rate} \times \text{volume per breath}.$$

### Circulation

The **heart** is a muscular pump with four chambers: two atria on top that receive blood and two ventricles below that pump it out. The right side sends blood to the lungs to pick up oxygen. The left side sends oxygen-rich blood to the body.

- **Arteries** carry blood away from the heart; their walls are thick and strong.
- **Veins** carry blood back to the heart; they have valves that prevent backflow.
- **Capillaries** are microscopic vessels with walls one cell thick, where materials pass between blood and cells.

Blood contains red blood cells (carry oxygen using **hemoglobin**), white blood cells (fight germs), platelets (help clotting) and liquid plasma.

Blood pumped each minute is

$$\text{cardiac output} = \text{stroke volume} \times \text{heart rate},$$

where stroke volume is the amount pumped per beat.

@example
title: Minute ventilation
problem: A student at rest breathes 14 times per minute and moves 500 mL of air with each breath. How many liters of air is that per minute?
step: Multiply the rate by the volume per breath: $14 \times 500\ \text{mL} = 7000\ \text{mL}$.
step: Convert milliliters to liters by dividing by $1000$.
step: $7000 \div 1000 = 7\ \text{L per minute}$.

@mistakes
- Saying most digestion and absorption happen in the stomach; they happen mostly in the small intestine.
- Saying all arteries carry oxygen-rich blood; the pulmonary arteries carry oxygen-poor blood to the lungs.
- Thinking food passes through the liver and pancreas; those organs add chemicals but food does not travel through them.

@practice self
prompt: Trace what happens to a bite of bread, from your mouth until its sugar is used by a muscle cell. Write 5 or more steps and name the organs and the system involved at each stage.
solution: 1. In the mouth, chewing and saliva begin breaking down the starch. 2. The esophagus moves the food to the stomach, which churns it. 3. In the small intestine, enzymes from the pancreas finish breaking starch into sugar (glucose), and the sugar is absorbed through the villi into the blood. 4. The circulatory system carries the glucose in the blood through the heart and arteries. 5. In capillaries in a muscle, the glucose passes into the muscle cell. 6. Mitochondria in the cell use the glucose and oxygen to release energy.
rubric: Follows the correct order mouth, esophagus, stomach, small intestine.
rubric: States that absorption into the blood happens in the small intestine.
rubric: Includes the circulatory system carrying sugar to the muscle.
rubric: Ends with cells using the sugar, with oxygen, to release energy.
hint: Follow the food in order, then follow the sugar once it enters the blood.
