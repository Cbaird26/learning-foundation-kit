You will then write a client memo comparing two planning options. Every dollar amount, rate and limit in the materials is an assumption for this exercise.
goal: Compute gross income, AGI, taxable income and tax liability from the facts, with a Code citation for each line.
goal: Apply the course rules on exclusions, deductions, depreciation, basis, capital gains, § 121 and timing in one integrated return.
goal: Write a clear client memo that recommends between two planning options and explains the tax effect of each.
step: Read the taxpayer facts and the assumptions in the material below. List every item and classify it as included, excluded, deductible, capitalized or personal, with the Code section.
step: Compute the net business income from the consulting business (§ 162, § 274(n), § 168). Show the depreciation and the equipment's adjusted basis at year end (§ 1016).
step: Compute the gain on the home sale (§ 1001, § 1012, § 1016) and the portion excluded under § 121.
step: Net the capital gains and losses from the home sale and the stock sales (§§ 1221, 1222) and compute AGI and taxable income (§§ 62, 63).
step: Apply the assumed rate schedules to compute the tax on ordinary income and on the net capital gain, then total the liability.
step: Evaluate two planning options: (A) electing § 179 expensing for the equipment, instead of regular MACRS, and (B) collecting the December invoice in December instead of January (a cash-method taxpayer includes it when received). Recompute the tax effect of each for this year, and note the effect on later years.
step: Write the memo to the client (about two pages): the question, a short answer, the computation as a table with citations, the two options, a recommendation, and the facts and assumptions you relied on.

Ignore self-employment tax except for an assumed above-the-line deduction of \$3,300 for half of it, and ignore § 199A.
material: **Home sale.** Maya bought her home five years ago for \$250,000, has used it as her principal residence for the entire time, and spent \$30,000 on a permanent kitchen remodel. She sold it this year for \$640,000 and paid \$30,000 in selling costs.

material: **Assumptions.** Standard deduction \$15,000. Her itemized deductions would total \$14,000. Single rate schedule for ordinary income: 10% up to \$11,000; 12% from \$11,000 to \$45,000; 22% from \$45,000 to \$95,000; 24% above \$95,000 (up to the amount in this problem). Net capital gain is taxed at a flat 15%. Apply the deduction against ordinary income first. Ignore the net investment income tax and the § 1250 recapture rules.

deliverable: A client memo of about two pages comparing the two planning options with a recommendation.

### Week by week

1. **Structure.** Gross income, minus § 62 deductions = AGI; minus standard or itemized deductions = taxable income; apply § 1 rates; subtract credits. Marginal versus average rates; deduction versus credit; sources of authority; Loper Bright and Skidmore weight.
2. **Gross income.** § 61 and Glenshaw Glass; loans, imputed income and unrealized gain are not income; barter at fair market value; § 61(a)(11) and § 108 (insolvency, bankruptcy).
3. **Exclusions.** § 102 and Duberstein, § 102(c); § 101, § 103, § 104(a)(2) (physical injury; punitive damages and interest taxable); § 119; § 132; carryover and stepped-up basis.
4. **Business deductions.** § 162 elements, § 263 capitalization, § 262 personal, § 274 limits, § 183, § 280A, Gilmore.
5. **Depreciation.** MACRS tables, § 179 first, bonus depreciation, land not depreciable, § 1016 basis reduction, § 1245 recapture.
6. **Personal deductions.** Standard versus itemized; medical floor, SALT cap, mortgage interest, charity; miscellaneous itemized deductions suspended; the 2025 reconciliation law's changes.
7. **Property transactions.** § 1001, amount realized, Crane and Tufts, recourse versus nonrecourse debt.
8. **Capital gains.** § 1221, holding period (more than one year), netting, § 1211(b) limit of \$3,000, carryover, § 1231, wash sales.
9. **Nonrecognition.** § 1031 (real property only), boot, 45 and 180 days, substituted basis; § 121 (\$250,000 / \$500,000, two of five years).
10. **Timing.** Cash, accrual, constructive receipt, economic performance, installment method, claim of right and § 1341.
11. **Assignment of income and family.** Earl, Horst, Blair; kiddie tax; filing status; § 1041; alimony after 2018.
12. **Procedure and ethics.** Limitations periods, notice of deficiency and the 90-day petition, Flora, penalties, Cheek, Circular 230, § 6694, Model Rule 1.2(d).

### Common errors

- Applying one bracket's rate to all income.
- Subtracting credits from income.
- Forgetting § 102(c), the SALT cap, the medical floor, or selling costs.
- Giving a donee a stepped-up basis, or an heir a carryover basis.
- Treating a sale on the anniversary date as long-term.
- Applying § 1031 to equipment.
- Using the stale rule of deference after Loper Bright.
- Stating current-year amounts from memory; use the numbers given.

@exam num
prompt: Rita has wages of \$40,000. She is also a sole proprietor with receipts of \$90,000 and these expenses: supplies \$7,000, substantiated client meals \$6,000 (50% deductible), client entertainment \$2,000, and a machine (5-year property) bought for \$30,000 for which she elects § 179 expensing of \$10,000 and uses a Year 1 rate of 20% on the remaining basis, with no bonus depreciation. She also has an above-the-line deduction of \$4,000 and takes an assumed standard deduction of \$15,000. What is her taxable income?
answer: 87000
check: 40000 + (90000 - 7000 - 0.5*6000 - (10000 + 0.20*(30000 - 10000)) ) - 4000 - 15000
unit: dollars
hint: Find the net business income first: deductible expenses include half of the meals and the § 179 plus MACRS depreciation, but not the entertainment.
hint: Net business income is \$66,000. Add the wages, subtract the above-the-line deduction to get AGI, then subtract the standard deduction.
solution: Meals deduction \$3,000. Depreciation: \$10,000 + 20% x \$20,000 = \$14,000. Deductions: \$7,000 + \$3,000 + \$14,000 = \$24,000, so net business income is \$66,000. AGI = \$40,000 + \$66,000 - \$4,000 = \$102,000. Taxable income = \$102,000 - \$15,000 = \$87,000.

prompt: Lee, who is single, sells his principal residence (owned and used for ten years) for \$700,000 and pays \$40,000 in selling costs. His adjusted basis, including a \$50,000 permanent improvement, is \$350,000. For the same year he has a short-term capital loss of \$10,000 and a long-term capital gain of \$4,000 from stock. Assume net capital gain is taxed at 15%. What is the tax on his net capital gain?
answer: 8100
check: 0.15*(max(0, (700000 - 40000) - 350000 - 250000) + 4000 - 10000)
unit: dollars
hint: Compute the gain on the home and subtract the § 121 exclusion before netting.
hint: Recognized home gain is \$60,000. Combine it with the \$4,000 long-term gain and the \$10,000 short-term loss.
solution: Amount realized is \$660,000; gain is \$310,000; after the \$250,000 exclusion \$60,000 is recognized as long-term capital gain. Net long-term gain is \$64,000, less the \$10,000 net short-term loss, gives a net capital gain of \$54,000. At 15% the tax is \$8,100.

@exam self
prompt: Nina was injured in an accident and settled her claim for \$100,000, allocated as \$65,000 for medical bills and pain from the physical injury, \$30,000 for punitive damages and \$5,000 for interest. She also inherited stock from her aunt. The aunt's basis was \$20,000, its fair market value at death was \$50,000, and Nina sold it for \$55,000. In a short memo, state how much of the settlement is included in her gross income, the amount and character of her gain on the stock, and the authorities for each conclusion.
hint: Apply § 104(a)(2) component by component.
hint: Use § 1014 for the basis of the inherited stock.
solution: Settlement: the \$65,000 compensatory damages for physical injury are excluded under § 104(a)(2). Punitive damages (\$30,000) are included (O'Gilvie) and interest (\$5,000) is included under § 61(a)(4), so \$35,000 is included. Stock: under § 1014 Nina's basis is the date-of-death value of \$50,000, so her realized and recognized gain under § 1001 is \$55,000 - \$50,000 = \$5,000. Under § 1223(9) it is long-term capital gain (§ 1222), taxed at the preferential rate. The aunt's \$20,000 basis is irrelevant.
rubric: Excludes the compensatory physical injury damages and includes punitive damages and interest, citing § 104(a)(2).
rubric: Computes the \$35,000 inclusion and the \$5,000 gain.
rubric: Uses the stepped-up basis under § 1014 and identifies the gain as long-term capital gain.
