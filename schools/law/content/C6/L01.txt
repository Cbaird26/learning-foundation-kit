@article
Lawyers meet numbers in settlement demands, sentencing data, hiring records and expert reports. Before you can test a claim about data, you must be able to **describe** it honestly. A good description answers two questions: where is the data centered, and how spread out is it?

### Measures of center

The **mean** is the sum of the values divided by how many there are: $\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum x_i$. The **median** is the middle value after sorting (the average of the two middle values when $n$ is even). The **mode** is the most frequent value.

The mean uses every value, so one extreme value moves it. The median ignores how far out the extremes lie. In a **right-skewed** data set (a long tail of large values, such as jury awards or salaries) the mean is pulled above the median. That is why reports about incomes and awards usually quote the median, and why a lawyer reading "the average award was \$52,500" should ask whether that is a mean, and what the median is.

### Measures of spread

The **range** is the maximum minus the minimum; it depends on only two values. The **interquartile range** (IQR) is the third quartile minus the first and describes the middle half of the data. The **sample standard deviation** measures typical distance from the mean:

$$s = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n-1}}.$$

We divide by $n-1$ rather than $n$ when the data are a sample used to learn about a larger population; the square of $s$ is the **sample variance**. Standard deviation has the same units as the data.

### Standardizing: the z-score

A **z-score** says how many standard deviations a value lies from the mean:

$$z = \frac{x - \bar{x}}{s}.$$

A z-score of $+2$ means two standard deviations above average. For roughly bell-shaped data about 95% of values fall within $|z| \le 2$, so $|z| > 2$ is unusual. Courts reviewing statistical disparities later in this course use exactly this idea, counting how many standard deviations separate what was observed from what was expected.

### Pictures and shape

A histogram or box plot shows shape: symmetric, skewed or having outliers. Always look before you summarize. Two data sets can share a mean and differ wildly in spread, and a single number can hide a bimodal pattern (two clusters).

@example
title: Jury awards with an extreme verdict
problem: Eight jury awards (in thousands of dollars) were $20, 25, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 200$. Find the mean, median, mode and range, and say which center describes a "typical" award.
step: Mean: the sum is $20+25+25+30+35+40+45+200 = 420$, so $\bar{x} = 420/8 = 52.5$ thousand dollars.
step: Median: the data are already sorted. With $n=8$ the middle values are the 4th and 5th, $30$ and $35$, so the median is $32.5$ thousand dollars.
step: Mode: $25$ occurs twice and no other value repeats, so the mode is $25$. Range: $200 - 20 = 180$.
step: The mean ($52.5$) exceeds seven of the eight awards because the single $200$ verdict pulls it up. The median ($32.5$) better describes a typical award; a fair report would give both and mention the outlier.

@mistakes
- Using $n$ instead of $n-1$ when computing the standard deviation of a sample.
- Forgetting to sort before finding the median, or taking the middle of an even-sized list as one value instead of averaging two.
- Reporting only the mean for skewed data such as awards or salaries, which hides the outlier's effect.
- Reading a z-score as a percentage of the data rather than a count of standard deviations.

tol: 0.005

solution: $\sum (x_i - 5)^2 = 9+1+1+1+0+0+4+16 = 32$. Then $s = \sqrt{32/7} \approx 2.14$.

@practice num
prompt: Ten claim files have a mean processing time of $50$ days. One file that took $80$ days is found to be a data-entry error and is removed. Give the mean of the remaining nine files as an exact fraction.
answer: 140/3
check: (10 * 50 - 80) / 9
hint: Recover the original total from the mean: total $= n \times$ mean.
hint: The original total is $500$ days. Subtract $80$ and divide by $9$.
solution: Original total $= 10(50) = 500$. New total $= 420$, so the new mean is $420/9 = 140/3 \approx 46.7$ days.

@practice mc
prompt: A report on salaries at a firm, which has a few very highly paid partners and many modestly paid staff, wants a single number describing the "typical" salary. Which choice is best?
choice*: The median, because it is not pulled upward by the few very large salaries.
choice: The mean, because it uses every salary and so is always the most representative.
choice: The mode, because it is the only measure that is never affected by skew.
choice: The range, because it shows how far apart the highest and lowest salaries are.
hint: Which measure changes least when a few extreme values are added?
solution: In right-skewed data the mean is dragged toward the long tail. The median reports the middle salary and resists outliers. The range measures spread, not center.

@quiz num
prompt: A bar-exam preparation test has mean score $62$ and sample standard deviation $8$. What is the z-score of a student who scored $74$?
answer: 3/2
check: (74 - 62) / 8
hint: Subtract the mean, then divide by the standard deviation.
hint: $74 - 62 = 12$.
solution: $z = (74-62)/8 = 1.5$, so the score is one and a half standard deviations above the mean.

@quiz ms
prompt: A histogram of billable hours per case is strongly right-skewed. Select all statements that must be true.
choice*: The mean is greater than the median.
choice*: A few cases have much larger hours than most.
choice: The median is greater than the mean.
choice: Most values lie above the mean.
choice: The standard deviation is zero.
hint: Picture a long tail of large values and where it drags the mean.
solution: A long right tail pulls the mean above the median, and the tail means a few cases with very large hours. In right-skewed data most values lie below the mean, and a standard deviation of zero would mean all values are identical.
