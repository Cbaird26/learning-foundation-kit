@article
A survey of $400$ consumers finds that $30\%$ confuse two brand names. The true percentage among all consumers is unknown. A **confidence interval** gives a range of plausible values for it, built from the sample so that the method captures the truth a stated fraction of the time.

### The interval for a proportion

Let $\hat{p} = x/n$ be the sample proportion. Its **standard error** estimates how much $\hat{p}$ varies from sample to sample:

$$SE = \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}.$$

The **margin of error** is $ME = z^* \cdot SE$ and the interval is

$$\hat{p} \pm z^* \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}.$$

For $95\%$ confidence $z^* = 1.96$; for $90\%$, $1.645$; for $99\%$, $2.576$. The method assumes a random sample and a sample large enough for the normal approximation (a common rule: at least $10$ expected successes and $10$ failures).

### What "95% confident" means

The confidence level describes the **procedure**: if we repeated the sampling many times and built an interval each time, about $95\%$ of those intervals would contain the true proportion. It does **not** say there is a $95\%$ probability that the true value lies in this particular interval (the true value is fixed; once the interval is computed it either contains it or does not), and it does **not** say that $95\%$ of individuals fall in the interval.

### What affects the width

- Larger $n$ shrinks $SE$ in proportion to $1/\sqrt{n}$: to halve the margin of error you need four times the sample.
- Higher confidence needs a larger $z^*$ and so a wider interval.
- $\hat{p}$ near $0.5$ gives the widest interval.

### Choosing a sample size

If you want margin of error at most $E$ and have no prior estimate, use the worst case $\hat{p} = 0.5$:

$$n \ge \frac{z^{*2}\,(0.25)}{E^2},$$

rounded **up** to the next whole number.

### Limits in litigation

A confidence interval measures only sampling error. It says nothing about bias from bad questions, nonresponse or an ill-defined population (Week 5). An interval from a flawed design is a precise statement about the wrong thing. Intervals for means follow the same logic with a $t$ critical value and $s/\sqrt{n}$.

@example
title: Confusion rate in a brand survey
problem: In a random survey of $400$ consumers, $120$ confused the two brands. Find the $95\%$ confidence interval for the proportion of all consumers who would be confused.
step: The sample proportion is $\hat{p} = 120/400 = 0.30$. Conditions: $120$ successes and $280$ failures are both at least $10$.
step: The standard error is $\sqrt{0.30 \times 0.70/400} = \sqrt{0.21/400} \approx 0.0229$.
step: The margin of error is $1.96 \times 0.0229 \approx 0.0449$.
step: The interval is $0.30 \pm 0.045$, or about $(0.255,\, 0.345)$.
step: Interpretation: we are $95\%$ confident that between about $25.5\%$ and $34.5\%$ of consumers would be confused, in the sense that the method captures the true value in about 95% of repeated surveys. It says nothing about whether the survey's questions were fair.

@mistakes
- Saying there is a 95% probability that the true proportion lies in the computed interval.
- Using $\hat{p}=0.5$ when computing the interval from data, rather than the observed $\hat{p}$.
- Rounding a required sample size down instead of up.
- Treating a narrow interval as proof the survey is reliable, when bias is the larger risk.

@practice num
prompt: A sample of $100$ items has $\hat{p} = 0.5$. Find the standard error of $\hat{p}$ as an exact fraction.
answer: 1/20
check: math.sqrt(0.5 * 0.5 / 100)
hint: $SE = \sqrt{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})/n}$.
hint: $\sqrt{0.25/100} = \sqrt{0.0025}$.
solution: $SE = \sqrt{0.25/100} = 0.05 = 1/20$.

@practice num
prompt: A random sample of $600$ jurors finds $\hat{p} = 0.4$ with some trait. Use $z^* = 1.96$ and find the margin of error of the $95\%$ interval.
answer: 0.0392
tol: 0.0005
check: 1.96 * math.sqrt(0.4 * 0.6 / 600)
hint: First find the standard error.
hint: $SE = \sqrt{0.24/600} = 0.02$.
solution: $SE = \sqrt{0.24/600} = \sqrt{0.0004} = 0.02$, so $ME = 1.96 \times 0.02 = 0.0392$.

@practice num
prompt: A pollster wants a $95\%$ margin of error of at most $0.03$ and has no prior estimate of the proportion. Using $z^* = 1.96$ and the worst case $\hat{p} = 0.5$, what is the smallest sample size that works?
answer: 1068
check: math.ceil(1.96 ** 2 * 0.25 / 0.03 ** 2)
hint: Use $n \ge z^{*2}(0.25)/E^2$.
hint: The bound is about $1067.1$; the sample size must be a whole number that meets the requirement.
solution: $n \ge 3.8416(0.25)/0.0009 = 1067.1$. Round up to $1068$.

@practice mc
prompt: A $95\%$ confidence interval for the proportion of customers who prefer a product is $(0.42, 0.50)$. Which interpretation is correct?
choice*: If the sampling procedure were repeated many times, about 95% of the intervals built this way would contain the true proportion.
choice: There is a 95% probability that this particular interval contains the true proportion, and the true proportion varies between 0.42 and 0.50 from customer to customer.
choice: Ninety-five percent of customers have a preference between 0.42 and 0.50.
choice: The sample proportion has a 95% chance of being exactly 0.46.
hint: The confidence level describes the method, not one interval or the individuals.
solution: The 95% refers to the long-run capture rate of the procedure. The other statements treat the interval as describing individuals or give a probability to a fixed unknown.

@practice num
prompt: In a random sample of $200$ residents, $56$ report a problem. Use $z^* = 1.96$ and give the upper limit of the $95\%$ confidence interval for the population proportion, rounded to three decimal places.
answer: 0.342
tol: 0.001
check: 56 / 200 + 1.96 * math.sqrt((56 / 200) * (1 - 56 / 200) / 200)
hint: Find $\hat{p} = 56/200$, then $SE$, then $ME$.
hint: $\hat{p} = 0.28$, $SE \approx 0.0317$, $ME \approx 0.0622$.
solution: $\hat{p} = 0.28$; $SE = \sqrt{0.28(0.72)/200} \approx 0.0317$; $ME \approx 0.0622$. Upper limit $= 0.28 + 0.0622 \approx 0.342$.

@quiz num
prompt: In a random sample of $150$ employees, $45$ say they were never trained. Use $z^* = 1.96$ and give the lower limit of the $95\%$ confidence interval for the population proportion, rounded to three decimal places.
answer: 0.227
tol: 0.001
check: 45 / 150 - 1.96 * math.sqrt((45 / 150) * (1 - 45 / 150) / 150)
hint: Compute $\hat{p}$ and $SE = \sqrt{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})/n}$.
hint: $\hat{p} = 0.30$ and $SE \approx 0.0374$. Subtract the margin of error from $\hat{p}$.
solution: $\hat{p} = 0.3$, $SE \approx 0.03742$, $ME \approx 0.0733$. The lower limit is $0.3 - 0.0733 \approx 0.227$.

@quiz mc

choice*: Multiply it by about four.
choice: Double it.
choice: Multiply it by about eight.
choice: Leave it unchanged but raise the confidence level.
hint: Margin of error is proportional to $1/\sqrt{n}$.
solution: Because $ME \propto 1/\sqrt{n}$, halving the margin needs $\sqrt{n}$ doubled, so $n$ must be four times as large.
