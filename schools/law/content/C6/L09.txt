@article
Many statistical reports in litigation (pay equity, sentencing, damages) are regressions. A regression describes how an outcome $y$ changes on average with a predictor $x$.

### Correlation

The **correlation coefficient** $r$ measures the strength and direction of a **linear** relationship, with $-1 \le r \le 1$:

$$r = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sqrt{\sum (x_i-\bar{x})^2 \sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2}}.$$

Values near $\pm 1$ mean points cluster near a line; near $0$ means no linear pattern (a strong curved pattern can still have $r \approx 0$). $r$ has no units and does not change if you rescale $x$ or $y$ by positive factors (a negative factor flips its sign). A single outlier can change it a lot, so always plot the data.

### The least-squares line

The **least-squares regression line** $\hat{y} = b_0 + b_1 x$ minimizes the sum of squared vertical errors (residuals). Its slope and intercept are

$$b_1 = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2} = r\,\frac{s_y}{s_x}, \qquad b_0 = \bar{y} - b_1 \bar{x}.$$

The slope is the average change in $y$ for a one-unit increase in $x$; the intercept is the predicted $y$ at $x=0$, which may be meaningless. Predicting far outside the range of the data (**extrapolation**) is risky.

### R squared

$R^2$ is the fraction of the variation in $y$ explained by the model. For one predictor it equals $r^2$. An $R^2$ of $0.64$ means the line accounts for $64\%$ of the variation; the other $36\%$ is unexplained.

### Reading a regression table

A report may present salary regressed on several variables:

| Variable | Estimate | Std. error | t | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intercept | 30.0 | 2.0 | 15.0 | < 0.001 |
| Years of experience | 2.5 | 0.4 | 6.25 | < 0.001 |
| Female (1 = yes) | -4.0 | 1.6 | -2.5 | 0.013 |

Each row's $t = \text{estimate}/\text{standard error}$. A coefficient is interpreted **holding the other variables fixed**: in this table, women with the same years of experience are paid on average $4.0$ (thousand dollars) less than men. An approximate 95% interval for a coefficient is estimate $\pm 1.96$ standard errors for large samples. The table says nothing about *why* the gap exists, and omitted variables (job level, hours, performance) may change the estimate. Week 10 takes up that problem.

### Warning signs

Check for outliers, for curvature, for **heteroskedasticity** (spread that changes with $x$), and for variables that are included although they are themselves affected by the alleged discrimination (a "tainted variable" such as job title).

@example
title: Fitting a line to experience and salary
problem: Five employees have experience $x = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ years and salary $y = 40, 45, 43, 52, 55$ (thousands of dollars). Find the least-squares line and predict the salary at $x = 4.5$.
step: Means: $\bar{x} = 3$ and $\bar{y} = 235/5 = 47$.
step: Deviation products: $\sum (x-\bar{x})(y-\bar{y}) = (-2)(-7) + (-1)(-2) + 0(-4) + (1)(5) + (2)(8) = 14+2+0+5+16 = 37$. Also $\sum (x-\bar{x})^2 = 4+1+0+1+4 = 10$.
step: Slope: $b_1 = 37/10 = 3.7$ thousand dollars per year. Intercept: $b_0 = 47 - 3.7(3) = 35.9$.
step: The line is $\hat{y} = 35.9 + 3.7x$. At $x = 4.5$: $\hat{y} = 35.9 + 16.65 = 52.55$ thousand dollars.
step: With $\sum (y-\bar{y})^2 = 158$, $r = 37/\sqrt{10 \times 158} \approx 0.931$ and $R^2 \approx 0.87$. Five points is a tiny sample, so this is an illustration of the arithmetic, not a basis for an expert opinion.

@mistakes
- Reading $r$ or a regression coefficient as proof that $x$ causes $y$.
- Interpreting a coefficient without "holding other variables fixed", or interpreting the intercept when $x=0$ is outside the data.
- Extrapolating the line far beyond the observed range.
- Thinking $R^2$ is the probability that the model is correct or that $r$ near $0$ means no relationship at all.

prompt: Two variables have correlation $r = 0.6$, and the standard deviations are $s_x = 10$ and $s_y = 5$. Find the slope of the least-squares line as an exact fraction.
answer: 3/10
check: 0.6 * 5 / 10
hint: Use $b_1 = r\,s_y/s_x$.

solution: $b_1 = 0.6(5)/10 = 0.3$.

@practice num
prompt: A fitted line for salary (in thousands of dollars) is $\hat{y} = 20 + 2.5x$, where $x$ is years of experience. What is the predicted salary, in thousands of dollars, for $x = 8$ years (within the range of the data)?
answer: 40
check: 20 + 2.5 * 8
hint: Substitute $x = 8$ into the equation.
hint: $2.5 \times 8 = 20$.
solution: $\hat{y} = 20 + 2.5(8) = 40$ thousand dollars.

@practice num
prompt: The correlation between hours of lawyer review and the number of errors found is $r = -0.8$. Find $R^2$ as an exact fraction.
answer: 16/25
check: (-0.8) ** 2
hint: For a single predictor, $R^2 = r^2$.
hint: The sign disappears when you square.
solution: $R^2 = (-0.8)^2 = 0.64 = 16/25$, so the line explains $64\%$ of the variation.

@practice num
prompt: In a pay regression, the coefficient on an indicator for female is $-4.0$ (thousand dollars) with standard error $1.6$. Find its $t$ statistic as an exact fraction.
answer: -5/2
check: -4.0 / 1.6
hint: $t = \text{estimate}/\text{standard error}$.
hint: Divide $-4.0$ by $1.6$.
solution: $t = -4.0/1.6 = -2.5$, which is beyond the usual $|t| \approx 2$ benchmark for significance at the 5% level.

@practice mc
prompt: A regression of salary (in thousands of dollars) on years of experience and an indicator for female reports a coefficient of $-4.0$ on the indicator. What does it mean?
choice*: Women are paid on average \$4,000 less than men with the same years of experience, according to the model.
choice: Every woman is paid exactly \$4,000 less than every man.
choice: Being female causes a \$4,000 pay cut, as proven by the regression.
choice: Women have 4 fewer years of experience than men on average.
hint: Remember the phrase "holding the other variables fixed".
solution: The coefficient describes an average difference at the same experience, within the model. It is not a rule for each person and does not by itself establish cause.

@quiz num
prompt: Data: $x = 1, 2, 3, 4$ and $y = 3, 5, 4, 8$. Find the slope of the least-squares line as an exact fraction.
answer: 7/5
check: np.polyfit([1, 2, 3, 4], [3, 5, 4, 8], 1)[0]
hint: $\bar{x} = 2.5$ and $\bar{y} = 5$.
hint: $\sum (x-\bar{x})(y-\bar{y}) = 7$ and $\sum (x-\bar{x})^2 = 5$.
solution: The numerator is $(-1.5)(-2) + (-0.5)(0) + (0.5)(-1) + (1.5)(3) = 7$ and the denominator is $2.25 + 0.25 + 0.25 + 2.25 = 5$, so the slope is $7/5 = 1.4$.

@quiz ms
prompt: Select all statements about the correlation coefficient $r$ that are correct.
choice*: It measures the strength of a linear relationship and is unchanged by converting one variable from dollars to thousands of dollars.
choice*: A single outlier can change it substantially.
choice: $r = 0$ proves that the variables are unrelated in every way.
choice: $r = 0.9$ proves that $x$ causes $y$.
choice: It can take any value between $-10$ and $10$.
hint: Think about what a curved pattern or a change of units does to the formula.
solution: $r$ is unit free and sensitive to outliers. A zero correlation rules out only a linear trend, a high correlation does not establish causation, and $r$ is always between $-1$ and $1$.
