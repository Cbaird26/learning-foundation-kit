@article
Divorce ends a valid marriage. **Annulment** declares that a valid marriage never existed. **Legal separation** leaves the marriage in place while a court regulates the spouses' separate lives. Each answers a different need.

### Annulment: void and voidable marriages

Recall from Week 1 that a **void** marriage (bigamy, incest or close kinship) is invalid from the start. An annulment decree is not strictly needed, but spouses ask for one to obtain a clear record and to resolve property and support. Anyone with an interest may attack a void marriage, even after a spouse dies, and a void marriage cannot be ratified.

A **voidable** marriage is valid until a court annuls it. Commonly recognized grounds:

- **Lack of age** or parental consent: an underage spouse (or a parent) may seek annulment; cohabitation after reaching the age of consent usually ratifies the marriage.
- **Lack of mental capacity**, including intoxication, at the time of the ceremony; cohabitation after regaining capacity ratifies.
- **Duress** or coercion, such as a forced marriage; ratified by voluntary cohabitation after the duress ends.
- **Fraud** going to the **essence of the marriage**. In most states this means more than a lie about money, social position, or character. Concealment of a spouse's intent never to have children or never to consummate the marriage, hidden inability to have intercourse, or a concealed serious communicable disease may qualify; misrepresenting wealth does not. Courts differ about the exact scope.
- **Physical incapacity** to consummate the marriage (impotence) that existed at the time of marriage, was unknown to the other spouse and is incurable.
- **Sham marriage** entered only for a purpose such as immigration status, in some states.

Only a party (and in the case of an underage spouse, a parent or guardian) can seek annulment of a voidable marriage, normally during both spouses' lifetimes. A party who learns of the defect and then freely continues living as spouses **ratifies** the marriage and is barred, and statutes often impose a time limit.

### Effects of annulment

Historically the marriage was treated as if it never existed, so no alimony or property division followed and children were illegitimate. Modern law has largely removed those harsh results.

- **Children** born or conceived to the parties are **legitimate** under the Uniform Marriage and Divorce Act and under the Uniform Parentage Act, which treat all children alike regardless of the parents' marital status. The court can order custody and child support exactly as in divorce.
- **Property and support.** Most states allow the court to divide property and award maintenance after annulment as in divorce, either by statute (the Uniform Marriage and Divorce Act is the model) or through the **putative spouse** doctrine for a good-faith party.
- **Religious annulment** is a decision within a faith community and has no civil effect; only a civil decree changes civil status.

### Legal separation

A decree of legal separation (also called separate maintenance or divorce from bed and board) leaves the spouses **married**. They cannot remarry, but a court may decide property, support, custody and parenting time. Most states authorize it. A spouse may choose it for religious reasons, to keep health-insurance or other benefits that depend on marital status, or because the spouses want to try a separation first; many states let a court convert it to divorce. A statute may also use an informal separation date as the end of the marital estate or to start a no-fault separation period, so lawyers must check which date counts.

### Comparing the three

| Remedy | Marriage afterward | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Divorce | Ended; may remarry | Valid marriage that has failed |
| Annulment | Treated as never valid | Void or voidable marriage |
| Legal separation | Continues; no remarriage | Religious, insurance or trial-period reasons |

### Fault versus no-fault in practice

In a state offering both, a petitioner chooses between a fault ground and a no-fault ground. Fault grounds add costs and hostility, and the petitioner must prove them, and the respondent can contest. They may be chosen where fault affects property or alimony, or where a statutory separation period for no-fault would delay the divorce. No-fault is quicker and cheaper and is the choice in most cases.

### Applying the rules

Decide: void or voidable? If voidable, did the claimant ratify, and is the ground recognized? Then identify what follows: custody and child support for children, and property and support as the state's statute or the putative spouse doctrine allows.

@example
title: Fraud that goes to the essence
problem: Eli marries Fay after Fay tells him she is a wealthy heir. She is not, but she loves him and has no intent to deceive about anything else. Eli seeks an annulment. A second case: Gil marries Hana, who told him she wanted children, though she had firmly decided before the wedding never to have any. Gil seeks an annulment. Compare the cases.
step: Rule: most states allow annulment only for fraud that goes to the essence of the marriage, not for a misstatement about wealth or status.
step: Eli. A misrepresentation about wealth is not the kind of fraud that goes to the essence of the marital relationship, so annulment is denied; his remedy, if any, is divorce.
step: Gil. A concealed, settled intent never to have children, in the face of a representation she wanted them, is treated by many courts as fraud going to the essence, particularly if the question was important and she lied about it. Annulment may be granted unless Gil ratified by living with Hana after learning the truth.
step: Conclusion: Eli loses; Gil may win, subject to ratification and the state's version of the rule.

@mistakes
- Saying an annulment makes the children illegitimate; modern statutes treat them as legitimate and the court orders custody and child support.
- Treating any lie before the wedding as grounds for annulment; most states require fraud going to the essence of the marriage.
- Forgetting ratification: cohabiting after learning of the defect or reaching age bars annulment of a voidable marriage.
- Assuming legal separation lets the spouses remarry or ends the marriage.

@practice self
prompt: Deliverable: compare fault and no-fault grounds in a supplied case. Nora and Omar have been married nine years and have one child. They live in a state that allows divorce on the ground of irretrievable breakdown and also allows divorce on the fault grounds of adultery and cruelty. Nora wants a divorce. Omar had an affair two years ago that ended, he has not been violent, and the spouses have lived apart for four months. Nora believes Omar's affair should affect the property division. In the state, fault is not a factor in property division. Compare the fault and no-fault routes for Nora and recommend one.
solution: Fault route: Nora could plead adultery. She would need to prove it with admissible evidence, Omar could contest or raise defenses such as condonation (if Nora forgave him and resumed relations) or recrimination where those defenses survive, and the litigation would be more expensive and hostile. Because fault is not a factor in property division here, proving adultery would not give her a larger share of property, so the extra burden brings little benefit. No-fault route: Nora must show only that the marriage is irretrievably broken. Omar's consent and any fault are irrelevant, and the proceeding is faster, cheaper and less damaging for co-parenting. The four-month separation matters only if the statute requires a longer separation as a no-fault ground; if the irretrievable breakdown ground has no separation period, she can proceed now. Recommendation: file on the no-fault ground. Fault could still be raised about custody only if the conduct affects the child.
rubric: Identifies the proof burdens and possible defenses on the fault route and the minimal showing on the no-fault route.
rubric: Connects the choice to the effect of fault on property division and to cost, speed and the co-parenting relationship.
rubric: Reaches a recommendation and notes that fault evidence matters to custody only if it affects the child.
hint: Ask what Nora gains from proving fault in a state where fault does not affect property.
