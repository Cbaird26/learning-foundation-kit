## Review guide: Family Law

**Weeks 1-2: Marriage and agreements.** A valid marriage needs capacity, consent, no impediment and formalities; void marriages (bigamy, close kinship) differ from voidable ones (age, duress, fraud, incapacity). Common-law marriage needs present agreement, cohabitation and holding out. A marriage valid where celebrated is generally valid everywhere; Loving, Zablocki, Turner and Obergefell protect the right to marry, and the Respect for Marriage Act requires recognition. Cohabitants rely on Marvin-type contract and equity claims. A premarital agreement is enforceable under UPAA § 6 unless it was involuntary, or unconscionable plus no disclosure and knowledge, and it cannot affect child support.

**Weeks 3-4: Ending marriages.** No-fault grounds exist everywhere. Domicile supports a decree ending status; personal jurisdiction is needed to cut off support (Williams, Estin, Vanderbilt). Annulment applies to void or voidable marriages, with ratification as a bar, and children are legitimate. Legal separation leaves the marriage in place.

**Weeks 5-6: Property and support.** Classify, value, divide. Community property divides equally in most states; equitable distribution is fair but not necessarily equal. Time rule for pensions; degrees are generally not property; Howell on military pay. Alimony types, factors, modification, termination and the post-2018 tax treatment; Orr v. Orr.

**Weeks 7-8: Children.** Income-shares guidelines; Bradley Amendment; support cannot be waived; Turner v. Rogers. Best interests; Palmore, Troxel, Santosky.

**Weeks 9-10: Parentage and adoption.** Birth, marital presumption, VAP, adjudication and intent; donors are not parents; Pavan, Johnson v. Calvert, Baby M; biology-plus cases. Adoption consent, termination (clear and convincing), ASFA, ICWA after Brackeen.

**Weeks 11-12: Protection and jurisdiction.** Ex parte and final orders; Castle Rock; Rahimi; VAWA full faith and credit. UCCJEA priority (home state, significant connection, emergency, exclusive continuing jurisdiction); UIFSA continuing exclusive jurisdiction; Hague Convention cases.

### Common errors

- Calling every defective marriage void.
- Treating unconscionability alone as enough under the UPAA.
- Forgetting separate property when totaling each spouse's share.
- Counting the UCCJEA six months from the wrong date.
- Applying the old tax rule to alimony.
- Treating family law rules as uniform across states.

prompt: Wynn and Xavier and their four-year-old child lived together in State A from the child's birth. On March 1, Wynn moves with the child to State B. Xavier remains in State A. On May 1, Wynn files in State B for divorce and custody. State B requires six months of residency by one spouse before a divorce petition may be filed. No custody order exists anywhere. Which statement is most accurate?
choice: State B may grant the divorce and decide custody, because Wynn and the child now live there.
choice: State B may decide custody because the child is present there, but must dismiss the divorce petition.
choice: State B may grant the divorce because domicile is enough, but State A alone may decide custody.
choice*: State B must dismiss the divorce petition for failure to meet the residency requirement and may not make the initial custody determination, because State A was the home state within six months before filing and Xavier still lives there.
hint: Treat the divorce and the custody questions separately.
solution: Divorce: a valid durational residency requirement (Sosna) has not been met after two months. Custody: under the UCCJEA, State B is not the home state; State A was the home state within six months before filing and a parent continues to live there, so State A has priority.
