@article
**Legal parentage** decides who has the rights and duties of a parent: custody, support, inheritance and decision-making. It can arise by **birth**, **marriage**, **acknowledgment**, **adjudication**, **adoption** (Week 10) or, in assisted reproduction, by **intent**. Parentage statutes vary by state, and many follow the **Uniform Parentage Act** (UPA 2002 or 2017).

### Establishing parentage

- **Birth mother.** The woman who gives birth is the legal mother in nearly all cases (an exception is a valid gestational surrogacy arrangement).
- **Marital presumption.** A child born during a marriage, or within a short period after it ends, is presumed to be the child of the mother's spouse. At common law the presumption was strong; modern statutes allow rebuttal, often by genetic testing, within a time limit. In *Michael H. v. Gerald D.* (1989), a plurality of the Court upheld a California statute that conclusively presumed that a child born to a married woman living with her husband was his child, over a biological father's due process claim.
- **Voluntary acknowledgment of parentage (VAP).** Federal law requires states to offer a simple acknowledgment, often signed at the hospital. It has the force of a judgment after a **60-day rescission period**, and after that it may be challenged only for **fraud, duress or material mistake of fact**.
- **Adjudication.** A court may declare parentage, most often by **genetic testing**, which is highly accurate and presumptive above a statutory threshold.
- **Presumptions from conduct.** UPA 2017 (which is gender-neutral) presumes parentage for a person who lived with the child for a period and openly held the child out as his or her own. Some states recognize **parentage by estoppel**, protecting a child's bond with the person who acted as parent.

### Unwed fathers and the Constitution

The Court has required a biological connection **plus** a relationship (sometimes called "biology plus").

- *Stanley v. Illinois* (1972): an unwed father who had raised his children was entitled to a hearing on fitness before the state removed them.
- *Quilloin v. Walcott* (1978): a father who never sought custody or supported the child could be bypassed in a stepparent adoption on a best-interests basis.
- *Caban v. Mohammed* (1979): a gender-based rule letting an unwed mother, but not an unwed father who had a developed relationship, block an adoption was unconstitutional.
- *Lehr v. Robertson* (1983): a biological father who had never established a relationship and had not registered with the state's putative father registry had no constitutional right to notice of the adoption. The opportunity to develop a relationship is protected, but biology alone is not enough.

### Assisted reproduction

**Donor insemination.** Under the UPA, a **donor** of eggs or sperm is not a parent of a resulting child, and a married person (and under UPA 2017, any person) who **consents** to the other's assisted reproduction **with the intent to be a parent** is a parent. *Pavan v. Smith* (2017) held that a state must list the female spouse of a woman who gave birth after donor insemination on the birth certificate, as it lists a husband in the same circumstance.

**Gestational surrogacy.** When a woman carries an embryo to which she has no genetic link for **intended parents**, many states apply the **intent test**. *Johnson v. Calvert* (California, 1993) held that where the genetic and the gestational mother are different, the woman who intended to bring about the birth and to raise the child is the natural mother. UPA 2017 provides a framework for enforceable gestational surrogacy agreements with safeguards (independent counsel, medical and psychological screening and an adjudication of parentage). State law runs from permissive (many states now authorize compensated gestational surrogacy, including New York since 2021 and Michigan since 2025) to restrictive; a few states refuse to enforce such agreements.

**Traditional surrogacy** (the surrogate is also the genetic mother) is treated more skeptically. *In re Baby M* (New Jersey, 1988) held that a paid surrogacy contract in which the surrogate gave up her parental rights was unenforceable; the court decided custody by the child's best interests, awarded it to the genetic father, and left the surrogate with her status as mother and visitation.

**Embryos and posthumous conception.** Courts disagree about disputes over frozen embryos when a couple divorces; many enforce a prior written agreement and otherwise balance the parties' interests (*Davis v. Davis*, Tennessee, 1992). A child conceived after a parent's death is generally treated as the parent's child for inheritance only if statutory requirements of consent and timing are met.

### Policy

The rules balance a child's need for two legally responsible adults, the stability of established relationships, the interest in biological ties, and respect for the parties' intentions in assisted reproduction.

### Applying the rules

Ask: who gave birth? Is there a marital presumption? Was a VAP signed and is it final? Is there an adjudication? In assisted reproduction, was there consent and intent, was the person a donor, and was there a valid surrogacy agreement? For an unwed father, ask whether he has done what the "biology plus" cases require.

@example
title: Donor insemination for an unmarried mother and her partner
problem: Ana, who is unmarried, conceives through insemination using a donor's sperm provided through a clinic. Ana's partner Bea signed a consent form and agreed to be a parent. The child is born. The state has adopted UPA 2017. Who are the legal parents?
step: Ana gave birth, so she is a parent.
step: A donor of sperm is not a parent under UPA 2017, so the donor has no rights or duties.
step: UPA 2017 provides that a person who consents to assisted reproduction with the intent to be a parent of the child is a parent, whatever the person's marital status. Bea signed consent and agreed to be a parent.
step: Conclusion: Ana and Bea are the legal parents.

@mistakes
- Treating biology as the only basis of parentage; marriage, acknowledgment, adjudication, adoption and intent all establish it.
- Treating a sperm donor as a parent merely because he is genetically related; under the UPA he is not.
- Assuming the surrogacy rules are uniform; states differ in whether and how they enforce surrogacy agreements.
- Forgetting that a signed VAP is challengeable only for fraud, duress or material mistake of fact after the rescission period.

solution: These cases hold that a biological link gives an unwed father an opportunity interest, and the Constitution protects the relationship he builds, rather than biology alone. In Quilloin, a father who never sought custody or supported the child could be bypassed in a stepparent adoption on a best-interests basis. In Lehr, a father who had never established a relationship and had not registered with the putative father registry had no due process or equal protection right to notice of the adoption. The link is that the father's rights depend on whether he seized the opportunity to develop a relationship (biology plus).
