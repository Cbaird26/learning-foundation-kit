@article
Things are made of **materials**. A spoon may be made of metal. A chair may be made of wood. A shirt is made of cloth.

Each material has **properties**. A property tells how a material looks or feels or acts. Here are some:

- **Hard or soft.** A rock is hard. A pillow is soft.
- **Rough or smooth.** Sandpaper is rough. Glass is smooth.
- **Bendy or stiff.** A rubber band is bendy. A wooden ruler is stiff.
- **Waterproof or not.** Plastic keeps water out. Paper gets soggy.
- **See-through or not.** You can see through glass but not through wood.

### Choose well

We pick materials for the job. A rain coat is waterproof. A window is see-through. A cooking pot is metal.

### Try it

With your grown-up, find five things in the kitchen. Touch each safely (nothing sharp or hot) and say if it is hard or soft, and rough or smooth.

@example
title: A boot for puddles
problem: Which material is best for a puddle boot: paper or rubber?
step: A puddle boot must keep feet dry. It needs a material that water cannot go through.
step: Paper gets soggy. Rubber is waterproof. So rubber is best.

@mistakes
- Thinking the object and the material are the same. A spoon is the object. Metal is the material.
- Thinking heavy means hard. A big wet sponge can be heavy and soft.
