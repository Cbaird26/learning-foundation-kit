@article
An appellate brief is a persuasive document with a fixed form. Courts enforce the form, and a brief that ignores it can be rejected. Learn the rules first, then use the space they give you to argue.

### Parts of the appellant's brief (FRAP 28(a))

In order, an appellant's principal brief contains: (1) a corporate disclosure statement if Rule 26.1 requires one; (2) a table of contents with page references; (3) a table of authorities, with references to the pages where each is cited; (4) a **jurisdictional statement**, giving the basis for the district court's jurisdiction, the basis for the court of appeals' jurisdiction (for example, 28 U.S.C. § 1291, final decisions of district courts, or § 1292(a)(1), interlocutory orders on injunctions) and the filing dates showing that the appeal is timely; (5) a **statement of the issues** presented for review; (6) a **statement of the case** (Week 6); (7) a **summary of the argument**, a succinct, clear statement of the argument actually made in the body; (8) the **argument**, setting out the appellant's contentions and the reasons for them, with citations to authorities and the record, including for each issue a concise statement of the standard of review (28(a)(8)(B)); (9) a short **conclusion** stating the precise relief sought; and (10) the certificate of compliance required by Rule 32(g)(1).

The **appellee's brief** must follow the same form, except that the appellee need not include the jurisdictional statement, the statement of the issues, the statement of the case or the statement of the standard of review unless dissatisfied with the appellant's (Rule 28(b)). The appellant may file a **reply brief** (Rule 28(c)), confined to answering the appellee's arguments.

### Length and form (FRAP 32)

A principal brief may not exceed 30 pages or 13,000 words, and a reply brief half of that (15 pages or 6,500 words) (Rule 32(a)(7)). Word counts exclude certain parts, such as the tables, the certificates and the cover (Rule 32(f)), and a certificate of compliance states the count. A brief in a proportionally spaced typeface must use a 14-point or larger font (Rule 32(a)(5)). Many circuits have local rules that differ in details, so check the circuit's rules. A court may also set shorter limits by order, as in this course's 3,500-word project.

### Timing (FRAP 4 and 31)

A notice of appeal in a civil case is due within 30 days after entry of the judgment or order appealed from, or 60 days if the United States or its officers are a party (Rule 4(a)(1)). The appellant's brief is due within 40 days after the record is filed, the appellee's within 30 days after service of the appellant's brief, and the reply within 14 days after service of the appellee's brief, but a reply must be filed at least 7 days before argument unless the court allows a later filing for good cause (Rule 31(a)(1)). Rule 26(a) governs counting, with the same method as in FRCP 6(a).

### Writing the brief

- **Statement of the issues:** frame each issue persuasively, in one sentence, so the answer you want is the natural one.
- **Summary of the argument:** it is the part judges are most likely to read twice. Make it a stand-alone, one-to-two page version of the argument that follows the headings.
- **Argument:** state the standard of review, then proceed by CREAC under argumentative headings. Cite the record for facts and authority for law.
- **Conclusion:** state the exact relief (reverse and remand with instructions, or affirm), not a general plea.
- **Tables and cites:** the tables of contents and authorities are generated at the end and checked for accuracy.

### Motions and memoranda use the same craft

The district court motion and memorandum from Week 7 follow the same form in miniature: a short motion that states the relief and grounds, and a memorandum with an introduction, facts, standard, argument and conclusion. A well-drafted motion states its relief in the first paragraph.

@example
title: Checking a brief against the rules
problem: Hypothetical: Appellee's counsel files a response brief of 11,800 words in a case where the appellant's jurisdictional statement and statement of the issues were accurate. The brief contains the table of contents, the table of authorities, a statement of the case that repeats the appellant's, a summary of argument, an argument with standards of review, a conclusion and a certificate of compliance. Is the brief compliant?
step: Check length: a principal brief may contain up to 13,000 words, and 11,800 is within the limit.
step: Check contents: the appellee need not include the jurisdictional statement, the issues statement or the standard-of-review statement unless dissatisfied with the appellant's (Rule 28(b)); including the statement of the case is optional.
step: Check required parts: contents, authorities, summary, argument, conclusion and certificate are present.
step: Conclude: the brief is compliant, and the repeated statement of the case is not required but wastes words unless it adds persuasive value.

@mistakes
- Omitting a required part (the standard of review for each issue, the jurisdictional statement or the certificate of compliance) or exceeding the word limit.
- Writing a summary of the argument that merely restates the headings or introduces new points.
- Ending with a vague conclusion instead of the precise relief sought.
- Missing the appeal deadline or the reply-brief deadline by miscounting or by forgetting the 7-days-before-argument rule.
