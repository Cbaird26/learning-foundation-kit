@review
**Week 1: Regulation.** State supreme courts regulate lawyers; the Model Rules bind only where adopted. Know Rule 8.4 (misconduct), Rules 5.1 to 5.3 (supervision), Rule 8.3 (reporting, subject to Rule 1.6) and Rule 8.5 (choice of law). MPRE: 60 questions, 2 hours, 10 unscored, scaled 50 to 150, passing scores set by each jurisdiction.

**Week 2: Formation and termination.** Relationship by manifestation and reasonable reliance; Rule 1.18 for prospective clients; Rule 1.2 allocation of decisions; Rule 1.16 mandatory and permissive withdrawal and the duties on termination.

**Week 3: Competence, diligence, communication.** Rules 1.1, 1.3, 1.4 and 1.14; settlement offers must be communicated; technology and AI require verification and protect confidentiality (Formal Opinion 512).

**Week 4: Fees and property.** Rule 1.5 factors, writing for contingent fees, no contingent fee in divorce or criminal defense, fee division with joint responsibility and written client agreement; Rule 1.15 trust accounts, disputed funds, five-year records; Rule 1.8(e).

**Week 5: Confidentiality.** Rule 1.6(a) covers all information relating to the representation; Rule 1.6(b)(1) to (7) are permissive and limited to what is reasonably necessary; Rule 1.6(c) reasonable efforts; Rule 4.4(b).

**Week 6: Privilege and work product.** Elements; facts are not privileged; Upjohn; crime-fraud and other exceptions; FRE 502; Hickman v. Taylor and FRCP 26(b)(3).

**Weeks 7 and 8: Conflicts.** Rule 1.7 and its four conditions, nonconsentable conflicts, Rule 1.8 specific rules; Rule 1.9, Rule 1.10 imputation and lateral screening with notice and certifications, Rules 1.11 and 1.12.

**Week 9: The court.** Rules 3.1 to 3.8, adverse controlling authority, false evidence and remedial measures, Nix v. Whiteside, Rule 3.3(c) overriding Rule 1.6.

**Week 10: Third parties.** Rules 4.1 to 4.4, Rule 1.13 up-the-ladder reporting, Rule 1.2(d).

**Week 11: Advertising and practice.** Rules 7.1 to 7.3 (specialty communications are in Rule 7.2(c)), 5.4, 5.5, 5.6; Ohralik, Shapero.

**Week 12: Judges, discipline, malpractice.** Rule 2.11, Rule 2.9, Rule 4.1 of the Judicial Code; reciprocal discipline; malpractice elements and case within a case; Rule 1.8(h).

**Self-check.** Can you state each of Rules 1.6(b), 1.7(b), 1.10(a)(2) and 3.3 from memory? Can you tell, for any fact pattern, whether the question asks about discipline, disqualification or liability? Can you separate confidentiality from privilege?

**Common errors.** Treating Model Rules as binding everywhere; using the unrelated-matter argument for a current-client conflict; accepting oral consent where a writing is required; treating Rule 1.6(b) as mandatory; claiming privilege for facts; forgetting that Rule 3.3(c) overrides Rule 1.6; and answering a malpractice question without proving the case within a case.

@exam mc
prompt: In 2023, Pru at Firm A drafted a supply contract for Vale Inc. In 2025 she joins Firm B, which represents Orla Co. in a suit against Vale over that contract. Pru will have no role; Firm B screens her at once, apportions her no fee from the case, but sends no notice to Vale. Under the ABA Model Rules, is Firm B likely to be disqualified?
choice*: Yes, because Rule 1.10(a)(2) requires prompt written notice to the former client in addition to timely screening and no fee share, and none was sent.
choice: No, because Pru will not work on the matter and has been screened with no fee share.
choice: Yes, because screening never prevents imputation of a lateral lawyer's conflict.
choice: No, because Vale is a former client of another firm, not of Firm B.
hint: List all conditions of Rule 1.10(a)(2).
hint: The matter is substantially related and adverse, so Pru's conflict arises under Rule 1.9.
solution: The matter is the same one Pru worked on, so Rule 1.9(a) bars her and Rule 1.10(a) imputes the conflict to the firm unless the Rule 1.10(a)(2) conditions are met: timely screen, no fee, prompt written notice and certifications on request. Without notice, the exception fails. Screening can work for laterals under the Model Rules.

@exam num
prompt: A case settles for \$300,000. The signed agreement gives the lawyer one-third of the recovery after litigation expenses of \$24,000 are deducted. A hospital has a valid lien of \$20,000 that must be paid out of the client's share, so the lawyer must hold that amount in trust for the hospital. How many dollars may the lawyer promptly distribute to the client?
answer: 164000
check: (300000 - 24000) * (1 - 1/3) - 20000
hint: Deduct expenses first, then compute the fee on what remains.
hint: Subtract the lawyer's fee from the net amount, then set aside the lien.
solution: Net after expenses: \$300,000 - \$24,000 = \$276,000. Fee: \$276,000 / 3 = \$92,000. Client's share: \$184,000. Hold \$20,000 for the lienholder (Rule 1.15(d)), leaving \$164,000 to distribute.
unit: dollars

@exam self
prompt: Dana is outside counsel for Helix Inc. Helix's CFO tells her privately that she backdated invoices that Helix has already filed as exhibits in a pending federal suit. The CFO then asks Dana to keep it secret and says she is also looking for personal advice. Helix's board is unaware. Analyze Dana's obligations under the Model Rules, including who the client is, what she must do about the filed exhibits, and whether Rule 1.6 limits her.
solution: Client: Dana represents Helix, not the CFO (Rule 1.13(a)); she must explain this to the CFO, who may have interests adverse to the company, and avoid giving the CFO personal advice other than to seek counsel (Rule 1.13(f), Rule 4.3). Candor: the exhibits are material evidence offered by the client; Dana now knows they are false, so Rule 3.3(a)(3) requires reasonable remedial measures, and Rule 3.3(c) makes the duty apply despite Rule 1.6. Organizational duty: Rule 1.13(b) requires reporting to higher authority, up to the board, because the CFO's conduct violates law and is likely to injure Helix. Order of steps: report to the CEO or audit committee and the board, urge withdrawal or correction of the exhibits; if the company will not correct them, Dana must take further remedial measures, including disclosure to the tribunal if necessary, and consider withdrawal under Rule 1.16(a)(1). Confidentiality: Rule 1.6 does not bar disclosure to the tribunal required by Rule 3.3; Rule 1.13(c) is an additional, permissive basis for disclosure outside the company. Do not disclose more than necessary.
rubric: Identifies the organization as the client, the corporate warning to the CFO and Rule 1.13(f).
rubric: Applies Rule 3.3(a)(3) and (c), including the override of Rule 1.6.
rubric: Describes up-the-ladder reporting under Rule 1.13(b) and the limits on outside disclosure.
rubric: Considers withdrawal and the extent of disclosure necessary.
hint: Start with who the client is, then ask what Dana knows about evidence already in court.
hint: Remember that the duty of candor continues to the end of the proceeding.
