@article
A client is entitled to a lawyer whose loyalty and independent judgment are undivided. Conflicts rules protect that loyalty. This week covers conflicts with **current** clients; next week, former clients and imputation.

### The general rule: Rule 1.7(a)

A **concurrent conflict of interest** exists if (1) the representation of one client will be **directly adverse** to another client, or (2) there is a **significant risk** that the representation of one or more clients will be **materially limited** by the lawyer's responsibilities to another client, a former client or a third person, or by a **personal interest** of the lawyer.

Direct adversity includes suing a current client, even in a matter unrelated to the lawyer's work for that client, and cross-examining a current client as a witness. Simply competing economically with another client is generally not direct adversity. Material limitation covers a lawyer who may pull punches because of loyalty to someone else: representing several parties with differing interests (such as co-defendants or a business and its owner), a lawyer's own financial stake in the matter, or a close relationship with opposing counsel or a party.

### Curing the conflict: Rule 1.7(b)

Even with a conflict, the lawyer may proceed if **all four** conditions hold:

1. the lawyer **reasonably believes** she can provide competent and diligent representation to each affected client;
2. the representation is **not prohibited by law**;
3. it does not involve the **assertion of a claim by one client against another client in the same litigation** or proceeding before a tribunal; and
4. each affected client gives **informed consent, confirmed in writing**.

**Informed consent** (Rule 1.0(e)) means agreement after the lawyer communicates adequate information and explanation about the material risks and reasonably available alternatives. **Confirmed in writing** (Rule 1.0(b)) means the client's consent is in writing, or the lawyer promptly sends a writing confirming oral consent. Conflicts that are **nonconsentable** include claims between co-clients in the same litigation, situations where the lawyer cannot reasonably believe she can represent both well (for example, two clients with flatly opposed positions in a negotiation, or a lawyer who would have to cross-examine one client for another), and cases barred by law. A lawyer may not escape a conflict by dropping a current client to turn it into a former client (the "hot potato" rule); the court applies the current-client standard.

**Joint representation** (for example of two business partners) raises special problems: information shared by one co-client is generally not confidential from the other, and if the clients' interests later diverge, the lawyer may have to withdraw from both. Lawyers should explain these points before taking the joint engagement.

### Specific current-client rules: Rule 1.8

- **(a) Business transactions** with a client are allowed only if (1) the terms are **fair and reasonable** and **fully disclosed in writing** in a manner the client can understand; (2) the client is advised in writing to seek **independent legal advice** and given a reasonable opportunity to do so; and (3) the client gives **informed consent, in a writing signed by the client**, to the terms and the lawyer's role. Taking equity in a client's startup in lieu of fees is a business transaction.
- **(b)** No use of information to the client's disadvantage without informed consent.
- **(c) Gifts**: a lawyer may not solicit a substantial gift from a client, or prepare an instrument giving the lawyer (or a person related to the lawyer) a substantial gift, unless the lawyer is related to the client.
- **(d)** No literary or media rights from a client before the representation ends.
- **(e)** Financial assistance, discussed in week 4.
- **(f) Third-party payment**: the client's informed consent, no interference with the lawyer's independent judgment or the client–lawyer relationship, and Rule 1.6 protection (see also Rule 5.4(c)). A lawyer retained by an insurer to defend an insured owes the insured the duties owed to a client.
- **(g) Aggregate settlements**: a lawyer who represents two or more clients may not take part in an aggregate settlement of their claims (or of criminal pleas) unless each client gives informed consent, **in a writing signed by the client**, after disclosure of the existence and nature of all the claims and each person's share.
- **(h) Malpractice**: no agreement prospectively limiting liability to a client for malpractice unless the client is independently represented in making the agreement; no settlement of a claim with an unrepresented client or former client unless advised in writing to seek independent counsel.
- **(i)** No proprietary interest in the cause of action, except a lien for fees and expenses or a reasonable contingent fee.
- **(j)** No **sexual relations** with a client unless a consensual relationship existed when the representation began.
- **(k)** These prohibitions are imputed to the firm's other lawyers (except (j)).

### Applying the rule

Work in order: Is there direct adversity or a significant risk of material limitation? If yes, can the lawyer reasonably believe she can represent each well? Is anything prohibited by law or a same-litigation claim? Only then ask whether each client gave informed consent confirmed in writing.

@example
title: Suing a current client in an unrelated matter
problem: Firm F represents Acme in a tax matter. Another client, Bea, wants F to sue Acme in an unrelated contract dispute. May F take Bea's case?
step: Identify the conflict: suing a current client is direct adversity under Rule 1.7(a)(1), even though the matters are unrelated.
step: Check cure: F must reasonably believe it can represent both Acme and Bea competently and diligently, the representation must be legal, and it is not a claim by one client against another in the same litigation, because the tax matter is a different proceeding.
step: Informed consent from both Acme and Bea, each confirmed in writing, is required.
step: Conclusion: F may take Bea's case only if all four conditions in Rule 1.7(b) are met; without Acme's consent it must decline. It cannot drop Acme to avoid the rule.

@mistakes
- Saying there is no conflict because the matters are unrelated; direct adversity to a current client needs no relation.
- Treating consent as enough for every conflict; some are nonconsentable.
- Obtaining oral consent without writing or confirmation.
- Forgetting that Rule 1.8(a) and (g) require specific writings, not just informed consent.

@practice self
prompt: A lawyer is asked to represent two friends, Cora and Dev, in forming an LLC to run a bakery. They have not yet agreed on equity splits or who will have control. Analyze whether there is a Rule 1.7 conflict, whether the lawyer may proceed and what she must tell them.
solution: Issue: whether joint representation of the co-founders presents a concurrent conflict. Rule: Rule 1.7(a)(2) bars representation where there is a significant risk that duties to one client will materially limit representation of the other; Rule 1.7(b) permits it if the lawyer reasonably believes she can represent each competently and diligently, it is lawful, there is no claim between them in litigation, and each gives informed consent confirmed in writing. Application: Cora and Dev have common goals in forming the business but potentially opposed interests on equity and control, a risk of material limitation. Forming a company for co-founders is commonly done jointly, and a lawyer can reasonably believe she can serve both if they have agreed on key terms or want neutral drafting. Conclusion: she may proceed with informed consent confirmed in writing, after explaining that information either shares with her is not confidential from the other, that she cannot advocate for one against the other on contested terms, that she may have to withdraw from both if interests diverge, and that each may consult independent counsel.

rubric: Applies all four conditions of Rule 1.7(b).
rubric: Explains confidentiality among co-clients and possible withdrawal.

@quiz mc
prompt: A lawyer agrees to prepare a startup's incorporation documents in exchange for 10 percent of its stock instead of fees. The client is unsophisticated. The lawyer explains the deal orally and the client agrees, but the lawyer gives no writing and does not mention independent counsel. Which statement is most accurate?
choice: The arrangement is permitted because the client understood it.
choice: The arrangement is permitted because equity in lieu of fees is a fee arrangement governed only by Rule 1.5.
choice: The arrangement is prohibited in all circumstances because lawyers may never hold client stock.
choice*: The arrangement is a business transaction with a client that violates Rule 1.8(a) because the required written disclosure, written advice about independent counsel and signed informed consent are missing.
hint: Equity in a client's business is a business transaction.
solution: Rule 1.8(a) requires fair terms fully disclosed in writing, written advice about independent counsel with an opportunity to seek it, and the client's informed consent in a signed writing. It does not forbid the transaction.
