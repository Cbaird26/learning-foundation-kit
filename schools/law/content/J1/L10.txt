@lesson
title: Surface area and volume
summary: Find the volume of a rectangular prism, including with fractional edges, and the surface area of a box from its net.
objective: Compute the volume of a rectangular prism with whole-number and fractional edges.
objective: Draw or read a net and find the surface area of a prism.
objective: Find a missing edge from a given volume.
reading: Khan Academy 6th grade math, the unit "Geometry" (look up "Volume with fractions", "Nets of 3D figures" and "Surface area").

@article
A **rectangular prism** is a box. It has $6$ faces, all rectangles, with opposite faces equal. Its dimensions are length $l$, width $w$ and height $h$.

### Volume

**Volume** measures the space inside a solid, in **cubic** units such as $\text{cm}^3$. Fill a box with unit cubes: one layer holds $l \times w$ cubes, and there are $h$ layers. So

$$V = lwh.$$

A box $5$ by $3$ by $4$ has volume $5 \times 3 \times 4 = 60$ cubic units. A **cube** with edge $s$ has volume $s^3$.

Edges may be fractions. A box $\frac{1}{2}$ by $\frac{3}{4}$ by $4$ has volume $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{4} \times 4 = \frac{3}{2}$ cubic units. Multiply fractions the same way as before.

### Nets

A **net** is a flat pattern that folds into a solid. A rectangular prism's net has six rectangles. Seeing the net helps you count every face once.

### Surface area

**Surface area** is the total area of all the faces, in **square** units. For a box with dimensions $l$, $w$, $h$, the faces come in three equal pairs:

$$SA = 2(lw + lh + wh).$$

For a $10 \times 4 \times 3$ box, the pairs are $10 \times 4 = 40$, $10 \times 3 = 30$ and $4 \times 3 = 12$. Add them and double: $2(40 + 30 + 12) = 164$ square units.

A **cube** with edge $s$ has six equal square faces, so $SA = 6s^2$.

### Working backward

Volume formulas also find missing edges. If $V = 90$, $l = 5$ and $w = 3$, then $15h = 90$, so $h = 6$.

### Which one to use?

Ask what the question is about. Wrapping paper, paint and labels cover the outside, so use surface area in square units. Water, sand and air fill the inside, so use volume in cubic units.

@example
title: Volume and surface area of a box
problem: Find the volume and surface area of a box that is $10$ cm long, $4$ cm wide and $3$ cm tall.
step: Volume: $10 \times 4 \times 3 = 120\ \text{cm}^3$.
step: Find the three face areas from the net: $10 \times 4 = 40$, $10 \times 3 = 30$ and $4 \times 3 = 12$.
step: Each face appears twice, so $SA = 2(40 + 30 + 12) = 164\ \text{cm}^2$.

@mistakes
- Giving surface area in cubic units or volume in square units.
- Counting only three faces, or counting the top and bottom once instead of twice.
- Using the surface area formula when the question asks how much a box can hold.

answer: 60
check: 5 * 3 * 4
unit: cm^3
hint: Multiply length, width and height.
hint: $5 \times 3 = 15$ is the number of cubes in one layer.
solution: $V = 5 \times 3 \times 4 = 60\ \text{cm}^3$.

@practice num

answer: 216
check: 6 ** 3
unit: cm^3
hint: A cube has equal length, width and height.
hint: Compute $6 \times 6 \times 6$.
solution: $V = 6^3 = 216\ \text{cm}^3$.

@practice num
prompt: The net of a box is made of rectangles: two that are $6$ by $4$, two that are $6$ by $3$ and two that are $4$ by $3$ (in centimeters). What is the surface area of the box in square centimeters?
answer: 108
check: 2 * (6 * 4 + 6 * 3 + 4 * 3)
unit: cm^2
hint: Find the area of one rectangle from each pair.
hint: The areas are $24$, $18$ and $12$. Each appears twice.
solution: $2(24 + 18 + 12) = 2 \times 54 = 108\ \text{cm}^2$.

@quiz num
prompt: A box has edges $\frac{2}{3}$ m, $\frac{3}{4}$ m and $6$ m. What is its volume in cubic meters?

check: (2/3) * (3/4) * 6
unit: m^3
hint: Multiply the three edge lengths.
hint: $\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$, then multiply by $6$.
solution: $\frac{1}{2} \times 6 = 3\ \text{m}^3$.
