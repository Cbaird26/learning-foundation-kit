@article
**Percent** means "per 100". The symbol $\%$ is a ratio with second part $100$: $35\%$ means $35$ out of every $100$, or $\frac{35}{100} = 0.35$. To change a percent to a decimal, divide by $100$.

### Three kinds of percent problem

Every percent problem links three numbers: the **part**, the **whole** and the **percent**.

$$\text{part} = \text{percent} \times \text{whole}.$$

1. **Find the part.** What is $25\%$ of $80$? Compute $0.25 \times 80 = 20$.
2. **Find the percent.** $18$ is what percent of $24$? Compute $\frac{18}{24} = 0.75 = 75\%$.
3. **Find the whole.** $15$ is $30\%$ of what number? The whole is $15 \div 0.30 = 50$.

### Ratio tables for percents

A ratio table makes the idea visible. The top row is the percent and the bottom row is the amount, and $100\%$ is the whole.

| Percent | 100 | 10 | 5 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price (\$) | 60 | 6 | 3 | 18 |

Finding $10\%$ means dividing by $10$. From $10\%$ you can build $5\%$ (half of it), $20\%$ (double) and $30\%$ (triple).

### Discounts and prices

A $20\%$ discount takes $20\%$ of the price off. You can pay $80\%$ of the price, so the **sale price** is $0.80 \times \text{price}$. Or find the discount first and subtract it. Both methods agree.

### Increase and decrease

A $10\%$ increase followed by a $10\%$ decrease does **not** return to the start, because the decrease is taken from a larger number. Always ask: percent of what?

@example
title: A discounted jacket
problem: A jacket costs \$45. It is on sale for 30% off. What is the sale price?
step: Find the discount. $30\% = 0.30$, so the discount is $0.30 \times 45 = 13.50$ dollars.
step: Subtract the discount from the price: $45 - 13.50 = 31.50$.
step: Check another way. You pay $100\% - 30\% = 70\%$ of the price: $0.70 \times 45 = 31.50$. The sale price is \$31.50.

@mistakes
- Using the wrong whole, such as taking a percent of the sale price instead of the original price.
- Forgetting to convert the percent to a decimal: $30\%$ of $45$ is $0.30 \times 45$, not $30 \times 45$.
- Giving the discount when the question asks for the sale price.

@practice num
prompt: A shirt costs \$40 and is 20% off. What is the sale price, in dollars?
answer: 32
check: 40 * (1 - 0.20)
unit: dollars
hint: You pay $80\%$ of the price.
hint: Compute $0.80 \times 40$.
solution: The discount is $0.20 \times 40 = 8$, and $40 - 8 = 32$. The sale price is \$32.

@quiz num

answer: 41.6
check: 64 * (1 - 0.35)
tol: 0.005

@quiz num
prompt: A game console costs \$50. Its price goes up by 10%, and then the new price goes down by 10%. What is the final price, in dollars?
answer: 49.5
check: 50 * 1.10 * 0.90
tol: 0.005
hint: Do the increase first, then take $10\%$ of the new price.
hint: After the increase the price is $50 \times 1.10$. Now multiply by $0.90$.
solution: $50 \times 1.10 = 55$. Then $55 \times 0.90 = 49.50$. The final price is \$49.50, not \$50.
