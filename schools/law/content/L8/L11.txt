@article
Interpretation is what courts do with text after the fact; drafting is how legislators and lawyers prevent disputes before they start. Every canon you have studied marks a place where drafting can fail, so a good drafter works backward from the canons.

### Ambiguity versus vagueness

A term is **ambiguous** when it has two or more distinct meanings in context; it is **vague** when its meaning has indistinct boundaries ("reasonable," "unsafe"). Vagueness is often deliberate (a standard that adapts), but it hands discretion to the agency or court. A criminal statute so vague that ordinary people cannot know what it forbids violates due process (*Johnson v. United States* (2015); *Sessions v. Dimaya* (2018)).

### Three sources of ambiguity

1. **Lexical**: a word with two senses ("bank," "motor vehicle"). Fix by defining it.
2. **Syntactic**: unclear grammatical scope. Examples: whether "knowingly" modifies every element (*Flores-Figueroa v. United States* (2009) read "knowingly" in the identity-theft statute to reach the element that the means of identification belongs to another person); whether a phrase modifies the whole series or only the last item (the last antecedent rule, *Barnhart v. Thomas*); the "and/or" problem; and the missing serial comma, which in *O'Connor v. Oakhurst Dairy* (1st Cir. 2017) produced an overtime-exemption list that the court found ambiguous and construed in the drivers' favor.
3. **Contextual**: unclear reference ("it," "such person") or an inconsistent use of terms across sections.

### Drafting tools

- **Define** key terms in one place. Use **"means"** for an exhaustive definition and **"includes"** for an illustrative, nonexclusive one. A statutory definition controls over ordinary meaning (*Burgess*).
- **Use one term for one idea**; never vary words for style, because courts presume different words carry different meanings.
- **Mandatory and permissive verbs**: "must" or "shall" imposes a duty; "may" confers discretion; "must not" prohibits. Avoid "should." Many drafting guides prefer "must" because "shall" is often misused.
- **Break lists into vertical enumerations** ("(1) ...; (2) ...; or (3) ...") with a single connector to show scope; avoid "and/or" and say "A, B or both."
- **State the actor, the act and the time.** Specify how days are counted ("within 30 days after the date of enactment," excluding the date of enactment).
- **Standards for delegations**: tell the agency what the goal is and what to consider; a clear grant of authority avoids a major questions fight.
- **Boilerplate that matters**: a **purpose** or **findings** clause; **effective date**; **severability clause** (courts presume severability and ask whether the remainder can function as Congress would have wanted, *Barr v. American Ass'n of Political Consultants* (2020), but an express clause strengthens the case); a **savings clause** (preserves existing rights or proceedings); **sunset** provisions; and rules of construction where the legislature wants to foreclose a canon.

### Policy

Clear drafting lowers litigation cost, protects notice and keeps policy decisions with the enacting body. Over-precision has costs: rigid text can fail on unforeseen facts. A drafter chooses precision where the stakes are high and standards where flexibility is needed, and says which.

### Applying it (IRAC)

Issue: where might a court see two meanings? Rule: identify the source (lexical, syntactic, contextual). Application: propose the redraft and test it against the original facts. Conclusion: confirm that the redraft yields the intended result for the problem case and does not create a new ambiguity.

@example
title: Redrafting a modifier problem
problem: A provision says: "Each applicant must submit a statement and a fee or a waiver form." The legislature meant that every applicant must submit a statement, and must also submit either a fee or a waiver form. Identify the ambiguity and redraft.
step: Locate the ambiguity: "and ... or" in one sentence permits two readings: (statement and fee) or (waiver form); or statement and (fee or waiver form).
step: Choose the intended structure: a statement from everyone, plus one of two payment-related items.
step: Redraft with a vertical list: "Each applicant must submit (1) a statement; and (2) either a fee or a waiver form."
step: Test it: an applicant who files only a waiver form is now plainly noncompliant because the statement is required. No new ambiguity: "either ... or" marks the alternatives.

@mistakes
- Using "and/or" instead of stating whether the items are alternatives, cumulative or both.
- Using "includes" when the definition is meant to be exhaustive, or "means" when it is meant to be illustrative.
- Using different words for the same concept in different sections.
- Relying on a title or findings clause to narrow clear operative text.

@practice mc
prompt: A drafter wants a provision that unmistakably imposes a duty on a city official to issue a report each year. Which clause best accomplishes that?
choice: "The official may issue a report each year."
choice: "The official should consider issuing a report each year."
choice*: "The official must issue a report each year."
choice: "The official is encouraged to issue a report each year."
hint: Which verb imposes an obligation?
solution: "Must" (or "shall") imposes a duty. "May" confers discretion, and "should" and "is encouraged" are hortatory.

@practice mc
prompt: A statute says that a vendor must provide "a receipt and/or a refund slip." The legislature wants every vendor to give a receipt, and to add a refund slip only when a refund is given. Which redraft best expresses that intent?
choice: "A vendor must provide a receipt or a refund slip."
choice: "A vendor must provide a receipt and a refund slip."
choice*: "A vendor must provide a receipt and, if the vendor gives a refund, a refund slip."
choice: "A vendor should provide a receipt or a refund slip."
hint: Say precisely when each item is required.
solution: The redraft makes the receipt mandatory and the refund slip conditional on a refund. The other choices change the duties or leave them optional.

@practice mc
prompt: A statute makes it a crime to "knowingly use, without authority, a means of identification of another person." The legislature wants to punish only those who know that the identification belongs to a real person. Which redraft best accomplishes this?
choice: "A person commits an offense by using a means of identification of another person."
choice: "A person who knowingly uses a means of identification commits an offense, and ignorance of whose it is does not matter."
choice*: "A person commits an offense by using, without authority, a means of identification while knowing that it belongs to another person."
choice: "A person who uses a means of identification, without authority, shall be punished."
hint: Place the mental state so that it plainly reaches the element in question.
solution: The correct redraft states the knowledge element explicitly ("knowing that it belongs to another person"). The others omit the element or negate it.

@practice self
prompt: Draft a definitions section and a severability clause for a statute regulating "electric scooters" on city sidewalks. The statute should cover any two-wheeled, motor-assisted device that carries a standing rider and has a top speed above 10 miles per hour, but not wheelchairs or mobility aids. Use "means" or "includes" correctly and explain your choices in two sentences.
hint: Decide which terms need to be exhaustive and which illustrative.
solution: Sec. 1. Definitions. In this chapter: (a) "Electric scooter" means a device that (1) has two wheels; (2) is propelled in whole or in part by an electric motor; (3) carries a rider who stands; and (4) is capable of a speed above 10 miles per hour on level ground. The term does not include a wheelchair or other mobility aid. (b) "Mobility aid" includes a wheelchair, a walker and any other device designed for use by a person with a mobility impairment. Sec. 2. Severability. If any provision of this chapter or its application to any person or circumstance is held invalid, the remainder of the chapter and its application to other persons or circumstances is not affected. Explanation: "Means" in (a) makes the scooter definition exhaustive, with the four elements joined by "and" in a list; "includes" in (b) leaves room for devices that cannot be listed. The severability clause supports preserving the rest of the chapter if one application fails.
rubric: Uses "means" for the exhaustive definition and "includes" for the illustrative one.
rubric: Lists the elements of the scooter definition with a clear connector and states the speed threshold.
rubric: Excludes mobility aids expressly.
rubric: Includes a workable severability clause.

@quiz num
prompt: A statute provides that an agency "must issue its decision within 30 days after the date of enactment," and states that the date of enactment is not counted. The statute is enacted on January 20 of a year that is not a leap year. On what day of February is the last day to issue the decision?
answer: 19
check: 20 + 30 - 31
hint: Do not count January 20 itself.
hint: January has 31 days; add 30 to the 20th and subtract the length of January.
solution: Counting begins on January 21. January 20 plus 30 days is February 19 ($20 + 30 - 31 = 19$).

@quiz mc
prompt: A drafter wants the definition of "dwelling" to cover only buildings used as a residence, with no other structures. Which formulation is best?
choice: "'Dwelling' includes a building used as a residence."
choice*: "'Dwelling' means a building used as a residence."
choice: "'Dwelling' should be read to cover residences."
choice: "'Dwelling' includes any structure the Secretary considers a residence."
hint: Which verb signals an exhaustive definition?
solution: "Means" is exhaustive. "Includes" is ordinarily illustrative and would allow other structures to qualify, and the choices saying "should be read to cover" and "any structure the Secretary considers a residence" leave the scope open.
