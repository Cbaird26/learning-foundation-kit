@article
Citation does three jobs: it gives credit to the people whose ideas and findings you use, lets readers find your sources, and shows how your argument rests on evidence. **Plagiarism** is presenting someone else's words or ideas as your own, whether on purpose or by carelessness. You cite quotations, paraphrases, data and any idea that is not common knowledge. **Common knowledge** is information readers across the field accept without a source (for example, that water boils at 100 degrees Celsius at sea level), but when in doubt, cite.

Two systems dominate high school and college writing: **APA** (common in the sciences, health and social sciences) and **MLA** (common in the humanities). Both use a short **in-text citation** pointing to a full entry in a list at the end. Always follow the current edition and your instructor's rules.

### APA (7th edition)

In-text, give the author and year: (Rivera & Chen, 2021). With a direct quotation add a page: (Rivera & Chen, 2021, p. 115). With three or more authors use the first author and "et al.": (Rivera et al., 2021). In a sentence: Rivera and Chen (2021) found that...

The list is titled **References**, in alphabetical order, with a **hanging indent**. A journal article:

Rivera, M. L., & Chen, T. (2021). Later school start times and adolescent sleep. *Journal of Adolescent Health Studies, 14*(2), 112-125.

Key features: last name and initials; year in parentheses; article title in **sentence case** (only the first word and proper nouns capitalized) and not italic; journal title and volume in italics, issue in parentheses; page range; and the DOI, written in its URL form, when one exists. A book:

Okafor, J. (2019). *Schools and sleep: A policy guide*. Greenfield Press.

(These sources are invented for practice.)

### MLA (9th edition)

In-text, give the author and page with no comma and no year: (Rivera and Chen 115). The list is titled **Works Cited**, alphabetical, with a hanging indent. An MLA entry is built from **core elements**: author, title of source, title of container, other contributors, version, number, publisher, date and location. A journal article:

Rivera, Maria L., and Tao Chen. "Later School Start Times and Adolescent Sleep." *Journal of Adolescent Health Studies*, vol. 14, no. 2, 2021, pp. 112-25.

Key features: full author names; article title in **title case** inside quotation marks; the container (journal) in italics; volume, number, year and pages. A book:

Okafor, Jide. *Schools and Sleep: A Policy Guide*. Greenfield Press, 2019.

### Quoting and paraphrasing

Use short **quotations** when the exact wording matters and put them in quotation marks with the citation. Longer quotations are set off as a block (APA: 40 or more words; MLA: more than four lines of prose). Otherwise **paraphrase**: restate the idea in your own words and structure, and still cite. A paraphrase that changes only a few words is still plagiarism.

### Annotated bibliography

An **annotated bibliography** lists sources, each followed by a short paragraph (usually 3 to 6 sentences). A good annotation does three things: **summarizes** the source's argument and method, **evaluates** its authority and quality, and explains **how it fits your project**. In APA, the citation comes first (hanging indent) and the annotation sits below it, indented.

@example
title: One APA annotated entry
problem: Write an APA annotated bibliography entry for the invented article by Rivera and Chen (2021) on later school start times. The study followed 1,200 students in one district for two years and found about 40 more minutes of weekday sleep after start times moved 60 minutes later.
step: Write the APA citation: Rivera, M. L., & Chen, T. (2021). Later school start times and adolescent sleep. *Journal of Adolescent Health Studies, 14*(2), 112-125.
step: Summarize in one or two sentences: the study followed 1,200 students for two years and found about 40 more minutes of weekday sleep after the start time moved 60 minutes later.
step: Evaluate: the sample is large and the design follows the same students over time, but it covers one district, so results may not apply everywhere.
step: Explain relevance: the article supplies direct evidence on sleep, which my paper connects to attendance and performance.

@mistakes
- Citing in-text with MLA style (page number, no year) in an APA paper, or the reverse.
- Capitalizing every word of an article title in an APA reference, or forgetting the quotation marks in MLA.
- Paraphrasing without a citation because the words are "my own."
- Writing an annotation that only summarizes, with no evaluation and no connection to the project.

@practice self
prompt: Write an annotated bibliography entry in APA style for this invented source. Authors: Daniela Ortiz and Priya Nair. Year: 2022. Article title: Free breakfast programs and school attendance. Journal: Journal of School Nutrition Research, volume 9, issue 1, pages 33-48. Content: a study of 14 elementary schools found that attendance rose by about 2 percentage points after breakfast became free; it did not randomize schools and relied on district records. Include the citation and a four-sentence annotation.
hint: Put the citation first in this order: authors with initials, year in parentheses, article title in sentence case, then the journal title and volume in italics, issue in parentheses, and pages.
hint: Then write four sentences: summarize the finding and method, evaluate strength and limit, and explain how the source fits your paper.
solution: Ortiz, D., & Nair, P. (2022). Free breakfast programs and school attendance. *Journal of School Nutrition Research, 9*(1), 33-48. Ortiz and Nair studied 14 elementary schools and found that attendance rose by about two percentage points after breakfast became free. The data come from district attendance records, which are consistent across schools, but schools were not randomized, so other changes may explain part of the gain. The article appears in a field-specific journal, which suggests expert review, although I would confirm that the journal is peer reviewed. This source gives direct evidence on attendance for my paper and also illustrates the limits of observational data.
rubric: The citation follows APA order, with initials, year in parentheses, sentence-case title and italic journal and volume.
rubric: The annotation summarizes the finding and method.
rubric: The annotation evaluates the source's strengths and limits.
rubric: The annotation explains how the source fits the project.
