@article
A **natural resource** is anything from nature that people use, such as air, water, soil, minerals, forests and energy sources.

### Renewable and nonrenewable

**Renewable resources** are replaced by nature fast enough to keep using them: sunlight, wind, flowing water, geothermal heat, and (if managed well) forests and fresh water. **Nonrenewable resources** exist in fixed amounts or form so slowly that, for humans, they cannot be replaced: coal, oil, natural gas, and metal ores. Uranium used in nuclear power is also nonrenewable, but nuclear plants release almost no carbon dioxide while operating.

### Fossil fuels

Coal, oil and natural gas are **fossil fuels**. They formed over many millions of years from the remains of ancient plants and tiny sea organisms that were buried and squeezed by heat and pressure. Burning them releases stored energy and also carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas. Fossil fuels supply most of the world's energy today.

### Renewable energy

- **Solar:** panels turn sunlight directly into electricity.
- **Wind:** moving air turns turbine blades.
- **Hydroelectric:** falling water turns turbines (dams change river habitats).
- **Geothermal:** heat from underground makes steam or warms buildings.
- **Biomass:** burning plant material or fuels made from it.

Each has costs and limits: sun and wind vary with weather, and every method has some environmental effect. Choices about energy involve trade-offs.

### Conservation

**Conservation** means using less and wasting less. Reducing, reusing and recycling save materials and energy. Better insulation, efficient bulbs and appliances, and planting trees all help.

### Measuring energy

**Power** is the rate of using energy and is measured in watts (W). **Energy** is power times time. Electricity is billed in **kilowatt-hours (kWh)**:
$$E\ (\text{kWh}) = \frac{P\ (\text{W}) \times t\ (\text{h})}{1000}.$$

### Climate versus weather in data

Weather data are the daily numbers; climate is their long-term average. To compare them, find the mean of the daily weather values and compare it with the long-term average, and look at how much individual days vary. A hot or cold week can sit above or below the climate average without changing it.

@example
title: Energy used by a television
problem: A television uses 200 W and is on for 4 hours. How many kilowatt-hours of energy does it use?
step: Multiply power by time: $200 \times 4 = 800$ watt-hours.
step: Convert to kilowatt-hours by dividing by $1000$: $\frac{800}{1000} = 0.8$ kWh.
step: Check units: watts times hours gives watt-hours, and dividing by $1000$ gives kilowatt-hours.

@mistakes
- Thinking renewable means unlimited or free of impact. Every energy source has costs.
- Mixing up watts (rate) with watt-hours (amount of energy).
- Saying fossil fuels are renewable because new ones are always forming. They form over millions of years.
- Forgetting to divide by 1,000 when changing watt-hours to kilowatt-hours.

unit: kWh
tol: 1%

solution: $\frac{1500 \times 2}{1000} = 3$ kWh.

@practice num
prompt: A 60 W bulb is replaced with a 10 W LED that gives the same light. Both are on 5 hours per day for 30 days. How many kilowatt-hours are saved?
answer: 7.5
check: (60 - 10) * 5 * 30 / 1000
unit: kWh
tol: 1%
hint: Find the difference in power first.
hint: The difference is 50 W. Multiply by the total hours (5 × 30) and divide by 1,000.
solution: Saved power $= 50$ W. Total time $= 150$ h. Energy saved $= \frac{50 \times 150}{1000} = 7.5$ kWh.

@practice self
prompt: The daily high temperatures (°C) in a city for one week in July were 31, 33, 29, 35, 30, 32 and 34. The 30-year average July high for the city is 32 °C. Compute the mean of the week and the range (highest minus lowest). Then explain whether this one week describes the city's weather or its climate, and why one hotter week would not change the climate average much.
hint: Add the seven values and divide by 7; for the range subtract the smallest from the largest.
solution: Sum $= 224$, so the mean is $224 \div 7 = 32$ °C. Range $= 35 - 29 = 6$ °C. The week's numbers describe weather, because they cover only seven days. They happen to match the 30-year average of 32 °C here, but individual days vary from $29$ to $35$ °C. One hotter week is a tiny part of the 30 years of data, so it would hardly change the average. Climate is the long-term average, which only changes if many years shift.
rubric: Correct mean (32 °C) and range (6 °C).
rubric: Identifies the one week as weather and the 30-year average as climate.
rubric: Explains that one week is a small part of a long record, so it changes the average very little.

@quiz num
prompt: A 800 W microwave oven runs for 15 minutes. How many kilowatt-hours does it use?
answer: 0.2
check: 800 * (15 / 60) / 1000
unit: kWh
tol: 1%
hint: Convert 15 minutes to hours first.
solution: $15$ min $= 0.25$ h, so energy $= \frac{800 \times 0.25}{1000} = 0.2$ kWh.
