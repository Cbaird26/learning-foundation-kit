@article
**Summary judgment** ends a case or claim before trial when there is nothing for a factfinder to decide. Under **FRCP 56(a)**, the court must grant it if the movant shows that there is **no genuine dispute as to any material fact** and the movant is **entitled to judgment as a matter of law**. It is the principal tool for resolving weak claims after discovery. It does not violate the Seventh Amendment, because it applies only when no reasonable jury could find for the nonmovant.

### The 1986 trilogy

**Celotex Corp. v. Catrett (1986).** A defendant who will not bear the burden of proof at trial can move by pointing to the **absence of evidence** supporting the plaintiff's claim; it need not produce evidence negating it. The burden of production then shifts to the plaintiff to identify evidence showing a genuine dispute on each element on which she bears the burden at trial. If she cannot, judgment is entered for the defendant.

**Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc. (1986).** A dispute is **genuine** if the evidence is such that a reasonable jury could return a verdict for the nonmovant. A fact is **material** if it might affect the outcome under the governing law. The judge's function is not to weigh evidence or decide credibility but to decide whether a trial is needed. The court applies the evidentiary standard that would govern at trial (for example, clear and convincing evidence).

**Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Zenith Radio Corp. (1986).** The nonmovant must do more than show "metaphysical doubt" about the facts, and when a claim is implausible in light of the record, she needs stronger evidence to survive.

### Viewing the evidence

The court views the record in the light **most favorable to the nonmovant**, drawing reasonable inferences in her favor and crediting her evidence (*Tolan v. Cotton*, 2014). It does not credit evidence that is blatantly contradicted by the record, such as an undisputed video, so that no reasonable jury could believe it (*Scott v. Harris*, 2007).

### Burdens

- If the **movant bears the burden of proof at trial** (for example, a plaintiff on her claim or a defendant on an affirmative defense), it must establish every essential element with evidence that no reasonable jury could disbelieve.
- If the **nonmovant bears the burden at trial**, the movant may show that the nonmovant lacks evidence (*Celotex*).

### Rule 56 mechanics

- **Timing (Rule 56(b)).** Unless a local rule or order sets a different time, a party may move at any time until **30 days after the close of all discovery**.
- **Support (Rule 56(c)(1)).** A party asserting that a fact is or is not genuinely disputed must cite particular parts of the record (depositions, documents, ESI, affidavits or declarations, admissions, interrogatory answers) or show that the cited materials do not establish a dispute or that the other side cannot produce admissible evidence.
- **Form of evidence (Rule 56(c)(2), (4)).** A party may object that material cannot be presented in a form that would be admissible at trial. Affidavits and declarations (28 U.S.C. § 1746) must be made on personal knowledge, set out facts that would be admissible and show that the affiant is competent to testify.
- **Need for more discovery (Rule 56(d)).** If a nonmovant shows by affidavit or declaration that, for specified reasons, she cannot present facts essential to justify her opposition, the court may defer or deny the motion, allow time for discovery or issue another appropriate order.
- **Partial relief (Rules 56(a), (g)).** A party may move for summary judgment on a claim, defense or part of one. If the court does not grant all relief, it may state which facts are not genuinely in dispute, and those facts are treated as established in the case.
- **Court's own initiative (Rule 56(f)).** After notice and a reasonable time to respond, the court may grant summary judgment for a nonmovant or on grounds not raised.
- **Reasons.** The court should state its reasons on the record (Rule 56(a)).
- **Appeal.** A denial of summary judgment is generally not immediately appealable (a notable exception is a collateral-order appeal from denial of qualified immunity).

### Related devices

A **Rule 12(c)** motion for judgment on the pleadings tests only the pleadings. **Rule 50** judgment as a matter of law applies at or after trial using the same standard (next lesson).

### Discovery planning

Because summary judgment turns on record evidence, a **discovery plan** under Rules 16(b) and 26(f) should identify the elements on which each side needs proof, the custodians, sources and ESI, the depositions to take, the experts, the deadlines (including the date to move for summary judgment) and privilege issues. Under the December 1, 2025 amendments, the plan must also address how privileged material withheld from production will be described (the timing and method of a Rule 26(b)(5)(A) log).

### Policy

Summary judgment saves jurors and parties the expense of trials that cannot succeed. Critics argue that it can be used too aggressively, taking factual disputes away from juries; the rule's text is meant to confine it to cases with no genuine dispute.

### Applying it

Identify who bears the burden at trial, state what the movant says is undisputed, check whether the nonmovant's cited evidence would let a reasonable jury find for her on each element, and conclude. Do not weigh credibility.
@example
title: Deciding a motion
problem: Rae sues Sal for negligence after falling on an icy path at Sal's store. Sal moves for summary judgment, arguing that Rae has no evidence that the ice had been on the path long enough for Sal to know of it. Rae's response includes her own declaration (based on personal knowledge) that she saw an employee walk past the ice twenty minutes before her fall, and a store log showing the path was last inspected five hours earlier. How should the court rule?
step: Identify the burden. Rae must prove notice at trial, so Sal may use Celotex by pointing to the absence of evidence of notice.
step: Check Rae's evidence. Her declaration is based on personal knowledge and is admissible. It, plus the log, would let a reasonable jury infer that the ice was visible and unaddressed for a substantial time.
step: View the facts in the light most favorable to Rae and do not weigh credibility. Sal may dispute the declaration at trial, but that is a credibility question for the jury.
step: Conclude that there is a genuine dispute about a material fact (notice), and the motion should be denied.
@mistakes
- Letting the judge weigh witness credibility on a summary judgment motion.
- Requiring the movant to disprove the nonmovant's claim even when the nonmovant bears the burden at trial (Celotex).
- Counting a dispute that is immaterial to the governing law as a bar to summary judgment.
- Overlooking Rule 56(d) when the nonmovant needs more discovery.
