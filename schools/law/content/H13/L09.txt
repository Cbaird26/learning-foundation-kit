@article
The two World Wars (1914 to 1918 and 1939 to 1945) were the deadliest conflicts in history and reshaped borders, economies, science and medicine.

### World War I

Historians list long-term causes by the acronym **MAIN**: **m**ilitarism (arms buildups), **a**lliances (the Triple Entente of France, Russia and Britain against the Triple Alliance of Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy), **i**mperial rivalries, and **n**ationalism, especially in the Balkans. The immediate trigger was the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 by a Bosnian Serb nationalist. Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia, and alliances pulled in Russia, Germany, France and Britain. Historians still debate how much responsibility each government bore; some stress German policy, others the system of alliances and mutual miscalculation.

The Western Front became a stalemate of trenches, machine guns and artillery. New weapons included poison gas, tanks and aircraft. Fighting also took place in Eastern Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Asia, and troops and laborers from colonies served on all sides. The war ended with the armistice of 11 November 1918. Russia's war effort collapsed in 1917, leading to revolution.

**Consequences:** the Austro-Hungarian, German, Russian and Ottoman empires ended; new states appeared; the Treaty of Versailles (1919) required Germany to accept responsibility for the war's damage and to pay reparations, which many Germans resented. The League of Nations was created but lacked strong enforcement. In 1918 to 1919 an influenza pandemic, sometimes called the "Spanish flu" although its origin is uncertain, killed an estimated 17 million to 50 million people or more worldwide (estimates vary widely), helped along by troop movements.

### The interwar years

Economic crises, including the Great Depression after 1929, weakened democracies. Fascist and militarist governments rose in Italy, Germany and Japan, while the Soviet Union developed a one-party communist state.

### World War II

In Europe the war began on 1 September 1939 with Germany's invasion of Poland. The Axis (Germany, Italy, Japan) fought the Allies (including Britain, the Soviet Union, the United States and China). In Asia, Japan had invaded China in 1937, and attacked Pearl Harbor in December 1941. Nazi Germany carried out the **Holocaust**, the systematic murder of about six million Jews and millions of others, including Roma, disabled people and political prisoners. The war in Europe ended in May 1945. After the United States used atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945, Japan surrendered. Historians and others still debate the decision.

### Consequences for medicine and international order

- Penicillin, discovered in 1928, was mass-produced during the war and saved many wounded soldiers; blood banks, trauma care and mobile surgical units advanced.
- Physicians took part in atrocities, including unethical experiments on prisoners. The **Nuremberg Code** (1947) required voluntary informed consent for research.
- The United Nations was founded in 1945, followed by the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) and the World Health Organization (1948).

### Explaining causes

For the project deliverable, separate **long-term causes** (conditions), **short-term causes** (triggers) and **consequences**. A single cause rarely explains a war.

@example
title: Spacing the wars
problem: World War I ended in 1918 and World War II began in 1939. How many years separated them, and what does this suggest about Versailles?
step: Compute $1939 - 1918 = 21$ years.
step: A gap of 21 years is a generation, so many people who fought in the first war were still alive in the second.
step: Historians debate whether Versailles was too harsh, too lenient or simply not enforced; most also stress the Great Depression and the rise of aggressive regimes as causes of the second war.

@mistakes
- Saying the assassination alone "caused" World War I; it triggered a crisis in an already tense system.
- Blaming the Treaty of Versailles as the sole cause of World War II.
- Describing the 1918 flu as having started in Spain; the name came from wartime press coverage.
- Treating the atomic bombings as settled; historians present several arguments, and the debate continues.
