@article
Two figures are **congruent** if one can be moved onto the other by sliding, turning or flipping, without stretching. For triangles this means all three pairs of corresponding sides are equal and all three pairs of corresponding angles are equal. We write $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$, and the **order of the letters matters**: it says $A$ matches $D$, $B$ matches $E$, $C$ matches $F$, so $AB = DE$, $\angle B \cong \angle E$, and so on.

Checking all six pairs is wasteful. Because a triangle is rigid, three well-chosen facts are enough.

### The congruence criteria

- **SSS (side-side-side):** all three pairs of sides are equal.
- **SAS (side-angle-side):** two pairs of sides and the angle **between** them are equal.
- **ASA (angle-side-angle):** two pairs of angles and the side **between** them are equal.
- **AAS (angle-angle-side):** two pairs of angles and a side that is **not** between them are equal. It follows from ASA because the third angles must also be equal (angles sum to $180^\circ$).
- **HL (hypotenuse-leg):** for right triangles only, the hypotenuses are equal and one pair of legs is equal.

### What does not work

**AAA** shows only that triangles have the same shape, not the same size: a triangle with angles $40^\circ, 60^\circ, 80^\circ$ can have any scale. **SSA** (two sides and a non-included angle) fails in general: with a given angle and two sides, the side opposite the angle may swing to two different positions and produce two different triangles. Never cite SSA or AAA as a reason.

### Picture a case

Draw segment $AB$ of length $5$. At $A$, draw a ray making a $40^\circ$ angle with $AB$, and mark $C$ on it $7$ units from $A$. The triangle $ABC$ is fixed: any other triangle with sides $5$ and $7$ and a $40^\circ$ angle between them is congruent by SAS. If the $40^\circ$ angle is not between the sides, you do not have SAS.

### Using congruence

Once triangles are congruent, every pair of corresponding parts is equal. This is abbreviated **CPCTC**: corresponding parts of congruent triangles are congruent. It is the main tool for finding unknown lengths and angles, and the key move in the proofs of the next lesson: first prove the triangles congruent by one of the criteria, then conclude something about parts.

Also remember that shared sides and vertical angles are free information. A side common to two triangles is equal to itself (the **reflexive property**), and vertical angles are equal, so both often supply a pair for the criterion.

@example
title: Congruent triangles from crossing segments
problem: Segments $AC$ and $BD$ cross at point $E$. $E$ is the midpoint of $AC$ and also the midpoint of $BD$. Show that $\triangle ABE \cong \triangle CDE$.
step: Since $E$ is the midpoint of $AC$, $AE = CE$. That is one pair of sides.
step: Since $E$ is the midpoint of $BD$, $BE = DE$. That is a second pair of sides.
step: The angles $\angle AEB$ and $\angle CED$ are vertical angles, so they are equal. This angle lies between the two pairs of sides.
step: Two sides and the included angle are equal, so $\triangle ABE \cong \triangle CDE$ by SAS. By CPCTC, $AB = CD$.

@mistakes
- Citing SSA as a criterion; an angle that is not between the two sides does not fix the triangle.
- Writing the congruence statement with the vertices in the wrong order, so the corresponding parts are mismatched.
- Using AAA as a congruence criterion; it only gives similar triangles.
- Applying HL to a triangle that has no right angle.

prompt: Which set of information is NOT enough, by itself, to prove two triangles congruent?
choice: Three pairs of equal sides.
choice: Two pairs of equal sides and the equal angle between them.
choice*: Two pairs of equal sides and an equal angle that is not between them.
choice: Two pairs of equal angles and the equal side between them.
hint: Remember which angle position makes SAS valid.
solution: Two sides and a non-included angle is SSA, which can give two different triangles. SSS, SAS and ASA are valid criteria.

@practice num
prompt: Triangle $PQR$ has vertices $P(0,0)$, $Q(6,0)$ and $R(0,8)$. Triangle $XYZ$ has vertices $X(2,1)$, $Y(8,1)$ and $Z(2,9)$. Both have a right angle (at $P$ and at $X$), and the legs match: $6$ and $8$. They are congruent by SAS. Find the length $YZ$.
answer: 10
check: math.sqrt(6**2 + 8**2)
hint: $YZ$ corresponds to $QR$, the hypotenuse.
hint: Use the Pythagorean theorem with legs $6$ and $8$.
solution: $QR = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10$, and $YZ = QR = 10$ by CPCTC.

@quiz num
prompt: $\triangle JKL \cong \triangle MNO$. $JK = 2x + 7$ and $MN = 4x - 5$, and $KL = 3x - 2$. Find the length of $\overline{NO}$.
answer: 16
check: 3 * ((7 + 5) / 2) - 2
hint: Find $x$ from the corresponding sides $JK$ and $MN$.
hint: $NO$ corresponds to $KL$.
solution: $2x + 7 = 4x - 5$ gives $x = 6$. Then $KL = 3(6) - 2 = 16$, and $NO = KL = 16$.
