@article
A **transformation** moves every point of a figure according to a rule. The original is the **preimage** and the result is the **image**.

### Rigid motions

A **rigid motion** (or isometry) keeps all distances and angles the same, so the image is congruent to the preimage.

- **Translation** by the vector $\langle a, b\rangle$ slides every point: $(x, y) \to (x + a, y + b)$.
- **Reflection** flips the figure across a line. Across the $x$-axis: $(x, y) \to (x, -y)$. Across the $y$-axis: $(x, y) \to (-x, y)$. Across the line $y = x$: $(x, y) \to (y, x)$.
- **Rotation** turns the figure about a point by an angle. About the origin, counterclockwise: $90^\circ$: $(x, y) \to (-y, x)$; $180^\circ$: $(x, y) \to (-x, -y)$; $270^\circ$: $(x, y) \to (y, -x)$.

### Congruence and similarity, defined by motions

Two figures are **congruent** if a sequence of rigid motions maps one onto the other. This explains why SSS, SAS and ASA work: they pin down a unique triangle up to a rigid motion.

### Dilations

A **dilation** with center $O$ and scale factor $k > 0$ moves every point along the ray from $O$ so that its distance from $O$ is multiplied by $k$. About the origin: $(x, y) \to (kx, ky)$. Lengths are multiplied by $k$, angles are unchanged, and each side is parallel to its image. If $k > 1$ the figure grows; if $0 < k < 1$ it shrinks. Two figures are **similar** if a sequence of rigid motions and dilations maps one onto the other.

### Symmetry

A figure has **line symmetry** if a reflection across some line maps it onto itself. A regular $n$-gon has $n$ lines of symmetry. A figure has **rotational symmetry** if a rotation of less than $360^\circ$ maps it onto itself; a regular $n$-gon is mapped onto itself by a turn of $\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$. A parallelogram that is not a rhombus or rectangle has rotational symmetry of $180^\circ$ but no line of symmetry.

### Compositions

Doing one transformation after another is a **composition**. Order can matter: reflecting then translating usually differs from translating then reflecting. To follow a point, apply each rule in turn and write the point after every step.

### Classifying a figure on the plane

Combine this week's tools with last week's: compute side vectors, slopes and distances, then decide which properties hold. A translation that maps one side onto the opposite side is a quick check for a parallelogram, and a parallelogram whose diagonals are perpendicular (product of slopes $-1$) is a rhombus.

@example
title: A composition of two transformations
problem: Point $P(2, 5)$ is reflected across the line $y = x$, and then the image is translated by $\langle -3, 1\rangle$. Find the final image.
step: Reflection across $y = x$ swaps the coordinates: $(2, 5) \to (5, 2)$.
step: Translation by $\langle -3, 1\rangle$ adds $-3$ to $x$ and $1$ to $y$: $(5, 2) \to (5 - 3,\ 2 + 1)$.
step: The final image is $(2, 3)$.

@mistakes
- Treating a dilation as a rigid motion; it changes lengths by the scale factor.
- Rotating with the wrong rule, for example using $(x, y) \to (y, -x)$ for a counterclockwise $90^\circ$ turn.
- Forgetting that the order of a composition can change the result.
- Claiming a figure is a rhombus or square without checking side lengths and slopes.

@practice self
prompt: Classify the quadrilateral with vertices $A(0, 0)$, $B(4, 1)$, $C(5, 5)$ and $D(1, 4)$. Use slopes and distances to decide whether it is a parallelogram, a rhombus, a rectangle or a square, and give the evidence for each claim.
hint: Compute the slopes of all four sides and the lengths of all four sides.
hint: To test for a right angle, check whether adjacent slopes multiply to $-1$.
solution: Slopes: $AB$: $\tfrac{1}{4}$; $DC$: $\tfrac{5-4}{5-1} = \tfrac{1}{4}$; $AD$: $4$; $BC$: $\tfrac{5-1}{5-4} = 4$. Opposite sides are parallel, so it is a parallelogram. Lengths: $AB = \sqrt{16 + 1} = \sqrt{17}$, $BC = \sqrt{1 + 16} = \sqrt{17}$, $CD = \sqrt{17}$, $DA = \sqrt{17}$: all four sides are equal, so it is a rhombus. Right angle test: slopes of adjacent sides $AB$ and $AD$ are $\tfrac{1}{4}$ and $4$, with product $1$, not $-1$, so the angles are not right angles. It is a rhombus that is not a rectangle or square. Check: diagonals $AC$ (slope $1$) and $BD$ (slope $\tfrac{4-1}{1-4} = -1$) have product $-1$, as the diagonals of a rhombus should.
rubric: Computes slopes for all four sides and shows both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
rubric: Computes all four side lengths and shows they are equal.
rubric: Tests the angle with slopes (product of slopes is $1$, not $-1$) and rules out rectangle and square.
rubric: States the most specific classification, a rhombus, with the evidence.

@quiz mc
prompt: Which transformation does NOT always produce an image congruent to the preimage?
choice: A reflection across a line.
choice: A rotation of $90^\circ$ about the origin.
choice*: A dilation with scale factor $2$.
choice: A translation by a vector.
hint: Congruent means the same size and shape.
solution: A dilation by $2$ doubles all lengths, so the image is similar but not congruent. Reflections, rotations and translations are rigid motions.
