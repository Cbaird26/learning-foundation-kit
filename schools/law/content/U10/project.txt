@review
**Week 1: Compensatory damages.** Remedies protect the expectation, reliance or restitution interest. Expectation is loss in value plus other loss minus cost avoided (§ 347); UCC cover is cover price minus contract price plus incidental damages (§ 2-712). Know the lesser-of rule, economic waste (*Jacob & Youngs*), present value, and nominal damages (*Uzuegbunam*).

**Week 2: Limits.** Certainty (§ 352), foreseeability (*Hadley*, § 2-715(2)), and mitigation (§ 350, *Parker*, *Rockingham County*, lost-volume sellers under § 2-708(2)).

**Weeks 3-4: Restitution.** Unjust enrichment elements; emergency services; express contract and the contract-price limit; change of position; breaching plaintiffs (§ 374); disgorgement of net profit attributable to the wrong. Constructive trust versus equitable lien, tracing and the lowest intermediate balance rule, bona fide purchasers, and *Montanile* and *Liu*.

**Weeks 5-6: Equitable relief.** *Winter* (four factors, likely irreparable harm), *eBay*, FRCP 65 (14-day TRO, security, specificity), *Grupo Mexicano*, *Trump v. CASA* (complete relief, no universal injunctions). Specific performance (land, unique goods, personal services and negative injunctions), abatement, laches, unclean hands.

**Weeks 7-8: Punitive damages and fees.** *Gore* guideposts, *State Farm* ratios, *Philip Morris*, Title VII caps. Declaratory judgments (*MedImmune*, discretion). American Rule, prevailing party (*Buckhannon*, *Lackey v. Stinnie*), lodestar, Rule 68.

**Weeks 9-10: Choice of law.** First Restatement (place of injury, place of contracting, procedure is forum law), *Klaxon*; interest analysis (false and true conflicts), Second Restatement §§ 6, 145, 146, 187, 188.

**Weeks 11-12: Judgments and constitutional limits.** Full faith and credit for judgments, no public-policy exception, litigated-jurisdiction rule (*Durfee*), *Baker v. GM*, comity for foreign-country judgments. *Hague*/*Shutts* test, *Sun Oil*, *Hyatt* (2016 and 2019), *Mallory*.

**Self-check.** Can you (1) compute each damages figure from the facts, (2) say which interest each measure protects, (3) state the four Winter factors and the eBay factors, (4) apply each choice-of-law approach to the same facts and compare, and (5) run the sister-state judgment checklist?

**Common errors.** Using the price actually paid for cover instead of a reasonable cover price; awarding both reliance and expectation; judging foreseeability at breach rather than at contracting; treating a possibility of harm as enough for a preliminary injunction; granting a nationwide injunction beyond complete relief; using the current account balance instead of the lowest intermediate balance; counting a defendant's wealth as a basis for punitive damages; using the place of the accident as the source of interest in a loss-allocation rule; refusing a sister-state judgment on public-policy grounds.

@exam self
prompt: Agent, domiciled in State B, defrauded Victim, domiciled in State A, of \$200,000, and used the money to buy shares now worth \$260,000. Agent is nearly insolvent. Victim sues in State A, which follows the Second Restatement, and obtains a judgment for the fraud. Write a memo section addressing: (1) the best remedy, (2) whether a federal court could preliminarily freeze the shares, and (3) whether State C, where Agent has other assets, must enforce the State A judgment.
solution: (1) Victim should seek a constructive trust over the shares, because fraud supports unjust enrichment, the money was traced directly into the shares, and a constructive trust gives the shares and their appreciation (\$260,000, which is \$60,000 more than the \$200,000 lien or damages measure) with priority over Agent's unsecured creditors; an equitable lien (\$200,000 plus interest) and damages are alternatives, but damages would leave Victim an unsecured creditor of an insolvent defendant. (2) Under Grupo Mexicano, a federal court may not freeze a defendant's assets before judgment to secure a damages claim on a legal cause of action, but Victim asserts an equitable interest in specific, traced assets, so a court may preserve those shares under the Winter four-factor test (likely success, likely irreparable harm because the shares can be sold and dissipated, balance of equities, public interest) with Rule 65(c) security. (3) If State A had personal and subject-matter jurisdiction, and Agent appeared or was properly subject to jurisdiction, the judgment is final and on the merits, and State C must give it full faith and credit: it cannot relitigate the merits or refuse it on public-policy grounds, though State C's law governs enforcement methods. If Agent defaulted without contacts with State A, Agent may attack jurisdiction collaterally.
rubric: Recommends a constructive trust over the shares, compares \$260,000 with \$200,000 and explains priority.
rubric: Distinguishes the Grupo Mexicano limit on freezing assets for a legal claim from preservation of specific assets in which the plaintiff claims an equitable interest, and applies Winter.
rubric: States the full-faith-and-credit rule for judgments, the no-public-policy-exception rule, and the jurisdictional defense for a defaulting defendant.
