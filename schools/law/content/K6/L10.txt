@article
An **opinion** tells what you think or feel. An **argument** is an opinion with good reasons that try to convince a reader.

### Fact or opinion?

A **fact** can be checked and proven true. An **opinion** is what someone thinks. It cannot be proven.

- Fact: "A week has seven days."
- Opinion: "Summer is the best season."

Opinion words: *best, worst, should, better, favorite, I think.*

### Parts of an opinion piece

1. **State your opinion.** Say what you think clearly. "Our class should have a garden."
2. **Give reasons.** A reason tells why. "A garden would teach us about plants."
3. **Support each reason** with a fact or example. "We could grow beans and watch them sprout."
4. **Conclude.** Say your opinion again and ask the reader to agree.

### Linking words

Use *because, for example, also, another reason, for these reasons.*

### Think about the other side

A strong writer thinks about what someone who disagrees might say. "Some people say a garden is messy, but we can clean up each Friday."

### Be polite

Argue about the idea, not about the person. Use kind words, and stick to facts.

### Example

"Our school should have more books in the library. First, books help us learn new things. For example, a book about space teaches us about planets. Second, reading is fun. Therefore, our school should buy more books."

@example
title: Find the reasons
problem: Read: "We should have recess every day. Recess gives us exercise. It also helps us pay attention in class." What is the opinion and what are the reasons?
step: Find the sentence that tells what the writer thinks: "We should have recess every day." That is the opinion.
step: Find the sentences that tell why: "Recess gives us exercise," and "It also helps us pay attention."
step: There are two reasons. Each one supports the opinion.

@mistakes
- Using a fact like "Dogs have four legs" as if it were an opinion.
- Giving only an opinion and no reasons.
- Making reasons that do not support the opinion.

@practice self
prompt: Write an opinion about what your family should do this weekend. Give two reasons and one example. End by saying your opinion again. Read it to your grown-up.
solution: "Our family should go to the park on Saturday. First, we can get exercise. Second, we can have a picnic. For example, we could bring sandwiches. For these reasons, we should go to the park."
rubric: The opinion is stated clearly.
rubric: There are two reasons that support it.
rubric: There is an example and a closing sentence.
hint: Start with "I think we should..."
