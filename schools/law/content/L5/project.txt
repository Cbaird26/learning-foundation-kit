@review
### Week by week

1. **Punishment and legality.** The purposes are retribution, general and specific deterrence, incapacitation and rehabilitation. Legality means no federal common-law crimes, void-for-vagueness, ex post facto (legislatures), *Bouie* (courts) and lenity.
2. **Act and omission.** A voluntary act (MPC § 2.01); omission only with a legal duty (statute, status, contract, assumed care, creation of peril); possession needs knowing receipt; *Robinson* bars status crimes.
3. **Mental states.** Specific versus general intent; MPC levels (purposely, knowingly, recklessly, negligently); element analysis, the recklessness default and the presumption of mens rea (*Morissette*, *Staples*, *Elonis*, *Counterman*).
4. **Mistake.** Fact: honest for specific intent, reasonable for general intent, none for strict liability; MPC § 2.04. Law: no defense, except knowledge-of-law elements (*Cheek*, *Rehaif*, *Ruan*), reliance on official statements, *Lambert*, and collateral mistakes.
5. **Causation.** But-for plus proximate cause; dependent versus independent intervening causes; *Burrage*; MPC § 2.03.
6. **Murder.** Four forms of malice; degrees and premeditation; MPC § 210.2.
7. **Manslaughter and felony murder.** Heat of passion and the MPC's extreme emotional disturbance; negligence manslaughter; felony-murder limits.
8. **Property crimes.** Larceny, embezzlement, false pretenses, robbery, burglary (intent at entry).
9. **Inchoate crimes.** Attempt (substantial step; impossibility), conspiracy (agreement, overt act, *Pinkerton*), solicitation.
10. **Accomplices.** Aid plus intent, *Rosemond*, natural and probable consequences, withdrawal, accessory after the fact.
11. **Justification.** Self-defense (reasonable belief, imminence, proportionality, aggressor, retreat), necessity.
12. **Excuse.** Duress (not for intentional murder), insanity (*M'Naghten*, MPC, federal test).

### Common errors

Treating recklessness as negligence; saying "intent" when the statute requires purpose; forgetting that causation is an element; calling a pickpocket a robber; missing the time of intent in burglary; applying duress to intentional murder; allowing deadly force against nondeadly force; writing a rule without applying it to the facts.

@exam self
prompt: Zoe, who owns a restaurant, asks Yan to burn it down for the insurance money and promises him \$5,000. Yan agrees, buys a gas can and drives to the restaurant at night, where he opens the unlocked back door and sets the fire. A firefighter responding to the blaze dies when a wall collapses. Analyze, in IRAC form, the liability of Zoe and Yan for conspiracy, arson and the firefighter's death. Assume a common-law jurisdiction whose arson statute covers burning any building, including one's own, to defraud an insurer, with a statute that makes a killing in the course of arson first-degree murder, and an overt act requirement for conspiracy.
hint: Take each defendant and each offense in turn.
hint: Address causation for the firefighter's death and who can be liable for it.
solution: Conspiracy: Zoe and Yan agreed to commit arson, each intended it, and Yan's purchase of the gas can and the drive are overt acts, so both are guilty (Zoe's request is also solicitation, which merges into the arson once Yan commits it). Arson: Yan is the principal who set the fire. Zoe is an accomplice because she solicited and encouraged it with intent that it occur, so she is liable as a principal. Death of the firefighter: arson is an enumerated felony, and a death caused during it is felony murder. Causation: but for the fire there would be no collapse, and a firefighter's response and the risk of collapse are foreseeable, so the death is proximately caused, and it is a killing by a felon in the course of the felony (agency satisfied because the fire was set by Yan). Yan is liable for first-degree felony murder. Zoe, as an accomplice to the arson and a co-felon, is liable too, and under the *Pinkerton* rule she would also be liable for foreseeable crimes. Conclusion: both are guilty of conspiracy and arson and of first-degree felony murder of the firefighter, subject to any limit the jurisdiction places on accomplices.
rubric: Finds conspiracy for both, with the overt act, and notes accomplice liability for Zoe on the arson.
rubric: Applies the felony-murder rule to the firefighter's death, including that the fire was the cause and a rescuer's death is foreseeable.
rubric: Reaches a conclusion for each defendant on each offense and notes the intent to kill is not required.
