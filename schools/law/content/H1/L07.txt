@article
An **exponent** counts repeated multiplication: $a^n$ means $n$ factors of $a$ multiplied together. In $2^5 = 2\cdot 2\cdot 2\cdot 2\cdot 2 = 32$, the $2$ is the **base** and the $5$ is the **exponent**.

### Start with a pattern

Write the powers of $2$ going down: $2^4 = 16$, $2^3 = 8$, $2^2 = 4$, $2^1 = 2$. Each step divides by $2$. Keep the pattern going: $2^0 = 1$, then $2^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}$, then $2^{-2} = \frac{1}{4}$. The pattern forces the definitions

$$a^0 = 1 \;\; (a \neq 0), \qquad a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}.$$

A negative exponent does **not** make the number negative. It means "take the reciprocal".

### The laws of exponents

For the same base $a \neq 0$ and whole-number exponents $m, n$:

| Law | Example |
|---|---|
| $a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$ | $x^3 \cdot x^4 = x^7$ |
| $\dfrac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$ | $\dfrac{x^7}{x^2} = x^5$ |
| $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$ | $(x^2)^3 = x^6$ |
| $(ab)^n = a^n b^n$ | $(2x)^3 = 8x^3$ |

Why does the first law work? $x^3 \cdot x^4 = (x x x)(x x x x)$, which is seven factors of $x$. These laws combine **products and quotients** of the **same base**. They say nothing about sums: $x^3 + x^4$ cannot be simplified.

### Roots as exponents

The **square root** $\sqrt{a}$ (for $a \ge 0$) is the non-negative number whose square is $a$, so $\sqrt{49} = 7$. The **$n$th root** $\sqrt[n]{a}$ undoes the $n$th power. Roots are exponents in disguise:

$$\sqrt[n]{a} = a^{1/n}, \qquad a^{m/n} = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m.$$

So $8^{2/3} = (\sqrt[3]{8})^2 = 2^2 = 4$, and $9^{-1/2} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{9}} = \dfrac{1}{3}$. Take the root first; it keeps the numbers small.

### Simplifying radicals

Look for the largest perfect-square factor and pull its root out, using $\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}$ for $a, b \ge 0$:

$$\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \cdot 2} = 6\sqrt{2}.$$

Be careful: $\sqrt{a + b}$ is **not** $\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$. Try $a = 9$, $b = 16$: $\sqrt{25} = 5$, but $3 + 4 = 7$.

To remove a root from a denominator (**rationalizing**), multiply top and bottom by that root:

$$\frac{6}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{6\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}\sqrt{3}} = \frac{6\sqrt{3}}{3} = 2\sqrt{3}.$$

Radicals with the same radicand add like ordinary like terms: $5\sqrt{2} + 3\sqrt{2} = 8\sqrt{2}$.

@example
title: Simplifying with several laws
problem: Simplify $\dfrac{(2x^3)^2 \, x^{-5}}{4x^2}$ and write the answer with a positive exponent.
step: Apply the power law to the bracket: $(2x^3)^2 = 2^2 \cdot x^6 = 4x^6$.
step: Multiply in the numerator by adding exponents: $4x^6 \cdot x^{-5} = 4x^{6 + (-5)} = 4x$.
step: Divide, subtracting exponents: $\dfrac{4x}{4x^2} = x^{1 - 2} = x^{-1}$.
step: A negative exponent means the reciprocal, so the answer is $\dfrac{1}{x}$.
step: Check with $x = 2$: the original is $\dfrac{(16)^2 \cdot 2^{-5}}{16} = \dfrac{256/32}{16} = \dfrac{8}{16} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and $\dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

@example
title: Combining radicals
problem: Simplify $\sqrt{50} + \sqrt{18}$.
step: Pull out the largest perfect square from each radicand: $\sqrt{50} = \sqrt{25 \cdot 2} = 5\sqrt{2}$.
step: In the same way, $\sqrt{18} = \sqrt{9 \cdot 2} = 3\sqrt{2}$.
step: Both terms are multiples of $\sqrt{2}$, so add the coefficients: $5\sqrt{2} + 3\sqrt{2} = 8\sqrt{2}$.
step: Check numerically: $\sqrt{50} + \sqrt{18} \approx 7.071 + 4.243 = 11.314$ and $8\sqrt{2} \approx 11.314$.

@mistakes
- Reading $x^{-2}$ as a negative number or as $-x^2$. It means $\dfrac{1}{x^2}$.
- Applying an exponent to only part of a bracket: $(2x)^3$ is $8x^3$, not $2x^3$.
- Mixing up the laws: $x^2 \cdot x^3 = x^5$ (add exponents), while $(x^2)^3 = x^6$ (multiply exponents).
- Splitting a sum under a root or inside a power: $\sqrt{a + b} \neq \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$.

@practice num
prompt: Evaluate $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{-2}$. Give an exact fraction.
answer: 9/4
check: (2/3)**-2
hint: A negative exponent means take the reciprocal first.
hint: Flip $\frac{2}{3}$ to $\frac{3}{2}$, then use the exponent $2$.
solution: $\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{-2} = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2} = \frac{9}{4}$.

@practice num
prompt: Evaluate $27^{2/3}$.
answer: 9
check: 27**(2/3)
hint: In the exponent $\frac{2}{3}$, the denominator $3$ means a cube root and the numerator $2$ means squaring.
hint: Take the cube root of $27$ first, then square the result.
solution: $27^{2/3} = (\sqrt[3]{27})^2 = 3^2 = 9$.

@practice mc
prompt: Which expression is equal to $(2x)^{-3}$?
choice*: $\dfrac{1}{8x^3}$
choice: $-8x^3$
choice: $\dfrac{1}{2x^3}$
choice: $-\dfrac{1}{8x^3}$
hint: The exponent $-3$ applies to the whole bracket, including the $2$.
hint: A negative exponent means reciprocal: $(2x)^{-3} = \frac{1}{(2x)^3}$. Now cube both the $2$ and the $x$.
solution: $(2x)^{-3} = \frac{1}{(2x)^3} = \frac{1}{8x^3}$. The negative exponent gives a reciprocal, not a negative number, and the cube applies to the $2$ as well as to $x$.

@practice num
prompt: Write $\sqrt{75} - \sqrt{12}$ in the form $a\sqrt{3}$. What is the whole number $a$?
answer: 3
check: (sqrt(75) - sqrt(12))/sqrt(3)
hint: Pull out perfect squares: $75 = 25 \cdot 3$ and $12 = 4 \cdot 3$.
hint: That gives $5\sqrt{3}$ and $2\sqrt{3}$. Now combine the like radicals.
solution: $\sqrt{75} = 5\sqrt{3}$ and $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$, so the difference is $3\sqrt{3}$ and $a = 3$. Check: $8.660 - 3.464 = 5.196 = 3(1.732)$.

@practice expr
prompt: Simplify $\dfrac{x^{-2}y^{3}}{x^{-5}y}$ and write the answer with positive exponents.
vars: x, y
answer: x^3 y^2
check: lambda x, y: x**-2*y**3/(x**-5*y)
hint: Subtract the exponents of each base separately: top minus bottom.
hint: For $x$ you need $-2 - (-5)$. For $y$ you need $3 - 1$.
solution: $x^{-2-(-5)} y^{3-1} = x^3 y^2$. Check with $x = y = 2$: $\frac{2^{-2}\cdot 8}{2^{-5}\cdot 2} = \frac{2}{1/16} = 32$ and $2^3 \cdot 2^2 = 32$.

@quiz num
prompt: Evaluate $\left(\dfrac{27}{8}\right)^{-2/3}$. Give an exact fraction.
answer: 4/9
check: (27/8)**(-2/3)
hint: Deal with the negative sign first: take the reciprocal of the fraction.
hint: Now you need $\left(\frac{8}{27}\right)^{2/3}$. Take the cube root of the top and the bottom, then square.
solution: $\left(\frac{27}{8}\right)^{-2/3} = \left(\frac{8}{27}\right)^{2/3} = \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{4}{9}$.

@quiz num
prompt: Write $\dfrac{10}{\sqrt{5}}$ in the form $a\sqrt{5}$. What is $a$?
answer: 2
check: (10/sqrt(5))/sqrt(5)
hint: Multiply the top and bottom by $\sqrt{5}$ so that the denominator becomes a whole number.
hint: $\sqrt{5}\cdot\sqrt{5} = 5$, so the fraction becomes $\frac{10\sqrt{5}}{5}$.
solution: $\frac{10}{\sqrt{5}} = \frac{10\sqrt{5}}{5} = 2\sqrt{5}$, so $a = 2$. Check: $10/2.236 = 4.472$ and $2(2.236) = 4.472$.
