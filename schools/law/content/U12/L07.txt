@article
Mediation is negotiation with a neutral helper. A **mediator** has no power to decide the case or impose a result: the parties stay in control, and an agreement binds them only once they sign it. (All clients and files here are hypothetical.)

### The process

Typical stages: the mediator's opening (explaining confidentiality and ground rules); opening statements by the parties or their lawyers, often in a joint session; private **caucuses** in which the mediator shuttles between the sides, tests assumptions ("reality testing"), and explores interests; bargaining; and closure, either an agreement written out and signed before anyone leaves, or a plan for follow-up. Many courts require or encourage mediation, and FRCP 16(a)(5) lists facilitating settlement as a purpose of pretrial conferences. Arbitration differs because the arbitrator decides and the award is typically binding.

### Styles

- **Facilitative:** the mediator asks questions and structures conversation to help the parties find their own solution.
- **Evaluative:** the mediator gives a view of the strengths of each side or a likely outcome, and may propose a figure.
- **Transformative:** the mediator focuses on improving the parties' communication and mutual recognition.

Lawyers choose a mediator whose style fits the dispute, and prepare client and statement accordingly.

### The lawyer's role

Prepare as for a negotiation (interests, BATNA, reservation point, target) and have the person with settlement authority attend. A mediation statement should be short and persuasive, addressed to the mediator and, in a joint session, also to the other side. Under Model Rule 1.4 the lawyer explains the process and each offer, and under Rule 1.2(a) the client decides. Rule 4.1 still applies in mediation: false statements of material fact are prohibited. A lawyer who serves as a mediator is a third-party neutral under Model Rule 2.4: the lawyer must inform unrepresented parties that the lawyer is not representing them, and explain the difference between a neutral and a lawyer when the lawyer knows or reasonably should know a party does not understand.

### Confidentiality

FRE 408 makes inadmissible, to prove or disprove the validity or amount of a disputed claim or to impeach by a prior inconsistent statement or contradiction, an offer or acceptance of consideration to compromise the claim, and conduct or statements made during compromise negotiations about the claim; in a criminal case, the rule has a limited exception for statements made to a public office in the exercise of regulatory, investigative or enforcement authority. It permits use for another purpose, for example proving a witness's bias or negating a claim of undue delay. Many states have adopted the **Uniform Mediation Act**, which creates a privilege allowing parties to refuse to disclose mediation communications, and the mediator to refuse too, subject to exceptions. States vary, so check local law and the mediation agreement. Evidence that is independently discoverable does not become protected merely because it was mentioned in mediation.

### Core terms of a settlement agreement

A settlement agreement is a contract, so it needs offer, acceptance and consideration, and ordinarily should be in a signed writing.

1. **Parties and recitals:** who is bound, including affiliates, and a brief background.
2. **Consideration:** the amount, payment dates, method, and who pays.
3. **Release:** scope (which claims, which released parties, known and unknown claims), and carve-outs for claims not released.
4. **No admission of liability.**
5. **Confidentiality and non-disparagement** binding the parties, if bargained for (with exceptions for law and advisers).
6. **Dismissal:** typically a stipulation of dismissal signed by all parties who have appeared under FRCP 41(a)(1)(A)(ii), stating whether with or without prejudice, and a statement of who bears costs and fees.
7. **Default and enforcement:** acceleration, interest, attorney's fees for breach, and a consent judgment for installment payments.
8. **Boilerplate:** governing law, forum, integration clause, amendments in writing, counterparts, and authority to sign.

Rule 5.6(b) forbids a lawyer to agree to a term restricting the lawyer's right to practice. Rule 1.8(g) requires signed informed consent from each client in an aggregate settlement. Class settlements require court approval under FRCP 23(e), and settlements involving minors or incapacitated persons often need court approval under state law.

### Enforcement in federal court

In *Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America* (1994) the Supreme Court held that a federal court has no ancillary jurisdiction to enforce a settlement agreement after dismissal, unless the dismissal order retains jurisdiction or incorporates the settlement's terms; otherwise enforcement requires an independent basis for jurisdiction, usually a new action in state court. So if you want the federal court to enforce, ask the court to retain jurisdiction or incorporate the terms in the dismissal order.

### Applying the method

For a mediation question, ask: who has authority, what is confidential, what can be admitted, and what must be in the writing to make the deal enforceable?

@mistakes
- Assuming a mediator's evaluation binds the parties.
- Leaving enforcement out of the dismissal order, so a federal court cannot enforce the settlement.
- Treating anything said in mediation as automatically inadmissible; independent evidence stays discoverable and FRE 408 has "another purpose" uses.
- Signing a release with a vague scope or no carve-outs for unknown claims the client wants to keep.
