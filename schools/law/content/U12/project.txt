A Delta Delivery van driven by Hal Pruitt turned right across the lane at Oak Street and knocked her down. She fractured her left wrist. The posted speed limit on Main Street is 25 miles per hour.

At his June 4 deposition, under oath, he said, "I think the light was yellow, but I was not watching it until I heard the brakes. The driver was looking at his phone, I think." Stood about 60 feet away. *Hal Pruitt* (driver): told Officer Dale Reed at the scene, "I never looked." At deposition: "I checked my mirror, I always check." Admitted he was on a delivery route with a 9:00 a.m. deadline and that his phone was in a holder on the dash. *Officer Reed*: arrived 10 minutes later; wrote the report; no skid marks measured. *Dr. Kim* (Delta's reconstruction witness): has a Ph.D. in mechanical engineering and has reconstructed accidents for eleven years; estimates Ana's speed at 18 to 22 miles per hour from a single photograph of her bicycle's position and a damage estimate; has not visited the scene and did not measure road conditions.

If Ana wins, assume a jury award of economic damages (medical \$18,000 plus lost wages) of \$25,200 plus \$30,000 for pain and suffering, reduced by 10% for comparative fault. Ana is paying her lawyer on a contingency of one-third of the gross recovery; for this project ignore the fee and treat the numbers as gross.

### Week by week

- **Week 1, interviewing:** three jobs (facts, goals, relationship); stages (opening, narrative, funnel follow-up, closing); open, closed and leading questions; privilege needs confidentiality; prospective clients under Model Rule 1.18; fee writing under Rule 1.5.
- **Week 2, investigation:** start from the elements; chart known, unknown and sources; preserve evidence; Rule 11(b)(3); work product and *Upjohn*; Rules 4.2, 4.3, 4.4 and 3.4; discovery limits (25 interrogatories, 10 depositions, 7 hours); the December 2025 amendments to Rules 16(b) and 26(f).
- **Week 3, counseling:** the client decides objectives, settlement, plea, jury waiver and testimony (Rule 1.2(a)); consult on means; communicate offers; Rule 1.2(d) limits; expected value $pV - C$.
- **Week 4, relationships:** Rule 1.4(a)(1) to (5), Rules 1.3, 1.5, 1.15 and 1.16; contingent-fee arithmetic; the counseling letter.
- **Week 5, negotiation theory:** distributive and integrative bargaining; positions and interests; BATNA, reservation point, target and ZOPA ($B - S$).
- **Week 6, tactics and ethics:** Rule 4.1 distinguishes facts and law from value estimates and intentions; Rules 1.8(g) and 5.6(b); FRCP 68; the negotiation plan.
- **Week 7, mediation and settlement:** non-binding process; FRE 408 and mediation privilege; settlement terms; dismissal and enforcement (*Kokkonen*).
- **Week 8, case theory:** theory and theme; evidence matrix; burdens; Rules 50 and 56; *Batson*; FRE 103(b).
- **Week 9, openings and direct:** no argument or opinion; FRE 611(c), 602, 612, 701, 803(5), 615 and new FRE 107; Rule 3.3 on false testimony.
- **Week 10, cross:** one fact per question; commit-credit-confront; FRE 608, 609, 613, 801(d)(1); bias is not collateral; FRE 702 as amended in 2023; *Smith v. Arizona*.
- **Week 11, exhibits and objections:** FRE 901, 902, 803(6), 801(d)(2), 1002 and 103; specific, timely objections.
- **Week 12, closing:** inferences from the record; no misstatement, opinion, golden rule or comment on silence (*Griffin*); damages arithmetic.

### Common errors

- Treating a position as an interest and never asking why.
- Deciding for the client whether to accept an offer, or failing to communicate it.
- Saying a false fact in negotiation and calling it "puffery."
- Leading on direct and asking "why" on cross.
- Offering an exhibit without authentication, or an objection without a specific ground.
- Arguing facts that were never admitted into evidence.

@exam self
prompt: (Hypothetical cumulative problem.) Your client Priya's landlord kept her \$1,800 security deposit, and she asks you about her options after you have learned the facts. The landlord's lawyer has offered \$1,200 and a mutual release, open for 7 days, and says "my client will never pay more than \$1,200." You estimate a 70% chance of recovering the full deposit plus a statutory penalty of \$1,800 in addition, but \$2,500 in costs through trial (assume the deposit recovery is the amount of the deposit). (a) Compute the expected net value of trial. (b) Write five sentences to Priya that explain her options and risks and the decision that is hers. (c) State whether the landlord's lawyer's statement violates Model Rule 4.1 and why. (d) Name two non-monetary terms you would seek and one term in the settlement you could not agree to as a lawyer.
hint: For the expected value, the recovery if she wins is the deposit plus the penalty.
hint: Classify the lawyer's statement as fact, law, value or intention, and recall Model Rule 5.6(b).
solution: (a) If Priya wins she recovers 1,800 + 1,800 = 3,600; expected value is 0.70 × 3,600 - 2,500 = 20, so trial is expected to net about \$20, far less than \$1,200 on these numbers. (b) "Priya, you can accept the \$1,200, counter, or sue. The offer is open for seven days. If we sue and win, we expect about \$3,600, but we estimate a 70% chance and \$2,500 in costs, so on average trial nets almost nothing, and a loss would leave you with nothing and costs. A counteroffer is possible, but the landlord may withdraw. I recommend countering at a higher figure and settling near the offer if the landlord refuses; the decision is yours." (c) The statement concerns the landlord's intention about an acceptable settlement, which the comment to Rule 4.1 ordinarily treats as not a statement of material fact, so ordinarily not a violation (some states are stricter). (d) Non-monetary terms: payment within 10 days, a neutral reference or no-reporting to tenant-screening agencies, a written statement that no deductions will be claimed. A lawyer could not agree to a term restricting the lawyer's own right to practice, such as a promise not to represent other tenants against the landlord (Rule 5.6(b)).
rubric: Computes the expected value correctly using the deposit plus the penalty (\$3,600) and notes it is an estimate.
rubric: Explains options, deadline and risks in plain language and leaves the decision to the client.
rubric: Correctly classifies the lawyer's statement under Rule 4.1 and the comment, noting state variation.
rubric: Names appropriate non-monetary terms and identifies Rule 5.6(b).
