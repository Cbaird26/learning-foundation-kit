### The order of a jury trial

Ordinarily: jury selection; opening statements (plaintiff or prosecution first; the defense may open then or reserve until its case); plaintiff's case (direct, cross, redirect, recross); defense case; rebuttal; jury instructions and closings (the timing of instructions varies); deliberation and verdict.

### Opening statement

The opening is **not evidence**. It tells the jury what the evidence will show. In many courts it should not be argument: avoid arguing the law, drawing heavy inferences or telling jurors what to conclude; the court has discretion over how much argument is allowed. Model Rule 3.4(e) prohibits alluding to any matter the lawyer does not reasonably believe is relevant or will be supported by admissible evidence, asserting personal knowledge of facts in issue except when testifying, and stating a personal opinion about the justness of a cause, the credibility of a witness, or the guilt or innocence of an accused. Do not promise testimony you cannot deliver, because jurors remember.

A strong opening:

1. **Starts with the theme** and a short story hook.
2. **Tells the story in order** with people, places and dates, using plain words.
3. **Previews the key witnesses and exhibits** and what each will say.
4. **Addresses bad facts** briefly and in your own framing.
5. **Names the burden** ("We will prove it more likely than not") and what you ask the jury to do.

Illustrative aids such as a timeline can help, but under new FRE 107 (effective December 1, 2024) the court may allow an illustrative aid to help the trier of fact understand evidence or argument if its utility in assisting comprehension is not substantially outweighed by the danger of unfair prejudice, confusing the issues, misleading the jury, undue delay or wasting time; it is not evidence and ordinarily does not go to the jury room, and it must be entered into the record.

### Direct examination

On direct, you elicit testimony from your own witness.

- **No leading questions** except as necessary to develop the witness's testimony (FRE 611(c)). The advisory committee's note and case law recognize preliminary matters and a young or forgetful witness as common examples, and the rule itself allows leading a hostile witness, an adverse party, or a witness identified with an adverse party. Courts allow leading for undisputed background in practice, in their discretion.
- **Personal knowledge** (FRE 602): the witness may testify only to what the witness perceived. **Lay opinion** (FRE 701) must be rationally based on perception, helpful, and not based on specialized knowledge within FRE 702.
- **Structure:** introduce the witness (humanize), set the scene, tell the story in chapters with headlines and transitions ("Let's turn to what happened on March 12"), and let the witness describe details. Ask open questions using "who, what, where, when, how, describe, explain."
- **Looping and pacing:** repeat key words from the answer in your next question to emphasize them.
- **Front the bad facts** (prior conviction, inconsistency) on direct so the jury hears them first from you.
- **Refreshing recollection (FRE 612):** if a witness forgets, you may show a writing or object to refresh memory; the writing is not itself evidence. If a witness uses a writing to refresh while testifying, an adverse party is entitled to have it produced, to inspect it, to cross-examine on it and to introduce the portions that relate to the testimony. **Recorded recollection (FRE 803(5))** applies if the witness still cannot testify fully and accurately; the record is read into evidence but is received as an exhibit only if offered by an adverse party.
- **Narrative answers** can be limited by the court under FRE 611(a).
- **Redirect** is limited to matters covered on cross, to repair damage.

### Preparing witnesses ethically

You may meet with a witness, explain the process, review the witness's prior statements and practice testimony. You may not counsel or assist a witness to testify falsely (Model Rule 3.4(b)), and you may not offer an inducement prohibited by law (a reasonable payment of expenses and compensation for lost time is allowed). If a lawyer comes to know that a witness called by the lawyer has offered material false evidence, Rule 3.3(a)(3) requires reasonable remedial measures, including disclosure to the tribunal if necessary. The lawyer may refuse to offer evidence, other than the testimony of a defendant in a criminal matter, that the lawyer reasonably believes is false (Rule 3.3(a)(3)). Under FRE 615, on a party's request the court must exclude witnesses from the courtroom so they cannot hear other testimony, except a party who is a natural person, one officer or employee designated as an entity party's representative, a person whose presence is essential to presenting the party's claim or defense, and a person authorized by statute.

@example
title: Turning a leading question into a direct question
problem: (Hypothetical.) A pedestrian saw a van enter an intersection. Plaintiff's counsel asks, "The light was red, wasn't it?" Defense objects: leading. Rewrite the questions so the witness gives the facts.
step: Identify the problem: the question suggests the answer, and the witness is friendly to the plaintiff, so FRE 611(c) bars it unless needed to develop testimony.
step: Ask for the scene: "Where were you standing at 8:15 that morning?" "What were you doing?"
step: Elicit the observation: "What, if anything, did you notice about the traffic signal facing the van?" "What color was it?"
step: Elicit the basis and details: "How far were you from the signal?" "What did you see the van do next?" Each answer builds personal knowledge for FRE 602.
@mistakes
- Arguing in the opening or stating personal opinions about credibility.
- Promising evidence you cannot get admitted.
- Using leading questions on a friendly witness on contested points.
- Treating a writing used to refresh recollection as automatically admissible, or forgetting that the adverse party can inspect it.
