An exhibit helps only if it gets into evidence, and a witness's answer helps only if it survives objection. (All clients and files here are hypothetical.)

### Authentication and identification

Under FRE 901(a), to authenticate an item the proponent must produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what the proponent claims it is. FRE 901(b) gives examples, including testimony of a witness with knowledge (901(b)(1)), non-expert comparison, **distinctive characteristics** such as contents, substance and internal patterns (901(b)(4)), and evidence about a process or system (901(b)(9)). **Self-authenticating** items (FRE 902) need no extrinsic proof: certified domestic public documents under seal, certified copies of public records, newspapers, certified domestic business records (902(11)), and electronic records and data copied from a device with a certification (902(13) and (14)). Authentication is a low threshold; the jury decides what weight to give.

### A foundation script for a document

1. Have the exhibit marked for identification (Exhibit 5).
2. Show it to opposing counsel and ask permission to approach the witness.
3. Hand it to the witness: "Do you recognize Exhibit 5?" "What is it?"
4. **Authenticate:** "How do you recognize it?" (wrote it, received it, signature, familiar handwriting, distinctive content).
5. **Relevance and hearsay route** (business record, party's statement, not offered for truth).
6. Offer: "Your Honor, we offer Exhibit 5 into evidence."
7. Ruling, then ask permission to **publish** it to the jury.

### Hearsay routes for a document

- **Business records (FRE 803(6)):** a record of an act, event or condition made at or near the time by, or from information transmitted by, someone with knowledge; kept in the course of a regularly conducted activity of a business; making the record was a regular practice of that activity; shown by the testimony of the custodian or another qualified witness, or by a certification under FRE 902(11) or (12); and the opponent does not show that the source or the method or circumstances of preparation indicate lack of trustworthiness. A record prepared for litigation ordinarily fails.
- **Opposing party's statement (FRE 801(d)(2)):** not hearsay when offered against the party, including statements by an agent or employee within the scope of that relationship (801(d)(2)(D)). The 2024 amendment adds that if a party's claim, defense or potential liability is directly derived from a declarant or the declarant's principal, a statement that would be admissible against the declarant or the principal under the rule is also admissible against the party.
- **Statement against interest (FRE 804(b)(3)):** the declarant must be unavailable; the 2024 amendment requires corroborating circumstances that clearly indicate trustworthiness for a statement against interest offered in a criminal case against the accused as well as for one offered to exculpate the accused.
- **Not for the truth:** notice, effect on the listener, state of mind.

### Best evidence rule

FRE 1002: to prove a writing's content, the original is required unless the rules or a statute say otherwise. Duplicates are admissible unless a genuine question about authenticity is raised (FRE 1003). Other evidence of content is allowed if the original is lost or destroyed without bad faith, cannot be obtained, or is not closely related to a controlling issue (FRE 1004). **Summaries** of voluminous records are allowed under FRE 1006 if the underlying records are made available.

### Illustrative aids: new FRE 107

Effective December 1, 2024, FRE 107 permits the court to allow a party to present an illustrative aid to help the trier of fact understand evidence or argument if the aid's utility in assisting comprehension is not substantially outweighed by the danger of unfair prejudice, confusing the issues, misleading the jury, undue delay, or wasting time. An illustrative aid is not evidence, may not go to the jury room unless all parties consent or the court for good cause orders, and must be entered into the record. If an aid summarizes voluminous records as evidence, it is a FRE 1006 summary, not a Rule 107 aid.

### Objections (FRE 103)

To preserve error, make a **timely objection or motion to strike** that states the **specific ground**, unless it is apparent from the context (FRE 103(a)(1)). If a ruling excludes evidence, the proponent makes an **offer of proof** unless the substance is apparent (103(a)(2)). Common objections and grounds:

| Objection | Basis |
|---|---|
| Hearsay | FRE 801 and 802 |
| Leading | FRE 611(c) |
| Lack of foundation or personal knowledge | FRE 602 and 901 |
| Relevance | FRE 401 and 402 |
| Unfair prejudice, confusion, cumulative | FRE 403 |
| Speculation or improper lay opinion | FRE 602 and 701 |
| Beyond the scope of direct | FRE 611(b) |
| Asked and answered, argumentative, compound, narrative | Court's control under FRE 611(a) |
| Best evidence | FRE 1002 |

Respond by naming the exception or non-hearsay purpose, offering to lay more foundation, or rephrasing. Object sparingly: too many objections irritate the jury.

### Applying the method

For each exhibit, prepare a one-page foundation card: the exhibit, its sponsoring witness, the questions, the hearsay route, and the likely objection with your response.
@example
title: Admitting a business record
problem: (Hypothetical.) Plaintiff offers a hospital bill through the hospital's billing manager. Defense objects: hearsay. Show the foundation.
step: Identify: "What is Exhibit 7?" "A statement of charges for Ana Ruiz's treatment on March 12."
step: Show regular practice: "Who prepares these entries?" "Billing staff, from the treatment record, at the time of service." "Is it the hospital's regular practice to make these records?" "Yes, in the course of our regular business."
step: Show custody: "Do you have access to these records in your job?" "Yes, I am the custodian."
step: Offer and respond: offer Exhibit 7 under FRE 803(6) and explain that the foundation shows each element; offer a FRE 902(11) certification as an alternative.
@mistakes
- Offering an exhibit without having it marked, shown to opposing counsel, and authenticated.
- Objecting generally ("Objection!") instead of stating the specific ground.
- Giving the wrong hearsay route for a litigation-prepared document.
- Treating an illustrative aid as evidence, or sending it to the jury room over objection.
