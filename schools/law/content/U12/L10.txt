@article
Direct builds your case, and cross tests the other side's. Good cross is short, controlled and aimed at points you will use in closing. (All clients and files here are hypothetical.)

### Scope and form

- **Scope (FRE 611(b)):** cross should be limited to the subject matter of the direct examination and matters affecting credibility, though the court may allow inquiry into additional matters as if on direct. Some states allow "wide-open" cross on any relevant matter.
- **Leading questions (FRE 611(c)):** ordinarily permitted on cross. If a witness is hostile or identified with the adverse party, you may lead even on direct.
- Under FRE 607, any party, including the party that called the witness, may attack the witness's credibility.

### Rules of thumb

1. **One fact per question.** "You were 40 feet away." "It was dark." "You had been awake for 20 hours." Each question is a short statement with a yes answer.
2. **Know the answer.** Use depositions and statements to pin it down; do not ask what you cannot control.
3. **Never ask why**, and do not repeat the direct or ask the witness to explain a damaging point.
4. **Organize in chapters** and end strongly (primacy and recency).
5. **Be courteous and firm.** Jurors dislike bullying.
6. **Sit down** when you have what you need.

### Impeachment by prior inconsistent statement

The steps are **commit, credit, confront**: (1) commit the witness to the testimony given today; (2) credit the earlier statement: establish when and how it was made, that it was accurate and complete, and that the witness had reason to be truthful (for a deposition, under oath with a court reporter); (3) confront the witness with the inconsistency by reading it exactly.

- FRE 613(a): when examining about a prior statement, you need not show or disclose it to the witness, but must show it or disclose its contents to an adverse party's lawyer on request.
- FRE 613(b), as amended effective December 1, 2024: extrinsic evidence of a prior inconsistent statement may not be admitted until after the witness has been given an opportunity to explain or deny it and the adverse party an opportunity to examine the witness about it, unless the court orders otherwise. Check the current text of the rule.
- A prior inconsistent statement is usually admissible only to impeach. But under **FRE 801(d)(1)(A)** a prior inconsistent statement given under penalty of perjury at a trial, hearing or other proceeding or in a deposition, and offered when the declarant testifies and is subject to cross-examination, is **not hearsay** and is substantive evidence.
- **Collateral matter rule:** extrinsic evidence of an inconsistency is generally not allowed on a collateral matter (one unrelated to the case that matters only to credibility); you may ask but are stuck with the answer. Bias is not collateral.
- **Prior consistent statements** are treated under FRE 801(d)(1)(B): not hearsay when offered to rebut an express or implied charge of recent fabrication or improper influence or motive, or to rehabilitate credibility when attacked on another ground.

### Other impeachment

- **Bias, interest or motive** (financial stake, family tie, a deal): a constitutional and evidentiary basis for cross; in a criminal case, cutting off cross on a witness's bias can violate the Confrontation Clause (*Davis v. Alaska* (1974)). Extrinsic evidence of bias is allowed (*United States v. Abel* (1984)).
- **Character for untruthfulness (FRE 608):** (a) opinion or reputation evidence of untruthful character, and evidence of truthful character only after the character has been attacked; (b) specific instances of conduct probative of truthfulness may be inquired into on cross, but extrinsic evidence is not allowed to prove them.
- **Criminal convictions (FRE 609):** a conviction for a crime punishable by death or imprisonment of more than a year is admissible against a witness who is not a defendant, subject to FRE 403; against a criminal defendant only if the probative value outweighs the prejudicial effect. A conviction for any crime requiring proof of a dishonest act or false statement must be admitted regardless of punishment. If more than 10 years have passed since conviction or release from confinement, whichever is later, the conviction is admissible only if probative value substantially outweighs prejudicial effect and there is notice.
- **Contradiction, capacity and perception:** the witness's ability to see, hear or remember, or other evidence that contradicts the witness.

### Cross-examining experts

Expect to examine the expert's qualifications, assumptions, data and methods. FRE 702, amended effective December 1, 2023, requires the proponent to show the court it is more likely than not that the expert's qualifications, the sufficiency of the basis, the reliability of the method and its reliable application meet the rule, so challenge the basis before trial. In criminal cases, *Smith v. Arizona* (2024) held that when an expert conveys an absent analyst's statements as the basis of the expert's opinion and those statements support the opinion only if true, they are offered for their truth, so the Confrontation Clause applies if they are testimonial.

### Applying the method

For each witness, write the three or four points you need from them, each in a chapter of short leading questions with the source (deposition page or exhibit) noted beside each question.
@example
title: Impeaching with a deposition
problem: (Hypothetical.) At trial, a witness says the van's light was "definitely green." At her deposition, under oath, she said, "I think it was yellow, but I was not watching until I heard the brakes." Show the impeachment.
step: Commit: "Today you told this jury the light was definitely green." "You were certain."
step: Credit: "You gave a deposition on June 4." "You were under oath." "A court reporter took down your words." "You had the chance to read and correct the transcript."
step: Confront: "At page 31, line 4, you were asked about the color of the light. You answered: 'I think it was yellow, but I was not watching until I heard the brakes.'" "You did not say 'definitely green' then."
step: Use: because it was a deposition under oath, FRE 801(d)(1)(A) makes it substantive evidence, not just impeachment. Stop there, and save the argument for closing.
@mistakes
- Asking "why" and letting a hostile witness repeat the harmful direct.
- Skipping the commit and credit steps, which lets the witness explain away the inconsistency.
- Trying to prove an inconsistency on a collateral matter with extrinsic evidence.
- Treating all prior inconsistent statements as substantive evidence; only those meeting FRE 801(d)(1)(A) are.
