@article
Most grievances against lawyers are not about brilliance or the lack of it. They are about silence, missed deadlines and money. The NextGen bar exam lists client relationship and management as a foundational skill, so expect questions on communication duties, fees and client funds. (All clients and files here are hypothetical.)

### The duty to communicate

Model Rule 1.4(a) requires a lawyer to: (1) promptly inform the client of any decision or circumstance requiring informed consent; (2) reasonably consult about the means of reaching the client's objectives; (3) keep the client reasonably informed about the status of the matter; (4) promptly comply with reasonable requests for information; and (5) consult about relevant limits on the lawyer's conduct when the client expects assistance the Rules do not permit. Rule 1.4(b) requires explanation sufficient for informed decisions. Rule 1.3 requires reasonable diligence and promptness, and Rule 1.1 requires competence. A lawyer who does not return calls for weeks, or does not tell the client of a settlement offer, violates Rule 1.4.

When a lawyer makes a material error, for example missing a filing deadline, the lawyer must tell the client promptly. The lawyer's own interest in avoiding liability may create a conflict under Rule 1.7(a)(2), which requires evaluation and possibly informed consent.

### Fees and expenses

Rule 1.5(a) forbids an unreasonable fee. Rule 1.5(b) requires communication of the scope and the basis or rate of the fee and expenses, preferably in writing. Under Rule 1.5(c) a contingent fee agreement must be in a writing signed by the client that states the method (the percentage, including any variation depending on stage), the expenses to be deducted, and **whether expenses are deducted before or after the fee is calculated**. At the end of the matter the lawyer must give a written statement of the outcome and, for a contingent fee, of how the remittance to the client was determined. Rule 1.5(d) bars contingent fees in domestic-relations matters tied to a divorce result or support, and in criminal defense. Rule 1.5(e) allows fee division between lawyers in different firms only under stated conditions, including client agreement in writing.

### Client money

Rule 1.15 requires keeping client funds and property separate from the lawyer's own, in a trust account. A settlement check belongs in the client trust account. Using it for firm expenses, even briefly, is conversion. Commingling a lawyer's own money in the account is also forbidden, except a small amount to pay bank charges. The lawyer must promptly deliver funds the client is entitled to, and hold in trust any funds in which both lawyer and client claim an interest until the dispute is resolved.

### Ending the relationship

A client may discharge a lawyer at any time. A lawyer must withdraw when continuing would violate the Rules, when the lawyer's condition materially impairs the representation, or when discharged (Rule 1.16(a)). Permissive withdrawal is allowed on grounds such as a client's failure to fulfil an obligation after warning (Rule 1.16(b)). When ordered by a tribunal, a lawyer must continue (Rule 1.16(c)). On termination, protect the client's interests: give notice, allow time to find other counsel, return papers and property, and refund any unearned fee (Rule 1.16(d)).

### The counseling letter

A counseling letter puts advice in a durable, understandable form. A strong one:

1. Opens with the question and a short answer or recommendation.
2. States the facts you relied on, so the client can correct them.
3. Lays out each option with its benefits, costs and risks, in plain language.
4. States the decision the client must make and by when, including any deadline.
5. Offers a recommendation (if appropriate), labeled as such, and the reasons.
6. Lists next steps, and invites questions.

Mark it "Privileged and Confidential: Attorney-Client Communication," send it only to the client at a secure address (Rule 1.6(c) requires reasonable efforts to prevent unauthorized disclosure), and avoid jargon. Use headings, short paragraphs and, where numbers matter, a small table.

### Applying the method

For any relationship problem: identify the duty (communicate, diligence, fees, funds, termination), recall the specific Rule, apply it to what the lawyer did or failed to do, and state what the lawyer should do now.

title: Net recovery under a contingent fee
problem: (Hypothetical.) A client's case settles for \$90,000. The signed agreement gives the lawyer one-third of the gross settlement and says expenses of \$6,000 are deducted from the client's share after the fee is computed. What does the client receive?
step: Compute the fee on the gross amount: $90{,}000 \div 3 = 30{,}000$.
step: Subtract the fee from the settlement: $90{,}000 - 30{,}000 = 60{,}000$.
step: Subtract the expenses from the client's share: $60{,}000 - 6{,}000 = 54{,}000$.
step: The client receives \$54,000, and Rule 1.5(c) requires a written closing statement showing this computation.
@mistakes
- Treating the order of fee and expense deductions as unimportant; it changes the client's net, so the writing must say which applies.
- Depositing a client's settlement check in the firm operating account "temporarily."
- Concealing a missed deadline instead of telling the client promptly.
- Writing a counseling letter full of legal terms the client cannot use to decide.

solution: Privileged and Confidential. Dear Marcus: You asked whether to sign the release.
