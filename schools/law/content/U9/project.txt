Harbor's inventory is worth \$150,000, Summit is owed \$200,000, Velo is owed \$60,000 (of which the e-bikes remaining in stock are worth \$45,000), and the judgment is for \$25,000.

@review
### Weeks 1-6: Article 2
- **Scope and formation (wk 1):** Article 2 covers sales of goods (predominant purpose for mixed contracts). Merchant status matters for firm offers (§ 2-205), merchant's confirmation (§ 2-201(2)) and other rules. Statute of frauds: \$500 or more, signed writing with a quantity, plus exceptions.
- **Terms (wk 2):** § 2-207: definite acceptance forms a contract unless expressly conditional; additional terms between merchants join unless the offer limits, they materially alter, or objection; different terms usually knock out; § 2-207(3) contract by conduct. Parol evidence (§ 2-202) and the hierarchy express terms, performance, dealing, usage.
- **Warranties (wk 3):** express (§ 2-313), merchantability (§ 2-314), fitness (§ 2-315), disclaimers (§ 2-316), remedy limits (§ 2-719), notice (§ 2-607(3)(a)), four-year limit (§ 2-725).
- **Risk of loss (wk 4):** shipment versus destination contracts, bailees, merchant versus non-merchant pickup, breach rules (§ 2-510).
- **Rejection and cure (wk 5):** perfect tender, installments, cure (§ 2-508), acceptance, revocation.
- **Remedies (wk 6):** cover, market, resale, lost profits, price action; incidental and consequential damages.

### Weeks 7-12: Article 9 and bankruptcy
- **Attachment (wk 7):** value, rights in collateral, authenticated agreement with description (or possession or control). Classify goods by use.
- **Perfection (wk 8):** filing in the debtor's location; control for deposit accounts; possession for money; title notation for vehicles; five-year life.
- **Priority (wk 9):** first to file or perfect; perfected beats unperfected; lien creditor and buyer rules.
- **PMSI and BIOC (wk 10):** 20 days for non-inventory; notice for inventory; buyer in ordinary course takes free.
- **Default (wk 11):** peaceful repossession, commercial reasonableness, notice, surplus and deficiency, redemption, consumer protections.
- **Bankruptcy (wk 12):** stay, strong-arm, preferences, § 506(a), cash collateral, cramdown and the 910-day rule.

### Self-check
- Can you say, for any Article 2 question, whether the parties are merchants and why it matters?
- Can you classify collateral and name its perfection method without notes?
- Can you rank four creditors and name the rule for each step?

### Common errors
- Confusing attachment with perfection; filing where the goods are rather than where the debtor is located; treating knowledge as decisive in priority; giving inventory PMSIs the 20-day grace period; ignoring that bankruptcy can avoid unperfected or late-perfected interests.

Before Vendor distributes the proceeds, Summit sends Vendor an authenticated demand for proceeds. answer: 84000
check: 120000 - (70000 - 4000 - 30000)
unit: dollars
hint: Section 9-615(a) applies proceeds to expenses, then to the debt of the secured party that made the disposition, then to subordinate secured parties that made a timely authenticated demand.
hint: Subtract what Summit receives from its \$120,000 claim.
solution: Proceeds of \$70,000 less \$4,000 expenses leaves \$66,000. @exam self
prompt: Buyer, a merchant, faxes a purchase order for 1,000 gadgets at \$10 each, delivery by June 1, with a one-year warranty. Seller, a merchant, faxes an acknowledgment of the same quantity, price and date that states, "Our acceptance is expressly conditional on your assent to our terms," which disclaim all warranties. Buyer does not respond. Seller delivers on May 20 and Buyer accepts and pays. The gadgets fail; Buyer sues, and Seller later repudiates a second contract for 1,000 gadgets at \$10 each, forcing Buyer to cover at \$12 per gadget for 1,000 gadgets with \$300 in incidental costs. In IRAC form, (a) decide which terms govern the first contract, and (b) compute Buyer's cover damages on the second.
hint: Part (a) is § 2-207(3); part (b) is the § 2-712 formula.
solution: (a) Issue: terms after conflicting forms. Rule: a response expressly conditional on assent is a counteroffer (§ 2-207(1)); conduct recognizing a contract creates one on the terms on which the writings agree plus UCC gap-fillers (§ 2-207(3)). Application: Seller's acknowledgment is expressly conditional, Buyer's silence is not assent, but performance forms a contract. The writings agree on goods, quantity, price and date; the conflicting warranty terms drop out and the implied warranty of merchantability applies. Conclusion: the disclaimer does not bind Buyer; Buyer may sue on the implied warranty (after timely notice under § 2-607(3)(a)). (b) Cover price is 1,000 times \$12, or \$12,000; the contract price is \$10,000; the difference is \$2,000, plus \$300 incidental damages, for \$2,300.
rubric: Treats the acknowledgment as a counteroffer and applies § 2-207(3).
rubric: Concludes the disclaimer is out and gap-fillers, including merchantability, apply.
rubric: Computes cover damages of \$2,300 with the formula stated.
