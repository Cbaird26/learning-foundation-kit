@article
Priority answers the question: **when two or more parties claim the same collateral and it is not worth enough to pay them all, who is paid first?** It matters most when the debtor is insolvent. Perfection is what makes a security interest strong against other claimants, so priority rules mostly compare dates of filing, perfection and attachment.

### Conflicting security interests: § 9-322(a)
For security interests in the **same collateral**:
1. **First to file or perfect.** Conflicting **perfected** security interests rank in the order of **filing or perfection, whichever is earlier**, provided there is **no period afterward** when the interest is neither filed nor perfected (§ 9-322(a)(1)). A financing statement may be filed **before** the security interest attaches, and its filing date counts: a secured party who files first and lends later still has the earlier priority date.
2. **Perfected beats unperfected** (§ 9-322(a)(2)).
3. **Among unperfected interests**, the **first to attach** wins (§ 9-322(a)(3)).

The Code does not care which secured party lent first or knew of the other. (**Knowledge** is irrelevant to this rule; the first-to-file rule rewards the party that gave public notice.) **Future advances** secured by the security agreement generally share the priority of the original filing (§ 9-323 limits this against a lien creditor to advances made within 45 days after the lien arises, or under a commitment), and an **after-acquired** clause works the same way, which is why a lender with an all-assets filing outranks later lenders on new inventory (subject to the purchase-money exception in next week's lesson). A secured party may agree to **subordinate** its interest to another's by contract (§ 9-339).

**Proceeds** generally take the priority of the original collateral (§ 9-322(b)(1)).

### Security interest versus lien creditor: § 9-317(a)(2)
A **lien creditor** is a creditor that has acquired a lien on the property by attachment, levy or the like (including an assignee for the benefit of creditors, a receiver, and a **trustee in bankruptcy** from the date of the petition). An **unperfected** security interest is **subordinate** to the rights of a person that becomes a lien creditor **before the security interest is perfected**. So:
- If the lien arises **before** perfection, the **lien creditor wins**, even if the security interest attached earlier and even if the secured party perfects the next day.
- If the secured party **perfects first**, the security interest has priority over a later lien creditor.
A purchase-money security interest has a 20-day grace period for this rule (§ 9-317(e)); see next lesson. In bankruptcy, the trustee has the rights of a hypothetical lien creditor on the petition date (11 U.S.C. § 544(a)(1)), so an unperfected security interest is avoidable by the trustee. That link is why perfection matters so much.

### Security interest versus a buyer
- **Unperfected security interest (§ 9-317(b)).** A **buyer** (other than a secured party) of goods, instruments, documents or chattel paper takes **free** of an unperfected security interest if the buyer gives **value** and **receives delivery** of the collateral **without knowledge** of the security interest and **before it is perfected**.
- **Perfected security interest (§ 9-315(a)(1)).** A security interest **continues** in collateral **notwithstanding its sale or other disposition**, unless the secured party **authorized** the disposition **free of** the security interest. A buyer who is not a buyer in the ordinary course (next lesson) therefore takes **subject to** a perfected security interest, and the secured party also has an interest in the identifiable **proceeds** (§ 9-315(a)(2)).

### Possessory liens: § 9-333
A person who in the ordinary course of business **furnishes services or materials with respect to goods** and keeps **possession** of the goods has a **possessory lien** by statute or common law (a mechanic's or repairer's lien). Under § 9-333(b) that lien has priority over a security interest in the goods **unless the lien is created by a statute that expressly provides otherwise**, even if the security interest was perfected earlier. The lien ends if the lienholder loses possession.

### Other priority points to remember
- **Federal tax liens** and certain statutory liens are governed by their own statutes.
- A **buyer in ordinary course** and a **purchase-money security interest** are the two big exceptions to first-to-file; they are the subject of next week's lesson.
- Priority between a security interest and **fixtures** or **accessions** has special rules not studied here.

### How to rank claims
List every claimant and, for each, the date of **attachment**, **filing**, and **perfection**. For secured parties, assign the priority date (the earlier of filing or perfection, if continuous). Lien creditors rank by the date the lien arose. Apply § 9-322 among secured parties, § 9-317(a)(2) against lien creditors, and then look for the special rules (PMSI, buyer in ordinary course, possessory lien).
@example
title: First to file wins, even if it lent second
problem: Debtor Inc. owns equipment. Lender B lends \$100,000 on January 10 under a signed security agreement covering the equipment, and files a financing statement on February 1. Lender A files a financing statement on January 5 under a signed authorization, lends \$100,000 on January 20 under a security agreement covering the same equipment. Debtor defaults owing each \$100,000, and the equipment will bring \$120,000. Who has priority?
step: Attachment and perfection dates for B: attached January 10 (value, rights, agreement). B filed February 1, so B was perfected February 1.
step: For A: filed January 5, but its interest attached only on January 20, so A was perfected January 20.
step: Priority dates under § 9-322(a)(1) are the earlier of filing or perfection, with no gap: A's date is January 5 (filing) and B's date is February 1.
step: A has priority even though B lent first. A receives \$100,000, B receives the remaining \$20,000 and is left with an unsecured deficiency claim for \$80,000.
@mistakes
- Awarding priority to the secured party that lent first or attached first. Among perfected interests the test is first to file or perfect.
- Thinking a later perfection cures a gap against an earlier lien creditor. A lien that arose before perfection wins.
- Letting the buyer's knowledge decide the contest with a perfected security interest. A buyer outside the ordinary course takes subject to it regardless of knowledge.
- Forgetting that a repairer who keeps possession usually beats an earlier perfected security interest.

@practice self
prompt: Bank has a perfected security interest, filed in the proper office, in Debtor Co.'s printing press, which is equipment of Debtor's business. Debtor, without Bank's permission, sells the press to Buyer, a printing shop that pays fair value, takes delivery and knows nothing of Bank's security interest. Debtor then defaults. In IRAC form, decide whether Buyer takes the press free of Bank's security interest, and what rights Bank has.
hint: Is Buyer a buyer in the ordinary course, and was the security interest perfected before Buyer took delivery?
solution: Issue: whether a good-faith buyer for value takes free of a perfected security interest in equipment sold without the secured party's consent. Rule: a security interest continues in collateral notwithstanding sale unless the secured party authorized the disposition free of the security interest (§ 9-315(a)(1)), and the secured party also has an interest in the identifiable proceeds (§ 9-315(a)(2)); a buyer takes free of an unperfected security interest only if it gave value and took delivery without knowledge before perfection (§ 9-317(b)); a buyer in ordinary course takes free of a security interest created by its seller (§ 9-320(a)). Application: Bank's interest was perfected before the sale, so § 9-317(b) does not help Buyer. Buyer's good faith and lack of knowledge are irrelevant, and the sale was not authorized free of the interest. Buyer is not a buyer in the ordinary course, since Debtor does not sell presses as a business. Conclusion: Buyer takes subject to Bank's security interest, so Bank may repossess and foreclose on the press after default, and it also has a claim to the identifiable proceeds Debtor received.
rubric: States § 9-315(a)(1): the security interest continues despite the sale.
rubric: Explains why § 9-317(b) and the buyer in ordinary course rule do not apply.
rubric: Notes Bank's rights against the collateral and the proceeds.
