@article
Last week's rule, "first to file or perfect," would let an all-assets lender with an after-acquired clause block every other lender on new assets. The **purchase-money** rules solve that: a creditor that **finances the acquisition** of a new asset can be first in that asset, if it follows the perfection rules.

### What is a PMSI? (§ 9-103)
A security interest in goods is a **purchase-money security interest** to the extent the goods are **purchase-money collateral** securing a **purchase-money obligation**, meaning an obligation incurred as **all or part of the price** of the collateral, or for **value given to enable the debtor to acquire rights in or the use of the collateral** if the value is in fact so used. A **seller** (financing its own sale) and a **third-party lender** (whose loan the debtor in fact uses to buy the goods) can both hold a PMSI. In **non-consumer-goods** transactions a PMSI is not lost because the security interest also secures other obligations (cross-collateralization), renewals or refinancings, but it is a PMSI only **to the extent** of the purchase-money obligation (§ 9-103(f)); consumer-goods transactions are left to the courts (§ 9-103(h)). The secured party has the burden of proving PMSI status in a non-consumer case if status is challenged (§ 9-103(g)).

### Super-priority for goods other than inventory: § 9-324(a)
A PMSI in **goods other than inventory** (and other than livestock that are farm products) has priority over a conflicting security interest in the same goods (including an earlier-filed after-acquired lender) **if the PMSI is perfected when the debtor receives possession of the collateral or within 20 days after**. So a lender or seller that files within 20 days of delivery of the new **equipment** wins, even though the all-assets lender filed earlier. The same 20-day rule applies against a **lien creditor** (§ 9-317(e)).

### Super-priority for inventory: § 9-324(b)
A PMSI in **inventory** gets priority only if **all** of the following hold:
1. it is **perfected when the debtor receives possession** of the inventory (no 20-day grace);
2. the PMSI holder **sends an authenticated notification** to the holder of any conflicting security interest that **filed** before the PMSI holder's filing (or perfection); and
3. that holder **receives** the notice **within five years before the debtor receives possession**, stating that the PMSI holder has or expects to acquire a PMSI in the debtor's inventory, and describing it.
Reason: an inventory lender relies on inventory as a revolving base and should be warned that a rival will fund specific new stock. A PMSI in inventory also has priority in **identifiable cash proceeds** received on or before delivery to a buyer (§ 9-324(b)), but not in accounts.

### Consumer goods
A **PMSI in consumer goods** is **automatically perfected** (§ 9-309(1)) on attachment, except in goods covered by a certificate-of-title statute (such as cars), where title notation is needed (§ 9-311).

### Buyers in the ordinary course (BIOC)
A **buyer in ordinary course of business** (§ 1-201(b)(9)) buys goods **in good faith**, **without knowledge that the sale violates the rights of another person** in the goods, **in the ordinary course** from a person **in the business of selling goods of that kind**. A pawnbroker is not a BIOC, and a buyer taking goods as security or in satisfaction of a debt is not one. Payment may be in cash or by exchange for other property.

Under **§ 9-320(a)**, a BIOC **takes free of a security interest created by the buyer's seller**, **even if the security interest is perfected and the buyer knows it exists**. Knowledge of the existence of a security interest is not knowledge that the sale violates it, but a buyer who knows that the security agreement forbids the sale is not in good faith. (A buyer of **farm products** from a farmer is excluded from § 9-320(a), and federal law governs some such sales.) The rule protects customers of a store and ensures that the dealer can sell inventory financed by a bank. The secured party's remedy is in the **proceeds**.

A BIOC does not take free of a security interest created by **someone other than its seller** (for example, the seller's predecessor). **Entrustment (§ 2-403(2))**: if the owner **entrusts** goods to a **merchant who deals in goods of that kind**, the merchant has power to transfer all of the owner's rights to a BIOC.

Other buyers: a buyer of **consumer goods** from a **consumer** takes free of an unperfected-by-filing security interest if the buyer gives value, buys for personal, family or household use, without knowledge, **before a financing statement is filed** (§ 9-320(b)); this rule matters because PMSIs in consumer goods are automatically perfected and usually unfiled.

### Ranking competing claims: a method
1. List all claimants; mark each as secured party, lien creditor or buyer.
2. For each secured party, note the type of collateral and whether it is a PMSI.
3. Apply special rules first (BIOC; possessory lien; PMSI with the right timing and notice).
4. Then apply § 9-322(a) and § 9-317(a)(2) to the rest.
@example
title: A purchase-money lender beats an earlier all-assets lender
problem: Bank A filed on January 1 a financing statement covering all of Debtor Co.'s existing and after-acquired equipment, and lent \$200,000. On March 1 Seller sells Debtor Co. a \$40,000 press (equipment), delivered the same day, and Debtor Co. signs a security agreement giving Seller a security interest in it to secure the price. Seller files a financing statement on March 15. Who has priority in the press?
step: Characterize the interest. Seller's interest secures the price of the goods, so it is a PMSI in equipment (not inventory or consumer goods).
step: Apply § 9-324(a). Debtor Co. received possession March 1. Seller's PMSI was perfected March 15, which is within 20 days after possession.
step: Bank A has an earlier filing and an after-acquired clause, and would win under the first-to-file rule, but the PMSI exception overrides it.
step: Conclusion: Seller has priority in the press (for the \$40,000 price). Had Seller filed on March 25, it would have been perfected after the 20-day period, and Bank A would have priority.
@mistakes
- Applying the 20-day grace period to inventory. For inventory the PMSI must be perfected at the time of possession and notice must be sent.
- Forgetting that a PMSI exists only to the extent of the purchase-money obligation, so other debts are not superpriority.
- Thinking a buyer in ordinary course must lack knowledge of the security interest. Knowledge that the security interest exists is not enough to defeat BIOC status; knowledge that the sale breaches it is.
- Letting a buyer in the ordinary course take free of a security interest created by a prior owner rather than by the buyer's own seller.
