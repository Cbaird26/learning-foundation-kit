@article
Risk of loss decides who bears the loss when goods are destroyed or damaged **without fault of either party** between contracting and the buyer's receipt. The common law tied risk to title; Article 2 does not. It uses **delivery terms and breach** instead. If the buyer bears the risk, the buyer must pay the price even though the goods are gone; if the seller bears it, the seller cannot collect, and must deliver again or return the price.

### Step 1: Did the parties agree?
An agreement allocating risk controls. Under § 2-319, **F.O.B. the place of shipment** (for example, "F.O.B. seller's warehouse") creates a **shipment contract**: risk passes to the buyer when the seller delivers the goods to the carrier. **F.O.B. the place of destination** creates a **destination contract**: risk passes when the goods are tendered at destination. "Ex-ship" and "no arrival, no sale" terms also exist. Some contracts (CIF, C&F) are shipment contracts.

### Step 2: Absent breach, by the type of contract (§ 2-509)
1. **Carrier is to move the goods.**
   - **Shipment contract** (no obligation to deliver to a particular destination; the default): risk passes to the buyer when the goods are **duly delivered to the carrier** (properly packed, with proper contract of carriage).
   - **Destination contract:** risk passes when the goods are **duly tendered at the destination** so that the buyer can take delivery.
2. **Goods held by a bailee and to be delivered without being moved**: risk passes to the buyer
   - on receipt of a **negotiable document of title**;
   - on the bailee's **acknowledgment** of the buyer's right to possession, if the document is non-negotiable or there is none; or
   - on the buyer's receipt of a **non-negotiable document** of title or other written direction to deliver, **after a reasonable time**.
3. **Other cases (the buyer picks up the goods at the seller's place).**
   - **Merchant seller:** risk passes on the **buyer's receipt** of the goods.
   - **Non-merchant seller:** risk passes on **tender of delivery** (when the seller makes the goods available and notifies the buyer).
   The difference rewards the merchant's usual role (merchants carry insurance and have control until the buyer takes the goods).

### Step 3: Does breach change the answer? (§ 2-510)
- If the seller tenders **nonconforming goods** so that the buyer has a right to reject, **risk stays on the seller** until the defect is **cured** or the buyer **accepts**.
- If the buyer **rightfully revokes acceptance**, the buyer may treat the risk as having rested on the seller from the beginning **to the extent of any deficiency in the buyer's insurance coverage**.
- If the **buyer repudiates or is in breach** with respect to conforming goods that are identified to the contract, the seller may treat the risk as resting on the buyer **for a commercially reasonable time**, to the extent of any deficiency in the **seller's** insurance coverage.

### Casualty to identified goods (§ 2-613)
Where the contract requires **specific goods** and the goods suffer casualty **without fault of either party before the risk passes**, then if the loss is **total** the contract is avoided; if the loss is **partial** the buyer may inspect and either treat the contract as avoided or accept the goods with a price reduction.

### Insurable interest (§ 2-501)
The buyer obtains an **insurable interest** in goods when they are **identified** to the contract, even before risk passes. The seller keeps an insurable interest as long as it holds title or a security interest. Insurable interest is not the same as risk.

### Applying it
For each scenario ask: Is there a contract term (F.O.B. or similar)? If none, is the carrier to move the goods, or is there a bailee, or does the buyer pick up? Is the seller a merchant? Was there a breach? Then say who bears the loss and what follows (the buyer owes the price; or the seller must replace the goods).
@example
title: F.O.B. the seller's city versus F.O.B. the buyer's city
problem: Seller, a merchant in Dallas, contracts to sell Buyer 50 laptops for \$40,000, "F.O.B. Dallas." Seller loads the conforming laptops on Carrier's truck. The truck is destroyed in a crash in Oklahoma, with no fault of either party. Buyer refuses to pay. Alter the term to "F.O.B. Buyer's warehouse in Tulsa" and compare.
step: Identify the type of contract. "F.O.B. Dallas" is the place of shipment, so it is a shipment contract (§ 2-319(1)(a)).
step: Apply § 2-509(1)(a). Risk passes to Buyer when the conforming goods are duly delivered to the carrier in Dallas. The crash occurred after that, so Buyer bears the loss and must pay \$40,000 (Buyer can claim against Carrier or its insurer).
step: Change the term. "F.O.B. Buyer's warehouse" is a destination contract, so under § 2-509(1)(b) risk passes only when the goods are tendered at the Tulsa warehouse.
step: The crash occurred before tender in Tulsa, so Seller bears the loss and cannot collect the price. Seller must replace the goods if the contract is not avoided under § 2-613 (these laptops are fungible, not specific).
@mistakes
- Using title to decide risk of loss. Under Article 2, title is irrelevant to risk.
- Presuming that every shipment is a destination contract. Absent an agreement to deliver to a destination, a shipment contract is presumed.
- Forgetting that a nonconforming tender keeps risk on the seller until cure or acceptance.
- Mixing up the merchant and non-merchant rules for goods the buyer picks up (receipt versus tender).

choice: Ana, because Ben had not yet received the piano.
choice: Ana, because the contract was for a particular piano, which means § 2-613 avoids the contract.
choice*: Ben, because a non-merchant seller's risk of loss passes on tender, and Ana tendered by making the piano available and notifying Ben.
choice: Ben, because risk always passes when the contract is signed.
hint: Merchant status changes the rule for goods that the buyer picks up.
solution: Under § 2-509(3) the risk of a non-merchant seller passes to the buyer on tender of delivery. Section 2-613 applies only when the casualty occurs before risk passes; here the loss occurred after tender.
