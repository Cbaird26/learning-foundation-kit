@article
A security interest that has **attached** is good against the debtor. To bind **third parties** (other creditors, buyers, a bankruptcy trustee) the secured party must **perfect**. A security interest is **perfected** when it has attached **and** all steps required for perfection have been taken (§ 9-308(a)). Perfection is a public signal that the debtor's property is encumbered.

### Methods
1. **Filing a financing statement** (§ 9-310(a)). The default method for most collateral. Filing may take place **before attachment** and before the security agreement is signed (§ 9-502(d)), if the debtor authorizes it, which signing a security agreement does (§ 9-509).
2. **Possession** (§ 9-313). A secured party may perfect by taking possession of **goods, instruments, tangible chattel paper, certificated securities and negotiable documents**. Possession is the **only** way to perfect a security interest in tangible **money** (§ 9-312(b)(3)); under the 2022 amendments, electronic money is perfected by control.
3. **Control** (§§ 9-104, 9-106, 9-107, 9-314). For **deposit accounts** (as original collateral), control is the **only** way to perfect (§ 9-312(b)(1)). A secured party has control of a deposit account if it is the bank where the account is held, if the debtor, secured party and bank have authenticated a **control agreement** under which the bank will follow the secured party's instructions without further debtor consent, or if the secured party becomes the bank's customer on the account. Control is also required for **letter-of-credit rights** (§ 9-312(b)(2)) and is available (alongside filing) for **investment property** and **electronic chattel paper**.
4. **Automatic perfection** (§ 9-309). Perfection occurs on attachment, without a filing, for among other things: a **purchase-money security interest in consumer goods** (a car is the exception, discussed below), and the **sale of payment intangibles** and **promissory notes** (and an insignificant assignment of accounts).
5. **Temporary perfection** (§ 9-312(e)): for 20 days in certain cases, such as instruments or certificated securities delivered to the debtor for sale or exchange.
6. **Certificate-of-title goods** (§ 9-311). For goods covered by a certificate of title (such as cars), a filed financing statement is **not** effective; the secured party perfects by having its lien **noted on the certificate** under the state's titling statute. **Exception:** a financing statement can perfect a security interest in **inventory** held for sale by a dealer (§ 9-311(d)).
7. **Controllable electronic records.** Under the 2022 amendments (Article 12 and conforming changes to Article 9, enacted in many states by 2026), a security interest in a **controllable electronic record** (certain crypto-assets, for example) can be perfected by filing or by control, and a secured party with control generally has priority over one that only filed. Check your state's enactment.

**Relationship:** a general rule is that a security interest can be perfected by filing unless the Code requires something else; if there is a special rule (money, deposit accounts, letter-of-credit rights, titled goods), follow it.

### Where to file (§§ 9-301, 9-307, 9-501)
The law of the **debtor's location** governs perfection by filing (§ 9-301(1)), and the filing is made in the **central filing office** (usually the Secretary of State). Location:
- an **individual**: principal residence;
- a **registered organization** (a corporation or LLC formed by a public filing): the **state of organization**;
- an **organization that is not a registered organization**: its place of business (or chief executive office, if more than one).
So a Delaware corporation headquartered in Georgia files in **Delaware**. For possessory security interests and for goods perfected by title notation, other rules apply (location of the goods or of the title system).

### A sufficient financing statement (§ 9-502, § 9-503)
It must give the **name of the debtor**, the **name of the secured party** (or its representative), and **indicate the collateral covered** (a supergeneric "all assets" is sufficient here, § 9-504). Debtor-name rules:
- **Registered organization:** the name on its **public organic record** (§ 9-503(a)(1)).
- **Individual:** the state's enacted alternative (A or B). Under Alternative A, the name on the debtor's unexpired **driver's license** is required if there is one.
- A **trade name** alone is not sufficient (§ 9-503(c)).
A name error does not defeat the filing unless it is **seriously misleading**, meaning a search of the filing office's records **using the filing office's standard search logic under the debtor's correct name does not disclose the financing statement** (§ 9-506(b), (c)). If the debtor **changes its name** so that the financing statement becomes seriously misleading, it remains effective only for collateral acquired before, or within **four months** after, the change, unless an amendment is filed within those four months (§ 9-507(c)). If the debtor moves to a new state, perfection continues for **four months** after the move (§ 9-316(a)(2)), unless re-perfected there.

### Duration (§ 9-515)
A filed financing statement is effective for **five years**. A **continuation statement** may be filed only within the **six months before** the five-year period expires, and extends effectiveness five more years. If it **lapses**, the security interest becomes unperfected and is deemed never perfected as against a purchaser for value.

### Proceeds (§ 9-315)
A perfected security interest in collateral continues in identifiable proceeds, and **perfection** continues if the filed financing statement covers the original collateral and the proceeds are of a type perfectable by filing in the same office, or are **identifiable cash proceeds**. Otherwise perfection in proceeds lasts **20 days** after the debtor receives them unless the secured party perfects them in another way.

### Applying it
For "attached and perfected" questions: (1) attachment elements; (2) type of collateral; (3) required method; (4) was it done correctly and in the right place, in time; (5) any lapse or location change.
@example
title: Wrong state, wrong result
problem: On May 1 Lender gives Maple Furniture, Inc. a \$200,000 loan secured by "all equipment and inventory" under a signed security agreement. Maple is incorporated in Delaware and has its only plant in Georgia. Lender files a correct financing statement, using Maple's exact registered name, with Georgia's filing office. Is Lender's security interest attached? Perfected?
step: Attachment. Lender gave value (the loan), Maple has rights in its equipment and inventory, and Maple authenticated a security agreement describing the collateral by UCC type. The interest attached.
step: Classify the collateral and method. Equipment and inventory are perfected by filing (§ 9-310(a)). No exception applies.
step: Find the place. Maple is a registered organization, so it is located in its state of organization, Delaware (§ 9-307(e)), and the law of Delaware governs perfection by filing (§ 9-301(1)).
step: The filing in Georgia, where the goods are, is the wrong place. The location of the goods does not decide where to file for perfection by filing.
step: Conclusion: attached but not perfected. Lender must file in Delaware's central filing office; until then it risks losing to a bankruptcy trustee or competing creditors.
@mistakes
- Filing where the goods are or where the debtor operates, rather than in the debtor's location under § 9-307.
- Trying to perfect a deposit account by filing (control is required) or money by filing (possession is required).
- Perfecting a security interest in a titled vehicle by filing a financing statement rather than noting the lien on the certificate (except dealer inventory).
- Filing a continuation statement too early (more than six months before lapse) or too late.
