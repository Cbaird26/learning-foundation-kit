@article
Performance in a sale of goods is a cycle: the seller **tenders** delivery, the buyer **inspects**, and the buyer then **accepts** or **rejects**. Each step has rules, and a misstep can forfeit a remedy.

### Seller's duty: tender (§ 2-503)
Tender requires the seller to **put and hold conforming goods at the buyer's disposition** and give the buyer notice reasonably necessary to take delivery, at a reasonable time and place. The buyer pays on tender (§ 2-507), and, unless the parties agree otherwise, a buyer has a **right to inspect** goods before payment or acceptance (§ 2-513), except for C.O.D. and similar contracts.

### The perfect tender rule (§ 2-601)
In a **single-delivery contract**, if the goods or the tender of delivery **fail in any respect to conform to the contract**, the buyer may (a) reject the whole, (b) accept the whole, or (c) accept any commercial unit or units and reject the rest. The rule is strict: a minor defect permits rejection. Its limits are important:
- **Good faith** (§ 1-304) and the seller's right to **cure** (§ 2-508).
- **Installment contracts** (§ 2-612): where goods are to be delivered in separate lots, the buyer may reject an installment only if the nonconformity **substantially impairs the value of that installment** and cannot be cured. The buyer must accept an installment with a curable defect if the seller gives adequate assurance of cure. A nonconformity that **substantially impairs the value of the whole contract** is a breach of the whole.
- The contract may limit rejection rights (for example, repair-or-replace remedies, subject to § 2-719).

### Cure (§ 2-508)
1. If the **time for performance has not expired**, the seller who **seasonably notifies** the buyer of intent to cure may do so within the contract time.
2. Even after the time has expired, where the seller **had reasonable grounds to believe** the nonconforming tender would be acceptable (with or without a money allowance), the seller may, upon seasonable notice, have a **further reasonable time** to substitute a conforming tender. Courts read "reasonable grounds" to include trade usage, prior dealings and the seller's reasonable belief.

### Rejection (§§ 2-602, 2-605)
Rejection must be made **within a reasonable time after delivery or tender** and the buyer must **seasonably notify the seller**. After rejection the buyer must hold the goods with reasonable care for a time sufficient to permit the seller to remove them; a **merchant buyer** must follow reasonable instructions of the seller about goods in its possession (§ 2-603) and may resell perishable goods. A buyer may not exercise ownership over rejected goods. The buyer who fails to **state a particular defect** that is ascertainable by reasonable inspection cannot rely on the unstated defect to justify rejection (or to establish default) **if the seller could have cured it** had the defect been stated, or if, between merchants, the seller requested a final written statement of all defects (§ 2-605).

### Acceptance (§ 2-606)
Acceptance occurs when the buyer, **after a reasonable opportunity to inspect**, (a) signifies that goods are conforming or that the buyer will take or retain them despite nonconformity; (b) **fails to make an effective rejection** after a reasonable opportunity to inspect; or (c) does **any act inconsistent with the seller's ownership**, such as reselling or consuming the goods. Acceptance of part of a commercial unit is acceptance of the whole unit.

### Effects of acceptance (§ 2-607)
The buyer must **pay at the contract rate** for goods accepted. Acceptance does not itself waive claims for nonconformity, but the buyer must **notify the seller of breach within a reasonable time after discovery** (§ 2-607(3)(a)) or be barred, and the **buyer bears the burden of proving** any breach. Acceptance precludes rejection of the goods accepted; the buyer's remedy is damages for accepted goods (§ 2-714) or, in the cases § 2-608 allows, revocation.

### Revocation of acceptance (§ 2-608)
The buyer may **revoke** acceptance of a lot or commercial unit whose nonconformity **substantially impairs its value to the buyer** if the buyer accepted it
(a) on the reasonable assumption that the nonconformity would be cured, and it has not been seasonably cured; or
(b) without discovery of the nonconformity, if the acceptance was reasonably induced by the difficulty of discovery before acceptance or by the seller's assurances.
Revocation must occur **within a reasonable time after the buyer discovers or should have discovered the ground** and **before any substantial change in the condition of the goods** not caused by their own defects, and it is **not effective until the buyer notifies the seller**. A buyer who revokes has the same rights and duties as one who rejected.

### Applying it
Order the questions: (1) Was the tender conforming (and is the contract a single delivery or installments)? (2) Can the seller cure? (3) Did the buyer reject effectively, or accept? (4) If it accepted, is revocation available, and did the buyer give notice?
@example
title: Rejection, cure and a second chance
problem: Seller, a merchant, contracts to deliver 100 blue desks to Buyer, a merchant, by June 30 in a single delivery. On June 10 Seller delivers 100 desks, but 20 are green. Buyer promptly rejects the whole delivery and notifies Seller, stating the color defect. On June 15 Seller notifies Buyer that it will deliver replacement blue desks. On June 25 it does so. May Buyer refuse the second delivery?
step: Classify the contract and tender. This is a single-delivery contract, so the perfect tender rule applies. A delivery with 20 wrong-color desks fails to conform in a respect, so Buyer may reject the whole, and did so effectively (promptly, with notice, with a stated defect).
step: Ask whether Seller may cure. The time for performance (June 30) had not expired when Seller tendered on June 10.
step: Apply § 2-508(1): Seller seasonably notified Buyer of its intention to cure, and cured within the contract time by delivering conforming goods on June 25.
step: Conclusion: Buyer may not refuse the conforming second delivery and must accept and pay at the contract price. Had Seller's cure come after June 30, it would have needed § 2-508(2), which requires reasonable grounds to believe the first tender would be acceptable.
@mistakes
- Applying perfect tender to an installment contract, where only a substantial impairment justifies rejection of an installment.
- Forgetting that the seller may cure within the contract time (and sometimes after it), which limits the perfect tender rule.
- Treating acceptance as final. A buyer who accepted may still revoke under § 2-608 or recover damages for breach of warranty, but must notify the seller promptly.
- Taking actions inconsistent with the seller's ownership of rejected goods (such as reselling them), which constitutes acceptance.

prompt: Buyer, a merchant, receives 500 units, inspects them for three days as is usual in the trade, and accepts them. rubric: Recognizes that rejection is no longer available but revocation of acceptance under § 2-608 is.
rubric: States the elements: substantial impairment, difficulty of discovery, reasonable time after discovery, no substantial change, notice.
rubric: Mentions notice under § 2-607(3)(a) and the alternative damages remedy.
