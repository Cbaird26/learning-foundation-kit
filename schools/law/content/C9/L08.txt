@article
Liabilities and equity are the two sources of a company's funding, and the legal differences between them drive priority in bankruptcy, control in a corporation, and the rights of every investor.

### Liabilities

A **liability** is a present obligation arising from past events, to be settled by transferring assets or providing services. **Current liabilities** include accounts payable, wages payable, taxes payable, unearned revenue and the portion of long-term debt due within a year. **Interest** on a note accrues over time:

$$\text{Interest} = \text{Principal} \times \text{Annual rate} \times \frac{\text{months}}{12}.$$

Long-term liabilities include bonds and term loans. A bond with a \$500,000 face amount and a 6% stated rate pays interest of \$30,000 a year (\$15,000 semiannually) regardless of what buyers paid for it; investors may pay more or less than face depending on market interest rates.

### Contingent liabilities

A **loss contingency** is an uncertain situation that may produce a loss depending on a future event, such as a lawsuit, a product warranty or a government investigation. Under U.S. GAAP (ASC 450):

- If a loss is **probable** and the amount can be **reasonably estimated**, accrue a liability and a loss. If only a range can be estimated and no amount in it is a better estimate, accrue the **low end** and disclose the range.
- If a loss is **reasonably possible** (more than remote, but not probable), do not accrue; **disclose** the nature and an estimate of the possible loss or range, or state that an estimate cannot be made.
- If a loss is remote, no disclosure is usually required.

Gain contingencies are not recognized until realized. Lawyers have a special role here: the **audit inquiry letter**. Auditors ask a company's lawyers about pending claims, and lawyers respond under the ABA's Statement of Policy on lawyers' responses, protecting client confidences and privilege while meeting the auditor's need. Because the accrual and disclosure thresholds shape reported income, they are common ground in disputes between companies and their auditors.

### Stockholders' equity

A corporation's equity includes: **common stock** at par value (a stated nominal amount per share); **additional paid-in capital (APIC)**, the amount received above par; **retained earnings**; and **treasury stock**, shares the company has repurchased, shown as a deduction from equity (under the cost method, at what the company paid). **Shares issued** include treasury shares; **shares outstanding** do not. Dividends are paid only on outstanding shares.

When a company issues $N$ shares of par value $p$ for price $P$ per share, cash is $NP$, common stock is $Np$, and APIC is $N(P - p)$. Declaring a cash dividend creates a liability (dividends payable) and reduces retained earnings; paying it reduces cash and the liability. A stock dividend moves amounts within equity; total equity is unchanged.

### Why lawyers care

State corporate statutes limit distributions to shareholders. Under the Model Business Corporation Act (§ 6.40) a corporation may not make a distribution if afterward it could not pay its debts as they come due (equity insolvency) or if its total assets would be less than its total liabilities (plus any senior preferences). Directors who approve unlawful distributions can be personally liable. Debt holders, by contrast, have a contractual right to payment and priority over equity in liquidation.

@example
title: Issuing stock, repurchasing and paying a dividend
problem: A corporation issues 10,000 shares of \$1 par common stock for \$15 per share. It later buys back 500 shares at \$18 as treasury stock and declares a dividend of \$0.50 per share. Find common stock, APIC, the cost of treasury stock and the dividend.
step: Cash received $= 10{,}000 \times 15 = 150{,}000$. Common stock at par $= 10{,}000 \times 1 = 10{,}000$.
step: APIC $= 10{,}000 \times (15 - 1) = 140{,}000$. Check: $10{,}000 + 140{,}000 = 150{,}000$.
step: Treasury stock (cost method) $= 500 \times 18 = 9{,}000$, a deduction from equity. Shares outstanding fall to $9{,}500$.
step: Dividend $= 9{,}500 \times 0.50 = 4{,}750$, because treasury shares receive no dividends. Retained earnings fall by \$4,750.
@mistakes
- Calculating dividends on issued shares (which include treasury stock) rather than on outstanding shares.
- Recording all proceeds of a share issue in common stock; the amount above par goes to APIC.
- Accruing nothing for a probable, estimable lawsuit loss because the case is not yet decided.
- Treating a stock repurchase as an asset; treasury stock reduces equity.
