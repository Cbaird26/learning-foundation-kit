@review
### Weeks 1 and 2: the equation and the entries
$\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}$ always holds. Debits increase assets, expenses and withdrawals; credits increase liabilities, equity and revenue. A trial balance catches arithmetic errors only.

### Weeks 3 and 4: the income statement and balance sheet
Net income $=$ revenues $-$ expenses; the multi-step statement shows gross profit, operating income, income before taxes and net income. The balance sheet classifies current and noncurrent items; retained earnings roll forward with net income less dividends.

### Weeks 5 and 6: cash and accrual
CFO starts with net income, adds back noncash expenses, removes gains and losses on sales, and adjusts for working capital (increases in current operating assets subtract; increases in current operating liabilities add). Accrual accounting records revenue when earned and expenses when incurred; adjusting entries handle prepaids, unearned revenue and accruals.

### Weeks 7 and 8: estimates and funding
FIFO gives the lowest COGS in rising prices; straight-line depreciation is (cost $-$ salvage)/life. Loss contingencies are accrued if probable and estimable (low end of a range with no best estimate), disclosed if reasonably possible. Dividends go only on outstanding shares; APIC is the amount above par.

### Weeks 9 and 10: time value and valuation
$PV = F/(1+r)^n$; ordinary annuity $PV = C(1 - (1+r)^{-n})/r$; perpetuity $C/r$; growing perpetuity $C_1/(r - g)$; equity value $=$ EV $-$ debt $+$ cash.

### Weeks 11 and 12: analysis and assurance
Current ratio, quick ratio, debt-to-equity, interest coverage, margins, ROE and turnover; use averages for stock-versus-flow ratios. Audit opinions: unmodified, qualified, adverse, disclaimer. Fraud triangle: pressure, opportunity, rationalization. A bank reconciliation adjusts both sides to the true balance.

### Self-check
- I can record a transaction and show that the equation still balances.
- I can build both statements from a trial balance and reconcile net income to CFO.
- I can compute FIFO COGS, straight-line depreciation, present values and the main ratios.
- I can explain, in a sentence, what each ratio says and which accounting choices can move it.
- I can say what an audit does not do.

### Common errors
Subtracting interest above operating income; adding depreciation twice or not at all; reversing the sign of a working-capital change; dividing dividends over issued rather than outstanding shares; using the wrong number of periods or the wrong rate in present values; using ending instead of average balances; and treating any one valuation or ratio as the answer.

Using FIFO, cost of goods sold is computed from the oldest units first. Operating expenses paid in cash are \$4,000. The store also records one year of straight-line depreciation on equipment costing \$30,000 with salvage value \$3,000 and a five-year life. Assume a flat 25% income tax rate and no interest. What is net income, in dollars?
answer: 3975
check: (250 * 80 - (100 * 20 + 150 * 22) - 4000 - (30000 - 3000) / 5) * (1 - 0.25)
unit: dollars
hint: Compute sales, then FIFO cost of goods sold: 100 units at \$20 and 150 units at \$22.
hint: Gross profit is \$14,700. Subtract operating expenses and depreciation before tax.
hint: Income before taxes is \$5,300; tax is 25% of that.
solution: Sales $= 250 \times 80 = 20{,}000$. FIFO COGS $= 2{,}000 + 3{,}300 = 5{,}300$. Gross profit $= 14{,}700$. Depreciation $= 27{,}000/5 = 5{,}400$. Income before taxes $= 14{,}700 - 4{,}000 - 5{,}400 = 5{,}300$. Net income $= 5{,}300 \times 0.75 = 3{,}975$.

@exam self
prompt: A buyer is considering a purchase of a company whose latest statements show: current ratio 1.5, quick ratio 0.9, debt-to-equity ratio 1.5, interest coverage 5.0, inventory turnover falling from 8 to 6 over two years and days sales outstanding rising from 34 to 52 days. The statements are reviewed but not audited, and a lawsuit with a reasonably possible loss of \$20,000 to \$60,000 has not been accrued. Write a short memo section (about 150 to 250 words) explaining what these facts suggest about liquidity, leverage and quality of earnings, how the unaccrued claim should have been treated, and two protections the buyer should seek in the purchase agreement.
hint: Separate liquidity (current and quick ratios), leverage (debt-to-equity and coverage) and quality of earnings (turnover and days sales outstanding).
hint: Recall the rule for a reasonably possible loss (disclose) and for a probable and estimable loss (accrue).
solution: Liquidity is adequate but not strong: current assets cover current liabilities 1.5 times, but the quick ratio of 0.9 means the company depends on selling inventory to pay its short-term debts. Leverage is moderate to high at \$1.50 of liabilities per dollar of equity, although coverage of 5.0 shows operating profit can service the interest if earnings hold. The more worrying signals concern earnings quality: inventory turnover falling from 8 to 6 suggests slower-moving or obsolete stock that may need a write-down, and days sales outstanding rising from 34 to 52 suggests customers are paying more slowly or revenue is being recognized early, so reported income may overstate cash generation. Because the statements are not audited, the buyer cannot rely on an independent opinion. Under U.S. GAAP a reasonably possible loss need not be accrued but should be disclosed, with the \$20,000 to \$60,000 range; if the loss became probable and estimable, at least \$20,000 (the low end of the range, absent a better estimate) should be accrued, so the buyer should confirm the claim's status with the company's counsel. Protections: a specific indemnity (backed by an escrow or holdback) for the pending claim and any other undisclosed liabilities, and representations and warranties on the accuracy of the financial statements, receivables and inventory, plus a purchase-price adjustment tied to a working-capital target.
rubric: Interprets liquidity (1.5 and 0.9), leverage (1.5 and 5.0) and quality of earnings (slowing turnover, rising days sales outstanding) separately and correctly.
rubric: States that a reasonably possible loss is disclosed, not accrued, and a probable and estimable loss is accrued (low end of the range absent a better estimate).
rubric: Names two concrete purchase-agreement protections (such as a specific indemnity with escrow, financial-statement and inventory representations, or a working-capital adjustment) and links each to a stated risk.
