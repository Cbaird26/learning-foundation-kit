### Near, not at

Consider $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}$. It is undefined at $x = 1$, but what does it do *near* $1$?

| $x$ | $0.9$ | $0.99$ | $0.999$ | $1.001$ | $1.01$ | $1.1$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $f(x)$ | $1.9$ | $1.99$ | $1.999$ | $2.001$ | $2.01$ | $2.1$ |

The outputs squeeze toward $2$ from both sides. We write $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 1}\frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1} = 2$.

### The informal definition

$\displaystyle\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$ means that $f(x)$ can be made as close to $L$ as we like by taking $x$ close enough to $a$, with $x \neq a$. The value $f(a)$ is irrelevant; only the behavior nearby matters.

**One-sided limits** use $x \to a^-$ (from the left) and $x \to a^+$ (from the right). The two-sided limit exists exactly when both one-sided limits exist and are equal.

### When a limit does not exist

- **Jump:** the one-sided limits are different numbers.
- **Unbounded:** $\frac{1}{x^2}$ grows without bound as $x \to 0$. We write $\lim = \infty$, which describes the behavior but is not a real number.
- **Oscillation:** $\sin\frac1x$ swings between $-1$ and $1$ infinitely often as $x \to 0$.

### Computing limits

1. **Try substitution.** For polynomials, and for rational functions whose denominator is not $0$ at $a$, the limit is just $f(a)$.
2. **If you get $\frac00$,** more algebra is needed: factor and cancel, or rationalize. For $x \neq 2$, $\frac{x^2 - 4}{x - 2} = x + 2$, so the limit as $x \to 2$ is $4$. The form $\frac00$ is a signal, not an answer.
3. **At infinity,** compare degrees as for horizontal asymptotes: $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty}\frac{3x^2 + 1}{x^2 - 5} = 3$.

### Continuity

$f$ is **continuous at $a$** if $f(a)$ is defined, $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$ exists, and the two are equal. Roughly, you can draw the graph through $a$ without lifting your pencil.

### The punchline: secants become tangents

Last week's secant slopes $\frac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h}$ depend on $h$. Their limit as $h \to 0$ is the **instantaneous rate of change**, or **derivative**:

$$f'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0}\frac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h}.$$

For $f(x) = x^2$ at $a = 3$ the quotient simplifies to $6 + h$, which approaches $6$: the slope of the tangent line at $(3, 9)$. Notice that we never put $h = 0$ into the original quotient, since that gives $\frac00$. We simplify, then ask where the result is heading. That is what a **limiting process** means.

@example
title: Two limits that look alike
problem: Decide whether each limit exists: (i) $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 3}\frac{x^2 - x - 6}{x - 3}$ and (ii) $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2}\frac{x^2 - x - 6}{x - 2}$.
step: (i) Substituting $x = 3$ gives $\frac{9 - 3 - 6}{0} = \frac00$, so more algebra is needed. Factor the numerator: $x^2 - x - 6 = (x - 3)(x + 2)$.
step: For $x \neq 3$ the quotient equals $x + 2$, which approaches $5$ as $x \to 3$. So limit (i) is $5$, even though the function is undefined at $x = 3$.
step: (ii) Substituting $x = 2$ gives numerator $4 - 2 - 6 = -4$ and denominator $0$. This is $\frac{-4}{0}$, not $\frac00$, so no factor cancels.
step: Just right of $2$ the numerator is near $-4$ and the denominator is a tiny positive number, so the quotient is a large negative number. Just left of $2$ the denominator is a tiny negative number, so the quotient is a large positive number.
step: The one-sided behaviors disagree ($-\infty$ and $+\infty$), so limit (ii) does not exist. Always evaluate numerator and denominator at the point first to decide which case you are in.

@example
title: From secant slopes to a tangent slope
problem: For $f(x) = x^2$, compute the secant slopes from $x = 3$ to $x = 3 + h$ for $h = 1,\ 0.1,\ 0.01,\ 0.001$, and find the limit as $h \to 0$.
step: The secant slope is $\dfrac{(3+h)^2 - 9}{h}$. For $h = 1$: $\dfrac{16 - 9}{1} = 7$.
step: For $h = 0.1$: $\dfrac{9.61 - 9}{0.1} = 6.1$. For $h = 0.01$: $\dfrac{9.0601 - 9}{0.01} = 6.01$. For $h = 0.001$: $6.001$.
step: Algebra explains the pattern: $\dfrac{9 + 6h + h^2 - 9}{h} = 6 + h$ for $h \neq 0$.
step: As $h \to 0$, $6 + h \to 6$. The tangent line to $y = x^2$ at $(3, 9)$ has slope $6$.

@mistakes
- Believing the limit must equal $f(a)$. The limit depends on nearby values only, and $f(a)$ may not even exist.
- Stopping at $\frac00$ and declaring "the limit does not exist". The form $\frac00$ means "simplify first".
- Checking only one side of $a$ and missing a jump.
- Setting $h = 0$ in a difference quotient before simplifying.

@practice num
prompt: Find $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{\sqrt{x + 9} - 3}{x}$. Give an exact value.
answer: 1/6
check: lim(lambda x: (math.sqrt(x + 9) - 3)/x, 0)
hint: Substituting gives $\frac00$. Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate $\sqrt{x+9} + 3$.
hint: The numerator becomes $(x + 9) - 9 = x$, which cancels with the denominator.
solution: $\dfrac{\sqrt{x+9} - 3}{x}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{x+9} + 3}{\sqrt{x+9} + 3} = \dfrac{x}{x(\sqrt{x+9} + 3)} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x+9} + 3}$, which approaches $\dfrac{1}{3 + 3} = \dfrac16$.

(b) Simplify the difference quotient symbolically. (c) In two or three sentences, explain what it means to let $h \to 0$, what number the slopes approach, and what that number represents. (c) Letting $h \to 0$ means making $h$ smaller and smaller (positive or negative) without ever making it equal to $0$, and asking what value the quotient is forced toward. We cannot put $h = 0$ into the original quotient because that gives $\frac00$, which has no value; the limit is a way of describing the trend instead.

@quiz num
prompt: Find $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty}\frac{3x^2 + 1}{x^2 - 5}$.
answer: 3
check: (lambda f: f(1e9))(lambda x: (3*x**2 + 1)/(x**2 - 5))
tol: 0.001
hint: Compare the degrees of the numerator and denominator.
hint: Equal degrees: divide every term by $x^2$, or take the ratio of leading coefficients.
solution: Dividing top and bottom by $x^2$ gives $\dfrac{3 + 1/x^2}{1 - 5/x^2}$. As $x \to \infty$ the fractions tend to $0$, so the limit is $\dfrac{3}{1} = 3$.

@quiz num
prompt: Find $\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0}\frac{(2 + h)^3 - 8}{h}$.
answer: 12
check: lim(lambda h: ((2 + h)**3 - 8)/h, 0)
hint: Expand $(2 + h)^3$.
hint: $(2+h)^3 = 8 + 12h + 6h^2 + h^3$. Cancel the $8$ and divide by $h$.
solution: $\dfrac{8 + 12h + 6h^2 + h^3 - 8}{h} = 12 + 6h + h^2$ for $h \neq 0$, which approaches $12$ as $h \to 0$. This is the slope of the tangent to $y = x^3$ at $x = 2$.
