@article
Analytic geometry puts shapes on a coordinate grid so that geometric facts become algebra.

### Lines and distances

The distance between $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is $d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$, and the midpoint averages the coordinates. Parallel lines have equal slopes; perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is $-1$. The perpendicular bisector of a segment passes through its midpoint with the negative reciprocal slope.

### Conics as distance conditions

A **conic section** is a slice of a double cone. Each conic is also the set of points that satisfy one distance condition.

| Conic | Condition | Standard form |
|---|---|---|
| Circle | distance $r$ from the center $(h,k)$ | $(x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2$ |
| Parabola | same distance from the focus and the directrix | $(x-h)^2 = 4p\,(y-k)$ |
| Ellipse | sum of distances to two foci is $2a$ | $\dfrac{(x-h)^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{(y-k)^2}{b^2} = 1$ |
| Hyperbola | difference of distances to two foci is $2a$ | $\dfrac{(x-h)^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{(y-k)^2}{b^2} = 1$ |

**Parabola** $(x-h)^2 = 4p(y-k)$: vertex $(h, k)$, focus $(h, k+p)$, directrix $y = k - p$. It opens upward when $p > 0$. Swap the roles of $x$ and $y$ for a sideways parabola.

**Ellipse** (horizontal, $a > b > 0$): vertices $(h \pm a, k)$, co-vertices $(h, k \pm b)$, foci $(h \pm c, k)$ with $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$. For a vertical ellipse, $a^2$ goes under the $y$-term.

**Hyperbola** (horizontal): vertices $(h \pm a, k)$, foci $(h \pm c, k)$ with $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$, and asymptotes $y - k = \pm\dfrac{b}{a}(x - h)$. Here $a^2$ always goes with the positive term.

### Recognize a conic by completing the square

Gather the $x$-terms and the $y$-terms, factor out any leading coefficients, and complete each square. Whatever you add inside a bracket, add *coefficient $\times$ that amount* to the other side. Then divide so the right side is $1$.

- Only one variable squared: a parabola.
- Both squared with the same sign: a circle (equal coefficients) or an ellipse.
- Both squared with opposite signs: a hyperbola.

Coordinate geometry problems often mix these tools: a conic's center is a midpoint, a radius is a distance, and tangent or perpendicular conditions turn into slope equations.

@example
title: Complete the square to identify an ellipse
problem: Put $4x^2 + 9y^2 - 16x + 18y - 11 = 0$ in standard form and find the center, vertices and foci.
step: Group and factor: $4(x^2 - 4x) + 9(y^2 + 2y) = 11$.
step: Complete each square. Inside the brackets add $4$ and $1$, so add $4 \cdot 4 = 16$ and $9 \cdot 1 = 9$ on the right: $4(x - 2)^2 + 9(y + 1)^2 = 11 + 16 + 9 = 36$.
step: Divide by $36$: $\dfrac{(x-2)^2}{9} + \dfrac{(y+1)^2}{4} = 1$. This is an ellipse with center $(2, -1)$, $a = 3$ and $b = 2$.
step: The larger denominator is under $x$, so the major axis is horizontal. Vertices: $(5, -1)$ and $(-1, -1)$.
step: $c^2 = 9 - 4 = 5$, so the foci are $(2 \pm \sqrt5,\, -1)$.

@example
title: A parabola from its focus and directrix
problem: Find the equation of the parabola with focus $(1, 3)$ and directrix $y = -1$.
step: The vertex is halfway between the focus and the directrix, at $(1, 1)$.
step: The focus is $p = 2$ units above the vertex, so the parabola opens upward with $p = 2$.
step: Use $(x - h)^2 = 4p(y - k)$: $(x - 1)^2 = 8(y - 1)$.
step: Check with the point $(5, 3)$: it satisfies $16 = 8(2)$. Its distance to the focus is $4$ and its distance to the directrix is $3 - (-1) = 4$.

@mistakes
- Reading the center with the wrong sign: $(x + 3)^2$ has $h = -3$.
- Forgetting to multiply by the leading coefficient when you add to the right side while completing the square.
- Using $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$ for a hyperbola. For a hyperbola $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$, and $a^2$ goes with the positive term.
- Not dividing by the constant: the right side of a standard form must equal $1$ before you read off $a^2$ and $b^2$.

@practice num
prompt: Find the distance between the points $(-2, 3)$ and $(4, -5)$.
answer: 10
check: math.dist((-2, 3), (4, -5))
hint: Use $d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$.
hint: The horizontal change is $6$ and the vertical change is $-8$.
solution: $d = \sqrt{6^2 + (-8)^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10$.

@practice tuple
prompt: The circle $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0$ has center $(h, k)$ and radius $r$. Enter $(h, k, r)$.
answer: (3, -2, 5)
check: (6/2, -4/2, math.sqrt((6/2)**2 + (4/2)**2 + 12))

hint: $x^2 - 6x + 9 = (x-3)^2$ and $y^2 + 4y + 4 = (y+2)^2$. Add $9 + 4$ to both sides.
solution: $(x-3)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 12 + 9 + 4 = 25$. The center is $(3, -2)$ and the radius is $5$.

@practice mc
prompt: Which equation represents an ellipse that is not a circle?
choice*: $x^2 + 4y^2 = 16$
choice: $x^2 - 4y^2 = 16$
choice: $x^2 + 4y = 16$
choice: $x^2 + y^2 = 16$
hint: Look at which variables are squared and at the signs of the squared terms.
hint: Same signs and different coefficients on $x^2$ and $y^2$ give an ellipse that is not a circle.
solution: $x^2 + 4y^2 = 16$ divides to $\frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1$, an ellipse with $a \ne b$. $x^2 - 4y^2 = 16$ is a hyperbola (opposite signs), $x^2 + 4y = 16$ a parabola (only $x$ is squared), $x^2 + y^2 = 16$ a circle.

@practice num
prompt: The ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{25} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$ has two foci on the $x$-axis. Find the distance between them.
answer: 8
check: 2 * root(lambda c: math.hypot(c, 3) - 5, 0, 10)
hint: Here $a^2 = 25$ and $b^2 = 9$. Which formula links $a$, $b$ and $c$ for an ellipse?
hint: $c^2 = a^2 - b^2 = 16$. The distance between the foci is $2c$.
solution: $c = \sqrt{25 - 9} = 4$, so the foci are $(\pm 4, 0)$ and the distance between them is $2c = 8$. Check: from the co-vertex $(0, 3)$ to the focus $(4, 0)$ the distance is $5 = a$.

@practice self
prompt: Deliverable practice. Put $9x^2 - 4y^2 - 36x - 8y - 4 = 0$ in standard form. Identify the conic, then give its center, vertices, foci and asymptotes. Explain each step of completing the square.
hint: Group the $x$-terms and the $y$-terms separately and factor out $9$ and $-4$ respectively.
hint: Mind the sign: factoring $-4$ out of $-4y^2 - 8y$ gives $-4(y^2 + 2y)$, so completing the square adds $-4 \cdot 1$ on the right.
solution: $9(x^2 - 4x) - 4(y^2 + 2y) = 4$. Complete squares: $9\big[(x-2)^2 - 4\big] - 4\big[(y+1)^2 - 1\big] = 4$, so $9(x-2)^2 - 4(y+1)^2 - 36 + 4 = 4$ and $9(x-2)^2 - 4(y+1)^2 = 36$. Divide by $36$: $\dfrac{(x-2)^2}{4} - \dfrac{(y+1)^2}{9} = 1$. This is a hyperbola with horizontal transverse axis, center $(2, -1)$, $a = 2$, $b = 3$, $c = \sqrt{4 + 9} = \sqrt{13}$. Vertices: $(0, -1)$ and $(4, -1)$. Foci: $(2 \pm \sqrt{13}, -1)$. Asymptotes: $y + 1 = \pm\tfrac32 (x - 2)$.
rubric: Completes both squares correctly and reaches $\frac{(x-2)^2}{4} - \frac{(y+1)^2}{9} = 1$.
rubric: Identifies a hyperbola with center $(2, -1)$, $a = 2$ and $b = 3$, and justifies it by the opposite signs.
rubric: Gives vertices $(0,-1)$ and $(4,-1)$ and foci $(2 \pm \sqrt{13}, -1)$ using $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$.
rubric: Gives asymptotes $y + 1 = \pm\frac{3}{2}(x - 2)$.

@quiz num
prompt: A hyperbola has equation $\dfrac{(x-1)^2}{4} - \dfrac{(y+2)^2}{25} = 1$. What is the positive slope of its asymptotes?
answer: 5/2
check: nderiv(lambda x: -2 + 5*math.sqrt((x - 1)**2/4 - 1), 1e4)
tol: 0.001
hint: The asymptotes are $y - k = \pm\frac{b}{a}(x - h)$. Identify $a$ and $b$ from the denominators.
hint: $a^2 = 4$ goes with the positive $x$-term, and $b^2 = 25$.
solution: $a = 2$ and $b = 5$, so the asymptote slopes are $\pm\frac{b}{a} = \pm\frac52$. The positive slope is $\frac52$.

@quiz expr
prompt: A parabola has focus $(2, 5)$ and directrix $y = 1$. Write its equation as $y$ in terms of $x$.
vars: x
lead: $y =$
answer: 3 + (x - 2)^2/8
check: lambda x: root(lambda y: math.hypot(x - 2, y - 5) - abs(y - 1), 1, 1000)
hint: The vertex is halfway between the focus and the directrix.
hint: The vertex is $(2, 3)$ and $p = 2$. Use $(x - h)^2 = 4p(y - k)$ and solve for $y$.
solution: The vertex is $(2, 3)$, and $p = 2$ because the focus is $2$ above the vertex. So $(x-2)^2 = 8(y-3)$, which gives $y = 3 + \frac{(x-2)^2}{8}$.
