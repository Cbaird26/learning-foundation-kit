@review
### Week by week

**Week 1: First possession, finders, gifts.** Possession, not pursuit, vests title in wild animals (*Pierson v. Post*). Classify found property as lost, mislaid, abandoned or embedded; the true owner beats every finder. A gift needs intent, delivery and acceptance; a gift causa mortis is revoked if the donor survives.

**Week 2: Adverse possession.** Actual, open and notorious, exclusive, hostile and continuous for the statutory period. Majority hostility test is objective. Tacking needs privity; disability tolls; the period does not run against a future interest until it is possessory.

**Week 3: Present estates.** Fee simple absolute, determinable ("so long as", reverter), subject to condition subsequent ("but if", right of entry), subject to executory limitation, fee tail (abolished or converted), life estate (no waste).

**Week 4: Future interests and RAP.** Reversion, remainders (vested, contingent, subject to open), executory interests. The Rule: must vest, if at all, within 21 years after a life in being. Class gifts are all or nothing. USRAP: 90-year wait-and-see.

**Week 5: Concurrent ownership.** Four unities; survivorship; a conveyance severs only the conveyed share; rent only on ouster; contribution; partition and heirs property.

**Week 6: Marital property.** Common-law title, equitable distribution, elective share, community property (separate: before marriage, gift, inheritance).

**Week 7: Landlord and tenant.** Estates, assignment versus sublease, habitability, no self-help, mitigation.

**Week 8: Contracts and deeds.** Statute of Frauds, marketable title at closing, equitable conversion, delivery of a deed, six covenants for title, constructive eviction elements.

**Week 9: Recording acts.** Race, notice, race-notice; BFP; kinds of notice; shelter rule; wild deeds; title insurance.

**Week 10: Mortgages.** Redemption cannot be waived; assumption versus subject to; due-on-sale; priority and distribution of sale proceeds.

**Week 11: Servitudes.** Easement creation (express, prior use, necessity, prescription, estoppel); real covenants versus equitable servitudes; *Shelley v. Kraemer*.

**Week 12: Zoning and takings.** Euclid deference; variances; nonconforming uses; Loretto, Lucas, Penn Central, Nollan/Dolan/Koontz/Sheetz, Kelo, Knick, Tyler.

### Common errors

Treating pursuit as possession; ignoring privity when tacking; confusing "so long as" with "but if"; applying the Rule Against Perpetuities to reversions; treating a will as severing a joint tenancy; reading race-notice as race only; letting a lender waive redemption; and requiring privity for an equitable servitude.

@exam self
prompt: Analyze this problem using the issues from the whole course. For the past 16 years, Marsh has driven over a 10-foot strip of Pell's land to reach her garage, openly and without Pell's permission or any written grant. The adverse possession and prescription period in the state is 15 years, and the state has a notice recording statute. Last month Pell sold the land to Dee, who paid full value, searched the records, and found nothing about Marsh. Dee has now blocked the strip with a fence. Marsh sues. Decide who prevails and why, addressing prescription, the recording act, notice and the remedy.
solution: Issue: whether Marsh holds an easement binding on Dee. Rule: a prescriptive easement arises from use that is open and notorious, adverse (without permission) and continuous for the statutory period; it vests by operation of law at the end of the period. Application: Marsh's driving was open and visible, without permission, and continuous for 16 years, which exceeds the 15-year period, so the easement vested before Pell sold. Recording acts protect a later purchaser against prior unrecorded conveyances, and an interest that arose by operation of law rather than by a written conveyance is generally not subject to them. In addition, Marsh's open use of the strip would have put a reasonable buyer on inquiry notice, so Dee cannot claim to be a bona fide purchaser without notice. Conclusion: Marsh holds a prescriptive easement that binds Dee. She may seek an injunction requiring removal of the fence and a declaratory judgment or quiet title decree to establish her right of way.
rubric: States and applies the elements of prescription and the 15-year period.
rubric: Explains why the recording act does not defeat the prescriptive easement and discusses inquiry notice.
rubric: Reaches a conclusion and names the remedy.
hint: Count the years first, then ask whether the easement existed when Dee bought.
