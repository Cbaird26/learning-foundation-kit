@lesson
title: Adding within 10
summary: Put groups together to find the total, and write adding sentences with sums up to 10.
objective: Understand that adding means putting groups together.
objective: Read and write an adding sentence such as $3 + 2 = 5$.
objective: Add two small numbers with a total of 10 or less.
reading: Khan Academy, Early math review: the topic "Addition" within 10.

@article
**Adding** means putting groups together to find how many in all.

### Put two groups together

You have 3 apples. Your friend gives you 2 more apples. Now count all of them: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. You have 5 apples.

We write this as a number sentence:

$$3 + 2 = 5$$

The **+** sign says "plus" or "and". The **=** sign says "is the same as". The answer to adding is called the **sum**. Here the sum is 5.

### Count on

You do not have to count from 1 each time. Start at the bigger number and count up. For $5 + 2$, start at 5 and say 6, 7. The sum is 7.

### Order does not matter

$2 + 4$ and $4 + 2$ both make 6. You can add in either order. Pick the easy way and start with the bigger number.

### Make 10

Some pairs make 10: $1 + 9$, $2 + 8$, $3 + 7$, $4 + 6$ and $5 + 5$. It is nice to know them by heart.

### Use fingers and objects

Use fingers, blocks or coins. Put one group down, put the other group beside it, then count them all.

@example
title: Adding with blocks
problem: You have 4 blocks. You get 3 more blocks. How many blocks do you have now?
step: Write the adding sentence: $4 + 3$.
step: Start at 4 and count on 3 more: 5, 6, 7.
step: $4 + 3 = 7$. You have 7 blocks.

@mistakes
- Counting the first group again and getting a sum that is one too big.
- Mixing up the + sign and the = sign.
- Forgetting to count on from the bigger number and counting all from 1 by mistake.

@practice num
prompt: $2 + 3 = $ what number?
answer: 5
check: 2 + 3
hint: Hold up 2 fingers on one hand and 3 on the other.
hint: Count all of the fingers.
solution: Put 2 and 3 together and count: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. So $2 + 3 = 5$.

@practice self

solution: For example, 3 beans and 4 beans make 7 beans, so $3 + 4 = 7$. Try $5 + 2 = 7$ and $6 + 3 = 9$ as well.

rubric: You counted the total.
rubric: You said an adding sentence that matched your groups.
