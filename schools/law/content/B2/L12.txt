@lesson
title: Repairing weak areas
summary: Turn an error log into a ranked repair plan, and re-test the most commonly missed recent changes in the law.
objective: Compute error rates by topic and rank topics for repair.
objective: Allocate limited study time in proportion to need.
objective: Diagnose an error as knowledge, application, reading or timing, and choose a matching fix.
objective: Restate the recent changes that are most often taught from outdated rules.
reading: NCBE exam information and sample questions; use your own error log from the timed sets in this course as the main resource.

@article
Repair work succeeds when it is **specific**. "Study more evidence" is a plan that fails; "write and recite the five requirements of FRE 702 and then do ten questions on expert testimony" is a plan that works.

### Step 1: rank by rate and by volume
For each topic, compute the **error rate** = (questions missed) / (questions attempted). Rank topics by rate, but weigh **exam weight** too: a 40 percent error rate on a subject with a few questions matters less than 30 percent on a heavily tested subject. Pick the **three weakest** topics, which is the block deliverable.

### Step 2: diagnose each error
- **Knowledge**: you could not state the rule. Fix: write a one-sentence rule statement with elements, then recite it from memory the next day.
- **Application**: you stated the rule but matched it to the wrong fact. Fix: work five fact patterns for that rule and underline the fact that satisfies each element.
- **Reading**: you missed a word such as "unless," "in the prosecution's case-in-chief," or "which statement is least accurate." Fix: underline the call of the question first.
- **Timing**: you rushed or lingered. Fix: pacing checkpoints every ten questions.

### Step 3: allocate time
Divide your available hours in proportion to your error counts (or weighted errors). If your errors are 18, 12 and 6 in three topics and you have 12 hours, the shares are $12 \times 18/36 = 6$, 4 and 2 hours. Schedule the work in short sessions on different days (spaced repetition): learn, recite the next day, re-test after about a week.

### Step 4: re-test the traps
Some errors recur because older outlines teach rules that have changed. Check yourself on these current rules:
- *Chevron* is overruled (*Loper Bright*, 2024): courts decide the best reading, giving an agency view *Skidmore* respect; earlier *Chevron*-based holdings stay in force.
- *Humphrey's Executor* is overruled (*Trump v. Slaughter*, 2026): FTC commissioners are removable at will; the Federal Reserve and non-executive-branch offices were left open.
- IEEPA does not authorize tariffs (*Learning Resources*, 2026).
- Universal injunctions likely exceed federal courts' equitable authority (*Trump v. CASA*, 2025).
- *Lemon* is abandoned (*Kennedy v. Bremerton*); *Roe* and *Casey* are overruled (*Dobbs*); the Second Amendment test is text and history (*Bruen*).
- Geofence location data obtained from Google is a search (*Chatrie*, 2026); warrantless home entry is allowed on an objectively reasonable basis to believe someone needs emergency aid (*Case v. Montana*, 2026); excessive force is judged on the totality (*Barnes v. Felix*, 2025).
- FRE 702 (2023) puts the burden on the proponent by a preponderance; FRE 613(b), 801(d)(2) and 804(b)(3) were amended in 2024; new FRE 107 governs illustrative aids.

### Step 5: re-measure
After repair, retake a short set in each repaired topic. If the error rate falls below your target (say 15 percent), move on; if not, change the method, not just the hours.

### Block deliverable
Complete a timed mixed set, then repair your three weakest topics with a written plan listing topic, error type, fix and schedule.

@example
title: From error log to plan
problem: Your log shows these misses: constitutional law 9 of 30 attempted, evidence 6 of 40, criminal procedure 8 of 20. Which topic has the highest error rate and what is it?
step: Compute each rate: constitutional law $9/30 = 30\%$; evidence $6/40 = 15\%$; criminal procedure $8/20 = 40\%$.
step: Rank: criminal procedure is highest at 40 percent.
step: Check exam weight: criminal procedure is a heavily tested area, so it is the first repair target.
step: Choose fixes by error type after reviewing the missed questions one by one.

@mistakes
- Re-reading outlines passively instead of reciting rules and doing questions.
- Ranking topics only by raw misses, without dividing by questions attempted.
- Spending all repair time on favorite topics.
- Never re-testing after repair, so no one knows whether it worked.

@practice mc
prompt: A student misses a question about the federal Commerce Clause. The student knew that Congress may regulate economic activity that substantially affects interstate commerce in the aggregate, but chose the answer treating a local home-grown crop for sale as noneconomic. How should the error be classified and fixed?
choice*: Application error: the student knew the rule but misclassified the facts, so work fact patterns that separate economic from noneconomic activity.
choice: Knowledge error: the student must memorize the rule again.
choice: Timing error: the student should read faster.
choice: Reading error: the student should never read the facts.
hint: Did the student know the rule?
solution: The student stated the rule correctly but applied it to the wrong category of facts, an application error. The fix is targeted practice on classification.

@practice mc
prompt: In 2019 the federal court of appeals for this circuit upheld an agency's interpretation of an ambiguous statute under Chevron deference, and that decision became final. Today a new plaintiff challenges the same interpretation in another case in a district court within the same circuit. What is the best analysis under current law?
choice: The court must defer to the agency's interpretation because Chevron is still the law.
choice*: The court decides the best reading of the statute itself, giving the agency's view Skidmore respect, but the earlier Chevron-based holding remains in force by statutory stare decisis.
choice: The earlier decision is void because Chevron was overruled.
choice: The agency's interpretation must be rejected because courts may never consider agency views.
hint: Recall the *Loper Bright* rule and what it said about prior holdings.
solution: *Loper Bright* (2024) overruled *Chevron*, directing courts to decide the best reading with *Skidmore* respect; earlier holdings that relied on Chevron remain in force by statutory stare decisis.

@practice ms

choice*: FTC commissioners are removable at will after *Trump v. Slaughter*, which overruled *Humphrey's Executor*.
choice*: The Establishment Clause is read by reference to historical practices and understandings, and the *Lemon* test is abandoned.
choice: Universal injunctions are routinely authorized, so a district court may block a federal policy nationwide whenever one plaintiff sues.

choice: The International Emergency Economic Powers Act authorizes the President to impose tariffs.
hint: Check each against the case it comes from.
solution: The accurate statements are the *Slaughter* removal rule, the history-based Establishment Clause approach and the proponent's burden under FRE 702. *CASA* limits universal injunctions, and *Learning Resources* held IEEPA does not authorize tariffs.

@quiz num
prompt: You have 10 hours to repair three topics in proportion to your errors, which are 20, 12 and 8. How many hours go to the topic with 20 errors?
answer: 5
check: 10 * 20 / (20 + 12 + 8)
hint: Find the share of total errors for that topic.

solution: The share is 20/40 = 1/2, and half of 10 hours is 5 hours.
