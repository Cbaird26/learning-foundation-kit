### Secured transactions (UCC Article 9): a summary for this course
A **security interest** secures payment or performance of an obligation. It **attaches** when (1) value is given, (2) the debtor has rights in the collateral, and (3) the debtor has authenticated a security agreement that describes the collateral (UCC § 9-203), or the secured party has possession. **Perfection** makes the interest effective against third parties, usually by filing a financing statement (§ 9-310), by possession, or by control for some collateral; a purchase-money security interest in consumer goods is perfected automatically on attachment (§ 9-309). **Priority** among conflicting security interests in the same collateral is generally first to file or perfect (§ 9-322). A **purchase-money security interest (PMSI)** can have priority over an earlier-filed interest if it is perfected within the time the Code allows (for equipment, 20 days after the debtor receives possession, § 9-324). A **buyer in the ordinary course** of business takes free of a security interest created by the seller even if perfected and known (§ 9-320(a)). On **default**, the secured party may take possession without breach of the peace (§ 9-609), and must dispose of collateral in a commercially reasonable way after reasonable notice (§§ 9-610, 9-611). Proceeds are applied to expenses, then the debt; any surplus goes to the debtor, and the debtor remains liable for a deficiency (§ 9-615).

### Conflict of laws: a summary for this course
**Choice of law in contracts**: courts generally honor the parties' chosen law unless it has no substantial relationship or conflicts with a fundamental policy of the state whose law would otherwise apply (Restatement (Second) § 187). **Torts**: the traditional rule applies the law of the place of injury; most states use the Restatement (Second)'s most significant relationship test (place of injury, place of conduct, domicile of parties, place of relationship). **Full faith and credit** requires a state to enforce another state's final judgment on the merits if the rendering court had jurisdiction, even if the judgment is wrong in law; defenses are limited to lack of jurisdiction and fraud. **Forum non conveniens** permits a court to dismiss when another forum is available and adequate and private and public interest factors favor it. These rules also appear alongside the personal jurisdiction cases (*International Shoe*, *Ford Motor Co.*, *Mallory v. Norfolk Southern*).

@example
title: Priority between two lenders
problem: Supplied law (summary written for this course): A security interest attaches when value is given, the debtor has rights in the collateral and the debtor authenticates a security agreement describing the collateral. Priority between perfected interests goes to the first to file or perfect. Facts: On January 5, Lender A files a financing statement against Debtor's equipment but lends nothing. On February 1, Lender B lends, takes a security agreement, and files. On March 1, Lender A lends and takes a security agreement. Who has priority?
step: Identify the rule: first to file or perfect determines priority (supplied).
step: Apply: A filed on January 5, before B filed on February 1; filing can precede attachment.
step: A's interest becomes perfected on March 1, when it attaches, but priority dates from the earlier filing.
step: Conclude: Lender A has priority over Lender B.

@example
title: Surplus after a sale
problem: Supplied law (summary written for this course): After default, proceeds of a disposition go first to the reasonable expenses of disposition and then to the secured debt; any surplus is owed to the debtor. Facts: Debtor owes \$18,000. Secured party sells the collateral for \$22,500 and incurs \$1,500 in expenses. How much surplus is owed?
step: Subtract expenses from proceeds: \$22,500 - \$1,500 = \$21,000.
step: Subtract the debt: \$21,000 - \$18,000 = \$3,000.
step: Conclude: the surplus owed to the debtor is \$3,000.

@mistakes
- Importing a rule from memory when the supplied excerpt says otherwise.
- Missing that filing can come before attachment, so priority runs from the filing date.
- Forgetting that expenses of disposition come off the proceeds first.
- Giving full faith and credit effect only to judgments that were correct on the law; a final judgment with jurisdiction is enforceable even if wrong.
