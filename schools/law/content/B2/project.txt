step: Assemble 50 multiple-choice questions: about 30 from this course's practice and quiz items across constitutional law, criminal law and procedure, evidence and business associations, and about 20 from NCBE's free sample questions for the subjects of this course. Do not look at the answers.
step: Set a 90-minute timer. Take the set in one sitting with no notes. Mark any question that took more than two minutes and write the clock time after each tenth question.

step: Score both sets. For every miss, write an error-log row: subject, topic, rule you should have used, error type, and fix.
step: Compute error rates by subject (misses divided by attempts) and by error type. Choose the three weakest topics, weighing exam weight.

step: Do the re-test within one to two weeks and record whether the rate fell. If it did not, change the method.
material: **Supplied law for the integrated set (summaries written for this course; use only these).** *Rule 1 (secured transactions).* A security interest attaches when value is given, the debtor has rights in the collateral, and the debtor has authenticated a security agreement describing the collateral. A financing statement filed before attachment is effective. Priority among perfected interests goes to the first to file or perfect. A buyer in the ordinary course takes free of a security interest created by its seller. After default, proceeds of a commercially reasonable disposition go first to reasonable expenses, then to the debt; the debtor owes any deficiency and is owed any surplus. *Rule 2 (choice of law).* In a tort case, the court applies the law of the state with the most significant relationship to the occurrence and the parties, considering the place of injury, the place of conduct, the parties' domicile, and where their relationship is centered. *Rule 3 (professional responsibility).* A lawyer shall not commingle client funds with the lawyer's own funds; funds received for a client must be held in a trust account and promptly delivered to the client, with any disputed portion kept in trust until resolved. A lawyer may not represent a client if the representation is directly adverse to another current client unless each client gives informed consent, confirmed in writing, and the lawyer reasonably believes competent representation is possible.

### Most common errors
- Teaching *Chevron* or *Humphrey's Executor* as current law.
- Applying a rule to the wrong actor (a federal law to a private person, or Miranda to non-custodial questioning).
- Missing that only voluntary statements may impeach after a Miranda violation.
- Confusing the evidentiary privilege with the duty of confidentiality.
- Leaving questions blank or over-spending time on one question.

prompt: A state prosecutes a corporation's CFO for fraud. The prosecution offers, through a police detective, a statement the CEO gave to investigators: "The CFO told me to backdate the contracts." The CEO has since moved abroad and will not testify; the defense has never cross-examined the CEO. What is the best analysis?
choice: Admissible, because the CFO's own statement is an opposing party's statement.
choice*: Inadmissible, because although the CFO's words are non-hearsay against the CFO, the CEO's out-of-court statement to investigators is testimonial hearsay and the Confrontation Clause bars it without cross-examination.
choice: Admissible, because the CEO was an employee speaking about his job.
choice: Inadmissible only because the detective was not present when the CFO spoke.
hint: Analyze each layer of the statement separately, then ask whether the outer layer is testimonial.
solution: The CFO's statement is an opposing party's statement (FRE 801(d)(2)(A)), but the CEO's statement to investigators is a second layer of hearsay that must independently be admissible. It is testimonial, the declarant is unavailable, and the defendant had no prior chance to cross-examine, so the Confrontation Clause bars it (*Crawford*).

@exam self
prompt: Write an integrated answer. A federal agency whose members have statutory for-cause removal protection issues a rule under an ambiguous statute. A regulated company sues in a district court, and the court enjoins enforcement of the rule against every company in the country. The company's lawyer, wishing to present the best case, asks the company's CFO to testify that the rule would bankrupt the company, though the CFO privately tells the lawyer that the company's finances would be unaffected. Identify and analyze the constitutional and administrative-law issues, the remedy issue, and the lawyer's professional responsibility issue.
hint: Take each issue in turn: removal, interpretation of the statute, injunction scope, and candor to the tribunal.
solution: Removal: after *Trump v. Slaughter* (2026), for-cause removal protection for FTC commissioners is unconstitutional, and the Court's reasoning in *Seila Law* and *Collins* already reached single-director agencies; whether it reaches a particular multi-member agency depends on its executive power, and the Federal Reserve and non-executive-branch offices were left open. Interpretation: under *Loper Bright* the court decides the best reading of the ambiguous statute itself, giving the agency's view only *Skidmore* respect. Remedy: under *Trump v. CASA* (2025), universal injunctions likely exceed equitable authority; relief is limited to complete relief for the plaintiff, so a nationwide injunction as to every company is likely improper. Professional responsibility: the lawyer knows the proposed testimony that the rule would bankrupt the company is false, so Rule 3.3 forbids offering it; if it is offered and given, the lawyer must take reasonable remedial measures, including disclosure to the tribunal if necessary, which overrides the confidentiality duty of Rule 1.6.
rubric: States the removal-power rule accurately and without overstating it.
rubric: Applies Loper Bright's best-reading approach with Skidmore respect.
rubric: Applies the CASA complete-relief limit to the nationwide injunction.
rubric: Identifies Rule 3.3 and the remedial steps, and explains why it overrides confidentiality.
