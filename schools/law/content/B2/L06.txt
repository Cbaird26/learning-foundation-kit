@article
For every out-of-court statement ask: **Is it offered for its truth? If so, is it excluded from the definition or covered by an exception? If criminal, does the Confrontation Clause bar it?**

### Hearsay and non-hearsay
**Hearsay** is a declarant's out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted (FRE 801(c)). A statement offered for another purpose (notice, effect on the listener, verbal acts such as contract terms, state of mind circumstantial evidence) is not hearsay. Conduct is a "statement" only if intended as an assertion.

**Statements that are not hearsay (FRE 801(d))**:
- *Prior statements of a testifying witness*: an inconsistent statement made under penalty of perjury at a proceeding or deposition; a consistent statement offered to rebut a charge of recent fabrication or improper motive (or other attack, in some circumstances); an identification of a person made after perceiving them.
- *An opposing party's statement* (FRE 801(d)(2)): the party's own statement, a statement the party adopted, a statement by a person authorized to speak, a statement by an agent or employee on a matter within the scope of the relationship while it existed, and a statement by a co-conspirator during and in furtherance of the conspiracy. The statement alone does not establish the authority or the conspiracy. Personal knowledge is not required. Rule 801(d)(2) was amended effective December 1, 2024: if a party's claim, defense or potential liability is directly derived from a declarant or the declarant's principal, a statement that would be admissible against that declarant or principal under Rule 801(d)(2) is also admissible against the party.

### Exceptions
**Availability immaterial (FRE 803)**: present sense impression; excited utterance; then-existing mental, emotional or physical condition (including a state-of-mind statement of intent, as to the declarant's own later acts); statements made for medical diagnosis or treatment; recorded recollection (read, not received as an exhibit, unless offered by the adverse party); records of a regularly conducted activity (business records, made at or near the time by a person with knowledge, kept in the course of regular business, with foundation by a custodian or certification); public records; the ancient documents rule; and reliable learned treatises used with an expert.

**Declarant unavailable (FRE 804)**: former testimony (the party against whom it is offered had a similar opportunity and motive to examine); statement under belief of imminent death (in a homicide prosecution or civil case, about the cause or circumstances of death); **statement against interest** (FRE 804(b)(3)); statement of personal or family history; and **forfeiture by wrongdoing** (the party caused the declarant's unavailability). FRE 804(b)(3) was amended effective December 1, 2024: if a statement against interest is offered in a criminal case, it must be supported by corroborating circumstances that clearly indicate its trustworthiness, considering the totality of circumstances and any corroborating or contradicting evidence, whether the statement is offered against or in favor of the accused. The **residual exception** (FRE 807) permits reliable hearsay that is more probative than other evidence reasonably obtainable. Hearsay within hearsay requires an exception for each layer (FRE 805).

### Confrontation Clause
In a criminal case, the Sixth Amendment bars **testimonial** hearsay unless the declarant is unavailable and the defendant had a prior opportunity to cross-examine (*Crawford v. Washington* (2004)). A statement is testimonial if its primary purpose is to create an out-of-court substitute for trial testimony; statements to police during an ongoing emergency are not (*Davis v. Washington* (2006)). Forfeiture by wrongdoing remains an exception. *Smith v. Arizona* (2024): when a testifying expert conveys an absent analyst's statements as the basis of the expert's opinion and those statements support the opinion only if true, they are offered for their truth, so the Confrontation Clause applies if they are testimonial. A substitute expert may give an independent opinion but may not simply relay the absent analyst's testimonial findings.

### Privileges
Federal privilege law in federal-question cases is federal common law (FRE 501); in diversity cases, state law governs. **Attorney-client**: a confidential communication between client and lawyer (or their agents) made to obtain or give legal advice; the client holds it; exceptions include the crime-fraud exception and disputes between the lawyer and client. FRE 502 limits waiver by inadvertent disclosure when reasonable precautions were taken and the holder promptly acts. **Marital**: two privileges, the *spousal testimonial* privilege (the witness-spouse may refuse to testify against the spouse in a criminal case; held by the witness-spouse; applies only during marriage) and the *marital communications* privilege (confidential communications during the marriage; either spouse; survives divorce). **Physician-patient** and **psychotherapist-patient** privileges: the latter is recognized in federal court (*Jaffee v. Redmond* (1996)). The Fifth Amendment privilege protects against compelled testimonial self-incrimination.

@example
title: Excited utterance
problem: Right after a crash, a bystander shouts, "The red truck ran the light!" The bystander cannot be found. Is the statement admissible to prove the truck ran the light?
step: Classify: an out-of-court statement offered for its truth, so hearsay unless an exception applies.
step: Rule: an excited utterance relates to a startling event and is made while the declarant is under its stress (FRE 803(2)).
step: Apply: the statement was shouted immediately after a crash and described it. The declarant's unavailability does not matter under FRE 803.
step: Conclude: admissible under the excited utterance exception.

@example
title: A substitute expert
problem: At a drug trial, a lab supervisor who did not do the test testifies that, based on an absent analyst's signed lab report, the substance was heroin. The report was prepared for the prosecution, and the analyst is not called. Defense objects. What is the best analysis?
step: Identify: an expert conveying an absent analyst's statements as the basis for the opinion (*Smith v. Arizona*).
step: Rule: if the statements support the opinion only if true, they are offered for their truth, and testimonial statements trigger the Confrontation Clause.
step: Apply: the report was prepared for the prosecution (testimonial) and the opinion depends on its truth.
step: Conclude: admission violates the Confrontation Clause unless the analyst was unavailable and previously cross-examined.

@mistakes
- Treating every out-of-court statement as hearsay; check whether it is offered for its truth.
- Applying the unavailability requirement to FRE 803 exceptions.
- Forgetting that a co-conspirator's statement must be during and in furtherance of the conspiracy.
- Confusing the two marital privileges: only the testimonial one belongs to the witness-spouse alone.
