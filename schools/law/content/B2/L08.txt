These subjects reward **form and process**: did the parties follow the formalities, which court has jurisdiction, and who takes under the instrument or by statute.

### Family law
**Marriage** requires capacity, consent and compliance with the licensing and solemnization rules of the state; a marriage valid where celebrated is generally valid everywhere unless it violates a strong public policy. Same-sex marriage is protected under *Obergefell v. Hodges* (2015). **Divorce** requires domicile of one spouse in the state for the statutory period; a court with only one spouse's domicile can end the marriage (ex parte divorce) but cannot adjudicate property or support claims without personal jurisdiction over the absent spouse (*Williams v. North Carolina*; *Estin v. Estin*). Every state allows no-fault divorce. **Property** is divided by equitable distribution in most states (marital property is acquired during marriage; separate property is premarital property and gifts or inheritances) or by equal division in community property states. **Alimony** (spousal support) is modifiable on changed circumstances unless the agreement says otherwise. **Child support** follows state guidelines and is modifiable prospectively on a substantial change in circumstances; past-due installments are vested and cannot be retroactively modified. **Custody** is decided by the best interests of the child; a parent has a fundamental interest in the child's care (*Troxel v. Granville* (2000): a fit parent's decision receives deference). Jurisdiction over custody under the **UCCJEA** lies with the child's **home state** (where the child lived with a parent for six consecutive months before the case) or the state that was the home state within the prior six months if a parent still lives there; the state with initial jurisdiction keeps exclusive continuing jurisdiction. **Premarital agreements** are enforceable if voluntary and fair, with disclosure.

### Wills
A will requires a testator with capacity (understands the nature of the act, the property, the natural objects of bounty and the plan), a writing signed by the testator (or by another in the testator's presence and at the testator's direction), and attestation by **two witnesses** who watch the signing or the testator's acknowledgment, usually in a single scene (UPC: witnesses may sign within a reasonable time). Some states accept a **holographic** will (handwritten and signed), and the UPC permits harmless-error dispensation by clear and convincing evidence. **Revocation** is by a later will or codicil, physical act (burning, tearing, cancellation) with intent, or operation of law (divorce revokes provisions for the ex-spouse in most states). **Lapse**: a gift to a beneficiary who predeceases the testator fails unless an **anti-lapse** statute substitutes the beneficiary's descendants (the statute usually covers specific relatives). **Ademption by extinction** applies to a specific devise of property no longer in the estate. **Abatement** orders reduction of gifts when assets are insufficient: residuary first, then general, then specific. Will contests rest on lack of capacity, **undue influence**, fraud or improper execution. The **elective share** protects a surviving spouse from disinheritance.

### Intestacy
Intestate property passes first to the surviving spouse and descendants as the statute provides (under the UPC, the spouse takes all if there are no descendants or parents, or if all the decedent's descendants are also the spouse's descendants and the spouse has no other descendants; otherwise the spouse takes a fixed sum plus a fraction). Descendants take **by representation**: the children of a deceased child take that child's share, usually divided equally (per stirpes). Absent spouse and descendants, parents, then siblings, and so on.

### Trusts
A trust requires an **intent** to create a trust, **trust property**, a **trustee** with duties (a court will not let a trust fail for want of a trustee), ascertainable **beneficiaries** (except for valid charitable trusts), and a lawful purpose. A **revocable** trust is treated as the settlor's property while the settlor is alive. A **spendthrift** clause bars the beneficiary from assigning, and creditors from reaching, the beneficiary's interest, but does not bind claims such as child support and alimony. The trustee owes a **duty of loyalty** (no self-dealing without authorization or court approval), a **duty of prudence**, duties to inform, to account, and to **diversify** investments. The **rule against perpetuities** requires that an interest vest, if at all, within 21 years after a life in being at its creation; many states have adopted a 90-year wait-and-see rule under the USRAP, and some have abolished the rule for trusts. A **resulting trust** and a **constructive trust** are remedies preventing unjust enrichment.

@example
title: Child custody jurisdiction
problem: Child lives with both parents in State A for three years. Mother moves with the child to State B. Two months later Father, who remains in State A, files for custody in State A. Which state may make the initial custody determination?
step: Identify the statute: the UCCJEA home-state rule.
step: Rule: the home state is the state where the child lived with a parent for six consecutive months before filing; a state that was the home state within six months before filing keeps jurisdiction if a parent still lives there.
step: Apply: State A was the home state until two months ago, and Father still lives there.
step: Conclude: State A has home-state jurisdiction; State B does not.

@example
title: Execution of a will
problem: Tess signs a will before only one witness, who signs. The jurisdiction follows the traditional rule and does not permit holographic wills. Is the will valid?
step: Identify the rule: attestation by two witnesses.
step: State it: a witnessed will requires two witnesses who attest.
step: Apply: only one witness attested, and the will is not holographic.
step: Conclude: the will is not validly executed (unless a harmless-error statute applies on clear and convincing evidence).

@mistakes
- Treating an ex parte divorce as ending alimony or property rights without personal jurisdiction.
- Retroactively reducing child support arrears.
- Applying the anti-lapse statute to any beneficiary regardless of relationship, or when the will provides an alternate taker.
- Letting a spendthrift clause bar claims for child support.
