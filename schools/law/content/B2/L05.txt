@article
Evidence answers follow a fixed path: **Is it relevant? Is it excluded by a specific rule? If it is hearsay or character, does an exception apply? Does Rule 403 still allow it?** The Federal Rules of Evidence (FRE) govern; many states follow them closely.

### Relevance
Evidence is relevant (FRE 401) if it has any tendency to make a fact of consequence more or less probable. Relevant evidence is admissible unless excluded (FRE 402). Under FRE 403, a court may exclude relevant evidence if its probative value is substantially outweighed by a danger of unfair prejudice, confusing the issues, misleading the jury, undue delay or needlessly cumulative evidence. **Conditional relevance** (FRE 104(b)) turns on a foundational fact being found by a jury.

### Categorical exclusions
- **Subsequent remedial measures** (FRE 407) are not admissible to prove negligence, culpable conduct, a defect or a need for a warning; they may be admitted for other purposes such as ownership, control or feasibility if contested, or impeachment.
- **Compromise offers and negotiations** (FRE 408) are not admissible to prove or disprove the validity or amount of a disputed claim; other purposes (bias, delay) are allowed.
- **Pleas and plea discussions** (FRE 410) with a prosecutor are not admissible against the defendant.
- **Liability insurance** (FRE 411) is not admissible to prove negligence, but may be offered for ownership or control.
- **Offers to pay medical expenses** (FRE 409) are not admissible to prove liability.

### Character and other acts
Evidence of a person's character is not admissible to prove conforming conduct on a given occasion (FRE 404(a)). Exceptions: in a criminal case, the accused may offer a pertinent trait, opening the door to prosecution rebuttal; the accused may offer a pertinent trait of the **victim**; character may be proved by reputation or opinion (FRE 405(a)); specific instances are used on cross-examination and when character is an essential element of a claim or defense. Under **FRE 404(b)**, other crimes, wrongs or acts are not admissible to show propensity but may prove motive, opportunity, intent, preparation, plan, knowledge, identity, absence of mistake or lack of accident; in a criminal case the prosecutor must give the accused written notice before trial (no request is needed since the 2020 amendment). **Habit** and routine practice (FRE 406) are admissible. **FRE 412** limits evidence of a sexual-assault victim's other sexual behavior or predisposition. **FRE 413-415** allow other sexual assaults or child molestation to show propensity in the federal system.

### Witnesses and impeachment
Any party may impeach any witness (FRE 607). **FRE 608(a)**: a witness's character for truthfulness may be attacked or supported by reputation or opinion testimony, but support only after an attack. **FRE 608(b)**: specific instances of conduct probative of untruthfulness may be asked about on cross-examination but extrinsic evidence is not permitted. **FRE 609**: a conviction may be used to attack credibility: a felony conviction is admissible in a civil case subject to FRE 403 balancing; against a criminal defendant, a felony is admissible only if probative value outweighs prejudice; any crime requiring proof of a dishonest act or false statement (crimen falsi) is admitted automatically. The ten-year limit counts from conviction or release, whichever is later, and requires a heightened balancing if exceeded.

**FRE 613**: a witness may be examined about a prior statement without showing it to the witness, but must disclose it on request. **FRE 613(b)** was amended effective December 1, 2024: extrinsic evidence of a prior inconsistent statement may not be admitted until the witness has had an opportunity to explain or deny and the adverse party an opportunity to examine, unless the court orders otherwise. Other impeachment methods: bias, sensory or mental capacity, contradiction.

### Experts and aids
**FRE 702** (amended December 1, 2023) requires the proponent to demonstrate to the court that it is more likely than not that the expert is qualified, the testimony helps the trier of fact, it rests on sufficient facts or data, it is the product of reliable principles and methods, and the expert's opinion reflects a reliable application of the methods to the facts. *Daubert* (1993) and *Kumho Tire* (1999) remain the framework for the gatekeeping. New **FRE 107** (effective December 1, 2024) governs illustrative aids (charts, summaries): they may be used if their utility in helping the trier of fact understand the evidence or argument is not substantially outweighed by the danger of unfair prejudice, confusing the issues, misleading the jury, undue delay or wasting time; they are not evidence and ordinarily do not go to the jury room.

@example
title: Remedial measure
problem: After Pat slips on a store's loose step, the store repairs it. In Pat's negligence suit, Pat offers the repair to show the store knew the step was dangerous. The store admits ownership and control. Is the evidence admissible?
step: Identify the rule: FRE 407, subsequent remedial measures.
step: State it: remedial measures are not admissible to prove negligence or culpable conduct.
step: Apply: Pat offers it to show knowledge of danger, an inference of fault; ownership and control are conceded, so no other purpose exists.
step: Conclude: inadmissible.

@example
title: Impeaching with a conviction
problem: In a civil case, a defense witness was convicted four years ago of misdemeanor making a false statement on a loan application. Can the plaintiff use it to impeach?
step: Identify the rule: FRE 609(a)(2), crimes involving a dishonest act or false statement.
step: Rule: such convictions must be admitted regardless of the punishment, within ten years.
step: Apply: the offense required proof of a false statement and is within ten years.
step: Conclude: admissible; no FRE 403 balancing is permitted for crimen falsi.

@mistakes
- Using FRE 407 to exclude measures taken by a third party or to exclude evidence offered for ownership or feasibility when contested.
- Admitting extrinsic evidence of specific untruthful acts (FRE 608(b)) rather than allowing questions only.
- Applying FRE 609's balancing to crimen falsi convictions; they are automatic.
- Stating the 702 standard as "any reasonable basis"; the proponent must prove each requirement more likely than not.

@quiz mc
prompt: In a product liability case, the plaintiff's expert offers an opinion based on a new technique. The defendant objects that the method is unreliable. The plaintiff says the objection goes to weight, not admissibility. What is the best analysis?
choice: The objection is for the jury, because reliability goes to weight.
choice*: The plaintiff, as proponent, must show the court that it is more likely than not that each requirement of FRE 702 is met, including reliability.
choice: The defendant must prove the method is unreliable.
choice: The court must admit the opinion if the expert is qualified.
hint: Recall the December 2023 amendment to Rule 702.
solution: FRE 702 puts the burden on the proponent to show the court that each requirement is met by a preponderance. The sufficiency of the basis and reliability are admissibility questions, not just weight.
