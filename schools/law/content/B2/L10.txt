@article
Professional responsibility is tested separately on the MPRE and woven into other subjects on the bar. Integrated questions often combine a Model Rule with privilege (evidence), the Sixth Amendment (criminal procedure) or entity law (business associations). Identify **who the client is**, then **which duty is in play**, then **the exception, if any**.

### Confidentiality and privilege
**Model Rule 1.6** forbids revealing information relating to the representation unless the client consents or an exception applies. The lawyer **may** reveal information to the extent reasonably necessary to prevent reasonably certain death or substantial bodily harm; to prevent a client's crime or fraud that is reasonably certain to cause substantial financial injury and in which the client used the lawyer's services; to rectify such a crime or fraud already committed; to secure legal advice about compliance; to establish a claim or defense in a dispute with the client; or to comply with law or a court order. The ethical duty is much broader than the **evidentiary privilege** (confidential client communications made for legal advice), and the crime-fraud exception to the privilege is separate.

### Conflicts of interest
**Rule 1.7**: a concurrent conflict exists if the representation of one client is directly adverse to another client, or there is a significant risk that it will be materially limited by duties to another client, a former client or the lawyer's own interests. A lawyer may proceed if the lawyer reasonably believes competent and diligent representation is possible, the representation is not prohibited by law or a claim by one client against another in the same proceeding, and each affected client gives **informed consent, confirmed in writing**. **Rule 1.9** protects former clients (no materially adverse representation in the same or substantially related matter without consent). **Rule 1.10** imputes a conflict to the firm, with screening allowed in limited cases. **Rule 1.8(a)** governs business transactions with a client: fair and reasonable terms, disclosed in writing in an understandable manner, written advice of the desirability of independent counsel with a reasonable opportunity to seek it, and the client's informed consent in a signed writing. **Rule 1.8(e)** generally bars financial assistance to a client, with exceptions for court costs and expenses of litigation.

### Fees and client property
**Rule 1.5**: fees must be reasonable; a contingent fee must be in writing, signed by the client, stating the percentage, expenses, and whether expenses are deducted before or after the fee; contingent fees are barred in most domestic-relations and criminal-defense matters. **Rule 1.15**: client funds are held in a separate trust account, not commingled with the lawyer's funds; the lawyer promptly notifies and delivers funds the client is entitled to receive and keeps records; disputed portions stay in trust. **Rule 5.4**: a lawyer may not share legal fees with a nonlawyer (with narrow exceptions).

### Candor to the tribunal
**Rule 3.3**: a lawyer must not knowingly make a false statement of fact or law to the tribunal or offer evidence the lawyer knows is false. If the lawyer's client offers material false evidence and the lawyer learns of it, the lawyer must take reasonable remedial measures, including, if necessary, disclosure to the tribunal; the duty applies through the end of the proceeding and overrides Rule 1.6. If a client intends to testify falsely, the lawyer should first remonstrate with the client, must not offer testimony the lawyer knows is false (the lawyer may refuse to offer other evidence the lawyer only reasonably believes is false, but not a criminal defendant's testimony), and (for a criminal defendant, as in most states) may not simply let the client present false testimony in narrative form. A lawyer may allow a criminal defendant to testify if the lawyer only reasonably believes (but does not know) the testimony is false. **Rule 3.4**: no obstructing access to evidence or paying for testimony beyond permitted expenses. **Rule 3.8**: a prosecutor must have probable cause for charges and disclose exculpatory evidence.

### Entity clients and supervision
**Rule 1.13**: the lawyer for an organization represents the organization, not its constituents. If the lawyer knows an officer is violating a duty to the organization or law likely to cause it substantial injury, the lawyer must proceed as is reasonably necessary in the organization's best interest, including referring the matter to higher authority, up to the board; the lawyer may reveal information outside the organization only in limited circumstances. **Upjohn Co. v. United States** (1981) confirms that the corporation's privilege covers employees' communications with corporate counsel made at the direction of management to secure legal advice. **Rules 5.1 and 5.3** require supervisory lawyers to make reasonable efforts to ensure compliance. **Rule 7.3** restricts live solicitation; **Rule 5.5** bars unauthorized practice.

### Integrated issues
- The **Sixth Amendment** right to effective counsel (*Strickland*) is a constitutional standard; a Model Rule violation is not by itself ineffective assistance.
- A **conflict** of interest can also be a Sixth Amendment issue in a criminal case if it adversely affects the representation.
- Evidence from a lawyer's client file may be protected by the **privilege** (evidence law), the **work-product doctrine** (procedure), and the **duty of confidentiality** (ethics), each with different exceptions.

@example
title: False testimony in a criminal trial
problem: During a criminal trial, defense counsel learns, from the client's admission, that the client plans to testify falsely about an alibi. Counsel has urged the client not to do so. What must counsel do?
step: Identify the rule: Model Rule 3.3 (candor to the tribunal) governs, overriding confidentiality.
step: Counsel knows the testimony would be false, so counsel first remonstrates with the client (done) and must not offer it.
step: If the client testifies falsely anyway, counsel must take reasonable remedial measures, including disclosure to the tribunal if necessary.
step: Conclude: counsel may not present the testimony and, if it is given, must take remedial steps.

@example
title: Net recovery under a contingent fee
problem: A client's written agreement provides a contingent fee of 30 percent of the gross recovery, with expenses of \$6,000 deducted from the client's share after the fee. The recovery is \$240,000. What does the client receive?
step: Compute the fee: $0.30 \times 240{,}000 = 72{,}000$.
step: Subtract the fee and expenses: $240{,}000 - 72{,}000 - 6{,}000$.
step: The client receives \$162,000.

@mistakes
- Treating the ethical duty of confidentiality and the evidentiary privilege as the same rule.
- Letting a criminal defendant give false testimony in narrative form; Rule 3.3 requires remedial measures.
- Treating the corporation's officers as the lawyer's clients under Rule 1.13.
- Placing client funds in the lawyer's operating account because the lawyer expects to earn them.

@practice self
prompt: Supplied rule text (summary written for this course). Rule A: A lawyer shall not make a false statement of fact to a tribunal and shall not offer evidence the lawyer knows to be false; if the lawyer has offered material evidence and later learns it is false, the lawyer shall take reasonable remedial measures, including, if necessary, disclosure to the tribunal. Rule B: A lawyer shall not reveal information relating to the representation unless an exception applies. Facts: In a civil trial, Lawyer calls her client, Ann, who testifies that she never signed a contract. After the lunch break Ann tells Lawyer privately that she did sign. Using only the supplied rules, explain what Lawyer must do.
hint: Decide which rule controls when the two conflict.
solution: Lawyer offered Ann's testimony, which she now knows is false and material. Rule A requires reasonable remedial measures. Lawyer should first urge Ann to correct the testimony; if Ann refuses, Lawyer must take further measures, including disclosure to the tribunal if necessary. Rule B's confidentiality duty yields because Rule A expressly requires disclosure when necessary.

@quiz ms
prompt: Under the Model Rules, in which of the following situations may a lawyer reveal information relating to the representation without the client's consent?
choice*: To prevent reasonably certain death or substantial bodily harm.
choice*: To establish a claim or defense in a fee dispute with the client.
choice: Because the lawyer finds the client's conduct morally offensive.
choice*: To prevent a client's crime or fraud that is reasonably certain to cause substantial financial injury, if the client used the lawyer's services.
choice: To tell the lawyer's spouse about a client's embarrassing facts.
hint: Recall the Rule 1.6(b) exceptions.
solution: The listed exceptions in Rule 1.6(b) cover bodily harm, fee disputes and crime or fraud using the lawyer's services. Personal discomfort or gossip is not an exception.

choice: Permissible, because the interest rate was disclosed.
choice*: Impermissible, because Lawyer did not advise the client in writing of the desirability of independent counsel and give a reasonable opportunity to obtain it, as Rule 1.8(a) requires.
