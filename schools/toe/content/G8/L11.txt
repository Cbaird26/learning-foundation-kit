objective: Report values with uncertainties and consistent significant figures, and write figure captions that stand alone.

@article
A draft report is the first complete version, not the first polished one. Its job is to put every claim next to the evidence for it, so that a reader (and, next week, a critic) can check it.

### Structure

1. **Abstract** (at most 150 words): the question, the method, the main numerical results with uncertainties, one limitation.
2. **Question and background**, with your three sources.
3. **Baseline**: assumptions, equations, units and the reproduction result.
4. **Methods**: enough to repeat the work, pointing to the reproducibility package.
5. **Results**: tables and figures for the baseline, the extension, the sensitivity analysis and the null and limit tests.
6. **Claim audit** (below).
7. **Limitations and open issues.**
8. **Reproducibility statement**: code, data, versions, seeds and the regeneration command.

Write the results first, the abstract last.

### Numbers

Round the uncertainty to one or two significant figures, then round the value to the same decimal place: $9.8696 \pm 0.0441\ \text{m/s}^2$ is reported as $9.87 \pm 0.04\ \text{m/s}^2$. Say what the $\pm$ means (here, one standard uncertainty), give units on every number, and keep the same digits in tables and text.

For a fit with $N$ points and $p$ parameters, the reduced chi-squared is $\chi^2_\nu = \chi^2/(N - p)$, where $\chi^2 = \sum_i (r_i/\sigma_i)^2$ and $r_i$ is the residual. Values near 1 suggest that the model and the uncertainties are consistent. Values much above 1 point to a wrong model or underestimated uncertainties; values much below 1 point to overestimated uncertainties or overfitting.

### Figures

Label each axis with the quantity and unit, define the error bars, draw model curves differently from data, and write a caption that says what is plotted and what to notice. The script that makes each figure belongs in the package.

### The claim audit

Make a table with one row per claim:

| claim | evidence | conditions | strength | alternatives | what would refute it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

A claim with no evidence is deleted or marked as conjecture. Keep three kinds of statement apart: an **observation** ("the RK4 period matches $4K(k)$ to $6\times10^{-7}$"), an **inference** ("so the code solves the equation correctly in this case"), and a **speculation** ("this suggests the same holds for ...").

### Calibrated language

Match the verb to the strength. "Proves" is almost never justified outside mathematics. "Demonstrates" needs strong, direct evidence. "Is consistent with" means the data do not exclude it. "Suggests" signals weak evidence. "We could not" reports a failure. Hedging that is not backed by a number is not calibration either: say how large the effect is and how well it is known.

@example
title: Auditing one overclaimed sentence
problem: A draft says: "Our simulation proves that the period of a pendulum increases by 1.74% at 30 degrees." Audit and rewrite it.
step: Identify the evidence: one RK4 run at one step size, no uncertainty, and no mention of an independent check. "Proves" is not justified by a single numerical run.
step: Identify the missing conditions: a rigid planar pendulum, no friction, a point bob and release from rest. These belong in the sentence, or in the sentence before it.
step: Match the strength to the evidence. The project has a step-halving study with observed order 4 and an AGM check, so the claim is a numerical demonstration within the model, to a stated tolerance.
step: Rewrite: "For a rigid planar pendulum released from rest at $30^\circ$, RK4 integration converged to a relative $10^{-6}$ gives a period $1.741\%$ longer than $2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$, in agreement with the elliptic-integral value to $10^{-6}$."
step: Add the refutation line to the audit table: "a converged period differing from $4\sqrt{L/g}\,K(\sin 15^\circ)$ by more than $10^{-6}$ relative would refute this."

@mistakes
- Quoting more digits than the uncertainty supports, or fewer digits in the value than in the uncertainty.
- Using "proves", "confirms" or "obviously" where the evidence only shows consistency.
- Presenting a figure without error bars, units or a statement of what the error bars mean.
- Leaving the limitations to the last paragraph, written in a hurry, instead of building them from the claim audit.

@practice mc
prompt: A pendulum period at $30^\circ$ was computed with RK4 at three step sizes (observed order 4) and checked against the elliptic-integral value and an independent AGM evaluation, with agreement to a relative $10^{-6}$. Which sentence is best calibrated to this evidence?
choice: "Our simulation proves the period grows by 1.74% at $30^\circ$."
choice*: "For a rigid planar pendulum released from rest at $30^\circ$, RK4 integration at three step sizes converges to a period 1.741% longer than the small-angle value, in agreement with the elliptic-integral result to a relative $10^{-6}$."
choice: "The simulation suggests that the period may possibly increase slightly with amplitude."
choice: "It is well known that the period grows by about 1.74% at $30^\circ$."
hint: Look for the sentence that states the conditions, the number and the level of agreement.
solution: The sentence about RK4 at three step sizes gives the conditions, the method, the size of the effect and the tolerance of agreement. The one that says "proves" overclaims, the one that says "may possibly increase slightly" is vague, and the "well known" one gives no evidence.

@practice num
prompt: A fit with two parameters to five points has residuals, in units of the standard uncertainty, of $0.5$, $-1.2$, $0.8$, $-0.3$ and $1.0$. What is the reduced chi-squared $\chi^2/(N - p)$? Round to two decimal places.
answer: 1.14
check: sum(r**2 for r in [0.5, -1.2, 0.8, -0.3, 1.0])/(5 - 2)
tol: 0.01
hint: $\chi^2$ is the sum of the squared residuals when they are in units of $\sigma$.
hint: Divide by the number of degrees of freedom, $N - p = 3$.
solution: $\chi^2 = 3.42$ and $N - p = 3$, so $\chi^2_\nu = 1.14$. This is close to 1, so the model and the uncertainties are consistent.

@practice mc
prompt: A fit gives $g = 9.8696$ with a standard uncertainty of $0.0441\ \text{m/s}^2$. Which is the best way to report it?
choice: $9.8696 \pm 0.0441\ \text{m/s}^2$
choice*: $9.87 \pm 0.04\ \text{m/s}^2$
choice: $9.9 \pm 0.04\ \text{m/s}^2$
choice: $9.87 \pm 0.4\ \text{m/s}^2$
hint: Round the uncertainty to one or two significant figures, then match the decimal place of the value.
solution: The uncertainty rounds to $0.04$, so the value is quoted to the hundredths place: $9.87 \pm 0.04$. Quoting $9.8696 \pm 0.0441$ shows digits that the uncertainty makes meaningless, $9.9 \pm 0.04$ rounds the value more coarsely than the uncertainty, and $9.87 \pm 0.4$ has the wrong power of ten.

@practice self
prompt: Rewrite this paragraph from a draft so that every claim is calibrated and tied to evidence. Add the conditions, a number with an uncertainty or tolerance where you can, and one limitation. Paragraph: "We have proven that our new correction is exactly right. The simulation matches theory perfectly, so the old formula is wrong. Everyone should now use the new method. Numerical errors are negligible."
hint: For each sentence, ask what evidence would support it and how strong that evidence is.
hint: "Perfectly", "exactly" and "negligible" each need a number.
solution: Model answer. "For a rigid planar pendulum without friction, adding the finite-bob correction to the baseline changes the small-angle period by $+0.20\%$ for $r/L = 0.1$, as predicted to first order. The RK4 results agree with the analytic prediction to a relative $10^{-6}$ (observed convergence order 4.0, checked against an independent AGM evaluation), so the correction is implemented correctly in the model. This does not show that the earlier point-mass formula is wrong in general: it is a good approximation when $r/L \ll 1$, and its error here is the 0.20% shift. The results have been tested only for $r/L = 0.1$ and for amplitudes up to $60^\circ$, with no friction or drag; the numerical error (below $10^{-6}$) is much smaller than the effect, but experimental validation is outstanding."
rubric: Replaces "proven", "exactly" and "perfectly" with stated agreement and a tolerance.
rubric: Does not claim that the old formula is wrong beyond what the evidence shows, and states when it remains adequate.
rubric: Gives the conditions and range that were actually tested.
rubric: States at least one limitation, such as what was not validated.

@quiz num
prompt: Two independent determinations of $g$ give $9.78 \pm 0.04$ and $9.84 \pm 0.03\ \text{m/s}^2$. Combine them with the inverse-variance weighted mean. Give the result in $\text{m/s}^2$ to three decimal places.
answer: 9.818
check: (9.78/0.04**2 + 9.84/0.03**2)/(1/0.04**2 + 1/0.03**2)
tol: 0.002
hint: The weights are $w_i = 1/\sigma_i^2$, and the mean is $\sum w_i x_i/\sum w_i$.
hint: $w_1 = 625$ and $w_2 = 1111.1$.
solution: $\bar{x} = (625 \times 9.78 + 1111.1 \times 9.84)/1736.1 = 9.818$. The combined standard uncertainty is $1/\sqrt{1736.1} = 0.024$, so the result is $9.82 \pm 0.02\ \text{m/s}^2$.

@quiz self
prompt: Write an abstract of at most 120 words for a report on the amplitude dependence of the pendulum period, using only these supplied results. RK4 period at $60^\circ$ matches $4K(0.5) = 6.743001$ (for $g/L = 1$) to a relative $6\times10^{-7}$, with observed order 4.0; an AGM evaluation of $K$ agrees to $10^{-6}$. The exact period exceeds $2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$ by 1% at an amplitude of $22.8^\circ$. The two-term series $1 + \theta_0^2/16$ is within 0.1% of the exact period up to about $41^\circ$ and fails at $45^\circ$ (0.14% error). Not tested: friction, finite bob size, experiments.
hint: Use the order: question, method, results with numbers, limitation.
solution: Model answer. "We ask at what amplitude the small-angle period of a rigid planar pendulum fails by 1%, and how far a two-term series extends the validity. We integrate the equation of motion with fourth-order Runge–Kutta and check it against the exact elliptic-integral period and an independent arithmetic–geometric-mean evaluation. At $60^\circ$ the integration reproduces the exact period to a relative $6\times10^{-7}$ with convergence order 4.0. The exact period exceeds $2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$ by 1% at $22.8^\circ$. The two-term series $1 + \theta_0^2/16$ stays within 0.1% up to about $41^\circ$ and fails at $45^\circ$ (0.14% error). Friction, finite bob size and experiments were not studied." (about 105 words)
rubric: Gives the question, the method and the main numerical results with tolerances.
rubric: Uses calibrated verbs and no overclaim such as "proves".
rubric: States what was not tested.
rubric: Stays within the word limit and contains only the supplied results.
