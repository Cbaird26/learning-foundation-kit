@article
The symmetries of a square move its corners around. Each symmetry shuffles the four corners, and doing two symmetries in a row shuffles them by the combined symmetry. That is a **group action**: a group acting on a set.

### Definition

A (left) **action** of $G$ on a set $X$ assigns to each $g\in G$ and $x\in X$ a point $g\cdot x\in X$ such that
$$e\cdot x=x,\qquad (gh)\cdot x=g\cdot(h\cdot x).$$
Each $g$ then acts as a bijection of $X$, so an action is the same as a homomorphism from $G$ into the group of bijections of $X$.

### Orbits and stabilizers

- The **orbit** of $x$ is $Gx=\{g\cdot x: g\in G\}$. Orbits partition $X$: $y=g\cdot x$ is an equivalence relation.
- The **stabilizer** of $x$ is $G_x=\{g\in G: g\cdot x=x\}$, a subgroup of $G$.

**Orbit-stabilizer theorem.** The map $gG_x\mapsto g\cdot x$ is a well-defined bijection from the left cosets of $G_x$ onto $Gx$, because
$$g\cdot x=h\cdot x\iff h^{-1}g\in G_x\iff gG_x=hG_x.$$
Hence $|Gx|=[G:G_x]$, and for finite $G$
$$|G|=|Gx|\cdot|G_x|.$$
Points in the same orbit have conjugate stabilizers: $G_{g\cdot x}=gG_xg^{-1}$.

### Examples

- The symmetries of a square (a group of order $8$, written $D_4$) act on the $4$ corners. One orbit of size $4$; each stabilizer has order $8/4=2$.
- Rotations of the plane act on points: the orbits are circles about the origin, and the stabilizer of any point other than the origin is trivial.
- A group acts on itself by **conjugation**, $g\cdot x=gxg^{-1}$. Orbits are conjugacy classes and the stabilizer of $x$ is its **centralizer** $C_G(x)=\{g: gx=xg\}$. So the class of $x$ has $|G|/|C_G(x)|$ elements.

In physics terms, the orbit of a state is the set of states related to it by symmetry, and the stabilizer is the symmetry the state still has. A magnetization pointing along one axis is fixed by the rotations about that axis only: the symmetry has been reduced to the stabilizer.

### Burnside's lemma

The number of orbits equals the average number of fixed points:
$$\#\text{orbits}=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}|X^g|,\qquad X^g=\{x: g\cdot x=x\}.$$

@example
title: An action of $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$ on $\mathbb{Z}_{18}$
problem: Let $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$ act on $X=\mathbb{Z}_{18}$ by $g\cdot x=x+3g \pmod{18}$. Find the orbit and stabilizer of $0$, and count the orbits.
step: This is an action: $0\cdot x=x$, and $g\cdot(h\cdot x)=x+3h+3g=(g+h)\cdot x$. It is well defined because $3\cdot12=36\equiv0\pmod{18}$.
step: The orbit of $0$ is $\{3g \bmod 18\}=\{0,3,6,9,12,15\}$, of size $6$.
step: The stabilizer is $\{g: 3g\equiv0\pmod{18}\}=\{0,6\}$, of order $2$. Check: $|G|=12=6\cdot2$.
step: All orbits have size $6$ (they are the sets $x+\{0,3,\dots,15\}$), so there are $18/6=3$ orbits. Burnside agrees: only $g=0,6$ fix points, each fixing all $18$, so $(18+18)/12=3$.

@example
title: Counting colorings with Burnside
problem: Color the three corners of a triangle red or blue. Two colorings are the same if one is a rotation of the other. How many distinct colorings are there?
step: The group is the rotations $\{e,r,r^2\}$ of order $3$, acting on the $2^3=8$ colorings.
step: The identity fixes all $8$ colorings.
step: A nontrivial rotation cycles all three corners, so a fixed coloring must have all corners the same color: it fixes $2$ colorings.
step: Burnside: $(8+2+2)/3=4$ distinct colorings (all red, all blue, one blue corner, one red corner).

@mistakes
- Using $|G|=|Gx|\,|G_x|$ for an infinite group, or confusing the stabilizer of a point with the set of points fixed by the whole group.
- Believing all points have the same stabilizer. Stabilizers along an orbit are conjugate, which is equal only for special actions.
- Writing the action axiom as $(gh)\cdot x=(g\cdot x)(h\cdot x)$; the correct rule is $(gh)\cdot x=g\cdot(h\cdot x)$.

answer: 2
check: len([1 for s in (1,-1) for k in range(4) if (s*0+k)%4==0])
hint: The stabilizer of $0$ consists of the maps with $s\cdot0+k\equiv0\pmod 4$.
hint: What does this force on $k$, and how many choices of $s$ remain?
solution: $s\cdot 0+k\equiv0$ forces $k=0$, and both $s=1$ and $s=-1$ are allowed. So the stabilizer is $\{i\mapsto i,\ i\mapsto -i\}$ of order $2$, consistent with $8/4=2$.

@practice self
prompt: Let a finite group $G$ act on a set $X$ and let $x\in X$. Prove the orbit-stabilizer theorem: $|Gx|=[G:G_x]$.
hint: Define $f(gG_x)=g\cdot x$ from the left cosets of $G_x$ to the orbit.
hint: Show $f$ is well defined and injective using $g\cdot x=h\cdot x\iff h^{-1}g\in G_x$, and note it is surjective by definition of the orbit.
solution: Let $f(gG_x)=g\cdot x$. For $g,h\in G$: $g\cdot x=h\cdot x\iff x=(g^{-1}h)\cdot x\iff g^{-1}h\in G_x\iff gG_x=hG_x$. The direction ($\Leftarrow$) shows $f$ is well defined; the direction ($\Rightarrow$) shows $f$ is injective. Every point of $Gx$ has the form $g\cdot x=f(gG_x)$, so $f$ is surjective. Thus $f$ is a bijection between the left cosets of $G_x$ and $Gx$, giving $|Gx|=[G:G_x]$.
rubric: Defines the map on cosets and addresses well-definedness.
rubric: Proves injectivity via $g\cdot x=h\cdot x\Rightarrow g^{-1}h\in G_x$.
rubric: Notes surjectivity and concludes the counting statement.

@quiz num
prompt: Color the four corners of a square red or blue. Two colorings are considered the same if one is a rotation (by a multiple of $90^\circ$) of the other. How many distinct colorings are there?
answer: 6
check: len({min(sum(c[(i+k)%4]*2**i for i in range(4)) for k in range(4)) for c in [(a,b,c0,d) for a in (0,1) for b in (0,1) for c0 in (0,1) for d in (0,1)]})
hint: Use Burnside with the rotation group of order $4$ acting on the $2^4=16$ colorings.
hint: The rotation by $90^\circ$ or $270^\circ$ fixes only the colorings with all corners equal; the rotation by $180^\circ$ fixes colorings where opposite corners agree.
solution: Fixed colorings: identity $16$; rotations by $90^\circ$ and $270^\circ$: $2$ each; rotation by $180^\circ$: $2^2=4$. Burnside: $(16+2+4+2)/4=6$.

@quiz self
prompt: Let $G$ act on $X$, let $x\in X$ and $g\in G$. Prove that $G_{g\cdot x}=gG_xg^{-1}$.
hint: Start with $h\in G_{g\cdot x}$, which means $h\cdot(g\cdot x)=g\cdot x$, and apply $g^{-1}$ to both sides.
hint: Each step should be an "if and only if", so both inclusions follow at once.
solution: For $h\in G$: $h\in G_{g\cdot x}\iff h\cdot(g\cdot x)=g\cdot x\iff (g^{-1}hg)\cdot x=x\iff g^{-1}hg\in G_x\iff h\in gG_xg^{-1}$. The second step applies $g^{-1}$ to both sides and uses the action axioms.
rubric: Translates membership in $G_{g\cdot x}$ into a statement about $g^{-1}hg$ acting on $x$ using the action axioms.
rubric: Obtains $g^{-1}hg\in G_x$ and rearranges to $h\in gG_xg^{-1}$.
rubric: Makes every step reversible (or proves both inclusions).
