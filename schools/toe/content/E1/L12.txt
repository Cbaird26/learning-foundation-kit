@article
Near the identity, a continuous group looks like a flat vector space. A tiny rotation by angle $\epsilon$ about the $z$-axis is $I+\epsilon L_z$ plus terms of order $\epsilon^2$. The matrix $L_z$ is a **generator**. The vector space of generators, with the commutator, is the **Lie algebra** and carries most of the information about the group.

### Definition

The **Lie algebra** of a matrix Lie group $G$ is
$$\mathfrak{g}=\{X: e^{tX}\in G\text{ for all real }t\},\qquad e^{X}=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{X^k}{k!}.$$
Equivalently, $\mathfrak{g}$ consists of the velocity vectors $\frac{d}{dt}g(t)\big|_{t=0}$ of smooth curves in $G$ through $I$.

To find $\mathfrak{g}$, differentiate the defining equation at $I$:

- $O(n)$, $SO(n)$: $A^TA=I$ gives $X^T+X=0$. So $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ is the set of **antisymmetric** real matrices, of dimension $n(n-1)/2$.
- $SL_n$: $\det e^{tX}=e^{t\operatorname{tr}X}=1$ gives $\operatorname{tr}X=0$.
- $U(n)$: $U^\dagger U=I$ gives $X^\dagger=-X$ (anti-Hermitian). $\mathfrak{su}(n)$ adds $\operatorname{tr}X=0$.

As a check, $\dim\mathfrak{so}(3)=3\cdot2/2=3$ (the three generators below) and $\dim\mathfrak{su}(2)=2^2-1=3$: the Lie algebra has the same dimension as its group.

### The bracket

The **commutator** $[X,Y]=XY-YX$ makes $\mathfrak{g}$ a Lie algebra:

- it is bilinear and antisymmetric: $[X,Y]=-[Y,X]$ (so $[X,X]=0$);
- the **Jacobi identity**: $[X,[Y,Z]]+[Y,[Z,X]]+[Z,[X,Y]]=0$;
- $\mathfrak{g}$ is closed: $X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}\Rightarrow[X,Y]\in\mathfrak{g}$.

If $G$ is abelian all brackets vanish. In general the bracket measures how far two generators are from commuting: $e^{sX}e^{tY}e^{-sX}e^{-tY}=I+st[X,Y]+\cdots$.

### Generators of rotations

$$L_x=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&-1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad L_y=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&0&0\\-1&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad L_z=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.$$
They are the derivatives at $\theta=0$ of the rotations about the three axes, and $R_k(\theta)=e^{\theta L_k}$. Their commutators are
$$[L_x,L_y]=L_z,\quad[L_y,L_z]=L_x,\quad[L_z,L_x]=L_y,$$
that is $[L_i,L_j]=\sum_k\epsilon_{ijk}L_k$ with the Levi-Civita symbol $\epsilon_{ijk}$.

### Quantum angular momentum

The rotation operator is $e^{-i\theta J_z/\hbar}$, so the Hermitian angular momentum operators are $J_k=i\hbar L_k$ in this representation. Then
$$[J_x,J_y]=(i\hbar)^2[L_x,L_y]=-\hbar^2L_z=i\hbar J_z.$$
The Pauli matrices satisfy $[\sigma_x,\sigma_y]=2i\sigma_z$ (and cyclically), so $T_k=-\tfrac i2\sigma_k$ obey $[T_x,T_y]=T_z$: $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ and $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ have the same bracket relations. The groups differ, though: $e^{2\pi T_z}=-I$ in $SU(2)$, whereas a full turn is the identity in $SO(3)$. In quantum mechanics, non-commuting generators correspond to observables that in general cannot have sharp values at the same time.

@example
title: Computing a commutator of rotation generators
problem: Verify that $[L_x,L_y]=L_z$.
step: Multiply: $L_xL_y=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}$. (Row 2 of $L_x$ is $(0,0,-1)$, which picks out minus row 3 of $L_y$.)
step: Multiply in the other order: $L_yL_x=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}$.
step: Subtract: $[L_x,L_y]=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}=L_z$.

@example
title: Exponentiating a generator
problem: Show that $e^{\theta L_z}$ is the rotation by $\theta$ about the $z$-axis.
step: Compute $L_z^2=\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,0)$ and $L_z^3=-L_z$.
step: The powers repeat: $L_z^{2m+1}=(-1)^mL_z$ and $L_z^{2m}=(-1)^{m-1}L_z^2$ for $m\ge1$. Group the series: $e^{\theta L_z}=I+L_z\left(\theta-\tfrac{\theta^3}{3!}+\cdots\right)+L_z^2\left(\tfrac{\theta^2}{2!}-\tfrac{\theta^4}{4!}+\cdots\right)$.
step: The brackets are $\sin\theta$ and $1-\cos\theta$, so $e^{\theta L_z}=I+\sin\theta\,L_z+(1-\cos\theta)L_z^2$.
step: Entry by entry this is $\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta&0\\ \sin\theta&\cos\theta&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}=R_z(\theta)$.

@mistakes
- Treating the Lie algebra as a group: $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ consists of antisymmetric matrices, which are not rotations (they are not even invertible in $3$ dimensions).
- Using the anticommutator $XY+YX$ instead of the commutator $XY-YX$.
- Mixing conventions: with $L_k$ real antisymmetric, $[L_x,L_y]=L_z$; with Hermitian $J_k=i\hbar L_k$, the relation is $[J_x,J_y]=i\hbar J_z$.
