title: Perturbation theory versus exact diagonalization for a weakly coupled model
overview: You will study the anharmonic oscillator $H = \frac{p^2}{2} + \frac{x^2}{2} + \lambda x^4$ (units $\hbar = m = \omega = 1$) in two ways: Rayleigh-Schrödinger perturbation theory in $\lambda$, and numerical diagonalization of $H$ in a truncated oscillator basis. Comparing them lets you quantify the error of the perturbative approximation, locate its regime of validity, and test it against a variational bound and an exactly solvable degenerate case.
goal: Build $H$ as a converged matrix in the oscillator basis and extract accurate low-lying energies as the benchmark.
goal: Compute first- and second-order perturbative energies for several levels from matrix elements, and check them against known analytic coefficients.
goal: Quantify how the error scales with $\lambda$ and state the regime in which each order of perturbation theory is trustworthy.
goal: Check the results with a variational bound and with a degenerate perturbation calculation that has an exact answer.
step: Construct $a$ and $a^\dagger$ as matrices and $x = (a + a^\dagger)/\sqrt2$. To avoid truncation artifacts, build $x$ with $N + 4$ states, form $x^4$, and keep the upper-left $N\times N$ block. Assemble $H(\lambda) = H_0 + \lambda x^4$ with $H_0 = \text{diag}(n + \frac12)$.
step: Test convergence: for $\lambda = 0.1$ and $\lambda = 1$, tabulate the three lowest eigenvalues for $N = 10, 20, 40, 80, 160$ and choose an $N$ at which they agree to $10^{-8}$. Use this as the exact reference.
step: Compute $E_n^{(1)} = \langle n|x^4|n\rangle$ and $E_n^{(2)} = \sum_{m\ne n}|\langle m|x^4|n\rangle|^2/(E_n^{(0)} - E_m^{(0)})$ for $n = 0, 1, 2$ directly from the matrix. For $n = 0$ check that you get $\frac34$ and $-\frac{21}{8}$. Note which matrix elements vanish by parity.
step: For $\lambda$ from $10^{-3}$ to $1$ (log-spaced), compute the absolute errors of the zeroth-plus-first-order and zeroth-plus-first-plus-second-order estimates against the reference. Plot them on log-log axes and fit the slopes; explain why they are close to $2$ and $3$.
step: Define the validity regime by the largest $\lambda$ for which the relative error of the second-order estimate stays below $1\%$ and below $5\%$, for each $n$. Explain why higher levels fail at smaller $\lambda$ using the condition $|V_{mn}| \ll |E_n^{(0)} - E_m^{(0)}|$ and the growth of $\langle x^4\rangle$ with $n$.
step: Variational check: for the Gaussian trial family $e^{-\alpha x^2}$ the energy is $E(\alpha) = \frac{\alpha}{2} + \frac{1}{8\alpha} + \frac{3\lambda}{16\alpha^2}$. Minimize it numerically for each $\lambda$, confirm it never falls below the exact ground energy, and compare its error with second-order perturbation theory.
step: Degenerate extension: for two coupled oscillators $H = H_0^{(1)} + H_0^{(2)} + \lambda x_1x_2$, apply degenerate perturbation theory to the two-fold level $E^{(0)} = 2$, and compare the predicted splitting with numerical diagonalization and with the exact normal-mode result $\omega_\pm = \sqrt{1 \pm \lambda}$.
step: Write up the results: state units and conventions, include the convergence table, the error plots and the validity regime, and say which assumptions each method needs.

@review
### Weeks 1-2: states and bases

Kets and bras (the bra is the conjugate transpose), completeness $\sum_n|n\rangle\langle n| = 1$, amplitudes $c_n = \langle n|\psi\rangle$, probabilities $|c_n|^2$, expectation values $\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle$. A change of basis is $\tilde c = U^\dagger c$, $\tilde A = U^\dagger AU$, with $U_{nm} = \langle n|\tilde m\rangle$. Eigenvalues, trace and expectation values do not change; matrix elements do.

### Weeks 3-4: spin and angular momentum

$S_k = \frac{\hbar}{2}\sigma_k$; the up state along $\hat n$ is $\cos\frac\theta2|\uparrow\rangle + e^{i\phi}\sin\frac\theta2|\downarrow\rangle$; probabilities between axes are $\cos^2(\gamma/2)$; rotations are $e^{-i\alpha\hat n\cdot\vec S/\hbar}$. Adding momenta gives $j = |j_1 - j_2|, \dots, j_1 + j_2$; two spin-1/2 give a triplet and a singlet, with $\vec S_1\cdot\vec S_2 = \frac{\hbar^2}{2}[S(S+1) - \frac32]$.

### Weeks 5-6: stationary perturbation theory

$E_n^{(1)} = V_{nn}$ and $E_n^{(2)} = \sum_{m\ne n}|V_{mn}|^2/(E_n^{(0)} - E_m^{(0)})$, valid when couplings are small compared with gaps. For a degenerate level, diagonalize $W_{ab} = \langle a|V|b\rangle$: its eigenvalues are the first-order shifts and its eigenvectors the good states.

### Weeks 7-8: variational method and WKB

$\langle H\rangle \ge E_0$ for any normalized, admissible trial state; optimize its parameters. WKB: $p = \sqrt{2m(E - V)}$ with $\int p\,dx = (n + \frac12)\pi\hbar$ (two soft turning points), $(n + \frac34)\pi\hbar$ (one hard wall), $n\pi\hbar$ (two hard walls); tunneling $T \approx e^{-2\gamma}$.

### Weeks 9-10: time dependence

$c_m^{(1)} = \frac{1}{i\hbar}\int V_{mi}e^{i\omega_{mi}t'}dt'$; a resonant drive gives $P \propto \sin^2(\Delta t/2)/\Delta^2$; sudden and adiabatic limits; golden rule $\Gamma = \frac{2\pi}{\hbar}|V_{fi}|^2\rho$ for $\hbar/\delta E \ll t \ll 1/\Gamma$; dipole selection rule $\Delta l = \pm1$; Rabi oscillation $\sin^2(\Omega t/2)$.

### Weeks 11-12: identical particles and scattering

Bosons are symmetric, fermions antisymmetric under exchange of the total state; use Slater determinants and pair spatial and spin symmetry. Scattering: $d\sigma/d\Omega = |f|^2$; Born amplitude $f = -\frac{m}{2\pi\hbar^2}\int e^{-i\vec q\cdot\vec r}V\,d^3r$; identical particles use $f(\theta) \pm f(\pi - \theta)$.

### Self-check

- Can you express one state and one operator in two bases and show $\langle A\rangle$ is unchanged?
- Can you find spin and total-spin probabilities?
- Can you choose between the degenerate and nondegenerate recipe and state why?
- Can you state what a variational bound assumes, and which WKB condition fits which walls?
- Can you name the time window for the golden rule?
- Can you build and normalize a symmetrized or antisymmetrized state?

### Common errors

- Missing complex conjugates in bras; confusing amplitudes with probabilities.
- Denominator signs and the $m = n$ term in perturbation sums; using nondegenerate formulas for degenerate levels.
- Unnormalized or inadmissible trial states; calling an estimate a bound.
- Using $n + \frac12$ for every WKB problem.
- Trusting first-order results when the probability or the correction is not small.

@exam self
prompt: For the anharmonic oscillator $H = \frac{p^2}{2} + \frac{x^2}{2} + \lambda x^4$ (units $\hbar = m = \omega = 1$), compare three descriptions of the ground-state energy. (a) Using the ladder-operator matrix elements of $x^4$ from $|0\rangle$, find the second-order perturbative energy. (b) Using the normalized Gaussian trial family $e^{-\alpha x^2}$ with $E(\alpha) = \frac{\alpha}{2} + \frac{1}{8\alpha} + \frac{3\lambda}{16\alpha^2}$, find the variational energy to second order in small $\lambda$, and the optimized value at $\lambda = 1$. (c) Explain which result is a rigorous bound, which is more reliable for small $\lambda$ and for $\lambda = 1$, and what other check you would use.
solution: (a) With $x = (a + a^\dagger)/\sqrt2$, the nonzero matrix elements from $|0\rangle$ are $\langle0|x^4|0\rangle = \frac34$, $\langle2|x^4|0\rangle = \frac{3\sqrt2}{2}$ and $\langle4|x^4|0\rangle = \frac{\sqrt6}{2}$. So $E_0 \approx \frac12 + \frac34\lambda + \lambda^2\left[\frac{9/2}{-2} + \frac{3/2}{-4}\right] = \frac12 + \frac34\lambda - \frac{21}{8}\lambda^2$. (b) Put $\alpha = \frac12 + \delta$: $\frac\alpha2 + \frac{1}{8\alpha} = \frac12 + \delta^2 + \cdots$ and $\frac{3\lambda}{16\alpha^2} = \frac{3\lambda}{4}(1 - 4\delta + \cdots)$. Minimizing over $\delta$ gives $\delta = \frac{3\lambda}{2}$ and $E \approx \frac12 + \frac34\lambda - \frac94\lambda^2$. At $\lambda = 1$ the stationarity condition $\frac12 - \frac{1}{8\alpha^2} - \frac{3}{8\alpha^3} = 0$ is solved by $\alpha = 1$, giving $E = \frac12 + \frac18 + \frac{3}{16} = \frac{13}{16} = 0.8125$. (c) The variational value is a rigorous upper bound for every $\lambda$, because the Gaussian is normalizable and admissible; perturbation theory has no such guarantee. For small $\lambda$ the second-order perturbative result is more accurate: it reproduces the exact coefficient $-\frac{21}{8}$, whereas the variational family gives $-\frac94$. The perturbation series is asymptotic (its coefficients grow factorially), so at $\lambda = 1$ it is useless, while the variational value remains a good estimate, about one percent above a converged numerical diagonalization. A check is the diagonalization in a truncated basis from the project, which converges for any $\lambda$ and gives the exact reference.
