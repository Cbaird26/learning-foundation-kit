@article
In QM I you described a state by a wavefunction $\psi(x)$. That is one *representation* of something more abstract: a **state vector** living in a complex vector space (a Hilbert space). **Dirac notation** lets you compute with that vector without committing to a basis, and it is the language of the rest of this course.

### Kets, bras and inner products

A **ket** $|\psi\rangle$ is a state vector. If you pick an orthonormal basis, a ket is a column of complex numbers. The matching **bra** $\langle\psi|$ is the conjugate transpose: a row whose entries are the complex conjugates of the column.

| Dirac notation | In a basis |
|---|---|
| $\lvert\psi\rangle = c_1\lvert 1\rangle + c_2\lvert 2\rangle$ | column $(c_1, c_2)^T$ |
| $\langle\psi\rvert = c_1^*\langle 1\rvert + c_2^*\langle 2\rvert$ | row $(c_1^*, c_2^*)$ |
| $\langle\phi\vert\psi\rangle$ | $\sum_n \phi_n^* \psi_n$ |

The **inner product** $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle$ is a complex number, the *amplitude* for finding $|\phi\rangle$ in $|\psi\rangle$. It obeys

$$\langle\phi|\psi\rangle^* = \langle\psi|\phi\rangle, \qquad \langle\psi|\psi\rangle \ge 0 .$$

A physical state is **normalized**: $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle = 1$.

### Orthonormal bases and completeness

States $|n\rangle$ form an orthonormal basis if $\langle m|n\rangle = \delta_{mn}$. Any state expands as

$$|\psi\rangle = \sum_n c_n |n\rangle, \qquad c_n = \langle n|\psi\rangle .$$

Substituting $c_n$ back shows the **completeness relation** $\sum_n |n\rangle\langle n| = 1$: inserting it is the standard trick for turning an abstract expression into sums of numbers.

The **Born rule** says that measuring an observable whose eigenstates are $|n\rangle$ gives outcome $n$ with probability $P_n = |\langle n|\psi\rangle|^2$. Normalization makes $\sum_n P_n = 1$.

### Operators and outer products

An operator $A$ maps kets to kets. Its **matrix elements** in a basis are $A_{mn} = \langle m|A|n\rangle$, and completeness gives $A = \sum_{m,n} A_{mn}\,|m\rangle\langle n|$.

The **outer product** $|a\rangle\langle b|$ is an operator: it acts as $|a\rangle\langle b|\,|\psi\rangle = \langle b|\psi\rangle\,|a\rangle$. With $|n\rangle$ normalized, $P_n = |n\rangle\langle n|$ is a **projector**.

The **adjoint** (Hermitian conjugate) reverses order and conjugates: $(|a\rangle\langle b|)^\dagger = |b\rangle\langle a|$ and $(AB)^\dagger = B^\dagger A^\dagger$. An operator is **Hermitian** if $A^\dagger = A$. Observables are Hermitian, so their eigenvalues are real and eigenvectors for different eigenvalues are orthogonal.

The **expectation value** of $A$ in a normalized state is

$$\langle A\rangle = \langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle = \sum_n a_n |\langle a_n|\psi\rangle|^2 ,$$

where $a_n$ are the eigenvalues. A bra on the left, a ket on the right: read it left to right as "bra, operator, ket".

@example
title: Probabilities and an expectation value in a two-level system
problem: In an orthonormal basis $|1\rangle, |2\rangle$ a system is in $|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{5}\left(3|1\rangle + 4i|2\rangle\right)$. The observable is $A = |1\rangle\langle 1| - |2\rangle\langle 2| + |1\rangle\langle 2| + |2\rangle\langle 1|$. Verify that $|\psi\rangle$ is normalized, find the probabilities of $|1\rangle$ and $|2\rangle$, and compute $\langle A\rangle$.
step: Normalization: $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle = \frac{|3|^2 + |4i|^2}{25} = \frac{9 + 16}{25} = 1$.
step: The amplitudes are $\langle 1|\psi\rangle = \frac{3}{5}$ and $\langle 2|\psi\rangle = \frac{4i}{5}$, so $P_1 = \frac{9}{25}$ and $P_2 = \frac{16}{25}$. They sum to $1$.
step: Apply $A$ using $|a\rangle\langle b|\,|\psi\rangle = \langle b|\psi\rangle |a\rangle$: $A|\psi\rangle = \frac{3}{5}|1\rangle - \frac{4i}{5}|2\rangle + \frac{4i}{5}|1\rangle + \frac{3}{5}|2\rangle = \frac{3 + 4i}{5}|1\rangle + \frac{3 - 4i}{5}|2\rangle$.
step: Form the bra by conjugating the coefficients: $\langle\psi| = \frac{3}{5}\langle 1| - \frac{4i}{5}\langle 2|$.
step: Take the inner product: $\langle A\rangle = \frac{3}{5}\cdot\frac{3 + 4i}{5} - \frac{4i}{5}\cdot\frac{3 - 4i}{5} = \frac{9 + 12i}{25} + \frac{-12i - 16}{25} = -\frac{7}{25}$. The imaginary parts cancel, as they must for a Hermitian $A$.

@mistakes
- Turning a ket into a bra without conjugating the coefficients, for example writing $\langle\psi| = \frac35\langle 1| + \frac{4i}{5}\langle 2|$ in the example above.
- Reporting the amplitude $\langle n|\psi\rangle$ as a probability instead of $|\langle n|\psi\rangle|^2$.
- Confusing $|a\rangle\langle b|$ (an operator) with $\langle a|b\rangle$ (a number), or reading $\langle b|a\rangle$ as equal to $\langle a|b\rangle$ instead of its complex conjugate.
- Using completeness with states that are not orthonormal.

@practice num
prompt: With $|\phi\rangle = |1\rangle + i|2\rangle$ and $|\psi\rangle = 2|1\rangle + 3|2\rangle$ in an orthonormal basis, compute the inner product $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle$. Give the answer as a complex number.
answer: 2-3i
check: np.vdot([1, 1j], [2, 3])
hint: The bra $\langle\phi|$ has conjugated coefficients: $\langle\phi| = \langle 1| - i\langle 2|$.
hint: Multiply the bra's coefficients by the ket's coefficients component by component, then add.
solution: $\langle\phi| = \langle 1| - i\langle 2|$, so $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle = (1)(2) + (-i)(3) = 2 - 3i$.

@practice num
prompt: In an orthonormal basis, the operator is $A = 2|1\rangle\langle 1| - |2\rangle\langle 2| + |1\rangle\langle 2| + |2\rangle\langle 1|$ and the state is $|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(|1\rangle + |2\rangle\right)$. Compute $\langle A\rangle$.
answer: 3/2

hint: Write $A$ as a matrix with entries $A_{mn} = \langle m|A|n\rangle$, then compute $\psi^\dagger A\psi$.
hint: The matrix is $\begin{pmatrix}2 & 1\\ 1 & -1\end{pmatrix}$ and the column is $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(1, 1)^T$.
solution: $A|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(3|1\rangle + 0\,|2\rangle\right)$. Then $\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\cdot\frac{3}{\sqrt2} = \frac{3}{2}$.

@practice self
prompt: Show that if $A$ is Hermitian then $\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle$ is real for every state $|\psi\rangle$.
solution: Take the complex conjugate of the number $\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle$. Using $\langle\phi|\chi\rangle^* = \langle\chi|\phi\rangle$ with $|\phi\rangle = |\psi\rangle$ and $|\chi\rangle = A|\psi\rangle$ gives $\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle^* = \langle\psi|A^\dagger|\psi\rangle$. Since $A^\dagger = A$ this equals $\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle$. A number equal to its own complex conjugate is real.
rubric: Starts from the complex conjugate of $\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle$.
rubric: Uses the rule $\langle\phi|A|\chi\rangle^* = \langle\chi|A^\dagger|\phi\rangle$ (or equivalent) correctly.
rubric: Invokes $A^\dagger = A$ and concludes that the number equals its conjugate.
hint: A complex number is real exactly when it equals its complex conjugate.
hint: How does the complex conjugate of an inner product relate to swapping the bra and the ket?

@quiz num
prompt: In an orthonormal basis let $|\phi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}\left(|1\rangle + (1 + i)|2\rangle\right)$ and $|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{5}\left(3|1\rangle + 4|2\rangle\right)$. Both are normalized. What is the probability $|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|^2$? Give an exact fraction.
answer: 13/15

hint: Conjugate the coefficients of $|\phi\rangle$ when forming the bra.
hint: $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{5\sqrt3}\left(3 + 4(1 - i)\right)$.
solution: $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle = \frac{7 - 4i}{5\sqrt3}$, so $|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|^2 = \frac{49 + 16}{75} = \frac{13}{15}$.
