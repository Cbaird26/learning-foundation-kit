@article
Two angular momenta in one system, such as the spins of two electrons or the spin and orbital motion of one electron, combine into a total $\vec J = \vec J_1 + \vec J_2$. The two sets of operators act on different subsystems, so $[J_{1i}, J_{2k}] = 0$, and $\vec J$ obeys the same algebra $[J_i, J_k] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ikl}J_l$. Total angular momentum is the conserved quantity when the interaction depends only on the relative orientation.

### Two bases for the same space

The **uncoupled basis** $|j_1 m_1\rangle|j_2 m_2\rangle$ has definite $J_{1z}$ and $J_{2z}$, and has $(2j_1+1)(2j_2+1)$ states. The **coupled basis** $|j, m\rangle$ has definite $J^2 = \hbar^2 j(j+1)$ and $J_z = \hbar m$. They are related by a unitary change of basis:

$$|j, m\rangle = \sum_{m_1 + m_2 = m} C^{jm}_{m_1 m_2}\;|j_1 m_1\rangle|j_2 m_2\rangle .$$

The numbers $C$ are the **Clebsch-Gordan coefficients**; they are real in the standard phase convention, and only terms with $m = m_1 + m_2$ appear.

### Which $j$ occur

For fixed $j_1$ and $j_2$ the allowed values of $j$ are

$$j = |j_1 - j_2|,\ |j_1 - j_2| + 1,\ \dots,\ j_1 + j_2 ,$$

each occurring once. The dimension count confirms it: $(2j_1+1)(2j_2+1) = \sum_j (2j+1)$. For $j_1 = 1$ and $j_2 = \frac{1}{2}$ this is $6 = 4 + 2$, from $j = \frac{3}{2}$ and $j = \frac{1}{2}$.

### Two spin-1/2 particles

Here $j = 1$ (the **triplet**) and $j = 0$ (the **singlet**), with $4 = 3 + 1$:

$$|1, 1\rangle = |\uparrow\uparrow\rangle,\quad |1, 0\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle + |\downarrow\uparrow\rangle\right),\quad |1, -1\rangle = |\downarrow\downarrow\rangle,$$
$$|0, 0\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle - |\downarrow\uparrow\rangle\right).$$

The triplet is symmetric under exchange of the two spins, the singlet antisymmetric. Because $S^2 = S_1^2 + S_2^2 + 2\vec S_1\cdot\vec S_2$,

$$\vec S_1\cdot\vec S_2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\left[S(S+1) - \tfrac{3}{2}\right]\hbar^2 = \begin{cases} +\hbar^2/4 & \text{triplet},\\ -3\hbar^2/4 & \text{singlet.}\end{cases}$$

### Building states with ladder operators

Start from the top state $|j_1 j_1\rangle|j_2 j_2\rangle = |j_1+j_2,\ j_1+j_2\rangle$ and lower with $J_- = J_{1-} + J_{2-}$, using

$$J_\pm|j, m\rangle = \hbar\sqrt{j(j+1) - m(m\pm 1)}\,|j, m\pm 1\rangle .$$

At each $m$, the new multiplet's top state is the combination orthogonal to the states already found (its overall sign is a convention).

### Probabilities

If the system is in an uncoupled state, the probability that the total angular momentum is $j$ is the sum of $|\langle j, m|\psi\rangle|^2$ over $m$, and $\langle j, m|\psi\rangle$ is a Clebsch-Gordan coefficient or a combination of them. This is the same machinery as measuring spin along a new axis: the coupled basis is simply another orthonormal basis.

@example
title: Total-spin probabilities for two spin-1/2 particles
problem: Two spin-1/2 particles are in the state $|\uparrow\rangle_1|\downarrow\rangle_2$. Find the probabilities of total spin $S = 1$ and $S = 0$, and the expectation value $\langle\vec S_1\cdot\vec S_2\rangle$.
step: Add and subtract the $m = 0$ coupled states: $|1,0\rangle + |0,0\rangle = \sqrt2\,|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle$. So $|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(|1,0\rangle + |0,0\rangle\right)$.
step: The amplitudes are $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}$ for the triplet state and $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}$ for the singlet, so $P(S=1) = \frac{1}{2}$ and $P(S=0) = \frac{1}{2}$.
step: Use the eigenvalues of $\vec S_1\cdot\vec S_2$: $\langle\vec S_1\cdot\vec S_2\rangle = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\hbar^2}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\left(-\frac{3\hbar^2}{4}\right) = -\frac{\hbar^2}{4}$.
step: Check directly with $\vec S_1\cdot\vec S_2 = S_{1z}S_{2z} + \frac{1}{2}(S_{1+}S_{2-} + S_{1-}S_{2+})$. The raising and lowering terms turn $|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle$ into $|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle$, which is orthogonal to it. Only $S_{1z}S_{2z} = \left(\frac{\hbar}{2}\right)\left(-\frac{\hbar}{2}\right) = -\frac{\hbar^2}{4}$ survives.

@example
title: Building a state in spin-1 coupled to spin-1/2
problem: Couple a spin-1 particle to a spin-1/2 particle. Construct $|\tfrac32, \tfrac12\rangle$ in terms of the uncoupled states, and find the probability that $m_1 = 0$ in this state.
step: The top state is $|\tfrac32, \tfrac32\rangle = |1,1\rangle|\uparrow\rangle$. Lower it with $J_- = J_{1-} + J_{2-}$.
step: On the left, $J_-|\tfrac32,\tfrac32\rangle = \hbar\sqrt{\tfrac{15}{4} - \tfrac34}\,|\tfrac32,\tfrac12\rangle = \hbar\sqrt3\,|\tfrac32,\tfrac12\rangle$.
step: On the right, $J_{1-}|1,1\rangle = \hbar\sqrt2\,|1,0\rangle$ and $J_{2-}|\uparrow\rangle = \hbar|\downarrow\rangle$. Hence $\sqrt3\,|\tfrac32,\tfrac12\rangle = \sqrt2\,|1,0\rangle|\uparrow\rangle + |1,1\rangle|\downarrow\rangle$.
step: Divide by $\sqrt3$: $|\tfrac32,\tfrac12\rangle = \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,|1,0\rangle|\uparrow\rangle + \sqrt{\tfrac13}\,|1,1\rangle|\downarrow\rangle$. The coefficients square-sum to $1$.
step: The probability of $m_1 = 0$ is the squared coefficient of $|1,0\rangle|\uparrow\rangle$, which is $\frac{2}{3}$. The orthogonal combination with $m = \frac12$ is $|\tfrac12,\tfrac12\rangle$.

@mistakes
- Allowing $j$ values outside $|j_1 - j_2| \le j \le j_1 + j_2$, or skipping one so that the dimension count fails.
- Writing a Clebsch-Gordan combination with $m \ne m_1 + m_2$ terms.
- Mixing up the symmetric triplet $|1,0\rangle$ (plus sign) with the antisymmetric singlet (minus sign).
- Adding probabilities for different $j$ instead of squaring the amplitude, or dropping the sum over $m$ when asking about total $j$.
