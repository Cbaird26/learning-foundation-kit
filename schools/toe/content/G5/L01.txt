@article
You have met statistical mechanics before; this week fixes the conventions used for the whole course and sharpens one idea: **an ensemble is a choice of what is held fixed, and the fluctuations of what is not fixed are physical response functions.**

### Conventions

- $k_B$ is Boltzmann's constant ($1.38\times10^{-23}\ \text{J/K}$), $T$ is the absolute temperature and $\beta = 1/(k_BT)$. When a problem says "set $k_B = 1$", temperature is measured in energy units. Formulas are in SI units unless stated.
- Microstates are labelled $s$, with energy $E_s$ and particle number $N_s$. For classical identical particles the sum over states becomes a phase-space integral with the factor $1/(N!\,h^{3N})$ (the $1/N!$ removes overcounting of identical particles).
- $\langle A\rangle$ is an ensemble average and $\Delta A = A - \langle A\rangle$, so $\langle(\Delta A)^2\rangle = \langle A^2\rangle - \langle A\rangle^2 \ge 0$.

### Three ensembles

| Ensemble | Fixed | Weight of microstate $s$ | Potential |
|---|---|---|---|
| Microcanonical | $E, V, N$ | $1/\Omega(E,V,N)$ | $S = k_B\ln\Omega$ |
| Canonical | $T, V, N$ | $e^{-\beta E_s}/Z$ | $F = -k_BT\ln Z$ |
| Grand canonical | $T, V, \mu$ | $e^{-\beta(E_s-\mu N_s)}/\Xi$ | $\Omega_G = -k_BT\ln\Xi$ |

Here $\Omega$ counts the microstates in a thin energy shell, $Z=\sum_s e^{-\beta E_s}$ and $\Xi=\sum_s e^{-\beta(E_s-\mu N_s)}$. For a homogeneous system in the thermodynamic limit (Week 2), $\Omega_G = -PV$.

### Fluctuations are derivatives

Differentiating $\ln Z$ with respect to $\beta$ at fixed $V, N$ brings down the energy:

$$\langle E\rangle = -\frac{\partial \ln Z}{\partial \beta}, \qquad \langle(\Delta E)^2\rangle = \frac{\partial^2 \ln Z}{\partial \beta^2} = -\frac{\partial\langle E\rangle}{\partial\beta}.$$

Since $d\beta = -dT/(k_BT^2)$ and $C_V = (\partial\langle E\rangle/\partial T)_{V,N}$,

$$\langle(\Delta E)^2\rangle = k_B T^2\, C_V .$$

Dimension check: $C_V$ is J/K, so $k_BT^2C_V$ is J$^2$, as a variance of an energy must be. Positivity of the variance forces $C_V \ge 0$ in the canonical ensemble.

The same trick in the grand canonical ensemble, differentiating $\ln\Xi$ with respect to $\mu$ at fixed $T, V$, gives

$$\langle N\rangle = k_BT\,\frac{\partial\ln\Xi}{\partial\mu}, \qquad \langle(\Delta N)^2\rangle = k_BT\left(\frac{\partial\langle N\rangle}{\partial\mu}\right)_{T,V}.$$

Thermodynamics (Gibbs–Duhem) gives $(\partial N/\partial\mu)_{T,V} = N^2\kappa_T/V$, with isothermal compressibility $\kappa_T = -\frac1V(\partial V/\partial P)_{T,N}$. Hence

$$\frac{\langle(\Delta N)^2\rangle}{\langle N\rangle^2} = \frac{k_BT\,\kappa_T}{V}.$$

For the classical ideal gas $\kappa_T = 1/P = V/(Nk_BT)$, so $\langle(\Delta N)^2\rangle = \langle N\rangle$: the number distribution is Poisson.

### Size scaling and its failure

Both $\langle E\rangle$ and $C_V$ are extensive, so the **relative** fluctuation obeys

$$\frac{\sqrt{\langle(\Delta E)^2\rangle}}{\langle E\rangle} \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt N}.$$

For $N\sim10^{23}$ this is about $3\times10^{-12}$: the ensembles then give the same thermodynamics. The scaling fails when $C_V/N$ or $\kappa_T$ diverges, which happens at critical points (Week 7). There fluctuations are not a nuisance but the main object of study.

### A numerical check

```python
import numpy as np
beta, eps = 0.8, 1.0
lnZ = lambda b: np.log(1 + np.exp(-b * eps))   # two levels: 0 and eps
h = 1e-4
var = (lnZ(beta + h) - 2 * lnZ(beta) + lnZ(beta - h)) / h**2
exact = eps**2 * np.exp(beta * eps) / (1 + np.exp(beta * eps))**2
print(var, exact)    # agree to about six digits
```

A finite-difference second derivative of $\ln Z$ really is the energy variance.

@example
title: Energy fluctuations of $N$ independent spins
problem: $N$ distinguishable, non-interacting spins each have energy $+\epsilon$ or $-\epsilon$ and are in contact with a bath at inverse temperature $\beta$. Find $\langle E\rangle$ and $\langle(\Delta E)^2\rangle$, and verify that the variance equals $k_BT^2C_V$.
step: One spin has $z = e^{\beta\epsilon} + e^{-\beta\epsilon} = 2\cosh\beta\epsilon$. The spins are independent and distinguishable, so $Z = z^N$ and $\ln Z = N\ln(2\cosh\beta\epsilon)$.
step: The mean energy is $\langle E\rangle = -\partial_\beta\ln Z = -N\epsilon\tanh\beta\epsilon$.
step: The variance is $\langle(\Delta E)^2\rangle = \partial_\beta^2\ln Z = -\partial_\beta\langle E\rangle = N\epsilon^2\,\text{sech}^2\beta\epsilon$.
step: Now compute $C_V$ directly: $C_V = \partial\langle E\rangle/\partial T = N\epsilon\,\text{sech}^2(\beta\epsilon)\cdot\epsilon/(k_BT^2) = N\epsilon^2\,\text{sech}^2(\beta\epsilon)/(k_BT^2)$. Multiplying by $k_BT^2$ reproduces the variance.
step: Limits: as $T\to0$, $\text{sech}^2\beta\epsilon\to 4e^{-2\beta\epsilon}\to0$ (the system freezes into its ground state); as $T\to\infty$ the variance tends to $N\epsilon^2$, the value for $2^N$ equally likely states. The rms width grows only as $\sqrt N$.

@example
title: Number fluctuations of a classical ideal gas
problem: Starting from the grand partition function of a classical ideal gas, find $\langle N\rangle$, $\langle(\Delta N)^2\rangle$ and $P$.
step: With one-particle partition function $Z_1 = V/\lambda^3$, where $\lambda = h/\sqrt{2\pi m k_BT}$, and fugacity $z = e^{\beta\mu}$: $\Xi = \sum_{N}\frac{(zZ_1)^N}{N!} = e^{zZ_1}$. The $1/N!$ is the identical-particle factor.
step: Then $\ln\Xi = zZ_1$, and $\langle N\rangle = k_BT\,\partial_\mu\ln\Xi = zZ_1$ because $\partial_\mu z = \beta z$.
step: The variance is $k_BT\,\partial_\mu\langle N\rangle = zZ_1 = \langle N\rangle$, so the relative fluctuation is $1/\sqrt{\langle N\rangle}$.
step: Pressure: $PV = k_BT\ln\Xi = \langle N\rangle k_BT$. The ideal gas law follows from $\Omega_G = -PV$.

@mistakes
- Writing $\langle(\Delta E)^2\rangle = k_BTC_V$. A dimension check (joules squared) catches the missing factor of $T$.
- Treating the canonical energy as fixed at $\langle E\rangle$; in the canonical ensemble $E$ fluctuates and only $T$ is fixed.
- Forgetting the $1/N!$ for identical classical particles, which breaks extensivity of $\ln Z$.
- Differentiating $\ln Z$ with respect to $T$ when the formula calls for $\partial/\partial\beta$ at fixed $V,N$, then losing the factor $-k_BT^2$ from the chain rule.
