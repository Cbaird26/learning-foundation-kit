@article
Last week the relative fluctuation of $E$ scaled as $N^{-1/2}$. This week we turn that remark into a precise statement: what limit is taken, what survives it, and why the three ensembles then agree.

### The limit

The **thermodynamic limit** takes $N\to\infty$ and $V\to\infty$ with the density $n = N/V$ and the temperature $T$ fixed. For particles with stable, short-range interactions (a repulsive core plus a force that decays fast enough; we state this without proof) the free energy per unit volume

$$f(T,n) = -\lim_{V\to\infty}\frac{k_BT}{V}\ln Z$$

exists. So $\ln Z = -\beta V f + o(V)$ is **extensive**, and intensive quantities such as $P$ and $\mu$ do not depend on the ensemble or on the shape of the container. Everything else in this course (phase transitions, singularities) only makes sense in this limit, because $\ln Z$ is analytic in $T$ for any finite system.

### Why the ensembles agree: the saddle point

Group the canonical sum by energy, with $\Omega(E) = e^{S(E)/k_B}$ and $S$ proportional to $N$:

$$Z = \int dE\;\Omega(E)\,e^{-\beta E} = \int dE\;\exp\!\Big[\tfrac{1}{k_B}S(E) - \beta E\Big].$$

The exponent is $N$ times a smooth function, so the integral is dominated by its maximum at $\bar E$ (**Laplace's method**). The maximum satisfies

$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial E}\Big|_{\bar E} = \frac{1}{T},$$

the microcanonical definition of temperature. Evaluating the exponent there gives

$$F = -k_BT\ln Z \simeq \bar E - T S(\bar E),$$

a **Legendre transform** from $S(E)$ to $F(T)$. The corrections to $\ln Z$ are of order $\ln N$, which vanish per particle.

### The Gaussian around the peak

Expand about $\bar E$. The linear term vanishes, and $\partial^2S/\partial E^2 = \partial(1/T)/\partial E = -1/(T^2C_V)$. Therefore

$$p(E) \propto \exp\!\Big[-\frac{(E-\bar E)^2}{2\,k_BT^2C_V}\Big].$$

The width is the $k_BT^2C_V$ of Week 1, now read as the curvature of the entropy. A concave $S(E)$ is the same thing as $C_V > 0$. Since $\bar E\propto N$ and the width is $\propto\sqrt N$, the peak is sharp in the limit.

### Stirling and the size of corrections

For factorials, $\ln N! = N\ln N - N + \tfrac12\ln(2\pi N) + O(1/N)$. The leading terms are extensive; the rest is $O(\ln N)$.

```python
from math import lgamma, log, pi
for N in (10, 100, 1000, 10**6):
    err = (lgamma(N + 1) - (N*log(N) - N)) / N     # error per particle
    print(N, err, 0.5*log(2*pi*N)/N)               # last two columns agree
```

The error per particle falls like $\ln N/N$, which is why dropping it is legitimate only after dividing by $N$.

### When equivalence fails

The argument needs an entropy that is extensive and concave in the limit. Two situations spoil it:

- **Long-range interactions** (for example gravity) make the energy non-additive. The microcanonical $S(E)$ can then be convex somewhere, giving $C_V < 0$, which a canonical system can never show since $k_BT^2C_V$ is a variance.
- **Small systems at first-order transitions** have a convex "intruder" in $S(E)$ caused by the interface free energy. Its relative size shrinks as $N\to\infty$ for short-range forces, leaving a straight segment of $s(e)$, the latent heat.

Always say which limit you take: $N\to\infty$ at fixed $n$ and $T$ is the standard one.

@example
title: Saddle point for $N$ independent spins
problem: $N$ spins have energy $\pm\epsilon$. With $n_+$ spins up there are $\binom{N}{n_+}$ microstates. Use Stirling's approximation to find $S(E)$, locate the canonical saddle point, and compare with Week 1.
step: Let $x = n_+/N$. The energy is $E = N\epsilon(2x-1)$ and Stirling gives $S/k_B = -N[x\ln x + (1-x)\ln(1-x)] + O(\ln N)$.
step: The canonical integrand is $\exp\{N\phi(x)\}$ with $\phi(x) = -x\ln x - (1-x)\ln(1-x) - \beta\epsilon(2x-1)$. Maximize over $x$.
step: $\phi'(x) = -\ln\frac{x}{1-x} - 2\beta\epsilon = 0$ gives $x = 1/(1+e^{2\beta\epsilon})$. This is the same condition as $\partial S/\partial E = 1/T$.
step: The energy per spin is $\epsilon(2x-1) = -\epsilon\tanh\beta\epsilon$, matching $\langle E\rangle = -N\epsilon\tanh\beta\epsilon$ from Week 1.
step: Since $\phi''(x) = -1/[x(1-x)]$, the Gaussian has variance $x(1-x)/N$ in $x$. Its width falls as $N^{-1/2}$: this is what "sharp in the thermodynamic limit" means.

@example
title: Energy distribution of an ideal gas
problem: For $N$ classical ideal-gas atoms, $\Omega(E)\propto E^{a-1}$ with $a = 3N/2$. Find the canonical distribution of $E$, its mean and variance, and the $N$ needed for a relative width below $10^{-3}$.
step: The canonical weight is $p(E)\propto E^{a-1}e^{-\beta E}$ for $E\ge0$ (a gamma distribution).
step: Normalization: $Z = \int_0^\infty E^{a-1}e^{-\beta E}\,dE = \Gamma(a)\,\beta^{-a}$, so $\ln Z = -a\ln\beta + \text{const}$.
step: Then $\langle E\rangle = -\partial_\beta\ln Z = a/\beta = \tfrac32Nk_BT$ and $\langle(\Delta E)^2\rangle = \partial_\beta^2\ln Z = a/\beta^2 = \tfrac32N(k_BT)^2$.
step: The relative width is $1/\sqrt a = \sqrt{2/(3N)}$. It is below $10^{-3}$ when $a > 10^6$, i.e. $N > \tfrac23\times10^6 \approx 6.7\times10^5$.
step: The most probable energy is $(a-1)/\beta$, which differs from the mean by only $k_BT$, negligible compared with $E\propto N$. In the limit the mean, the mode and the microcanonical energy coincide.

@mistakes
- Saying $\ln Z$ is extensive "for any $N$". Extensivity holds only up to corrections of order $\ln N$ (or surface terms) that vanish per particle in the limit.
- Taking $V\to\infty$ at fixed $N$ (the ideal-gas limit) instead of fixed density $n = N/V$.
- Expecting a phase transition in a finite system: $Z$ is a finite sum of exponentials, hence analytic in $T$.
- Claiming that negative heat capacity is impossible for every system. It is impossible in the canonical ensemble, but a microcanonical long-range system can have it.

@quiz num
prompt: For $N$ independent spins with energies $\pm\epsilon$ at fixed energy $E = -N\epsilon/2$, use the Stirling entropy $S/k_B = -N[x\ln x + (1-x)\ln(1-x)]$ with $E = N\epsilon(2x-1)$ to find the microcanonical temperature, $k_BT/\epsilon$. Round to three significant figures.
answer: 1.82
check: 1/nderiv(lambda e: -((1+e)/2*log((1+e)/2) + (1-e)/2*log((1-e)/2)), -0.5, 1)
tol: 0.01
hint: Here $x = 1/4$. Use $1/T = \partial S/\partial E$ with $E$ in units of $N\epsilon$.
hint: $\frac{dS}{dx} = -Nk_B\ln\frac{x}{1-x}$ and $\frac{dE}{dx} = 2N\epsilon$.
solution: $\frac{1}{T} = \frac{dS/dx}{dE/dx} = \frac{k_B}{2\epsilon}\ln\frac{1-x}{x} = \frac{k_B}{2\epsilon}\ln3$ at $x = 1/4$. So $k_BT/\epsilon = 2/\ln3\approx1.82$. This agrees with the canonical relation $\tanh\beta\epsilon = 1/2$.
