@article
**Conventions.** The Ising model has spins $s_i = \pm1$ on a lattice with $H = -J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}s_is_j - h\sum_i s_i$. Each nearest-neighbor pair is counted once, $J>0$ is ferromagnetic, and $h$ is an energy (the moment is set to $1$). Write $K = \beta J$. Boundary conditions are periodic. The free energy per spin is $f = -k_BT\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\ln Z$ and the magnetization per spin is $m = \langle s\rangle = -\partial f/\partial h$.

### Why interactions are hard

With interactions $e^{-\beta H}$ no longer factorizes into single-particle pieces, so $Z$ is not a product. There are two honest strategies: **exact solutions** in special cases, and **controlled expansions** in a small parameter such as density or $K$. Mean-field theory (Week 6) is a third, uncontrolled-in-general route.

### Exact: the one-dimensional Ising chain

Write the Boltzmann weight bond by bond, $e^{-\beta H} = \prod_i T_{s_is_{i+1}}$ with
$T_{ss'} = \exp[Kss' + \tfrac{\beta h}{2}(s+s')]$. Summing over all spins gives a matrix product, so $Z = \text{Tr}\,T^N = \lambda_+^N + \lambda_-^N$. At $h=0$

$$T = \begin{pmatrix} e^{K} & e^{-K}\\ e^{-K} & e^{K}\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \lambda_+ = 2\cosh K,\quad \lambda_- = 2\sinh K .$$

Since $\lambda_+ > \lambda_-$, $\ln Z/N \to \ln\lambda_+$ as $N\to\infty$:

$$f = -k_BT\ln(2\cosh K), \qquad \frac EN = -J\tanh K .$$

The eigenvalue ratio controls correlations: $\langle s_0s_r\rangle = (\lambda_-/\lambda_+)^r = (\tanh K)^r = e^{-r/\xi}$, with

$$\xi = \frac{1}{\ln\coth K}\ \approx\ \tfrac12 e^{2K}\quad (K\gg1).$$

With a field, $\lambda_\pm = e^K\cosh\beta h \pm\sqrt{e^{2K}\sinh^2\beta h + e^{-2K}}$ and

$$m = \frac{\sinh\beta h}{\sqrt{\sinh^2\beta h + e^{-4K}}} .$$

As $h\to0$, $m\to0$ for every $T>0$: there is **no spontaneous magnetization and no phase transition** at finite temperature. The reason is a domain-wall argument. On an open chain, one antiparallel bond (a domain wall) costs energy $2J$, but the wall can sit at any of $\sim N$ places, an entropy $k_B\ln N$. So $\Delta F = 2J - k_BT\ln N < 0$ for large $N$, and order is destroyed. The correlation length diverges only as $T\to0$, so $T_c = 0$.

### Controlled expansion: the virial series

For a classical gas with pair potential $u(r)$ the pressure expands in the density,

$$\frac{P}{k_BT} = n + B_2(T)\,n^2 + B_3(T)\,n^3 + \cdots, \qquad B_2(T) = -\frac12\int d^3r\,\big(e^{-\beta u(r)} - 1\big).$$

The small parameter is $n$ times the volume of an interaction region, so the series is controlled for dilute gases. For hard spheres of diameter $\sigma$, $B_2 = \frac{2\pi}{3}\sigma^3 > 0$; attraction makes $B_2$ smaller, and it vanishes at the **Boyle temperature**. This classical $B_2$ adds to the quantum-statistics correction of Week 3.

### Controlled expansion: high temperature

Use $e^{Kss'} = \cosh K\,(1 + ss'\tanh K)$ on every bond. Summing over spins removes any term in which some spin appears an odd number of times, leaving

$$Z = 2^N(\cosh K)^{N_b}\sum_{\text{closed graphs}}(\tanh K)^{\#\text{bonds}},$$

with $N_b$ the number of bonds. This is a series in $\tanh K$, small at high temperature. For the periodic chain only the empty graph and the full ring survive: $Z = 2^N\cosh^N\!K\,(1 + \tanh^N\!K)$, which equals $\lambda_+^N + \lambda_-^N$.

### What to carry forward

Interactions produce correlations, correlations feed the susceptibility ($\chi\propto\sum_r G(r)$), and the correlation length $\xi$ is the quantity that diverges at a critical point. Always state the limit and its order: here $N\to\infty$ first, then $h\to0$.

@example
title: The $h=0$ chain at $K=1$
problem: For the periodic one-dimensional Ising chain with $J = k_BT$ (so $K=1$) and $h=0$, find $f$, the energy per spin and the correlation length.
step: The eigenvalues are $\lambda_+ = 2\cosh1 = 3.086$ and $\lambda_- = 2\sinh1 = 2.350$.
step: For large $N$, $f = -k_BT\ln\lambda_+ = -k_BT\ln3.086 = -1.127\,k_BT = -1.127\,J$.
step: The energy per spin is $-J\tanh1 = -0.762\,J$, equal to $-J\langle s_is_{i+1}\rangle$ as it must be.
step: $\lambda_-/\lambda_+ = \tanh 1 = 0.7616$, so $\xi = -1/\ln0.7616 = 3.67$ lattice spacings. At $K=1$ a spin is correlated with its neighbours over a few sites only.

@example
title: Boyle temperature of a square-well gas
problem: Particles interact with $u = \infty$ for $r<\sigma$, $u = -\epsilon$ for $\sigma<r<1.5\sigma$ and $u=0$ beyond. Find $B_2(T)$ and the Boyle temperature.
step: The Mayer function is $e^{-\beta u}-1 = -1$ for $r<\sigma$, $e^{\beta\epsilon}-1$ for $\sigma<r<1.5\sigma$ and $0$ beyond.
step: Integrate over shells of area $4\pi r^2$: $\int(e^{-\beta u}-1)\,d^3r = -\frac{4\pi}{3}\sigma^3 + (e^{\beta\epsilon}-1)\frac{4\pi}{3}\sigma^3(1.5^3 - 1)$.
step: Multiply by $-\frac12$: $B_2 = \frac{2\pi}{3}\sigma^3\,[\,1 - 2.375\,(e^{\beta\epsilon}-1)\,]$.
step: Setting $B_2=0$ gives $e^{\beta\epsilon} = 3.375/2.375 = 1.421$, so $\beta\epsilon = 0.351$ and $k_BT_B = 2.85\,\epsilon$. Below $T_B$ attraction dominates ($B_2<0$); above it the hard core dominates.

@mistakes
- Keeping the $\lambda_-^N$ term as if it mattered in the thermodynamic limit, or dropping it for small $N$ where it does matter.
- Concluding there is a transition in one dimension because $\xi$ grows at low $T$: it diverges only at $T = 0$.
- Using $\int(e^{-\beta u} - 1)d^3r$ without the factor $-\frac12$, or with $e^{-\beta u}$ instead of the Mayer function (the integral then diverges at large $r$).
- Treating the expansion parameters $n\sigma^3$ and $\tanh K$ as small without checking: near a critical point the series stops being controlled.

@practice self
prompt: Derive the partition function of the periodic one-dimensional Ising chain at $h=0$ with a transfer matrix. Show that $f = -k_BT\ln(2\cosh K)$ in the thermodynamic limit, and explain why the smaller eigenvalue does not contribute to $f$.
hint: Write $e^{-\beta H}$ as a product of $2\times2$ matrix elements $T_{s_is_{i+1}}$ and sum over the interior spins.
hint: The sum is a trace of $T^N$; diagonalize $T$ using the symmetric and antisymmetric combinations of the two spin states.
solution: With $T_{ss'} = e^{Kss'}$, $Z = \sum_{\{s\}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}T_{s_is_{i+1}} = \text{Tr}\,T^N$ because of the periodic closure $s_{N+1} = s_1$. The eigenvectors $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$ give $\lambda_+ = e^K + e^{-K} = 2\cosh K$ and $\lambda_- = e^K - e^{-K} = 2\sinh K$. So $Z = (2\cosh K)^N + (2\sinh K)^N$. Since $\lambda_-/\lambda_+ = \tanh K<1$, $\ln Z = N\ln\lambda_+ + \ln[1 + (\lambda_-/\lambda_+)^N]$ and the last term tends to $0$ (it is exponentially small, and is not extensive). Hence $f = -k_BT\ln(2\cosh K)$.
