@article
**Conventions.** The $O(n)$ model has unit-length spins $\vec S_i$ with $n$ components ($n=1$ Ising, $n=2$ XY, $n=3$ Heisenberg) and $H = -J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\vec S_i\cdot\vec S_j$ on a hypercubic lattice of spacing $a$. For XY spins, $\vec S_i = (\cos\theta_i,\sin\theta_i)$. At low $T$ the angles vary slowly and $H\simeq E_0 + \frac{\rho_s}{2}\int d^dx\,(\nabla\theta)^2$ with **spin stiffness** $\rho_s = Ja^{2-d}$ ($=J$ in $d=2$). Wave vectors are cut off at $\Lambda\sim\pi/a$, and $L$ is the system size.

### Goldstone modes

An Ising system breaks its discrete symmetry by choosing one of two states. A continuous symmetry is broken by choosing a direction on a sphere, and slowly rotating that direction costs almost no energy. The $n-1$ transverse (angular) fluctuations are **Goldstone modes**: their correlation function $G_\perp(k) = k_BT/(\rho_sk^2)$ has no gap. A signature is the transverse susceptibility $\chi_\perp = m/h$, which diverges as $h\to0$ at every $T<T_c$, not only at $T_c$.

### Spin waves and the Mermin–Wagner theorem

At Gaussian level the mean-square angle is

$$\langle\theta^2\rangle = \frac{k_BT}{\rho_s}\int_{1/L}^{\Lambda}\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}\,\frac{1}{k^2}.$$

- $d=3$: the integral converges as $L\to\infty$, so order survives at low $T$.
- $d=2$: $\langle\theta^2\rangle = \frac{k_BT}{2\pi\rho_s}\ln(\Lambda L)$ diverges logarithmically.
- $d=1$: it grows like $L$.

So with short-range interactions a continuous symmetry cannot be spontaneously broken at $T>0$ in $d\le2$ (**Mermin–Wagner theorem**). It does not apply to the Ising model, which has no Goldstone modes and orders in $d=2$.

### Algebraic order in the 2D XY model

For Gaussian angles, $G(r) = \langle\cos[\theta(r)-\theta(0)]\rangle = \exp[-\tfrac12\langle(\theta(r)-\theta(0))^2\rangle]$, and the exponent is $\frac{k_BT}{2\pi\rho_s}\ln(r/a)$. Therefore

$$G(r)\sim\Big(\frac{r}{a}\Big)^{-\eta}, \qquad \eta(T) = \frac{k_BT}{2\pi\rho_s}.$$

This **quasi-long-range order** is neither the plateau of true order nor the exponential decay of a disordered phase, and its exponent varies continuously with $T$. Vortex pairs unbind at the Kosterlitz–Thouless transition, where the *renormalized* stiffness obeys $k_BT_{KT} = \frac{\pi}{2}\rho_s(T_{KT})$, i.e. $\eta = 1/4$. Simulations of the square-lattice model give $k_BT_{KT}\simeq0.893\,J$, below the bare spin-wave estimate $\frac{\pi}{2}J$, because vortices lower $\rho_s$.

### A coarse-graining step: spin waves in a shell

Integrate out the modes in the shell $\Lambda/b<k<\Lambda$ in $d=2$. At Gaussian level they decouple from the slow modes, so they leave $\rho_s$ unchanged, but they shorten the spin. Their angle variance is

$$\langle\delta\theta^2\rangle_{\rm shell} = \frac{k_BT}{\rho_s}\int_{\Lambda/b}^{\Lambda}\frac{d^2k}{(2\pi)^2k^2} = \eta\ln b,$$

so a block spin is shorter by the factor $\langle\cos\delta\theta\rangle = e^{-\frac12\langle\delta\theta^2\rangle} = b^{-\eta/2}$. Restoring unit length means rescaling the spin by $b^{\eta/2}$ (field renormalization). The correlation function then satisfies $G(r) = b^{-\eta}G(r/b)$, which is exactly algebraic decay: $\eta$ is the anomalous dimension of the whole line of fixed points.

### The $O(n)$ model: where $n$ enters

For $\beta F = \int d^dx\,[\frac12(\nabla\vec\phi)^2 + \frac r2\vec\phi^{\,2} + \frac u4(\vec\phi^{\,2})^2]$ a shell step at one loop gives

$$r' = b^2\big[r + (n+2)\,u\,I_1\big], \qquad u' = b^{4-d}\big[u - (n+8)\,u^2 I_2\big],$$

where $I_1 = \int_{\rm shell}\frac{1}{k^2+r}$ and $I_2 = \int_{\rm shell}\frac{1}{(k^2+r)^2}$. The factors $n+2$ and $n+8$ count Wick contractions among the components; this is how the symmetry enters. With $\epsilon = 4-d$ and a dimensionless coupling $\tilde u$, the fixed point is $\tilde u^* = \epsilon/(n+8)$ and

$$y_t = 2 - \frac{n+2}{n+8}\,\epsilon, \qquad \nu = \frac{1}{y_t}.$$

For $d=3$ ($\epsilon=1$) this gives $\nu\approx0.600,\ 0.625,\ 0.647$ for $n=1,2,3$, against best estimates $0.630,\ 0.672,\ 0.711$. The trend that $\nu$ grows with $n$ is right, and the first-order values are within about $10\%$. As $n\to\infty$, $y_t\to d-2$, the exact spherical-model result.

@example
title: Algebraic decay in the 2D XY model
problem: A 2D XY model has $\rho_s = J$ and is at $k_BT = 0.3\,J$. Find $\eta$, and the ratio $G(100a)/G(10a)$. Compare with a three-dimensional magnet.
step: With $\rho_s = J$, $\eta = k_BT/(2\pi\rho_s) = 0.3/(2\pi) = 0.0477$.
step: Since $G(r)\propto r^{-\eta}$, $G(100a)/G(10a) = 10^{-\eta} = 10^{-0.0477} = 0.896$.
step: The correlation falls by only about $10\%$ over a decade in distance, so at low $T$ the 2D system looks nearly ordered on modest scales, even though $G\to0$ as $r\to\infty$.
step: In $d=3$ the spin-wave integral converges, so $G(r)\to m^2>0$ and there is true long-range order.

@example
title: One shell step for the XY model
problem: Coarse grain the 2D XY model with $b=2$ at $\eta = 0.1$. By what factor does the block spin shrink, and how is the spin rescaled?
step: The shell variance is $\langle\delta\theta^2\rangle_{\rm shell} = \eta\ln b = 0.1\ln2 = 0.0693$.
step: The block-spin length shrinks by $e^{-\frac12\langle\delta\theta^2\rangle} = e^{-0.0347} = 0.966$, which equals $b^{-\eta/2} = 2^{-0.05}$.
step: To keep unit-length spins we rescale by $b^{\eta/2} = 1.035$.
step: Because $\rho_s$ is not changed at Gaussian level, $T$ and hence $\eta$ are fixed under the step: a line of fixed points. Vortices, not spin waves, make $\rho_s$ flow and end the line at $T_{KT}$.

@mistakes
- Applying Mermin–Wagner to the Ising model. It requires a continuous symmetry, and the 2D Ising model orders at $T_c>0$.
- Concluding that the 2D XY model has no phase transition: it has none of the symmetry-breaking type, but the Kosterlitz–Thouless transition separates algebraic from exponential decay.
- Using the bare $J$ in $k_BT_{KT} = \frac{\pi}{2}\rho_s$; the relation involves the renormalized stiffness at $T_{KT}$.
- Reading the $O(n)$ results at first order in $\epsilon$ as exact: they are an expansion that is quantitatively rough at $\epsilon = 1$.

@practice self
prompt: Explain a coarse-graining step for the 2D XY model at low temperature using spin-wave theory. Say what is integrated out, how the block spin changes, what rescaling restores the original form, what does not change at this level, and how this reproduces $G(r)\sim r^{-\eta}$.
hint: Integrate the Gaussian modes in a momentum shell and compute the angle variance they carry.
hint: Compare $\langle\cos\delta\theta\rangle$ for Gaussian $\delta\theta$ with $e^{-\langle\delta\theta^2\rangle/2}$, then iterate the step.
solution: Integrate out the Gaussian spin waves in the shell $\Lambda/b<k<\Lambda$.
