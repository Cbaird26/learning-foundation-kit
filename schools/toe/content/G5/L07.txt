@article
**Conventions.** The reduced temperature is $t = (T - T_c)/T_c$, $h$ is the field conjugate to the order parameter $m$, and $d$ is the spatial dimension. An exponent such as $\gamma$ is defined by $\gamma = \lim_{t\to0}\ln\chi/\ln(1/|t|)$, so prefactors and corrections do not matter. We assume the exponents are the same on both sides of $T_c$ (justified by scaling next week).

### Critical exponents

| Exponent | Quantity | Power law |
|---|---|---|
| $\alpha$ | specific heat | $C\sim|t|^{-\alpha}$ |
| $\beta$ | order parameter ($t<0$, $h=0$) | $m\sim(-t)^{\beta}$ |
| $\gamma$ | susceptibility ($h=0$) | $\chi\sim|t|^{-\gamma}$ |
| $\delta$ | critical isotherm ($t=0$) | $h\sim|m|^{\delta}\,\text{sgn}\,m$ |
| $\nu$ | correlation length | $\xi\sim|t|^{-\nu}$ |
| $\eta$ | correlation at $T_c$ | $G(r)\sim r^{-(d-2+\eta)}$ |

A critical point is a singularity of the free energy in the thermodynamic limit, which is why Week 2 insisted on that limit.

### Landau theory

Near $T_c$ the order parameter is small, so expand the free energy density in powers of $m$. For an Ising-type system symmetry under $m\to-m$ forbids odd powers at $h=0$, and the only temperature dependence that matters is in the quadratic coefficient:

$$f(m) = f_0 + \tfrac{a}{2}\,t\,m^2 + \tfrac{b}{4}\,m^4 - hm, \qquad a,b>0 .$$

The equilibrium $m$ minimizes $f$: $\;atm + bm^3 = h$. This gives

- $h=0$, $t<0$: $m = \sqrt{a/b}\,(-t)^{1/2}$, so $\beta = \tfrac12$;
- $t=0$: $h = bm^3$, so $\delta = 3$;
- $\chi = \partial m/\partial h|_{h=0} = 1/(at)$ for $t>0$ and $1/(2a|t|)$ for $t<0$, so $\gamma = 1$ with amplitude ratio $2$;
- $f_{\min} = f_0 - a^2t^2/(4b)$ for $t<0$ and $f_0$ above: $C = -T\partial_T^2 f$ jumps by $a^2/(2bT_c)$, a finite discontinuity, so $\alpha = 0$.

These are the mean-field exponents of Week 6, now derived from symmetry alone.

### Landau–Ginzburg: spatial variation

Allow $m(\mathbf r)$ to vary and add the lowest-order gradient term (we absorb $\beta$ into the coefficients):

$$\beta F[m] = \int d^dr\Big[\tfrac12 r_0 m^2 + \tfrac12 c\,(\nabla m)^2 + \tfrac14 u\,m^4 - hm\Big], \qquad r_0\propto t .$$

At Gaussian level ($u$ neglected, $t>0$) Fourier modes decouple and

$$G(k) = \frac{1}{r_0 + ck^2} = \frac{\chi}{1+\xi^2k^2}, \qquad \chi = \frac1{r_0},\quad \xi = \sqrt{c/r_0}\propto t^{-1/2}.$$

So $\nu = \tfrac12$ and, since $G(k)\propto k^{-2}$ at $t=0$, $\eta = 0$ in mean-field theory.

### Universality

Experiments and exact solutions show that exponents depend only on the spatial dimension, the symmetry of the order parameter (Ising $\mathbb Z_2$, XY $O(2)$, Heisenberg $O(3)$) and the range of the interactions, not on lattice details.

| | $\alpha$ | $\beta$ | $\gamma$ | $\delta$ | $\nu$ | $\eta$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean field | 0 | 1/2 | 1 | 3 | 1/2 | 0 |
| 2D Ising (exact) | 0 (log) | 1/8 | 7/4 | 15 | 1 | 1/4 |
| 3D Ising (numerical) | 0.110 | 0.326 | 1.237 | 4.79 | 0.630 | 0.036 |

The 3D Ising class also describes the liquid–gas critical point and uniaxial magnets with short-range forces.

### The Ginzburg criterion

When can mean-field theory be trusted? Compare the Gaussian fluctuations averaged over one correlation volume with $m^2$. The fluctuation is

$$\langle\delta m^2\rangle_\xi \sim \int_{k<1/\xi}\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{1}{ck^2 + r_0} \sim \xi^{2-d},$$

while $m^2\sim|t|$ and $\xi\sim|t|^{-1/2}$. Hence

$$\frac{\langle\delta m^2\rangle_\xi}{m^2}\sim|t|^{(d-4)/2} .$$

For $d<4$ this grows as $t\to0$: mean-field theory fails inside a critical window $|t|\lesssim t_G$. For $d>4$ it shrinks, so mean-field exponents are exact. The marginal value $d_c = 4$ is the **upper critical dimension**; at $d=4$ one finds logarithmic corrections. The window $t_G$ is tiny when many particles lie within a correlation volume (conventional superconductors) and large for short-range magnets and fluids.

@example
title: Landau exponents step by step
problem: For $f(m) = \frac a2 tm^2 + \frac b4m^4 - hm$ find $\beta$, $\gamma$ and $\delta$.
step: Minimize: $f'(m) = atm + bm^3 - h = 0$.
step: At $h=0$ and $t<0$ the nonzero solution is $m^2 = -at/b$, so $m = \sqrt{a/b}\,(-t)^{1/2}$ and $\beta = 1/2$.
step: At $t=0$ the equation is $h = bm^3$, so $m\propto h^{1/3}$ and $\delta = 3$.
step: For $\chi$ differentiate $h(m) = atm + bm^3$: $dh/dm = at + 3bm^2$. For $t>0$, $m=0$ and $\chi = 1/(at)$. For $t<0$, $m^2 = -at/b$ gives $dh/dm = -2at = 2a|t|$ and $\chi = 1/(2a|t|)$. Either way $\gamma = 1$, and the amplitude below $T_c$ is half that above.

@example
title: Predicting a correlation length with two sets of exponents
problem: Scattering gives $\xi = 2.0\ \text{nm}$ at $t = 10^{-2}$ in a three-dimensional short-range magnet. Predict $\xi$ at $t = 10^{-4}$ with $\nu = 1/2$ (mean field) and $\nu = 0.630$ (3D Ising), and decide which to trust.
step: Since $\xi\propto t^{-\nu}$, reducing $t$ by a factor $100$ multiplies $\xi$ by $100^{\nu}$.
step: Mean field: $100^{1/2} = 10$, so $\xi = 20\ \text{nm}$.
step: 3D Ising: $100^{0.630} = 18.2$, so $\xi = 36\ \text{nm}$.
step: A short-range magnet in $d=3<4$ has a critical window $|t|\lesssim t_G$ where fluctuations invalidate mean field. Once $t$ is inside that window, trust the 3D Ising value; far above it, mean-field behaviour can still describe the data, with a crossover in between.

@mistakes
- Quoting mean-field exponents for real three-dimensional systems close to $T_c$; they hold only outside the critical window or above $d=4$.
- Forgetting that $\chi$ below $T_c$ has amplitude $1/(2a|t|)$, not $1/(a|t|)$, in Landau theory.
- Reading an exponent from the slope of $\chi$ versus $t$ instead of $\ln\chi$ versus $\ln|t|$, or fitting too far from $T_c$.
- Believing universality means all critical points share exponents; the class depends on $d$, symmetry and range.

answer: 1.09

answer: -1/(2*a*t)

@quiz num
prompt: In $d=3$ the Ginzburg ratio $R(t) = \langle\delta m^2\rangle_\xi/m^2$ scales as $|t|^{-1/2}$. Suppose $R = 0.1$ at $|t| = 0.04$. At what $|t| = t_G$ does $R$ reach $1$, signalling the end of mean-field validity?
answer: 4e-4
check: (0.1*0.04**0.5)**2
tol: 1%
hint: Write $R(t) = C|t|^{-1/2}$ and use the given point to find $C$.
hint: Then solve $C\,t_G^{-1/2} = 1$.
solution: $C = R\,|t|^{1/2} = 0.1\times0.2 = 0.02$. Setting $0.02\,t_G^{-1/2} = 1$ gives $t_G = 0.02^2 = 4\times10^{-4}$. Mean-field exponents are unreliable for $|t|\lesssim4\times10^{-4}$.

@quiz num
prompt: A Landau free energy density is $f = f_0(T) + \frac a2tm^2 + \frac b4m^4$ with $t = (T - T_c)/T_c$, $a = 2$, $b = 1$ (in J/m$^3$) and $T_c = 100\ \text{K}$. Using $C = -T\,\partial^2f_{\min}/\partial T^2$, find the discontinuity of the specific heat per volume at $T_c$ (below minus above).
answer: 0.02
check: -100*nderiv(lambda T: -(2*(T-100)/100)**2/(4*1), 99.0, 2)
tol: 1%
unit: J/(m^3 K)
hint: Below $T_c$, $f_{\min} - f_0 = -a^2t^2/(4b)$; above $T_c$ it is $0$.
hint: Differentiate twice with respect to $T$ using $t = (T - T_c)/T_c$, then multiply by $-T_c$.
solution: Below $T_c$, $\partial_T^2 f_{\min} = -a^2/(2bT_c^2)$, so $\Delta C = T_c\,a^2/(2bT_c^2) = a^2/(2bT_c) = 4/(2\times100) = 0.02\ \text{J/(m}^3\text{K)}$.
