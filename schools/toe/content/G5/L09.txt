**Conventions.** The couplings $\mathbf K = (K_1, K_2,\dots)$ are reduced, meaning $\beta$ is absorbed: for the Ising chain $K=\beta J$. A renormalization transformation with length factor $b>1$ is a map $\mathbf K' = R_b(\mathbf K)$. The free energy per site is $\phi(\mathbf K) = \lim\ln Z/N$.

### Coarse graining

Near a critical point the correlation length $\xi$ is much larger than the lattice spacing, so the microscopic details of a single site cannot matter. A **renormalization-group (RG) transformation** makes that precise in three steps:

1. **Coarse grain.** Sum over (or average) the short-distance degrees of freedom, for example every second spin or the fast Fourier modes. The partition function is unchanged, since $Z$ is only being summed in stages.
2. **Rescale.** Measure lengths in units of the new lattice spacing $b\,a$. The system looks smaller: $\xi' = \xi/b$.
3. **Renormalize.** Rescale the field if needed, and read off the new couplings $\mathbf K'$ of the effective Hamiltonian.

Iterating gives a flow in coupling space. The free energy obeys $\phi(\mathbf K) = g(\mathbf K) + b^{-d}\phi(\mathbf K')$, with a smooth analytic piece $g$ from the traced-out degrees of freedom.

### Fixed points and universality

A **fixed point** $\mathbf K^* = R_b(\mathbf K^*)$ is scale invariant, so $\xi = 0$ or $\xi = \infty$. Points flowing to a fixed point with $\xi=\infty$ form the **critical surface**; the critical point of every model that lies on it is controlled by that single fixed point. Linearizing, $\delta\mathbf K' = \mathsf M\,\delta\mathbf K$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_i = b^{y_i}$:

- $y_i>0$: **relevant**, deviations grow (temperature $t$, field $h$);
- $y_i<0$: **irrelevant**, deviations die out, leaving only corrections to scaling;
- $y_i=0$: **marginal**.

Since $\xi' = \xi/b$ and $t' = b^{y_t}t$, we get $\xi\sim|t|^{-1/y_t}$:

$$\nu = \frac{1}{y_t} = \frac{\ln b}{\ln\lambda_t}, \qquad 2-\alpha = \frac{d}{y_t}, \qquad \Delta = \frac{y_h}{y_t}.$$

This is where the scaling form of Week 8 and hyperscaling come from. **Universality** is the statement that models in the basin of attraction of one fixed point share all exponents.

### Exact decimation of the Ising chain

Take $b=2$ and sum over every second spin. For the remaining neighbours $s_1, s_3$ require

$$\sum_{s_2=\pm1}e^{Ks_2(s_1+s_3)} = A\,e^{K's_1s_3} .$$

For $s_1 = s_3$ this reads $2\cosh2K = Ae^{K'}$ and for $s_1 = -s_3$ it reads $2 = Ae^{-K'}$. Dividing,

$$e^{2K'} = \cosh2K, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \tanh K' = \tanh^2K,$$

and the constant is $A^2 = 4\cosh2K$, which feeds the free-energy recursion $\phi(K) = \tfrac12\ln2 + \tfrac14\ln\cosh2K + \tfrac12\phi(K')$. Because $\tanh^2K<\tanh K$, $K'<K$ always: the flow runs to the high-temperature fixed point $K^*=0$ ($\xi=0$, stable), and the only other fixed point is $K^*=\infty$ ($T=0$, unstable). **Every finite $K$ flows to $K^*=0$: there is no phase transition at $T>0$.**

Near $T=0$ use $x = e^{-2K}$. Then $\tanh K = (1-x)/(1+x)$ and the recursion becomes $x' = 2x/(1+x^2)\approx2x$. The eigenvalue is $\lambda = 2 = b^{y}$, so $y_t = 1$ and $\nu = 1$ in the variable $x$, matching $\xi\simeq\frac1{2x}$ from Week 5.

```python
import numpy as np
K = 1.0
for step in range(5):
    xi = 1.0 / np.log(1.0 / np.tanh(K))      # exact correlation length of the chain
    print(step, round(K, 4), round(xi, 3))
    K = 0.5 * np.log(np.cosh(2 * K))         # one decimation with b = 2
```

The printed $\xi$ halves at each step, as $\xi' = \xi/b$ demands.

### Beyond one dimension

For $d>1$ decimation generates next-neighbour and multi-spin couplings and cannot be done exactly. The **Migdal–Kadanoff** approximation first moves bonds, strengthening each surviving bond to $b^{d-1}K$, and then decimates $b-1$ spins along a line: $\tanh K' = \tanh^b(b^{d-1}K)$. It is uncontrolled but reproduces the exact chain for $d=1$ and gives a finite critical coupling for $d=2$.

@example
title: One decimation step for the chain
problem: Starting from $K=1$, find $K'$ after one decimation with $b=2$, and the correlation lengths before and after.
step: Use $\tanh K' = \tanh^2K$. With $\tanh1 = 0.7616$, $\tanh K' = 0.5800$.
step: Invert: $K' = \text{artanh}(0.5800) = \tfrac12\ln\frac{1.5800}{0.4200} = 0.6625$. (This agrees with $\tfrac12\ln\cosh2 = \tfrac12\ln3.762$.)
step: The exact correlation length is $\xi(K) = 1/\ln\coth K$. For $K=1$, $\xi = 3.67$. For $K' = 0.6625$, $\xi' = 1.84$, half as large.
step: This is the RG statement $\xi' = \xi/b$: the new chain has spacing $2a$, so in its own units it is half as correlated, and it is closer to the $K^*=0$ fixed point.

@example
title: Migdal–Kadanoff fixed point in two dimensions
problem: For $d=2$, $b=2$ the Migdal–Kadanoff recursion is $\tanh K' = \tanh^2(2K)$. Find the nontrivial fixed point and compare the implied $T_c$ with the exact and mean-field values.
step: A fixed point satisfies $\tanh K^* = \tanh^2(2K^*)$. For small $K$ the left side $\approx K$ exceeds the right side $\approx4K^2$; for large $K$ the right side is larger.
step: Solving numerically gives $K^* = 0.3047$, so $k_BT_c = J/K^* = 3.28\,J$.
step: Exact (Onsager): $k_BT_c = 2.269\,J$. Mean field: $4J$. Migdal–Kadanoff lies between them.
step: The flow is unstable at $K^*$: for $K>K^*$ it runs to $K=\infty$ (ordered phase), for $K<K^*$ to $K=0$ (disordered). The unstable direction at $K^*$ is the thermal one, whose eigenvalue gives $\nu$.

@mistakes
- Forgetting to rescale lengths: coarse graining alone changes the lattice spacing, so $\xi$ in lattice units changes by $1/b$ only after rescaling.
- Calling a fixed point "critical" without checking that $\xi=\infty$ there; $K^*=0$ in the chain is trivial ($\xi = 0$).
- Reading the exponent from $\lambda$ instead of $\ln\lambda/\ln b$: $\nu = \ln b/\ln\lambda_t$, not $1/\lambda_t$.
- Treating Migdal–Kadanoff exponents as exact; the approximation is uncontrolled and gives $\nu\approx1.34$ in $d=2$ instead of $1$.
