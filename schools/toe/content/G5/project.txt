goal: Solve the mean-field self-consistency equation for $q = 4$, compare it quantitatively with the simulation, and explain which assumptions fail and where the mean-field description is still accurate.

@review
This guide collects the results and methods you need for the cumulative exam. Conventions: $\beta = 1/(k_BT)$; simulations use $J = k_B = 1$; $t = (T - T_c)/T_c$; $q$ is the lattice coordination number.

### Week by week

1. **Ensembles.** Microcanonical $S = k_B\ln\Omega$, canonical $F = -k_BT\ln Z$, grand canonical $\Omega_G = -k_BT\ln\Xi$. Fluctuations are derivatives: $\langle(\Delta E)^2\rangle = k_BT^2C_V$ and $\langle(\Delta N)^2\rangle = k_BT\,\partial\langle N\rangle/\partial\mu$.
2. **Thermodynamic limit.** $N, V\to\infty$ at fixed density. The saddle point gives a Legendre transform and a Gaussian of width $\sqrt{k_BT^2C_V}$; relative fluctuations scale as $N^{-1/2}$.
3. **Quantum gases.** $\langle n\rangle = 1/(e^{\beta(\varepsilon-\mu)}\pm1)$; $\varepsilon_F = \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}(6\pi^2n/g)^{2/3}$; $C_V = \frac{\pi^2}{2}Nk_BT/T_F$; $N_0/N = 1-(T/T_c)^{3/2}$.
4. **Response functions.** $\chi_T = \beta\langle(\Delta M)^2\rangle = \beta m_0^2N\sum_rG(r)$; $C_P - C_V = TV\alpha^2/\kappa_T$.
5. **Interacting systems.** Transfer matrix $Z = \lambda_+^N + \lambda_-^N$, $\xi = 1/\ln\coth K$, no transition in 1D; virial coefficient $B_2$.
6. **Mean field.** $m = \tanh[\beta(h+qJm)]$, $k_BT_c = qJ$, $\beta = \frac12$, $\gamma = 1$, $\delta = 3$.
7. **Critical behavior.** Exponent definitions, Landau theory, universality, Ginzburg criterion with $d_c = 4$.
8. **Scaling.** $f_s = |t|^{2-\alpha}\Phi(h/|t|^\Delta)$ gives the Rushbrooke and Widom relations and, with hyperscaling (for $d<4$), Josephson; Fisher also needs the scaling form of the correlation function.
9. **Renormalization.** Decimation $\tanh K' = \tanh^2K$; fixed points; $\nu = \ln b/\ln\lambda_t$; relevant versus irrelevant.
10. **Continuous symmetries.** Goldstone modes, Mermin–Wagner, $\eta = k_BT/(2\pi\rho_s)$ in 2D XY; shell steps; $y_t = 2 - \frac{n+2}{n+8}\epsilon$.
11. **Monte Carlo.** Detailed balance, $\Delta E = 2s\sum_{\rm nn}s$, burn-in, $\delta\bar A = \sqrt{2\tau_{\rm int}\sigma^2/M}$.
12. **Finite size.** $m = L^{-\beta/\nu}\mathcal M(tL^{1/\nu})$, Binder crossing at $T_c$, $\chi_{\max}\sim L^{\gamma/\nu}$, $\tau\sim L^z$.

### Self-check

- Derive $\langle(\Delta E)^2\rangle = k_BT^2C_V$ and state what is held fixed in each ensemble.
- Derive the mean-field equation and $T_c$ from a single-spin argument, and give one regime where it works.
- Solve the 1D Ising chain by transfer matrix and explain why it cannot order.
- Derive $\beta + \gamma = \Delta$ and the Rushbrooke relation from the scaling form.
- Explain a decimation or shell step, saying what is integrated out and what is rescaled.
- Show that Metropolis satisfies detailed balance and compute an error bar with $\tau_{\rm int}$.

### Common errors

- Missing the $1/N!$ for identical classical particles, or $g$ in $\varepsilon_F$ and $T_c$.
- Writing $k_BTC_V$ instead of $k_BT^2C_V$ (check the units).
- Using $T_F$ or the mean-field $T_c$ as if they were exact transition temperatures.
- Applying hyperscaling to mean-field exponents in $d<4$, or mean-field exponents near a real three-dimensional $T_c$.
- Forgetting that $\langle M\rangle = 0$ in any finite system, and reading $T_c$ off one lattice size.
- Quoting $\sigma/\sqrt M$ for correlated data, or averaging over the equilibration transient.

@exam num
prompt: A one-dimensional Ising chain (infinite, $h=0$) has $J = 1$, moment $m_0 = 1$ and $k_BT = 1/0.6$, i.e. $K = \beta J = 0.6$. Using the transfer-matrix magnetization in a small field, $m(h) = \sinh\beta h/\sqrt{\sinh^2\beta h + e^{-4K}}$, find the zero-field susceptibility per spin $\chi/N$. (It must equal $\beta\sum_rG(r)$ with $G(r) = (\tanh K)^{|r|}$.) Round to three significant figures.
answer: 1.99
check: nderiv(lambda h: sinh(0.6*h)/sqrt(sinh(0.6*h)**2 + exp(-2.4)), 0.0, 1)
tol: 1%
hint: Differentiate $m(h)$ at $h=0$. Near $h=0$, $\sinh\beta h\approx\beta h$, so $m\approx\beta h/\sqrt{e^{-4K}}$.
hint: This gives $\chi/N = \beta e^{2K}$. Check it with the correlation sum $1 + 2\sum_{r\ge1}(\tanh K)^r = e^{2K}$.
solution: For small $h$, $m\approx\beta h\,e^{2K}$, so $\chi/N = \beta e^{2K} = 0.6\,e^{1.2} = 0.6\times3.320 = 1.99$. The correlation route gives $\beta\sum_rG(r) = \beta(1+t)/(1-t) = \beta e^{2K}$ with $t = \tanh K$, the same value.

@exam num
prompt: A finite-size study of a 2D Ising-type model gives (simulated numbers) slopes $dU_L/dT$ at the Binder crossing of $-0.55$ for $L=16$ and $-1.10$ for $L=32$, and susceptibility peaks $\chi_{\max}(16) = 25.0$ and $\chi_{\max}(32) = 84.09$. Use $dU_L/dT\propto L^{1/\nu}$ and $\chi_{\max}\propto L^{\gamma/\nu}$ to estimate the exponent $\gamma$. Round to three significant figures.
answer: 1.75
check: (log(84.09/25.0)/log(2)) / (log(1.10/0.55)/log(2))
tol: 0.02
hint: Doubling $L$ multiplies the slope by $2^{1/\nu}$ and $\chi_{\max}$ by $2^{\gamma/\nu}$.
hint: Find $1/\nu$ and $\gamma/\nu$ as base-2 logarithms of the two ratios, then $\gamma = (\gamma/\nu)\,\nu$.
solution: The slope ratio is $1.10/0.55 = 2$, so $1/\nu = \log_22 = 1$ and $\nu = 1$. The peak ratio is $84.09/25.0 = 3.364$, so $\gamma/\nu = \log_23.364 = 1.75$. Then $\gamma = 1.75\times1 = 1.75$, the exact 2D Ising value $7/4$ (and hyperscaling $2-\alpha = d\nu = 2$ gives $\alpha = 0$).

@exam self
prompt: A student simulates the 2D Ising model on lattices with $L = 16, 32, 64$ and finds Binder-cumulant crossings near $T = 2.27$, $\chi_{\max}\propto L^{1.75}$ and $|m|(T_c)\propto L^{-0.125}$. (a) Derive the mean-field equation for the Ising model with coordination number $q$, and state its prediction for $T_c$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ for $q=4$. (b) Compare these with the simulation and the exact values. (c) Explain the disagreement using the Ginzburg criterion and the idea of universality, and state where mean-field theory remains accurate. (d) Explain how finite-size scaling allowed the simulation to extract exponents although no finite lattice has a true singularity.
hint: Replace neighbours by their average to get an effective field; then linearize at $h=0$ to find $T_c$ and expand to cubic order for $\beta$.
hint: For (c) compare fluctuations in a correlation volume with $m^2$, and ask which dimension makes the ratio grow as $t\to0$.
hint: For (d) recall that at $t = 0$ the correlation length is cut off at $L$.
solution: (a) Spin $s_i$ feels $h_{\rm eff} = h + qJm$ and so $\langle s\rangle = \tanh[\beta(h+qJm)]$; self-consistency gives $m = \tanh(\beta qJm)$ at $h=0$. A nonzero solution exists for $k_BT<qJ$, so $k_BT_c^{\rm MF} = 4J$. Expanding the $\tanh$ gives $m\propto(1 - T/T_c)^{1/2}$, i.e. $\beta = 1/2$, and $\chi = 1/(k_B(T-T_c))$, i.e. $\gamma = 1$. (b) The simulation gives $T_c\approx2.27J$, $\gamma/\nu = 1.75$ and $\beta/\nu = 0.125$, consistent with the exact $2.269J$, $7/4$ and $1/8$ for $\nu = 1$, so $\gamma = 7/4$ and $\beta = 1/8$. Mean field overestimates $T_c$ by $76\%$ and gives the wrong exponents. (c) The Ginzburg ratio goes as $|t|^{(d-4)/2}$, which grows as $t\to0$ for $d<4$, so mean-field exponents fail close to $T_c$ in $d=2$; universality places the model in the 2D Ising class. Mean field is accurate at high temperature (it reproduces the first correction to $\chi$) and becomes exact for infinite-range coupling or $d>4$. (d) Finite-size scaling uses $m = L^{-\beta/\nu}\mathcal M(tL^{1/\nu})$ and $\chi = L^{\gamma/\nu}\mathcal X(tL^{1/\nu})$: at $T_c$ the only length is $L$, so power laws in $L$ at $T_c$, the shift of the $\chi$ peak and the crossing of $U_L$ give the exponents.
rubric: Derives the effective field and $m = \tanh[\beta(h+qJm)]$, and gives $k_BT_c = 4J$, $\beta = \frac12$, $\gamma = 1$ for $q = 4$.
rubric: Compares with the numbers $T_c = 2.269J$, $\beta = 1/8$, $\gamma = 7/4$, noting that $\gamma/\nu$ and $\beta/\nu$ from the simulation need $\nu=1$ to give $\beta$ and $\gamma$.
rubric: Uses the Ginzburg criterion ($d_c = 4$) and universality to explain the failure, and names a regime where mean field works.
rubric: Explains finite-size scaling: the system size cuts off the correlation length, giving power laws in $L$ at $T_c$ and a scaling collapse in $tL^{1/\nu}$.
