objective: Apply Kirchhoff's junction and loop rules with a consistent sign convention to circuits with several sources and loops.

@article
A source of **electromotive force** (emf) $\varepsilon$ does work on charge to keep a potential difference across a circuit. A real battery has **internal resistance** $r$, so when it delivers current $I$ its terminal voltage is

$$V_\text{terminal} = \varepsilon - Ir.$$

### Series and parallel

Resistors in **series** carry the same current, and their voltages add:

$$R_\text{eq} = R_1 + R_2 + \cdots$$

Resistors in **parallel** share the same voltage, and their currents add:

$$\frac{1}{R_\text{eq}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \cdots$$

Two resistors in parallel give $R_\text{eq} = R_1R_2/(R_1+R_2)$, always smaller than either one. To analyze a network, collapse it step by step to one resistor, find the total current, then expand back out, tracking current and voltage at each stage.

### Kirchhoff's rules

When a network is not reducible, use two conservation laws.

- **Junction rule** (charge conservation): the currents entering a junction equal the currents leaving it.
- **Loop rule** (energy conservation, since $\vec E$ is conservative): the potential changes around any closed loop sum to zero.

**Sign convention.** Choose a current direction for each branch (a wrong guess just produces a negative answer), choose a direction of travel around each loop, and then:

| You cross... | Potential change |
|---|---|
| a resistor along the assumed current | $-IR$ |
| a resistor against the assumed current | $+IR$ |
| a source from $-$ to $+$ terminal | $+\varepsilon$ |
| a source from $+$ to $-$ terminal | $-\varepsilon$ |

Write one independent equation per unknown current: junction equations for all but one junction, plus enough loop equations.

### Energy balance as a check

Every solution must satisfy conservation of energy:

$$\sum \varepsilon_k I_k \;=\; \sum I_j^2 R_j,$$

with the left side the power delivered by the sources (a source that is being charged contributes a negative term) and the right side the heat in every resistor, including internal resistances. If the two sides disagree, there is a sign or arithmetic error somewhere, even if each loop equation looked fine.

### Using the tools

A good order of work is: redraw the circuit, label currents and polarity, count unknowns, write equations, solve (by hand or with `numpy.linalg.solve`), then run the power check and test a limit (for example, $r \to 0$ should give the ideal battery).

@example
title: A resistor network with a real battery
problem: A battery with $\varepsilon = 14\ \text{V}$ and internal resistance $r = 1.0\ \Omega$ drives $R_1 = 4.0\ \Omega$ in series with the parallel pair $R_2 = 6.0\ \Omega$ and $R_3 = 3.0\ \Omega$. Find the current in each resistor and check the energy balance.
step: Reduce the parallel pair: $\dfrac{1}{R_{23}} = \dfrac16 + \dfrac13 = \dfrac12$, so $R_{23} = 2.0\ \Omega$.
step: Everything is now in series: $R_\text{tot} = r + R_1 + R_{23} = 1.0 + 4.0 + 2.0 = 7.0\ \Omega$, so $I = \varepsilon/R_\text{tot} = 2.0\ \text{A}$ through the battery and $R_1$.
step: Voltages: the terminal voltage is $14 - (2.0)(1.0) = 12\ \text{V}$; $R_1$ takes $(2.0)(4.0) = 8\ \text{V}$, leaving $4\ \text{V}$ across both $R_2$ and $R_3$.
step: Branch currents: $I_2 = 4/6 = 0.67\ \text{A}$ and $I_3 = 4/3 = 1.33\ \text{A}$. The junction rule checks: $0.67 + 1.33 = 2.0\ \text{A}$.
step: Energy balance. The source supplies $\varepsilon I = 28\ \text{W}$. The dissipation is $I^2r = 4\ \text{W}$, $I^2R_1 = 16\ \text{W}$, $I_2^2R_2 = 2.67\ \text{W}$ and $I_3^2R_3 = 5.33\ \text{W}$, which total $28\ \text{W}$. The balance holds.

@mistakes
- Getting a sign wrong when crossing a resistor against the assumed current or a source from $+$ to $-$. Write the table in the margin and follow it every time.
- Forgetting to invert when adding parallel resistances, or assuming equal currents in parallel branches (they share equal voltages instead).
- Treating the terminal voltage as equal to the emf while the battery is delivering current.

@practice num
prompt: Two resistors of $12\ \Omega$ and $4.0\ \Omega$ are connected in parallel. What is their equivalent resistance?
answer: 3.0
check: 1/(1/12 + 1/4.0)
unit: ohms
hint: In parallel the reciprocals of the resistances add.
hint: $\dfrac{1}{R_\text{eq}} = \dfrac{1}{12} + \dfrac{1}{4}$. Add the fractions and invert.
solution: $\dfrac{1}{R_\text{eq}} = \dfrac{1}{12} + \dfrac{3}{12} = \dfrac{4}{12} = \dfrac13$, so $R_\text{eq} = 3.0\ \Omega$, smaller than either resistor.

@practice num
prompt: A battery with $\varepsilon = 9.0\ \text{V}$ and internal resistance $0.30\ \Omega$ delivers $2.5\ \text{A}$ to a load. What is its terminal voltage?
answer: 8.25
check: 9.0 - 2.5*0.30
unit: V
hint: Part of the emf is lost across the internal resistance.
hint: $V_\text{terminal} = \varepsilon - Ir$.
solution: $V = 9.0 - (2.5)(0.30) = 9.0 - 0.75 = 8.25\ \text{V}$.

choice: $-10\ \text{V}$
choice*: $+10\ \text{V}$
choice: $+2.5\ \text{V}$
choice: $0$
hint: Current flows from high to low potential through a resistor. Going against the current is going from low to high potential.
hint: The magnitude is $IR = 10\ \text{V}$. Decide the sign from the direction of travel relative to the current.
solution: Moving against the current takes you from the low-potential end to the high-potential end, so the potential rises by $IR = (2.0)(5.0) = +10\ \text{V}$.

@practice expr
prompt: A total current $I$ enters a parallel pair of resistors $R_1$ and $R_2$. Find the current through $R_1$.
vars: I, R_1, R_2
lead: $I_1 =$
answer: I*R_2/(R_1 + R_2)
check: lambda I, R_1, R_2: (I*(R_1*R_2/(R_1 + R_2)))/R_1
hint: Both resistors have the same voltage across them. Find that voltage from the total current and the equivalent resistance.
hint: $V = I\,\dfrac{R_1R_2}{R_1+R_2}$ and $I_1 = V/R_1$.
solution: $V = I\,R_\text{eq} = I\,\dfrac{R_1R_2}{R_1+R_2}$, so $I_1 = \dfrac{V}{R_1} = I\,\dfrac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}$. The smaller resistor takes the larger share of the current.

@practice num
prompt: Two batteries and three resistors form a circuit with two nodes, A (top) and B (bottom), joined by three parallel branches. Branch 1 contains a $12\ \text{V}$ battery and $2.0\ \Omega$; branch 2 contains a $6.0\ \text{V}$ battery and $3.0\ \Omega$; branch 3 contains only $6.0\ \Omega$. Both batteries have their positive terminals toward A. Let $I_2$ be the current in branch 2, positive when it flows from B up through the battery toward A. Give $I_2$ to three significant figures.
answer: -0.667
check: lin([[2.0, 0.0, 6.0], [0.0, 3.0, 6.0], [1.0, 1.0, -1.0]], [12.0, 6.0, 0.0])[1]
unit: A
tol: 1%
hint: Let $I_1$, $I_2$ flow up through branches 1 and 2 and $I_3$ flow down through branch 3. Write the junction rule at A, then one loop equation through branches 1 and 3, and another through branches 2 and 3.
hint: The equations are $I_1 + I_2 = I_3$, $12 - 2I_1 - 6I_3 = 0$ and $6 - 3I_2 - 6I_3 = 0$. Solve for $I_2$.

Energy check: the $12$ V battery supplies $24$ W and the $6$ V battery absorbs $4$ W, so $20$ W is supplied; the resistors dissipate $8 + 1.33 + 10.67 = 20$ W.

@quiz num
prompt: A $5.0\ \Omega$ resistor is in series with a parallel pair of $12\ \Omega$ and $4.0\ \Omega$ resistors, all connected across an ideal $24\ \text{V}$ battery. Find the power dissipated in the $4.0\ \Omega$ resistor.
answer: 20.25
check: lin([[-1.0, 1.0, 1.0], [5.0, 12.0, 0.0], [0.0, 12.0, -4.0]], [0.0, 24.0, 0.0])[2]**2*4.0
unit: W
tol: 1%
hint: Reduce the parallel pair to find the total current and the voltage across the pair.
hint: The pair is $3.0\ \Omega$, so $R_\text{tot} = 8.0\ \Omega$ and $I = 3.0\ \text{A}$. The voltage across the pair is $24 - 3.0(5.0)$.
solution: $I = 24/8.0 = 3.0\ \text{A}$. The pair has $24 - (3.0)(5.0) = 9.0\ \text{V}$ across it. The $4.0\ \Omega$ resistor then dissipates $V^2/R = 81/4.0 = 20.25\ \text{W}$.

@quiz num
prompt: A battery with $\varepsilon = 12\ \text{V}$ and internal resistance $0.40\ \Omega$ drives a single load of $5.6\ \Omega$. What fraction of the total power produced by the battery is dissipated in the load? Give a decimal to three significant figures.
answer: 0.933
check: ((12.0/(5.6 + 0.40))**2*5.6)/(12.0*(12.0/(5.6 + 0.40)))
tol: 1%
hint: Find the current first, then compare the power in the load with the power the emf supplies.
hint: $I = \varepsilon/(R + r)$. The load gets $I^2R$ and the emf supplies $\varepsilon I$.
solution: $I = 12/6.0 = 2.0\ \text{A}$. The load dissipates $I^2R = 22.4\ \text{W}$, the battery supplies $\varepsilon I = 24\ \text{W}$, and the internal resistance dissipates $1.6\ \text{W}$. The fraction is $22.4/24 = R/(R+r) = 0.933$.
