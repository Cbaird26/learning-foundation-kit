@article
Circuits with capacitors and inductors respond to changes gradually. We look first at the **transient** response after a switch is thrown, then at the **steady state** reached when an AC source drives the circuit for a long time.

### RC transients

Connect a battery $\varepsilon$, a resistor $R$ and an uncharged capacitor $C$ in series at $t=0$. The loop rule gives $\varepsilon - IR - q/C = 0$ with $I = dq/dt$, and the solution is

$$q(t) = C\varepsilon\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \quad I(t) = \frac{\varepsilon}{R}\,e^{-t/\tau}, \qquad \tau = RC.$$

After one time constant the capacitor has 63% of its final charge; after five it has 99.3%. At $t=0$ the capacitor acts like a wire and at long times like a broken connection (the opposite of the inductor in week 10). Discharging through $R$ alone gives $q = q_0e^{-t/\tau}$. Whatever $R$ is, half of the energy $C\varepsilon^2$ supplied by the battery ends up in the capacitor and half in the resistor.

### LC oscillation

With no resistance, $L\,\ddot q + q/C = 0$, a simple harmonic oscillator with

$$\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}.$$

Energy slides back and forth between $q^2/2C$ (electric) and $\tfrac12LI^2$ (magnetic), and the total stays constant. A resistor drains the energy, giving oscillations with an envelope $e^{-Rt/2L}$.

### The driven series RLC circuit

An AC source $v(t) = V_0\cos\omega t$ has rms value $V_\text{rms} = V_0/\sqrt2$. Each element has its own response:

| Element | Opposition | Phase of current relative to voltage |
|---|---|---|
| Resistor | $R$ | in phase |
| Inductor | $X_L = \omega L$ | lags by $90^\circ$ |
| Capacitor | $X_C = 1/(\omega C)$ | leads by $90^\circ$ |

In series, the inductor and capacitor voltages are opposite in phase, so

$$Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2}, \qquad I_0 = \frac{V_0}{Z}, \qquad \tan\phi = \frac{X_L - X_C}{R},$$

where $\phi$ is the angle by which the source voltage leads the current. Because the reactances do not add arithmetically with $R$, never write $Z = R + X_L + X_C$.

### Power and resonance

The average power delivered is

$$P = I_\text{rms}V_\text{rms}\cos\phi = I_\text{rms}^2R,$$

since only the resistor dissipates. The factor $\cos\phi = R/Z$ is the **power factor**. Mixing peak and rms values is a common error, since $I_\text{rms} = I_0/\sqrt2$.

At the **resonance** frequency $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC}$, $X_L = X_C$, so $Z = R$ is a minimum, $\phi = 0$ and the current is largest. The individual voltage amplitudes across $L$ and $C$ can each be much larger than the source voltage even though they cancel in the loop sum. Sharper resonances have a higher quality factor $Q = \omega_0L/R$.

@example
title: A driven series RLC circuit
problem: A series circuit has $R = 24\ \Omega$, $L = 60\ \text{mH}$ and $C = 20\ \mu\text{F}$, driven by a source with $V_\text{rms} = 52\ \text{V}$ at $\omega = 1000\ \text{rad/s}$. Find the rms current, the phase angle and the average power, and say whether the circuit is above or below resonance.
step: Reactances: $X_L = \omega L = (1000)(0.060) = 60\ \Omega$ and $X_C = \dfrac{1}{\omega C} = \dfrac{1}{(1000)(20\times10^{-6})} = 50\ \Omega$.
step: Impedance: $Z = \sqrt{24^2 + (60 - 50)^2} = \sqrt{676} = 26\ \Omega$.
step: Current: $I_\text{rms} = V_\text{rms}/Z = 52/26 = 2.0\ \text{A}$.
step: Phase: $\tan\phi = \dfrac{10}{24}$, so $\phi = 22.6^\circ$. The sign is positive, so the circuit is inductive and the voltage leads the current.
step: Power: $P = I_\text{rms}^2R = (2.0)^2(24) = 96\ \text{W}$. Check with the power factor: $V_\text{rms}I_\text{rms}\cos\phi = (52)(2.0)(24/26) = 96\ \text{W}$.
step: Resonance is at $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC} = 913\ \text{rad/s}$. The drive at $1000\ \text{rad/s}$ is above it, consistent with $X_L > X_C$.

@mistakes
- Forgetting that an uncharged capacitor passes full current at $t=0$ and blocks current at long times (the inductor does the reverse).
- Adding $R$, $X_L$ and $X_C$ arithmetically instead of using $Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2}$.
- Using the peak current in $P = I^2R$ without the factor $\tfrac12$, or the rms voltage together with the peak current.

@practice num
prompt: A capacitor of $5.0\ \mu\text{F}$ charges through a $2.0\ \text{k}\Omega$ resistor from an uncharged state. How long does it take to reach $90\%$ of its final charge? Give the answer in milliseconds.
answer: 23.0
check: root(lambda t: 1 - exp(-t/(2000.0*5.0e-6)) - 0.90, 0, 1)*1e3
unit: ms
tol: 1%
hint: Find the time constant first, then solve $1 - e^{-t/\tau} = 0.90$.
hint: $e^{-t/\tau} = 0.10$, so $t = \tau\ln 10$.
solution: $\tau = RC = (2000)(5.0\times10^{-6}) = 10\ \text{ms}$. Then $t = \tau\ln10 = (10)(2.303) = 23.0\ \text{ms}$.

@practice num
prompt: A $9.0\ \text{V}$ battery charges an initially uncharged $5.0\ \mu\text{F}$ capacitor through a $2.0\ \text{k}\Omega$ resistor. What is the capacitor voltage $15\ \text{ms}$ after the switch closes?
answer: 6.99
check: ivp(lambda t, q: (9.0 - q/5.0e-6)/2000.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.015)/5.0e-6
unit: V
tol: 1%
hint: The time constant is $10$ ms. Use $V_C = \varepsilon(1 - e^{-t/\tau})$.
hint: $t/\tau = 1.5$, and $e^{-1.5} = 0.223$.
solution: $\tau = RC = 10\ \text{ms}$, so $V_C = 9.0\left(1 - e^{-1.5}\right) = 9.0(0.777) = 6.99\ \text{V}$.

@practice mc
prompt: A series RLC circuit is driven exactly at its resonance frequency. Which statement is correct?
choice*: The impedance has its minimum value $R$ and the current is in phase with the source voltage.
choice: The impedance has its maximum value and the current is zero.
choice: The voltages across the inductor and the capacitor are both zero.
choice: The current leads the source voltage by $90^\circ$.
hint: At resonance $X_L = X_C$. What does that do to $Z$ and to $\tan\phi$?
hint: The inductor and capacitor voltages are opposite in phase and equal in amplitude, so they cancel in the loop sum but are not zero.
solution: With $X_L = X_C$, $Z = R$ (its minimum), $\tan\phi = 0$ and the current is a maximum and in phase with the source. The inductor and capacitor voltages are individually nonzero, and can be larger than the source voltage, but they cancel each other in the loop.

@practice expr
prompt: Find the impedance magnitude $Z$ of a series circuit with resistance $R$, inductance $L$ and capacitance $C$ driven at angular frequency $\omega$.
vars: R, L, C, omega
lead: $Z =$
answer: sqrt(R^2 + (omega*L - 1/(omega*C))^2)
check: lambda R, L, C, omega: abs(R + I*omega*L + 1/(I*omega*C))
hint: Treat the reactances as perpendicular to $R$: add $R$ and the net reactance like the legs of a right triangle.
hint: $X_L = \omega L$, $X_C = 1/(\omega C)$ and the net reactance is $X_L - X_C$.
solution: $Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2} = \sqrt{R^2 + \left(\omega L - \dfrac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2}$. In complex notation this is the magnitude of $R + i\omega L + 1/(i\omega C)$.

@practice num
prompt: A series circuit has $R = 15\ \Omega$, $L = 0.050\ \text{H}$ and $C = 40\ \mu\text{F}$, driven by a source of $60\ \text{V}$ rms at $100\ \text{Hz}$. Find the average power dissipated in the circuit.
answer: 183
check: (60.0/abs(15.0 + I*2*pi*100*0.050 + 1/(I*2*pi*100*40e-6)))**2*15.0
unit: W
tol: 1%
hint: Convert $f$ to $\omega = 2\pi f$, find $X_L$ and $X_C$, then $Z$ and $I_\text{rms}$.
hint: $X_L \approx 31.4\ \Omega$, $X_C \approx 39.8\ \Omega$, $Z \approx 17.2\ \Omega$. Then $P = I_\text{rms}^2R$.
solution: $\omega = 628\ \text{rad/s}$, $X_L = 31.4\ \Omega$, $X_C = 1/(628\cdot40\times10^{-6}) = 39.8\ \Omega$, so $Z = \sqrt{15^2 + (8.37)^2} = 17.2\ \Omega$. Then $I_\text{rms} = 60/17.2 = 3.49\ \text{A}$ and $P = I_\text{rms}^2R = (3.49)^2(15) \approx 183\ \text{W}$.

@quiz num
prompt: An LC circuit has $L = 25\ \text{mH}$ and $C = 4.0\ \mu\text{F}$ with negligible resistance. Find its natural oscillation frequency in hertz.
answer: 503
check: 1/(2*pi*sqrt(25e-3*4.0e-6))
unit: Hz
tol: 1%
hint: The angular frequency of an LC circuit is $1/\sqrt{LC}$. Convert to ordinary frequency at the end.
hint: $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{(0.025)(4.0\times10^{-6})} = 3162\ \text{rad/s}$.
solution: $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC} = 3.16\times10^{3}\ \text{rad/s}$, so $f = \omega_0/2\pi \approx 503\ \text{Hz}$.

@quiz num
prompt: A $10\ \mu\text{F}$ capacitor charged to $100\ \text{V}$ discharges through a $50\ \text{k}\Omega$ resistor. How much energy is dissipated in the resistor during the first $0.50\ \text{s}$? Give the answer in millijoules.
answer: 43.2
check: quad(lambda t: (100.0/50e3*exp(-t/(50e3*10e-6)))**2*50e3, 0, 0.50)*1e3
unit: mJ
tol: 1%
hint: Find the time constant. How does it compare with $0.50$ s?
hint: The initial energy is $\tfrac12CV^2 = 50$ mJ. The energy remaining in the capacitor after time $t$ is $\tfrac12CV^2e^{-2t/\tau}$.
solution: $\tau = RC = 0.50\ \text{s}$, so $t = \tau$. The capacitor energy falls from $50\ \text{mJ}$ to $50e^{-2} = 6.8\ \text{mJ}$, so the resistor has dissipated $50(1 - e^{-2}) = 43.2\ \text{mJ}$.
