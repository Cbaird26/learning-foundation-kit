@article
Real confining potentials have finite depth. Take $V(x) = 0$ for $|x| < a$ and $V(x) = V_0 > 0$ for $|x| > a$. A **bound state** has $0 < E < V_0$. Classically such a particle never leaves the well, but its wavefunction does extend into the walls.

### The two regions

Inside, $\psi'' = -k^2\psi$ with $k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$, so $\psi$ is a sine or cosine. Outside, $\psi'' = \kappa^2\psi$ with

$$\kappa = \frac{\sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}}{\hbar},$$

so $\psi$ is a combination of $e^{\pm\kappa x}$. Normalizability rejects the growing exponential, leaving $\psi \propto e^{-\kappa|x|}$ outside. The density $|\psi|^2$ falls by a factor $1/e$ over a distance $1/(2\kappa)$: the **penetration depth** is of order $1/\kappa$.

### Continuity conditions

Integrate the Schrödinger equation across an edge $x_0$:

$$\psi'(x_0^+) - \psi'(x_0^-) = \frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\int_{x_0^-}^{x_0^+}\bigl[V(x) - E\bigr]\psi\,dx.$$

If $V$ makes only a finite jump, the right side shrinks to zero with the interval, so **both $\psi$ and $\psi'$ are continuous**. At the walls of the infinite well $V$ is infinite, which is why $\psi'$ is allowed to jump there.

### Even and odd states

The potential is symmetric, so eigenfunctions have definite parity. For an even state, $\psi = A\cos kx$ inside and $Be^{-\kappa|x|}$ outside. Matching $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at $x = a$ and dividing eliminates the amplitudes. Define $z = ka$ and $z_0 = \dfrac{a}{\hbar}\sqrt{2mV_0}$, so that $\kappa a = \sqrt{z_0^2 - z^2}$. The conditions are

$$\text{even: } z\tan z = \sqrt{z_0^2 - z^2}, \qquad \text{odd: } -z\cot z = \sqrt{z_0^2 - z^2}, \qquad 0 < z < z_0.$$

They are transcendental, so solve them numerically or graphically (where curves cross). The energy follows from $E = V_0\,(z/z_0)^2$.

### What the solutions tell you

- The number of bound states is the number of integers $n \ge 1$ with $(n-1)\pi/2 < z_0$. There is **always at least one** (even) state in one dimension.
- Parity alternates: the ground state is even, the next is odd, and so on.
- **Limit $V_0 \to \infty$:** then $\kappa \to \infty$, $\psi$ must vanish at $x = \pm a$, and $ka = n\pi/2$. This gives $E_n = n^2\pi^2\hbar^2/[2m(2a)^2]$, the infinite well of width $2a$.
- For finite $V_0$, every level lies **below** its infinite-well value, because $\psi$ leaks into the walls, which acts like a wider box.
- For a very shallow well, $z_0 \ll 1$, there is one weakly bound state with $\kappa a \approx z_0^2$. It is barely bound and extends far outside the well.

@example
title: Deriving the even-state condition
problem: Derive the quantization condition for the even bound states of the finite square well and check it against the infinite well.
step: For an even state, $\psi = A\cos kx$ for $|x|<a$ and $\psi = Be^{-\kappa x}$ for $x>a$. The left side $x<-a$ follows by symmetry.
step: Continuity of $\psi$ at $x = a$: $A\cos ka = Be^{-\kappa a}$.
step: Continuity of $\psi'$ at $x = a$: $-Ak\sin ka = -\kappa Be^{-\kappa a}$.
step: Divide the second equation by the first. The amplitudes cancel: $k\tan ka = \kappa$. Multiplying by $a$ gives $z\tan z = \sqrt{z_0^2 - z^2}$.
step: Limit check: as $z_0 \to \infty$ the right side grows without bound, which requires $\tan z \to \infty$, so $z \to \pi/2$. That is $ka = \pi/2$, the lowest infinite-well level for width $2a$.

@example
title: Counting bound states
problem: An electron is in a finite well of full width $2a = 1.0\ \text{nm}$ and depth $V_0 = 10\ \text{eV}$. How many bound states exist, and what is the parity of the highest one? Use $\hbar = 1.055\times10^{-34}\ \text{J s}$, $m = 9.109\times10^{-31}\ \text{kg}$.
step: Compute $z_0 = \dfrac{a}{\hbar}\sqrt{2mV_0} = \dfrac{0.5\times10^{-9}}{1.055\times10^{-34}}\sqrt{2(9.109\times10^{-31})(1.602\times10^{-18})} \approx 8.10$.
step: State $n$ exists if $(n-1)\pi/2 < z_0$, that is $n - 1 < 2z_0/\pi = 5.15$. So $n \le 6$ and there are six bound states.
step: Parity alternates starting from even for $n=1$, so $n = 6$ is odd.
step: Limit check: the infinite-well level of the same width is $E_n^{\infty} = n^2(0.376\ \text{eV})$, and the finite levels lie below these values. For $n = 6$ the infinite value $13.5\ \text{eV}$ exceeds $V_0$, but the finite well still binds this state below $10\ \text{eV}$.

@mistakes
- Imposing $\psi' = 0$ or $\psi = 0$ at the finite edges. Only continuity of $\psi$ and $\psi'$ is required.
- Keeping the growing exponential $e^{+\kappa x}$ in the outer region, which makes $\psi$ non-normalizable.
- Mixing up $k$ and $\kappa$, which use $E$ and $V_0 - E$ respectively.
- Forgetting that bound states need $0 < E < V_0$ and that only a finite number exist.

choice*: Integrating the Schrödinger equation across the edge gives a jump in $\psi'$ equal to $\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\int (V - E)\psi\,dx$, which vanishes for a finite jump in $V$ but not for an infinite one.
choice: Because $\psi$ is discontinuous at the walls of the infinite well.
choice: Because $\psi'$ can only be continuous where $\psi \neq 0$.
choice: Because a finite well has quantized energies and an infinite well does not.
hint: Integrate $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi'' + V\psi = E\psi$ over a tiny interval around the edge.
solution: The integral of $(V - E)\psi$ over a vanishing interval is zero if $V$ is bounded, so $\psi'$ cannot jump. When $V\to\infty$ the integral need not vanish, so $\psi'$ may jump (and $\psi$ stays continuous, equal to zero at the wall).

@practice self
prompt: Show that in the limit $V_0\to\infty$, the even-state condition $k\tan ka = \kappa$ reproduces the energies of an infinite well of width $2a$. Also explain why the finite-well energies are lower than the infinite-well ones.
hint: Look at what happens to $\kappa$ and to the equation $\tan ka = \kappa/k$ as $V_0\to\infty$ with $E$ fixed.
hint: Where must $\cos ka$ vanish for $\tan ka$ to become infinite?
solution: As $V_0\to\infty$ at fixed $E$, $\kappa = \sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}/\hbar \to \infty$, so $\tan ka = \kappa/k \to \infty$ and $\cos ka \to 0$, giving $ka = (2j-1)\pi/2$ for the even states. The odd condition $-\cot ka = \kappa/k \to\infty$ gives $\sin ka\to0$, so $ka = j\pi$. Together $ka = n\pi/2$, so $E_n = \hbar^2k^2/2m = n^2\pi^2\hbar^2/[2m(2a)^2]$, the infinite well of width $L = 2a$. For finite $V_0$ the wavefunction leaks into the walls, so the effective box is wider than $2a$ and the wavelength inside is longer, which lowers the energy.
rubric: Shows that $\kappa\to\infty$ forces $\tan ka\to\infty$ (even) and $\cot ka \to -\infty$ (odd).
rubric: Obtains $ka = n\pi/2$ and the correct infinite-well energy for width $2a$.
rubric: Explains the lower finite-well energies through leakage into the walls (a longer effective wavelength).

@quiz ms
prompt: Select every correct statement about a one-dimensional symmetric finite square well.
choice*: A bound state exists for every depth $V_0 > 0$ and width, however small.
choice: The ground state is odd under $x\to -x$.
choice*: Each bound-state energy is lower than the corresponding level of an infinite well with the same width.

solution: The lowest solution is always even, so the ground state is even, not odd. The other three statements are true: in one dimension the even equation always has a root for $z_0 > 0$; wavefunction leakage lowers each energy; and $V_0\to\infty$ forces $\psi\to0$ at the edges, giving the infinite-well levels.
