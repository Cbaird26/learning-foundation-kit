@article
The simplest bound system is a particle confined to $0 < x < L$ by perfectly rigid walls: $V = 0$ inside and $V = \infty$ outside. A particle cannot be where $V$ is infinite, so $\psi = 0$ for $x \le 0$ and $x \ge L$. Since $\psi$ must be continuous, it must vanish at the walls: $\psi(0) = \psi(L) = 0$.

### Solving the well

Inside, the time-independent equation is $\psi'' = -k^2\psi$ with $k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$. The general solution is $\psi = A\sin kx + B\cos kx$.

- $\psi(0) = 0$ forces $B = 0$.
- $\psi(L) = 0$ forces $\sin kL = 0$, so $kL = n\pi$ with $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ ($n = 0$ gives $\psi \equiv 0$, and negative $n$ repeats the same state up to sign).

Boundary conditions have quantized the energy:

$$E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2} = n^2E_1, \qquad \psi_n(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\,\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}.$$

The factor $\sqrt{2/L}$ normalizes $\psi_n$, because the average of $\sin^2$ over a period is $\tfrac12$. The levels grow as $n^2$, so they spread out. The lowest energy is **not zero**: squeezing the particle into width $L$ costs kinetic energy ($E_1 \propto 1/L^2$). Note $E_n = n^2h^2/(8mL^2)$ in terms of $h$.

### Orthonormality and expansion

The eigenfunctions are **orthonormal**:

$$\int_0^L \psi_m(x)\,\psi_n(x)\,dx = \delta_{mn}.$$

Any allowed initial state can be written $\Psi(x,0) = \sum_n c_n\psi_n(x)$ with

$$c_n = \int_0^L \psi_n(x)\,\Psi(x,0)\,dx.$$

An energy measurement yields $E_n$ with probability $|c_n|^2$, $\sum|c_n|^2 = 1$, and $\langle E\rangle = \sum_n |c_n|^2E_n$. These are constant in time. The state at later times is $\Psi(x,t) = \sum_n c_n\psi_n(x)e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}$.

### Stationary state versus stationary density

Two ideas are easy to mix up:

- In a **stationary state** $\psi_n e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}$, the wavefunction's phase rotates but $|\Psi|^2$ never changes. There are $n - 1$ interior nodes, fixed in place.
- In a **superposition** of different energies, the probability density oscillates at angular frequencies $(E_m - E_n)/\hbar$. In the well, $E_2 - E_1 = 3E_1$, so a mix of $n = 1$ and $n = 2$ has density oscillating at $\omega = 3E_1/\hbar$ and a mean position $\langle x\rangle$ that sloshes about $L/2$.

Symmetry can hide motion: $\psi_1$ and $\psi_3$ are both symmetric about $L/2$, so a mix of those two has oscillating density but a fixed $\langle x\rangle = L/2$. Always ask what *quantity* you are tracking.

@example
title: Energies of an electron in a nanometre well
problem: An electron is confined in an infinite well of width $L = 1.0\ \text{nm}$. Find $E_1$ in eV and the wavelength of the photon emitted in the $n = 2 \to n = 1$ transition. Use $h = 6.626\times10^{-34}\ \text{J s}$, $m = 9.109\times10^{-31}\ \text{kg}$, $1\ \text{eV} = 1.602\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}$ and $hc = 1240\ \text{eV nm}$.
step: Use $E_1 = h^2/(8mL^2) = \dfrac{(6.626\times10^{-34})^2}{8(9.109\times10^{-31})(10^{-9})^2}$.
step: The numerator is $4.39\times10^{-67}$ and the denominator is $7.29\times10^{-48}$, so $E_1 = 6.02\times10^{-20}\ \text{J} = 0.376\ \text{eV}$.
step: The levels scale as $n^2$, so $E_2 = 4E_1$ and the transition energy is $E_2 - E_1 = 3E_1 = 1.13\ \text{eV}$.
step: The photon wavelength is $\lambda = hc/\Delta E = 1240/1.13 \approx 1.1\times10^{3}\ \text{nm}$, in the infrared.

@example
title: Density of a mix of the two lowest states
problem: A particle in the well starts in $\Psi(x,0) = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\,[\psi_1(x) + \psi_2(x)]$. Find $|\Psi(x,t)|^2$ at $x = L/4$ and describe how it changes.
step: Evaluate the eigenfunctions at $x = L/4$: $\psi_1 = \sqrt{2/L}\,\sin(\pi/4) = 1/\sqrt{L}$ and $\psi_2 = \sqrt{2/L}\,\sin(\pi/2) = \sqrt{2/L}$.
step: For real eigenfunctions, $|\Psi|^2 = \tfrac12\psi_1^2 + \tfrac12\psi_2^2 + \psi_1\psi_2\cos\omega t$ with $\omega = (E_2 - E_1)/\hbar = 3E_1/\hbar$.
step: Substituting: $|\Psi(L/4,t)|^2 = \dfrac{1}{2L} + \dfrac{1}{L} + \dfrac{\sqrt2}{L}\cos\omega t = \dfrac{1}{L}\left(\tfrac32 + \sqrt2\cos\omega t\right)$.
step: At $t=0$ this is $2.91/L$ and half a period later it is $0.09/L$. The density at this point swings by a factor of more than $30$, even though each component alone is a stationary state.

@mistakes
- Keeping the cosine term. The condition $\psi(0) = 0$ removes it.
- Allowing $n = 0$ or claiming the ground-state energy is zero; $E_1 = \pi^2\hbar^2/2mL^2 > 0$.
- Writing $E_n \propto n$ instead of $n^2$, or normalizing with $\sqrt{1/L}$ instead of $\sqrt{2/L}$.
- Saying a superposition of stationary states is itself stationary. Its energy probabilities are fixed, but its density generally oscillates.

@practice num
prompt: A particle in an infinite well of width $L = 0.50\ \text{nm}$ has the unnormalized state $\psi(x) = A\sin(3\pi x/L)$ inside the well. Find the positive constant $A$ that normalizes it, in $\text{nm}^{-1/2}$.
answer: 2
check: 1/sqrt(quad(lambda x: sin(3*pi*x/0.5)**2, 0, 0.5))
unit: nm^-1/2
hint: Normalization requires $|A|^2\int_0^L \sin^2(3\pi x/L)\,dx = 1$.
hint: The integral of $\sin^2$ over a whole number of half-periods is $L/2$.
solution: $|A|^2 L/2 = 1$, so $A = \sqrt{2/L} = \sqrt{2/0.50} = 2\ \text{nm}^{-1/2}$. The factor $3$ in the sine does not change the constant.

@practice ms
prompt: A particle in an infinite well is in the state $\Psi = (\psi_1 + \psi_2)/\sqrt2$ at $t = 0$. Select every statement that is true.
choice*: The probability of measuring $E_1$ is $\tfrac12$ at every later time.
choice: $|\Psi(x,t)|^2$ is independent of time, because $\psi_1$ and $\psi_2$ are each stationary states.
choice*: $|\Psi(x,t)|^2$ oscillates with angular frequency $3E_1/\hbar$.
choice*: $\psi_1(x)\,e^{-iE_1t/\hbar}$ alone has a time-independent density even though its wavefunction changes with time.
hint: Separate the questions: energy probabilities, probability density, and the wavefunction itself.
hint: Cross terms in $|\Psi|^2$ carry $\cos[(E_2-E_1)t/\hbar]$.
solution: The coefficients $c_n$ keep fixed magnitude, so energy probabilities are constant. The density has a cross term at $\omega = (E_2 - E_1)/\hbar = 3E_1/\hbar$, so it is not constant. A single stationary state has a rotating phase but a fixed density.

@quiz mc
prompt: A particle in an infinite well is in the state $\Psi = (\psi_1 + \psi_3)/\sqrt2$. Which statement is correct?
choice: The density is constant in time because both components are stationary states.
choice*: The density oscillates with angular frequency $8E_1/\hbar$, but $\langle x\rangle$ stays at $L/2$ because both components are symmetric about the centre.
choice: The density oscillates with angular frequency $3E_1/\hbar$, and $\langle x\rangle$ oscillates about $L/2$.
choice: The density oscillates with angular frequency $8E_1/\hbar$, and $\langle x\rangle$ oscillates about $L/2$.
hint: Find $E_3 - E_1$ first, then ask whether $\psi_1$ and $\psi_3$ have the same or opposite symmetry about $x = L/2$.
hint: The cross term in $\langle x\rangle$ involves $\int \psi_1\,(x - L/2)\,\psi_3\,dx$.
solution: $E_3 - E_1 = 8E_1$, so the density oscillates at $8E_1/\hbar$. Both $\psi_1$ and $\psi_3$ are even about $L/2$, so the cross-term integral for $\langle x\rangle$ vanishes and $\langle x\rangle = L/2$ at all times.

@quiz num
prompt: A particle in an infinite well has $E_n = n^2E_1$ with $E_1 = 0.50\ \text{eV}$. Its state is $\Psi = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}\psi_1 + \sqrt{\frac23}\,\psi_3$ at $t=0$. Find $\langle E\rangle$ in eV. Give an exact value.
answer: 19/6
check: sum(abs(c)**2*n**2*0.5 for c, n in zip([1/sqrt(3), sqrt(2/3)], [1, 3]))
unit: eV
hint: The probabilities of $E_1$ and $E_3$ are $|c_1|^2$ and $|c_3|^2$.
hint: $E_3 = 9E_1 = 4.5\ \text{eV}$.
solution: $\langle E\rangle = \tfrac13(0.50) + \tfrac23(4.50) = \tfrac16 + 3 = \tfrac{19}{6} \approx 3.17\ \text{eV}$, the same at every time.
