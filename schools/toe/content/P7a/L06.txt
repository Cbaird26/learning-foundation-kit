@article
A particle with $E < V_0$ approaching a barrier is classically turned back. Quantum mechanically, the wavefunction decays inside the barrier but does not vanish, so some probability emerges on the far side. This is **tunneling**, and it drives nuclear alpha decay and the scanning tunneling microscope.

### Scattering states and current

Free-particle waves $e^{ikx}$ cannot be normalized, so we compare **fluxes**. The **probability current** is

$$J = \frac{\hbar}{m}\,\mathrm{Im}\!\left(\psi^{*}\,\frac{d\psi}{dx}\right).$$

For $\psi = Ae^{ikx}$, $J = |A|^2\hbar k/m$: density times velocity. With incident, reflected and transmitted currents, the **reflection coefficient** is $R = |J_{\text{refl}}|/J_{\text{inc}}$ and the **transmission coefficient** is $T = J_{\text{trans}}/J_{\text{inc}}$, with $R + T = 1$.

### The potential step

Take $V = 0$ for $x<0$ and $V = V_0$ for $x>0$, with $E > V_0$ and a wave incident from the left:

$$\psi = e^{ik_1x} + r\,e^{-ik_1x}\ (x<0), \qquad \psi = t\,e^{ik_2x}\ (x>0),$$

with $k_1 = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$ and $k_2 = \sqrt{2m(E-V_0)}/\hbar$. Continuity of $\psi$ at $x=0$ gives $1 + r = t$; continuity of $\psi'$ gives $k_1(1-r) = k_2t$. Hence

$$r = \frac{k_1 - k_2}{k_1 + k_2}, \quad t = \frac{2k_1}{k_1+k_2}, \quad R = |r|^2, \quad T = \frac{k_2}{k_1}|t|^2 = \frac{4k_1k_2}{(k_1+k_2)^2}.$$

The factor $k_2/k_1$ in $T$ comes from the different velocities. Even though $E > V_0$, some reflection occurs, which is impossible classically.

### The rectangular barrier

Let $V = V_0$ for $0<x<L$ and $V=0$ elsewhere. For $E<V_0$ the wave inside is a mix of $e^{\pm\kappa x}$ with $\kappa = \sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}/\hbar$. Matching at both edges gives

$$T = \left[1 + \frac{V_0^2\sinh^2(\kappa L)}{4E(V_0 - E)}\right]^{-1} \;\approx\; 16\,\frac{E}{V_0}\Bigl(1 - \frac{E}{V_0}\Bigr)e^{-2\kappa L} \quad (\kappa L \gg 1).$$

Tunneling is exponentially sensitive to width and to the square root of the mass, since $\kappa \propto \sqrt{m(V_0-E)}$. For $E > V_0$, replace $\sinh$ by $\sin$ and $\kappa$ by $k_2 = \sqrt{2m(E-V_0)}/\hbar$:

$$T = \left[1 + \frac{V_0^2\sin^2(k_2L)}{4E(E - V_0)}\right]^{-1},$$

which equals $1$ when $k_2L = n\pi$ (resonant transmission).

### Limiting-case checks

- $V_0 \to 0$ or $L \to 0$: $T \to 1$ (no obstacle).
- $L \to \infty$ with $E<V_0$: $T \to 0$.
- $E \to V_0$: $T \to \left[1 + mV_0L^2/(2\hbar^2)\right]^{-1}$.
- Step with $k_2 = k_1$ ($V_0=0$): $R = 0$.

@example
title: A potential step above the barrier energy
problem: A particle with $E = 2V_0$ approaches a step of height $V_0$ from the left. Find $R$ and $T$ and check the result.
step: The wave numbers are $k_1 = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$ and $k_2 = \sqrt{2m(E - V_0)}/\hbar = k_1\sqrt{1 - V_0/E} = k_1/\sqrt2 \approx 0.707\,k_1$.
step: Match $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at $x = 0$ to get $r = \dfrac{k_1 - k_2}{k_1 + k_2} = \dfrac{1 - 0.707}{1 + 0.707} \approx 0.172$.
step: Then $R = r^2 \approx 0.0294$ and $T = \dfrac{4k_1k_2}{(k_1+k_2)^2} = \dfrac{4(0.707)}{(1.707)^2} \approx 0.9706$.
step: Check: $R + T = 1$. Limiting case: letting $V_0 \to 0$ makes $k_2 \to k_1$ and $R \to 0$, as it should.

@example
title: Tunneling of an electron through a barrier
problem: An electron with $E = 1.0\ \text{eV}$ meets a barrier of height $V_0 = 2.0\ \text{eV}$ and width $L = 0.50\ \text{nm}$. Estimate $T$ with the thick-barrier formula and compare with the exact one. Take $\hbar = 1.055\times10^{-34}\ \text{J s}$, $m = 9.109\times10^{-31}\ \text{kg}$.
step: $\kappa = \sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}/\hbar = \sqrt{2(9.109\times10^{-31})(1.602\times10^{-19})}/(1.055\times10^{-34}) \approx 5.12\ \text{nm}^{-1}$.
step: $\kappa L \approx 2.56$, which is large enough for the thick-barrier formula. The prefactor is $16\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac12 = 4$ and $e^{-2\kappa L} = e^{-5.12} \approx 0.0060$, so $T \approx 0.024$.
step: The exact formula has $\frac{V_0^2}{4E(V_0-E)} = 1$ and $\sinh^2(2.56) \approx 41.4$, giving $T = 1/(1 + 41.4) \approx 0.0236$. The estimate is within about $1.5\%$.
step: Doubling the width to $1.0\ \text{nm}$ would give $T \approx 4e^{-10.2} \approx 1.4\times10^{-4}$, about $170$ times smaller. This is why tunneling currents are so sensitive to distance.

@mistakes
- Writing $T = |t|^2$ for a step. The transmitted current also carries the factor $k_2/k_1$.
- Concluding that a particle with $E < V_0$ cannot cross, or that it "loses energy" inside the barrier. The transmitted particle has the original energy.
- Matching only $\psi$ and forgetting $\psi'$, which leaves too few equations.
- Using the thick-barrier approximation when $\kappa L$ is of order $1$ or smaller.

answer: 1/9
check: lin([[1, -1], [-2.0, -1.0]], [-1, -2.0])[0]**2
hint: Continuity of $\psi$ gives $1 + r = t$ and continuity of $\psi'$ gives $k_1(1 - r) = k_2 t$.
hint: Eliminate $t$ to get $r = (k_1 - k_2)/(k_1 + k_2)$, then square it.
solution: $r = \dfrac{2 - 1}{2 + 1} = \dfrac13$, so $R = r^2 = \dfrac19$. Then $T = \dfrac89$, and $\dfrac{4k_1k_2}{(k_1 + k_2)^2} = \dfrac{8}{9}$ agrees.

@practice self
prompt: Starting from the exact barrier formula $T = \left[1 + \dfrac{V_0^2\sinh^2\kappa L}{4E(V_0-E)}\right]^{-1}$ for $E<V_0$, check its behaviour in the limits $L\to0$, $L\to\infty$ and $E\to V_0$. State what each limit says physically.
hint: Expand $\sinh x$ for small $x$, and note that $\kappa\to0$ as $E\to V_0$.
hint: For $E\to V_0$ use $\sinh^2\kappa L\approx \kappa^2L^2$ and $\kappa^2 = 2m(V_0-E)/\hbar^2$.
solution: As $L\to0$, $\sinh\kappa L\to0$, so $T\to1$: no barrier, full transmission. As $L\to\infty$, $\sinh^2\kappa L\to\infty$, so $T\to0$ like $e^{-2\kappa L}$: a thick barrier blocks the particle. As $E\to V_0$, $\kappa\to0$ and $\sinh^2\kappa L\approx\kappa^2L^2 = 2m(V_0-E)L^2/\hbar^2$; the factor $V_0-E$ cancels the denominator and $T\to[1 + mV_0L^2/(2\hbar^2)]^{-1}$, finite and less than $1$ for $L>0$.
rubric: Gets $T\to1$ for $L\to0$ and $T\to0$ for $L\to\infty$, with the physical meaning.
rubric: Expands $\sinh$ correctly for small $\kappa L$ and cancels the factor $V_0-E$ in the $E\to V_0$ limit.
rubric: Obtains $[1 + mV_0L^2/(2\hbar^2)]^{-1}$ and notes it is between $0$ and $1$.

@quiz expr
prompt: A wave $e^{ik_1x}$ is incident from the left on a potential step, where the wave number on the right is $k_2$ (with $E > V_0$). Give the reflection coefficient $R$ in terms of $k_1$ and $k_2$.
vars: k_1, k_2
lead: $R =$
answer: ((k_1 - k_2)/(k_1 + k_2))^2
check: lambda k_1, k_2: lin([[1, -1], [-k_1, -k_2]], [-1, -k_1])[0]**2
hint: Match $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at the step to find the reflected amplitude $r$.
hint: $R = |r|^2$ with $r = (k_1 - k_2)/(k_1 + k_2)$.
solution: $1 + r = t$ and $k_1(1 - r) = k_2t$ give $r = \dfrac{k_1 - k_2}{k_1 + k_2}$, so $R = \left(\dfrac{k_1 - k_2}{k_1 + k_2}\right)^2$.
