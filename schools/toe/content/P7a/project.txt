@project
title: Bound states of a finite quantum well, solved and verified
overview: You will model an electron in a one-dimensional finite square well, solve it analytically by matching the wavefunction and its derivative, and then verify every result numerically by diagonalizing a finite-difference Hamiltonian. The numerical wavefunctions must be explicitly normalized and used to compute probabilities and the uncertainty product. You finish with a short report that tests limiting cases and explains any disagreement.
goal: Derive and solve the even and odd quantization conditions for the finite well, and state how many bound states exist and what their parities are.
goal: Build a numerical Hamiltonian, obtain energies and wavefunctions, and show agreement with the analytic energies to a stated tolerance.
goal: Normalize every wavefunction explicitly and use it to compute the forbidden-region probability, $\langle x^2\rangle$, $\sigma_x$, $\sigma_p$ and the product $\sigma_x\sigma_p/\hbar$.
goal: Test at least two limiting cases and a convergence study, and explain the trends you see.
step: Set up the model: an electron in a well of half-width $a = 0.30$ nm and depth $V_0 = 5.0$ eV, using $\hbar^2/2m = 0.0381\ \text{eV nm}^2$. Compute $z_0 = a\sqrt{V_0/(\hbar^2/2m)}$ and predict the number and parity of the bound states from $(n-1)\pi/2 < z_0$.
step: Analytic solution: write $\psi$ in the three regions, keep only the decaying exponential outside, impose continuity of $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at $x = \pm a$, and derive $z\tan z = \sqrt{z_0^2 - z^2}$ (even) and $-z\cot z = \sqrt{z_0^2 - z^2}$ (odd). Solve each with a root finder and convert to energies with $E = V_0(z/z_0)^2$.
step: Analytic normalization: for each bound state express the outside amplitude in terms of the inside amplitude using continuity, then fix the remaining constant from $\int|\psi|^2dx = 1$, splitting the integral into inside and outside parts. Write the result as a formula you can evaluate.
step: Numerical solution: discretize $x \in [-X, X]$ on a uniform grid with spacing $\Delta x$. Build the tridiagonal Hamiltonian with diagonal $2\hbar^2/(2m\Delta x^2) + V_i$ and off-diagonals $-\hbar^2/(2m\Delta x^2)$, diagonalize it with numpy (for example `numpy.linalg.eigh`), and keep the eigenvalues with $0 < E < V_0$.
step: Explicit normalization check: rescale each eigenvector so that $\sum_i|\psi_i|^2\Delta x = 1$, and then confirm with an independent integration (trapezoid rule) that the total probability is $1$ to within $10^{-6}$ and that different states are orthogonal. Use the normalized states to compute the probability of $|x| > a$, $\langle x^2\rangle$, $\sigma_x$, $\sigma_p$ (from finite differences) and check $\sigma_x\sigma_p \ge \hbar/2$.
step: Compare and plot: tabulate analytic and numerical energies with relative errors, and plot each $|\psi|^2$ shifted to its energy over the potential.
step: Limits and convergence: raise $V_0$ (for example 5, 50, 500 eV) and show the energies approach the infinite-well values $n^2\pi^2\hbar^2/[2m(2a)^2]$. Halve $\Delta x$ repeatedly to see how the energy error scales, and vary $X$ to confirm that results do not depend on the box size.
step: Write the report: state conventions and constants, include the derivation, tables and figures, and a discussion that identifies the dominant sources of error.
deliverable: A written report (PDF or notebook) containing the analytic derivation, the comparison table, labelled figures, and a statement of all constants, units and conventions.
deliverable: Commented Python code that reproduces every number and figure, including a printout of the normalization and orthogonality checks.
deliverable: A one-paragraph discussion of the limiting cases and convergence tests, including the observed error scaling with $\Delta x$.
rubric: Analytic correctness: the wavefunction in each region is right, both $\psi$ and $\psi'$ are matched, the quantization conditions are correct, and the number and parity of bound states are stated and justified.
rubric: Numerical method: the discretization is defined with $\Delta x$ and $X$ reported, and the numerical energies agree with the analytic ones to a stated tolerance (for example $0.5\%$) in a complete table.
rubric: Explicit normalization: each state is normalized by an explicit sum or integral, the printout shows total probability and orthogonality, and the normalized states are used for all probabilities and expectation values.
rubric: Physical checks: the uncertainty product is at least $\hbar/2$ for every state, and the forbidden-region probability is reported and interpreted.
rubric: Limits and convergence: at least two limiting cases are tested numerically with the trend explained, and discrepancies are traced to a cause.

@review
Every problem in this course follows one pattern: **write the Hamiltonian, apply boundary conditions, find the allowed energies, normalize, then compute probabilities and averages.** Use this guide to test whether you can do each item from a blank page.

### Week by week

1. **Amplitudes.** Add amplitudes for indistinguishable alternatives and probabilities for distinguishable ones; $P = |c|^2$ and $\sum|c_n|^2 = 1$; $\lambda = h/p$.
2. **Wavefunctions.** $\rho = |\psi|^2$; $\int|\psi|^2dx = 1$; $\langle f\rangle = \int f|\psi|^2dx$; $\langle p\rangle = \int\psi^*(-i\hbar\psi')dx$; $\sigma_x^2 = \langle x^2\rangle - \langle x\rangle^2$.
3. **Schrödinger evolution.** $i\hbar\,\partial_t\Psi = \hat H\Psi$. Stationary states $\psi_ne^{-iE_nt/\hbar}$ have constant density; superpositions oscillate at $\omega = \Delta E/\hbar$; the $|c_n|^2$ never change.
4. **Infinite well.** $E_n = n^2\pi^2\hbar^2/2mL^2$, $\psi_n = \sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L)$, $c_n = \int\psi_n\Psi\,dx$.
5. **Finite wells.** $\psi$ and $\psi'$ are continuous; $z\tan z = \sqrt{z_0^2 - z^2}$ (even) and $-z\cot z = \sqrt{z_0^2 - z^2}$ (odd); at least one bound state; $V_0\to\infty$ recovers the infinite well.
6. **Tunneling.** Currents give $R + T = 1$; step $R = [(k_1-k_2)/(k_1+k_2)]^2$; barrier $T \approx 16\frac{E}{V_0}(1 - \frac{E}{V_0})e^{-2\kappa L}$.
7. **Operators.** Hermitian operators have real eigenvalues; outcomes are eigenvalues with probabilities $|c_n|^2$; $[\hat x,\hat p] = i\hbar$.
8. **Uncertainty.** $\sigma_A\sigma_B \ge \frac12|\langle[\hat A,\hat B]\rangle|$; commuting observables are compatible.
9. **Oscillator.** $E_n = (n+\frac12)\hbar\omega$, $x_0 = \sqrt{\hbar/m\omega}$, turning points $x_0\sqrt{2n+1}$, leakage into the forbidden region.
10. **Ladder operators.** $[\hat a,\hat a^\dagger] = 1$; $\hat H = \hbar\omega(\hat N + \frac12)$; $\hat a|n\rangle = \sqrt n|n-1\rangle$; $\hat x = \frac{x_0}{\sqrt2}(\hat a + \hat a^\dagger)$.
11. **Angular momentum.** $L^2 = \hbar^2l(l+1)$, $L_z = m\hbar$ with $2l+1$ values; $\hat L_\pm$ ladders; spin-$\frac12$.
12. **Hydrogen.** $E_n = -13.6\ \text{eV}/n^2$; $l \le n-1$; $n^2$ orbitals; $P(r) = 4\pi r^2|\psi|^2$; $\Delta l = \pm1$, $\Delta m = 0,\pm1$.

### Self-check

- Normalize a given $\psi$ and compute $\langle x\rangle$, $\langle p\rangle$ and $\sigma_x$ without notes.
- Explain why a superposition's density oscillates while its energy probabilities stay fixed.
- Set up the matching conditions for a step, a finite well and a barrier, and check one limit.
- Evaluate a commutator by acting on a test function.
- Get $\langle x^2\rangle$ in $|n\rangle$ with ladder operators.
- List the allowed $(n,l,m)$ and decide whether a transition is allowed.

### Common errors

- Using $\psi$ instead of $|\psi|^2$, or reading probabilities before normalizing.
- Calling a stationary state "constant" (its phase rotates; its density does not).
- Setting $\psi' = 0$ at finite walls, or mixing up $k$ and $\kappa$.
- Using $T = |t|^2$ for a step without the factor $k_2/k_1$.
- Treating $\langle A\rangle$ as a possible outcome; ordering slips in commutators.
- Writing $E_n = n\hbar\omega$, $|\mathbf L| = l\hbar$, or $2l$ states instead of $2l+1$.

prompt: An electron is in an infinite well of width $L = 1.0\ \text{nm}$ with initial state $\Psi(x,0) = A\,x(L-x)$ for $0\le x\le L$. Take $\hbar = 1.055\times10^{-34}\ \text{J s}$, $m = 9.109\times10^{-31}\ \text{kg}$ and $1\ \text{eV} = 1.602\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}$. Normalize the state and find the mean energy $\langle E\rangle = \langle\hat p^2/2m\rangle$ in eV.
answer: 0.381
check: (1.055e-34)**2/(2*9.109e-31)*quad(lambda s: 30*(1 - 2*s)**2, 0, 1)/(1e-9)**2/1.602e-19
unit: eV
tol: 1%
hint: Normalize first: $|A|^2\int_0^Lx^2(L-x)^2dx = 1$ gives $A = \sqrt{30/L^5}$.
hint: Since $\Psi$ vanishes at the walls, $\langle E\rangle = \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\int|\Psi'|^2dx$, and $\Psi' = A(L - 2x)$.
hint: $\int_0^L(L-2x)^2dx = L^3/3$, so $\langle E\rangle = 5\hbar^2/(mL^2)$.
solution: With $A^2 = 30/L^5$, $\langle E\rangle = \dfrac{\hbar^2}{2m}\cdot\dfrac{30}{L^5}\cdot\dfrac{L^3}{3} = \dfrac{5\hbar^2}{mL^2} = 6.11\times10^{-20}\ \text{J} = 0.381\ \text{eV}$. This is only $1.3\%$ above $E_1 = 0.376\ \text{eV}$, because the state is $99.86\%$ the ground state ($P_1 = 960/\pi^6$).

@exam num
prompt: A harmonic oscillator is prepared at $t = 0$ in $\Psi = (|0\rangle + |1\rangle)/\sqrt2$, where $|0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle$ are the normalized ground and first excited number states. Using $\hat x = \frac{x_0}{\sqrt2}(\hat a + \hat a^\dagger)$ and $\hat p = \frac{i\hbar}{\sqrt2\,x_0}(\hat a^\dagger - \hat a)$, find $\sigma_x\sigma_p/\hbar$ at $t = 0$. Give an exact value, and check it against the uncertainty principle.
answer: sqrt(2)/2
check: (lambda a, v: (lambda x, p: np.sqrt((v @ x @ x @ v - (v @ x @ v)**2).real)*np.sqrt((v @ p @ p @ v - (v @ p @ v)**2).real))((a + a.T)/np.sqrt(2), 1j*(a.T - a)/np.sqrt(2)))(np.diag(np.sqrt(np.arange(1, 14)), 1), np.array([1, 1] + [0]*12)/np.sqrt(2))
hint: The needed matrix elements are $\langle1|\hat x|0\rangle = x_0/\sqrt2$ and $\langle1|\hat p|0\rangle = i\hbar/(\sqrt2\,x_0)$; the operators $\hat x^2$ and $\hat p^2$ do not connect $n=0$ with $n=1$.
hint: $\langle x\rangle = x_0/\sqrt2$ and $\langle p\rangle = 0$. For the second moments use $\langle n|\hat x^2|n\rangle = (n+\tfrac12)x_0^2$ and $\langle n|\hat p^2|n\rangle = (n+\tfrac12)\hbar^2/x_0^2$, averaged with weights $\frac12$ each.
solution: $\langle x^2\rangle = \tfrac12(\tfrac12 + \tfrac32)x_0^2 = x_0^2$, so $\sigma_x^2 = x_0^2 - \tfrac12x_0^2 = \tfrac12x_0^2$. Likewise $\langle p^2\rangle = \hbar^2/x_0^2$ and $\langle p\rangle = 0$, so $\sigma_p = \hbar/x_0$. Then $\sigma_x\sigma_p = \hbar/\sqrt2 \approx 0.707\,\hbar$, which is above the minimum $\hbar/2$ as required.
