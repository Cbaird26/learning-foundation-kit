@article
Energy is conserved. Thermodynamics adds a bookkeeping rule: energy crosses the boundary of a system in two ways, as **heat** $Q$ (driven by a temperature difference) and as **work** $W$ (a force acting through a displacement). What the system stores is its **internal energy** $U$.

### Sign convention

This course uses

$$\Delta U = Q + W,$$

where $Q$ is heat added **to** the system and $W$ is work done **on** the system. Compressing a gas gives $W > 0$; letting it expand gives $W < 0$. Many books write $\Delta U = Q - W_{\text{by}}$, with $W_{\text{by}} = -W$. Both are correct, but never mix them in one problem: write your convention at the top of the page.

### Work

For a slow (quasi-static) change of volume at system pressure $P$,

$$\delta W = -P\,dV, \qquad W = -\int_{V_i}^{V_f} P\,dV,$$

which is minus the area under the curve on a $P$–$V$ diagram. The area depends on the path, so $W$ and $Q$ separately depend on the path. But $U$ is a **state function**: $\Delta U$ depends only on the initial and final states. That is why $Q + W$ comes out path independent.

### Heat capacities and the ideal gas

The heat capacity at constant volume is $C_V = (\partial U/\partial T)_V$. For an ideal gas, $U$ depends only on $T$:

$$U = \tfrac{f}{2}\,nRT, \qquad \Delta U = nC_{V,m}\Delta T \ \text{ for every process.}$$

Here $f$ counts the quadratic energy terms: $f=3$ for a monatomic gas and $f=5$ for a diatomic gas at room temperature, so $C_{V,m} = \tfrac f2 R$. Heating at constant pressure also does work $-P\Delta V = -nR\Delta T$, hence

$$C_{P,m} = C_{V,m} + R, \qquad \gamma = \frac{C_P}{C_V} = \frac{f+2}{f}.$$

### Four standard processes (ideal gas)

| Process | $W$ (on gas) | $Q$ | $\Delta U$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isochoric ($V$ fixed) | $0$ | $nC_{V,m}\Delta T$ | $nC_{V,m}\Delta T$ |
| Isobaric ($P$ fixed) | $-P\Delta V = -nR\Delta T$ | $nC_{P,m}\Delta T$ | $nC_{V,m}\Delta T$ |
| Isothermal ($T$ fixed) | $-nRT\ln(V_f/V_i)$ | $+nRT\ln(V_f/V_i)$ | $0$ |
| Adiabatic ($Q = 0$) | $\Delta U$ | $0$ | $nC_{V,m}\Delta T$ |

For a **reversible** adiabatic process, $PV^{\gamma}$ and $TV^{\gamma-1}$ are constant.

### An energy-balance routine

1. Name the system and its boundary.
2. Write the sign convention.
3. For each step, find $\Delta U$ from the temperature change (ideal gas) and $W$ from the area.
4. Get $Q = \Delta U - W$ and check its sign against physical intuition.
5. For a cycle, $\Delta U = 0$, so $Q_{\text{net}} = -W_{\text{net}}$: a clockwise loop on a $P$–$V$ diagram absorbs net heat equal to its enclosed area and delivers that much work.

@example
title: Isothermal expansion of a monatomic gas
problem: $1.00$ mol of a monatomic ideal gas at $300$ K expands slowly and isothermally to twice its volume. Find $\Delta U$, $W$ and $Q$. Take $R = 8.314$ J/(mol K).
step: The temperature is constant and $U$ of an ideal gas depends only on $T$, so $\Delta U = 0$.
step: The work done on the gas is $W = -\int P\,dV = -nRT\ln(V_f/V_i) = -(1.00)(8.314)(300)\ln 2 \approx -1.73\times10^3$ J.
step: The first law gives $Q = \Delta U - W = 0 + 1.73\times10^3$ J $= +1.73$ kJ.
step: Interpretation: the gas does $1.73$ kJ of work on its surroundings ($W<0$) and takes in exactly that much heat ($Q>0$) so its energy does not change.

@example
title: Heating a gas at constant pressure
problem: $2.00$ mol of a monatomic ideal gas is heated at constant pressure from $300$ K to $400$ K. Find $\Delta U$, $W$ and $Q$.
step: For an ideal gas $\Delta U = nC_{V,m}\Delta T = (2.00)(\tfrac32)(8.314)(100) \approx 2494$ J.
step: At constant pressure $W = -P\Delta V = -nR\Delta T = -(2.00)(8.314)(100) \approx -1663$ J. The gas expands, so $W<0$.
step: $Q = \Delta U - W = 2494 + 1663 = 4157$ J.
step: Check with $Q = nC_{P,m}\Delta T = (2.00)(\tfrac52)(8.314)(100) = 4157$ J. The two routes agree.

@mistakes
- Mixing conventions: using $\Delta U = Q - W_{\text{by}}$ but inserting the work done on the gas (or the reverse).
- Assuming $\Delta U = 0$ for every isothermal process. That holds for an ideal gas, but not, for example, for ice melting at $0\,^\circ$C.
- Using $C_V$ for heat added at constant pressure; you need $C_P$ there, while $\Delta U = nC_V\Delta T$ holds for every ideal-gas process.
- Treating $Q$ or $W$ as state functions and trying to assign them to a single state.

@practice num
prompt: A gas absorbs $500$ J of heat while doing $200$ J of work on its surroundings. Using $\Delta U = Q + W$ with $W$ the work done on the gas, find $\Delta U$ in joules.
answer: 300
check: 500 + (-200)
unit: J
hint: "Does $200$ J of work on its surroundings" means the work done on the gas is negative.
hint: $W = -200$ J and $Q = +500$ J.
solution: $\Delta U = Q + W = 500 + (-200) = +300$ J.

@practice num
prompt: A gas at constant pressure $2.00\times10^{5}$ Pa expands from $1.00\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3$ to $4.00\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3$. What is the work done on the gas, in joules? Give the sign.
answer: -600
check: -quad(lambda V: 2.0e5, 1.0e-3, 4.0e-3)
unit: J
hint: At constant pressure the integral $\int P\,dV$ is just $P\,\Delta V$.
hint: $W = -P\Delta V$, and the gas expands, so expect a negative number.
solution: $W = -P\Delta V = -(2.00\times10^{5})(3.00\times10^{-3}) = -600$ J. The gas does $600$ J of work on its surroundings.

@practice expr
prompt: An ideal gas of $n$ moles at fixed temperature $T$ is compressed or expanded slowly from volume $V_i$ to volume $V_f$. Write the work done **on** the gas in terms of $n$, $R$, $T$, $V_i$ and $V_f$.
vars: n, R, T, V_i, V_f
lead: $W =$
answer: -n*R*T*ln(V_f/V_i)
check: lambda n, R, T, V_i, V_f: -quad(lambda V: n*R*T/V, V_i, V_f)
hint: Start from $W = -\int P\,dV$ and use $P = nRT/V$ for the ideal gas.
hint: With $T$ constant, $\int dV/V = \ln(V_f/V_i)$; keep the leading minus sign.
solution: $W = -\int_{V_i}^{V_f}\dfrac{nRT}{V}\,dV = -nRT\ln\dfrac{V_f}{V_i}$. It is negative for expansion ($V_f > V_i$) and positive for compression.

@practice num
prompt: $1.00$ mol of a monatomic ideal gas ($\gamma = 5/3$) at $300$ K is compressed reversibly and adiabatically from $4.0$ L to $0.50$ L. What is the final temperature in kelvin?
answer: 1200
check: root(lambda T: T*0.5**(2/3) - 300*4.0**(2/3), 100, 5000)
unit: K
hint: For a reversible adiabatic process $TV^{\gamma-1}$ is constant.
hint: Here $\gamma - 1 = 2/3$ and the volume ratio is $8$, so $T_f = T_i\,8^{2/3}$.
solution: $T_f = T_i\,(V_i/V_f)^{\gamma-1} = 300\,(8)^{2/3} = 300\times 4 = 1200$ K. The compression work equals $\Delta U = \tfrac32 nR(900\ \text{K})$ because $Q = 0$.

@practice num
prompt: A monatomic ideal gas goes around a cycle. Step $1\to2$: isobaric expansion at $2.00\times10^{5}$ Pa from $1.00\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3$ to $3.00\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3$. Step $2\to3$: cooling at constant volume until the pressure is $1.00\times10^{5}$ Pa. Step $3\to1$: a straight line on the $P$–$V$ diagram back to state 1. How much heat does the gas absorb during step $1\to2$, in joules?
answer: 1000
check: 1.5*(2.0e5*3.0e-3 - 2.0e5*1.0e-3) + quad(lambda V: 2.0e5, 1.0e-3, 3.0e-3)
unit: J
hint: For a monatomic ideal gas $U = \tfrac32 nRT = \tfrac32 PV$, so $\Delta U$ follows from the end states alone.
hint: Find $\Delta U$ and the work done on the gas ($W = -P\Delta V$), then use $Q = \Delta U - W$.
solution: $\Delta U = \tfrac32(P_2V_2 - P_1V_1) = \tfrac32(600 - 200) = 600$ J. $W = -P\Delta V = -400$ J. So $Q = \Delta U - W = 600 + 400 = 1000$ J. (Over the whole cycle $\Delta U = 0$ and the enclosed area is $\tfrac12(2.00\times10^{-3})(1.00\times10^{5}) = 100$ J, so the net heat absorbed is $+100$ J.)

@quiz num
prompt: $2.00$ mol of an ideal gas at $350$ K is compressed slowly and isothermally to one third of its original volume. Take $R = 8.314$ J/(mol K). How much heat does the gas absorb? Give the sign; a negative value means heat leaves the gas.
answer: -6394
check: quad(lambda V: 2.0*8.314*350/V, 1.0, 1/3)
unit: J
tol: 1%
hint: For an ideal gas at constant temperature $\Delta U = 0$, so $Q = -W$.
hint: $W = -nRT\ln(V_f/V_i)$ with $V_f/V_i = 1/3$; then flip the sign to get $Q$.
solution: $W = -nRT\ln(1/3) = +nRT\ln 3 = (2.00)(8.314)(350)(1.0986) \approx +6394$ J is done on the gas. Since $\Delta U = 0$, $Q = -W \approx -6394$ J: about $6.39$ kJ of heat leaves the gas.

@quiz ms
prompt: Select all statements that are correct for an ideal gas.
choice*: The change in internal energy between two states is the same along every path joining them.
choice*: For a closed loop on a $P$–$V$ diagram traversed clockwise, the net heat absorbed equals the net work done by the gas.
choice: In a reversible isothermal expansion the heat absorbed is zero.
choice*: Heat and work are individually path dependent, although their sum $Q + W$ is not.
choice: In an adiabatic compression the internal energy of the gas decreases.
hint: Decide first which quantities are state functions.
hint: Use $\Delta U = 0$ for the isothermal and cyclic cases, and $Q = 0$ for the adiabatic case.
solution: $U$ is a state function, so the statement about $\Delta U$ along every path holds; $Q$ and $W$ are not, so the statement about their path dependence holds. Around a cycle $\Delta U = 0$, giving $Q_{\rm net} = -W_{\rm net}$, which is the net work done by the gas for a clockwise loop. In an isothermal expansion $\Delta U = 0$ and $W < 0$, so $Q = -W > 0$. In an adiabatic compression $Q = 0$ and $W > 0$, so $\Delta U > 0$.
