@article
The first law only says energy is conserved. A cup of coffee cools to room temperature, and it never spontaneously heats back up, although that would conserve energy too. The second law supplies the direction.

### Two equivalent statements

- **Kelvin–Planck:** no cyclic device can absorb heat from a single reservoir and convert all of it into work.
- **Clausius:** no cyclic process has the sole effect of moving heat from a colder body to a hotter one.

### Heat engines

An engine absorbs $Q_h$ from a hot reservoir at $T_h$, delivers work $W_{\text{out}}$ and rejects $Q_c$ to a cold reservoir at $T_c$ (all as positive magnitudes). Over a cycle $\Delta U = 0$, so

$$W_{\text{out}} = Q_h - Q_c, \qquad \eta = \frac{W_{\text{out}}}{Q_h} = 1 - \frac{Q_c}{Q_h}.$$

No engine working between two reservoirs beats a reversible (Carnot) engine, whose efficiency depends only on the two temperatures:

$$\eta \le \eta_C = 1 - \frac{T_c}{T_h} \qquad (\text{kelvin}).$$

### Entropy

For a reversible process the **entropy** changes by

$$dS = \frac{\delta Q_{\rm rev}}{T}.$$

$S$ is a state function, so for any process, reversible or not, $\Delta S$ is found by integrating $\delta Q_{\rm rev}/T$ along **any reversible path** joining the same end states. Useful results:

- Heating with constant heat capacity $C$: $\Delta S = C\ln(T_f/T_i)$.
- Phase change at temperature $T$: $\Delta S = mL/T$.
- Ideal gas: $\Delta S = nC_V\ln(T_f/T_i) + nR\ln(V_f/V_i)$.
- Free expansion into vacuum: $Q = 0$, yet $\Delta S = nR\ln(V_f/V_i) > 0$, because the end states are the same as for a reversible isothermal expansion.

A **reservoir** is large enough that its temperature does not change when it exchanges heat $Q$, so its entropy change is exactly $Q/T$.

### The second law as an entropy statement

$$\Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta S_{\text{system}} + \Delta S_{\text{surroundings}} \;\ge\; 0,$$

with equality only for reversible processes. The entropy of one system can fall, as when water freezes, provided the surroundings gain at least as much. When heat $Q$ passes from a body at $T_h$ to one at $T_c$, the total change is $Q\left(\frac{1}{T_c} - \frac{1}{T_h}\right) > 0$. In week 5 we will see entropy as a count of microstates, $S = k_B\ln\Omega$.

### A recipe

1. Convert every temperature to kelvin.
2. Identify the initial and final states of the system and of each reservoir.
3. For each, invent a reversible path and add up $\int \delta Q_{\rm rev}/T$.
4. Sum the changes and read the sign: $>0$ irreversible, $=0$ reversible, $<0$ impossible.

@example
title: Heat conduction between two reservoirs
problem: $1000$ J of heat flows directly from a reservoir at $500$ K to a reservoir at $300$ K. Find the total entropy change and say whether the process is allowed.
step: Each reservoir keeps its temperature, so each has $\Delta S = Q/T$ with its own sign of $Q$.
step: The hot reservoir loses $1000$ J: $\Delta S_h = -\dfrac{1000}{500} = -2.00$ J/K.
step: The cold reservoir gains $1000$ J: $\Delta S_c = +\dfrac{1000}{300} = +3.33$ J/K.
step: The total is $\Delta S = +1.33$ J/K $> 0$, so the process is allowed and irreversible. The reverse flow would give $-1.33$ J/K, which the second law forbids.

@example
title: The Carnot limit and entropy balance
problem: An engine runs between reservoirs at $600$ K and $300$ K and absorbs $2000$ J from the hot one in each cycle. Find the largest possible work per cycle and the total entropy change for that case.
step: The largest efficiency is the Carnot value: $\eta_C = 1 - 300/600 = 0.50$.
step: The largest work is $W_{\text{out}} = 0.50\times 2000 = 1000$ J, so the engine rejects $Q_c = 2000 - 1000 = 1000$ J to the cold reservoir.
step: The engine returns to its starting state each cycle, so only the reservoirs change: $\Delta S = -\dfrac{2000}{600} + \dfrac{1000}{300} = -3.33 + 3.33 = 0$. Zero total entropy change is the signature of a reversible engine.
step: If an engine produced more than $1000$ J of work it would reject less than $1000$ J, and $\Delta S = -3.33 + Q_c/300 < 0$ would violate the second law.

@mistakes
- Using Celsius temperatures in $\eta_C = 1 - T_c/T_h$ or in $\Delta S = Q/T$.
- Computing $\Delta S$ of an irreversible process as $Q_{\text{actual}}/T$. You must use a reversible path with the same end states; in a free expansion $Q = 0$ but $\Delta S > 0$.
- Claiming that entropy can never decrease. Only the total of system plus surroundings cannot decrease.
- Writing $\Delta S = C\,\Delta T/T$ or $C\,\Delta T/T_{\text{avg}}$ instead of integrating to get $C\ln(T_f/T_i)$.

@practice num
prompt: An engine operates between a hot reservoir at $600$ K and a cold reservoir at $450$ K. What is the largest efficiency any such engine can have? Give it as a fraction of $1$, not as a percentage.
answer: 0.25
check: 1 - 450/600
hint: The upper bound is the Carnot efficiency.
hint: $\eta_C = 1 - T_c/T_h$ with both temperatures already in kelvin.
solution: $\eta_C = 1 - \dfrac{450}{600} = 0.25$.

@practice num
prompt: $0.500$ kg of ice melts reversibly at $0\,^\circ$C (take $273.15$ K) with $L_f = 3.34\times10^{5}$ J/kg. What is the entropy change of the ice, in J/K?
answer: 611.4
check: 0.500*3.34e5/273.15
unit: J/K
tol: 1%
hint: The temperature stays fixed during melting, so $\Delta S = Q/T$.
hint: $Q = mL_f$, and the sign is positive because heat flows into the ice.
solution: $\Delta S = \dfrac{mL_f}{T} = \dfrac{(0.500)(3.34\times10^{5})}{273.15} \approx 611$ J/K.

@practice num
prompt: $2.00$ kg of water is heated slowly from $20.0\,^\circ$C to $80.0\,^\circ$C. Take $c = 4186$ J/(kg K), independent of temperature. What is the entropy change of the water, in J/K?
answer: 1559
check: quad(lambda T: 2.00*4186/T, 293.15, 353.15)
unit: J/K
tol: 1%
hint: Integrate $dS = mc\,dT/T$ with temperatures in kelvin.
hint: $\Delta S = mc\ln(T_f/T_i)$ with $T_i = 293.15$ K and $T_f = 353.15$ K.
solution: $\Delta S = mc\ln\dfrac{T_f}{T_i} = (2.00)(4186)\ln\dfrac{353.15}{293.15} \approx 8372(0.1862) \approx 1559$ J/K.

@practice expr
prompt: $n$ moles of a monatomic ideal gas ($C_V = \tfrac32 R$ per mole) go from temperature $T_1$ and volume $V_1$ to temperature $T_2$ and volume $V_2$. Write the entropy change of the gas in terms of $n$, $R$, $T_1$, $T_2$, $V_1$ and $V_2$.
vars: n, R, T_1, T_2, V_1, V_2
lead: $\Delta S =$
answer: n*R*(3/2*ln(T_2/T_1) + ln(V_2/V_1))
check: lambda n, R, T_1, T_2, V_1, V_2: quad(lambda T: 1.5*n*R/T, T_1, T_2) + quad(lambda V: n*R/V, V_1, V_2)
hint: $S$ is a state function: pick a convenient reversible path, for example heat at constant volume and then expand isothermally.
hint: At constant volume $\delta Q_{\rm rev} = nC_V\,dT$; at constant temperature $\delta Q_{\rm rev} = P\,dV = nRT\,dV/V$.
solution: Heating at $V_1$ gives $\int nC_V\,dT/T = \tfrac32 nR\ln(T_2/T_1)$. Expanding isothermally at $T_2$ gives $\int nR\,dV/V = nR\ln(V_2/V_1)$. The sum is $\Delta S = nR\left[\tfrac32\ln(T_2/T_1) + \ln(V_2/V_1)\right]$.

@practice num
prompt: $1.00$ kg of water at $80.0\,^\circ$C is mixed in an insulated container with $1.00$ kg of water at $20.0\,^\circ$C. Take $c = 4186$ J/(kg K), independent of temperature. What is the total entropy change of the two samples, in J/K?
answer: 36.2
check: quad(lambda T: 1.00*4186/T, 353.15, 323.15) + quad(lambda T: 1.00*4186/T, 293.15, 323.15)
unit: J/K
tol: 1%
hint: By symmetry of the heat capacities, the final temperature is the average, $50.0\,^\circ$C or $323.15$ K.
hint: Add $mc\ln(T_f/T_i)$ for the hot sample (negative) and for the cold sample (positive).
solution: $T_f = 323.15$ K. $\Delta S = 4186\left[\ln\dfrac{323.15}{353.15} + \ln\dfrac{323.15}{293.15}\right] = 4186(-0.08878 + 0.09743) \approx +36.2$ J/K. It is positive, as it must be for an irreversible process.

@quiz num
prompt: A real engine absorbs $800$ J from a reservoir at $500$ K and rejects $600$ J to a reservoir at $300$ K in each cycle. How much entropy is generated in the reservoirs per cycle, in J/K? (The engine itself returns to its initial state.)
answer: 0.4
check: -800/500 + 600/300
unit: J/K
tol: 0.01
hint: Only the two reservoirs change state. Add $-Q_h/T_h$ and $+Q_c/T_c$.
hint: Hot reservoir: $-800/500$. Cold reservoir: $+600/300$.
solution: $\Delta S = -\dfrac{800}{500} + \dfrac{600}{300} = -1.6 + 2.0 = +0.4$ J/K. This is positive, so the engine is allowed but irreversible. Its efficiency, $200/800 = 0.25$, is below the Carnot value $0.40$.

@quiz mc
prompt: Which of the following devices is forbidden by the second law?
choice*: A cyclic engine that absorbs $1000$ J from a single reservoir at $400$ K and delivers $1000$ J of work, with no other effect.
choice: A cyclic engine of efficiency $0.30$ working between reservoirs at $600$ K and $300$ K.
choice: A refrigerator that uses electrical work to move heat from a cold interior to a warmer room.
choice: An isolated gas that expands freely into an evacuated chamber.
hint: Compare each claim with the Kelvin–Planck and Clausius statements and with the Carnot bound.
solution: The engine that converts heat from a single reservoir entirely into work, with no other effect, is forbidden by Kelvin–Planck. An efficiency of $0.30$ is below the Carnot limit $0.50$. A refrigerator is allowed when work is supplied. Free expansion has $\Delta S = nR\ln(V_f/V_i) > 0$ for the isolated gas, so it is allowed.
