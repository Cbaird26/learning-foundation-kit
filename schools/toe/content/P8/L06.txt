@article
Last week we counted microstates at fixed energy. Real systems sit in contact with surroundings at fixed temperature, so their energy fluctuates. The probability of finding a system in microstate $i$ with energy $E_i$ is then set by a **Boltzmann factor**.

### The Boltzmann distribution

$$p_i = \frac{e^{-\beta E_i}}{Z}, \qquad \beta = \frac{1}{k_BT}, \qquad Z = \sum_i e^{-\beta E_i}.$$

The sum runs over microstates. If an energy level $E_n$ has degeneracy $g_n$ it contributes $g_n e^{-\beta E_n}$ to the sum:

$$Z = \sum_n g_n\,e^{-\beta E_n}.$$

The ratio of two probabilities is $p_j/p_i = e^{-\beta(E_j - E_i)}$, so states with energy much greater than $k_BT$ are exponentially unlikely. $Z$ acts as a count of the states that are thermally accessible. Shifting every energy by the same constant $E_0$ multiplies $Z$ by $e^{-\beta E_0}$ but leaves every probability unchanged.

### The two-level system

For levels at $0$ and $\epsilon$, $Z = 1 + e^{-\beta\epsilon}$, and the upper level has probability $p_1 = e^{-\beta\epsilon}/(1 + e^{-\beta\epsilon})$. At $T\to0$ only the ground state is occupied; at $T\to\infty$ both levels have probability $\tfrac12$.

### Thermodynamics from $Z$

The mean energy, free energy and entropy follow from $Z$:

$$\langle E\rangle = \sum_i p_iE_i = -\frac{\partial \ln Z}{\partial\beta}, \qquad F = -k_BT\ln Z, \qquad S = -\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial T}\right)_V = k_B\left(\ln Z + \beta\langle E\rangle\right).$$

These agree with $F = \langle E\rangle - TS$. For the two-level system, $\langle E\rangle = \epsilon/(e^{\beta\epsilon}+1)$.

### Independent subsystems

If a system consists of independent parts A and B, a microstate is a pair of microstates, energies add, and the double sum factorizes:

$$Z = Z_A Z_B .$$

Then $\ln Z$ is a sum, so $\langle E\rangle$, $F$ and $S$ all add. For $N$ distinguishable, independent, identical parts with single-part partition function $z$, $Z = z^N$. For identical particles in the dilute classical limit one divides by $N!$; we use this in week 8.

### Procedure

1. List the energy levels and their degeneracies.
2. Write $Z = \sum g_n e^{-\beta E_n}$, with $k_B$ and the energies in matching units.
3. Compute probabilities $p_n = g_n e^{-\beta E_n}/Z$ for a level, or divide by $g_n$ for a single state.
4. Get $\langle E\rangle$ from $-\partial_\beta\ln Z$ and $F$ from $-k_BT\ln Z$.
5. Check the limits $T\to0$ ($Z\to g_0$ if $E_0 = 0$) and $T\to\infty$ ($Z\to$ the number of states).

@example
title: A three-level system
problem: A system has three non-degenerate levels at $0$, $\epsilon$ and $2\epsilon$ and is at temperature $T$ with $k_BT = \epsilon$. Find $Z$, the three probabilities and $\langle E\rangle$.
step: With $\beta\epsilon = 1$, $Z = 1 + e^{-1} + e^{-2} = 1 + 0.3679 + 0.1353 = 1.5032$.
step: The probabilities are $p_0 = 1/1.5032 = 0.665$, $p_1 = 0.3679/1.5032 = 0.245$ and $p_2 = 0.1353/1.5032 = 0.090$. They add to $1$.
step: The mean energy is $\langle E\rangle = \epsilon\,(0.245) + 2\epsilon\,(0.090) \approx 0.425\,\epsilon$.
step: Sanity check: as $T\to\infty$ the three probabilities become $\tfrac13$ and $\langle E\rangle\to\epsilon$. At our $k_BT=\epsilon$ the average is well below that, as expected.

@example
title: A degenerate excited level
problem: A system has a non-degenerate ground state at energy $0$ and a triply degenerate excited level at energy $\epsilon$. At $k_BT = \epsilon/2$, find $Z$, the probability of the excited level, and $F$.
step: With $\beta\epsilon = 2$, $Z = 1 + 3e^{-2} = 1 + 0.4060 = 1.4060$. The factor $3$ counts the three excited states.
step: The probability that the system is in the ground state is $1/1.4060 = 0.711$. The probability of the excited level, summed over its three states, is $0.4060/1.4060 = 0.289$. Each single excited state has probability $0.096$.
step: The free energy is $F = -k_BT\ln Z = -\tfrac{\epsilon}{2}\ln(1.4060) \approx -0.170\,\epsilon$.

@mistakes
- Leaving out the degeneracy factor $g_n$ when summing over levels, or counting a level's states and also multiplying by $g_n$.
- Mixing units, for example using $\epsilon$ in eV with $k_B$ in J/K, so that $\beta\epsilon$ is not dimensionless.
- Adding partition functions for independent subsystems instead of multiplying them.
- Treating $e^{-\beta E_i}$ as a probability without dividing by $Z$, or reading the probability of a degenerate level as the probability of one of its states.

answer: epsilon/(exp(beta*epsilon) + 1)
check: lambda epsilon, beta: -nderiv(lambda b: log(1 + exp(-b*epsilon)), beta)

@practice self
prompt: Two independent subsystems A and B have partition functions $Z_A$ and $Z_B$, and their energies add. Show that the combined system has $Z = Z_AZ_B$ and that $\langle E\rangle = \langle E_A\rangle + \langle E_B\rangle$.
hint: A microstate of the combined system is a pair $(a, b)$ with energy $E_a + E_b$.
hint: Write $e^{-\beta(E_a+E_b)} = e^{-\beta E_a}e^{-\beta E_b}$ so that the double sum splits. Then take $\ln$ and differentiate.
solution: A microstate of the whole is a pair $(a,b)$ with $E = E_a + E_b$. Then $Z = \sum_{a,b} e^{-\beta(E_a + E_b)} = \left(\sum_a e^{-\beta E_a}\right)\left(\sum_b e^{-\beta E_b}\right) = Z_AZ_B$. Hence $\ln Z = \ln Z_A + \ln Z_B$, and $\langle E\rangle = -\partial_\beta\ln Z = -\partial_\beta\ln Z_A - \partial_\beta\ln Z_B = \langle E_A\rangle + \langle E_B\rangle$. Likewise $F = F_A + F_B$.
rubric: Labels combined microstates as pairs $(a,b)$ with additive energies.
rubric: Factorizes the double sum into a product of two single sums, justified by the exponent splitting.
rubric: Takes the logarithm and the $\beta$-derivative to obtain additive mean energies.

@quiz num

answer: 0.5379
check: sum(E*g*math.exp(-E) for E, g in [(0, 1), (1, 2), (2, 1)])/sum(g*math.exp(-E) for E, g in [(0, 1), (1, 2), (2, 1)])
tol: 0.0005

hint: $Z = 1 + 2e^{-1} + e^{-2}$. You may notice that $Z = (1+e^{-1})^2$.

This equals twice the mean energy of one two-level system, $2/(e+1)=0.5379$, because the level scheme is that of two independent two-level systems.

prompt: Select all correct statements about a system with a finite number of states.
choice*: As $T\to0$ with the ground-state energy set to zero, $Z$ approaches the degeneracy of the ground state.

choice*: The Helmholtz free energy is $F = -k_BT\ln Z$.
choice: For two independent subsystems, $Z = Z_A + Z_B$.
hint: Consider the limits $\beta\to\infty$ and $\beta\to0$ of each Boltzmann factor.
hint: Probabilities depend only on energy differences. Independent subsystems multiply their counts.
solution: At $T\to0$ only the ground-state Boltzmann factors survive, each equal to $1$, giving $g_0$. At $T\to\infty$ every factor tends to $1$, giving the number of states. A common shift cancels between numerator and $Z$. $F = -k_BT\ln Z$ is the standard result. Independent subsystems satisfy $Z = Z_AZ_B$, not a sum.
