overview: Pick one simple model, $N$ non-interacting particles in a box at fixed density, and compare its Maxwell–Boltzmann, Fermi–Dirac and Bose–Einstein descriptions numerically. Find the temperature below which the classical description fails, test your estimate against exact numerical results, and explain the valid temperature regime in physical terms.
goal: Compute and compare the chemical potential, occupation numbers and pressure given by the three statistics for the same box.
goal: Locate the crossover temperature from $n\lambda_T^3$ and check it against the numerical deviation from classical behavior.
goal: Explain, quantitatively and in words, where each description is valid and why fermions and bosons deviate from the classical result in opposite directions.
step: Choose one system with mass $m$, number density $n$ and spin degeneracy $g_s$ (for example conduction electrons in copper, liquid helium-3 as a fermion gas, liquid helium-4 as a boson gas with $g_s=1$, or a cold rubidium gas). Write down all constants in SI units.
step: Write Python functions for $\lambda_T = h/\sqrt{2\pi mk_BT}$ and $n\lambda_T^3$, and define the degeneracy temperature $T_d$ by $n\lambda_{T_d}^3 = g_s$. Evaluate $T_d$ for your system.
step: Use the dimensionless condition $n\lambda_T^3/g_s = \frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty \frac{\sqrt u\,du}{\zeta^{-1}e^{u}\pm1}$ with fugacity $\zeta = e^{\beta\mu}$ (upper sign for fermions, lower for bosons). Solve it for $\zeta(T)$ with a root finder, and compare $\mu/k_BT$ for the three statistics, where the Maxwell–Boltzmann value is $\ln(n\lambda_T^3/g_s)$.
step: Treat bosons only for $T>T_c$, where $\mu<0$, and compute $T_c$ from $n\lambda_{T_c}^3 = g_s\,\zeta(3/2)$. For fermions check the low-temperature limit $\mu\to\varepsilon_F = \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\left(6\pi^2n/g_s\right)^{2/3}$.
step: Compute the pressure from $P = \tfrac23E/V$, which gives $\dfrac{P}{nk_BT} = \tfrac23\,\dfrac{\int u^{3/2}(\zeta^{-1}e^u\pm1)^{-1}du}{\int u^{1/2}(\zeta^{-1}e^u\pm1)^{-1}du}$. Plot $P/(nk_BT)$ against $T/T_d$ on a logarithmic axis for all three statistics.
step: Compare the numerical deviation at high $T$ with the first quantum correction $P/(nk_BT)\approx 1\pm n\lambda_T^3/(2^{5/2}g_s)$ (upper sign for fermions). Find the temperature at which the deviation reaches $10\%$ and compare it with $T_d$.
step: Repeat the $T_d$ calculation for at least two other systems (for example nitrogen gas at 1 atm and a degenerate metal), and decide which are classical at $300$ K.
step: Write the report and rehearse a one-minute spoken explanation of the main result.

## Key results, week by week

1. **Temperature and heat.** Zeroth law; kelvin scale; $PV = Nk_BT$; $\langle K\rangle = \tfrac32k_BT$; $Q = mc\,\Delta T$ and $Q = mL$; calorimetry as energy conservation.
2. **First law.** $\Delta U = Q + W$ with $W$ done on the system; $W = -\int P\,dV$; the four ideal-gas processes; $C_P = C_V + nR$; $TV^{\gamma-1}$ constant on a reversible adiabat.
3. **Entropy and second law.** $\eta_C = 1 - T_c/T_h$; $dS = \delta Q_{\rm rev}/T$ along a reversible path; $\Delta S_{\rm total}\ge0$.
4. **Potentials.** $U$, $H$, $F$, $G$ with their natural variables; Maxwell relations from exact differentials; $F$ at fixed $(T,V)$ and $G$ at fixed $(T,P)$.
5. **Microstates and ensembles.** $S = k_B\ln\Omega$; $1/T = \partial S/\partial E$; Stirling's approximation; binomial and stars-and-bars counting.
6. **Partition functions.** $Z = \sum g_ne^{-\beta E_n}$; $p_i = e^{-\beta E_i}/Z$; $\langle E\rangle = -\partial_\beta\ln Z$; $F = -k_BT\ln Z$; $Z = Z_AZ_B$ for independent parts.
7. **Canonical ensemble.** The bath derivation; $\sigma_E^2 = k_BT^2C_V$; equipartition; oscillator and paramagnet.
8. **Ideal gas.** $z = V/\lambda_T^3$, $Z_N = z^N/N!$; $P$, $\langle E\rangle$, the Sackur–Tetrode entropy, $\mu = k_BT\ln(n\lambda_T^3)$; validity $n\lambda_T^3\ll1$.
9. **Grand canonical ensemble.** $\Xi$, $\langle N\rangle = k_BT\,\partial_\mu\ln\Xi$, $\Phi = -PV$, Poisson number fluctuations, orbital occupations.
10. **Quantum statistics.** Fermi–Dirac, Bose–Einstein and Maxwell–Boltzmann occupations; $\varepsilon_F$, $T_F$ and $T_c$; Planck's law.
11. **Heat capacity.** $C_V = \sigma_E^2/k_BT^2$; frozen degrees of freedom; Schottky and Einstein curves; the $T^3$ phonon and linear-$T$ electron laws.
12. **Phase transitions.** First-order versus continuous; Clausius–Clapeyron; Landau theory; finite-size rounding.

## Self-check list

- I can state my sign convention and use it consistently in an energy balance.
- I can find $\Delta S$ for an irreversible process by choosing a reversible path.
- I can derive a Maxwell relation from the correct potential.
- I can write $Z$ for a finite set of levels with degeneracies, and then get $p_i$, $\langle E\rangle$, $F$ and $S$.
- I can derive $\langle E\rangle$ and its variance from $\ln Z$ and connect the variance to $C_V$.
- I can give a number that says whether a classical ideal gas is valid.
- I can compare Fermi–Dirac, Bose–Einstein and Maxwell–Boltzmann occupations for a given $\beta(\varepsilon-\mu)$.
- I can read a $C(T)$ curve and say what is active, frozen or collective.
- I can explain why a finite system has no true phase transition.

## Most common errors

- Using Celsius in any formula that contains $T$ (gas law, $\eta_C$, $\Delta S = Q/T$, Boltzmann factors).
- Mixing sign conventions for work, or treating $Q$ and $W$ as state functions.
- Computing the entropy of an irreversible process as $Q_{\rm actual}/T$.
- Missing degeneracy factors or the spin factor $g_s$; missing the Gibbs factor $1/N!$.
- Adding partition functions of independent subsystems instead of multiplying them.
- Applying equipartition or classical statistics when $k_BT$ is below a level spacing or $n\lambda_T^3\gtrsim1$.
- Reading a smooth peak in a finite system's $C(T)$ as a phase transition.

prompt: $N$ independent two-level systems with spacing $\epsilon$, where $\epsilon/k_B = 10.0$ K, are heated slowly at fixed $\epsilon$ from $5.00$ K to $10.0$ K. Use the partition function of one system to find the entropy change per system, $\Delta S/(Nk_B)$. Give four decimal places.
answer: 0.2169
check: quad(lambda T: nderiv(lambda t: 10.0/(math.exp(10.0/t) + 1.0), T)/T, 5.0, 10.0)
tol: 0.0005
hint: For each temperature, $S/k_B = \ln Z + \beta\langle E\rangle$ per system with $Z = 1 + e^{-\beta\epsilon}$. Alternatively, integrate $C/T$ from $5$ K to $10$ K.
hint: At $5.00$ K, $\beta\epsilon = 2$; at $10.0$ K, $\beta\epsilon = 1$. Evaluate $\ln Z + \beta\langle E\rangle$ at both and subtract.
solution: At $5.00$ K ($x=2$): $Z = 1.1353$, $\langle E\rangle/\epsilon = 1/(e^2+1) = 0.1192$, so $S/k_B = \ln1.1353 + 2(0.1192) = 0.1269 + 0.2384 = 0.3653$. At $10.0$ K ($x=1$): $Z = 1.3679$, $\langle E\rangle/\epsilon = 0.2689$, so $S/k_B = 0.3133 + 0.2689 = 0.5822$. The difference is $0.2169$. The same value follows from $\int C\,dT/T$, linking weeks 3, 6, 7 and 11. It is below the high-temperature limit $\ln2 = 0.693$.

prompt: Treat liquid $^3$He as an ideal gas of spin-$\tfrac12$ fermions ($g_s=2$) of mass $m = 5.008\times10^{-27}$ kg at number density $n = 1.6\times10^{28}\ \text{m}^{-3}$. For non-relativistic free particles, $P = \tfrac23E/V$. Take $\hbar = 1.0546\times10^{-34}$ J s and $k_B = 1.381\times10^{-23}$ J/K. What is the pressure at $T=0$, in bar ($1\ \text{bar} = 10^{5}$ Pa)? Give three significant figures.
answer: 4.32
check: (lambda c: (lambda eF: (2/3)*quad(lambda e: c*e**1.5, 0, eF)*1.381e-23/1e5)(root(lambda E: quad(lambda e: c*math.sqrt(e), 0, E) - 1.6e28, 0.1, 100)))((1/(2*math.pi**2))*(2*5.008e-27*1.381e-23/1.0546e-34**2)**1.5)
unit: bar
tol: 1%
hint: Find the Fermi energy from $n = \dfrac{1}{3\pi^2}\left(\dfrac{2m\varepsilon_F}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}$, then use that the mean energy per particle at $T=0$ is $\tfrac35\varepsilon_F$.
hint: $P = \tfrac23\,(E/V) = \tfrac23\cdot\tfrac35\,n\varepsilon_F = \tfrac25\,n\varepsilon_F$.
solution: $\varepsilon_F = \dfrac{\hbar^2}{2m}(3\pi^2n)^{2/3} = 6.75\times10^{-23}$ J, so $T_F = 4.89$ K. Then $P = \tfrac25 n\varepsilon_F = 0.4(1.6\times10^{28})(6.75\times10^{-23}) = 4.32\times10^{5}$ Pa $= 4.32$ bar. This degeneracy pressure is purely quantum: a classical gas would exert no pressure at $T=0$.

@exam self
prompt: A single quantum harmonic oscillator with levels $E_n = (n+\tfrac12)\hbar\omega$ is in equilibrium with a heat bath at temperature $T$. Let $x = \hbar\omega/k_BT$. (a) Find its partition function and show that $F = k_BT\ln[2\sinh(x/2)]$. (b) Derive $\langle E\rangle$, $S$ and $C$. (c) Find the limits of $\langle E\rangle$, $S$ and $C$ as $x\to0$ and $x\to\infty$, and interpret them using equipartition, frozen degrees of freedom and the third law.
hint: Sum the geometric series for $Z$ and rewrite it with $\sinh$. Then use $\langle E\rangle = -\partial_\beta\ln Z$, $F = -k_BT\ln Z$, $S = (\langle E\rangle - F)/T$ and $C = d\langle E\rangle/dT$.
hint: For the limits use $\sinh y\approx y$ for small $y$, and $\coth y\approx1+2e^{-2y}$ for large $y$.
solution: (a) $Z = \sum_n e^{-x(n+1/2)} = \dfrac{e^{-x/2}}{1-e^{-x}} = \dfrac{1}{2\sinh(x/2)}$, so $F = -k_BT\ln Z = k_BT\ln[2\sinh(x/2)]$. (b) $\langle E\rangle = -\partial_\beta\ln Z = \tfrac12\hbar\omega\coth(x/2)$. Then $S = (\langle E\rangle - F)/T = k_B\left[\tfrac x2\coth\tfrac x2 - \ln\left(2\sinh\tfrac x2\right)\right]$ and $C = d\langle E\rangle/dT = k_B\,\dfrac{(x/2)^2}{\sinh^2(x/2)}$. (c) For $x\to0$ (high $T$): $\langle E\rangle\to k_BT$, $C\to k_B$, which is equipartition for two quadratic terms, and $S\approx k_B(1-\ln x)$ keeps growing logarithmically with $T$. For $x\to\infty$ (low $T$): $\langle E\rangle\to\tfrac12\hbar\omega$, the zero-point energy; $C\approx k_Bx^2e^{-x}\to0$, because the oscillator is frozen out; and $S\approx k_B(x+1)e^{-x}\to0$, in agreement with the third law because the ground state is unique, $\Omega=1$.
rubric: Sums the geometric series correctly to get $Z = 1/[2\sinh(x/2)]$ and the free energy.
rubric: Obtains $\langle E\rangle$, $S$ and $C$ by correct differentiation or the relations $S = (\langle E\rangle - F)/T$ and $C = d\langle E\rangle/dT$.
rubric: Gives the correct limits: $C\to k_B$ and $\langle E\rangle\to k_BT$ at high $T$; $\langle E\rangle\to\hbar\omega/2$, $C\to0$ and $S\to0$ at low $T$.
rubric: Interprets the limits physically: equipartition, freezing out below $\hbar\omega\sim k_BT$, and a unique ground state giving $S\to0$.
