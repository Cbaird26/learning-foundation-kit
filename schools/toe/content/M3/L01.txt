@lesson
title: Space curves
summary: Parametrize a curve in the plane or in space and compute its velocity, speed, unit tangent vector and arc length.
objective: Write a parametrization $\mathbf r(t)$ for a line segment, a circle, a helix, or an intersection of two surfaces.
objective: Compute velocity, speed, acceleration and the unit tangent vector from $\mathbf r(t)$.
objective: Find arc length by integrating speed, and explain why integrating the velocity vector gives displacement instead.
reading: MIT 18.02SC Multivariable Calculus, Unit 1 (Vectors and Matrices): the sessions on parametric curves, velocity and acceleration.

@article
Think of a bee flying around a room. At each instant it is at one point, so its position is a vector that depends on time:

$$\mathbf r(t)=\langle x(t),\,y(t),\,z(t)\rangle .$$

This is a **parametrization**. The input $t$ is the **parameter**, and the set of points the bee visits is the **curve**. The curve is the path in space; the parametrization also records *when* each point is visited.

### Calculus one component at a time

From M2 you know that a derivative is the limit of a difference quotient. For a vector function the limit works component by component:

$$\mathbf r'(t)=\langle x'(t),\,y'(t),\,z'(t)\rangle .$$

Each derivative has a name and a meaning:

- **Velocity** $\mathbf v=\mathbf r'(t)$ is a vector that points the way the bee is moving.
- **Speed** $|\mathbf v|=\sqrt{x'^2+y'^2+z'^2}$ is a number: how fast it moves.
- **Acceleration** $\mathbf a=\mathbf r''(t)$.
- **Unit tangent** $\mathbf T=\mathbf v/|\mathbf v|$, defined wherever $\mathbf v\neq\mathbf 0$. It keeps only the direction.

Why is $\mathbf v$ tangent to the curve? The vector $\mathbf r(t+h)-\mathbf r(t)$ is a chord of the curve. Dividing by $h$ rescales it, and as $h\to 0$ the direction of the chord turns into the direction of the tangent line.

### Parametrizing standard curves

| Curve | Parametrization |
|---|---|
| Segment from $P$ to $Q$ | $P+t(Q-P)$, $0\le t\le 1$ |
| Circle of radius $a$ in the $xy$-plane | $\langle a\cos t,\ a\sin t,\ 0\rangle$, $0\le t\le 2\pi$ |
| Graph $y=g(x)$ | $\langle t,\ g(t)\rangle$ |
| Helix of radius $a$ | $\langle a\cos t,\ a\sin t,\ ct\rangle$ |

For a curve cut out by two surfaces, let one surface fix two of the coordinates in terms of $t$ and read off the third from the other surface. The cylinder $x^2+y^2=1$ and the plane $z=y$ meet in $\langle\cos t,\ \sin t,\ \sin t\rangle$.

### Arc length

During a short time $dt$ the bee travels a distance $|\mathbf v|\,dt$. Adding these pieces gives

$$L=\int_a^b|\mathbf r'(t)|\,dt .$$

Integrating the *vector* $\mathbf r'$ instead gives $\mathbf r(b)-\mathbf r(a)$, the displacement, which is not the distance traveled.

One curve has many parametrizations. The functions $\langle\cos 2t,\sin 2t\rangle$ and $\langle\cos t,\sin t\rangle$ trace the same circle, but the first goes twice as fast. Their velocities and speeds differ, their unit tangents agree at each point of the circle, and the length of one trip around the circle is $2\pi$ for both.

@example
title: Velocity, unit tangent and length of a helix
problem: A particle moves along $\mathbf r(t)=\langle 2\cos t,\ 2\sin t,\ 3t\rangle$. Find its velocity and speed, the unit tangent vector at $t=\pi/2$, and the length of the path for $0\le t\le 2\pi$.
step: Differentiate each component: $\mathbf v(t)=\langle -2\sin t,\ 2\cos t,\ 3\rangle$.
step: The speed is $|\mathbf v|=\sqrt{4\sin^2 t+4\cos^2 t+9}=\sqrt{13}$, a constant.
step: At $t=\pi/2$ the velocity is $\langle -2,\ 0,\ 3\rangle$, so $\mathbf T=\frac{1}{\sqrt{13}}\langle -2,\ 0,\ 3\rangle$.
step: The integrand is constant, so $L=\int_0^{2\pi}\sqrt{13}\,dt=2\pi\sqrt{13}$.
step: Sanity check: unroll the cylinder. One turn has horizontal length $4\pi$ and rises $6\pi$, so the hypotenuse is $\sqrt{16\pi^2+36\pi^2}=2\pi\sqrt{13}$.

@example
title: Parametrizing an intersection of surfaces
problem: The cylinder $x^2+y^2=4$ meets the plane $x+z=3$ in a closed curve. Parametrize it and find the velocity at $t=0$.
step: The cylinder says that $(x,y)$ lies on a circle of radius $2$, so take $x=2\cos t$ and $y=2\sin t$.
step: The plane gives $z=3-x=3-2\cos t$. So $\mathbf r(t)=\langle 2\cos t,\ 2\sin t,\ 3-2\cos t\rangle$ with $0\le t\le 2\pi$.
step: Check a point: at $t=0$ we get $(2,0,1)$. Then $x^2+y^2=4$ and $x+z=3$, as required.
step: Differentiate: $\mathbf v(t)=\langle -2\sin t,\ 2\cos t,\ 2\sin t\rangle$, so $\mathbf v(0)=\langle 0,\ 2,\ 0\rangle$ and $\mathbf T(0)=\langle 0,1,0\rangle$.

@mistakes
- Integrating $\mathbf r'(t)$ for arc length. You must integrate the length $|\mathbf r'(t)|$; the vector integral is only the displacement.
- Treating the parametrization as if it were the curve: $\langle\cos t,\sin t\rangle$ and $\langle\cos 2t,\sin 2t\rangle$ are the same circle with different velocities.
- Reporting the velocity when the unit tangent is requested, or dividing by $|\mathbf r(t)|$ instead of $|\mathbf r'(t)|$.
- Confusing speed $|\mathbf r'(t)|$ with $\frac{d}{dt}|\mathbf r(t)|$. On a circle centered at the origin the second is $0$ but the first is not.

@practice tuple
prompt: Let $\mathbf r(t)=\langle t^2,\ t^3,\ 5t\rangle$. Find the velocity vector at $t=2$.
answer: (4, 12, 5)
check: (nderiv(lambda s: s**2, 2), nderiv(lambda s: s**3, 2), nderiv(lambda s: 5*s, 2))
hint: Differentiate each component separately.
hint: $\mathbf r'(t)=\langle 2t,\ 3t^2,\ 5\rangle$. Now put in $t=2$.
solution: $\mathbf v(t)=\langle 2t,\,3t^2,\,5\rangle$, so $\mathbf v(2)=\langle 4,\,12,\,5\rangle$.

@practice num
prompt: A particle moves along $\mathbf r(t)=\langle 3\cos t,\ 3\sin t,\ 4t\rangle$. What is its speed at $t=1$?
answer: 5
check: norm([nderiv(lambda s: 3*cos(s), 1), nderiv(lambda s: 3*sin(s), 1), nderiv(lambda s: 4*s, 1)])
hint: Write down the velocity vector first: $\langle -3\sin t,\ 3\cos t,\ 4\rangle$.
hint: Speed is the length of the velocity. Use $\sin^2 t+\cos^2 t=1$.
solution: $|\mathbf v|=\sqrt{9\sin^2 t+9\cos^2 t+16}=\sqrt{25}=5$ for every $t$, in particular at $t=1$.

@practice mc
prompt: Which parametrization starts at $P=(1,2,3)$ when $t=0$, ends at $Q=(4,6,3)$ when $t=1$, and moves along the straight segment between them?
choice*: $\langle 1+3t,\ 2+4t,\ 3\rangle$
choice: $\langle 1+4t,\ 2+6t,\ 3\rangle$
choice: $\langle 3t,\ 4t,\ 3\rangle$
choice: $\langle 4-3t,\ 6-4t,\ 3\rangle$
hint: Use $P+t(Q-P)$. Compute $Q-P$ first.
hint: Test each choice at $t=0$ and $t=1$.
solution: $Q-P=\langle 3,4,0\rangle$, so $\mathbf r(t)=\langle 1+3t,\,2+4t,\,3\rangle$. The choice $\langle 1+4t,\ 2+6t,\ 3\rangle$ ends at $(5,8,3)$ because it adds the coordinates of $Q$ instead of the difference. The choice $\langle 3t,\ 4t,\ 3\rangle$ starts at $(0,0,3)$. The choice $\langle 4-3t,\ 6-4t,\ 3\rangle$ runs from $Q$ to $P$.

@practice num
prompt: Find the length of the curve $\mathbf r(t)=\langle 2t,\ t^2,\ \ln t\rangle$ for $1\le t\le e$. Give an exact answer.
answer: e^2
check: quad(lambda t: norm([nderiv(lambda s: 2*s, t), nderiv(lambda s: s**2, t), nderiv(lambda s: log(s), t)]), 1, e)
hint: Compute $|\mathbf r'(t)|^2=4+4t^2+t^{-2}$. This is a perfect square.
hint: $4+4t^2+t^{-2}=(2t+t^{-1})^2$, so the speed is $2t+1/t$ for $t>0$.
solution: $\mathbf r'=\langle 2,\,2t,\,1/t\rangle$ and $|\mathbf r'|=2t+1/t$. Then $L=\int_1^e(2t+1/t)\,dt=\left[t^2+\ln t\right]_1^e=(e^2+1)-1=e^2$.

@practice num
prompt: For $\mathbf r(t)=\langle t,\ t^2,\ t^3\rangle$, find the angle in radians between the velocity and the acceleration at $t=1$. Round to three decimal places.
answer: 0.377
tol: 0.001
check: arccos(dot([nderiv(lambda s: s, 1), nderiv(lambda s: s**2, 1), nderiv(lambda s: s**3, 1)], [nderiv(lambda s: s, 1, 2), nderiv(lambda s: s**2, 1, 2), nderiv(lambda s: s**3, 1, 2)])/(norm([nderiv(lambda s: s, 1), nderiv(lambda s: s**2, 1), nderiv(lambda s: s**3, 1)])*norm([nderiv(lambda s: s, 1, 2), nderiv(lambda s: s**2, 1, 2), nderiv(lambda s: s**3, 1, 2)])))
hint: Find $\mathbf v(1)$ and $\mathbf a(1)$ first.
hint: $\mathbf v(1)=\langle 1,2,3\rangle$ and $\mathbf a(1)=\langle 0,2,6\rangle$. Use $\cos\theta=\dfrac{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a}{|\mathbf v||\mathbf a|}$.
solution: $\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a=0+4+18=22$, $|\mathbf v|=\sqrt{14}$, $|\mathbf a|=\sqrt{40}$. So $\cos\theta=22/\sqrt{560}\approx 0.9297$ and $\theta\approx 0.377$ rad.

@quiz num
prompt: A particle moves along $\mathbf r(t)=\langle \cos 2t,\ \sin 2t,\ t^2\rangle$. Find the magnitude of its acceleration at $t=0.5$. Give an exact answer.
answer: 2sqrt(5)
check: norm([nderiv(lambda s: cos(2*s), 0.5, 2), nderiv(lambda s: sin(2*s), 0.5, 2), nderiv(lambda s: s**2, 0.5, 2)])
hint: Differentiate twice: the second derivative of $\cos 2t$ is $-4\cos 2t$.
hint: $\mathbf a(t)=\langle -4\cos 2t,\ -4\sin 2t,\ 2\rangle$. Take its length.
solution: $|\mathbf a|=\sqrt{16\cos^2 2t+16\sin^2 2t+4}=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt5$, for every $t$.

@quiz tuple
prompt: For $\mathbf r(t)=\langle 2t,\ t^3,\ 3t^2\rangle$, find the unit tangent vector at $t=1$.
answer: (2/7, 3/7, 6/7)
check: [nderiv(lambda s: 2*s, 1)/norm([nderiv(lambda s: 2*s, 1), nderiv(lambda s: s**3, 1), nderiv(lambda s: 3*s**2, 1)]), nderiv(lambda s: s**3, 1)/norm([nderiv(lambda s: 2*s, 1), nderiv(lambda s: s**3, 1), nderiv(lambda s: 3*s**2, 1)]), nderiv(lambda s: 3*s**2, 1)/norm([nderiv(lambda s: 2*s, 1), nderiv(lambda s: s**3, 1), nderiv(lambda s: 3*s**2, 1)])]
hint: First find $\mathbf v(1)$, then divide by its length.
hint: $\mathbf v(t)=\langle 2,\ 3t^2,\ 6t\rangle$, so $\mathbf v(1)=\langle 2,3,6\rangle$.
solution: $\mathbf v(1)=\langle 2,3,6\rangle$ has length $\sqrt{4+9+36}=7$, so $\mathbf T(1)=\langle 2/7,\ 3/7,\ 6/7\rangle$.
