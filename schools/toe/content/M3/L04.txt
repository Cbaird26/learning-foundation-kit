@article
Many real problems ask for the best value of one quantity while another is held fixed: the largest area for a given amount of fence, the cheapest design that meets a strength requirement. This is **constrained optimization**.

### Unconstrained critical points

Without constraints, a smooth function can have a local extremum only where $\nabla f=\mathbf 0$. Such a point is a **critical point**. For two variables, classify it with the discriminant

$$D=f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2 .$$

- $D>0$ and $f_{xx}>0$: local minimum. $D>0$ and $f_{xx}<0$: local maximum.
- $D<0$: saddle point, which rises in one direction and falls in another.
- $D=0$: the test is inconclusive.

### One constraint: Lagrange multipliers

To optimize $f$ subject to $g(x,y)=k$, move along the constraint curve. At a constrained extremum you cannot improve $f$ by moving along the curve, so the level curve of $f$ touches the constraint curve without crossing it. They are tangent there, so their normal vectors are parallel:

$$\nabla f=\lambda\,\nabla g,\qquad g(x,y)=k .$$

The unknowns are $x$, $y$ and $\lambda$, and there are three equations. With three variables and one constraint you get four equations in four unknowns.

**Recipe.**

1. Write the component equations $f_x=\lambda g_x$, $f_y=\lambda g_y$ (and $f_z=\lambda g_z$).
2. Eliminate $\lambda$ to relate the variables, and substitute into the constraint.
3. List every candidate point and evaluate $f$ at each one. If the constraint set is closed and bounded, such as a circle or a sphere, and $\nabla g\neq\mathbf 0$ on it, the largest and smallest of these values are the true maximum and minimum.

These conditions are necessary, not sufficient. A solution may be a maximum, a minimum, or neither, so you must compare values or argue from the geometry.

### What does $\lambda$ mean?

Let $m(k)$ be the optimal value of $f$ when the constraint level is $k$. Then

$$\frac{dm}{dk}=\lambda .$$

The multiplier is the **price of the constraint**: it tells you how much the optimal value changes per unit change in $k$, so that $\Delta m\approx\lambda\,\Delta k$ for a small change. Its units are the units of $f$ divided by the units of $k$. If $f$ is profit and $k$ is a budget, $\lambda$ is the extra profit per extra unit of budget.

@example
title: A product on a line, and the meaning of the multiplier
problem: Maximize $f(x,y)=xy$ subject to $2x+3y=12$ with $x,y>0$. Find $\lambda$, and use it to estimate the new maximum if the constraint becomes $2x+3y=13$.
step: With $g=2x+3y$ we have $\nabla f=\langle y,x\rangle$ and $\nabla g=\langle 2,3\rangle$. The equations are $y=2\lambda$, $x=3\lambda$ and $2x+3y=12$.
step: Substituting, $6\lambda+6\lambda=12$, so $\lambda=1$, $x=3$, $y=2$, and the maximum is $f=6$.
step: Since $\lambda=dm/dk=1$, raising $k$ from $12$ to $13$ should raise the maximum by about $1$, to about $7$.
step: Check exactly: for a general level $k$ the same algebra gives $x=k/4$, $y=k/6$, so $m(k)=k^2/24$. Then $m(13)=169/24\approx 7.04$, in agreement.

@example
title: Extremes on a circle
problem: Find the maximum and minimum of $f(x,y)=x+2y$ on the circle $x^2+y^2=5$.
step: With $g=x^2+y^2$, the equations are $1=2\lambda x$ and $2=2\lambda y$. Neither $x$ nor $y$ can be $0$ here, because the left sides are nonzero.
step: Dividing the second equation by the first gives $y=2x$.
step: Substitute into the constraint: $x^2+4x^2=5$, so $x=\pm 1$. The candidate points are $(1,2)$ and $(-1,-2)$.
step: Evaluate: $f(1,2)=5$ and $f(-1,-2)=-5$. The circle is closed and bounded, so the maximum is $5$ and the minimum is $-5$.

@mistakes
- Treating the Lagrange equations as sufficient: finding one solution and calling it the maximum without comparing it with the other candidates.
- Forgetting the constraint equation itself, so that $\lambda$ cannot be determined.
- Dividing by a variable that could be zero and losing a candidate point; check that case separately.
- Classifying a critical point by $f_{xx}$ alone and ignoring the sign of $D$.

@practice tuple
prompt: Find the critical point $(x,y)$ of $f(x,y)=x^2+xy+y^2-3x$.
answer: (2, -1)
check: lin([[2, 1], [1, 2]], [3, 0])
hint: Set $f_x=0$ and $f_y=0$ and solve the two equations together.
hint: $f_x=2x+y-3=0$ and $f_y=x+2y=0$.
solution: From $x+2y=0$ we get $x=-2y$. Then $2(-2y)+y=3$ gives $y=-1$ and $x=2$. The critical point is $(2,-1)$.

@practice mc
prompt: What kind of critical point does $f(x,y)=x^2-3xy+y^2$ have at the origin?
choice: A local minimum, because $f_{xx}>0$.
choice: A local maximum.
choice*: A saddle point.
choice: The second-derivative test is inconclusive.
hint: Compute $f_{xx}$, $f_{yy}$ and $f_{xy}$, then the discriminant $D$.
solution: $f_{xx}=2$, $f_{yy}=2$, $f_{xy}=-3$, so $D=4-9=-5<0$. A negative discriminant means a saddle point; the sign of $f_{xx}$ alone is not enough.

@practice num
prompt: Find the maximum value of $f(x,y)=3x+y$ on the circle $x^2+y^2=10$.
answer: 10
check: max(3*sqrt(10)*cos(t) + sqrt(10)*sin(t) for t in np.linspace(0, 2*pi, 200001))
hint: Write $\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g$ with $g=x^2+y^2$: $3=2\lambda x$ and $1=2\lambda y$.
hint: Dividing the equations gives $x=3y$. Substitute into $x^2+y^2=10$, then compare $f$ at the two points.
solution: $x=3y$ and $10y^2=10$ give $y=\pm 1$, so the candidates are $(3,1)$ and $(-3,-1)$. Then $f=10$ and $f=-10$, so the maximum is $10$.

@practice num
prompt: Find the minimum value of $x^2+y^2+z^2$ subject to $x+2y+2z=9$.
answer: 9
check: (lambda s: s[0]**2 + s[1]**2 + s[2]**2)(lin([[2, 0, 0, -1], [0, 2, 0, -2], [0, 0, 2, -2], [1, 2, 2, 0]], [0, 0, 0, 9]))
hint: Use $\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g$ with $\nabla f=\langle 2x,2y,2z\rangle$ and $\nabla g=\langle 1,2,2\rangle$.
hint: The point is $(x,y,z)=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(1,2,2)$. Substitute into the constraint to find $\lambda$.
solution: $(x,y,z)=\frac{\lambda}{2}(1,2,2)$ and $x+2y+2z=\frac{\lambda}{2}\cdot 9=9$, so $\lambda=2$ and the point is $(1,2,2)$. The minimum value is $1+4+4=9$. This is the squared distance from the origin to the plane.

@practice num
prompt: Let $m(k)$ be the minimum of $x^2+y^2$ subject to $x+2y=k$. Solve the Lagrange system at $k=10$, then use the multiplier to estimate how much $m$ changes when $k$ increases from $10$ to $10.5$.
answer: 2
tol: 0.01
check: (lambda fs: nderiv(fs, 10)*0.5)(lambda k: (lambda y0: (k - 2*y0)**2 + y0**2)(root(lambda y: nderiv(lambda u: (k - 2*u)**2 + u**2, y), -100, 100)))
hint: With $g=x+2y$ the equations are $2x=\lambda$ and $2y=2\lambda$, plus $x+2y=10$.
hint: Solve for $\lambda$. Then $\Delta m\approx\lambda\,\Delta k$ with $\Delta k=0.5$.
solution: $x=\lambda/2$ and $y=\lambda$, so $\lambda/2+2\lambda=10$ and $\lambda=4$ (the minimum is at $(2,4)$, with $m=20$). Then $\Delta m\approx 4\cdot 0.5=2$. The exact new minimum is $k^2/5=22.05$, so the estimate is close.

@quiz tuple
prompt: Maximize $f(x,y)=xy^2$ subject to $x+y=3$ with $x,y\ge 0$. Give the maximizing point $(x,y)$.
answer: (1, 2)
check: (lambda x0: (x0, 3 - x0))(root(lambda x: nderiv(lambda u: u*(3 - u)**2, x), 0.5, 2.5))
hint: $\nabla f=\langle y^2,\ 2xy\rangle$ and $\nabla g=\langle 1,1\rangle$, so $y^2=2xy$.
hint: Either $y=0$, which gives $f=0$, or $y=2x$. Use the second case in the constraint.
solution: $y=2x$ and $x+2x=3$ give $x=1$, $y=2$, with $f=4$. The other candidates $(0,3)$ and $(3,0)$ give $f=0$, so the maximum is at $(1,2)$.

@quiz ms
prompt: Which statements about the conditions $\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g$ and $g=k$ are correct?
choice*: At a constrained extremum the level curve of $f$ is tangent to the constraint curve.
choice*: A solution of the system may be a maximum, a minimum, or neither, so the candidates must be compared.
choice: Every solution of the system is a constrained maximum.
choice: The multiplier $\lambda$ must be positive.
hint: Think about what the conditions guarantee: they are necessary at an extremum, but is the converse true?
solution: Tangency is the geometric content of $\nabla f\parallel\nabla g$. The system only produces candidates, which can be maxima, minima or neither. The sign of $\lambda$ depends on the problem; for example, it is negative if raising $k$ lowers the optimal value.
