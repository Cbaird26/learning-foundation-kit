@article
The same law that pulls a dropped ball toward the ground also holds the Moon in orbit. In this lesson we use $G = 6.67\times10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2/\text{kg}^2$, Earth's mass $M_E = 5.97\times10^{24}$ kg and Earth's radius $R_E = 6.37\times10^6$ m.

### The law of gravitation

Any two masses attract each other with a force of magnitude

$$F = \frac{G m_1 m_2}{r^2},$$

directed along the line joining them. For spherically symmetric bodies, $r$ is the distance between centers. The acceleration of a small object at distance $r$ from a planet of mass $M$ is

$$g(r) = \frac{GM}{r^2}.$$

At Earth's surface this gives $g = GM_E/R_E^2 \approx 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. It is an inverse-square law: doubling the distance cuts the force to a quarter.

### Circular orbits

For a satellite in a circular orbit, gravity supplies the inward force:

$$\frac{GMm}{r^2} = \frac{mv^2}{r} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}, \qquad T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM}}.$$

Squaring the period gives $T^2 = \dfrac{4\pi^2}{GM}\,r^3$. This is the third of Kepler's three laws of planetary motion: the square of the period is proportional to the cube of the orbit's size. For an ellipse, $r$ is replaced by the semi-major axis $a$. The other two laws are that orbits are ellipses with the central body at one focus, and that the line from the central body to the planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times, which is conservation of angular momentum. For two bodies circling the same central mass,

$$\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{a_2}{a_1}\right)^3.$$

### Energy in a gravitational field

The potential energy of two masses is

$$U(r) = -\frac{GMm}{r},$$

which is zero at infinite separation and negative when bound. Near the surface, differences of $U$ reduce to $mg\,\Delta h$. In a circular orbit, $K = \tfrac12 mv^2 = GMm/(2r)$, so the total energy is $E = K + U = -GMm/(2r)$.

To **escape**, an object needs to reach $r \to \infty$ with zero speed, so $E \ge 0$ at launch:

$$\tfrac12 mv^2 - \frac{GMm}{R} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}}.$$

This is $\sqrt2$ times the circular-orbit speed at the surface. For Earth it is about $1.12\times10^4$ m/s.

### Weightlessness

At the altitude of a space station ($400$ km), $g$ is still about $8.7\ \text{m/s}^2$. Astronauts feel weightless because they and the station fall with the same acceleration, so there is no normal force pressing them against the floor. Orbiting is falling around the planet and missing it.

```python
import math
G, M, R = 6.67e-11, 5.97e24, 6.37e6
print(math.sqrt(2 * G * M / R))   # about 11180 (m/s)
```

@example
title: A satellite 400 km above the surface
problem: Find the speed and period of a satellite in a circular orbit $400$ km above Earth's surface. Use $G = 6.67\times10^{-11}$, $M_E = 5.97\times10^{24}$ kg and $R_E = 6.37\times10^6$ m.
step: The orbital radius is measured from Earth's center: $r = 6.37\times10^6 + 0.40\times10^6 = 6.77\times10^6$ m.
step: Gravity supplies the centripetal force, so $v = \sqrt{GM/r} = \sqrt{(3.98\times10^{14})/(6.77\times10^6)} = \sqrt{5.88\times10^7}$.
step: $v \approx 7.67\times10^3$ m/s, about $7.7$ km/s.
step: Period: $T = 2\pi r/v = 2\pi(6.77\times10^6)/(7.67\times10^3) \approx 5.55\times10^3$ s, which is about $92$ minutes.

@example
title: Escape speed and the orbital speed at the surface
problem: Compare Earth's escape speed with the speed of a (hypothetical) satellite skimming the surface.
step: The surface-skimming circular orbit has $v_c = \sqrt{GM_E/R_E} = \sqrt{3.98\times10^{14}/6.37\times10^6} \approx 7.9\times10^3$ m/s.
step: The escape condition $\tfrac12 v^2 = GM_E/R_E$ gives $v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{2GM_E/R_E} = \sqrt2\,v_c$.
step: Numerically $v_{\text{esc}} \approx 1.414 \times 7.9\times10^3 \approx 1.12\times10^4$ m/s.
step: The result does not depend on the mass of the escaping object. A single speed works for a pebble or a spacecraft, ignoring air resistance.

@mistakes
- Measuring $r$ from the planet's surface instead of from its center.
- Treating astronauts as weightless because gravity is absent. Gravity is nearly as strong as on the ground; they are in free fall.
- Using $U = mgh$ for large changes in height, where the field is no longer uniform.
- Forgetting the square when scaling gravity: doubling the distance divides $g$ by four, not by two.
