title: A rod with heat loss: eigenfunction expansion versus Green function
overview: Solve the boundary value problem $-u''+\kappa^2u=f(x)$ on $[0,1]$ with $u(0)=0$ and $u'(1)=0$ in two independent ways, by an eigenfunction expansion and by a Green function, and compare both with a closed form and a numerical reference. Take $\kappa=2$ and $f(x)=x$, then repeat with a second source of your choice. The aim is a verified and quantified comparison in which every domain assumption is stated.
goal: Derive the eigenpairs of $-d^2/dx^2$ on the stated domain, expand the source, and obtain the series solution with a justified statement about convergence.
goal: Construct the Green function from homogeneous solutions, verify its boundary conditions, symmetry, continuity and derivative jump, and obtain the solution as $\int_0^1G(x,\xi)f(\xi)\,d\xi$.
goal: Compare both solutions with a closed form and a finite-difference reference, and report the error as a function of the number of terms and of the grid spacing.
step: State the problem. Write the operator $\mathcal L=-d^2/dx^2+\kappa^2$ with its domain $\{u\in C^2[0,1]: u(0)=0,\ u'(1)=0\}$ and the inner product $\langle f,g\rangle=\int_0^1\bar fg\,dx$. Use the Lagrange identity to show that $\mathcal L$ is symmetric on this domain and use the Rayleigh quotient to show that every eigenvalue is at least $\kappa^2>0$, so a Green function exists.
step: Eigenfunction expansion. Find the eigenfunctions $\phi_n=\sqrt2\sin(\mu_nx)$ with $\mu_n=(n-\tfrac12)\pi$ and eigenvalues $\mu_n^2+\kappa^2$. Compute $f_n=\langle\phi_n,f\rangle$ for $f=x$, write $u_N=\sum_{n\le N}\frac{f_n}{\mu_n^2+\kappa^2}\phi_n$, and check Bessel's inequality and Parseval's identity for $f$. State in what sense the series converges and how the coefficients decay.
step: Green function. Choose $u_1$ satisfying the condition at $0$ and $u_2$ satisfying the condition at $1$ (for example $\sinh\kappa x$ and $\cosh\kappa(1-x)$). Compute the Wronskian, write $G=-u_1(x_<)u_2(x_>)/(pW)$, and verify in writing: the two boundary conditions, continuity at $x=\xi$, the jump $-1$ of $\partial_xG$, and the symmetry $G(x,\xi)=G(\xi,x)$. Evaluate $u(x)=\int_0^1G(x,\xi)\,\xi\,d\xi$ exactly or with quadrature.
step: Closed form and reference. Solve the ODE directly (particular solution plus homogeneous solutions) and implement a second-order finite-difference solver in Python with $N$ interior points. Confirm that the closed form satisfies the equation and both boundary conditions.
step: Error study. For $N=1,2,4,8,16,32$ terms tabulate the maximum error of $u_N$ against the closed form on a fine grid, and for several grid spacings $h$ the maximum error of the finite-difference solution. Estimate the observed convergence orders and explain them from the coefficient decay and from the truncation error.
step: Second source. Repeat steps 2 to 5 with another source, for example $f=1$ (closed form $u=\kappa^{-2}\big(1-\cosh\kappa(1-x)/\cosh\kappa\big)$) or a narrow Gaussian near $x=0.3$. Explain any difference in convergence, noting the effect of the source not satisfying $f(0)=0$.
step: Reconcile. Show analytically why the two methods agree: expand $G(x,\xi)=\sum_n\phi_n(x)\phi_n(\xi)/(\mu_n^2+\kappa^2)$ and insert it in $\int Gf$. Write up the comparison, the domain assumptions, and when each method is preferable.
deliverable: A written report of two to four pages containing the derivations, the verification of $G$ with each property checked, and the comparison with the closed form.
deliverable: A Python script or notebook that reproduces the error tables, with the observed convergence rates and a short explanation of each.
deliverable: A one-minute spoken explanation, prepared without notes, of why the eigenfunction series and the Green function integral give the same solution.

## Week-by-week summary

- **Week 1, function spaces.** Inner product $\langle f,g\rangle=\int\bar fg\,w\,dx$, Cauchy–Schwarz, Bessel's inequality, Parseval's identity, Gram–Schmidt. Completeness means mean-square convergence, not pointwise convergence.
- **Week 2, Sturm–Liouville.** Form $-(pu')'+qu=\lambda wu$, domain with separated real BCs, Lagrange identity, real eigenvalues, orthogonality in weight $w$, sign of $\lambda$ before solving.
- **Week 3, special functions.** Gamma and beta functions; for Legendre, Bessel and Hermite know the equation, interval, weight, normalization, recurrence and generating function.
- **Week 4, asymptotics.** Asymptotic series definition, optimal truncation, Watson's lemma, Laplace's method, Stirling's formula. Always compare with an accurate value.
- **Week 5, complex integration.** Cauchy's theorem and formulas, Laurent series, residues at poles of any order, the residue theorem, the ML inequality.
- **Week 6, contour methods.** Close where the exponential decays, justify the arc (ML or Jordan), indent around real poles ($-i\pi\operatorname{Res}$), keyhole for branch cuts, causal response integrals.
- **Week 7, transforms.** State the convention. Derivative, shift and convolution rules, Plancherel, heat kernel, Laplace transform of derivatives and Bromwich inversion.
- **Week 8, distributions.** Action on test functions, $\langle T',\varphi\rangle=-\langle T,\varphi'\rangle$, $\delta(g(x))$, weak convergence, Plemelj formula; products of singular distributions are undefined.
- **Week 9, Green functions.** $\mathcal LG=\delta$, BCs, continuity, derivative jump $-1/p$, $G=-u_1(x_<)u_2(x_>)/(pW)$, symmetry, eigenfunction expansion, solvability when $0$ is an eigenvalue.
- **Week 10, integral equations.** Fredholm and Volterra, separable kernels, Neumann series, Fredholm alternative, characteristic values $=1/\mu_n$.
- **Week 11, variational methods.** Euler–Lagrange equation, essential versus natural BCs, Rayleigh quotient as an upper bound, Rayleigh–Ritz and min–max.
- **Week 12, perturbation.** Regular expansions, secular terms, eigenvalue shifts to second order, boundary layers and composite approximations, stated error orders.

## Most common errors

- Dropping the weight $w$ or the complex conjugate in an inner product.
- Treating mean-square convergence as pointwise convergence.
- Forgetting $2\pi i$, using the wrong orientation, or enclosing poles outside the contour.
- Closing the contour in the half-plane where the exponential grows.
- Mixing transform conventions, or forgetting the initial-value terms in $\mathcal L[f'']$.
- Treating $\delta$ as an ordinary function, or omitting $1/|g'|$ in $\delta(g(x))$.
- Wrong sign in the Green function jump, or building a Green function when $0$ is an eigenvalue.
- Reporting a Rayleigh quotient or perturbation result with no error statement.

The integral operator with kernel $G$ has eigenvalues $\mu_n=1/(n\pi)^2$ and orthonormal eigenfunctions $\phi_n=\sqrt2\sin n\pi x$. Compute $\int_0^1\!\int_0^1G(x,\xi)^2\,dx\,d\xi$ from the closed form of $G$, then use $G(x,\xi)=\sum_n\mu_n\phi_n(x)\phi_n(\xi)$ together with Parseval's identity to find the exact value of $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-4}$.
answer: pi^4/90
check: sum(1.0/n**4 for n in range(1, 20001))
hint: The closed form is $G=x_<(1-x_>)$. By symmetry, integrate over $\xi<x$ and double the result.
hint: $\iint G^2=2\int_0^1\int_0^x\xi^2(1-x)^2\,d\xi\,dx=\tfrac23\int_0^1x^3(1-x)^2dx=\tfrac23B(4,3)$.

prompt: For $k>0$ let $I(k)=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos kx}{(x^2+1)^2}\,dx$. (a) Evaluate $I(k)$ by contour integration, stating which half-plane you close in and justifying why the arc contributes nothing. (b) Confirm the result independently with the convolution theorem, using the convention $\hat f(k)=\int f(x)e^{-ikx}dx$ and the transform of $1/(x^2+1)$. (c) Check the value at $k\to0$ against a direct integral or Plancherel's theorem.
hint: Write $\cos kx$ as the real part of $e^{ikx}$ and close in the upper half-plane. The pole at $z=i$ is double.
hint: For the residue use $\dfrac{d}{dz}\Big[\dfrac{e^{ikz}}{(z+i)^2}\Big]_{z=i}$.
hint: The transform of $1/(x^2+1)$ is $\pi e^{-|k|}$ (week 6 with $a=1$). The transform of a product is $\frac1{2\pi}$ times the convolution of the transforms.
solution: (a) The integrand $F(z)=e^{ikz}/(z^2+1)^2$ has $|e^{ikz}|\le1$ in the upper half-plane and $|F|\le(R^2-1)^{-2}$ on a semicircle of radius $R>1$, so the arc is bounded by $\pi R/(R^2-1)^2\to0$. The only enclosed singularity is the double pole at $z=i$. Its residue is $\dfrac{d}{dz}\Big[\dfrac{e^{ikz}}{(z+i)^2}\Big]_{z=i}=\dfrac{ik\,e^{-k}}{(2i)^2}-\dfrac{2e^{-k}}{(2i)^3}=-\dfrac{i}{4}(1+k)e^{-k}$. Hence $\int\dfrac{e^{ikx}}{(x^2+1)^2}dx=2\pi i\cdot\Big(-\dfrac i4\Big)(1+k)e^{-k}=\dfrac\pi2(1+k)e^{-k}$. The sine part is odd and vanishes, so $I(k)=\dfrac\pi2(1+k)e^{-k}$. (b) With $g=1/(x^2+1)$ and $\hat g=\pi e^{-|k|}$, $\widehat{g^2}=\dfrac1{2\pi}(\hat g*\hat g)=\dfrac\pi2\int e^{-|q|-|k-q|}dq=\dfrac\pi2(1+|k|)e^{-|k|}$, since the integral over $q<0$, $0<q<k$ and $q>k$ gives $\tfrac12e^{-k}+ke^{-k}+\tfrac12e^{-k}$. This agrees with (a). (c) At $k\to0$, $I\to\pi/2$, and $\int_{-\infty}^\infty(x^2+1)^{-2}dx=\pi/2$ (also $\tfrac1{2\pi}\int\pi^2e^{-2|k|}dk=\tfrac\pi2$ by Plancherel).
