## Week 13 review: functional analysis for quantum theory

### Key results and methods, week by week

1. **Normed spaces.** The three norm axioms; $\ell^p$, sup and $L^1$ norms. Norms are equivalent in finite dimension but not in infinite dimension ($t^n$ in $C[0,1]$).
2. **Completeness.** Cauchy versus convergent. Template: candidate limit, membership, convergence in norm. $C[0,1]$ is complete in the sup norm and not in $\|\cdot\|_1$. A space is complete exactly when absolutely convergent series converge.
3. **Bounded linear maps.** Bounded $\iff$ continuous. Operator norm by an upper bound plus a test vector. Matrices: largest singular value.
4. **Dual spaces.** $X^*$ is complete. Hölder duality $\frac1p+\frac1q=1$; $\int|w|$ for weights. Hahn–Banach gives $\|x\|=\sup_{\|\phi\|\le1}|\phi(x)|$.
5. **Measure.** $\sigma$-algebras, countable additivity, continuity from below, null sets, "almost everywhere".
6. **Integration.** Simple functions; monotone convergence, Fatou, dominated convergence; the spike $n\mathbf 1_{(0,1/n)}$ shows why domination is needed.
7. **$L^p$ spaces.** Classes modulo null sets, Hölder, Minkowski, Riesz–Fischer. Inclusions depend on the measure space.
8. **Convergence.** Uniform, a.e., $L^p$, in measure, weak; the counterexamples $x^n$, spike, typewriter, $e_n$.
9. **Hilbert spaces.** Inner product, Cauchy–Schwarz, parallelogram law, orthonormal bases, Bessel and Parseval.
10. **Projections.** Nearest point exists when $M$ is closed and $H$ is complete; $P^2=P=P^*$; normal equations; Riesz representation.
11. **Compact operators.** Relatively compact image of the ball; finite-rank limits; $\operatorname{diag}(d_k)$ is compact iff $d_k\to0$; Hilbert–Schmidt kernels; weak null becomes norm null.
12. **Spectral theorem.** Compact self-adjoint operators have an orthonormal eigenbasis with real eigenvalues tending to $0$ and $\|T\|=\max|\lambda_k|$. Without compactness, self-adjointness or equal domains the conclusion fails.

### Self-check list

- Can I prove: the reverse triangle inequality; completeness of $\ell^\infty$; bounded $\iff$ continuous; continuity of measure from below; $f\ge0$ and $\int f=0\Rightarrow f=0$ a.e.; completeness of $L^1$ by series; Cauchy–Schwarz; existence of a nearest point; the Riesz representation theorem; $\|T\|=\max|\lambda_k|$?
- Can I produce, from memory: a Cauchy sequence without a limit; a.e. convergence without $L^1$ convergence; $L^1$ convergence without a.e. convergence; a weakly null sequence that is not norm null; a bounded operator that is not compact; a symmetric operator that is not self-adjoint?
- For an unfamiliar operator, can I list its domain, its norm, its adjoint with domain, and its compactness, before choosing a theorem?

### Common errors

- Calling a space complete without naming the norm.
- Treating $\|Tx\|<\infty$ for each $x$ as boundedness.
- Using dominated convergence with no integrable bound independent of $n$.
- Assuming one $L^p$ space sits inside another on any measure space.
- Projecting onto a subspace that is not closed.
- Calling an operator compact because it is bounded, or self-adjoint because a formula is symmetric.
- Quoting the spectral theorem for a non-compact operator and expecting eigenvectors.

@exam self
prompt: Let $T$ be a compact self-adjoint operator on an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space $H$ with $\ker T=\{0\}$. Prove: (a) $H$ has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors $e_k$ of $T$, with eigenvalues $\lambda_k\neq0$ and $\lambda_k\to0$; (b) the range of $T$ is dense in $H$ but is not all of $H$; (c) the inverse $T^{-1}$, defined on the range of $T$, is unbounded.
hint: For (a) apply the spectral theorem, using that $\ker T=\{0\}$ and that the identity operator is not compact when $\dim H=\infty$.
hint: For (c) evaluate $T^{-1}$ on the basis vectors. For (b), build $x=\sum_jc_je_{k_j}$ with coefficients decaying faster than the eigenvalues, and show that $Ty=x$ is impossible.
solution: (a) By the spectral theorem the eigenvectors with non-zero eigenvalues, together with an orthonormal basis of $\ker T=\{0\}$, form an orthonormal basis of $H$. Since $\dim H=\infty$ there are infinitely many $e_k$, and compactness gives $\lambda_k\to0$. (b) Each $e_k=T(e_k/\lambda_k)$ lies in the range, so the range contains the span of an orthonormal basis, which is dense. Choose $k_1<k_2<\cdots$ with $|\lambda_{k_j}|\le4^{-j}$ and put $x=\sum_j2^{-j}e_{k_j}\in H$. If $x=Ty$ then $\langle e_k,y\rangle=\langle e_k,x\rangle/\lambda_k$, so $\|y\|^2\ge\sum_j\frac{4^{-j}}{\lambda_{k_j}^2}\ge\sum_j\frac{4^{-j}}{16^{-j}}=\sum_j4^j=\infty$, which is impossible. So $x\notin\operatorname{ran}T$. (c) $T^{-1}e_k=\lambda_k^{-1}e_k$, so $\|T^{-1}e_k\|=1/|\lambda_k|\to\infty$ while $\|e_k\|=1$. No constant bounds $\|T^{-1}x\|$ by $\|x\|$, so $T^{-1}$ is unbounded. This is why inverting a smoothing operator, such as the inverse of the Green's-function operator for a differential equation, leads to an unbounded operator with a domain.
rubric: Applies the spectral theorem correctly in (a), using $\ker T=\{0\}$ to get a complete eigenbasis and compactness for $\lambda_k\to0$.
rubric: Shows density of the range from the eigenbasis and gives a valid argument that some vector is not in the range.
rubric: Shows $\|T^{-1}e_k\|=1/|\lambda_k|\to\infty$, concludes $T^{-1}$ is unbounded, and comments on what this means for the domain of $T^{-1}$.

prompt: A student makes five claims about operators. Select every claim that is correct.
choice*: The integral operator on $L^2(0,1)$ with kernel $x+y$ is compact and self-adjoint, so its norm equals the largest absolute value of its eigenvalues.
choice: Multiplication by $x$ on $L^2(0,1)$ is bounded and self-adjoint, so the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators gives an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions.
choice: The functions $f_n=\sqrt n\,\mathbf 1_{(1-1/n,1)}$ converge to $0$ almost everywhere and are bounded in $L^2$, so $f_n\to0$ in $L^2$.
choice*: If $P$ is the orthogonal projection onto a closed subspace $M\neq\{0\}$ of a Hilbert space, then $\|P\|=1$ and every eigenvalue of $P$ is $0$ or $1$.
choice: In $\ell^2$ the vectors $e_n$ converge weakly to $0$ and $\|e_n\|=1$ for all $n$, so the weak limit must have norm $1$.
hint: For each claim, name the earlier week and theorem it relies on, and check every hypothesis of that theorem.
solution: The kernel $x+y$ is real, symmetric and square integrable, so the operator is compact and self-adjoint, and for such operators $\|T\|=\max_k|\lambda_k|$. Multiplication by $x$ is not compact and has no eigenfunctions. For $f_n$, $\|f_n\|_2=1$ for every $n$, so $f_n\not\to0$ in $L^2$; a.e. convergence with bounded norms does not give norm convergence. Projections satisfy $P^2=P$, so $\lambda^2=\lambda$ for eigenvalues, and $\|P\|=1$ since $P$ fixes $M$. For $e_n$ the weak limit is $0$, which has norm $0\le\liminf\|e_n\|$; norms can drop in a weak limit.
