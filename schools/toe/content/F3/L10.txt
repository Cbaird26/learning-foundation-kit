@article
A **series** is a sum of the terms of a sequence. The **sigma notation**

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k = a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n$$

says: let the index $k$ run from the lower bound to the upper bound and add the summand $a_k$. The number of terms is upper minus lower plus one, so $\sum_{k=0}^{9}$ has $10$ terms. Sums are linear: $\sum (c\,a_k + b_k) = c\sum a_k + \sum b_k$, and $\sum_{k=1}^{n} 1 = n$.

### Arithmetic series: pair the terms

Write the sum forwards and backwards, and add the two rows term by term:

$$\begin{aligned} S_n &= a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n \\ S_n &= a_n + a_{n-1} + \dots + a_1 \end{aligned}$$

Every column adds to $a_1 + a_n$, and there are $n$ columns, so $2S_n = n(a_1 + a_n)$:

$$S_n = \frac{n}{2}(a_1 + a_n) = \frac{n}{2}\big(2a_1 + (n-1)d\big).$$

In words: the number of terms times the average of the first and last. With $a_k = k$ this gives $1 + 2 + \dots + n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$.

### Geometric series: multiply and subtract

Let $S_n = a + ar + ar^2 + \dots + ar^{n-1}$. Multiply by $r$ to shift every term:

$$rS_n = ar + ar^2 + \dots + ar^{n}.$$

Subtract: almost everything cancels, leaving $S_n - rS_n = a - ar^n$. Factor and divide, for $r \neq 1$:

$$S_n = a\,\frac{1 - r^n}{1 - r}.$$

If $r = 1$ every term equals $a$ and $S_n = na$.

### Infinite geometric series

If $|r| < 1$, then $r^n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, so the partial sums settle down to

$$\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a r^k = \frac{a}{1 - r}.$$

Here $a$ is the **first term actually present**. If $|r| \ge 1$ (with $a \neq 0$) the partial sums do not settle and the series **diverges**. For example $0.\overline{36} = \dfrac{0.36}{1 - 0.01} = \dfrac{4}{11}$.

### Telescoping sums

Some sums collapse because neighboring terms cancel. Since $\dfrac{1}{k(k+1)} = \dfrac1k - \dfrac1{k+1}$, we get $\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{k(k+1)} = 1 - \dfrac{1}{n+1}$.

### Test every formula on a small case

A derived formula is a claim about all $n$, so check it at $n = 1$, $2$ and $3$ where you can add by hand. If $S_3 = a + ar + ar^2$ for $a = 2$ and $r = 3$ is $2 + 6 + 18 = 26$, the formula must give $2\cdot\frac{1 - 27}{1 - 3} = 26$. Small tests catch off-by-one errors fast.

@example
title: Sum of an arithmetic series
problem: Find the sum of the first $40$ terms of $5 + 8 + 11 + \dots$
step: The common difference is $d = 3$ and $a_1 = 5$.
step: The last term is $a_{40} = 5 + 39 \cdot 3 = 122$.
step: Use $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(a_1 + a_n)$: $S_{40} = 20(5 + 122) = 20 \cdot 127 = 2540$.
step: Sanity check with the other form: $\frac{40}{2}(2 \cdot 5 + 39 \cdot 3) = 20(127) = 2540$.

@example
title: Finite and infinite geometric sums
problem: Compute $\sum_{k=1}^{6} 5\cdot 2^{k-1}$ and $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 4\left(\tfrac13\right)^{k}$.
step: First sum: the first term is $a = 5$, the ratio is $r = 2$, and there are $n = 6$ terms.
step: $S_6 = 5\cdot\dfrac{1 - 2^6}{1 - 2} = 5 \cdot 63 = 315$. Check by adding: $5 + 10 + 20 + 40 + 80 + 160 = 315$.
step: Second sum: the first term present is at $k = 1$, namely $a = 4\cdot\frac13 = \frac43$, and $r = \frac13$ with $|r| < 1$.
step: $S = \dfrac{4/3}{1 - 1/3} = \dfrac{4/3}{2/3} = 2$.

@mistakes
- Miscounting terms: $\sum_{k=0}^{9}$ has $10$ terms, not $9$.
- Using $\frac{a}{1-r}$ with $|r| \ge 1$, or using the wrong first term when the index does not start at the exponent $0$.
- Using "average of first and last times $n$" for a geometric series. It works only for arithmetic series.
- Trusting a formula without testing it at $n = 1, 2, 3$.

@practice num
prompt: Evaluate $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{5}(2k + 1)$.
answer: 35
check: sum(2*k + 1 for k in range(1, 6))
hint: Write out the five terms.
hint: The terms are $3, 5, 7, 9, 11$.
solution: $3 + 5 + 7 + 9 + 11 = 35$. Alternatively, $2\cdot\frac{5\cdot 6}{2} + 5 = 30 + 5 = 35$.

@practice num
prompt: Find the sum of the first $30$ terms of the arithmetic series $4 + 9 + 14 + \dots$
answer: 2295
check: sum(4 + 5*i for i in range(30))
hint: First find $d$ and the $30$th term.
hint: $a_{30} = 4 + 29 \cdot 5$. Then use $\frac{n}{2}(a_1 + a_n)$.
solution: $a_{30} = 4 + 145 = 149$, so $S_{30} = \frac{30}{2}(4 + 149) = 15 \cdot 153 = 2295$.

@practice num
prompt: Evaluate $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{7} 64\left(\tfrac12\right)^{k}$. Give an exact value.
answer: 255/2
check: sum(64 * 0.5**k for k in range(8))
hint: This is geometric with first term $64$ and ratio $\frac12$. Count the terms.
hint: There are $8$ terms. Use $S_n = a\frac{1 - r^n}{1 - r}$.
solution: $S_8 = 64\cdot\dfrac{1 - (1/2)^8}{1 - 1/2} = 128\left(1 - \tfrac{1}{256}\right) = 128 - \tfrac12 = \tfrac{255}{2} = 127.5$.

@practice num
prompt: Evaluate the infinite series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} 6\left(-\tfrac12\right)^{k}$.
answer: 4
check: nsum(lambda k: 6 * (-0.5)**k, 0, 200)
hint: Check that $|r| < 1$ first. Then use the first term and the ratio.
hint: The first term is $6$ and the ratio is $-\frac12$. The sum is $\frac{a}{1 - r}$.
solution: Since $|-\frac12| < 1$ the series converges to $\dfrac{6}{1 - (-1/2)} = \dfrac{6}{3/2} = 4$.

@practice self
prompt: Deliverable practice. (a) Derive the formula for the sum of the first $n$ terms of an arithmetic series by writing the sum forwards and backwards. (b) Derive the formula for the sum of the first $n$ terms of a geometric series by multiplying by $r$ and subtracting. (c) Test each formula on a small case by adding the terms directly: use $3 + 7 + 11 + 15$ for (a) and $2 + 6 + 18 + 54$ for (b).
hint: In (a), each column of the forward and backward rows adds to the same value. How many columns are there?
hint: In (b), after subtracting $rS_n$ from $S_n$ only two terms survive.
solution: (a) $S_n = a_1 + \dots + a_n$ and, reversed, $S_n = a_n + \dots + a_1$. Adding column by column, each of the $n$ columns equals $a_1 + a_n$ (because moving one term forward in the first row and back in the second keeps the total the same). So $2S_n = n(a_1 + a_n)$ and $S_n = \frac n2 (a_1 + a_n)$. (b) $S_n = a + ar + \dots + ar^{n-1}$ and $rS_n = ar + \dots + ar^n$. Subtracting, $S_n - rS_n = a - ar^n$, so $S_n(1 - r) = a(1 - r^n)$ and $S_n = a\frac{1 - r^n}{1 - r}$ for $r \neq 1$. (c) For $3 + 7 + 11 + 15 = 36$: $n = 4$, $\frac42(3 + 15) = 36$. For $2 + 6 + 18 + 54 = 80$: $a = 2$, $r = 3$, $n = 4$, $2\cdot\frac{1 - 81}{1 - 3} = 2 \cdot 40 = 80$.
rubric: Part (a) pairs terms so that every column sums to $a_1 + a_n$, counts $n$ columns, and divides by $2$.
rubric: Part (b) multiplies by $r$, subtracts so the middle terms cancel, and states the condition $r \neq 1$.
rubric: Both formulas are tested against a direct sum ($36$ and $80$) and agree.

@quiz num
prompt: Evaluate $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{99}\frac{1}{k(k+1)}$. Give an exact value.
answer: 99/100
check: sum(1/(k*(k + 1)) for k in range(1, 100))
hint: Split each fraction: $\frac{1}{k(k+1)} = \frac{1}{k} - \frac{1}{k+1}$.
hint: Write out the first few terms of the split sum and see what cancels.
solution: The sum telescopes: $\left(1 - \tfrac12\right) + \left(\tfrac12 - \tfrac13\right) + \dots + \left(\tfrac1{99} - \tfrac1{100}\right) = 1 - \tfrac{1}{100} = \tfrac{99}{100}$.

@quiz expr
prompt: Find a closed-form expression in $n$ for $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(3k - 2)$.
vars: n
lead: $S_n =$
answer: n(3n - 1)/2
check: lambda n: np.polyval(np.polyfit([0, 1, 2, 3], [sum(3*k - 2 for k in range(1, m + 1)) for m in range(4)], 2), n)
hint: This is an arithmetic series with $a_k = 3k - 2$. What are $a_1$ and $a_n$?
hint: Use $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(a_1 + a_n)$ with $a_1 = 1$ and $a_n = 3n - 2$.
solution: $a_1 = 1$ and $a_n = 3n - 2$, so $S_n = \frac n2(1 + 3n - 2) = \frac{n(3n - 1)}{2}$. Check: $S_3 = 1 + 4 + 7 = 12$ and $\frac{3 \cdot 8}{2} = 12$.
