@article
Newtonian gravity says $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$: a second-order equation for the field, linear in the source. We want a relativistic analogue: a tensor equation, second order in the metric, with $T_{\mu\nu}$ on the right. Conventions as in Week 1: signature $(-+++)$, the Riemann and Ricci conventions stated there, and $c = 1$.

### The field equations

The left side must be a symmetric tensor built from curvature, second order in $g_{\mu\nu}$, and divergence-free, because $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$. The Einstein tensor $G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 R\,g_{\mu\nu}$ does this by the Bianchi identity of Week 5. Since $\nabla_\lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 0$, a constant multiple of $g_{\mu\nu}$ may be added:
$$G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda\, g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\, T_{\mu\nu}.$$
With $c$ restored the right side is $8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}/c^4$. The constant $8\pi G$ is fixed by requiring the Newtonian limit of Week 8.

### Trace-reversed form and vacuum

In four dimensions $G^\mu{}_\mu = R - 2R = -R$. Tracing the equation gives $R = 4\Lambda - 8\pi G\,T$, and substituting back:
$$R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\left(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\, T\, g_{\mu\nu}\right) + \Lambda\, g_{\mu\nu}.$$
In vacuum ($T_{\mu\nu} = 0$) with $\Lambda = 0$ this reduces to $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$. Such spacetimes are **Ricci-flat**, not necessarily flat: black holes and gravitational waves are vacuum solutions with non-zero Riemann tensor.

Moving $\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ to the right defines an effective vacuum fluid, $T^{(\Lambda)}_{\mu\nu} = -\dfrac{\Lambda}{8\pi G}\,g_{\mu\nu}$, with
$$\rho_\Lambda = \frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G}, \qquad p_\Lambda = -\rho_\Lambda .$$
$\Lambda > 0$ is positive vacuum energy density with negative pressure. This is the form met in Week 6, and it is why $\Lambda$ matters for cosmology in Week 12.

### From an action

The equations follow from varying
$$S = \frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\left(R - 2\Lambda\right) + S_{\rm matter}$$
with respect to $g^{\mu\nu}$. Using $\delta\left(\sqrt{-g}\,R\right) = \sqrt{-g}\,G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ up to a boundary term, $\delta\sqrt{-g} = -\tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$, and $\delta S_{\rm matter} = -\tfrac12\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\,T_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$, we obtain
$$\frac{1}{16\pi G}\left(G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}\right) - \frac12 T_{\mu\nu} = 0 .$$

### What the equations determine

There are $10$ equations for the $10$ independent components of $g_{\mu\nu}$, but:

- the four contracted Bianchi identities $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ relate them, so only $6$ are independent;
- four of the metric functions can be changed freely by a coordinate transformation.

So the equations fix the geometry up to coordinates. They also *imply* $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, so matter equations of motion (for dust, geodesic motion) follow from the field equations.

### Strategy

Guess the metric form from a symmetry (static, spherical, homogeneous), compute $G_{\mu\nu}$ with the methods of Weeks 3 to 5, and equate to $8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}$. Weeks 9 and 12 do this for the two most important cases.

@example
title: Dust and the Ricci component $R_{00}$
problem: Use the trace-reversed equation with $\Lambda = 0$ to find $R_{00}$ for dust at rest with rest density $\rho$.
step: For dust at rest $T_{00} = \rho$ and the trace is $T = g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu} = -\rho$.
step: Insert into $R_{00} = 8\pi G\left(T_{00} - \tfrac12 T g_{00}\right)$ with $g_{00} = -1$ (to leading order): $R_{00} = 8\pi G\left(\rho - \tfrac12(-\rho)(-1)\right)$.
step: Simplify: $R_{00} = 8\pi G\left(\rho - \tfrac12\rho\right) = 4\pi G\rho$.
step: In Week 8 we show $R_{00} \approx \nabla^2\Phi$ for weak, static fields, so this reproduces $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$. The factor $\tfrac12$ from trace reversal is what gives the Newtonian coefficient.

@example
title: Curvature of a cosmological-constant vacuum
problem: For $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ and $\Lambda \neq 0$, find $R_{\mu\nu}$ and $R$, and the sign of the effective vacuum energy density.
step: Trace-reversed form with $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ gives $R_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$.
step: Contract: $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu} = 4\Lambda$, consistent with $R = 4\Lambda - 8\pi G T$.
step: From $G_{\mu\nu} = -\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ we get $G_{00} = +\Lambda$ because $g_{00} = -1$. The effective energy density is $\rho_\Lambda = \Lambda/(8\pi G)$.
step: So $\Lambda > 0$ corresponds to positive vacuum energy and (as Week 12 shows) accelerated expansion, and $\Lambda < 0$ to negative energy density.

@mistakes
- Forgetting the trace term when moving to $R_{\mu\nu}$: the source is $T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 T g_{\mu\nu}$, not $T_{\mu\nu}$.
- Concluding that a vacuum solution is flat. $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ does not imply $R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = 0$ in four dimensions.
- Dropping the factor $c^4$ in SI units: the coupling is $8\pi G/c^4$.
- Changing the sign of $\Lambda$ when moving it across the equals sign, or mixing it with the sign convention of another text.
