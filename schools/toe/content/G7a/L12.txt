@article
On the largest scales the universe looks the same at every place (homogeneous) and in every direction (isotropic). The most general spacetime with these symmetries has the **FLRW** metric. Conventions as before: signature $(-+++)$, $c = 1$ in formulas, Riemann and Ricci conventions of Week 1.

### The FLRW metric

$$ds^2 = -dt^2 + a(t)^2\left[\frac{dr^2}{1 - kr^2} + r^2\left(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2\right)\right], \qquad k = 0, +1, -1.$$
Here $t$ is cosmic time (the proper time of comoving observers at fixed $r, \theta, \phi$), $a(t)$ is the **scale factor**, and $k$ is the sign of the spatial curvature (flat, spherical, hyperbolic). With comoving distance $\chi$, the proper distance at fixed $t$ is $d = a(t)\chi$. It grows at the rate $\dot d = H d$, with the **Hubble parameter** $H = \dot a/a$: Hubble's law.

For $k = 0$ the only non-zero Christoffel symbols are $\Gamma^0_{ij} = a\dot a\,\delta_{ij}$ and $\Gamma^i_{0j} = H\delta^i_j$. For $k \neq 0$ there are additional purely spatial ones.

### Redshift

Radial light rays satisfy $dt = a\,d\chi$ (flat case). Two successive wave crests emitted at $t_e$ and $t_e + \delta t_e$ and received at $t_0$ and $t_0 + \delta t_0$ travel the same comoving distance, so $\delta t_e/a_e = \delta t_0/a_0$. Wavelengths therefore stretch with space:
$$1 + z = \frac{\lambda_0}{\lambda_e} = \frac{a(t_0)}{a(t_e)} .$$
For massive particles the comoving momentum is conserved, so the physical momentum falls as $1/a$.

### The Friedmann equations

Take a perfect fluid at rest in the comoving frame, $T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho + p)u^\mu u^\nu + p\,g^{\mu\nu}$. For $k = 0$ the Einstein tensor has $G_{00} = 3H^2$ and $G_{ij} = -\left(2\ddot a/a + H^2\right)a^2\delta_{ij}$. The Einstein equations with a cosmological constant give
$$H^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho - \frac{k}{a^2} + \frac\Lambda3, \qquad \frac{\ddot a}{a} = -\frac{4\pi G}{3}\left(\rho + 3p\right) + \frac\Lambda3 .$$
With $c$ restored, $k$ becomes $kc^2$ and $\rho$ is a mass density. Energy conservation, $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu 0} = 0$, gives
$$\dot\rho + 3H\left(\rho + p\right) = 0 .$$
It follows from the other two equations. For $p = w\rho$ with constant $w$:
$$\rho \propto a^{-3(1 + w)} ,$$
so dust ($w = 0$) dilutes as $a^{-3}$, radiation ($w = 1/3$) as $a^{-4}$ (the number density dilutes as $a^{-3}$ and each photon also loses energy as $1/a$), and vacuum energy ($w = -1$) stays constant. The **critical density** is $\rho_c = 3H^2/(8\pi G)$: for $\Lambda = 0$, the universe is spatially flat exactly when $\rho = \rho_c$.

### Simple solutions

For $k = 0$ and a single fluid with $w \neq -1$, inserting $a \propto t^n$ gives $n = \dfrac{2}{3(1 + w)}$ and $H = n/t$:

- dust: $a \propto t^{2/3}$, $H = \dfrac{2}{3t}$, age $= \dfrac{2}{3H_0}$;
- radiation: $a \propto t^{1/2}$, $H = \dfrac1{2t}$;
- $\Lambda$ only: $a \propto e^{Ht}$ with $H = \sqrt{\Lambda/3}$ constant (de Sitter).

The expansion accelerates, $\ddot a > 0$, when $\rho + 3p < 0$, i.e. when $w < -1/3$. Ordinary matter and radiation always decelerate the expansion. In the first two solutions $a \to 0$ at a finite time in the past, a singularity of the model. The expansion is a change in the geometry between comoving observers; it is not a force acting on bound systems such as atoms or galaxies.

@example
title: Cosmological redshift from a null geodesic
problem: Show that light emitted at $t_e$ and received at $t_0$ is redshifted by $1 + z = a(t_0)/a(t_e)$ in a flat FLRW universe.
step: For a radial null ray $ds^2 = 0$ gives $-dt^2 + a^2 d\chi^2 = 0$, so $d\chi = dt/a(t)$ along the ray.
step: Between emission and reception the comoving distance is $\chi = \int_{t_e}^{t_0} dt/a$, which is fixed for comoving source and observer.
step: For a second crest emitted a short time $\delta t_e$ later, the integral must be the same, so $\delta t_0/a(t_0) = \delta t_e/a(t_e)$.
step: Wavelength is proportional to the time between crests, so $\lambda_0/\lambda_e = \delta t_0/\delta t_e = a(t_0)/a(t_e)$, which gives $1 + z = a(t_0)/a(t_e)$.

@example
title: The age of a flat, dust-filled universe
problem: A flat universe contains only dust. Find $a(t)$ and the age in terms of $H_0$, and evaluate it for $H_0 = 70$ km/s/Mpc.
step: Dust has $\rho \propto a^{-3}$, so the Friedmann equation gives $\dot a^2 \propto 1/a$, i.e. $a^{1/2}\,da \propto dt$ and $a \propto t^{2/3}$ with $a(0) = 0$.
step: Then $H = \dot a/a = \dfrac{2}{3t}$, so the age $t_0$ satisfies $H_0 = \dfrac{2}{3t_0}$, giving $t_0 = \dfrac{2}{3H_0}$.
step: Convert: $70\ \text{km/s/Mpc} = 70\times10^3/(3.086\times10^{22})\ \text{s}^{-1} = 2.27\times10^{-18}\ \text{s}^{-1}$.
step: $t_0 = 2/(3\times2.27\times10^{-18}\ \text{s}^{-1}) = 2.94\times10^{17}$ s $\approx 9.3$ Gyr. This is shorter than the ages of the oldest stars, which shows that a dust-only model fails and $\Lambda$ matters today.

@mistakes
- Treating cosmological redshift as a Doppler shift from motion through space. It comes from the stretching of space, $1 + z = a_0/a_e$, and agrees with Doppler only for small $z$.
- Using $\rho \propto a^{-3}$ for radiation. Radiation scales as $a^{-4}$.
- Confusing comoving and proper distance. $d = a(t)\chi$.
- Believing acceleration requires negative energy density. It requires negative pressure, $p < -\rho/3$.
