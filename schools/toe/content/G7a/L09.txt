@article
Outside a spherical, non-rotating mass the Einstein equations in vacuum have a unique solution. **Birkhoff's theorem** says any spherically symmetric vacuum solution is static and has the form below, even if the source is collapsing or pulsating.

**Units.** From now on in formulas we set $G = c = 1$, so $M$ is a length (it stands for $GM/c^2$) and $r_s = 2M$. Numerical problems restore SI units explicitly. Signature $(-+++)$ as before.

### The metric

With $f(r) = 1 - 2M/r$,
$$ds^2 = -f\,dt^2 + \frac{dr^2}{f} + r^2\left(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\, d\phi^2\right).$$
The coordinate $r$ is the **areal radius**: spheres of constant $r$ have area $4\pi r^2$. The proper radial distance $\int dr/\sqrt f$ is larger than $\Delta r$. For $r \gg M$, $g_{tt} \approx -(1 + 2\Phi)$ with $\Phi = -M/r$, which identifies $M$ as the mass (Week 8). A static observer at $r$ has $d\tau = \sqrt{f}\,dt$, so light of frequency $\nu_{\rm emit}$ emitted at $r$ is received at infinity with $\nu_\infty = \sqrt{f(r)}\,\nu_{\rm emit}$.

### Conserved quantities and the radial equation

The metric is independent of $t$ and $\phi$, so with a dot for $d/d\tau$ the quantities
$$E = f\,\dot t, \qquad L = r^2\dot\phi$$
are conserved (energy and angular momentum per unit mass). Spherical symmetry lets us choose the orbital plane $\theta = \pi/2$. Using $g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu = -1$ gives
$$\dot r^2 + V_{\rm eff}(r) = E^2, \qquad V_{\rm eff} = \left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right)\left(1 + \frac{L^2}{r^2}\right) = 1 - \frac{2M}{r} + \frac{L^2}{r^2} - \frac{2ML^2}{r^3}.$$
The first three terms are the Newtonian effective potential (up to an additive constant and an overall factor of $2$); the last term is new. It overwhelms the centrifugal barrier at small $r$, so $V_{\rm eff}$ falls to zero at $r = 2M$ instead of rising without limit, and it is responsible for the features below.

### Circular orbits

Setting $V_{\rm eff}' = 0$ gives, for $r > 3M$,
$$L^2 = \frac{M r^2}{r - 3M}, \qquad E = \frac{1 - 2M/r}{\sqrt{1 - 3M/r}}, \qquad \frac{d\phi}{dt} = \sqrt{\frac{M}{r^3}} .$$
The last result is Kepler's third law, exact in coordinate time. An orbit is stable where $V_{\rm eff}'' > 0$, which requires $r > 6M$: the **innermost stable circular orbit** (ISCO) is at $r = 6M$. Between $3M$ and $6M$ circular orbits exist but are unstable, and none exist below $3M$.

### Light

For null geodesics $g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu = 0$ gives $\dot r^2 + f L^2/r^2 = E^2$. There is an unstable circular photon orbit at the **photon sphere** $r = 3M$. Rays with impact parameter $b = L/E$ below $b_c = 3\sqrt3\,M$ are captured; above it they escape with a deflection that approaches $4M/b$ at large $b$.

### Perihelion advance

For a bound orbit with semi-major axis $a$ and eccentricity $e$, the $-2ML^2/r^3$ term makes the ellipse precess. Per orbit, in the direction of motion,
$$\Delta\phi = \frac{6\pi M}{a\,(1 - e^2)} \quad\left(= \frac{6\pi GM}{c^2 a(1 - e^2)}\right).$$

@example
title: Deriving the effective potential
problem: Derive $\dot r^2 + V_{\rm eff} = E^2$ for a timelike geodesic in the plane $\theta = \pi/2$.
step: The metric does not depend on $t$ or $\phi$, so $E = f\dot t$ and $L = r^2\dot\phi$ are constant along the geodesic, where $f = 1 - 2M/r$.
step: The normalization of a timelike geodesic is $-f\dot t^2 + \dot r^2/f + r^2\dot\phi^2 = -1$. Substitute $\dot t = E/f$ and $\dot\phi = L/r^2$: $-\dfrac{E^2}{f} + \dfrac{\dot r^2}{f} + \dfrac{L^2}{r^2} = -1$.
step: Multiply by $f$ and rearrange: $\dot r^2 = E^2 - f\left(1 + \dfrac{L^2}{r^2}\right)$.
step: So $V_{\rm eff} = \left(1 - \dfrac{2M}{r}\right)\left(1 + \dfrac{L^2}{r^2}\right)$. Expanding gives the Newtonian terms plus $-2ML^2/r^3$.

@example
title: The innermost stable circular orbit
problem: Find $E$ and $L$ for the circular orbit at $r = 6M$ and the fraction of rest energy released if a particle spirals in from infinity to that orbit.
step: Circular orbits have $\dot r = 0$ and $V_{\rm eff}' = 0$. Solving gives $L^2 = Mr^2/(r - 3M)$ and $E = (1 - 2M/r)/\sqrt{1 - 3M/r}$.
step: At $r = 6M$: $L^2 = M\cdot 36M^2/(3M) = 12M^2$, so $L = 2\sqrt3\,M$.
step: At $r = 6M$: $E = \dfrac{1 - 1/3}{\sqrt{1 - 1/2}} = \dfrac{2/3}{1/\sqrt2} = \dfrac{2\sqrt2}{3} \approx 0.943$.
step: A particle at infinity at rest has $E = 1$, so about $1 - 0.943 = 5.7\%$ of its rest energy can be radiated before it reaches the ISCO.

@mistakes
- Treating $r$ as the distance from the centre. It is the areal radius; the proper radial distance is larger.
- Using the Newtonian effective potential: without the term $-2ML^2/r^3$ there is no ISCO and no precession.
- Forgetting to restore $GM/c^2$ for a length, or $GM/c^3$ for a time, when converting an answer in units of $M$ to metres or seconds.
- Mixing coordinate time $t$ and proper time $\tau$: $\Omega = d\phi/dt = \sqrt{M/r^3}$ is per unit coordinate time.

@practice expr

vars: r, L
domain: r: 2.5..8; L: 0..4
lead: $V_{\rm eff} =$
answer: (1 - 2/r)*(1 + L^2/r^2)
check: lambda r, L: 1.0 - (1 - 2/r)*((1 - 2/r)*(1.0/(1 - 2/r))**2 - r**2*(L/r**2)**2 - 1)
