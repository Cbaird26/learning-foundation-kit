@article
Last week's metric has two worrying places, $r = 2M$ and $r = 0$. This week we decide what each one means. We keep $G = c = 1$ in formulas, signature $(-+++)$, and $f = 1 - 2M/r$.

### Radial light rays and the tortoise coordinate

For a radial null ray, $0 = -f\,dt^2 + dr^2/f$, so
$$\frac{dt}{dr} = \pm\frac1f, \qquad \frac{dr}{dt} = \pm f .$$
Define the **tortoise coordinate** $r_*$ by $dr_* = dr/f$:
$$r_* = r + 2M\ln\left|\frac{r}{2M} - 1\right| .$$
Radial rays are then $t \pm r_* = \text{const}$, exactly as in flat space. As $r \to 2M$ from outside, $r_* \to -\infty$: a light ray needs an **infinite coordinate time** to reach $r = 2M$, since its coordinate speed $dr/dt = f \to 0$.

### $r = 2M$ is a coordinate singularity

At $r = 2M$, $g_{tt} \to 0$ and $g_{rr} \to \infty$. But scalar curvature invariants are finite there; for example
$$R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = \frac{48M^2}{r^6}.$$
This diverges only at $r = 0$, which is a genuine **curvature singularity**. Tidal accelerations scale as $M/r^3$ and are finite at the horizon: a large black hole is not locally dangerous to cross.

### Regular coordinates

Replace $t$ by the advanced time $v = t + r_*$. This gives the ingoing **Eddington-Finkelstein** form
$$ds^2 = -f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr + r^2\left(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2\right).$$
All components are finite at $r = 2M$ and the metric is non-degenerate, so the spacetime continues smoothly inward. Ingoing radial null rays are $v = \text{const}$; the other family obeys $dr/dv = f/2$.

### The event horizon

The **event horizon** is the boundary of the region from which no signal can reach infinity. For Schwarzschild it is $r = 2M$, a null surface: the Killing vector $\partial_t$ has norm $g_{tt} = -f$, timelike outside, null on the horizon and spacelike inside. Inside, $r$ becomes a timelike coordinate. Every future-directed timelike or null path then has decreasing $r$ and ends at $r = 0$ in finite proper time (finite affine parameter, for a null path). The longest proper time possible between the horizon and the singularity is $\pi M$.

### Free fall and the distant view

For radial free fall with $L = 0$ from rest at $r_0$, so $E^2 = 1 - 2M/r_0$, the equation of Week 9 gives $\dot r^2 = 2M\left(\frac1r - \frac1{r_0}\right)$. The proper time to reach $r = 0$ is finite:
$$\tau = \frac\pi2\sqrt{\frac{r_0^3}{2M}} .$$
A distant observer instead sees the infalling object's light redshifted and dimmed, and the image fades on a time of order $4M$. The crossing itself is never seen: coordinate time $t \to \infty$ at the horizon, but proper time is finite.

### A semiclassical remark

Quantum field theory on this background gives the horizon a temperature $T_H = \hbar c^3/(8\pi G M k_B)$, about $6\times10^{-8}$ K for one solar mass. This is not a prediction of the classical equations used in this course. The maximally extended Schwarzschild solution also contains a white-hole region and a second exterior; a black hole formed by collapse has neither.

@example
title: Coordinate time to reach the horizon diverges
problem: A radial light ray moves inward from $r_0 > 2M$. Show that its coordinate time to reach $r = 2M$ is infinite.
step: For an ingoing ray $dt = -dr/f$, so the time to go from $r_0$ down to $r_1 > 2M$ is $\Delta t = \int_{r_1}^{r_0} \dfrac{dr}{1 - 2M/r} = \int_{r_1}^{r_0}\left(1 + \dfrac{2M}{r - 2M}\right)dr$.
step: Integrate: $\Delta t = (r_0 - r_1) + 2M\ln\dfrac{r_0 - 2M}{r_1 - 2M}$.
step: Let $r_1 \to 2M$. The logarithm grows without bound, so $\Delta t \to \infty$.
step: The divergence belongs to the coordinate $t$, not to the ray. In $v = t + r_*$ the same ray is $v = \text{const}$ and crosses $r = 2M$ smoothly. Its affine parameter is finite there.

@example
title: Proper time of radial infall
problem: A particle falls radially from rest at $r_0$. Find its proper time to reach $r = 0$, and the value for $r_0 = 2M$.
step: With $L = 0$ and $E^2 = 1 - 2M/r_0$, the radial equation $\dot r^2 = E^2 - f$ becomes $\dot r^2 = 2M\left(\dfrac1r - \dfrac1{r_0}\right)$.
step: Then $d\tau = \sqrt{\dfrac{r\,r_0}{2M\,(r_0 - r)}}\;dr$ on the inward leg. Substitute $r = r_0\sin^2\chi$ to get $d\tau = \sqrt{\dfrac{r_0}{2M}}\;2r_0\sin^2\chi\,d\chi$.
step: Integrating from $\chi = 0$ to $\pi/2$ gives $\tau = \dfrac\pi2\sqrt{\dfrac{r_0^3}{2M}}$.
step: For $r_0 = 2M$ this is $\tau = \dfrac\pi2\sqrt{\dfrac{8M^3}{2M}} = \pi M$, the maximum proper time inside a Schwarzschild horizon.

@mistakes
- Treating $r = 2M$ as a physical singularity. Curvature scalars are finite there and an infalling observer crosses it in finite proper time.
- Believing that an infalling observer takes infinite time to cross because a distant observer never sees the crossing. Distant coordinate time is not the observer's proper time.
- Forgetting that $r$ is spacelike outside and timelike inside, so inside one cannot hold $r$ fixed.
- Quoting travel times in $M$ without converting back to seconds with $GM/c^3$.

@practice self
prompt: Starting from the Schwarzschild metric, introduce $v = t + r_*$ with $dr_* = dr/f$ and show that $ds^2 = -f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr + r^2d\Omega^2$. Then explain why this shows that $r = 2M$ is not a curvature singularity.
hint: Write $dt = dv - dr/f$ and substitute into $-f\,dt^2$.
hint: The cross terms combine; check that $g_{vv}$, $g_{vr}$ and the determinant stay finite at $r = 2M$.
solution: With $dt = dv - dr/f$, $-f\,dt^2 = -f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr - dr^2/f$. The last term cancels $+dr^2/f$ in the original metric, leaving $ds^2 = -f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr + r^2d\Omega^2$. In the $(v, r)$ block the metric $\begin{pmatrix} -f & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $-1$ for all $r$, so it is finite and invertible at $r = 2M$, and the chart extends to $r < 2M$. A smooth metric with finite curvature there shows the singularity of the original chart was an artifact of $t$.
