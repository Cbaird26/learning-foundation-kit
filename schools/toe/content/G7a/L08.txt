@article
General relativity must reduce to Newtonian gravity where Newtonian gravity works. This week we check that, and we see where the theory says something new. Conventions: signature $(-+++)$; $c = 1$ unless $c$ is shown.

### Assumptions

1. **Weak field:** $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}$ with $|h_{\mu\nu}| \ll 1$; we work to first order in $h$.
2. **Static:** $\partial_t h_{\mu\nu} = 0$.
3. **Slow motion:** $|dx^i/dt| \ll 1$, so $d\tau \approx dt$ and $dx^i/d\tau \ll dt/d\tau$.

With $c$ restored the smallness condition is $|\Phi|/c^2 \ll 1$.

### Step 1: geodesics give the force law

For slow motion the geodesic equation reduces to $\dfrac{d^2x^i}{dt^2} \approx -\Gamma^i_{00}$. In a static field
$$\Gamma^i_{00} = -\tfrac12\,\partial_i h_{00}.$$
Newton says $\ddot x^i = -\partial_i\Phi$. The two agree if $h_{00} = -2\Phi$, i.e.
$$g_{00} = -\left(1 + \frac{2\Phi}{c^2}\right),$$
with the additive constant fixed by $\Phi \to 0$ at infinity. The proper time of a clock at rest is $d\tau = \sqrt{-g_{00}}\,dt \approx (1 + \Phi/c^2)\,dt$: clocks lower in the potential run slow. Between two heights this is the redshift of Week 2, with $\Delta\Phi = gh$.

### Step 2: the field equation gives Poisson

To first order and for a static field, only one term of the Ricci tensor survives:
$$R_{00} = R^i{}_{0i0} \approx \partial_i\Gamma^i_{00} = -\tfrac12\nabla^2 h_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi .$$
Week 7 gave $R_{00} = 4\pi G\rho$ for non-relativistic matter, so
$$\boxed{\;\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho\;}$$
This fixes the constant $8\pi G$ in Einstein's equation. The Riemann component $R^i{}_{0j0} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi$ also turns the geodesic deviation equation into the Newtonian tidal acceleration of Week 2, $\ddot S^i = -\partial_i\partial_j\Phi\,S^j$.

### Step 3: what is new, the spatial metric

The remaining Einstein equations for a static, non-relativistic source require the spatial metric to change too:
$$ds^2 = -(1 + 2\Phi)\,dt^2 + (1 - 2\Phi)\,d\vec x^{\,2} \qquad (c = 1).$$
For a null ray $ds^2 = 0$ gives a coordinate speed $|d\vec x/dt| \approx 1 + 2\Phi$. Light behaves as in a medium with refractive index $n = 1 - 2\Phi$, which is larger near a mass (where $\Phi < 0$), so rays bend towards it. For a point mass, $\Phi = -GM/r$, a ray with impact parameter $b$ is deflected by
$$\hat\alpha = \frac{4GM}{b\,c^2},$$
**twice** the value obtained from $g_{00}$ alone (the Newtonian corpuscle result). For a ray grazing the Sun, $\hat\alpha \approx 1.75''$.

### Validity and corrections

The expansion parameters are $\Phi/c^2$ and $v^2/c^2$. For the Earth's surface $|\Phi|/c^2 \sim 7\times 10^{-10}$; for the Sun's surface $\sim 2\times10^{-6}$; for a neutron star $\sim 0.2$, so the weak-field treatment fails there. Higher-order terms are the **post-Newtonian** corrections. Mercury's perihelion advance (Week 9) is the classic example.

@example
title: Poisson's equation from the Ricci component
problem: Starting from $g_{00} = -(1 + 2\Phi)$ for a static weak field, show that the $00$ Einstein equation gives $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$ for dust.
step: To first order and static, $R_{00} = R^\rho{}_{0\rho0}$ reduces to $\partial_i\Gamma^i_{00}$, because time derivatives vanish and products of $\Gamma$ are second order.
step: $\Gamma^i_{00} = -\tfrac12\,\partial_i g_{00} = \partial_i\Phi$, so $R_{00} = \partial_i\partial_i\Phi = \nabla^2\Phi$.
step: The trace-reversed Einstein equation for dust at rest gave $R_{00} = 4\pi G\rho$ (Week 7).
step: Equating the two expressions: $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$, Poisson's equation, with exactly the Newtonian coefficient.

@example
title: Light grazing the Sun
problem: Estimate the deflection of a light ray grazing the Sun, using $GM_\odot = 1.327\times10^{20}\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}^2$, $b = R_\odot = 6.96\times10^{8}$ m and $c = 2.998\times10^{8}$ m/s.
step: Effective index $n = 1 - 2\Phi/c^2 = 1 + 2GM/(rc^2)$. The transverse gradient at distance $b$ is $\partial_b n$, and the deflection is $\hat\alpha = \int \partial_b n\,dz$ along the ray.
step: With $r^2 = b^2 + z^2$, $\partial_b n = -\dfrac{2GM\,b}{c^2 (b^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}$, and $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dz}{(b^2 + z^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{2}{b^2}$. The magnitude is $\hat\alpha = 4GM/(bc^2)$.
step: Numerically $\hat\alpha = \dfrac{4(1.327\times10^{20})}{(6.96\times10^8)(8.988\times10^{16})} = 8.49\times10^{-6}$ rad.
step: Convert: $8.49\times10^{-6}\times 206265 \approx 1.75$ arcseconds.

@mistakes
- Sign slip $h_{00} = +2\Phi$. In signature $(-+++)$ the correct statement is $g_{00} = -(1 + 2\Phi/c^2)$, with $\Phi < 0$ near a mass.
- Using only $g_{00}$ for light and getting half the deflection. The spatial part $g_{ij} = (1 - 2\Phi)\delta_{ij}$ contributes the other half.
- Testing weakness of the field with $\Phi$ in SI units. The right test is $|\Phi|/c^2 \ll 1$.
- Confusing coordinate time with proper time: $d\tau = \sqrt{-g_{00}}\,dt$.

@practice self
prompt: Deliverable (recover a weak-field result): using the metric $ds^2 = -(1 + 2\Phi)\,dt^2 + (1 - 2\Phi)\,d\vec x^{\,2}$ with $\Phi = -GM/r$ and $c = 1$, derive the deflection angle $\hat\alpha = 4GM/b$ for a light ray with impact parameter $b$.
hint: Set $ds^2 = 0$ to find the coordinate speed, and interpret it as a refractive index $n(r)$.
hint: The deflection is the integral along the straight path of the transverse gradient of $n$; use $\int dz\,(b^2 + z^2)^{-3/2} = 2/b^2$.
solution: Null rays have $(1 + 2\Phi)\,dt^2 = (1 - 2\Phi)\,dx^2$, so $|dx/dt| \approx 1 + 2\Phi$ and $n = 1/|dx/dt| \approx 1 - 2\Phi = 1 + 2GM/r$. Using only $g_{00}$ gives $n = 1 - \Phi$ and half this value, $2GM/b$.
