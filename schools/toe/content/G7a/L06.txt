@article
In Newtonian gravity, mass density sources the potential $\Phi$. In relativity, energy, momentum and stress all gravitate, and the field equations are tensor equations, so the source must be a symmetric tensor $T^{\mu\nu}$. As in the earlier weeks, signature $(-+++)$ and $c = 1$.

### Meaning of the components

In an inertial frame, $T^{\mu\nu}$ is the flux of the $\mu$-th component of four-momentum across a surface of constant $x^\nu$:

- $T^{00}$ is the **energy density**;
- $T^{0i}$ is the **momentum density**, which equals the energy flux (for $c = 1$);
- $T^{ij}$ is the **stress**: the flux of $i$-momentum in direction $j$. Its diagonal entries are pressures.

It is symmetric, $T^{\mu\nu} = T^{\nu\mu}$. With $c$ restored $T^{00}$ is an energy density in $\text{J/m}^3$.

### Dust and perfect fluids

**Dust** (pressureless matter) has $T^{\mu\nu} = \rho_0\,u^\mu u^\nu$, with $\rho_0$ the rest-frame density. A **perfect fluid** is isotropic in its rest frame, with no viscosity or heat flow:
$$T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho + p)\,u^\mu u^\nu + p\,g^{\mu\nu}.$$
At rest, $u^\mu = (1, 0, 0, 0)$ and $g^{00} = -1$ give $T^{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(\rho, p, p, p)$. The trace is $T = g_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu} = -\rho + 3p$. With an equation of state $p = w\rho$:

| matter | $w$ | trace |
|---|---|---|
| dust | $0$ | $-\rho$ |
| radiation | $1/3$ | $0$ |
| cosmological constant | $-1$ | $-4\rho$ |

A cosmological constant has $T_{\mu\nu} = -\rho_\Lambda\, g_{\mu\nu}$, which has the same form in every frame: vacuum energy has no preferred rest frame.

### Fields as matter

For a scalar field with potential $V(\phi)$,
$$T_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi - g_{\mu\nu}\left[\tfrac12\, g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha\phi\,\partial_\beta\phi + V(\phi)\right],$$
so a homogeneous field $\phi(t)$ behaves as a perfect fluid with $\rho = \tfrac12\dot\phi^2 + V$ and $p = \tfrac12\dot\phi^2 - V$. For electromagnetism (units with $c = 1$ and no factors of $4\pi$ or $\epsilon_0$) $T^{\mu\nu} = F^{\mu\alpha}F^\nu{}_\alpha - \tfrac14 g^{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}$, which is traceless. In general $T_{\mu\nu} = -\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\dfrac{\delta S_{\rm matter}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}$.

### Conservation

Local energy and momentum conservation is the tensor equation
$$\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0 ,$$
which is $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ in flat space. For slow dust, the $\nu = 0$ component is the continuity equation $\partial_t\rho + \nabla\cdot(\rho\vec v) = 0$, and in curved spacetime the equation forces dust particles onto geodesics.

### Energy conditions

For a perfect fluid, with $t^\mu$ any timelike or null vector:

- **null:** $\rho + p \ge 0$;
- **weak** ($T_{\mu\nu}t^\mu t^\nu \ge 0$ for timelike $t$): $\rho \ge 0$ and $\rho + p \ge 0$;
- **strong:** $\rho + 3p \ge 0$ and $\rho + p \ge 0$;
- **dominant:** $\rho \ge |p|$.

Ordinary matter and radiation satisfy all of them. A cosmological constant ($p = -\rho$) violates the strong energy condition. Next week, $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ and $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ combine to make Einstein's equation consistent.

@example
title: A perfect fluid seen by a moving observer
problem: A radiation-like fluid has $\rho = 1$ and $p = 1/3$ in its rest frame. An observer sees it moving at $v = 0.6$ along $x$. Find $T^{00}$, $T^{0x}$ and $T^{xx}$ and check that the trace vanishes.
step: With $u^\mu = \gamma(1, v, 0, 0)$ and $\gamma^2 = 25/16$, write $T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho + p)u^\mu u^\nu + p\,g^{\mu\nu}$ with $\rho + p = 4/3$.
step: $T^{00} = \tfrac43\gamma^2 - p = \tfrac43\cdot\tfrac{25}{16} - \tfrac13 = \tfrac{25}{12} - \tfrac{4}{12} = \tfrac{7}{4}$. In general $T^{00} = \gamma^2(\rho + v^2 p)$: pressure raises the energy density seen by a moving observer.
step: $T^{0x} = (\rho + p)\gamma^2 v = \tfrac43\cdot\tfrac{25}{16}\cdot\tfrac35 = \tfrac54$ and $T^{xx} = (\rho + p)\gamma^2v^2 + p = \tfrac{25}{12}\cdot\tfrac{9}{25} + \tfrac13 = \tfrac34 + \tfrac13 = \tfrac{13}{12}$. The components $T^{yy} = T^{zz} = p = \tfrac13$ are unchanged.
step: Trace: $-T^{00} + T^{xx} + T^{yy} + T^{zz} = -\tfrac{21}{12} + \tfrac{13}{12} + \tfrac{8}{12} = 0$, as it is for radiation in every frame.

@example
title: Equation of state of a homogeneous scalar field
problem: A homogeneous scalar field has kinetic term $K = \tfrac12\dot\phi^2$ and potential $V > 0$. Find $w = p/\rho$ and its limiting values.
step: From $\rho = K + V$ and $p = K - V$ we get $w = \dfrac{K - V}{K + V}$.
step: If $V \gg K$ (a slowly rolling field), $w \to -1$: the field acts like a cosmological constant.
step: If $K = V$, then $p = 0$ and $w = 0$, like dust. If $K \gg V$, $w \to +1$ (a "stiff" fluid).
step: Since $K \ge 0$ and $V > 0$, $-1 < w \le 1$: the equation of state of a scalar field cannot go below $-1$.

@mistakes
- Using the $(+---)$ form $(\rho + p)u^\mu u^\nu - p\,g^{\mu\nu}$ in this course. In signature $(-+++)$ the check is $T^{00} = \rho$ in the rest frame.
- Treating energy density as a scalar. $\rho$ is a rest-frame quantity, and a moving observer sees $T^{00} = \gamma^2(\rho + v^2p)$.
- Forgetting that pressure gravitates: it enters the strong energy condition as $\rho + 3p$ and the trace as $-\rho + 3p$.
- Assuming $p \ge 0$ always. Vacuum energy has $p = -\rho$.
