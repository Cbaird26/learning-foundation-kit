@review
This guide lists the key result and method of each week, then a self-check and the most common errors. Conventions throughout: signature $(-+++)$, $c = 1$ (with $G = c = 1$ in Weeks 9 and 10 formulas), $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \cdots$, $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$, and $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}$.

### Week by week

1. **Special relativity.** Interval $\Delta s^2$ classifies separations; boosts preserve it; $p^\mu p_\mu = -m^2$.
2. **Equivalence principle.** Local frames are inertial; tidal terms limit the principle; hyperbolic motion, Rindler coordinates and redshift $gh/c^2$.
3. **Metrics and connections.** $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12 g^{\lambda\sigma}(\cdots)$ is not a tensor; $\nabla$ is metric compatible.
4. **Geodesics.** $\ddot x^\mu + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\dot x^\nu\dot x^\rho = 0$; use $L = \tfrac12 g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu$, conserved $p_\alpha$ and the normalization.
5. **Curvature.** Riemann, its symmetries, $n^2(n^2-1)/12$ components, Ricci, Bianchi identities; a sphere has $R = 2/a^2$.
6. **Stress-energy.** $T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho + p)u^\mu u^\nu + p\,g^{\mu\nu}$, trace $-\rho + 3p$, $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, energy conditions.
7. **Einstein equations.** Trace-reversed form, vacuum is Ricci-flat, $\Lambda$ as $p = -\rho$, 10 equations minus identities.
8. **Newtonian limit.** $g_{00} = -(1 + 2\Phi)$, $R_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$, light deflection $4GM/bc^2$.
9. **Schwarzschild.** $\dot r^2 + V_{\rm eff} = E^2$; ISCO $6M$, photon sphere $3M$, $b_c = 3\sqrt3M$, precession $6\pi M/(a(1-e^2))$.
10. **Horizons.** Tortoise coordinate, Eddington-Finkelstein, $r = 2M$ is a coordinate singularity, $\tau \le \pi M$ inside.
11. **Linearized gravity.** $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}$, TT gauge, strain $h/2$, quadrupole formula, $f_{\rm gw} = 2f_{\rm orb}$.
12. **FLRW.** $1 + z = a_0/a_e$; Friedmann equations; $\rho \propto a^{-3(1+w)}$; acceleration iff $w < -1/3$.

### Methods to master

- Choose coordinates from the symmetries, then list the conserved quantities before any heavy calculation.
- Compute Christoffel symbols from the Lagrangian, and curvature only for independent components.
- Always check a result in a limit: weak field, slow motion, flat space, or large $r$.

### Self-check

- I can state my signature, units and curvature signs without looking.
- I can derive $V_{\rm eff}$ and the circular-orbit conditions from scratch.
- I can explain, in words, why $r = 2M$ is not a curvature singularity.
- I can recover $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$ from the Einstein equations.
- I can derive the Friedmann equations' content: redshift, continuity and scaling laws.

### Most common errors

- Mixing signatures: a $(+---)$ formula with a $(-+++)$ metric.
- Reading $\Gamma \neq 0$ as curvature, or Ricci-flat as flat.
- Using $t$ instead of proper time or an affine parameter; treating $r$ as proper distance.
- Dropping the spatial part of the metric in light deflection (half the correct answer).
- Setting $f_{\rm gw}$ equal to the orbital frequency, and mixing comoving with proper distance.

@exam num
prompt: A Schwarzschild black hole has mass parameter $M$, in units $G = c = 1$. Clock A is held static at $r = 8M$. Clock B moves on the circular geodesic at the same radius $r = 8M$. Over the same interval of coordinate time $t$, what is the ratio $\Delta\tau_B/\Delta\tau_A$ of the proper times they record? Give an exact value.
answer: sqrt(5/6)
check: (lambda f, r, Om: math.sqrt(f - r**2*Om**2)/math.sqrt(f))(1 - 2/8.0, 8.0, math.sqrt(1/8.0**3))
hint: Clock A has $d\tau_A = \sqrt{1 - 2M/r}\;dt$. For clock B use $d\tau^2 = f\,dt^2 - r^2d\phi^2$ with $d\phi/dt = \Omega$.
hint: Circular geodesics have $\Omega = \sqrt{M/r^3}$ in coordinate time (Week 9).
hint: Then $d\tau_B^2 = \left(1 - 2M/r - M/r\right)dt^2 = (1 - 3M/r)\,dt^2$.
solution: With $\Omega^2 = M/r^3$ we get $d\tau_B^2 = (f - r^2\Omega^2)\,dt^2 = (1 - 3M/r)\,dt^2$, while $d\tau_A^2 = (1 - 2M/r)\,dt^2$. At $r = 8M$: $d\tau_B/dt = \sqrt{5/8}$ and $d\tau_A/dt = \sqrt{3/4}$, so the ratio is $\sqrt{(5/8)/(3/4)} = \sqrt{5/6} \approx 0.913$. The orbiting clock runs slower than the static one, because of its motion.

@exam num
prompt: A flat universe contains pressureless matter with density parameter $\Omega_m = 0.3$ today and a cosmological constant with $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.7$, treated as a fluid with $p = -\rho$ (Week 7). Matter dilutes as $\rho_m \propto a^{-3}$ and $\rho_\Lambda$ is constant. Use the acceleration equation $\ddot a/a = -\tfrac{4\pi G}{3}(\rho + 3p)$ summed over both fluids, with $a = 1$ today, to find the redshift $z$ at which the expansion switches from deceleration to acceleration. Give the answer to four decimal places.
answer: 0.6711
check: 1/root(lambda a: -0.5*0.3*a**(-3) + 0.7, 0.1, 1.0) - 1
tol: 0.5%
hint: Write each density as $\Omega_i\rho_c$ and note that $\rho_c = 3H_0^2/(8\pi G)$ appears as an overall factor.
hint: For matter $\rho + 3p = \rho_m$. For the cosmological constant $\rho + 3p = -2\rho_\Lambda$. Acceleration changes sign when $\rho_m = 2\rho_\Lambda$.
hint: Solve $0.3\,a^{-3} = 2(0.7)$ for $a$, then use $1 + z = 1/a$.
solution: $\ddot a/a = -\tfrac{4\pi G}{3}(\rho_m - 2\rho_\Lambda) = H_0^2\left(-\tfrac12\Omega_m a^{-3} + \Omega_\Lambda\right)$. This vanishes when $a^3 = \Omega_m/(2\Omega_\Lambda) = 0.3/1.4 = 3/14$, so $a = 0.5984$ and $z = 1/a - 1 = 0.6711$. Earlier ($z$ larger) matter dominates and the expansion decelerates; later it accelerates.

@exam self
prompt: A particle falls radially into a Schwarzschild black hole of mass $M$ (units $G = c = 1$), starting from rest at infinity. (a) Using the conserved energy and the normalization condition, show that $\dot r^2 = 2M/r$ and $dt/d\tau = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1}$. (b) Find the proper time taken to fall from $r = 6M$ to $r = 2M$. (c) Show that the coordinate time to reach $r = 2M$ diverges while the proper time is finite, and say what a distant observer sees. (d) Restore $G$ and $c$ and show that for $r \gg GM/c^2$ the result for $\dot r$ matches Newtonian free fall from rest at infinity.
hint: Start from rest at infinity means $E = 1$, and radial means $L = 0$. Use $\dot r^2 + V_{\rm eff} = E^2$ with $V_{\rm eff} = 1 - 2M/r$.
hint: For (b) integrate $d\tau = dr/|\dot r|$, and for (c) use $dt/dr = (dt/d\tau)/\dot r$ and integrate near $r = 2M$.
hint: For (d), Newtonian energy conservation with zero total energy gives $v^2 = 2GM/r$.
solution: (a) $E = f\dot t = 1$ gives $dt/d\tau = 1/f$. The normalization $-f\dot t^2 + \dot r^2/f = -1$ then gives $\dot r^2 = 1 - f = 2M/r$, so $\dot r = -\sqrt{2M/r}$ for infall. (b) $\Delta\tau = \int_{2M}^{6M}\sqrt{r/2M}\,dr = \dfrac{2}{3\sqrt{2M}}\left[(6M)^{3/2} - (2M)^{3/2}\right] = \left(4\sqrt3 - \tfrac43\right)M \approx 5.59\,M$. (c) $\dfrac{dt}{dr} = \dfrac{1}{f\,\dot r}$ has magnitude $\dfrac{1}{(1 - 2M/r)\sqrt{2M/r}}$, which behaves like $\dfrac{2M}{r - 2M}$ near $r = 2M$, so $\Delta t$ diverges logarithmically. The proper time stays finite. A distant observer sees the particle slow, redden and fade without seeing it cross. (d) With $c$ restored, $\dot r^2 = 2GM/r$ for $r \gg GM/c^2$ since $dt/d\tau \to 1$, which is $\tfrac12 v^2 - GM/r = 0$, the Newtonian result. Relativistic corrections enter at order $GM/(rc^2)$.
rubric: Obtains $\dot r^2 = 2M/r$ and $dt/d\tau = 1/f$ from $E = 1$, $L = 0$ and the normalization.
rubric: Evaluates the proper-time integral correctly to get $(4\sqrt3 - 4/3)M$ or $\approx 5.59M$.
rubric: Shows the logarithmic divergence of coordinate time at $r = 2M$ and contrasts it with finite proper time.
rubric: Recovers Newtonian free fall in the weak-field limit and identifies the order of the first correction.
