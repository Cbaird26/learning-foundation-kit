@article
Think of an analytic function $w = f(z)$ as a map from the $z$-plane to the $w$-plane. Take a smooth curve $z(t)$ through $z_0$. Its image $f(z(t))$ has tangent $f'(z_0)\,z'(t_0)$ by the chain rule. Multiplying by the complex number $f'(z_0)$ **rotates every tangent direction by $\arg f'(z_0)$ and scales lengths by $\lvert f'(z_0)\rvert$**. So the angle between any two curves at $z_0$ is preserved, in size and orientation. A map with this property is **conformal**; analytic with $f'(z_0) \neq 0$ is the condition. Where $f'(z_0) = 0$ the angles are multiplied: $z^2$ doubles angles at $0$.

### Basic maps

| map | action |
|---|---|
| $w = az + b$ | rotate by $\arg a$, scale by $\lvert a\rvert$, translate by $b$ |
| $w = z^2$ | first quadrant onto the upper half-plane (angles double at $0$) |
| $w = e^z$ | strip $0 < \operatorname{Im}z < \pi$ onto the upper half-plane; horizontal lines become rays, vertical segments become arcs |
| $w = \operatorname{Log}z$ | inverse of $e^z$: upper half-plane onto the strip $0 < \operatorname{Im}w < \pi$ |
| $w = 1/z$ | circles and lines onto circles and lines; swaps inside and outside of the unit circle |
| $w = \dfrac{z - i}{z + i}$ | upper half-plane onto the unit disk |

### Mobius maps

A **Mobius transformation** is $w = \dfrac{az + b}{cz + d}$ with $ad - bc \neq 0$. It maps circles and lines to circles and lines, compositions of Mobius maps are Mobius maps, and any three distinct points can be sent to any three distinct points by exactly one such map. To see where a disk goes, find where its boundary circle goes and test a point.

### How to map a domain and track its boundary

1. Break the boundary into pieces: line segments and arcs.
2. Parametrize each piece and compute its image under $f$.
3. The interior goes to the region on the same side of the image boundary: a conformal map preserves orientation, so the region stays on the **left** of the boundary as you traverse it counterclockwise. Or simply test one interior point.
4. Check corners. The angle at a corner is preserved when $f' \neq 0$ there, and multiplied by $m$ when $f' = 0$, where $m$ is the order of the zero of $f(z) - f(z_0)$ at the corner ($m = 2$ for $z^2$ at $0$).

### Why physicists care

If $\varphi(u, v)$ is harmonic in the $w$-plane and $w = f(z)$ is analytic, then $\varphi(f(z))$ is harmonic in the $z$-plane, and boundary values are carried along the boundary. So to solve a planar electrostatics or ideal-flow problem in an awkward domain, map it to a simple one (a half-plane or a disk), solve there, and pull the answer back. For instance, in the upper half-plane with potential $0$ on the positive real axis and $V_0$ on the negative real axis, the solution is $\varphi = \dfrac{V_0}{\pi}\arg z$.

@example
title: Mapping a quarter disk with $z^2$
problem: Find the image of the quarter disk $D = \{\lvert z\rvert < 1,\ 0 < \arg z < \pi/2\}$ under $w = z^2$.
step: The boundary has three pieces. Piece A: the segment $z = x$, $0 \le x \le 1$, maps to $w = x^2 \in [0, 1]$, the segment from $0$ to $1$.
step: Piece B: the arc $z = e^{i\theta}$, $0 \le \theta \le \pi/2$, maps to $w = e^{2i\theta}$, the upper unit semicircle from $1$ to $-1$.
step: Piece C: the segment $z = iy$, $y$ from $1$ down to $0$, maps to $w = -y^2$, which runs from $-1$ to $0$ along the negative real axis.
step: Together the three images bound the upper half-disk. Test a point: $z = 0.5e^{i\pi/4} \in D$ maps to $0.25e^{i\pi/2} = 0.25i$, which is in the upper half-disk. So $w(D) = \{\lvert w\rvert < 1,\ \operatorname{Im}w > 0\}$.
step: Corner check: at $z = 0$ the corner has angle $\pi/2$, and since $f'(0) = 0$ the angle doubles to $\pi$, which is why the image has a straight boundary there.

@example
title: Mapping the half-plane onto the disk with $\frac{z-i}{z+i}$
problem: Show that $w = \dfrac{z - i}{z + i}$ maps the upper half-plane onto the unit disk.
step: Use $\lvert w\rvert = \dfrac{\lvert z - i\rvert}{\lvert z + i\rvert}$. This is the ratio of the distance from $z$ to $i$ over the distance from $z$ to $-i$.
step: Boundary: for real $z = x$, $\lvert x - i\rvert = \lvert x + i\rvert$, so $\lvert w\rvert = 1$. The real axis maps into the unit circle.
step: Interior: $z$ is in the upper half-plane exactly when it is closer to $i$ than to $-i$, i.e. $\lvert z - i\rvert < \lvert z + i\rvert$, i.e. $\lvert w\rvert < 1$.
step: Landmarks: $z = i \mapsto 0$, $z = 0 \mapsto -1$, and $z \to \infty \mapsto 1$. Since a Mobius map is one-to-one on the sphere, the half-plane maps onto the whole disk.

@mistakes
- Using a map at a point where $f'(z_0) = 0$ and still expecting angles to be preserved.
- Mapping only the corners of a region; map the boundary pieces (a straight edge can become an arc) and then test an interior point.
- Forgetting that $e^z$ is periodic: it is one-to-one only on a strip of height $2\pi$ (or less).
- Assuming $1/z$ maps lines to lines; a line not through $0$ maps to a circle through $0$.

hint: $(2 - i)^2 = 3 - 4i$ and $(2+i)(2-i) = 5$.
solution: $w = \dfrac{2 - i}{2 + i} = \dfrac{(2 - i)^2}{5} = \dfrac{3 - 4i}{5}$. Its modulus is $\sqrt{9 + 16}/5 = 1$, as expected for a point on the real axis.

@practice num
prompt: A Mobius transformation $w(z)$ sends $0 \mapsto 1$, $1 \mapsto 0$ and $-1 \mapsto \infty$. Find $w(i)$ in the form $x + iy$.
answer: -i
check: 1 - (2*1j)/(1j + 1)
hint: Since $w(1) = 0$ and $w(-1) = \infty$, the transformation has the form $w = k\,\dfrac{z - 1}{z + 1}$.
hint: Use $w(0) = 1$ to find $k$.
solution: $w = k\dfrac{z-1}{z+1}$ and $w(0) = -k = 1$, so $k = -1$ and $w = \dfrac{1 - z}{1 + z}$. Then $w(i) = \dfrac{1 - i}{1 + i} = \dfrac{(1-i)^2}{2} = \dfrac{-2i}{2} = -i$.

@practice self
prompt: Find the image of the half-annulus $A = \{1 < \lvert z\rvert < 2,\ 0 < \arg z < \pi\}$ under $w = \operatorname{Log}z$ (principal branch). Track each boundary piece.
hint: Use $\operatorname{Log}z = \ln\lvert z\rvert + i\arg z$ and map the four boundary pieces: two arcs and two real segments.
hint: The inner arc has $\lvert z\rvert = 1$, so $\operatorname{Re}w = 0$. What does the outer arc give? What do the segments on the positive and negative real axes give?
solution: With $w = u + iv$, $u = \ln\lvert z\rvert$ and $v = \arg z$. Inner arc $\lvert z\rvert = 1$, $0 \le \arg z \le \pi$: $u = 0$, $0 \le v \le \pi$, a vertical segment. Outer arc $\lvert z\rvert = 2$: $u = \ln 2$, $0 \le v \le \pi$, a vertical segment. Positive real segment $z = x \in [1, 2]$: $v = 0$, $u = \ln x \in [0, \ln 2]$. Negative real segment $z = -x$, $x \in [1, 2]$: $v = \pi$, $u \in [0, \ln 2]$. These four segments bound the rectangle $0 < u < \ln 2$, $0 < v < \pi$. An interior test point $z = 1.5i$ maps to $\ln 1.5 + i\pi/2$, which is inside the rectangle. The map is one-to-one and analytic with $f' = 1/z \neq 0$, so $A$ maps conformally onto this rectangle.

answer: 5/2
check: np.max(np.abs(2*np.exp(1j*np.linspace(0, 2*np.pi, 20001)) + 0.5*np.exp(-1j*np.linspace(0, 2*np.pi, 20001))))
hint: Parametrize $z = 2e^{it}$ and compute $z + 1/z$ in terms of $\cos t$ and $\sin t$.
hint: $z + \frac1z = 2e^{it} + \frac12e^{-it} = \frac52\cos t + \frac32 i\sin t$.
solution: $f(2e^{it}) = \left(2 + \tfrac12\right)\cos t + i\left(2 - \tfrac12\right)\sin t = \tfrac52\cos t + \tfrac32\,i\sin t$. This is the ellipse with semi-axes $\tfrac52$ (along the real axis) and $\tfrac32$ (along the imaginary axis). The semi-major axis is $\tfrac52$.

@quiz mc
prompt: Under $w = 1/z$, the vertical line $\operatorname{Re}z = 1$ maps to which curve?
choice*: the circle $\lvert w - \tfrac12\rvert = \tfrac12$
choice: the line $\operatorname{Re}w = 1$

choice: the line $\operatorname{Re}w = \tfrac12$
hint: Parametrize the line as $z = 1 + iy$ and write $w = 1/z = u + iv$.
hint: Show that $u^2 + v^2 = u$ for all $y$.
solution: For $z = 1 + iy$, $w = \dfrac{1 - iy}{1 + y^2}$, so $u = \dfrac{1}{1 + y^2}$ and $v = \dfrac{-y}{1 + y^2}$. Then $u^2 + v^2 = \dfrac{1}{1 + y^2} = u$, i.e. $(u - \tfrac12)^2 + v^2 = \tfrac14$. This is a circle through $0$ (a line not through $0$ becomes a circle through $0$).
