@article
Let $a$ be an isolated singularity of $f$ with Laurent series $\sum c_n(z-a)^n$ on a punctured disk. The **residue** is the coefficient of $(z-a)^{-1}$:

$$\operatorname{Res}(f, a) = c_{-1} = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\lvert z-a\rvert = \varepsilon}f(z)\,dz.$$

Why this coefficient? Integrate the series term by term over the small circle. By Week 4, each power $(z-a)^n$ with $n \neq -1$ integrates to zero, and $(z-a)^{-1}$ gives $2\pi i$.

### The residue theorem

Let $\gamma$ be a simple closed counterclockwise contour, and let $f$ be analytic on $\gamma$ and inside it except at finitely many points $a_1, \dots, a_m$ inside $\gamma$. Then

$$\oint_\gamma f(z)\,dz = 2\pi i\sum_{k=1}^{m}\operatorname{Res}(f, a_k).$$

To see why, deform $\gamma$ to small disjoint circles around the $a_k$ (Week 5); each contributes $2\pi i\operatorname{Res}(f,a_k)$. Integration becomes algebra: locate singularities, compute residues, add.

### Computing residues

- **Removable singularity:** the residue is $0$ (no negative powers).
- **Simple pole:** $\operatorname{Res}(f,a) = \lim_{z\to a}(z-a)f(z)$. If $f = g/h$ with $g(a) \neq 0$, $h(a) = 0$ and $h'(a) \neq 0$, then
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,a) = \frac{g(a)}{h'(a)}.$$
- **Pole of order $m$:**
$$\operatorname{Res}(f,a) = \frac{1}{(m-1)!}\lim_{z\to a}\frac{d^{m-1}}{dz^{m-1}}\Big[(z-a)^m f(z)\Big].$$
Using a larger $m$ than the true order still gives the right answer, only with more differentiation.
- **Essential singularity, or whenever the derivatives get messy:** expand in a Laurent series and read off $c_{-1}$. For instance $\operatorname{Res}(e^{1/z}, 0) = 1$.

### A routine for residue problems

1. Find every singularity of $f$ and classify it: removable, a pole (with its order), or essential.
2. Keep only those inside $\gamma$; ignore the others, however close to the contour they are.
3. Compute each remaining residue with the cheapest valid method.
4. Add the residues, multiply by $2\pi i$, and check the orientation of $\gamma$.

Residues also have a physical meaning. In planar ideal flow with velocity $(u, v)$, the integral $\oint (u - iv)\,dz$ around a singularity equals the circulation plus $i$ times the net outward flux, so $2\pi i$ times a residue of the complex velocity encodes a vortex and a source at once.

### A useful check

If $f(z)$ decays at least as fast as $1/z^2$ for large $\lvert z\rvert$, the residues at **all** its finite singularities add up to $0$ (enlarge the contour and apply the ML inequality). If your sum is not $0$ in such a case, a residue is wrong.

@example
title: A double pole and a simple pole
problem: Evaluate $\oint_{\lvert z\rvert = 2}\dfrac{e^z}{z^2(z-1)}\,dz$, counterclockwise.
step: The singularities are $z = 0$ (a pole of order $2$) and $z = 1$ (a simple pole). Both are inside $\lvert z\rvert = 2$.
step: At the simple pole, $\operatorname{Res}(f,1) = \lim_{z\to1}(z-1)f(z) = \dfrac{e^1}{1^2} = e$.
step: At the double pole, $\operatorname{Res}(f,0) = \dfrac{d}{dz}\left[z^2f(z)\right]_{z=0} = \dfrac{d}{dz}\left[\dfrac{e^z}{z-1}\right]_{z=0}$.
step: Compute $\dfrac{d}{dz}\dfrac{e^z}{z-1} = \dfrac{e^z(z-1) - e^z}{(z-1)^2} = \dfrac{e^z(z-2)}{(z-1)^2}$, which equals $-2$ at $z = 0$.
step: The integral is $2\pi i\,(e + (-2)) = 2\pi i\,(e - 2)$.

@example
title: Residues with $g/h'$
problem: Evaluate $\oint_{\lvert z\rvert = 4}\dfrac{dz}{\sin z}$, counterclockwise.
step: The singularities are the zeros of $\sin z$, at $z = k\pi$. Inside $\lvert z\rvert = 4$ are $z = 0, \pm\pi$, because $\pi \approx 3.14 < 4 < 2\pi$.
step: Each zero is simple, since $(\sin z)' = \cos z = \pm1 \neq 0$ there. With $g = 1$ and $h = \sin z$: $\operatorname{Res}(f,k\pi) = \dfrac{1}{\cos k\pi} = (-1)^k$.
step: The residues are $1$ at $0$, and $-1$ at each of $\pm\pi$.
step: The sum is $1 - 1 - 1 = -1$, so the integral is $-2\pi i$.

@mistakes
- Including singularities that lie outside $\gamma$, or forgetting one inside.
- Using $\lim (z-a)f(z)$ at a pole of higher order; that limit is infinite. Use the derivative formula or a series.
- Differentiating $f$ instead of $(z-a)^mf(z)$, or forgetting the factor $\frac{1}{(m-1)!}$.
- Forgetting orientation: a clockwise contour gives $-2\pi i\sum\operatorname{Res}$.
- Assuming a removable singularity has residue $f(a)$; its residue is $0$.

@practice self
prompt: Explain why the residue theorem holds: why $\oint_\gamma f\,dz = 2\pi i\sum_k\operatorname{Res}(f, a_k)$ when $f$ is analytic inside and on $\gamma$ except at isolated points $a_k$ inside.
hint: Surround each singularity by a small circle and use the deformation principle from Week 5.
hint: On each small circle use the Laurent series of $f$ and integrate it term by term.
solution: Choose disjoint small circles $C_k$ around the points $a_k$, all inside $\gamma$. The function $f$ is analytic on the region between $\gamma$ and the circles, so by Cauchy's theorem for multiply connected regions (deformation), $\oint_\gamma f\,dz = \sum_k\oint_{C_k}f\,dz$. On each $C_k$, $f$ has a Laurent series $\sum_n c_n^{(k)}(z-a_k)^n$ that converges uniformly on the circle, so we may integrate term by term. Each $\oint_{C_k}(z-a_k)^n\,dz$ is $0$ for $n \neq -1$ and $2\pi i$ for $n = -1$. Hence $\oint_{C_k}f\,dz = 2\pi i\,c_{-1}^{(k)} = 2\pi i\operatorname{Res}(f,a_k)$, and summing over $k$ gives the theorem.
