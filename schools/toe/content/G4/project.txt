@review
### Week by week

**Week 1, states and observables.** States are rays; observables are Hermitian operators with $A=\sum_a aP_a$; the Born rule is $p(a)=\langle\psi|P_a|\psi\rangle$, summed over degenerate eigenvectors; $\Delta A\,\Delta B\ge\frac12|\langle[A,B]\rangle|$.

**Week 2, dynamics.** $U=e^{-iHt/\hbar}$; $\dot A_H=\frac i\hbar[H,A_H]$; expectation values agree across pictures; the interaction picture isolates $V$.

**Week 3, symmetries.** $[H,G]=0$ gives conservation and labels; $T(a)^\dagger XT(a)=X+a$; parity selection rules; antiunitary $\Theta$ and Kramers degeneracy for $\Theta^2=-1$.

**Week 4, rotations.** $[J_i,J_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}J_k$; $R=e^{-i\phi\hat n\cdot\mathbf J/\hbar}$; $J_\pm$ coefficients $\sqrt{j(j+1)-m(m\pm1)}$; $d^j_{m'm}(\beta)$; a $2\pi$ rotation gives $-1$ for spin-1/2.

**Week 5, coupling.** Triangle rule and dimension count; Clebsch-Gordan coefficients by lowering with the Condon-Shortley sign; $\mathbf J_1\cdot\mathbf J_2=\frac12(\mathbf J^2-\mathbf J_1^2-\mathbf J_2^2)$.

**Week 6, identical particles.** Bosons symmetric, fermions antisymmetric; counts $\binom{N+d-1}{N}$ and $\binom dN$; singlet with symmetric space, triplet with antisymmetric space; exchange term $\mp2|\langle a|x|b\rangle|^2$.

**Week 7, approximations.** $E^{(1)}=V_{nn}$, $E^{(2)}=\sum_{m\ne n}|V_{mn}|^2/(E_n-E_m)$; degenerate case: diagonalize $V$ in the subspace; variational upper bound; WKB.

**Week 8, transitions.** $P=\frac{4|V_{fi}|^2}{\hbar^2\omega^2}\sin^2\frac{\omega t}2$; golden rule $\Gamma=\frac{2\pi}\hbar|V_{fi}|^2\rho$; Rabi formula; sudden and adiabatic limits.

**Week 9, scattering operators.** $d\sigma/d\Omega=|f|^2$; $T=V+VG_0^{(+)}T$; Born amplitude; optical theorem $\sigma=\frac{4\pi}k{\rm Im}f(0)$.

**Week 10, partial waves.** $\sigma=\frac{4\pi}{k^2}\sum(2l+1)\sin^2\delta_l$; unitarity bound; hard sphere $4\pi R^2$; scattering length.

**Week 11, density matrices.** Hermitian, positive, trace one; ${\rm Tr}\rho A$; purity; Bloch ball; $\dot\rho=-\frac i\hbar[H,\rho]$; $S=-{\rm Tr}\rho\ln\rho$.

**Week 12, measurement and entanglement.** Partial trace; Schmidt decomposition; $S(\rho_A)$; POVMs; CHSH bounds $2$ and $2\sqrt2$.

### Self-check list

- I can state the inner-product convention, the rotation sign, the plane-wave normalization and the logarithm base I am using.
- I can derive the Heisenberg equation, the ladder coefficients and the golden rule without notes.
- For any two-particle state I can decide the exchange symmetry and the allowed spin states.
- For any approximation I can say what it guarantees and when it fails.
- I can trace out a subsystem and compare the purity of the global and reduced states.

### Most common errors

- Forgetting to conjugate the bra coefficients, or using the wrong sign in the Heisenberg equation.
- Applying nondegenerate perturbation theory to degenerate levels.
- Dropping $(2l+1)$ or $1/k^2$ in partial-wave cross sections, or using $\mathbf k'+\mathbf k$ for the momentum transfer.
- Treating a mixture as a superposition, or tracing out the wrong factor.

prompt: Two spin-1/2 particles with $H=\frac{J}{\hbar^2}\mathbf S_1\cdot\mathbf S_2$ start in the product state $|\!\uparrow\downarrow\rangle$. At the time when $Jt/\hbar=\pi/3$, find the purity ${\rm Tr}\rho_1^2$ of the reduced density matrix of particle 1.
answer: 5/8
check: (lambda sx, sy, sz: (lambda H: (lambda w, V: (lambda psi: float(np.real(np.trace((lambda M: (M @ M.conj().T) @ (M @ M.conj().T))(psi.reshape(2, 2))))))((V @ np.diag(np.exp(-1j * w * np.pi / 3)) @ V.conj().T) @ np.array([0, 1, 0, 0])))(*np.linalg.eigh(H)))(sum(np.kron(s, s) for s in (sx, sy, sz)) / 4))(np.array([[0, 1], [1, 0]]), np.array([[0, -1j], [1j, 0]]), np.diag([1.0, -1.0]))
hint: Write $|\!\uparrow\downarrow\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|1,0\rangle+|0,0\rangle)$. The triplet has energy $J/4$ and the singlet $-3J/4$.
hint: The relative phase is $e^{-iJt/\hbar}$, so the state is, up to a global phase, $\cos\frac{Jt}{2\hbar}|\!\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-i\sin\frac{Jt}{2\hbar}|\!\downarrow\uparrow\rangle$.
hint: In this state $\rho_1$ is diagonal, because the states of particle 2 that go with $\uparrow$ and $\downarrow$ are orthogonal.
solution: With $\varphi=Jt/2\hbar=\pi/6$ the state is $\cos\varphi|\!\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-i\sin\varphi|\!\downarrow\uparrow\rangle$ up to a global phase. Tracing out particle 2 gives $\rho_1=\mathrm{diag}(\cos^2\varphi,\sin^2\varphi)=\mathrm{diag}(\frac34,\frac14)$, because the partner states $|\!\downarrow\rangle$ and $|\!\uparrow\rangle$ are orthogonal. The purity is $\frac9{16}+\frac1{16}=\frac58$. The particle is entangled with its partner at this time, even though the global state is pure.

prompt: Units $m=\hbar=1$ and $k=1$. In the first Born approximation a particle scatters from $V(r)=e^{-r}/r$. Find the fraction $\sigma_0/\sigma_{\rm tot}$ of the Born total cross section carried by the s-wave, where $f_0=\frac12\int_{-1}^{1}f(\theta)\,d\cos\theta$ is the $l=0$ partial amplitude and $\sigma_0=4\pi|f_0|^2$.
answer: 5*ln(5)^2/16
tol: 0.001
check: (lambda f: (4 * np.pi * (0.5 * quad(lambda th: f(th) * np.sin(th), 0, np.pi)) ** 2) / (2 * np.pi * quad(lambda th: f(th) ** 2 * np.sin(th), 0, np.pi)))(lambda th: -(2 / (2 * np.sin(th / 2))) * quad(lambda r: np.exp(-r) * np.sin(2 * np.sin(th / 2) * r), 0, 60))
hint: First find $f^{(1)}=-\frac{2}{1+q^2}$ with $q^2=2k^2(1-\cos\theta)$, so $f=-\frac{2}{3-2c}$ with $c=\cos\theta$.
hint: Integrate over $c$ from $-1$ to $1$ for both $f_0=\frac12\int f\,dc$ and $\sigma_{\rm tot}=2\pi\int|f|^2dc$.
hint: Use $\int\frac{dc}{3-2c}=-\frac12\ln(3-2c)$ and $\int\frac{dc}{(3-2c)^2}=\frac{1}{2(3-2c)}$.
solution: $f_0=\frac12\int_{-1}^1\frac{-2}{3-2c}dc=-\int_{-1}^1\frac{dc}{3-2c}=-\frac12\ln5$, so $\sigma_0=4\pi\cdot\frac14(\ln5)^2=\pi(\ln5)^2\approx8.14$. The fraction is $\frac{\pi(\ln5)^2}{16\pi/5}=\frac{5(\ln5)^2}{16}\approx0.809$. The s-wave cross section respects its unitarity bound $4\pi/k^2\approx12.6$.

prompt: A spin-1 particle has $H=\frac{A}{\hbar^2}S_z^2+\frac{B}{\hbar^2}\left(S_x^2-S_y^2\right)$ with $A>B>0$. (a) Find the exact energy eigenvalues and eigenstates in the basis $|m\rangle$. (b) The particle starts in $|m=+1\rangle$. Find the probability to find $m=-1$ at time $t$. (c) Name a symmetry of $H$ that explains why $|0\rangle$ decouples from $|\pm1\rangle$, and explain why time reversal does not force any degeneracy here. (d) Treating the $B$ term as a perturbation, say why nondegenerate perturbation theory fails for the levels near $A$ and what degenerate perturbation theory gives, and compare with part (a).
hint: Write $S_x^2-S_y^2=\frac12(S_+^2+S_-^2)$ and use $S_+^2|-1\rangle=2\hbar^2|+1\rangle$.
hint: In the subspace $\{|+1\rangle,|-1\rangle\}$ the Hamiltonian is $\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B&A\end{pmatrix}$, and $|0\rangle$ is an eigenstate with energy $0$.
hint: For (c) consider the rotation $e^{-i\pi S_z/\hbar}$ and the value of $\Theta^2$ for integer spin.
solution: (a) $\langle+1|S_x^2-S_y^2|-1\rangle=\hbar^2$ and $S_z^2$ is $\hbar^2\mathrm{diag}(1,0,1)$. So in $\{|+1\rangle,|-1\rangle\}$, $H=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B&A\end{pmatrix}$ with eigenvalues $A\pm B$ and eigenstates $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|{+1}\rangle\pm|{-1}\rangle)$; $|0\rangle$ is an eigenstate with energy $0$. (b) Write $|{+1}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|\phi_+\rangle+|\phi_-\rangle)$. The amplitude for $|{-1}\rangle$ is $\frac12\left(e^{-i(A+B)t/\hbar}-e^{-i(A-B)t/\hbar}\right)=-ie^{-iAt/\hbar}\sin(Bt/\hbar)$, so $P(m=-1)=\sin^2(Bt/\hbar)$. (c) $H$ commutes with the rotation $R_z(\pi)=e^{-i\pi S_z/\hbar}$, which has eigenvalue $-1$ on $|{\pm1}\rangle$ and $+1$ on $|0\rangle$, so it cannot connect them. Time reversal is a symmetry, but for integer spin $\Theta^2=+1$, so Kramers' theorem does not apply and no degeneracy is forced. (d) For $H_0=AS_z^2/\hbar^2$ the levels $|{\pm1}\rangle$ are degenerate with energy $A$, and $V_{+1,-1}=B$ is a coupling between degenerate states, so the nondegenerate formulas have a zero denominator. Degenerate theory diagonalizes $V$ in the subspace, giving $A\pm B$ and the states $\frac1{\sqrt2}(|{+1}\rangle\pm|{-1}\rangle)$, which is already exact; $|0\rangle$ is unshifted because $V|0\rangle=0$.
