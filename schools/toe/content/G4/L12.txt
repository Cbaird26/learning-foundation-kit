@article
Composite systems are where quantum states stop being describable one part at a time. This lesson combines the density matrix of the previous lesson with the tensor-product structure.

### Composite systems and the Schmidt decomposition

For systems $A$ and $B$ the joint space is $\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B$. A pure state is a **product state** if $|\psi\rangle=|a\rangle\otimes|b\rangle$; otherwise it is **entangled**. Every pure bipartite state has a **Schmidt decomposition**
$$|\psi\rangle=\sum_i\sqrt{\lambda_i}\;|a_i\rangle\otimes|b_i\rangle,\qquad\lambda_i>0,\quad\sum_i\lambda_i=1,$$
with orthonormal sets $\{|a_i\rangle\}$, $\{|b_i\rangle\}$. The state is entangled exactly when more than one $\lambda_i$ is nonzero. If $M$ is the matrix of components, $\psi_{ab}$, then $\lambda_i$ are the squared singular values of $M$.

### Reduced states

Observables of $A$ alone have the form $O_A\otimes\mathbb 1$. Their statistics are reproduced by the **reduced density matrix**
$$\rho_A={\rm Tr}_B\,\rho_{AB},\qquad \langle O_A\otimes\mathbb 1\rangle={\rm Tr}\bigl(\rho_AO_A\bigr),$$
where the partial trace sums over a basis of $B$: $\rho_A=\sum_j(\mathbb 1\otimes\langle j|)\,\rho_{AB}\,(\mathbb 1\otimes|j\rangle)$. For a pure $|\psi\rangle$ with component matrix $M$ (rows label $A$, columns label $B$), $\rho_A=MM^\dagger$ and $\rho_B=(M^\dagger M)^T$. Both reduced states have the Schmidt weights $\lambda_i$ as their nonzero eigenvalues. Hence:

- a pure bipartite state is entangled if and only if its reduced state is **mixed**;
- the **entanglement entropy** $S=-\sum_i\lambda_i\ln\lambda_i=S(\rho_A)=S(\rho_B)$ measures it, and it vanishes for product states;
- the global state can be pure while every part is mixed. The Bell singlet $\frac1{\sqrt2}(|\!\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-|\!\downarrow\uparrow\rangle)$ has $\rho_A=\rho_B=\frac12\mathbb 1$.

### Generalized measurements

Besides projective measurements, an apparatus coupled to an ancilla gives a **POVM**: operators $E_m=M_m^\dagger M_m\ge0$ with $\sum_mE_m=\mathbb 1$, probabilities $p_m={\rm Tr}(E_m\rho)$ and post-measurement state $M_m\rho M_m^\dagger/p_m$. Every POVM can be realized as a projective measurement on a larger system. Decoherence, the leakage of coherence into an unobserved environment, turns a pure superposition into an effective mixture of pointer states for the system alone; it explains why interference disappears but does not by itself say why a single outcome occurs.

### Correlations and the CHSH inequality

For spin-1/2 particles in the singlet, measure $\sigma_{\hat a}=\hat a\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ on $A$ and $\sigma_{\hat b}$ on $B$ with unit vectors $\hat a,\hat b$:
$$E(\hat a,\hat b)=\langle\sigma_{\hat a}\otimes\sigma_{\hat b}\rangle=-\hat a\cdot\hat b .$$
For coplanar axes at angles $a,b$ this is $-\cos(a-b)$. The **CHSH quantity** is
$$S=E(a,b)-E(a,b')+E(a',b)+E(a',b').$$
Any local hidden-variable model obeys $|S|\le2$. Quantum mechanics reaches $|S|=2\sqrt2$ (Tsirelson's bound) but not more. The settings $a=0$, $a'=\pi/2$, $b=\pi/4$, $b'=3\pi/4$ achieve it. These correlations cannot be used to signal: whatever $A$ measures, $\rho_B$ is unchanged, since $\rho_B=\sum_m p_m\rho_B^{(m)}$ averages over $A$'s outcomes.

@example
title: Tracing out a subsystem
problem: Let $|\psi\rangle=\cos\alpha|00\rangle+\sin\alpha|11\rangle$ with $\alpha=\pi/6$. Find $\rho_A$, compare the purity and entropy of the global and reduced states, and interpret.
step: Write $\rho_{AB}=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$ and trace over $B$. The cross terms contain $\langle0|1\rangle_B=0$, so $\rho_A=\cos^2\alpha\,|0\rangle\langle0|+\sin^2\alpha\,|1\rangle\langle1|=\mathrm{diag}\left(\tfrac34,\tfrac14\right)$.
step: Purity: the global state is pure, ${\rm Tr}\rho_{AB}^2=1$, but ${\rm Tr}\rho_A^2=\frac9{16}+\frac1{16}=\frac58<1$, so the reduced state is mixed.
step: Entropy: $S(\rho_{AB})=0$, while $S(\rho_A)=-\frac34\ln\frac34-\frac14\ln\frac14\approx0.562$ nats. By the Schmidt decomposition, the same value holds for $\rho_B$.
step: Interpretation: the joint outcomes of $\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z$ are perfectly correlated, yet $A$'s own $\sigma_z$ result is random with $\langle\sigma_z\rangle_A=\frac12$. The randomness lives in the correlations: the information is in the pair, not in the parts.

@example
title: CHSH value for the singlet
problem: For the singlet, evaluate $S=E(a,b)-E(a,b')+E(a',b)+E(a',b')$ at $a=0$, $a'=\pi/2$, $b=\pi/4$, $b'=3\pi/4$.
step: Use $E(a,b)=-\cos(a-b)$.
step: $E(a,b)=-\cos\frac\pi4=-\frac{\sqrt2}2$, $E(a,b')=-\cos\frac{3\pi}4=+\frac{\sqrt2}2$, $E(a',b)=-\cos\frac\pi4=-\frac{\sqrt2}2$, and $E(a',b')=-\cos\frac\pi4=-\frac{\sqrt2}2$.
step: Then $S=-\frac{\sqrt2}2-\frac{\sqrt2}2-\frac{\sqrt2}2-\frac{\sqrt2}2=-2\sqrt2$, so $|S|=2\sqrt2\approx2.83>2$: the local bound is violated and Tsirelson's bound is saturated.

@mistakes
- Tracing over the wrong subsystem, or treating the partial trace as a projection rather than a sum over a basis of $B$.
- Concluding that a mixed reduced state means the global state is mixed; for a pure global state it signals entanglement.
- Evaluating CHSH with the wrong sign pattern; only one of the four terms carries the minus sign.
- Interpreting perfect correlations as signalling; $\rho_B$ does not depend on $A$'s choice.

@practice num
prompt: Two qubits are in the state $\frac{1}{\sqrt3}(|00\rangle+|01\rangle+|10\rangle)$, where the first label is $A$ and the second is $B$. Qubit $B$ is measured in the basis $|\pm\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|0\rangle\pm|1\rangle)$, ignoring $A$. What is the probability of the result $|+\rangle$?
answer: 5/6
check: (lambda v, P: float(np.real(np.conj(v) @ np.kron(np.eye(2), P) @ v)))(np.array([1, 1, 1, 0]) / np.sqrt(3), 0.5 * np.array([[1, 1], [1, 1]]))
hint: The probability is $\langle\psi|\mathbb 1\otimes|+\rangle\langle+|\,|\psi\rangle$, which uses only $\rho_B$.
hint: Collect the terms by $A$: $|\psi\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt3}\bigl(|0\rangle(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)+|1\rangle|0\rangle\bigr)$ and find $\rho_B$.
solution: $\rho_B=\frac13\left[(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)(\langle0|+\langle1|)+|0\rangle\langle0|\right]=\frac13\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$. Then $\langle+|\rho_B|+\rangle=\frac12\cdot\frac13(2+1+1+1)=\frac56$.

prompt: Two qubits are in the state $\cos\frac\pi8|00\rangle+\sin\frac\pi8|11\rangle$. Find the purity ${\rm Tr}\rho_A^2$ of the reduced state of the first qubit.
answer: 3/4
check: (lambda psi: (lambda M: float(np.real(np.trace((M @ M.conj().T) @ (M @ M.conj().T)))))(psi.reshape(2, 2)))(np.array([np.cos(np.pi / 8), 0, 0, np.sin(np.pi / 8)]))
hint: Here $\rho_A=\mathrm{diag}(\cos^2\alpha,\sin^2\alpha)$ with $\alpha=\pi/8$.
hint: ${\rm Tr}\rho_A^2=\cos^4\alpha+\sin^4\alpha=1-2\cos^2\alpha\sin^2\alpha=1-\frac12\sin^22\alpha$.
solution: ${\rm Tr}\rho_A^2=1-\frac12\sin^2\frac\pi4=1-\frac12\cdot\frac12=\frac34$. The global state is pure (purity $1$), but the first qubit alone is mixed.

@practice mc
prompt: A bipartite system $AB$ is in a pure state. Which statement is correct?
choice*: The state is entangled if and only if the reduced state $\rho_A$ is mixed.
choice: The state is entangled if and only if $\rho_A$ is pure.
choice: If $\rho_A$ is mixed, then $\rho_{AB}$ must be mixed too.
choice: Entanglement requires the two subsystems to have equal dimension.
hint: Use the Schmidt decomposition: the eigenvalues of $\rho_A$ are the Schmidt weights.
solution: For a pure state the nonzero eigenvalues of $\rho_A$ are the Schmidt weights $\lambda_i$. A single nonzero weight means a product state and a pure $\rho_A$; more than one means entanglement and a mixed $\rho_A$. The global state remains pure whatever $\rho_A$ is, and no equal-dimension condition is needed.

@practice num
prompt: Two spin-1/2 particles are in the singlet state. Spin $A$ is measured along $\hat a$ at angle $a$ and spin $B$ along $\hat b$ at angle $b$ (both in the $x$-$z$ plane, measured from $z$), with $E(a,b)=\langle\sigma_a\otimes\sigma_b\rangle$. Find $|S|$ where $S=E(a,b)-E(a,b')+E(a',b)+E(a',b')$ for $a=0$, $a'=\pi/2$, $b=\pi/4$, $b'=3\pi/4$.
answer: 2*sqrt(2)
check: (lambda psi, sx, sz: (lambda E: abs(E(0, np.pi / 4) - E(0, 3 * np.pi / 4) + E(np.pi / 2, np.pi / 4) + E(np.pi / 2, 3 * np.pi / 4)))(lambda a, b: float(np.real(np.conj(psi) @ np.kron(np.cos(a) * sz + np.sin(a) * sx, np.cos(b) * sz + np.sin(b) * sx) @ psi))))(np.array([0, 1, -1, 0]) / np.sqrt(2), np.array([[0, 1], [1, 0]]), np.diag([1.0, -1.0]))
hint: For the singlet, $E(a,b)=-\cos(a-b)$.
hint: Three of the four values of $E$ equal $-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$; the value $E(a,b')=+\frac{\sqrt2}2$ is positive, but it enters $S$ with a minus sign.
solution: $E(a,b)=-\frac{\sqrt2}2$, $E(a,b')=+\frac{\sqrt2}2$, $E(a',b)=-\frac{\sqrt2}2$, $E(a',b')=-\frac{\sqrt2}2$. So $S=-\frac{\sqrt2}2-\frac{\sqrt2}2-\frac{\sqrt2}2-\frac{\sqrt2}2=-2\sqrt2$ and $|S|=2\sqrt2$.

answer: (3-sqrt(5))/6, (3+sqrt(5))/6
check: list(np.linalg.eigvalsh((lambda M: M @ M.conj().T)(np.array([[1, 1], [1, 0]]) / np.sqrt(3))))

@quiz num
prompt: Two qubits are in the pure state $|\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}|00\rangle+\frac12|10\rangle+\frac12|11\rangle$ (first label $A$, second $B$). Find the entanglement entropy $S(\rho_A)=-{\rm Tr}\rho_A\ln\rho_A$ in nats.
answer: 0.4165
tol: 0.001

@quiz ms
prompt: Select all correct statements.
choice*: For the singlet state, each qubit's reduced density matrix is $\frac12\mathbb 1$.
choice*: Local hidden-variable models obey $|S_{\rm CHSH}|\le2$.
choice*: Quantum mechanics allows $|S_{\rm CHSH}|$ up to $2\sqrt2$ but no larger.
choice: By choosing her measurement setting, Alice can change Bob's reduced density matrix and so send him a signal.
hint: Bob's reduced state is an average over Alice's outcomes, so it does not depend on her setting.
solution: The statements about the singlet's reduced states and the two CHSH bounds are standard (the singlet is maximally entangled; the CHSH bounds are $2$ for local models and $2\sqrt2$ for quantum theory). The statement that Alice can signal by her choice of setting is false: $\rho_B=\sum_mp_m\rho_B^{(m)}$ is the same for every setting of $A$ (no-signalling), so correlations cannot carry a message.
