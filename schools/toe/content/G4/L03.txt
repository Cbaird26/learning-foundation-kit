@article
A **symmetry** is a transformation of states that leaves every transition probability unchanged, $|\langle\phi'|\psi'\rangle|^2=|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|^2$. **Wigner's theorem** says that such a transformation can be implemented on rays by an operator that is either **unitary** or **antiunitary**. Almost everything in this lesson follows from that statement together with one question: does the symmetry operator commute with the Hamiltonian?

### Symmetry and conservation

A continuous symmetry is generated by a Hermitian operator $G$: $U(\epsilon)=e^{-i\epsilon G/\hbar}$, where $\epsilon$ is the transformation parameter. Such a $U$ is a symmetry of the dynamics when $U^\dagger HU=H$, which for every $\epsilon$ is equivalent to
$$[H,G]=0 .$$
Then the Heisenberg equation $\dot G_H=\frac i\hbar[H,G_H]=0$ shows that $G$ is conserved, and $H$ and $G$ have a common eigenbasis. Energy eigenstates can be labelled by the eigenvalue of $G$. If a level is **nondegenerate**, its eigenstate must also be an eigenstate of $G$. When the symmetry operators do not commute with each other, the eigenspaces of $H$ carry representations of the symmetry group and degeneracies follow.

### Translations

The translation operator $T(a)=e^{-iaP/\hbar}$ moves states by $a$: $T(a)|x\rangle=|x+a\rangle$ and $(T(a)\psi)(x)=\psi(x-a)$. Then
$$T(a)^\dagger X\,T(a)=X+a .$$
A Hamiltonian invariant under all translations commutes with $P$, so momentum is conserved. If only discrete translations by a lattice spacing are symmetries, the conserved quantity is the eigenvalue of that single $T$, a phase $e^{iqa}$.

### Parity

Parity is a discrete symmetry with no generator: $\Pi|x\rangle=|-x\rangle$, $(\Pi\psi)(x)=\psi(-x)$. It satisfies $\Pi=\Pi^\dagger=\Pi^{-1}$ and $\Pi^2=1$, so its eigenvalues are $\pm1$, and $\Pi X\Pi=-X$, $\Pi P\Pi=-P$. Any wavefunction splits as $\psi=\psi_++\psi_-$ with $\psi_\pm(x)=\frac12[\psi(x)\pm\psi(-x)]$. If $[H,\Pi]=0$, eigenstates of a nondegenerate level have definite parity. **Selection rule:** if $\Pi O\Pi=-O$ (an odd operator, such as $X$) and $|a\rangle,|b\rangle$ have parities $\pi_a,\pi_b$, then $\langle a|O|b\rangle=\pi_a\pi_b\,(-1)\langle a|O|b\rangle$, so the matrix element vanishes unless $\pi_a\pi_b=-1$.

### Time reversal

Time reversal $\Theta$ is **antiunitary**: $\Theta(c_1|\phi\rangle+c_2|\psi\rangle)=c_1^*\Theta|\phi\rangle+c_2^*\Theta|\psi\rangle$ and $\langle\Theta\phi|\Theta\psi\rangle=\langle\phi|\psi\rangle^*$. It reverses momentum and spin. For a spin-1/2 in the $\sigma_z$ basis, $\Theta=-i\sigma_yK$ with $K$ complex conjugation, and
$$\Theta^2=-1 \quad\text{(spin-1/2)},\qquad \Theta^2=+1\quad\text{(spinless particle)} .$$
**Kramers degeneracy:** if $[H,\Theta]=0$ and $\Theta^2=-1$, every energy level is at least twofold degenerate. If $\Theta|\psi\rangle=\lambda|\psi\rangle$, then $\Theta^2|\psi\rangle=\Theta(\lambda|\psi\rangle)=\lambda^*\lambda|\psi\rangle=|\lambda|^2|\psi\rangle$, whose coefficient is positive, contradicting $-1$. So $\Theta|\psi\rangle$ is a different state of the same energy, and it is orthogonal to $|\psi\rangle$.

A symmetry that is only approximate yields only approximate conservation; a magnetic field breaks time reversal, and an electric field breaks parity.

@example
title: Translation symmetry on a three-site ring
problem: Three sites $|0\rangle,|1\rangle,|2\rangle$ form a ring and $T|j\rangle=|j+1\rangle$ (indices mod $3$). The Hamiltonian is $H=-J(T+T^\dagger)$ with $J>0$. Find the energies and their degeneracies using the symmetry.
step: $T$ is unitary and $T^3=1$, so its eigenvalues are cube roots of unity, $e^{iq}$ with $q=2\pi k/3$ and $k=0,1,2$.
step: The eigenvectors are $|k\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt3}\sum_je^{-iqj}|j\rangle$. Indeed $T|k\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt3}\sum_je^{-iqj}|j+1\rangle=e^{iq}|k\rangle$.
step: $H$ is a function of $T$, so $[H,T]=0$ and each $|k\rangle$ is an eigenstate: $H|k\rangle=-J(e^{iq}+e^{-iq})|k\rangle=-2J\cos q\,|k\rangle$.
step: Energies: $k=0$ gives $-2J$; $k=1,2$ both give $-2J\cos\frac{2\pi}{3}=+J$. The states $k=1,2$ are exchanged by the reflection $j\to-j$, which commutes with $H$ but not with $T$, so the twofold degeneracy is forced by symmetry.

@mistakes
- Treating every symmetry as unitary; time reversal is antiunitary, so it conjugates complex coefficients.
- Applying the parity selection rule with the wrong operator parity: $X$ and $P$ are odd under parity.
- Assuming a nondegenerate level is unaffected by a symmetry; its state must be an eigenstate of the symmetry operator.
- Treating a symmetry as exact when an applied field breaks it.

@practice self
prompt: Prove Kramers degeneracy. Suppose $\Theta$ is antiunitary, $\Theta^2=-1$ and $[H,\Theta]=0$. Show that for any energy eigenstate $|\psi\rangle$, the state $\Theta|\psi\rangle$ has the same energy and is orthogonal to $|\psi\rangle$.
hint: For the energy, use $H\Theta|\psi\rangle=\Theta H|\psi\rangle$ and note that $E$ is real.
hint: For orthogonality, use the antiunitary property in the form $\langle\Theta a|\Theta b\rangle=\langle b|a\rangle$ with $a=\psi$ and $b=\Theta\psi$.
solution: Energy: $H\Theta|\psi\rangle=\Theta H|\psi\rangle=\Theta E|\psi\rangle=E^*\Theta|\psi\rangle=E\,\Theta|\psi\rangle$, so $\Theta|\psi\rangle$ has energy $E$. Orthogonality: take $a=\psi$ and $b=\Theta\psi$ in $\langle\Theta a|\Theta b\rangle=\langle b|a\rangle$. The left side is $\langle\Theta\psi|\Theta^2\psi\rangle=-\langle\Theta\psi|\psi\rangle$ because $\Theta^2=-1$. The right side is $\langle\Theta\psi|\psi\rangle$. Hence $\langle\Theta\psi|\psi\rangle=-\langle\Theta\psi|\psi\rangle=0$. The two states are orthogonal, with the same energy, so the level is at least twofold degenerate.
rubric: Shows the energy of $\Theta|\psi\rangle$ equals $E$, noting that $E$ is real and $\Theta$ conjugates the number.
rubric: Uses the antiunitary inner-product property correctly, with a complex conjugate.
rubric: Uses $\Theta^2=-1$ to obtain $\langle\psi|\Theta\psi\rangle=-\langle\psi|\Theta\psi\rangle$.
rubric: Concludes that the states are orthogonal, hence distinct, so the level is at least twofold degenerate.

@quiz num
prompt: A particle in an infinite square well of width $L$ has eigenstates $\psi_n$ with alternating parity about the centre of the well. The parity selection rule forbids $\langle3|x|1\rangle$. Find the magnitude of the allowed element $|\langle2|x|1\rangle|$ in units of $L$.
answer: 16/(9*pi^2)
check: abs(quad(lambda x: 2 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * x) * x * np.sin(np.pi * x), 0, 1))
hint: Use $\psi_n(x)=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L)$ on $[0,L]$ and set $L=1$. A shift of origin changes $\langle m|x|n\rangle$ only for $m=n$.
hint: Integrate $2x\sin(2\pi x)\sin(\pi x)$ from $0$ to $1$, using a product-to-sum identity.
solution: $2\sin(2\pi x)\sin(\pi x)=\cos(\pi x)-\cos(3\pi x)$, and $\int_0^1x\cos(n\pi x)dx=\frac{(-1)^n-1}{n^2\pi^2}$. So the integral is $-\frac{2}{\pi^2}+\frac{2}{9\pi^2}=-\frac{16}{9\pi^2}$, and the magnitude is $\frac{16}{9\pi^2}\approx0.180$.
