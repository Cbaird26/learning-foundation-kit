@article
Dynamics answers one question: how does a system prepared at time $t_0$ look at later times? The answer must preserve probabilities, so it must be a **unitary** map. We state the conventions and then compute the same quantity in two ways.

### The Schrödinger picture

States evolve and operators of the observables are fixed (unless they carry explicit time dependence). With a Hermitian Hamiltonian $H$,
$$i\hbar\,\frac{d}{dt}|\psi(t)\rangle=H|\psi(t)\rangle .$$
If $H$ does not depend on time, the solution is $|\psi(t)\rangle=U(t)|\psi(0)\rangle$ with
$$U(t)=e^{-iHt/\hbar},\qquad U^\dagger U=1 .$$
In the eigenbasis $H|E_n\rangle=E_n|E_n\rangle$ this means: if $|\psi(0)\rangle=\sum_nc_n|E_n\rangle$, then $|\psi(t)\rangle=\sum_nc_ne^{-iE_nt/\hbar}|E_n\rangle$. Each component only acquires a phase, so energy probabilities $|c_n|^2$ are constant, but relative phases move and make other observables change. A state with a single energy is **stationary**: all expectation values are time independent. If $H$ depends on time, $U$ is a time-ordered exponential and the formula above is wrong.

### The Heisenberg picture

Freeze the states at their $t=0$ values and move the time dependence into the operators:
$$A_H(t)=U^\dagger(t)\,A_S\,U(t).$$
Differentiating (for time-independent $H$ and $A_S$) gives the **Heisenberg equation of motion**
$$\frac{dA_H}{dt}=\frac{i}{\hbar}\,[H,A_H].$$
The sign is $+i/\hbar$ with the commutator in the order $[H,A]$. Expectation values are identical in the two pictures,
$$\langle\psi(t)|A_S|\psi(t)\rangle=\langle\psi(0)|A_H(t)|\psi(0)\rangle ,$$
because both equal $\langle\psi(0)|U^\dagger A_SU|\psi(0)\rangle$. Taking the expectation value of the equation of motion gives Ehrenfest's relation $\frac{d}{dt}\langle A\rangle=\frac i\hbar\langle[H,A]\rangle$. An observable that commutes with $H$ is conserved.

### Two exactly solvable cases

For a spin-1/2 with $H=\omega S_z$ and $[S_x,S_y]=i\hbar S_z$ (cyclic), the Heisenberg equations are $\dot S_x=-\omega S_y$ and $\dot S_y=\omega S_x$, so
$$S_x(t)=S_x\cos\omega t-S_y\sin\omega t,\qquad S_y(t)=S_y\cos\omega t+S_x\sin\omega t,\qquad S_z(t)=S_z.$$
For the harmonic oscillator $H=\frac{p^2}{2m}+\frac12m\omega^2x^2$ with $[x,p]=i\hbar$,
$$x_H(t)=x\cos\omega t+\frac{p}{m\omega}\sin\omega t,\qquad p_H(t)=p\cos\omega t-m\omega x\sin\omega t .$$
Because these are linear, the expectation values follow the classical trajectory exactly. Equal-time commutators such as $[x_H(t),p_H(t)]=i\hbar$ do not change, since $U$ is unitary.

### The interaction picture

For $H=H_0+V$ define $|\psi_I(t)\rangle=e^{iH_0t/\hbar}|\psi_S(t)\rangle$ and $V_I(t)=e^{iH_0t/\hbar}Ve^{-iH_0t/\hbar}$. Then
$$i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}|\psi_I\rangle=V_I(t)|\psi_I\rangle ,$$
so the states change only because of $V$, while operators carry the trivial $H_0$ evolution. This is the natural setting for perturbation theory in a later lesson.

| picture | states | operators |
|---|---|---|
| Schrödinger | evolve with $H$ | fixed |
| Heisenberg | fixed | evolve with $H$ |
| interaction | evolve with $V_I$ | evolve with $H_0$ |

@example
title: One precession, two pictures
problem: A spin-1/2 with $H=\omega S_z$ is prepared in $|+x\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|\uparrow\rangle+|\downarrow\rangle)$. Find $\langle S_y\rangle(t)$ in the Schrödinger picture and in the Heisenberg picture, and compare.
step: Schrödinger picture. The eigenvalues of $H$ are $\pm\hbar\omega/2$ for $|\uparrow\rangle,|\downarrow\rangle$, so $|\psi(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigl(e^{-i\omega t/2}|\uparrow\rangle+e^{+i\omega t/2}|\downarrow\rangle\bigr)$.
step: For components $(c_\uparrow,c_\downarrow)$ one finds $\langle S_y\rangle=\hbar\,\mathrm{Im}(c_\uparrow^*c_\downarrow)$. Here $c_\uparrow^*c_\downarrow=\frac12e^{i\omega t}$, so $\langle S_y\rangle=\frac\hbar2\sin\omega t$.
step: Heisenberg picture. Use $[S_z,S_x]=i\hbar S_y$ and $[S_z,S_y]=-i\hbar S_x$. Then $\dot S_x=\frac{i\omega}{\hbar}[S_z,S_x]=-\omega S_y$ and $\dot S_y=\frac{i\omega}{\hbar}[S_z,S_y]=\omega S_x$.
step: Differentiating once more gives $\ddot S_y=-\omega^2S_y$, with $S_y(0)=S_y$ and $\dot S_y(0)=\omega S_x$. Hence $S_y(t)=S_y\cos\omega t+S_x\sin\omega t$.
step: In $|+x\rangle$ we have $\langle S_y\rangle=0$ and $\langle S_x\rangle=\hbar/2$, so $\langle S_y\rangle(t)=\frac\hbar2\sin\omega t$, identical to the Schrödinger result.

@mistakes
- Writing $A_H(t)=U\,A\,U^\dagger$ with the factors reversed; the correct order is $U^\dagger A U$.
- Using the wrong sign or order in $\dot A_H=\frac i\hbar[H,A_H]$; the Schrödinger equation has $-i/\hbar$, the Heisenberg equation has $+i/\hbar$.
- Evolving both the state and the operator, which counts the evolution twice.
- Applying $e^{-iHt/\hbar}$ to a time-dependent Hamiltonian without time ordering.

@practice num
prompt: A two-level system has $H=\frac{\hbar\Omega}{2}\sigma_x$ and starts in $|\uparrow\rangle$. What is the probability to find it in $|\downarrow\rangle$ at time $t=2\pi/(3\Omega)$?
answer: 3/4
check: (lambda w, V: abs((V @ np.diag(np.exp(-1j * w * 2 * np.pi / 3)) @ V.conj().T)[1, 0]) ** 2)(*np.linalg.eigh(0.5 * np.array([[0, 1], [1, 0]])))
hint: Work in units with $\hbar=\Omega=1$. The propagator is $U=\cos(t/2)-i\sin(t/2)\sigma_x$.
hint: The amplitude to reach $|\downarrow\rangle$ is $-i\sin(t/2)$.
solution: $U=\cos\frac{\Omega t}{2}-i\sigma_x\sin\frac{\Omega t}{2}$, so the amplitude for $|\downarrow\rangle$ is $-i\sin\frac{\Omega t}{2}$ and $p=\sin^2\frac{\Omega t}2=\sin^2\frac\pi3=\frac34$.

@practice mc
prompt: Which equation is the correct Heisenberg equation of motion for an operator $A_H(t)=U^\dagger A_SU$ when $H$ and $A_S$ do not depend on time?
choice*: $\dfrac{dA_H}{dt}=\dfrac{i}{\hbar}[H,A_H]$
choice: $\dfrac{dA_H}{dt}=-\dfrac{i}{\hbar}[H,A_H]$
choice: $\dfrac{dA_H}{dt}=\dfrac{i}{\hbar}[A_H,H]$
choice: $\dfrac{dA_H}{dt}=\dfrac{1}{i\hbar}\,H A_H$
hint: Differentiate $U^\dagger A_SU$ using $\dot U=-\frac{i}{\hbar}HU$ and $\dot U^\dagger=\frac i\hbar U^\dagger H$.
solution: $\dot A_H=\dot U^\dagger A_SU+U^\dagger A_S\dot U=\frac i\hbar U^\dagger(HA_S-A_SH)U=\frac i\hbar[H,A_H]$, using $[H,U]=0$. The other forms either flip the sign or are not commutators.

@practice num
prompt: A particle of mass $m=1$ in the potential $\frac12m\omega^2x^2$ with $\omega=2$ (units $\hbar=1$) has $\langle x\rangle=1$ and $\langle p\rangle=4$ at $t=0$. Find $\langle x\rangle$ at $t=\pi/8$.
answer: 3sqrt(2)/2
check: (lambda M, y0, t: float((lambda w, V: (V @ np.diag(np.exp(w * t)) @ np.linalg.inv(V) @ y0)[0])(*np.linalg.eig(M)).real))(np.array([[0.0, 1.0], [-4.0, 0.0]]), np.array([1.0, 4.0]), np.pi / 8)
hint: For a quadratic Hamiltonian, Ehrenfest's relations are exact: $\frac{d}{dt}\langle x\rangle=\langle p\rangle/m$ and $\frac{d}{dt}\langle p\rangle=-m\omega^2\langle x\rangle$.
hint: Use $\langle x\rangle(t)=\langle x\rangle_0\cos\omega t+\frac{\langle p\rangle_0}{m\omega}\sin\omega t$ and note $\omega t=\pi/4$.
solution: $\langle x\rangle(t)=\cos(2t)+\frac{4}{2}\sin(2t)$. At $t=\pi/8$, $2t=\pi/4$, so $\langle x\rangle=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}+2\cdot\frac{\sqrt2}{2}=\frac{3\sqrt2}{2}$.

lead: $\langle S_x\rangle/\hbar =$
answer: -sin(omega*t)/2

@practice self
prompt: A spin-1/2 has $H=\omega S_y$ and starts in $|\uparrow\rangle$. Derive $\langle S_z\rangle(t)$ twice: once in the Schrödinger picture by computing $|\psi(t)\rangle$, and once in the Heisenberg picture from commutators. Show that the results agree.
hint: In the Schrödinger picture use $e^{-i\phi\sigma_y/2}=\cos\frac\phi2-i\sigma_y\sin\frac\phi2$ with $\phi=\omega t$.
hint: In the Heisenberg picture you need $[S_y,S_z]=i\hbar S_x$ and $[S_y,S_x]=-i\hbar S_z$.
solution: Schrödinger: $|\psi(t)\rangle=\bigl(\cos\frac{\omega t}{2}-i\sigma_y\sin\frac{\omega t}{2}\bigr)|\uparrow\rangle=\cos\frac{\omega t}2|\uparrow\rangle+\sin\frac{\omega t}2|\downarrow\rangle$, because $\sigma_y|\uparrow\rangle=i|\downarrow\rangle$. Then $\langle S_z\rangle=\frac\hbar2\bigl(\cos^2\frac{\omega t}2-\sin^2\frac{\omega t}2\bigr)=\frac\hbar2\cos\omega t$. Heisenberg: $\dot S_z=\frac{i\omega}\hbar[S_y,S_z]=-\omega S_x$ and $\dot S_x=\frac{i\omega}\hbar[S_y,S_x]=\omega S_z$, so $\ddot S_z=-\omega^2S_z$ and $S_z(t)=S_z\cos\omega t-S_x\sin\omega t$. In $|\uparrow\rangle$, $\langle S_z\rangle=\hbar/2$ and $\langle S_x\rangle=0$, so $\langle S_z\rangle(t)=\frac\hbar2\cos\omega t$. The two pictures agree.

@quiz num
prompt: A system has two energy levels $E_1=0$ and $E_2=2\hbar$ (so the Bohr frequency is $2$) and starts in $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|1\rangle+|2\rangle)$. The observable $X=|1\rangle\langle2|+|2\rangle\langle1|$ is measured at $t=\pi/6$. What is $\langle X\rangle$?
answer: 1/2
check: (lambda t: (lambda U, v: float(np.real(np.conj(U @ v) @ np.array([[0, 1], [1, 0]]) @ (U @ v))))(np.diag(np.exp(-1j * np.array([0.0, 2.0]) * t)), np.array([1, 1]) / np.sqrt(2)))(np.pi / 6)
hint: Each energy component only gets a phase $e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}$.
hint: $\langle X\rangle=2\,\mathrm{Re}(c_1^*c_2)$, which depends only on the relative phase $(E_2-E_1)t/\hbar$.
solution: $c_1^*c_2=\frac12e^{-i(E_2-E_1)t/\hbar}=\frac12e^{-2it}$, so $\langle X\rangle=\cos 2t=\cos\frac\pi3=\frac12$.

@quiz ms
prompt: Select all statements that are true for the Heisenberg picture with a time-independent Hamiltonian.
choice*: Operators carry the time dependence, $A_H(t)=U^\dagger A_SU$.
choice*: Expectation values of any observable agree with those of the Schrödinger picture.
choice*: The equal-time commutator $[x_H(t),p_H(t)]$ equals $i\hbar$ at all times.
choice: The state vector satisfies $i\hbar\,\partial_t|\psi_H\rangle=H|\psi_H\rangle$.
hint: Unitary conjugation preserves algebraic relations such as commutators.
hint: In the Heisenberg picture the state is the $t=0$ state and does not move.
solution: The statements that operators carry the time dependence, that expectation values agree with the Schrödinger picture, and that $[x_H(t),p_H(t)]=i\hbar$ all follow from $A_H=U^\dagger A_SU$ and $U^\dagger U=1$: conjugation preserves products, hence commutators, and $\langle\psi(0)|U^\dagger A_SU|\psi(0)\rangle$ is the Schrödinger expectation value. The statement that the state vector obeys the Schrödinger equation is false: the Heisenberg state is constant.
