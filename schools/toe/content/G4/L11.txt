@article
A state vector describes a system we have prepared perfectly. Real preparations are noisy, and a subsystem of an entangled pair has no state vector of its own. The **density matrix** handles both.

### Definition and properties

If a system is prepared in the normalized state $|\psi_i\rangle$ with probability $p_i$ (the $|\psi_i\rangle$ need not be orthogonal),
$$\rho=\sum_ip_i\,|\psi_i\rangle\langle\psi_i| .$$
Any such $\rho$ satisfies: (1) $\rho=\rho^\dagger$; (2) $\rho\ge0$, meaning all eigenvalues are nonnegative; (3) ${\rm Tr}\,\rho=1$. Conversely any matrix with these properties is a density matrix. A state is **pure** when $\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$, equivalently $\rho^2=\rho$, equivalently ${\rm Tr}\rho^2=1$; otherwise it is **mixed**, and $\frac1d\le{\rm Tr}\rho^2<1$ in dimension $d$. The **purity** ${\rm Tr}\rho^2$ is $\sum_j\lambda_j^2$ in terms of the eigenvalues $\lambda_j$ of $\rho$.

### Statistics

Probabilities and expectation values need only $\rho$:
$$\langle A\rangle={\rm Tr}(\rho A),\qquad p(a)={\rm Tr}(P_a\rho),\qquad \rho\to\frac{P_a\rho P_a}{p(a)}\ \text{after result }a.$$
Consequently, **different ensembles can give the same $\rho$**, and then no measurement can tell them apart. For example $\frac12\bigl(|\!\uparrow\rangle\langle\uparrow|+|\!\downarrow\rangle\langle\downarrow|\bigr)=\frac12\bigl(|{+x}\rangle\langle{+x}|+|{-x}\rangle\langle{-x}|\bigr)=\frac12\mathbb 1$. A mixture is *not* a superposition: the superposition $|{+x}\rangle$ has off-diagonal elements (coherences) in the $\sigma_z$ basis; the mixture $\frac12\mathbb 1$ has none.

### The qubit and the Bloch ball

Every qubit density matrix is
$$\rho=\tfrac12\bigl(\mathbb 1+\mathbf r\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\bigr),\qquad \mathbf r={\rm Tr}(\rho\,\boldsymbol\sigma),\quad|\mathbf r|\le1 .$$
Pure states lie on the surface $|\mathbf r|=1$ and $\mathbb 1/2$ is at the centre. The eigenvalues are $\frac12(1\pm|\mathbf r|)$, and ${\rm Tr}\rho^2=\frac12(1+|\mathbf r|^2)$.

### Dynamics and thermal states

For a unitary evolution $\rho(t)=U\rho(0)U^\dagger$, which is equivalent to the **von Neumann equation**
$$i\hbar\,\dot\rho=[H,\rho],\qquad\text{i.e.}\quad\dot\rho=-\frac i\hbar[H,\rho].$$
The sign is opposite to the Heisenberg equation for operators. For a qubit with $H=\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}\sigma_z$ the Bloch vector precesses about $z$ at angular frequency $\omega$, and its length does not change: purity is conserved by unitary dynamics. A system in equilibrium at inverse temperature $\beta=1/k_BT$ has the thermal state
$$\rho_\beta=\frac{e^{-\beta H}}{Z},\qquad Z={\rm Tr}\,e^{-\beta H},$$
which commutes with $H$ and is therefore stationary.

### Entropy

The von Neumann entropy is
$$S(\rho)=-{\rm Tr}\,\rho\ln\rho=-\sum_j\lambda_j\ln\lambda_j ,$$
in nats (natural logarithm; use $\log_2$ for bits). It is zero exactly for pure states and equals $\ln d$ for the maximally mixed state $\mathbb 1/d$. Unitary evolution leaves it unchanged. In the next lesson we use the density matrix to describe a subsystem of an entangled state: the **reduced density matrix** $\rho_A={\rm Tr}_B\rho_{AB}$.

@example
title: A mixture of non-orthogonal states
problem: A qubit is prepared in $|\!\uparrow\rangle$ with probability $\frac12$ and in $|{+x}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|\!\uparrow\rangle+|\!\downarrow\rangle)$ with probability $\frac12$. Find $\rho$, its Bloch vector, its purity and its entropy.
step: $\rho=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}+\frac12\cdot\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}3/4&1/4\\1/4&1/4\end{pmatrix}$. It is Hermitian with trace $1$, and $\det\rho=\frac3{16}-\frac1{16}=\frac18>0$, so it is positive.
step: Bloch vector: $r_x=2\rho_{12}=\frac12$, $r_y=0$, $r_z=\rho_{11}-\rho_{22}=\frac12$. Then $|\mathbf r|=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}$, which is inside the ball, so the state is mixed.
step: Purity: ${\rm Tr}\rho^2=\frac12(1+|\mathbf r|^2)=\frac34$. Equivalently the eigenvalues are $\frac12(1\pm\frac1{\sqrt2})=0.854,\ 0.146$, and $0.854^2+0.146^2=0.75$.
step: Entropy: $S=-0.854\ln0.854-0.146\ln0.146\approx0.1352+0.2813=0.4165$ nats, well below the maximum $\ln2=0.693$.

@example
title: A thermal qubit
problem: A qubit with $H=\frac\varepsilon2\sigma_z$ is in equilibrium at a temperature where $e^{\beta\varepsilon}=2$. Find $\rho$, $\langle\sigma_z\rangle$ and $S$.
step: The levels are $\pm\varepsilon/2$, so $Z=e^{-\beta\varepsilon/2}+e^{\beta\varepsilon/2}$ and the populations are $p_\uparrow=\frac{e^{-\beta\varepsilon/2}}{Z}=\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta\varepsilon}}=\frac13$ for the upper level ($\sigma_z=+1$) and $p_\downarrow=\frac23$.
step: $\rho=\mathrm{diag}(\frac13,\frac23)$ in the $\sigma_z$ basis; it has no coherences because it commutes with $H$.
step: $\langle\sigma_z\rangle={\rm Tr}(\rho\sigma_z)=\frac13-\frac23=-\frac13$.
step: $S=-\frac13\ln\frac13-\frac23\ln\frac23=\ln3-\frac23\ln2\approx0.6365$ nats.

@mistakes
- Treating a mixture as a superposition: $\frac12\mathbb 1$ has no coherences, while $|{+x}\rangle\langle{+x}|$ does.
- Assuming a density matrix determines a unique ensemble; it does not.
- Writing the von Neumann equation as $\dot\rho=+\frac i\hbar[H,\rho]$.
- Mixing logarithm bases between entropy values, or using ${\rm Tr}\rho^2=1$ as a normalization condition (it is ${\rm Tr}\rho=1$).

answer: 0.58

solution: ${\rm Tr}\rho^2=0.7^2+0.3^2=0.49+0.09=0.58$.

@practice num
prompt: A qubit with $H=\frac\varepsilon2\sigma_z$ is in thermal equilibrium at a temperature with $e^{\beta\varepsilon}=3$. What is $\langle\sigma_z\rangle$?
answer: -1/2
check: (lambda b: (lambda p: (p[0] - p[1]) / (p[0] + p[1]))(np.exp(-b * np.array([0.5, -0.5]))))(np.log(3))
hint: The upper level ($\sigma_z=+1$, energy $+\varepsilon/2$) has population $\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta\varepsilon}}$.
solution: $p_\uparrow=\frac{1}{1+3}=\frac14$ and $p_\downarrow=\frac34$, so $\langle\sigma_z\rangle=\frac14-\frac34=-\frac12$.

@practice self
prompt: Show that the ensemble $\{|\!\uparrow\rangle,|\!\downarrow\rangle\}$ with probabilities $\frac12,\frac12$ and the ensemble $\{|{+x}\rangle,|{-x}\rangle\}$ with probabilities $\frac12,\frac12$ have the same density matrix. Explain why no measurement distinguishes them, and why this differs from preparing the pure state $|{+x}\rangle$.
hint: Write $|{\pm x}\rangle\langle{\pm x}|=\frac12(\mathbb 1\pm\sigma_x)$.
hint: All measurement statistics are functions of $\rho$ alone: $p(a)={\rm Tr}(P_a\rho)$.
solution: The first ensemble gives $\rho=\frac12(|\!\uparrow\rangle\langle\uparrow|+|\!\downarrow\rangle\langle\downarrow|)=\frac12\mathbb 1$. The second gives $\rho=\frac12\cdot\frac12(\mathbb 1+\sigma_x)+\frac12\cdot\frac12(\mathbb 1-\sigma_x)=\frac12\mathbb 1$. Since every outcome probability and expectation value is ${\rm Tr}(P\rho)$ or ${\rm Tr}(\rho A)$, a function of $\rho$ alone, the two preparations are statistically identical. The pure state $|{+x}\rangle$ has $\rho=\frac12(\mathbb 1+\sigma_x)$, which differs by the coherence term: a $\sigma_x$ measurement gives $+1$ with certainty for it and $\pm1$ with equal probability for $\frac12\mathbb 1$.
rubric: Computes both ensembles and shows they equal $\frac12\mathbb 1$.
rubric: States that measurement statistics depend on the ensemble only through $\rho$.
rubric: Distinguishes the mixture from the superposition using the $\sigma_x$ expectation value or the coherence term.

@practice num
prompt: A qubit is prepared in $|0\rangle$ with probability $\frac34$ and in $|{+}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)$ with probability $\frac14$. Compute the von Neumann entropy $S=-{\rm Tr}\rho\ln\rho$ in nats.
answer: 0.3353
tol: 0.001
check: (lambda w: -sum(x * np.log(x) for x in w if x > 1e-12))(np.linalg.eigvalsh(0.75 * np.outer([1, 0], [1, 0]) + 0.25 * np.outer([1, 1], [1, 1]) / 2))
hint: Build $\rho=\begin{pmatrix}7/8&1/8\\1/8&1/8\end{pmatrix}$ and find its eigenvalues from ${\rm Tr}\rho=1$ and $\det\rho=\frac{3}{32}$.
hint: The eigenvalues are $\frac12\bigl(1\pm\sqrt{1-4\det\rho}\bigr)$.
solution: $\lambda_\pm=\frac12\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-\frac38}\right)=\frac12(1\pm0.7906)$, that is $0.8953$ and $0.1047$. Then $S=-0.8953\ln0.8953-0.1047\ln0.1047=0.0990+0.2363\approx0.3353$ nats.

@quiz num
prompt: A qubit with $H=\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}\sigma_z$ starts in $\rho(0)=\frac12(\mathbb 1+0.6\,\sigma_x)$. What is $\langle\sigma_x\rangle$ at time $t$ with $\omega t=\pi/3$?
answer: 3/10
check: (lambda U, rho0, sx: float(np.real(np.trace(U @ rho0 @ U.conj().T @ sx))))(np.diag(np.exp(-0.5j * (np.pi / 3) * np.array([1, -1]))), 0.5 * (np.eye(2) + 0.6 * np.array([[0, 1], [1, 0]])), np.array([[0, 1], [1, 0]]))
hint: Evolve with $\rho(t)=U\rho(0)U^\dagger$ or note that the Bloch vector precesses about $z$ by the angle $\omega t$.
hint: Precession about $z$ rotates $(r_x,r_y)$ by $\omega t$ and leaves its length unchanged.
solution: The initial Bloch vector is $(0.6,0,0)$. After rotation by $\omega t=\pi/3$ about $z$, $r_x=0.6\cos\frac\pi3=0.3$ (and $r_y=\pm0.6\sin\frac\pi3$). So $\langle\sigma_x\rangle=\frac{3}{10}$.

@quiz ms
prompt: Select all matrices that are valid density matrices.
choice*: $\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/2\\1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix}$
choice: $\begin{pmatrix}3/4&1/2\\1/2&1/4\end{pmatrix}$
choice*: $\begin{pmatrix}1/2&i/4\\-i/4&1/2\end{pmatrix}$
choice: $\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$
hint: Check Hermiticity, trace $1$, and nonnegative eigenvalues (for a $2\times2$ matrix with trace $1$, equivalent to $\det\ge0$).
solution: The matrix with all entries $\frac12$ is the pure state $|{+x}\rangle$. The matrix with diagonal $\frac34,\frac14$ and off-diagonal $\frac12$ has trace $1$ but $\det=\frac3{16}-\frac14=-\frac1{16}<0$, so it has a negative eigenvalue. The matrix with off-diagonal entries $\pm i/4$ is Hermitian with trace $1$ and $\det=\frac14-\frac1{16}=\frac3{16}>0$. The identity matrix has trace $2$ (it equals $2\cdot\mathbb 1/2$).
