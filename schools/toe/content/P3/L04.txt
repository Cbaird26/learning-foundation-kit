@article
Two oscillators that can push on each other do something a single oscillator cannot: they pass energy back and forth. The key tool for understanding them is the **normal mode**.

### Setting up

Two identical masses $m$ slide on a frictionless line. Each is attached to a wall by a spring of constant $k$, and a middle spring of constant $\kappa$ joins them. With $x_1, x_2$ the displacements from equilibrium, the middle spring is stretched by $x_2 - x_1$, so

$$m\ddot x_1 = -kx_1 - \kappa(x_1 - x_2), \qquad m\ddot x_2 = -kx_2 - \kappa(x_2 - x_1).$$

In matrix form, with $\mathbf x = (x_1, x_2)^{T}$,

$$m\ddot{\mathbf x} = -K\mathbf x, \qquad K = \begin{pmatrix} k+\kappa & -\kappa \\ -\kappa & k+\kappa \end{pmatrix}.$$

### Normal modes as an eigenvalue problem

A **normal mode** is a motion in which every coordinate oscillates at one common frequency: $\mathbf x(t) = \mathbf a\cos(\omega t + \phi)$. Substituting gives

$$K\mathbf a = m\omega^2\,\mathbf a.$$

The vector $\mathbf a$ is an eigenvector of $K$ (the **mode shape**) and $m\omega^2$ is its eigenvalue. Setting $\det(K - m\omega^2 I) = 0$ gives $(k+\kappa-m\omega^2)^2 = \kappa^2$, so $k+\kappa-m\omega^2 = \pm\kappa$:

| mode | shape $\mathbf a$ | $\omega^2$ |
|---|---|---|
| in phase | $(1,\ 1)$ | $\omega_1^2 = k/m$ |
| out of phase | $(1,\ -1)$ | $\omega_2^2 = (k+2\kappa)/m$ |

In the in-phase mode the middle spring is never stretched, so it plays no role. In the out-of-phase mode it is stretched by $2x_1$, which stiffens the motion. If the masses or springs differ, solve $\det(K - \omega^2 M) = 0$ with $M = \mathrm{diag}(m_1, m_2)$; the method is the same.

### General motion

The equations are linear, so any sum of solutions is a solution:

$$\mathbf x(t) = C_1\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}\cos(\omega_1 t+\phi_1) + C_2\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\end{pmatrix}\cos(\omega_2 t+\phi_2).$$

The four constants $C_1, \phi_1, C_2, \phi_2$ are fixed by $x_1(0)$, $x_2(0)$, $\dot x_1(0)$ and $\dot x_2(0)$. Released from rest, $\phi_1 = \phi_2 = 0$ and

$$C_1 = \tfrac12\big(x_1(0) + x_2(0)\big), \qquad C_2 = \tfrac12\big(x_1(0) - x_2(0)\big).$$

### Beats

Pull mass 1 aside by $a$ and release both masses from rest. Then $C_1 = C_2 = a/2$, and the identities for $\cos P \pm \cos Q$ give

$$x_1 = a\cos\!\Big(\frac{\omega_2-\omega_1}{2}\,t\Big)\cos\!\Big(\frac{\omega_1+\omega_2}{2}\,t\Big), \qquad x_2 = a\sin\!\Big(\frac{\omega_2-\omega_1}{2}\,t\Big)\sin\!\Big(\frac{\omega_1+\omega_2}{2}\,t\Big).$$

A fast oscillation at the mean frequency sits inside a slow envelope. Mass 1's envelope vanishes at $t = \pi/(\omega_2-\omega_1)$, when all the energy has moved to mass 2, and returns to full strength after $2\pi/(\omega_2-\omega_1)$. These are **beats**: they appear because the two modes slowly drift out of phase. Weak coupling ($\kappa \ll k$) gives $\omega_2 - \omega_1 \approx \omega_1\kappa/k$, so the weaker the coupling, the slower the energy exchange.

@example
title: Normal modes of a symmetric pair
problem: Two $0.50\ \text{kg}$ masses are each attached to a wall by a spring with $k = 40\ \text{N/m}$ and joined by a middle spring with $\kappa = 10\ \text{N/m}$. Find the normal-mode frequencies and shapes, and the time for energy to return to the same mass.
step: The stiffness matrix is $K = \begin{pmatrix}50 & -10\\-10 & 50\end{pmatrix}\ \text{N/m}$, so $K/m = \begin{pmatrix}100 & -20\\-20 & 100\end{pmatrix}\ \text{s}^{-2}$.
step: The eigenvalues $\lambda = \omega^2$ satisfy $(100-\lambda)^2 = 20^2$, so $\lambda = 80$ or $120\ \text{s}^{-2}$.
step: For $\lambda = 80$ the first row reads $20a_1 - 20a_2 = 0$, so $a_1 = a_2$. This is the in-phase mode $(1,1)$ with $\omega_1 = \sqrt{80} \approx 8.94\ \text{rad/s}$, matching $\sqrt{k/m}$.
step: For $\lambda = 120$ the first row reads $-20a_1 - 20a_2 = 0$, so $a_2 = -a_1$. This is the out-of-phase mode $(1,-1)$ with $\omega_2 = \sqrt{120} \approx 10.95\ \text{rad/s}$, matching $\sqrt{(k+2\kappa)/m}$.
step: The frequency difference is $\omega_2 - \omega_1 \approx 2.01\ \text{rad/s}$, so energy returns to the same mass every $2\pi/2.01 \approx 3.13\ \text{s}$.

@example
title: Decomposing an initial condition into modes
problem: The masses of a symmetric pair are released from rest with $x_1(0) = 3.0\ \text{cm}$ and $x_2(0) = -1.0\ \text{cm}$. Write $x_1(t)$ and $x_2(t)$ in terms of $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$.
step: Released from rest means $\phi_1 = \phi_2 = 0$, so $x_1(t) = C_1\cos\omega_1 t + C_2\cos\omega_2 t$ and $x_2(t) = C_1\cos\omega_1 t - C_2\cos\omega_2 t$.
step: At $t = 0$ these read $x_1(0) = C_1 + C_2 = 3.0$ and $x_2(0) = C_1 - C_2 = -1.0$.
step: Adding and subtracting: $C_1 = \tfrac12(3.0 - 1.0) = 1.0\ \text{cm}$ and $C_2 = \tfrac12(3.0 + 1.0) = 2.0\ \text{cm}$.
step: Therefore $x_1 = 1.0\cos\omega_1 t + 2.0\cos\omega_2 t$ and $x_2 = 1.0\cos\omega_1 t - 2.0\cos\omega_2 t$ (in cm). Both velocities vanish at $t = 0$, as required.

@mistakes
- Getting the signs of the off-diagonal terms wrong. The middle spring stretches by $x_2 - x_1$, so each row of $K$ has diagonal $k + \kappa$ and off-diagonal $-\kappa$.
- Reporting the eigenvalue $m\omega^2$ (or $\omega^2$) as the frequency. The mode frequencies are the square roots of the eigenvalues of $K/m$.
- Mixing up the beat envelope with the mode frequencies. The envelope factors have angular frequency $(\omega_2-\omega_1)/2$, and complete energy exchange takes $\pi/(\omega_2-\omega_1)$.
- Taking $C_1 = x_1(0) + x_2(0)$ without the factor $\tfrac12$.

@practice num
prompt: Two identical $1.0\ \text{kg}$ masses are each joined to a wall by a spring with $k = 36\ \text{N/m}$ and to each other by a middle spring with $\kappa = 14\ \text{N/m}$. In the in-phase mode, where $x_1 = x_2$, find the angular frequency.
answer: 6
check: min(np.sqrt(np.linalg.eigvalsh(np.array([[50.0, -14.0], [-14.0, 50.0]]))))
unit: rad/s
hint: When $x_1 = x_2$, the middle spring is not stretched.
hint: Each mass then behaves as if it were on its wall spring alone: $\omega^2 = k/m$.
solution: With $x_1 = x_2$ the middle spring exerts no force, so $m\ddot x = -kx$ and $\omega = \sqrt{36/1.0} = 6\ \text{rad/s}$. This is the lower of the two eigenvalues of $K = \begin{pmatrix}50 & -14\\-14 & 50\end{pmatrix}$, namely $36$.

choice*: The masses move in opposite directions and the middle spring is stretched and compressed, which adds stiffness.
choice: The masses move together and the middle spring is stretched and compressed, which adds stiffness.
choice: The masses move in opposite directions and the middle spring is never stretched.
choice: The masses move together and the middle spring is never stretched.
hint: Consider which mode makes the middle spring do work.
hint: Compare $\omega_1^2 = k/m$ with $\omega_2^2 = (k+2\kappa)/m$.
solution: The out-of-phase mode $(1,-1)$ stretches the middle spring by $2x_1$, so the effective stiffness rises to $k + 2\kappa$ and its frequency is the higher one. In the in-phase mode the middle spring is never stretched.

@practice list
prompt: A $1.0\ \text{kg}$ mass and a $2.0\ \text{kg}$ mass lie on a frictionless line. The first is joined to a left wall by a spring of $2.0\ \text{N/m}$, the second to a right wall by a spring of $4.0\ \text{N/m}$, and the two masses to each other by a spring of $2.0\ \text{N/m}$. Find the two normal-mode angular frequencies in rad/s, giving exact values.
answer: sqrt(2), sqrt(5)
check: list(np.sqrt(np.sort(np.real(np.linalg.eigvals(np.dot(np.linalg.inv(np.diag([1.0, 2.0])), np.array([[4.0, -2.0], [-2.0, 6.0]])))))))
hint: Write $m_1\ddot x_1 = -2x_1 - 2(x_1 - x_2)$ and $m_2\ddot x_2 = -4x_2 - 2(x_2 - x_1)$, then divide each equation by its mass.
hint: You should find $\ddot x_1 = -4x_1 + 2x_2$ and $\ddot x_2 = x_1 - 3x_2$. Look for solutions $\propto \cos\omega t$ and require a nonzero determinant.
solution: The equations are $\ddot x_1 = -4x_1 + 2x_2$ and $\ddot x_2 = x_1 - 3x_2$, so $\omega^2$ is an eigenvalue of $\begin{pmatrix}4 & -2\\-1 & 3\end{pmatrix}$. The characteristic equation is $\lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 10 = 0$, so $\lambda = 2$ or $5$ and $\omega = \sqrt2,\ \sqrt5\ \text{rad/s}$.

@quiz list
prompt: Two $1.0\ \text{kg}$ masses are joined to each other by a spring of $2.0\ \text{N/m}$. The first is also joined to a left wall by a spring of $2.0\ \text{N/m}$ and the second to a right wall by a spring of $5.0\ \text{N/m}$. Find the two normal-mode angular frequencies in rad/s, giving exact values.
answer: sqrt(3), sqrt(8)
check: list(np.sqrt(np.linalg.eigvalsh(np.array([[4.0, -2.0], [-2.0, 7.0]]))))
hint: The stiffness matrix is $K = \begin{pmatrix}k_1+\kappa & -\kappa\\-\kappa & k_2+\kappa\end{pmatrix}$, and $m = 1.0\ \text{kg}$.
hint: Solve $\det(K - \lambda I) = 0$ for $\lambda = \omega^2$.
solution: $K = \begin{pmatrix}4 & -2\\-2 & 7\end{pmatrix}$, so $\lambda^2 - 11\lambda + 24 = 0$ and $\lambda = 3$ or $8$. Hence $\omega = \sqrt3,\ \sqrt8\ \text{rad/s}$.

@quiz num
prompt: Two weakly coupled identical pendulums have normal-mode frequencies $f_1 = 0.500\ \text{Hz}$ and $f_2 = 0.520\ \text{Hz}$. Pendulum 1 is displaced and released from rest while pendulum 2 hangs at rest. After how long is the energy first completely transferred to pendulum 2?
answer: 25
check: pi/(2*pi*(0.520 - 0.500))
unit: s
hint: Complete transfer happens when the beat envelope of pendulum 1 first vanishes, at $t = \pi/(\omega_2-\omega_1)$.
hint: Convert the frequency difference to angular frequency, $\omega_2 - \omega_1 = 2\pi(f_2-f_1)$.
solution: $\omega_2 - \omega_1 = 2\pi(0.020) = 0.040\pi\ \text{rad/s}$, so $t = \pi/(0.040\pi) = 25\ \text{s}$.
