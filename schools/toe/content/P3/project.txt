@review
This guide collects the key results of the course. Work through it with your notes closed, then check yourself against the lessons.

### Week by week

1. **Simple oscillators.** $\ddot x = -\omega_0^2x$ with $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$, solution $A\cos(\omega_0t + \phi)$, $A = \sqrt{x_0^2 + (v_0/\omega_0)^2}$, energy $\tfrac12kA^2$. The small-angle pendulum has $\omega_0 = \sqrt{g/L}$.
2. **Damping.** $\ddot x + \gamma\dot x + \omega_0^2x = 0$, envelope $e^{-\gamma t/2}$, $\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2/4}$, $Q = \omega_0/\gamma$, critical damping at $\gamma = 2\omega_0$. Fit $\ln A$ against $t$ to get the decay time.
3. **Driving and resonance.** Steady state at the driving frequency, $A = (F_0/m)/\sqrt{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + \gamma^2\omega^2}$, phase lag from $\operatorname{atan2}(\gamma\omega, \omega_0^2 - \omega^2)$, amplitude peak at $\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2/2}$, half-power width $\gamma$.
4. **Coupled modes.** $K\mathbf a = m\omega^2\mathbf a$; in-phase $\omega^2 = k/m$ and out-of-phase $\omega^2 = (k + 2\kappa)/m$; beats repeat every $2\pi/(\omega_2 - \omega_1)$.
5. **Wave equation.** $y_{tt} = v^2y_{xx}$ with $v = \sqrt{T/\mu}$; solutions $f(x - vt) + g(x + vt)$.
6. **Traveling waves.** $v = \omega/k = \lambda f$, transverse speed up to $\omega A$, power $\tfrac12\mu\omega^2A^2v$.
7. **Standing waves.** $2A\sin kx\cos\omega t$; fixed ends give $f_n = nv/2L$.
8. **Boundary conditions.** A fixed end inverts a pulse and a free end ($\partial y/\partial x = 0$) does not; fixed-free strings have $f_n = (2n - 1)v/4L$; at a junction $A_r/A_i = (v_2 - v_1)/(v_2 + v_1)$.
9. **Fourier synthesis.** $c_n = \tfrac2L\int_0^L h\sin(n\pi x/L)\,dx$; a midpoint pluck has $c_n = 8h\sin(n\pi/2)/(n^2\pi^2)$; jumps give $1/n$ and the Gibbs overshoot.
10. **Dispersion.** $v_p = \omega/k$, $v_g = d\omega/dk$, and a Gaussian pulse grows as $\sigma_0\sqrt{1 + (\omega''t/2\sigma_0^2)^2}$.
11. **Interference.** $d\sin\theta = m\lambda$, $\Delta y = \lambda D/d$, $I = 4I_0\cos^2(\delta/2)$, thin films with $2nt = (m + \tfrac12)\lambda_0$.
12. **Diffraction and polarization.** $a\sin\theta = m\lambda$, $I_0(\sin\beta/\beta)^2$, missing orders, Malus's law $I_0\cos^2\theta$.

### Self-check

- Can I derive $\ddot x = -\omega_0^2x$, the damped and driven versions, and the wave equation from Newton's second law?
- Given a matrix $K$, can I find modes and frequencies and build a solution from initial conditions?
- Can I test a proposed wave function against its equation by differentiating twice?
- Can I say which modes a given boundary condition or pluck position excites?
- Can I state the approximations behind each formula and check them with numbers?

### Common errors

- Mixing $f$ and $\omega$, or $\lambda$ and $k$, which gives stray factors of $2\pi$.
- Forgetting that amplitude decays as $e^{-\gamma t/2}$ but energy as $e^{-\gamma t}$.
- Reporting $\omega^2$ instead of $\omega$ from an eigenvalue problem.
- Reading the coefficient in $y_{tt} = 9y_{xx}$ as the speed, instead of $v^2$.
- Using $\lambda = L/n$ instead of $2L/n$, and giving the free end the same reflection as the fixed end.
- Mixing up the slit width $a$ (envelope) and the slit separation $d$ (fringes).

@exam num
prompt: Two identical $0.40\ \text{kg}$ masses slide on a frictionless line. Each is joined to a wall by a spring of $60\ \text{N/m}$, and a spring of $6.0\ \text{N/m}$ joins the masses to each other. Each mass also feels a drag force $-b\dot x$ with $b = 0.040\ \text{kg/s}$. Mass 1 is pulled aside and released from rest while mass 2 is at rest. Assume the damping shifts the mode frequencies negligibly. How many beat periods (the time for the energy to return to mass 1) elapse before the amplitude envelope has fallen to $1/e$ of its initial value? Give three significant figures.
answer: 3.72
check: (2/(0.040/0.40))/(2*pi/np.ptp(np.sqrt(np.linalg.eigvalsh(np.array([[66.0, -6.0], [-6.0, 66.0]])/0.40))))
tol: 1%
hint: Find the two normal-mode frequencies first: $\omega_1 = \sqrt{k/m}$ and $\omega_2 = \sqrt{(k + 2\kappa)/m}$.
hint: Equal damping on both masses gives every mode the same damping rate $\gamma = b/m$, so the envelope falls as $e^{-\gamma t/2}$ and reaches $1/e$ at $t = 2/\gamma$.
hint: The beat period is $2\pi/(\omega_2 - \omega_1)$. Divide the envelope time by it.
solution: $\omega_1 = \sqrt{60/0.40} = 12.25\ \text{rad/s}$ and $\omega_2 = \sqrt{72/0.40} = 13.42\ \text{rad/s}$, so the beat period is $2\pi/1.169 \approx 5.375\ \text{s}$. The damping rate is $\gamma = 0.040/0.40 = 0.10\ \text{s}^{-1}$, so the envelope falls to $1/e$ at $t = 2/\gamma = 20\ \text{s}$. The number of beat periods is $20/5.375 \approx 3.72$.

@exam list
prompt: A stiff string fixed at both ends has mode frequencies $f_n = nf_1\sqrt{1 + Bn^2}$ with $f_1 = 100\ \text{Hz}$ and $B = 4.0\times10^{-4}$, and its mode shapes remain $\sin(n\pi x/L)$. It is plucked at its midpoint and released from rest. List the frequencies, in hertz to one decimal place, of the three lowest-frequency components present in its motion.
answer: 100.0, 300.5, 502.5
check: [100.0*n*sqrt(1 + 4.0e-4*n**2) for n in range(1, 8) if abs(quad(lambda x: x*sin(n*pi*x), 0, 0.5) + quad(lambda x: (1.0 - x)*sin(n*pi*x), 0.5, 1.0)) > 1e-6][:3]
tol: 0.1
hint: Which modes have a node at the midpoint, and so cannot be excited by a midpoint pluck?
hint: Only odd modes are present. Evaluate $f_n$ for $n = 1, 3, 5$ from the formula given.
solution: A midpoint pluck has coefficients $c_n \propto \sin(n\pi/2)/n^2$, which vanish for even $n$, so the lowest components are $n = 1, 3, 5$. Then $f_1 = 100\sqrt{1.0004} = 100.02$, $f_3 = 300\sqrt{1.0036} = 300.54$ and $f_5 = 500\sqrt{1.01} = 502.49$, that is $100.0,\ 300.5,\ 502.5\ \text{Hz}$. The components are not exact multiples of $100\ \text{Hz}$. This dispersion is why the plucked shape never exactly re-forms.

@exam num
prompt: Laser light passes through a double slit with slit separation $d = 0.30\ \text{mm}$ and slit width $a = 0.060\ \text{mm}$. On a screen $1.8\ \text{m}$ away, the third bright fringe from the center is $9.0\ \text{mm}$ from the central maximum. What is the intensity of that third bright fringe relative to the central bright fringe? Give three significant figures.
answer: 0.255
check: (lambda lam, th: (sin(pi*0.060e-3*sin(th)/lam)/(pi*0.060e-3*sin(th)/lam))**2)(9.0e-3*0.30e-3/(3*1.8), arctan(9.0e-3/1.8))
tol: 1%
hint: Use the fringe position to find the wavelength, $\lambda = y\,d/(mD)$, and check that the small-angle approximation is safe here.
hint: Both bright fringes have $\cos^2 = 1$, so the ratio of their intensities is the ratio of the single-slit envelope factors $(\sin\beta/\beta)^2$.
hint: The central fringe has $\beta = 0$, so its envelope factor is $1$. For the third fringe, $\beta = \pi a\sin\theta/\lambda$.
solution: $\lambda = (9.0\times10^{-3})(0.30\times10^{-3})/(3 \times 1.8) = 500\ \text{nm}$ and $\sin\theta \approx y/D = 5.0\times10^{-3}$, small enough that the small-angle approximation holds to better than $0.01\%$. Then $\beta = \pi(6.0\times10^{-5})(5.0\times10^{-3})/(5.0\times10^{-7}) = 0.6\pi = 1.885$, and $(\sin\beta/\beta)^2 = (0.951/1.885)^2 \approx 0.255$. The third bright fringe is about $25\%$ as bright as the central one. (Here $d/a = 5$, so the fifth order would be missing.)
