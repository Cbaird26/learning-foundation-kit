@article
Shake one end of a rope tied to a wall, and at certain frequencies the rope settles into a pattern that seems to stand still: some points never move (**nodes**) and the points between them swing widely (**antinodes**). This is a **standing wave**.

### Two traveling waves make a standing wave

Add two equal waves traveling in opposite directions:

$$A\sin(kx - \omega t) + A\sin(kx + \omega t) = 2A\sin(kx)\cos(\omega t).$$

The spatial pattern $\sin kx$ stays put, and only its size oscillates, as $\cos\omega t$. Every point moves at the same frequency with its own amplitude $2A|\sin kx|$. Nodes sit where $\sin kx = 0$, that is $x = n\lambda/2$, so neighbouring nodes are half a wavelength apart, with an antinode midway between them. Because the nodes never move, no energy flows through them: a standing wave stores energy but does not transport it.

### Separating the variables

Look for solutions of the wave equation of the form $y = X(x)\,q(t)$. Then $X''/X = \ddot q/(v^2q)$, and since one side depends only on $x$ and the other only on $t$, both equal a constant, written $-k^2$:

$$X'' = -k^2X, \qquad \ddot q = -\omega^2 q, \qquad \omega = vk.$$

Each spatial **mode shape** comes with a time dependence that is exactly the simple harmonic oscillator of Lesson 1.

### Fixed ends

For a string of length $L$ tied down at both ends, $y(0,t) = y(L,t) = 0$, so $X(0) = X(L) = 0$. Then $X = \sin kx$ and $\sin kL = 0$:

$$k_n = \frac{n\pi}{L}, \qquad \lambda_n = \frac{2L}{n}, \qquad f_n = \frac{nv}{2L} = n f_1, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$$

Mode $n$ has $n$ antinodes and $n-1$ interior nodes. The lowest frequency $f_1 = v/2L = \frac{1}{2L}\sqrt{T/\mu}$ is the **fundamental**, and the rest are **harmonics**, whole-number multiples of it. Shorter, tighter or lighter strings sound higher.

### General motion

The equation is linear, so any sum of modes is also a solution:

$$y(x,t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sin\!\Big(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\Big)\big[a_n\cos\omega_n t + b_n\sin\omega_n t\big], \qquad \omega_n = \frac{n\pi v}{L}.$$

The constants $a_n, b_n$ are fixed by the initial shape and velocity; Lesson 9 shows how. A chain of $N$ beads has $N$ normal modes, and the continuous string is the limit with infinitely many.

@example
title: Frequencies and nodes of a fixed string
problem: A string of length $0.60\ \text{m}$ with $\mu = 0.0050\ \text{kg/m}$ is held at tension $72\ \text{N}$ between two fixed points. Find $f_1$, $f_3$, $\lambda_3$ and the positions of the nodes of mode 3.
step: The wave speed is $v = \sqrt{T/\mu} = \sqrt{72/0.0050} = \sqrt{14400} = 120\ \text{m/s}$.
step: The fundamental is $f_1 = v/2L = 120/1.20 = 100\ \text{Hz}$, so $f_n = 100n\ \text{Hz}$ and $f_3 = 300\ \text{Hz}$.
step: The wavelength of mode 3 is $\lambda_3 = 2L/3 = 0.40\ \text{m}$.
step: Mode 3 is $\sin(3\pi x/L)$, which vanishes at $x = 0,\ 0.20,\ 0.40,\ 0.60\ \text{m}$. The two interior nodes are at $0.20\ \text{m}$ and $0.40\ \text{m}$, and there are three antinodes.
step: Raising the tension to $128\ \text{N}$ multiplies every frequency by $\sqrt{128/72} = 4/3$, so $f_1$ becomes about $133\ \text{Hz}$.

@example
title: Two traveling waves become a standing wave
problem: Two waves on a string are $y_1 = 0.010\sin(5x - 100t)$ and $y_2 = 0.010\sin(5x + 100t)$ (SI units). Show that their sum is a standing wave, and find the wave speed, the node positions and the antinode amplitude.
step: Use $\sin(a - b) + \sin(a + b) = 2\sin a\cos b$ with $a = 5x$ and $b = 100t$: $y = 0.020\sin(5x)\cos(100t)$.
step: This has a fixed spatial shape $\sin 5x$ multiplying a purely oscillating factor $\cos 100t$, so it is a standing wave.
step: Each component moves at $v = \omega/k = 100/5 = 20\ \text{m/s}$, and the wavelength is $\lambda = 2\pi/5 \approx 1.26\ \text{m}$.
step: Nodes occur where $\sin 5x = 0$, so $x = n\pi/5 = 0,\ 0.63,\ 1.26,\dots\ \text{m}$, spaced by $\lambda/2 \approx 0.63\ \text{m}$.
step: At the antinodes, $x = 0.31,\ 0.94,\dots\ \text{m}$, the amplitude is $0.020\ \text{m}$, twice the amplitude of each traveling wave.

@mistakes
- Using $\lambda_n = L/n$ instead of $\lambda_n = 2L/n$. A half wavelength fits in the length for $n = 1$.
- Miscounting nodes: mode $n$ has $n - 1$ interior nodes, not $n$, because the two ends are nodes too.
- Treating a standing wave as a traveling one. Nodes do not move, and no net energy flows along the string.
- Thinking the harmonics are $n^2 f_1$. The frequencies are evenly spaced: $f_n = nf_1$.

@practice num
prompt: A string of length $0.50\ \text{m}$ is fixed at both ends, and waves travel along it at $200\ \text{m/s}$. Find its fundamental frequency.
answer: 200
check: 200.0/(2*0.50)
unit: Hz
hint: For fixed ends, half a wavelength fits in the string in the fundamental mode.
hint: $\lambda_1 = 2L$, and $f = v/\lambda$.
solution: $\lambda_1 = 2L = 1.0\ \text{m}$, so $f_1 = v/\lambda_1 = 200/1.0 = 200\ \text{Hz}$.

solution: $v = \sqrt{100/0.010} = 100\ \text{m/s}$, so $f_1 = 100/1.0 = 100\ \text{Hz}$ and $f_3 = 300\ \text{Hz}$.

@practice list
prompt: A $1.2\ \text{m}$ string is fixed at both ends and vibrates in its $n = 4$ mode. Give the positions, in metres from one end, of the interior nodes (not counting the two fixed ends).
answer: 0.3, 0.6, 0.9
check: [1.2*j/4 for j in range(1, 4)]
hint: Nodes are where $\sin(n\pi x/L) = 0$.
hint: For $n = 4$ the nodes are at $x = jL/4$ with $j = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$.
solution: $\sin(4\pi x/1.2) = 0$ when $x = j(1.2)/4 = 0.3j$. The interior nodes ($j = 1, 2, 3$) are at $0.3,\ 0.6$ and $0.9\ \text{m}$.

@practice mc
prompt: A string fixed at both ends vibrates in its $n = 2$ mode. Which statement is true?
choice*: The midpoint is a node, and the two halves of the string move in opposite directions.
choice: The midpoint is an antinode, and the whole string moves up and down together.
choice: The pattern travels along the string at the wave speed.
choice: Every point on the string oscillates with the same amplitude.
hint: Sketch $\sin(2\pi x/L)$ and see where it vanishes.
hint: A node in the middle separates two antinodes, one on each side.
solution: The mode shape $\sin(2\pi x/L)$ is zero at $x = L/2$ and has opposite signs on the two halves, so the halves move oppositely. A standing wave does not travel, and its amplitude varies from point to point.

@practice num
prompt: A string of length $1.5\ \text{m}$ fixed at both ends is found to resonate at $240\ \text{Hz}$ and at the next higher resonance, $320\ \text{Hz}$. What is the wave speed on the string?
answer: 240
check: 2*1.5*(320.0 - 240.0)
unit: m/s
hint: Neighbouring resonances of a string with two fixed ends are spaced by one fundamental frequency, because $f_{n+1} - f_n = f_1$.
hint: Find $f_1$ from the spacing, then use $f_1 = v/2L$.
solution: $f_1 = 320 - 240 = 80\ \text{Hz}$ (these are $n = 3$ and $n = 4$). Then $v = 2Lf_1 = 2(1.5)(80) = 240\ \text{m/s}$.
