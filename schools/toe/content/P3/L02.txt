@article
Real oscillators lose energy to friction, drag or radiation. The simplest model is **viscous damping**: a drag force proportional to velocity, $F = -b\dot x$ with $b > 0$ (SI unit kg/s). Newton's second law for a mass on a spring becomes

$$m\ddot x + b\dot x + kx = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ddot x + \gamma\dot x + \omega_0^2 x = 0, \qquad \gamma = \frac{b}{m},\quad \omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}.$$

In this course $\gamma = b/m$ is the **damping rate**. Some books write $2\beta$ for it, so always check the definition when you read elsewhere.

### Solving it

Try $x = e^{st}$. Then $s^2 + \gamma s + \omega_0^2 = 0$, so

$$s = -\frac{\gamma}{2} \pm \sqrt{\frac{\gamma^2}{4} - \omega_0^2}.$$

The sign of the quantity under the root gives three cases. When it is negative the roots are complex, and the solution is

$$x(t) = A\,e^{-\gamma t/2}\cos(\omega_d t + \phi), \qquad \omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \frac{\gamma^2}{4}}.$$

- **Underdamped** ($\gamma < 2\omega_0$): the motion above, a cosine of slightly lower frequency inside a decaying **envelope** $Ae^{-\gamma t/2}$.
- **Critically damped** ($\gamma = 2\omega_0$): $x(t) = (c_1 + c_2 t)\,e^{-\gamma t/2}$. This is the fastest return to equilibrium without oscillating.
- **Overdamped** ($\gamma > 2\omega_0$): two real negative roots $s_\pm$ and $x(t) = c_1e^{s_+t} + c_2e^{s_-t}$, a slow creep back with no oscillation.

Critical damping needs $b_c = 2\sqrt{km} = 2m\omega_0$.

### Decay time, energy and Q

The envelope falls by a factor $1/e$ in the time $\tau = 2/\gamma$. Energy goes as amplitude squared, so $E \propto e^{-\gamma t}$, with time constant $1/\gamma$. The **quality factor**

$$Q = \frac{\omega_0}{\gamma}$$

is the number of radians of phase swept while the energy falls by $1/e$. For large $Q$ the oscillator loses about a fraction $2\pi/Q$ of its energy per cycle and rings for many cycles.

### Fitting an envelope

Peak heights $A_n$ measured at times $t_n$ should lie on $A_0e^{-t/\tau}$. Taking logarithms turns the curve into a straight line:

$$\ln A_n = \ln A_0 - \frac{t_n}{\tau}.$$

Fit a line to $\ln A$ versus $t$ by least squares. The intercept gives $\ln A_0$ (the initial envelope height), and minus the slope gives $1/\tau = \gamma/2$. Curvature in the plot means the decay is not purely viscous; dry friction, for example, makes the envelope fall linearly instead.

Two successive peaks, one damped period $T_d = 2\pi/\omega_d$ apart, have ratio $A_n/A_{n+1} = e^{\gamma T_d/2}$. The **logarithmic decrement** is $\delta = \ln(A_n/A_{n+1}) = \gamma T_d/2$.

@example
title: Classify and characterize a damped oscillator
problem: A $0.50\ \text{kg}$ mass on a spring with $k = 50\ \text{N/m}$ moves through a fluid with $b = 0.50\ \text{kg/s}$. Classify the motion and find $\gamma$, $\omega_d$, the amplitude decay time $\tau$ and $Q$.
step: The natural frequency is $\omega_0 = \sqrt{50/0.50} = 10\ \text{rad/s}$ and the damping rate is $\gamma = b/m = 1.0\ \text{s}^{-1}$.
step: Compare with $2\omega_0 = 20\ \text{s}^{-1}$. Since $1.0 < 20$, the motion is underdamped.
step: The damped frequency is $\omega_d = \sqrt{100 - 0.25} \approx 9.987\ \text{rad/s}$, only about $0.1\%$ below $\omega_0$.
step: The amplitude decay time is $\tau = 2/\gamma = 2.0\ \text{s}$, and the energy decay time is $1/\gamma = 1.0\ \text{s}$.
step: The quality factor is $Q = \omega_0/\gamma = 10$: about ten radians (roughly 1.6 cycles) elapse while the energy falls by a factor of $e$.

@example
title: Damping rate from two successive peaks
problem: Two successive positive peaks of a damped oscillation are $8.0\ \text{cm}$ and $6.4\ \text{cm}$, separated by $0.63\ \text{s}$. Find $\gamma$ and the amplitude decay time $\tau$.
step: Successive peaks are one damped period apart, so $T_d = 0.63\ \text{s}$ and $A_n/A_{n+1} = e^{\gamma T_d/2}$.
step: The logarithmic decrement is $\delta = \ln(8.0/6.4) = \ln 1.25 = 0.2231$.
step: Then $\gamma = 2\delta/T_d = 2(0.2231)/0.63 \approx 0.71\ \text{s}^{-1}$.
step: The decay time is $\tau = 2/\gamma \approx 2.8\ \text{s}$: every $2.8\ \text{s}$ the envelope shrinks by a factor of $e$.

@mistakes
- Forgetting the factor of $2$: with $\ddot x + \gamma\dot x + \omega_0^2x = 0$ the amplitude decays as $e^{-\gamma t/2}$, but the energy decays as $e^{-\gamma t}$.
- Using $\omega_0$ in place of $\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2/4}$; the difference is tiny for large $Q$ but large near critical damping.
- Fitting a straight line to $A$ versus $t$ instead of to $\ln A$ versus $t$.
- Comparing $b$ directly with $\omega_0$; the critical condition is $\gamma = b/m = 2\omega_0$.

@practice num
prompt: A $0.20\ \text{kg}$ mass on a spring with $k = 20\ \text{N/m}$ is immersed in oil. What damping constant $b$ makes the motion critically damped?
answer: 4
check: 2*sqrt(20*0.20)
unit: kg/s
hint: Critical damping means $\gamma = 2\omega_0$.
hint: Since $\gamma = b/m$, this gives $b = 2m\omega_0 = 2\sqrt{km}$.
solution: $b_c = 2\sqrt{km} = 2\sqrt{(20)(0.20)} = 2(2.0) = 4.0\ \text{kg/s}$.

prompt: A $0.25\ \text{kg}$ mass on a spring with $k = 100\ \text{N/m}$ experiences a drag force $-b\dot x$ with $b = 6.0\ \text{kg/s}$. Find the damped angular frequency $\omega_d$.

hint: $\omega_0^2 = 400\ \text{s}^{-2}$ and $\gamma = 24\ \text{s}^{-1}$; then use $\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2/4}$.
solution: $\omega_0^2 = 100/0.25 = 400$ and $\gamma/2 = 12$, so $\omega_d = \sqrt{400 - 144} = \sqrt{256} = 16\ \text{rad/s}$.

@practice num
prompt: The peak displacement of a damped oscillator was recorded at $t = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4\ \text{s}$ with the values $A = 10.0, 7.8, 6.1, 4.7, 3.7\ \text{cm}$. Fit a straight line to $\ln A$ versus $t$ by least squares and report the amplitude decay time $\tau$ of the envelope $A_0e^{-t/\tau}$, to two significant figures.
answer: 4.0
check: -1/np.polyfit([0, 1, 2, 3, 4], np.log([10.0, 7.8, 6.1, 4.7, 3.7]), 1)[0]
tol: 2%
unit: s
hint: Take the natural logarithm of each amplitude. The model $\ln A = \ln A_0 - t/\tau$ is a straight line in $t$.
hint: The least-squares slope of $\ln A$ against $t$ equals $-1/\tau$.
hint: Compute the slope with a calculator or with np.polyfit(t, np.log(A), 1); then take the negative reciprocal.
solution: The logarithms are $2.303, 2.054, 1.808, 1.548, 1.308$. The least-squares slope is about $-0.2495\ \text{s}^{-1}$, so $\tau = 1/0.2495 \approx 4.0\ \text{s}$. Equivalently $\gamma = 2/\tau \approx 0.50\ \text{s}^{-1}$, and the envelope drops by about $22\%$ each second.

@practice num
prompt: An underdamped oscillator's envelope falls from $5.0\ \text{cm}$ to $3.0\ \text{cm}$ in $4.0\ \text{s}$. Find the damping rate $\gamma$, rounded to three decimal places.
answer: 0.255
check: 2*log(5.0/3.0)/4.0
tol: 1%
unit: 1/s
hint: The envelope is $A_0e^{-\gamma t/2}$. Write the ratio of the two amplitudes.
hint: $\ln(5.0/3.0) = \gamma(4.0)/2$.
solution: $\ln(5/3) = 0.5108 = \gamma(4.0)/2$, so $\gamma = 2(0.5108)/4.0 \approx 0.255\ \text{s}^{-1}$.

@quiz num
prompt: The amplitude of a ringing oscillator halves every $3.0\ \text{s}$, and its natural frequency is $\omega_0 = 8.0\ \text{rad/s}$. Find the quality factor $Q$ to three significant figures.
answer: 17.3
check: 8.0/(2*log(2)/3.0)
tol: 1%
hint: The envelope is $e^{-\gamma t/2}$. Halving in $3.0\ \text{s}$ means $e^{-3\gamma/2} = \tfrac12$.
hint: Solve for $\gamma$, then use $Q = \omega_0/\gamma$.
solution: $\gamma = 2\ln 2/3.0 \approx 0.462\ \text{s}^{-1}$, so $Q = 8.0/0.462 \approx 17.3$.

@quiz ms
prompt: Which statements about the underdamped oscillator $\ddot x + \gamma\dot x + \omega_0^2x = 0$ are true?
choice*: The amplitude envelope decays as $e^{-\gamma t/2}$.
choice*: The energy decays twice as fast, in the exponent, as the amplitude envelope.
choice: The damped frequency $\omega_d$ is slightly greater than $\omega_0$.
choice: Critical damping gives the longest possible time to return to equilibrium.
hint: Square the envelope to get the energy.
hint: Compare $\omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2/4}$ with $\omega_0$, and recall what critical damping is for.
solution: The envelope is $e^{-\gamma t/2}$, and energy is proportional to its square, $e^{-\gamma t}$. The damped frequency is lower than $\omega_0$, and critical damping gives the fastest non-oscillatory return, not the longest.
