@article
**Boundary conditions** are the rules at the ends of a medium. They decide how pulses reflect and which standing waves, and therefore which resonant frequencies, can exist.

### Fixed end

A string tied to a wall has $y = 0$ there. Put the wall at $x = 0$ with the string on $x > 0$. Write the left-moving incident wave as $f(x + vt)$ and the reflected wave as $g(x - vt)$. Then $y(0,t) = f(vt) + g(-vt) = 0$ for all $t$, so $g(u) = -f(-u)$. The reflected pulse is **inverted**: a phase change of $\pi$.

### Free end

Attach the end to a massless ring sliding without friction on a vertical rod. The string can then exert no transverse force on the ring, which requires zero slope: $\partial y/\partial x = 0$. This gives $f'(vt) + g'(-vt) = 0$, so $g(u) = f(-u)$. The reflected pulse has the **same sign**, and at the moment of reflection the end moves through twice the height of the incident pulse.

### Resonances

Take a node at $x = 0$, so $X = \sin kx$. The condition at $x = L$ then selects the modes.

- **Fixed at $L$:** $\sin kL = 0$, so $k_n = n\pi/L$ and $f_n = nv/2L$.
- **Free at $L$:** $X'(L) = k\cos kL = 0$, so $k_nL = (n - \tfrac12)\pi$ and $f_n = (2n-1)\,\dfrac{v}{4L}$, for $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$

For the same $L$ and $v$, the fixed-free string has a fundamental $v/4L$ that is half the fixed-fixed value $v/2L$, and it contains only odd multiples of that fundamental. Its fundamental is a quarter wavelength, $\lambda = 4L$, with a node at the fixed end and an antinode at the free end. (A pipe closed at one end behaves the same way.)

### Joining two strings

Let a string with wave speed $v_1$ (for $x < 0$) be tied to a string with speed $v_2$ (for $x > 0$), under the same tension. A wave of amplitude $A_i$ comes in from the left. At the knot the displacement must be continuous, and so must the slope (the tensions are equal, so otherwise the massless knot would feel a net force). These two conditions give

$$A_r = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{v_2 + v_1}\,A_i, \qquad A_t = \frac{2v_2}{v_1 + v_2}\,A_i = A_i + A_r.$$

If the second string is heavier ($v_2 < v_1$), the reflection is inverted. The limits recover the two ends: $v_2 \to 0$ gives $A_r = -A_i$ (fixed), and $v_2 \to \infty$ gives $A_r = +A_i$ (free). The reflected power fraction is $(A_r/A_i)^2$, and the transmitted fraction is $4v_1v_2/(v_1+v_2)^2$; they add to $1$.

@example
title: Resonances with a fixed end and a free end
problem: A string of length $0.50\ \text{m}$ carries waves at $200\ \text{m/s}$. It is fixed at $x = 0$ and free at $x = L$. Find its three lowest resonant frequencies and compare them with those of the same string fixed at both ends.
step: A node at $x = 0$ gives $X = \sin kx$. The free end requires $X'(L) = k\cos kL = 0$.
step: So $kL = \pi/2,\ 3\pi/2,\ 5\pi/2,\dots$, that is $k_n = (2n-1)\pi/2L$.
step: The frequencies are $f_n = vk_n/2\pi = (2n-1)v/4L = (2n-1)(200)/2.0 = 100(2n-1)\ \text{Hz}$, giving $100,\ 300,\ 500\ \text{Hz}$.
step: With both ends fixed, $f_n = nv/2L = 200n\ \text{Hz}$: $200,\ 400,\ 600\ \text{Hz}$. The fixed-free fundamental is half as high, and the even harmonics are missing.
step: For $n = 1$ the wavelength is $4L = 2.0\ \text{m}$: a quarter wave fits, with a node at the fixed end and an antinode at the free end.

@example
title: A pulse meeting a heavier string
problem: Two strings under the same tension $100\ \text{N}$ are joined, with $\mu_1 = 0.010\ \text{kg/m}$ and $\mu_2 = 0.040\ \text{kg/m}$. A pulse of height $6.0\ \text{cm}$ travels from string 1 toward the junction. Find the reflected and transmitted heights and the fraction of power reflected.
step: The speeds are $v_1 = \sqrt{100/0.010} = 100\ \text{m/s}$ and $v_2 = \sqrt{100/0.040} = 50\ \text{m/s}$.
step: The reflected amplitude is $A_r = \dfrac{v_2 - v_1}{v_2 + v_1}A_i = \dfrac{-50}{150}(6.0) = -2.0\ \text{cm}$. The minus sign means the reflected pulse is inverted.
step: The transmitted amplitude is $A_t = A_i + A_r = 4.0\ \text{cm}$, which agrees with $2v_2/(v_1+v_2) = 2/3$ times $6.0\ \text{cm}$.
step: The power reflected is $(A_r/A_i)^2 = 1/9 \approx 11\%$, and $4v_1v_2/(v_1+v_2)^2 = 8/9$ is transmitted. The fractions add to $1$.

@mistakes
- Saying a free end inverts the pulse. The fixed end inverts it, since $y$ must vanish there; the free end reflects with the same sign.
- Writing $y = 0$ for a free end. The free-end condition is zero slope, $\partial y/\partial x = 0$.
- Using $f_n = nv/2L$ for a fixed-free string. Only odd multiples of $v/4L$ occur.
- Forgetting that both displacement and slope are continuous at a junction, or using the formula $A_r = (v_2 - v_1)/(v_2 + v_1)$ with $v_1$ and $v_2$ swapped.

prompt: An upward-pointing pulse travels along a string toward its far end. What does the reflected pulse look like if the end is (i) tied to a rigid wall, and (ii) attached to a ring that slides freely on a frictionless vertical rod?
choice*: (i) a downward (inverted) pulse; (ii) an upward pulse of the same sign.
choice: (i) an upward pulse; (ii) a downward (inverted) pulse.
choice: (i) a downward pulse; (ii) a downward pulse.
choice: (i) an upward pulse; (ii) no reflected pulse.
hint: At a rigid wall the displacement must be zero at all times. What reflected pulse cancels the incident one there?
hint: At the free end the slope must be zero, and a reflected pulse of the same sign makes the slopes cancel.
solution: At the fixed end the incident and reflected displacements must cancel at the wall, so the reflection is inverted. At the free end the slopes must cancel, which requires a reflected pulse of the same sign, so the end moves up to twice the pulse height.

@practice num
prompt: A string of length $0.60\ \text{m}$ carries waves at $120\ \text{m/s}$. It is fixed at one end and free at the other. What is its fundamental frequency?
answer: 50
check: 120.0*root(lambda k: cos(k*0.60), 0.1, 4.0)/(2*pi)
unit: Hz
hint: The fixed end is a node and the free end is an antinode. How much of a wavelength fits in $L$ for the lowest mode?
hint: The lowest mode is a quarter wavelength, $\lambda = 4L$, and $f = v/\lambda$.
solution: The fundamental has $\lambda = 4L = 2.4\ \text{m}$, so $f_1 = v/\lambda = 120/2.4 = 50\ \text{Hz}$. Equivalently $\cos kL = 0$ gives $kL = \pi/2$.

@practice list
prompt: A string of length $0.60\ \text{m}$ carries waves at $120\ \text{m/s}$. It is fixed at one end and free at the other. List its three lowest resonant frequencies in hertz.
answer: 50, 150, 250
check: [120.0*r/(2*pi) for r in allroots(lambda k: cos(k*0.60), 0.1, 14.0)]
hint: The free-end condition is $\cos kL = 0$.
hint: The solutions are $kL = \pi/2,\ 3\pi/2,\ 5\pi/2$. Convert each $k$ to a frequency with $f = vk/2\pi$.
solution: $f_n = (2n-1)v/4L = (2n-1)(50)\ \text{Hz}$, so $50,\ 150$ and $250\ \text{Hz}$: odd multiples of the fundamental.

@practice num
prompt: Two strings under equal tension are joined. A wave of amplitude $3.0\ \text{cm}$ travels on the first string, where its speed is $80\ \text{m/s}$, toward the junction with a second string where the speed is $120\ \text{m/s}$. Find the signed amplitude $A_r$ of the reflected wave in cm (negative means inverted).
answer: 0.6
check: lin([[1, -1], [1/80.0, 1/120.0]], [-3.0, 3.0/80.0])[0]
unit: cm
hint: At the junction the displacement is continuous, $A_i + A_r = A_t$, and so is the slope, $(A_i - A_r)/v_1 = A_t/v_2$.
hint: Eliminate $A_t$ to get $A_r = \dfrac{v_2 - v_1}{v_2 + v_1}A_i$.
solution: $A_r = \dfrac{120 - 80}{120 + 80}(3.0) = 0.20(3.0) = 0.60\ \text{cm}$. It is positive because the second string is lighter, so the reflection is not inverted.

answer: 375
check: 270.0*allroots(lambda k: cos(k*0.90), 0.1, 20.0)[2]/(2*pi)
unit: Hz
hint: The free end requires $\cos kL = 0$, which gives odd multiples of $\pi/2$ for $kL$.
hint: The third resonance has $n = 3$, so $kL = 5\pi/2$.
solution: $f_n = (2n-1)v/4L$ with $n = 3$: $f_3 = 5(270)/(4 \times 0.90) = 1350/3.6 = 375\ \text{Hz}$.

@quiz num
prompt: A pulse of height $2.0\ \text{cm}$ travels on a string where the wave speed is $60\ \text{m/s}$ toward a junction with a second string, under the same tension, where the speed is $120\ \text{m/s}$. Find the height of the transmitted pulse in cm, to three significant figures.
answer: 2.67
check: lin([[1, -1], [1/60.0, 1/120.0]], [-2.0, 2.0/60.0])[1]
tol: 1%
unit: cm
hint: The displacement and slope are continuous at the junction.
hint: Use $A_t = \dfrac{2v_2}{v_1 + v_2}A_i$.
solution: $A_t = \dfrac{2(120)}{60 + 120}(2.0) = \dfrac{4}{3}(2.0) \approx 2.67\ \text{cm}$. The reflected pulse is $+0.67\ \text{cm}$, and $2.0 + 0.67 = 2.67$.
