@article
A conductor's surface carries induced charge that we do not know in advance, and that charge shapes the field everywhere. Solving for it directly is hard. The **method of images** sidesteps the difficulty using uniqueness from Week 4: any function that satisfies Poisson's equation in the region of interest, with only the real charges there, and takes the right value on the boundary, **is** the potential. So we invent fictitious *image charges* outside the region, placed so that the boundary condition holds. They are bookkeeping devices, not physical charges.

### Point charge above a grounded plane

Put $q$ at $(0,0,d)$ above the grounded plane $z=0$, and an image $-q$ at $(0,0,-d)$. For $z>0$

$$V=\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+(z-d)^2}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+(z+d)^2}}\right],$$

which vanishes on $z=0$ and has the right singularity at $q$. The induced surface charge is $\sigma=\epsilon_0E_z|_{z=0}$:

$$\sigma(s)=-\frac{q\,d}{2\pi\,(s^2+d^2)^{3/2}},\qquad Q_{\text{induced}}=-q.$$

The force on $q$ equals the force from the image: an attraction of magnitude $\dfrac{q^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0(2d)^2}$. The work needed to bring $q$ in from infinity is $W=-\dfrac{q^2}{16\pi\epsilon_0d}$, **half** the energy of the two-charge system $\pm q$ separated by $2d$, because the field exists on one side of the plane only.

### Grounded sphere

A charge $q$ at distance $a>R$ from the centre of a grounded sphere of radius $R$ has an image on the same line, inside the sphere:

$$q'=-\frac{R}{a}\,q,\qquad b=\frac{R^2}{a}.$$

Check $V=0$ at the two points where the line meets the sphere. The total charge induced on the sphere is $q'$.

### Rules of the method

1. Fix the region where the answer is wanted and the boundary data.
2. Put images only **outside** that region.
3. Verify the boundary condition on the whole boundary.
4. Use the images for fields and forces inside the region only; the field inside the conductor is zero.

@example
title: Induced charge on a grounded plane
problem: A charge $q$ sits a height $d$ above a grounded conducting plane. Find the induced surface charge density and show that the total induced charge is $-q$.
step: Replace the plane by an image $-q$ at depth $d$. At a plane point at distance $s$ from the axis, the charge $q$ gives $E_z=-\dfrac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{d}{(s^2+d^2)^{3/2}}$ and the image gives the same $z$-component. The horizontal components cancel.
step: Hence $E_z=-\dfrac{q\,d}{2\pi\epsilon_0(s^2+d^2)^{3/2}}$ and $\sigma=\epsilon_0E_z=-\dfrac{q\,d}{2\pi(s^2+d^2)^{3/2}}$, negative everywhere and largest directly below the charge.
step: Integrate over the plane with $dA=2\pi s\,ds$: $Q=-q\,d\int_0^\infty\dfrac{s\,ds}{(s^2+d^2)^{3/2}}=-q\,d\Big[-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{s^2+d^2}}\Big]_0^\infty=-q\,d\cdot\dfrac1d=-q$.
step: Every field line leaving $q$ ends on the plane, which matches the total induced charge $-q$.

@example
title: Verifying the image of a grounded sphere
problem: A charge $q$ is at distance $a$ from the centre of a grounded sphere of radius $R<a$. Check that an image $q'=-qR/a$ at $b=R^2/a$ makes $V=0$ at the two points of the sphere on the axis.
step: Nearest point, distance $R$ from the centre. Distance to $q$: $a-R$. Distance to the image: $R-b=R-\dfrac{R^2}{a}=\dfrac{R(a-R)}{a}$.
step: The potential there is proportional to $\dfrac{q}{a-R}+\dfrac{q'}{R-b}=\dfrac{q}{a-R}-\dfrac{qR}{a}\cdot\dfrac{a}{R(a-R)}=0$.
step: Farthest point. Distances: $a+R$ to $q$ and $R+b=\dfrac{R(a+R)}{a}$ to the image. Then $\dfrac{q}{a+R}-\dfrac{qR}{a}\cdot\dfrac{a}{R(a+R)}=0$.
step: Both points give $V=0$. A full check at general angle uses the law of cosines, and uniqueness then says this image potential is the exterior potential.

@mistakes
- Placing an image inside the region where the potential is wanted: that changes the problem.
- Using the full two-charge energy $-q^2/(8\pi\epsilon_0d)$ for the real system; only half of the field exists.
- Using the plane's image ($-q$ at depth $d$) for a sphere, where both the charge $-qR/a$ and the position $R^2/a$ differ.
- Treating image charges as physically present on the conductor; the real induced charge is spread over its surface.

answer: 899
unit: nN
tol: 1%

hint: Apply Coulomb's law with separation $2d=0.20\ \text{m}$.

@practice num
prompt: A charge $q$ is a height $d$ above a grounded plane. For $z>0$ the potential is $V=\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\big(\frac{1}{|\mathbf r-d\hat{\mathbf z}|}-\frac{1}{|\mathbf r+d\hat{\mathbf z}|}\big)$. Find the induced surface charge density at the point of the plane directly below the charge, in units of $q/d^2$.
answer: -1/(2pi)
check: -nderiv(lambda z: 1/abs(z - 1) - 1/(z + 1), 0.0)/(4*pi)
hint: On a conductor, $\sigma=\epsilon_0E_n$, and the normal into the region $z>0$ is $+\hat{\mathbf z}$.
hint: On the axis $V(z)=\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\big(\frac{1}{d-z}-\frac{1}{d+z}\big)$ for $0\le z<d$. Compute $E_z=-dV/dz$ at $z=0$.
solution: $\frac{dV}{dz}\Big|_0=\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\Big(\frac{1}{d^2}+\frac{1}{d^2}\Big)=\frac{q}{2\pi\epsilon_0d^2}$, so $E_z=-\frac{q}{2\pi\epsilon_0d^2}$ and $\sigma=\epsilon_0E_z=-\frac{q}{2\pi d^2}$.

@practice num
prompt: A point charge $q=5.0\ \text{nC}$ lies at distance $a=0.50\ \text{m}$ from the centre of a grounded conducting sphere of radius $R=0.20\ \text{m}$. Find the image charge, in nanocoulombs.
answer: -2
unit: nC
tol: 0.01
check: root(lambda qp: 5/(0.5 - 0.2) + qp/(0.2 - 0.2**2/0.5), -50, 50)
hint: The image sits on the line joining the centre and the charge, at distance $b=R^2/a$ from the centre.
hint: Demand $V=0$ at the nearest point of the sphere: $\frac{q}{a-R}+\frac{q'}{R-b}=0$.
solution: $b=0.04/0.50=0.08\ \text{m}$. Then $\frac{5.0}{0.30}+\frac{q'}{0.12}=0$ gives $q'=-2.0\ \text{nC}$, which equals $-qR/a$.

@practice mc
prompt: What justifies replacing a grounded conductor by image charges when finding the field outside it?
choice*: The image potential satisfies Poisson's equation in the region with only the real charges there and has $V=0$ on the conductor's surface, so by uniqueness it is the potential.
choice: The induced charges on the conductor really are located at the image positions.
choice: Image charges are physical charges that appear inside the conductor when the real charge approaches.
choice: Superposition guarantees the field inside the conductor equals the image field.
hint: Which theorem says that a function with the right equation and the right boundary data cannot be wrong?
hint: Ask where the image charges sit relative to the region in which the field is wanted.
solution: The images lie outside the region of interest, so the potential obeys the correct Poisson equation there, and by construction $V=0$ on the boundary. Uniqueness then says it is the potential in that region. The images are not real, and the true induced charge is on the surface.

@practice self
prompt: Find the work needed to bring a point charge $q$ from infinity to a height $d$ above a grounded conducting plane. Then explain why the result is half the energy of the pair $+q$ and $-q$ separated by $2d$.
hint: At height $z$ the force on $q$ is the attraction to its image at distance $2z$. The external agent must supply the opposite force.
hint: Integrate the external force from $z=\infty$ down to $z=d$.
solution: At height $z$ the attractive force is $\frac{q^2}{16\pi\epsilon_0z^2}$ toward the plane, so the external force is $+\frac{q^2}{16\pi\epsilon_0z^2}\hat{\mathbf z}$. Then $W=\int_\infty^d\frac{q^2}{16\pi\epsilon_0z^2}dz=-\frac{q^2}{16\pi\epsilon_0d}$. The two-charge system has energy $-\frac{q^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0(2d)}=-\frac{q^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0d}$. The real field exists only above the plane, and by reflection symmetry that half-space holds half of the field energy of the pair, so the real work is half. Treating the image as a real charge that also moves would double count.
rubric: Uses the force from the image with separation $2z$ and sign convention for the external force.
rubric: Integrates correctly from infinity to $d$ and gets $-q^2/(16\pi\epsilon_0d)$.
rubric: Computes the two-charge energy $-q^2/(8\pi\epsilon_0d)$.
rubric: Explains the factor of one half by the field existing in one half-space only.

@quiz num
prompt: A point charge $q$ sits at $(d,d)$ in the region $x>0$, $y>0$ bounded by two grounded conducting half-planes $x=0$ and $y=0$ meeting at a right angle. Find the magnitude of the force on $q$ in units of $q^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0d^2)$.
answer: sqrt(2)/4 - 1/8
check: norm(sum(s*np.array([1 - px, 1 - py])/((1 - px)**2 + (1 - py)**2)**1.5 for s, px, py in [(-1, -1, 1), (-1, 1, -1), (1, -1, -1)]))
hint: Reflect $q$ across each plane in turn, then reflect the images across the other plane. How many images are there and what are their signs?
hint: The images are $-q$ at $(-d,d)$, $-q$ at $(d,-d)$ and $+q$ at $(-d,-d)$. Add the three Coulomb forces as vectors.
solution: The two nearby images each attract with magnitude $1/4$ (separation $2d$), giving components $(-\tfrac14,0)$ and $(0,-\tfrac14)$. The far image at $(-d,-d)$ repels with magnitude $\frac{1}{8}$ directed along $(1,1)/\sqrt2$, giving $\frac{1}{8\sqrt2}$ in each component. The net is $\big(-\tfrac14+\tfrac{1}{8\sqrt2}\big)(1,1)$, whose magnitude is $\sqrt2\big(\tfrac14-\tfrac{1}{8\sqrt2}\big)=\tfrac{\sqrt2}{4}-\tfrac18\approx0.2286$, directed toward the corner.
