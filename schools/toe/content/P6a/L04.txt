@article
Week 3 gave us a differential equation. Before solving it in clever ways, we need to know that a solution found by a trick (a guess, a symmetry argument, an image charge) is the only one. The **uniqueness theorem** says it is, provided the boundary data are complete.

### Statement

Let $\mathcal V$ be a volume with boundary $S$ containing a fixed charge density $\rho$. Poisson's equation $\nabla^2V=-\rho/\epsilon_0$ has at most one solution when we specify

- **Dirichlet data**: $V$ at every point of $S$; or
- **Neumann data**: $\partial V/\partial n$ (that is, $-E_n$) at every point of $S$, in which case $V$ is unique up to an additive constant; or
- **mixed data**: $V$ on part of $S$ and $\partial V/\partial n$ on the rest.

For conductors the data are either the potential of each conductor, or the total charge on each conductor, together with the condition at infinity (for example $V\to0$).

### Proof: the difference of two solutions

Suppose $V_1$ and $V_2$ both solve the problem and let $W=V_1-V_2$. Then $\nabla^2W=0$ in $\mathcal V$, and $W=0$ (or $\partial W/\partial n=0$) on the parts of $S$ where data are given. The identity $\nabla\cdot(W\nabla W)=|\nabla W|^2+W\nabla^2W=|\nabla W|^2$ and the divergence theorem give

$$\int_{\mathcal V}|\nabla W|^2\,d\tau=\oint_SW\,\nabla W\cdot d\mathbf A=0.$$

A continuous non-negative integrand with zero integral vanishes everywhere, so $\nabla W=\mathbf 0$ and $W$ is constant: zero for Dirichlet data, an arbitrary constant for Neumann data.

### Why it matters

- A function that satisfies the equation **and** all the boundary data is the solution, however you found it. This justifies guessing, symmetry arguments and the method of images in Week 5.
- Inside a closed grounded conductor with no charge, $V=0$ throughout: electrostatic shielding.
- Uniqueness is about the potential in the region, not about the sources. A point charge and a uniformly charged sphere of the same total charge give the same potential outside the sphere.

### A checklist for boundary data

1. Name the region and say what charge it contains.
2. List every boundary surface, including infinity, and say what is known on each.
3. Check the data cover the whole boundary exactly once: nowhere missing, and not both $V$ and $\partial V/\partial n$ imposed independently on the same patch.
4. Verify that the candidate satisfies the equation and every item of data.

@example
title: Why two solutions cannot differ
problem: Two functions $V_1$ and $V_2$ both satisfy $\nabla^2V=-\rho/\epsilon_0$ inside a region and both equal the same given values on its boundary $S$. Show that $V_1=V_2$.
step: Define $W=V_1-V_2$. By linearity $\nabla^2W=-\rho/\epsilon_0+\rho/\epsilon_0=0$ inside the region, and $W=0$ on $S$.
step: Expand $\nabla\cdot(W\nabla W)=\nabla W\cdot\nabla W+W\nabla^2W$. The second term vanishes, leaving $|\nabla W|^2$.
step: Integrate over the region and apply the divergence theorem: $\int|\nabla W|^2\,d\tau=\oint_SW\,\nabla W\cdot d\mathbf A$. Since $W=0$ on $S$, the right side is zero.
step: The integrand $|\nabla W|^2$ is continuous and non-negative with zero integral, so $\nabla W=\mathbf 0$ everywhere and $W$ is constant. Because $W=0$ on $S$, that constant is $0$, so $V_1=V_2$.

@example
title: Accepting a guessed solution
problem: A conducting sphere of radius $a$ is held at potential $V_0$ in empty space, with $V\to0$ at infinity. Show that $V=V_0\,a/r$ for $r>a$ is the potential, and find the charge on the sphere.
step: State the data: region $r>a$ with no charge; Dirichlet data $V=V_0$ on $r=a$ and $V\to0$ as $r\to\infty$.
step: Verify the candidate. $\nabla^2(1/r)=\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\big(r^2\cdot(-1/r^2)\big)=0$ for $r>a$, $V(a)=V_0$, and $V\to0$ at infinity.
step: By uniqueness this is the potential, and no other function can satisfy the same conditions.
step: The field is $E_r=V_0a/r^2$, so at the surface $\sigma=\epsilon_0V_0/a$ and $Q=4\pi a^2\sigma=4\pi\epsilon_0aV_0$.

@mistakes
- Specifying both $V$ and $\partial V/\partial n$ independently on the same patch of boundary; the problem is then over-determined and usually has no solution.
- Leaving part of the boundary unspecified, for example forgetting the condition at infinity.
- Reading uniqueness as "the charges are unique": it only says the potential in the region is.
- Accepting a candidate that satisfies Laplace's equation but matches the boundary data only approximately.

prompt: A region contains a known fixed charge density. Which of these boundary data on the entire boundary surface are enough to determine $V$ uniquely (up to an additive constant at most)?
choice*: The value of $V$ at every boundary point.
choice*: The normal derivative $\partial V/\partial n$ at every boundary point.
choice*: The value of $V$ on one part of the boundary and $\partial V/\partial n$ on the rest.
choice: The value of $V$ on only half of the boundary, with nothing specified on the other half.
hint: In the proof, the surface term $\oint W\,\nabla W\cdot d\mathbf A$ must vanish at every point of the boundary.
hint: At each boundary point, either $W=0$ or $\partial W/\partial n=0$ makes the integrand vanish; check which options guarantee this everywhere.
solution: The surface term vanishes if, at each point, either $W=0$ or $\partial W/\partial n=0$. This holds for full Dirichlet, full Neumann, or a mixture that covers every point exactly once. Leaving half the boundary unspecified leaves $W$ free there, so uniqueness is not guaranteed.

@practice num
prompt: A conducting sphere of radius $a$ carries charge $+Q$ and is surrounded by a concentric thin conducting shell of radius $2a$. By symmetry the field between them is radial, $E_r=Q/(4\pi\epsilon_0r^2)$. Find $V(a)-V(2a)$ in units of $Q/(4\pi\epsilon_0a)$.
answer: 1/2
check: quad(lambda r: 1/r**2, 1, 2)
hint: $V(a)-V(2a)=\int_a^{2a}E_r\,dr$.
hint: Write $r=au$ so the integral becomes $\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0a}\int_1^2u^{-2}\,du$.
solution: $\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_a^{2a}\frac{dr}{r^2}=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\Big(\frac1a-\frac1{2a}\Big)=\frac12\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0a}$. By uniqueness (the total charge on each conductor is given), this radial field is the field.

choice: Charges outside the shell exert no force on anything inside, because forces cannot pass through matter.

@practice self
prompt: A point charge $q$ sits at the centre of a grounded spherical conducting shell of inner radius $R$. (a) State precisely the region, the equation and the boundary data that make this problem well posed. (b) Verify that $V=\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\big(\frac1r-\frac1R\big)$ satisfies them. (c) Explain why no other function can.
hint: Inside the shell the potential obeys $\nabla^2V=-q\,\delta^3(\mathbf r)/\epsilon_0$ with $V=0$ at $r=R$.
hint: Use $\nabla^2(1/r)=-4\pi\delta^3(\mathbf r)$ and the fact that a constant is harmonic. For (c), consider the difference of two candidates, in which the point charge singularity cancels.
solution: (a) Region $0<r<R$ (or the whole ball with the point source); equation $\nabla^2V=-q\,\delta^3(\mathbf r)/\epsilon_0$; Dirichlet data $V(R)=0$. (b) $\nabla^2\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0r}=-\frac{q}{\epsilon_0}\delta^3(\mathbf r)$ and the constant term has zero Laplacian, so the equation holds; at $r=R$ the bracket is $\frac1R-\frac1R=0$. (c) If $V_1$ and $V_2$ both satisfy these conditions, $W=V_1-V_2$ has no source, so it is harmonic throughout the ball and vanishes on $r=R$. Then $\int|\nabla W|^2d\tau=\oint W\nabla W\cdot d\mathbf A=0$, so $W$ is constant and equal to $0$.
rubric: States the region, the Poisson equation with the point source, and the Dirichlet condition at $r=R$.
rubric: Checks the equation (using $\nabla^2(1/r)=-4\pi\delta^3$) and the boundary value.
rubric: Forms the difference $W$ of two solutions and notes it is source-free and zero on the boundary.
rubric: Completes the argument with $\int|\nabla W|^2d\tau=0$ and concludes $W\equiv0$.

@quiz ms
prompt: Which of these statements follow from the uniqueness theorem?
choice*: A function that satisfies Poisson's equation in a region and takes the prescribed values on the whole boundary is the potential, however it was found.
choice*: Inside a closed grounded conducting shell containing no charge, $V=0$ everywhere inside.
choice: A solution exists for any boundary data one cares to specify.
choice: Two different charge distributions can never produce the same potential outside a given surface.
hint: Uniqueness says there is at most one solution, nothing about existence, and it is about the potential inside the region considered.
hint: Test the claim that two different charge distributions can never produce the same potential outside a surface, using a point charge and a uniformly charged sphere of the same total charge.
solution: The claims that a function meeting Poisson's equation and the prescribed boundary values is the potential, and that $V=0$ inside an empty closed grounded shell, are direct consequences. Uniqueness does not prove existence, and the claim that different charge distributions never give the same potential outside a surface is false: a point charge and a uniform sphere with the same total charge give the same potential outside the sphere.
