@article
Steady currents produce magnetic fields that circle around them. In magnetostatics every current is steady, so $\nabla\cdot\mathbf J = 0$ and nothing changes in time. In SI units the field obeys

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf B = 0, \qquad \nabla\times\mathbf B = \mu_0\mathbf J,$$

with $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$ T m/A. The first equation says there are no magnetic monopoles: the flux through any closed surface is zero. The second has the integral form of **Ampère's law**, $\oint\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf l = \mu_0 I_{\text{enc}}$, where the circulation direction and the sign of $I_{\text{enc}}$ are linked by the right-hand rule.

### Biot–Savart law

The direct way to find $\mathbf B$ is to add up the contribution of every current element. For a wire carrying current $I$, let $\mathbf u = \mathbf r - \mathbf r'$ point from the source element at $\mathbf r'$ to the field point:

$$\mathbf B(\mathbf r) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi}\oint\frac{d\mathbf l'\times\mathbf u}{u^3}.$$

Three results are worth knowing by heart:

- An infinitely long straight wire: $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi s}$ at perpendicular distance $s$, circling the wire.
- A straight segment: $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi s}(\sin\theta_2 - \sin\theta_1)$, where $\theta_1,\theta_2$ are the angles of the two ends measured from the perpendicular to the wire, positive in the direction of the current.
- A circular loop of radius $R$, on its axis: $B_z = \dfrac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}}$.

### Ampère's law with symmetry

Ampère's law is always true, but it determines $\mathbf B$ only when symmetry lets you pull $B$ out of the integral. Choose an Amperian loop along which $B$ is constant and tangent, then solve. A long solenoid with $n$ turns per unit length has $B = \mu_0 nI$ inside and $0$ outside. A toroid with $N$ turns has $B = \mu_0 NI/(2\pi s)$ inside its core.

### Boundary conditions

Apply $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B = 0$ to a thin pillbox and Ampère's law to a thin loop straddling a surface current $\mathbf K$ (current per unit length). With $\hat{\mathbf n}$ pointing from side 1 to side 2,

$$B_{2\perp} = B_{1\perp}, \qquad \mathbf B_2 - \mathbf B_1 = \mu_0\,\mathbf K\times\hat{\mathbf n}.$$

For an infinite sheet, symmetry gives $\mathbf B_2 = -\mathbf B_1$, so $|\mathbf B| = \mu_0K/2$ on each side.

### Force between wires

A current element feels $d\mathbf F = I\,d\mathbf l\times\mathbf B$. Two long parallel wires a distance $d$ apart carrying $I_1$ and $I_2$ exert on each other a force per unit length $\mu_0I_1I_2/(2\pi d)$, attractive when the currents are parallel.

@example
title: Field on the axis of a circular loop
problem: A circular loop of radius $R = 5.0$ cm carries $I = 2.0$ A. Use the Biot–Savart law to find the field on its axis at $z = 12$ cm from the center.
step: Put the loop in the $xy$-plane. A source element is $d\mathbf l' = R\,d\phi\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}$, and the vector from it to the axis point is $\mathbf u = -R\hat{\mathbf s} + z\hat{\mathbf z}$ with $u = \sqrt{R^2+z^2}$.
step: Take the cross product: $d\mathbf l'\times\mathbf u = R\,d\phi\,(R\hat{\mathbf z} + z\hat{\mathbf s})$.
step: The $\hat{\mathbf s}$ parts cancel between the elements at $\phi$ and $\phi+\pi$. Only the $z$-part survives: $B_z = \dfrac{\mu_0I}{4\pi}\dfrac{R^2}{u^3}\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}d\phi = \dfrac{\mu_0IR^2}{2(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}}$.
step: Numbers: $R^2 + z^2 = (0.050)^2 + (0.120)^2 = 0.0169$ m², so $u = 0.130$ m and $u^3 = 2.197\times10^{-3}$ m³.
step: $B_z = \dfrac{(4\pi\times10^{-7})(2.0)(0.050)^2}{2(2.197\times10^{-3})} \approx 1.43\times10^{-6}$ T, along $+z$ when the current circulates counterclockwise seen from $+z$.

@example
title: Field inside a thick wire
problem: A long straight wire of radius $a = 2.0$ mm carries $I = 10$ A spread uniformly over its cross-section. Use Ampère's law to find $B$ at $s = 1.0$ mm from the axis.
step: By symmetry $\mathbf B = B(s)\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}$. Take a circle of radius $s$ about the axis as the Amperian loop: $\oint\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf l = 2\pi sB$.
step: The current density is $J = I/(\pi a^2)$, so for $s < a$ the loop encloses $I_{\text{enc}} = J\pi s^2 = Is^2/a^2$.
step: Ampère's law gives $2\pi sB = \mu_0Is^2/a^2$, so $B = \dfrac{\mu_0Is}{2\pi a^2}$ for $s<a$. For $s>a$ the whole current is enclosed and $B = \dfrac{\mu_0I}{2\pi s}$.
step: Numbers: $B = (2\times10^{-7})(10)(1.0\times10^{-3})/(2.0\times10^{-3})^2 = 5.0\times10^{-4}$ T.
step: Check: at $s = a$ both formulas give $\mu_0I/(2\pi a)$, so $B$ is continuous across the wire's surface, as it must be when there is no surface current.

@mistakes
- Using Ampère's law to "find" $B$ for a finite wire or a single loop. The law holds there too, but without symmetry $B$ cannot be pulled out of the integral; use Biot–Savart.
- Counting the whole current when the Amperian loop lies inside the conductor; only the fraction $Is^2/a^2$ is enclosed for $s<a$.
- Adding the Biot–Savart contributions as scalars. The direction of $d\mathbf l'\times\mathbf u$ varies, and components such as the radial part on a loop's axis cancel.

@practice mc
prompt: Two long, straight, parallel wires a distance $d$ apart carry currents $I_1$ and $I_2$ in the same direction. What is the force between them?
choice: They repel, with force per unit length $\mu_0I_1I_2/(2\pi d)$, like charges.
choice*: They attract, with force per unit length $\mu_0I_1I_2/(2\pi d)$.
choice: They attract, with force per unit length $\mu_0I_1I_2/(4\pi d)$.
choice: There is no force, because the field of each wire is perpendicular to the current in the other.
hint: Find the field of wire 1 at the position of wire 2 first, then use $d\mathbf F = I_2\,d\mathbf l\times\mathbf B_1$.
solution: Wire 1 produces $B_1 = \mu_0I_1/(2\pi d)$ at wire 2, perpendicular to $I_2$. The force per unit length on wire 2 is $I_2B_1 = \mu_0I_1I_2/(2\pi d)$, and the right-hand rule shows it points toward wire 1, so parallel currents attract. A field perpendicular to the current gives the largest force, not zero.

@practice self
prompt: A surface current density $\mathbf K$ (current per unit length) flows on a surface with unit normal $\hat{\mathbf n}$ pointing from side 1 to side 2. Use a thin pillbox and a thin rectangular Amperian loop straddling the surface to justify $B_{2\perp} = B_{1\perp}$ and $\mathbf B_{2\parallel} - \mathbf B_{1\parallel} = \mu_0\,\mathbf K\times\hat{\mathbf n}$. Then apply them to the wall of a long solenoid, where $K = nI$.
hint: For the pillbox use $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B = 0$ in integral form and let the height shrink to zero.
hint: For the loop, take two long sides of length $\ell$ parallel to the surface along a unit tangent $\hat{\mathbf t}$, one on each side, and let the other two sides shrink to zero.
solution: Pillbox: $\oint\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf a = 0$. With faces parallel to the surface and height going to zero, only the two faces contribute, so $B_{2\perp} - B_{1\perp} = 0$. Loop: orient it so its normal is $\hat{\mathbf n}\times\hat{\mathbf t}$. Then the circulation tends to $\ell\,\hat{\mathbf t}\cdot(\mathbf B_2 - \mathbf B_1)$ and the enclosed current is $\ell\,\mathbf K\cdot(\hat{\mathbf n}\times\hat{\mathbf t}) = \ell\,\hat{\mathbf t}\cdot(\mathbf K\times\hat{\mathbf n})$. Ampère's law gives $\hat{\mathbf t}\cdot(\mathbf B_2 - \mathbf B_1) = \mu_0\hat{\mathbf t}\cdot(\mathbf K\times\hat{\mathbf n})$ for every tangent $\hat{\mathbf t}$, hence $\mathbf B_{2\parallel} - \mathbf B_{1\parallel} = \mu_0\mathbf K\times\hat{\mathbf n}$. Solenoid: take side 1 inside and side 2 outside, so $\hat{\mathbf n} = \hat{\mathbf s}$ and $\mathbf K = nI\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}$. Then $\mu_0\mathbf K\times\hat{\mathbf n} = \mu_0nI\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\times\hat{\mathbf s} = -\mu_0nI\,\hat{\mathbf z}$. With $\mathbf B = \mathbf 0$ outside, the field inside is $\mu_0nI\,\hat{\mathbf z}$.
rubric: Uses a pillbox with $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B = 0$ to show the normal component is continuous.
rubric: Uses a thin loop with Ampère's law and identifies the circulation as $\ell\,\hat{\mathbf t}\cdot(\mathbf B_2 - \mathbf B_1)$.
rubric: Evaluates the enclosed current in terms of $\mathbf K$ and $\hat{\mathbf n}\times\hat{\mathbf t}$ and obtains the jump $\mu_0\mathbf K\times\hat{\mathbf n}$.
rubric: Applies the result to the solenoid wall and recovers $B = \mu_0 nI$ inside with zero outside.

@practice num
prompt: A square loop of side $0.20$ m carries $I = 4.0$ A. Using the result for a finite straight segment, find the magnitude of $\mathbf B$ at the center of the loop. Take $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$ T m/A.
answer: 2.26e-5
check: 4*quad(lambda l: 1e-7*4.0*0.1/(0.1**2 + l**2)**1.5, -0.1, 0.1)
unit: T
tol: 1%
hint: Each side is a straight segment at perpendicular distance $s = 0.10$ m from the center, extending $0.10$ m to either side of the foot of the perpendicular.
hint: At each end $\sin\theta = 0.10/\sqrt{0.10^2 + 0.10^2}$, so one side gives $\dfrac{\mu_0I}{4\pi s}\cdot2\sin\theta$. All four sides produce fields in the same direction.
solution: Here $\sin\theta = 1/\sqrt2$, so one side gives $B = \dfrac{\mu_0I}{4\pi s}\cdot\dfrac{2}{\sqrt2} = \dfrac{(10^{-7})(4.0)}{0.10}\sqrt2 \approx 5.66\times10^{-6}$ T. The four sides add, so $B = 4(5.66\times10^{-6}) \approx 2.26\times10^{-5}$ T.

@quiz num
prompt: A toroid has $N = 400$ turns and carries $I = 1.5$ A. Find the field in its core at a distance $s = 8.0$ cm from the symmetry axis. Take $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$ T m/A.
answer: 1.5e-3
check: 4*pi*1e-7*400*1.5/(2*pi*0.08)
unit: T
tol: 1%

@quiz ms
prompt: An infinite sheet in the plane $z = 0$ carries a uniform surface current $\mathbf K = (3.0\ \text{A/m})\,\hat{\mathbf y}$. Select all statements that are correct.
choice*: Just above the sheet, $\mathbf B$ points along $+\hat{\mathbf x}$.
choice*: $|\mathbf B| = \mu_0K/2$ on both sides, independent of the distance from the sheet.
choice: The tangential component of $\mathbf B$ is continuous across the sheet.
choice: $\mathbf B$ falls off like $1/|z|$ away from the sheet.
hint: Use $\mathbf B_2 - \mathbf B_1 = \mu_0\mathbf K\times\hat{\mathbf n}$ with $\hat{\mathbf n} = \hat{\mathbf z}$, and the symmetry $\mathbf B_{\text{above}} = -\mathbf B_{\text{below}}$.
solution: With $\hat{\mathbf n} = \hat{\mathbf z}$, $\mu_0\mathbf K\times\hat{\mathbf n} = \mu_0K\,\hat{\mathbf y}\times\hat{\mathbf z} = \mu_0K\,\hat{\mathbf x}$. Symmetry makes $\mathbf B_{\text{above}} = -\mathbf B_{\text{below}}$, so $\mathbf B_{\text{above}} = +\tfrac12\mu_0K\hat{\mathbf x}$ and $\mathbf B_{\text{below}} = -\tfrac12\mu_0K\hat{\mathbf x}$. The magnitude is uniform. The tangential component jumps by $\mu_0K$, and an infinite sheet's field does not decay with distance.
