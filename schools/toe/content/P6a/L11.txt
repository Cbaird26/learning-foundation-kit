@article
Atoms are tiny current loops, from electron orbits and spins. In most materials the loops point randomly and cancel, but an applied field, or the material's own ordering, can line them up. The result is described by the **magnetization** $\mathbf M$, the magnetic dipole moment per unit volume (units A/m). In **diamagnets** the induced loops oppose the applied field (a tiny effect), in **paramagnets** they align with it (a small effect), and in **ferromagnets** the response is strong, nonlinear and shows hysteresis.

### Bound currents

A magnetized object produces exactly the field of two **bound current** distributions,

$$\mathbf J_b = \nabla\times\mathbf M, \qquad \mathbf K_b = \mathbf M\times\hat{\mathbf n},$$

with $\hat{\mathbf n}$ the outward normal. A uniformly magnetized object has $\mathbf J_b = \mathbf 0$ and only a surface current, like the wall of a solenoid. Its dipole moment is $\mathbf m = \int\mathbf M\,d\tau$.

### The field H

The total current is $\mathbf J_f + \mathbf J_b$, so $\nabla\times\mathbf B = \mu_0(\mathbf J_f + \nabla\times\mathbf M)$. Moving the magnetization term to the left defines

$$\mathbf H = \frac{\mathbf B}{\mu_0} - \mathbf M, \qquad \nabla\times\mathbf H = \mathbf J_f, \qquad \oint\mathbf H\cdot d\mathbf l = I_{f,\text{enc}}.$$

The curl of $\mathbf H$ involves free current only, but its divergence $\nabla\cdot\mathbf H = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf M$ need not vanish, so you can find $\mathbf H$ from $\mathbf J_f$ alone only when symmetry allows. $\mathbf B$ always obeys $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B = 0$ and is the field that exerts forces. For **linear** materials,

$$\mathbf M = \chi_m\mathbf H, \qquad \mathbf B = \mu\mathbf H, \qquad \mu = \mu_0\mu_r, \qquad \mu_r = 1 + \chi_m.$$

### Interface conditions

With $\hat{\mathbf n}$ pointing from medium 1 to medium 2, the pillbox and loop arguments from the magnetostatics lesson give

$$B_{2\perp} = B_{1\perp}, \qquad \mathbf H_{2\parallel} - \mathbf H_{1\parallel} = \mathbf K_f\times\hat{\mathbf n}.$$

With no free surface current, $B_\perp$ and $\mathbf H_\parallel$ are continuous, just as $D_\perp$ and $\mathbf E_\parallel$ were for dielectrics. These rules solve magnetic circuits: in a core with a narrow air gap, $B$ is the same in the core and the gap, while $H$ is far larger in the gap.

### A uniformly magnetized sphere

Take a sphere of radius $R$ with $\mathbf M = M\hat{\mathbf z}$. Its surface current is $\mathbf K_b = M\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}$. Matching the interior and exterior solutions gives a uniform interior field,

$$\mathbf B_{\text{in}} = \tfrac23\mu_0\mathbf M, \qquad \mathbf H_{\text{in}} = -\tfrac13\mathbf M,$$

and an exterior field that is exactly a dipole field with $\mathbf m = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}R^3\mathbf M$. Inside, $\mathbf H$ points against $\mathbf M$: the magnetic version of a depolarizing field.

@example
title: A uniformly magnetized sphere
problem: A sphere of radius $R = 2.0$ cm is uniformly magnetized, $\mathbf M = M\hat{\mathbf z}$ with $M = 8.0\times10^{5}$ A/m. Find its bound currents, dipole moment, interior field, and the field on the $z$-axis at $r = 0.20$ m. Take $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$ T m/A.
step: Uniform $\mathbf M$ gives $\mathbf J_b = \nabla\times\mathbf M = \mathbf 0$. The surface current is $\mathbf K_b = M\hat{\mathbf z}\times\hat{\mathbf r} = M\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}$, strongest at the equator.
step: The dipole moment is $m = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}R^3M = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}(0.020)^3(8.0\times10^{5}) \approx 26.8$ A m².
step: Inside, the field is uniform: $B = \tfrac23\mu_0M = \tfrac23(4\pi\times10^{-7})(8.0\times10^{5}) \approx 0.67$ T along $+z$, and $H = B/\mu_0 - M = -M/3$ points against $\mathbf M$.
step: Outside, the field is exactly a dipole field. On the axis, $B = \dfrac{\mu_0m}{2\pi r^3} = \dfrac{(2\times10^{-7})(26.8)}{(0.20)^3} \approx 6.7\times10^{-4}$ T.
step: Check continuity of $B_\perp$ at the north pole: just outside, $B_r = \mu_0m/(2\pi R^3) = \tfrac23\mu_0M$, equal to the interior value.

@example
title: A toroid with a magnetic core
problem: A toroid of $N = 500$ turns carries $I = 0.20$ A and is wound on a core with $\mu_r = 1500$. At the core's mean radius $s = 6.0$ cm, find $H$, $B$ and $M$, and compare $B$ with the value without the core.
step: Ampère's law for $\mathbf H$ counts only free current, which threads the loop $N$ times: $2\pi sH = NI$, so $H = \dfrac{NI}{2\pi s} = \dfrac{(500)(0.20)}{2\pi(0.060)} \approx 265$ A/m. The core does not change $H$.
step: In a linear material $B = \mu_r\mu_0H = 1500\,(4\pi\times10^{-7})(265) \approx 0.50$ T.
step: The magnetization is $M = B/\mu_0 - H = (\mu_r - 1)H \approx 3.98\times10^{5}$ A/m.
step: Without the core, $B_0 = \mu_0H \approx 3.3\times10^{-4}$ T. The core multiplies $B$ by $\mu_r = 1500$, so the bound currents supply about $99.9\%$ of the field.

@mistakes
- Treating $\mathbf H$ as the fundamental field. Forces on moving charges depend on $\mathbf B$; $\mathbf H$ is a bookkeeping field tied to free current.
- Writing $\mathbf B = \mu_0\mathbf H$ inside matter. The correct relation is $\mathbf B = \mu_0(\mathbf H + \mathbf M)$, or $\mathbf B = \mu\mathbf H$ for a linear material.
- Assuming $\mathbf H$ vanishes wherever there is no free current. Its curl vanishes, but $\nabla\cdot\mathbf H = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf M$ gives it sources at the surface of a magnet.
- Using $\mathbf M = \chi_m\mathbf H$ for a ferromagnet; the response is nonlinear and depends on history.

@practice mc
prompt: A permanent magnet is uniformly magnetized and there are no free currents anywhere. A student argues that $\oint\mathbf H\cdot d\mathbf l = 0$ for every loop, so $\mathbf H = \mathbf 0$ and therefore $\mathbf B = \mu_0\mathbf M$ inside. What is wrong?
choice*: Zero circulation only means $\nabla\times\mathbf H = \mathbf 0$. Since $\nabla\cdot\mathbf H = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf M \neq 0$ at the surface, $\mathbf H$ is nonzero (for a sphere $\mathbf H_{\text{in}} = -\mathbf M/3$).
choice: $\oint\mathbf H\cdot d\mathbf l$ must equal the bound current, not zero.
choice: Nothing is wrong; $\mathbf H = \mathbf 0$ and $\mathbf B = \mu_0\mathbf M$ hold inside every magnet.
choice: $\mathbf B$ should equal $\mu_0\mathbf H$, which is zero, so $\mathbf B$ vanishes inside.
hint: A field with zero curl everywhere is not necessarily zero. Ask what produces its divergence.
solution: From $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B = 0$ and $\mathbf B = \mu_0(\mathbf H + \mathbf M)$ we get $\nabla\cdot\mathbf H = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf M$, which is nonzero where $\mathbf M$ ends at the surface. So $\mathbf H$ is a curl-free but nonzero field. For a uniformly magnetized sphere $\mathbf H_{\text{in}} = -\mathbf M/3$ and $\mathbf B_{\text{in}} = \tfrac23\mu_0\mathbf M$, not $\mu_0\mathbf M$.

@practice num
prompt: A sphere of radius $3.0$ cm is uniformly magnetized with $M = 5.0\times10^{5}$ A/m. Find the magnitude of $\mathbf B$ at its center. Take $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$ T m/A.
answer: 0.419
check: quad(lambda t: 4*pi*1e-7*5.0e5*sin(t)*sin(t)**2/2, 0, pi)
unit: T
tol: 1%
hint: The surface current is $K_b = M\sin\theta$. A ring between $\theta$ and $\theta + d\theta$ carries $dI = M\sin\theta\,R\,d\theta$ and has radius $R\sin\theta$.
hint: Alternatively, use the uniform interior field of a uniformly magnetized sphere, which does not depend on $R$.
solution: The interior field is uniform, $B = \tfrac23\mu_0M = \tfrac23(4\pi\times10^{-7})(5.0\times10^{5}) \approx 0.419$ T, independent of $R$. The ring sum agrees: each ring at the center gives $\mu_0\,dI\,(R\sin\theta)^2/(2R^3)$, so $B = \tfrac{\mu_0M}{2}\int_0^\pi\sin^3\theta\,d\theta = \tfrac{\mu_0M}{2}\cdot\tfrac43$.

@practice self
prompt: Two linear magnetic materials with permeabilities $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$ meet at a flat interface with no free surface current. Use the interface conditions to show that field lines refract according to $\tan\theta_2/\tan\theta_1 = \mu_2/\mu_1$, with angles measured from the normal. What does this imply for field lines passing from air into a plate of high-permeability iron?
hint: Which components of $\mathbf B$ and $\mathbf H$ are continuous when $\mathbf K_f = \mathbf 0$?
hint: Write $H_\parallel = B_\parallel/\mu$, then divide the tangential relation by the normal one.
solution: With $\mathbf K_f = \mathbf 0$, $B_\perp$ and $\mathbf H_\parallel$ are continuous. In terms of the magnitudes $B_1$, $B_2$ and the angles from the normal, $B_1\cos\theta_1 = B_2\cos\theta_2$ and $B_1\sin\theta_1/\mu_1 = B_2\sin\theta_2/\mu_2$. Dividing the second by the first gives $\tan\theta_1/\mu_1 = \tan\theta_2/\mu_2$, so $\tan\theta_2/\tan\theta_1 = \mu_2/\mu_1$. From air to iron ($\mu_2\gg\mu_1$), $\tan\theta_2\gg\tan\theta_1$: inside the iron the lines run almost parallel to the surface, so iron channels flux, which is why it is used for cores and shields.
rubric: States that $B_\perp$ and $\mathbf H_\parallel$ are continuous when there is no free surface current.
rubric: Expresses both conditions with $B_1$, $B_2$, $\theta_1$, $\theta_2$ using $H_\parallel = B_\parallel/\mu$.
rubric: Divides the two relations to obtain $\tan\theta_2/\tan\theta_1 = \mu_2/\mu_1$.
rubric: Interprets the result: lines bend toward the surface in the high-permeability medium.

@practice num
prompt: A sphere of radius $R$ is uniformly magnetized with $\mathbf M = (5.0\times10^{5}\ \text{A/m})\,\hat{\mathbf z}$. Find $B_z$ at the equatorial point $(5R, 0, 0)$, in the plane $z = 0$ at distance $5R$ from the center. Take $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$ T m/A.
answer: -1.68e-3
check: 1e-7*5.0e5*quad2(lambda t, p: sin(t)**2*(sin(t) - 5*cos(p))/(26 - 10*sin(t)*cos(p))**1.5, 0, pi, 0, 2*pi)
unit: T
tol: 1%
hint: Outside the sphere the field is exactly that of a point dipole with $m = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}R^3M$ at the center.
hint: At the equator $\hat{\mathbf r}\perp\mathbf m$, so $\dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi r^3}[3(\mathbf m\cdot\hat{\mathbf r})\hat{\mathbf r} - \mathbf m]$ reduces to $-\dfrac{\mu_0\mathbf m}{4\pi r^3}$.
solution: With $m = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}R^3M$ and $r = 5R$, $B_z = -\dfrac{\mu_0m}{4\pi r^3} = -\dfrac{\mu_0M}{3\cdot125} = -\dfrac{(4\pi\times10^{-7})(5.0\times10^{5})}{375} \approx -1.68\times10^{-3}$ T. The field at the equator points against $\mathbf M$, like the return flux of any dipole.

@quiz num
prompt: A closed ring of iron with mean circumference $L = 0.40$ m and $\mu_r = 1000$ carries a winding of $N = 1000$ turns with $I = 0.50$ A. A narrow air gap of width $g = 2.0$ mm is cut across the ring. Neglecting fringing, find the field $B$ in the core. Take $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}$ T m/A.
answer: 0.262
check: lin([[1000*4*pi*1e-7, 0, -1], [0, 4*pi*1e-7, -1], [0.398, 0.002, 0]], [0, 0, 500])[2]
unit: T
tol: 1%
hint: $B_\perp$ is continuous, so the same $B$ threads the core and the gap: $H_{\text{core}} = B/(\mu_r\mu_0)$ and $H_{\text{gap}} = B/\mu_0$.
hint: Apply Ampère's law for $\mathbf H$ around the ring: $H_{\text{core}}(L - g) + H_{\text{gap}}\,g = NI$.
solution: Substituting, $\dfrac{B}{\mu_0}\left[\dfrac{L-g}{\mu_r} + g\right] = NI$, so $B = \dfrac{\mu_0NI}{g + (L-g)/\mu_r} = \dfrac{(4\pi\times10^{-7})(500)}{0.0020 + 0.000398} \approx 0.262$ T. The gap is only $0.5\%$ of the path but takes about $83\%$ of $\oint\mathbf H\cdot d\mathbf l$.

@quiz ms
prompt: A very long rod is uniformly magnetized along its axis, $\mathbf M = M\hat{\mathbf z}$, and there are no free currents. Select all statements that are correct far from the ends.
choice*: The bound volume current $\mathbf J_b$ is zero.
choice*: The field inside the rod is $B = \mu_0M$.
choice: $\mathbf H = \mathbf M$ inside the rod.
choice: There is no bound current anywhere, because there is no free current.
hint: The side of the rod carries $\mathbf K_b = \mathbf M\times\hat{\mathbf n}$. Compare it with the wall of a solenoid.
solution: Uniform $\mathbf M$ gives $\mathbf J_b = \nabla\times\mathbf M = \mathbf 0$, but the side surface carries $K_b = M$ circulating around the axis, like a solenoid with $nI = M$. So $B = \mu_0M$ inside, and $H = B/\mu_0 - M = 0$. Bound currents exist whether or not free currents do.
