@article
Push a child on a swing at the swing's natural rhythm and the amplitude builds up; push at a mismatched rhythm and it does not. That is **resonance**, and the mathematics is already in your hands.

### Undamped forcing

Take $y'' + \omega_0^2y = \cos\omega t$ with $\omega \neq \omega_0$. The trial $y_p = a\cos\omega t$ gives $(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)a = 1$, so
$$y_p = \frac{\cos\omega t}{\omega_0^2 - \omega^2}.$$
The amplitude $1/|\omega_0^2 - \omega^2|$ grows without limit as $\omega \to \omega_0$. Starting from rest, $y(0) = y'(0) = 0$, the solution is
$$y = \frac{\cos\omega t - \cos\omega_0 t}{\omega_0^2 - \omega^2} = \frac{2}{\omega_0^2 - \omega^2}\,\sin\frac{(\omega_0 - \omega)t}{2}\,\sin\frac{(\omega_0 + \omega)t}{2}.$$
A fast oscillation at the average frequency is multiplied by a slow envelope: these are **beats**.

### Pure resonance

When $\omega = \omega_0$, the forcing $\cos\omega_0 t$ is itself a homogeneous solution, so the trial $a\cos\omega_0 t$ gives $0 = \cos\omega_0 t$ and fails. The **modification rule**: multiply the trial by $t$ (by $t^2$ for a repeated root). Trying $y_p = t(a\cos\omega_0 t + b\sin\omega_0 t)$ gives
$$y_p = \frac{t}{2\omega_0}\sin\omega_0 t.$$
The envelope $t/(2\omega_0)$ grows linearly forever. The same rule applies to exponentials: for $y'' - 3y' + 2y = e^t$, where $e^t$ is a homogeneous solution, use $y_p = Ate^t$.

### Damped systems: finite but large

With damping, the transient dies and the steady response to $\cos\omega t$ stays finite. For $y'' + by' + ky = \cos\omega t$ (unit mass) the steady response is $A\cos(\omega t - \varphi)$ with
$$A(\omega) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(k - \omega^2)^2 + b^2\omega^2}}, \qquad \tan\varphi = \frac{b\omega}{k - \omega^2},$$
where $\varphi \in (0,\pi)$ is the phase lag. Maximizing $A$ is the same as minimizing the quantity under the root. Writing $u = \omega^2$, the minimum is at $u = k - b^2/2$:
$$\omega_r = \sqrt{k - \tfrac{b^2}{2}}, \qquad A_{\max} = \frac{1}{b\sqrt{k - b^2/4}}.$$
The peak exists only if $b^2 < 2k$, and it sits slightly *below* the natural frequency $\sqrt{k}$. Less damping gives a taller, narrower peak.

@example
title: Pure resonance from rest
problem: Solve $y'' + 4y = \cos 2t$ with $y(0) = 0$ and $y'(0) = 0$.
step: The natural frequency is $\omega_0 = 2$ and the forcing frequency is $2$, so $\cos 2t$ is a homogeneous solution. Use the modified trial $y_p = t(a\cos 2t + b\sin 2t)$.
step: Differentiate twice: $y_p'' = 2(-2a\sin 2t + 2b\cos 2t) + t(-4a\cos 2t - 4b\sin 2t)$. Then $y_p'' + 4y_p = -4a\sin 2t + 4b\cos 2t$; the terms containing $t$ cancel.
step: Match to $\cos 2t$: $4b = 1$ and $a = 0$. So $y_p = \frac{t}{4}\sin 2t$.
step: The general solution is $y = \frac t4\sin 2t + C_1\cos 2t + C_2\sin 2t$. Since $y_p(0) = 0$ and $y_p'(0) = 0$, the conditions give $C_1 = 0$ and $C_2 = 0$.
step: $y = \frac t4 \sin 2t$: oscillation at frequency $2$ inside the envelope $\pm t/4$, which grows without bound.

@example
title: The resonant peak of a damped oscillator
problem: For $y'' + 2y' + 10y = \cos\omega t$, find the frequency at which the steady-state amplitude peaks, and the peak amplitude.
step: The amplitude is $A = D^{-1/2}$ with $D(\omega) = (10 - \omega^2)^2 + 4\omega^2$. Maximizing $A$ means minimizing $D$.
step: Let $u = \omega^2$: $D = (10 - u)^2 + 4u$, and $dD/du = -2(10 - u) + 4 = 0$ gives $u = 8$.
step: So $\omega_r = \sqrt8 = 2\sqrt2 \approx 2.83$, which is below the natural frequency $\sqrt{10} \approx 3.16$. Here $b^2 = 4 < 2k = 20$, so the peak exists.
step: At $u = 8$: $D = 2^2 + 32 = 36$, so $A_{\max} = 1/6$. This matches $\dfrac{1}{b\sqrt{k - b^2/4}} = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt9}$.

@mistakes
- Using $\cos\omega t/(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)$ when $\omega = \omega_0$; the denominator is zero and the formula no longer applies.
- After multiplying by $t$, keeping only one trigonometric term; the trial is $t(a\cos\omega_0 t + b\sin\omega_0 t)$.
- Saying the damped peak occurs at the natural frequency; it occurs at $\sqrt{k - b^2/2}$, and not at all if $b^2 \ge 2k$.
- Believing damped resonance is unbounded; with damping the peak amplitude $1/(b\omega_d)$ is finite, though large when $b$ is small.

prompt: Solve $y'' + 16y = 8\cos 4t$ with $y(0) = 0$ and $y'(0) = 0$, and give $y(3\pi/8)$ as an exact value.
answer: -3pi/8
check: np.ravel(ivp(lambda t, y: [y[1], 8*cos(4*t) - 16*y[0]], [0, 0], 0, 3*pi/8))[0]
hint: The forcing frequency equals the natural frequency $\omega_0 = 4$, so use $y_p = t(a\cos 4t + b\sin 4t)$.
hint: The formula $y_p = \dfrac{t}{2\omega_0}F\sin\omega_0 t$ applies for forcing $F\cos\omega_0 t$; then use the zero initial conditions.
solution: The resonant response is $y_p = \frac{8t}{2\cdot 4}\sin 4t = t\sin 4t$. Since $y_p(0) = 0$ and $y_p'(0) = 0$, no homogeneous part is needed: $y = t\sin 4t$. Then $y(3\pi/8) = \frac{3\pi}{8}\sin\frac{3\pi}{2} = -\frac{3\pi}{8}$.
