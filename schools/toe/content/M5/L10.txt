@article
A **phase portrait** draws trajectories of $\mathbf{x}' = A\mathbf{x}$ in the $(x_1, x_2)$ plane. Time is hidden; arrows show the direction of motion. When $\det A \ne 0$ the origin is the only equilibrium, and the portrait near it is determined by the eigenvalues.

### The classification

| eigenvalues | type of origin | stability |
|---|---|---|
| real, both negative | node (sink) | asymptotically stable |
| real, both positive | node (source) | unstable |
| real, opposite signs | saddle | unstable |
| complex $\alpha \pm i\beta$, $\alpha < 0$ | spiral sink | asymptotically stable |
| complex $\alpha \pm i\beta$, $\alpha > 0$ | spiral source | unstable |
| purely imaginary $\pm i\beta$ | center | stable, not asymptotically |

**Asymptotically stable** means every nearby solution tends to the origin. At a center, solutions circle forever on closed curves: they stay near but never converge.

### Geometry of real eigenvalues

An eigenvector line is a trajectory: a solution that starts on the line along $\mathbf{v}_k$ stays on it and moves toward the origin if $\lambda_k < 0$, away if $\lambda_k > 0$. For a sink with $\lambda_1 = -1$ (slow) and $\lambda_2 = -3$ (fast), the solution is $c_1e^{-t}\mathbf{v}_1 + c_2e^{-3t}\mathbf{v}_2$. The fast term dies first, so trajectories approach the origin tangent to the **slow** eigenvector $\mathbf{v}_1$. For a saddle, only the two half-lines of the stable eigenvector reach the origin; every other trajectory is eventually swept out along the unstable direction.

### Spirals and centers: sense of rotation

For complex eigenvalues the eigenvalues give the rate of rotation and growth, but not the direction. To find the direction, compute the velocity at one test point such as $(1, 0)$. For $A = \begin{pmatrix}a & b\\ c & d\end{pmatrix}$ it is $A(1,0)^T = (a, c)$: if $c > 0$ the trajectory moves upward there, so the rotation is counterclockwise.

### Trace-determinant plane

Let $\tau = \operatorname{tr}A$ and $\Delta = \det A$, so that $\lambda = \frac12\left(\tau \pm \sqrt{\tau^2 - 4\Delta}\right)$.

- $\Delta < 0$: saddle.
- $\Delta > 0$ and $\tau^2 > 4\Delta$: node, a sink if $\tau < 0$ and a source if $\tau > 0$.
- $\Delta > 0$ and $\tau^2 < 4\Delta$: spiral, a sink if $\tau < 0$, a source if $\tau > 0$, and a center if $\tau = 0$.
- The parabola $\tau^2 = 4\Delta$ is the borderline with a repeated eigenvalue.
- $\Delta = 0$: a zero eigenvalue, so the equilibria form a line.

The origin is asymptotically stable exactly when $\tau < 0$ and $\Delta > 0$. For a damped oscillator $y'' + by' + cy = 0$ the matrix $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-c&-b\end{pmatrix}$ has $\tau = -b$ and $\Delta = c$, so overdamped, critically damped and underdamped are exactly node, parabola and spiral.

@example
title: An overdamped oscillator is a node
problem: Classify the origin for $A = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1\\ -2 & -3\end{pmatrix}$ and find the direction in which trajectories approach it.
step: $\tau = -3$ and $\Delta = 2$. The discriminant is $\tau^2 - 4\Delta = 9 - 8 = 1 > 0$, with $\Delta > 0$ and $\tau < 0$: a node that is a sink.
step: Eigenvalues: $\lambda = \frac12(-3 \pm 1) = -1$ and $-2$.
step: Eigenvector for $\lambda = -1$: $(A + I)\mathbf{v} = 0$ gives $v_1 + v_2 = 0$, so $\mathbf{v} = (1, -1)$. For $\lambda = -2$: $2v_1 + v_2 = 0$, so $(1, -2)$.
step: The slow direction is $\lambda = -1$. Almost every trajectory approaches the origin tangent to the line $x_2 = -x_1$.

@example
title: A center and its direction
problem: Classify the origin for $A = \begin{pmatrix}1 & -4\\ 2 & -1\end{pmatrix}$ and find the sense of rotation.
step: $\tau = 0$ and $\Delta = -1 + 8 = 7 > 0$. The eigenvalues are $\pm i\sqrt7$: a center.
step: Solutions are periodic with angular frequency $\sqrt7$, moving on closed ellipses (not circles, because $A$ is not a rotation matrix). They are stable but not asymptotically stable.
step: Test point $(1, 0)$: $A(1,0)^T = (1, 2)$, which points up and to the right. The motion is counterclockwise.

@mistakes
- Deciding by the trace alone: a negative trace with $\Delta < 0$ is still a saddle.
- Confusing a node with a spiral; look at the sign of $\tau^2 - 4\Delta$, not of $\tau$.
- Calling a center asymptotically stable; its orbits neither approach nor leave the origin.
- Guessing the sense of rotation instead of computing the velocity at a test point like $(1, 0)$.

@practice mc
prompt: Classify the origin for $\mathbf{x}' = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1\\ -4 & -2\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{x}$.
choice*: spiral sink
choice: node sink
choice: saddle
choice: center
hint: Compute the trace and the determinant, then the discriminant $\tau^2 - 4\Delta$.
hint: $\tau = -2$, $\Delta = 4$, so $\tau^2 - 4\Delta = 4 - 16$.
solution: $\tau^2 - 4\Delta = -12 < 0$, so the eigenvalues are complex, $\lambda = -1 \pm i\sqrt3$. The real part is negative, so the origin is a spiral sink.

@practice mc
prompt: Classify the origin for $\mathbf{x}' = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 2\\ 3 & 2\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{x}$.
choice*: saddle
choice: node source
choice: spiral source
choice: node sink
hint: The determinant alone can decide this one.
solution: $\Delta = 2 - 6 = -4 < 0$, so the eigenvalues are real with opposite signs: $\lambda = 4$ and $\lambda = -1$ (the trace is $3$, so $\lambda^2 - 3\lambda - 4 = 0$). The origin is a saddle.

@practice num
prompt: The damped oscillator $y'' + cy' + 4y = 0$ has the system matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1\\ -4 & -c\end{pmatrix}$. For which $c > 0$ does the origin change from a spiral sink to a node sink?
answer: 4
check: root(lambda c: np.trace(np.array([[0, 1], [-4, -c]]))**2 - 4*np.linalg.det(np.array([[0, 1], [-4, -c]])), 0.1, 10)
hint: A spiral has $\tau^2 - 4\Delta < 0$ and a node has $\tau^2 - 4\Delta > 0$. The change happens where it is zero.
hint: Here $\tau = -c$ and $\Delta = 4$.
solution: $\tau^2 - 4\Delta = c^2 - 16$, which is zero at $c = 4$. For $0 < c < 4$ the origin is a spiral sink (underdamped); for $c > 4$ it is a node sink (overdamped); $c = 4$ is critical damping.

choice*: The origin is a spiral sink.
choice*: Trajectories rotate clockwise.
choice*: Every solution tends to the origin as $t \to \infty$.
choice: The origin is a saddle.
choice: There are straight-line trajectories through the origin.
hint: Compute $\tau$, $\Delta$ and the velocity at the test point $(1, 0)$.
solution: $\tau = -2$ and $\Delta = 5$, so $\tau^2 - 4\Delta = -16 < 0$ with $\tau < 0$: a spiral sink, and all solutions tend to $0$. At $(1,0)$ the velocity is $(-1, -2)$, which points downward, so the rotation is clockwise. Straight-line trajectories need real eigenvectors, and the eigenvalues $-1 \pm 2i$ are not real.

@practice num
prompt: For $\mathbf{x}' = \begin{pmatrix}-4 & 1\\ 0 & -1\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{x}$, almost every trajectory approaches the origin tangent to a line through the origin with slope $m$. Find $m$.
answer: 3
check: (lambda w, v: v[1, np.argmax(w)]/v[0, np.argmax(w)])(*np.linalg.eig(np.array([[-4, 1], [0, -1]])))
hint: The eigenvalues are $-4$ and $-1$. Which one decays more slowly?
hint: Find an eigenvector for $\lambda = -1$: solve $(A + I)\mathbf{v} = 0$.
solution: The slow eigenvalue is $-1$. $(A + I)\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix}-3 & 1\\ 0 & 0\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{v} = 0$ gives $v_2 = 3v_1$, so $\mathbf{v} = (1, 3)$ and the slope is $m = 3$. The fast eigenvector $(1, 0)$ decays like $e^{-4t}$ and matters only far from the origin.
