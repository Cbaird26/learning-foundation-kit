@article
A function is **periodic** with period $2L$ if $f(x+2L)=f(x)$ for all $x$. The central idea of Fourier analysis is that such a function can be built from the simplest periodic pieces: sines and cosines whose periods divide $2L$. A musical note is a fundamental tone plus overtones; a Fourier series is the mathematical version of that statement.

### The series and its coefficients

The **Fourier series** of $f$ is
$$f(x)\sim\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(a_n\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}+b_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right),$$
with coefficients
$$a_n=\frac1L\int_{-L}^{L}f(x)\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx\quad(n\ge 0),\qquad b_n=\frac1L\int_{-L}^{L}f(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx\quad(n\ge 1).$$
The symbol $\sim$ means "has this series"; whether the series gives back $f$ is a separate question, answered below. The factor $\tfrac12$ on $a_0$ lets one formula cover $n=0$ as well, and then $\tfrac{a_0}{2}$ is just the **average value** of $f$ over a period. When $L=\pi$ the functions are simply $\cos nx$ and $\sin nx$ and the prefactor is $\frac1\pi$.

### Symmetry saves work

Recall that $f$ is **even** if $f(-x)=f(x)$ and **odd** if $f(-x)=-f(x)$. Cosine is even, sine is odd, and an odd function integrates to zero over $[-L,L]$. Hence:

- if $f$ is even, every $b_n=0$ and $a_n=\frac2L\int_0^L f(x)\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx$ (a pure cosine series);
- if $f$ is odd, every $a_n=0$ (including $a_0$) and $b_n=\frac2L\int_0^L f(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx$ (a pure sine series).

### What does the series converge to?

Call $f$ **piecewise smooth** if it has finitely many pieces, each with a continuous derivative, and finite one-sided limits at the breaks. For such $f$ the series converges at every point to
$$\frac{f(x^-)+f(x^+)}{2}.$$
At a point of continuity this is $f(x)$; at a jump it is the midpoint of the jump. Near a jump the partial sums overshoot by roughly $9\%$ of the jump size (the Gibbs phenomenon, studied in week 2).

### A first computation

Let $f(x)=x$ on $(-\pi,\pi)$, extended with period $2\pi$. It is odd, so $a_n=0$ and
$$b_n=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi x\sin nx\,dx=\frac2\pi\left[-\frac{x\cos nx}{n}+\frac{\sin nx}{n^2}\right]_0^\pi=\frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n}.$$
So
$$x\sim 2\left(\sin x-\frac{\sin 2x}{2}+\frac{\sin 3x}{3}-\cdots\right).$$
Put $x=\pi/2$: only odd $n$ survive and $\frac\pi2=2\left(1-\frac13+\frac15-\cdots\right)$, which is the classical alternating series for $\frac\pi4$. A formula about $\pi$ has fallen out of a function that looks nothing like a wave.

@example
title: The square wave
problem: Find the Fourier series of the square wave with $f(x)=-1$ for $-\pi<x<0$ and $f(x)=1$ for $0<x<\pi$, extended with period $2\pi$.
step: The function is odd, so every $a_n=0$ (including $a_0$), and $b_n=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \sin nx\,dx$.
step: Integrate: $\int_0^\pi\sin nx\,dx=\frac{1-\cos n\pi}{n}=\frac{1-(-1)^n}{n}$.
step: Therefore $b_n=\frac{2(1-(-1)^n)}{n\pi}$, which equals $\frac{4}{n\pi}$ for odd $n$ and $0$ for even $n$.
step: Assemble the series: $f(x)\sim\frac4\pi\left(\sin x+\frac{\sin 3x}{3}+\frac{\sin 5x}{5}+\cdots\right)$.
step: Check the jump at $x=0$: every sine term vanishes there, so the series gives $0=\frac{f(0^-)+f(0^+)}{2}$, the midpoint of the jump from $-1$ to $1$, exactly as the convergence statement predicts.

@mistakes
- Forgetting that the constant term of the series is $\frac{a_0}{2}$, not $a_0$ (the factor $\frac12$ in the series is what lets the formula for $a_n$ also hold at $n=0$).
- Using $\cos nx$ and $\sin nx$ when the period is $2L\ne 2\pi$: the correct functions are $\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}$ and $\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}$, with prefactor $\frac1L$.
- Writing "the series equals $f(x)$" at a jump; it equals the midpoint $\frac12[f(x^-)+f(x^+)]$.
- Using the shortcut $\frac2L\int_0^L$ for a function that is neither even nor odd.

@practice num
prompt: Let $f(x)=x^2$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$, extended with period $2\pi$. Compute $a_0=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\,dx$.
answer: 2*pi^2/3
check: quad(lambda x: x**2, -pi, pi)/pi
hint: $f$ is even, so $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}x^2\,dx=2\int_0^\pi x^2\,dx$.
hint: $\int_0^\pi x^2\,dx=\frac{\pi^3}{3}$; now double it and divide by $\pi$.
solution: $a_0=\frac1\pi\cdot\frac{2\pi^3}{3}=\frac{2\pi^2}{3}$. The constant term of the series is $\frac{a_0}{2}=\frac{\pi^2}{3}$, the average of $x^2$ over a period.

@practice num
prompt: For $f(x)=x^2$ on $(-\pi,\pi)$, compute $a_3=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}x^2\cos 3x\,dx$.
answer: -4/9
check: quad(lambda x: x**2*cos(3*x), -pi, pi)/pi
hint: $f$ is even, so $a_n=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi x^2\cos nx\,dx$. Integrate by parts twice.
hint: $\int x^2\cos nx\,dx=\frac{x^2\sin nx}{n}+\frac{2x\cos nx}{n^2}-\frac{2\sin nx}{n^3}$.
solution: At $x=\pi$ the antiderivative equals $\frac{2\pi(-1)^n}{n^2}$ and at $x=0$ it is $0$, so $a_n=\frac2\pi\cdot\frac{2\pi(-1)^n}{n^2}=\frac{4(-1)^n}{n^2}$. For $n=3$ this is $-\frac49$.

answer: 1/2 - cos(2x)/2
check: lambda x: sin(x)**2

hint: Solve that identity for $\sin^2x$.
solution: From $\cos2x=1-2\sin^2x$ we get $\sin^2x=\frac12-\frac12\cos2x$. So $\frac{a_0}{2}=\frac12$, $a_2=-\frac12$, and all other coefficients are zero, because the coefficients of a finite trigonometric sum are the Fourier coefficients (by orthogonality, week 2).

@practice num
prompt: Let $f(x)=x$ on $(-2,2)$, extended with period $4$, so $L=2$. Compute $b_1=\frac12\int_{-2}^{2}f(x)\sin\frac{\pi x}{2}\,dx$.
answer: 4/pi
check: quad(lambda x: x*sin(pi*x/2), -2, 2)/2
hint: $f$ is odd and $\sin\frac{\pi x}{2}$ is odd, so the integrand is even and $b_1=\int_0^2 x\sin\frac{\pi x}{2}\,dx$.
hint: Integrate by parts: $\int x\sin kx\,dx=-\frac{x\cos kx}{k}+\frac{\sin kx}{k^2}$ with $k=\frac\pi2$.
solution: With $k=\frac\pi2$ the antiderivative at $x=2$ is $-\frac{2\cos\pi}{\pi/2}+\frac{\sin\pi}{k^2}=\frac4\pi$, and at $x=0$ it is $0$. So $b_1=\frac4\pi$.

@quiz num
prompt: Let $f(x)=|x|$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$, extended with period $2\pi$. Compute the coefficient $a_1$.
answer: -4/pi
check: quad(lambda x: abs(x)*cos(x), -pi, pi)/pi
hint: $|x|$ is even, so $a_1=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi x\cos x\,dx$.
hint: $\int x\cos x\,dx=x\sin x+\cos x$.
solution: $\int_0^\pi x\cos x\,dx=[x\sin x+\cos x]_0^\pi=(-1)-(1)=-2$. So $a_1=\frac2\pi(-2)=-\frac4\pi$.

@quiz ms
prompt: Select all true statements about the Fourier series of a $2L$-periodic piecewise smooth function $f$.
choice*: The constant term $\frac{a_0}{2}$ equals the average value of $f$ over one period.
choice*: If $f$ is odd, then $a_0$ and every $a_n$ are zero.
choice: At a jump discontinuity the series converges to the value of $f$ at the jump point.
choice: The series always uses $\cos nx$ and $\sin nx$, whatever the period $2L$ is.
hint: Compare each statement with the formulas $a_n=\frac1L\int_{-L}^L f\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx$ and the convergence statement.
hint: Check what an odd integrand does over a symmetric interval.
solution: $\frac{a_0}{2}=\frac{1}{2L}\int_{-L}^{L}f\,dx$ is the average, and for odd $f$ every cosine integral has an odd integrand, so all $a_n$ (including $a_0$) vanish. At a jump the series gives the midpoint of the one-sided limits, and for period $2L$ the functions are $\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}$, $\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}$.
