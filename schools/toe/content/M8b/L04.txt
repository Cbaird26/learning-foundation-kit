@article
A blurry photograph, a moving average of a stock price, an echo in a hall: in each, the output at a point is a weighted average of the input near that point. That operation is **convolution**.

### Definition

$$(f*g)(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(y)\,g(x-y)\,dy.$$
Flip $g$, slide it so that it sits at $x$, multiply by $f$, and take the area. Convolution is commutative ($f*g=g*f$), associative, and linear in each factor. If $g\ge0$ and $\int g=1$, then $(f*g)(x)$ is a weighted average of $f$ with weights taken from $g$ around $x$.

### Smoothing and approximate identities

Convolution inherits the better behavior of its factors. Two unit boxes convolve to a continuous triangle; a jumpy $f$ convolved with a smooth $g$ is smooth. If $g_\varepsilon(x)=\frac1\varepsilon g\!\left(\frac x\varepsilon\right)$ is a tall, narrow bump of total area $1$, then $f*g_\varepsilon\to f$ as $\varepsilon\to0$: the bump acts as an **approximate identity**. The heat kernel (week 6) is exactly such a family.

### The convolution theorem

$$\widehat{f*g}(\xi)=\hat f(\xi)\,\hat g(\xi).$$
To see it, write $e^{-i\xi x}=e^{-i\xi y}\,e^{-i\xi(x-y)}$ in the double integral and substitute $z=x-y$; the integral splits into $\int f(y)e^{-i\xi y}dy\int g(z)e^{-i\xi z}dz$. So convolution in $x$ is multiplication in $\xi$. Dually, $\widehat{fg}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\hat f*\hat g$.

### Frequency content

Two consequences. First, a Gaussian $g$ has a Gaussian $\hat g$, tiny at high frequency, so convolving with it multiplies high-frequency components by tiny numbers: a **low-pass filter**. Second, since $\widehat{e^{i\omega x}f}(\xi)=\hat f(\xi-\omega)$, multiplying by a cosine moves the spectrum:
$$\widehat{f(x)\cos\omega x}(\xi)=\tfrac12\big[\hat f(\xi-\omega)+\hat f(\xi+\omega)\big].$$
A wave packet $e^{-x^2/2}\cos\omega x$ therefore has two Gaussian bumps in its spectrum, at $\xi=\pm\omega$. The carrier frequency $\omega$ sets the position of the bumps, and the width of the Gaussian envelope sets their width: a wider packet gives narrower bumps.

### Convolution on a computer

A grid version of the integral is a discrete sum times the spacing $\Delta x$:

```python
import numpy as np

dx = 0.01
x = np.linspace(-5, 5, 1001)               # grid with spacing dx
box = np.where(np.abs(x) < 0.5, 1.0, 0.0)  # unit box centred at 0
hat = np.convolve(box, box, mode="same") * dx
print(round(hat[500], 1), round(hat[550], 1))   # about 1.0 and 0.5
```

The result is the triangle that peaks at $1$ (at $x=0$) and falls linearly to $0$ at $x=\pm1$; index $550$ is $x=0.5$.

@example
title: Convolving two boxes
problem: Let $f=g=\chi_{[0,1]}$ (equal to $1$ on $[0,1]$ and $0$ elsewhere). Find $(f*g)(x)$ for all $x$.
step: $f(y)g(x-y)=1$ exactly when $0\le y\le1$ and $0\le x-y\le1$, that is, $y\in[\max(0,x-1),\min(1,x)]$. So $(f*g)(x)$ is the length of that interval.
step: For $0\le x\le1$ the interval is $[0,x]$, so $(f*g)(x)=x$.
step: For $1\le x\le2$ the interval is $[x-1,1]$, so $(f*g)(x)=2-x$. For $x<0$ or $x>2$ the interval is empty and the value is $0$.
step: The result is a triangle of height $1$ at $x=1$ and base $[0,2]$. It is continuous although $f$ and $g$ jump, and its transform $\hat f^{\,2}$ decays like $\frac{1}{\xi^2}$ instead of $\frac1\xi$.

@example
title: Frequency content of a wave packet
problem: Compute $\hat f(\xi)$ for $f(x)=e^{-x^2/2}\cos 5x$ and describe which frequencies $f$ contains.
step: Write $\cos5x=\frac12\left(e^{5ix}+e^{-5ix}\right)$, so $f=\frac12e^{5ix}e^{-x^2/2}+\frac12e^{-5ix}e^{-x^2/2}$.
step: The modulation rule $\widehat{e^{i\omega x}h}(\xi)=\hat h(\xi-\omega)$ with $\hat h(\xi)=\sqrt{2\pi}e^{-\xi^2/2}$ gives $\hat f(\xi)=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{2}\left[e^{-(\xi-5)^2/2}+e^{-(\xi+5)^2/2}\right]$.
step: Interpretation: the spectrum has two Gaussian bumps of width about $1$ centered at $\xi=\pm5$. The signal oscillates at angular frequency $5$ inside a Gaussian envelope; the bumps overlap negligibly because $5$ is much larger than their width.
step: A wider envelope such as $e^{-x^2/8}$ would give narrower bumps, still centered at $\pm5$.

@mistakes
- Forgetting to flip: $\int f(y)g(x-y)dy$ is not $\int f(y)g(y)dy$ and not $\int f(y)g(y-x)dy$.
- Writing $\widehat{f*g}=\hat f*\hat g$ (that is the transform of the product, up to $\frac1{2\pi}$).
- Expecting the support of $f*g$ to be the overlap of the supports; it is contained in their sum, so two boxes of length $1$ convolve to a triangle of base $2$.
- Forgetting the grid spacing $\Delta x$ when approximating a convolution integral by a discrete sum.

prompt: Let $f(x)=e^{-x^2/2}\cos 3x$. (a) Compute $\hat f(\xi)$. (b) Sketch or describe $|\hat f|$ and say what it tells you about the frequencies in $f$. (c) What would change in $\hat f$ if the envelope were $e^{-x^2/32}$ instead?
hint: Write $\cos3x$ as $\frac12(e^{3ix}+e^{-3ix})$ and use $\widehat{e^{i\omega x}h}(\xi)=\hat h(\xi-\omega)$.
hint: For (c), write the new envelope as $h(x/4)$ with $h(x)=e^{-x^2/2}$ and apply the scaling rule.
solution: (a) $\cos3x=\frac12(e^{3ix}+e^{-3ix})$, so by the modulation rule $\hat f(\xi)=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{2}\left[e^{-(\xi-3)^2/2}+e^{-(\xi+3)^2/2}\right]$. (b) $|\hat f|$ has two Gaussian bumps of width about $1$ centered at $\xi=\pm3$, almost non-overlapping, and each of height $\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}2\approx1.25$. So $f$ is essentially a single oscillation at angular frequency $3$ (period $\frac{2\pi}{3}$) with a smooth envelope; the other frequencies are absent. (c) With envelope $e^{-x^2/32}=h(x/4)$ the scaling rule gives $\hat h$ four times taller and four times narrower, $4\sqrt{2\pi}e^{-8\xi^2}$; the bumps stay centered at $\pm3$ but become much narrower, because a longer packet is closer to a pure tone.
