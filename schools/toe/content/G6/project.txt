You deliver executable code, environment instructions and a report that you can defend in a practical and oral review.
goal: Reproduce a benchmark quantity with a known reference value to a justified tolerance, with uncertainties that account for numerical error and, where relevant, statistical dependence.
goal: Demonstrate that the result is verified (order of accuracy, stability limit, invariants or residuals) and validated by a second, independent route.
goal: Deliver a package that a stranger can rerun in a clean environment, together with an honest statement of assumptions and limitations.
step: Choose one benchmark with a reference value you can cite from a textbook or article. Good choices are a Kepler orbit with eccentricity 0.6 over 1000 periods (energy, angular momentum, period), the 1D heat equation against its Fourier series, the 2D Ising model by Metropolis against the exact critical temperature $T_c=2/\ln(1+\sqrt2)$ in units of $J/k_B$, or a Poisson problem with a manufactured solution. Write down the target quantity, the reference, the tolerance and the model equations.
step: Implement the method as a script driven by a config file, with no magic numbers, random streams from one `SeedSequence`, and unit tests that use known answers.
step: Verify the code. Run a convergence study on at least three resolutions and compare the observed order with theory; derive and test the stability limit; check invariants or residuals; inspect any subtraction of nearly equal numbers for cancellation.
step: Validate independently. Obtain the same quantity by a second route (a different algorithm or library routine, an analytic limit or a published table) and compare, with uncertainties. For stochastic parts, estimate the integrated autocorrelation time, the effective sample size and a blocking plateau.
step: Probe robustness. Vary step size, tolerance, seed and system size; record the spread; identify each numerical artifact you meet (instability, drift, absorption, cancellation, finite-size effect) and how you recognized it.
step: Package the work: pinned environment file, one `run_all` command that regenerates every number and figure, a run record with commit, versions, seeds and parameters, and tests that pass with explicit tolerances.
step: Write the report (at most six pages): claim, method, verification evidence, validation evidence, results with uncertainties, assumptions and limitations, and the checks that failed or surprised you.
step: Prepare for the practical and oral review: rehearse rerunning a key result from a clean checkout, changing one parameter live, and explaining where each uncertainty comes from.
deliverable: Executable code, config, tests and environment instructions that regenerate all results in a clean environment with one command.
deliverable: A report of at most six pages with the reproduced result and its uncertainty, the verification and validation evidence, and a disclosure of assumptions and limitations.

@review
## Week 13 review: Graduate computational physics

### Week by week

**Weeks 1-2, floating point and complexity.** binary64 has $\varepsilon=2^{-52}$ and $u=2^{-53}\approx1.1\times10^{-16}$; each operation satisfies $fl(x\circ y)=(x\circ y)(1+\delta)$, $|\delta|\le u$. Beware absorption and cancellation; fix with algebra (`expm1`, `log1p`, conjugates) or `math.fsum`. Flop counts: matrix product $2n^3$, LU $\tfrac23n^3$, triangular solve $n^2$, FFT $n\log n$. Scaling exponent $k=\log_2[T(2n)/T(n)]$. Condition number $\kappa_f=|xf'/f|$ measures sensitivity independent of the algorithm.

**Weeks 3-4, linear solvers and eigenvalues.** $PA=LU$ with partial pivoting; never form $A^{-1}$. Residual versus error: $\|e\|/\|x\|\le\kappa\|r\|/\|b\|$. Jacobi converges iff $\rho(M)<1$; CG needs about $\sqrt\kappa$ iterations. Power iteration converges like $|\lambda_2/\lambda_1|^k$; the Rayleigh quotient is quadratically accurate; the eigenpair residual is the backward error; use `eigh` for symmetric matrices.

**Weeks 5-6, ODE integrators and symplectic methods.** Local error $O(h^{p+1})$, global error $O(h^p)$; observed order $\log_2(e_h/e_{h/2})$; `rtol` and `atol` control local error. Symplectic Euler and Verlet conserve a modified Hamiltonian, so the energy error is bounded with no drift; RK4 and explicit Euler drift. Measure both $\max|\Delta E|$ and the slope.

**Weeks 7-8, PDE discretization and stability.** Three-point stencil error $\tfrac{h^2}{12}u''''$; verify with manufactured solutions; Neumann conditions via ghost nodes. Explicit limits: Euler and Heun $z\ge-2$, RK4 $z\ge-2.785$. Von Neumann: FTCS heat needs $r\le\tfrac12$; upwind needs $0\le\nu\le1$; central-in-space advection with forward Euler is unstable. CFL is necessary, not sufficient. Predict, then bracket.

**Weeks 9-10, Monte Carlo and uncertainty.** $\mathrm{SE}=s/\sqrt N$; sample size scales as $1/\mathrm{SE}^2$. Seed with `default_rng`, spawn streams from `SeedSequence`. Inversion and Metropolis sampling. For correlated data $N_{\rm eff}=N/(2\tau_{\rm int})$ and the error is larger by $\sqrt{2\tau_{\rm int}}$; use blocking plateaus or block bootstrap.

**Weeks 11-12, inference and workflows.** Gaussian MLE is weighted least squares; use `lstsq`, not normal equations; $\operatorname{Cov}=\sigma^2(A^TA)^{-1}$; $\chi^2_\nu\approx1$; Gaussian posterior precisions add. Test pipelines on synthetic data over many seeds. Record commit, versions, seeds and parameters; compare floats with tolerances; disclose assumptions and limitations.

### Self-check

- I can predict the step-size limit of a scheme and show a run that brackets it.
- I can state which quantity (residual, error, energy, $\tau_{\rm int}$) a given check measures and what it cannot prove.
- I can produce a convergence table and read the observed order.
- I can give an error bar for correlated data and explain how I chose the block size.
- I can rerun my project from a clean checkout and say what I did not test.

### Common errors

- Trusting a small residual when $\kappa$ is large.
- Mixing up local and global error, or judging order from two resolutions.
- Reading a stable run as accurate, or smooth initial data as proof of stability.
- Using $s/\sqrt N$ for Markov chain output, or an i.i.d. bootstrap on correlated data.
- Reusing one seed for "independent" replicates.
- Comparing floats with `==`, or loosening tolerances until tests pass.

prompt: Weeks 4, 5, 7, 8. The heat equation $u_t=u_{xx}$ on $(0,1)$ with $u=0$ at both ends is discretized with $n=20$ intervals and the three-point stencil, giving $\dot{\mathbf u}=M\mathbf u$ with $M=\frac1{h^2}\,\text{tridiag}(1,-2,1)$ on the 19 interior nodes. Time stepping uses classical RK4. Its real-axis stability limit is the negative root $z^*$ of $|R(z)|=1$, where $R(z)=1+z+\tfrac{z^2}{2}+\tfrac{z^3}{6}+\tfrac{z^4}{24}$. How many steps of the largest stable size are needed to reach $t=0.1$? Round up to an integer.
answer: 58
check: math.ceil(0.1 / (abs(root(lambda z: abs(1 + z + z**2 / 2 + z**3 / 6 + z**4 / 24) - 1, -3.0, -2.5)) / max(abs(np.linalg.eigvalsh((np.eye(19, k=1) + np.eye(19, k=-1) - 2 * np.eye(19)) * 400)))))
hint: The most negative eigenvalue of $M$ is $-\frac{4}{h^2}\sin^2\frac{19\pi}{40}$ with $h=1/20$ (week 7).
hint: The stability condition is $\Delta t\,|\lambda_{\max}|\le|z^*|$ with $z^*\approx-2.785$ (week 8).
hint: The number of steps is $\lceil 0.1/\Delta t_{\max}\rceil$.
solution: With $1/h^2=400$, $|\lambda_{\max}|=1600\sin^2(19\pi/40)\approx1590.2$. The RK4 limit is $z^*\approx-2.7853$, so $\Delta t_{\max}=2.7853/1590.2\approx1.7516\times10^{-3}$. Then $0.1/\Delta t_{\max}\approx57.09$, which rounds up to $58$ steps. (Explicit Euler would need $\Delta t\le2/1590.2$ and $80$ steps.)

@exam num
prompt: Weeks 9, 10, 11. A Metropolis sampler targets the posterior of a parameter $\mu$ whose posterior standard deviation is $1$. The chain's autocorrelation function is $\rho_k=0.95^{k}$. How many chain steps (ignoring burn-in) are needed so that the standard error of the posterior-mean estimate is $0.005$? Give an integer.
answer: 1560000
check: (1 + 2 * sum(0.95**k for k in range(1, 3000))) / 0.005**2
tol: 1%
hint: The standard error of a correlated mean is $\sigma\sqrt{2\tau_{\rm int}/N}$.
hint: $2\tau_{\rm int}=1+2\sum_{k\ge1}0.95^k=1+2\cdot19$.
hint: Solve $\sigma^2\,(2\tau_{\rm int})/N=0.005^2$ for $N$.
solution: $\sum_{k\ge1}0.95^k=19$, so $2\tau_{\rm int}=39$. Then $N=39\sigma^2/\mathrm{SE}^2=39/(2.5\times10^{-5})=1.56\times10^{6}$. The effective sample size is $N/39=40\,000$, which is what an independent sampler would need; the naive formula $s/\sqrt N$ would wrongly declare that $40\,000$ chain steps suffice.

@exam self
prompt: Weeks 1, 5, 6, 8, 12. Plan the numerical experiment for the benchmark "Kepler orbit with eccentricity $e=0.6$ over 1000 periods" (units $GM=1$, semi-major axis $1$, start at pericenter $r=1-e$, $v=\sqrt{(1+e)/(1-e)}$, exact period $2\pi$). Compare velocity Verlet with RK4. Specify the step sizes, the diagnostics, the behavior you predict for each method and why, how you will validate the results independently, and what you will record so that the run can be reproduced.
hint: Use two or three fixed step sizes and a diagnostic that distinguishes bounded oscillation from secular drift.
hint: Think of an independent reference: a tight-tolerance adaptive integrator, and the exact period and invariants.
solution: Setup. Use fixed steps $h=2\pi/N_s$ with $N_s=200,400,800$ steps per orbit (so $h\approx0.031$ down to $0.008$), integrate $1000$ orbits, and store energy $E=\tfrac12v^2-1/r$ and angular momentum $L=x\dot y-y\dot x$ at every step. Minimal Verlet code:

```python
import numpy as np

def accel(r):
    return -r / np.linalg.norm(r) ** 3

def verlet_step(r, v, h):
    v = v + 0.5 * h * accel(r)
    r = r + h * v
    v = v + 0.5 * h * accel(r)
    return r, v
```
Diagnostics: $\max|\Delta E/E_0|$, the slope of a linear fit of $\Delta E$ against $t$, the drift of $L$, and the phase error (position error after $n$ orbits compared with the exact periodic orbit).

Predictions. Verlet is symplectic and time-reversible: $\Delta E$ oscillates with amplitude $O(h^2)$ and no secular drift (amplitude reduced by about $4$ when $h$ is halved); $L$ is conserved to rounding error because kicks are central and drifts are along $v$; the phase error grows linearly with time (an $O(h^2)$ error in the orbital frequency). RK4 is not symplectic: $\Delta E$ and $L$ drift steadily, at a rate that falls steeply as $h$ shrinks (measure the factor per halving rather than assuming it). Which method has the smaller energy error after 1000 orbits therefore depends on $h$: Verlet's error is bounded but not small, RK4's grows linearly in time from a much smaller rate, so RK4 can still be ahead at 1000 orbits and the crossover appears only for longer runs or larger $h$. Compare at equal cost, since RK4 needs four force evaluations per step and Verlet one. At very small $h$ rounding error also adds a slow random-walk growth of order $\sqrt{n}\,u$.

Verification and validation. Check the observed orders from the phase error at a fixed time (2 for Verlet, 4 for RK4). Compare with `solve_ivp` using `DOP853` at `rtol=atol=1e-13` and with the exact period and exact $E_0=-1/2$, $L_0=\sqrt{1-e^2}$. Test the stability limit by raising $h$ until Verlet leaves the bounded regime.

Reproducibility. The experiment is deterministic, so record the commit, Python/NumPy/SciPy versions, the config (steps per orbit, number of orbits, $e$), the thread settings and the machine, and compare re-runs with tolerances (for instance `rtol=1e-10` on $\Delta E$) because the last bits may change across libraries. State the limits: the result is for one eccentricity and fixed steps; adaptive steps would destroy the bounded-energy property.
rubric: Gives concrete fixed step sizes, the number of orbits and the diagnostics (energy, angular momentum, phase error) stored at every step.
rubric: Predicts bounded $O(h^2)$ energy error and no drift for Verlet and a steady, rapidly shrinking drift for RK4, and plans to measure the scalings and the crossover at equal cost rather than assume them.
rubric: Provides an independent validation (tight-tolerance adaptive integrator and exact period or invariants) and a convergence or stability check.
rubric: Lists reproducibility records (commit, versions, config, threads), tolerance-based comparisons and stated limitations.
