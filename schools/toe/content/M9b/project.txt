@review
This guide collects the results and methods you need for the sphere project and the cumulative final. Work through it with a blank page: for each line, reproduce the statement and one example before you look anything up.

### Week by week

- **Weeks 1-2, curves, surfaces and charts.** A surface is given by a parametrization $\mathbf r(u,v)$ with tangent vectors $\mathbf r_u$ and $\mathbf r_v$. A change of chart has a Jacobian $\partial x'^a/\partial x^b$. Know where each chart fails: the poles of spherical coordinates, the origin of polar coordinates.
- **Weeks 3-4, vectors, covectors, tensors.** Vector components transform with the Jacobian, covector components with its inverse. A tensor picks up one Jacobian factor per index, and contracting an upper with a lower index gives a tensor with two fewer indices (a scalar when none are left).
- **Weeks 5-6, metrics and forms.** The metric gives lengths $\int\sqrt{g_{ij}\dot x^i\dot x^j}\,dt$, angles, index raising and lowering, and the area element $\sqrt{\det g}\,dx\,dy$. A form is integrated by pulling it back to the parameters; $d$ raises the degree by one and $d^2=0$.
- **Weeks 7-8, connections and covariant derivatives.** For the Levi-Civita connection, $\Gamma^k_{ij} = \tfrac12 g^{kl}(\partial_i g_{jl} + \partial_j g_{il} - \partial_l g_{ij})$. Then $\nabla_i V^k = \partial_i V^k + \Gamma^k_{ij}V^j$ and $\nabla_i\omega_j = \partial_i\omega_j - \Gamma^k_{ij}\omega_k$. The symbols $\Gamma^k_{ij}$ are not a tensor, but $\nabla_iV^k$ is, and $\nabla g = 0$.
- **Weeks 9-10, geodesics and parallel transport.** Geodesics with an affine parameter satisfy $\ddot x^k + \Gamma^k_{ij}\dot x^i\dot x^j = 0$. Parallel transport along a curve solves $\dot V^k + \Gamma^k_{ij}\dot x^i V^j = 0$ and preserves lengths and angles. Around a loop on a curved surface the vector comes back rotated.
- **Weeks 11-12, curvature and intrinsic geometry.** With $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}$, the Ricci tensor is $R_{\sigma\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\rho\nu}$ and $R = g^{\sigma\nu}R_{\sigma\nu}$, which equals $2K$ in two dimensions. Anything built from $g_{ij}$ alone is intrinsic; how the surface sits in space is extrinsic. A cylinder and a cone are intrinsically flat, a sphere is not.

### Self-check

- Can you get the metric of a surface from its embedding and invert it?
- Can you list the nonzero Christoffel symbols of a diagonal metric and check $\Gamma^k_{ij} = \Gamma^k_{ji}$?
- Can you transform a vector, a covector and the metric between two charts?
- Can you write the geodesic equations, test a given curve, and spot a conserved quantity when the metric does not depend on a coordinate?
- Can you compute one curvature component and explain why $K=1/a^2$ for a sphere of radius $a$?

### Common errors

- Treating $\Gamma^k_{ij}$ as a tensor, or dropping the correction term in $\nabla_iV^k$ (and its minus sign for covectors).
- Swapping the Jacobian and its inverse for vectors and covectors.
- Forgetting $\sin^2\theta$ in $g_{\phi\phi}$. The sphere's Christoffel symbols do not depend on $a$, but $K$ and $R$ do.
- Using a non-affine parameter in the geodesic equation, or assuming every circle of latitude is a geodesic (only the equator is).
- Mixing curvature sign conventions: state yours at the top of the calculation.
- Calling something intrinsic just because it was computed in coordinates; ask whether $g_{ij}$ alone determines it.

At the point $\theta = \pi/6$, $\phi = 0$ the tangent vector $V = \partial_\theta$ is parallel transported once around the circle of latitude $\theta = \pi/6$ ($\phi$ increasing from $0$ to $2\pi$) with the Levi-Civita connection. Find the angle between the transported vector and the original vector, in radians between $0$ and $\pi$, rounded to three decimal places.
answer: 0.842

hint: On the circle, with $\phi$ as parameter, $\dot x^i = (0,1)$, so $\dot V^k + \Gamma^k_{ij}\dot x^i V^j = 0$ becomes two linear equations for $V^\theta$ and $V^\phi$ with constant coefficients.
hint: Differentiate the first equation and substitute the second to get $\ddot V^\theta = -\cos^2\theta_0\,V^\theta$. Solve it with $V^\theta(0)=1$, $V^\phi(0)=0$, evaluate at $\phi = 2\pi$, and use $\cos\psi = g(V,V_0)/|V_0|^2$ for the angle.
solution: The factor $9$ cancels, so $\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta$ and $\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi} = \cot\theta$. Along $\theta=\theta_0$ with $\phi$ as parameter, $\dot V^\theta = \sin\theta_0\cos\theta_0\,V^\phi$ and $\dot V^\phi = -\cot\theta_0\,V^\theta$. With $V(0) = (1,0)$ this gives $V^\theta = \cos(\phi\cos\theta_0)$ and $V^\phi = -\sin(\phi\cos\theta_0)/\sin\theta_0$. At $\phi = 2\pi$, $\theta_0 = \pi/6$: $V^\theta = \cos(\sqrt3\,\pi)$. Since $g_{\theta\phi} = 0$ and $|V| = |V_0|$, $\cos\psi = V^\theta$, so $\psi = \arccos\cos(\sqrt3\,\pi) = 2\pi - \sqrt3\,\pi = \pi(2-\sqrt3) \approx 0.842$ rad. This equals $K$ times the area of the enclosed cap, $2\pi(1-\cos\theta_0)$, for any radius.

@exam self
prompt: The upper half-plane $y>0$ has the metric $ds^2 = (dx^2 + dy^2)/y^2$. (a) Find all nonzero Christoffel symbols. (b) Write the two geodesic equations and show that the vertical line $x = x_0$, traversed as $y(s) = Ae^{ks}$, is a geodesic. (c) Using $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}$, compute $R^y{}_{xyx}$ and the scalar curvature $R$. (d) Integrate the area form $\sqrt{\det g}\,dx\wedge dy$ over $0\le x\le 2$, $1\le y\le 4$. (e) Name one quantity from (a)-(d) that would change in another chart and one that would not.
hint: Because $g_{xx} = g_{yy} = y^{-2}$ depends only on $y$, the only nonzero derivative of the metric is $\partial_y g_{xx} = \partial_y g_{yy} = -2y^{-3}$. Feed it into the Christoffel formula.
hint: For (c), keep the Ricci contraction in mind: $R_{xx} = R^y{}_{xyx}$ plus a term that vanishes, and in two dimensions $R_{ij}$ is proportional to $g_{ij}$.
solution: (a) $\Gamma^x_{xy} = \Gamma^x_{yx} = -1/y$, $\Gamma^y_{xx} = 1/y$, $\Gamma^y_{yy} = -1/y$; all others vanish. (b) $\ddot x - \frac2y\dot x\dot y = 0$ and $\ddot y + \frac1y(\dot x^2 - \dot y^2) = 0$. On $x = x_0$, $\dot x = \ddot x = 0$, so the first holds, and the second reduces to $\ddot y = \dot y^2/y$. For $y = Ae^{ks}$ both sides equal $k^2 y$. (c) $R^y{}_{xyx} = \partial_y\Gamma^y_{xx} - \partial_x\Gamma^y_{yx} + \Gamma^y_{yy}\Gamma^y_{xx} - \Gamma^y_{xx}\Gamma^x_{yx} = -\frac1{y^2} - 0 - \frac1{y^2} + \frac1{y^2} = -\frac1{y^2}$. So $R_{xx} = -g_{xx}$, and likewise $R_{yy} = -g_{yy}$, $R_{xy} = 0$; hence $R = g^{ij}R_{ij} = -2$ and $K = -1$ (sign convention: sphere positive). (d) Area $= \int_0^2\int_1^4 y^{-2}\,dy\,dx = 2\left(1 - \tfrac14\right) = \tfrac32$. (e) The Christoffel symbols change with the chart (they are not tensor components); the scalar curvature $-2$ and the area $3/2$ do not.
