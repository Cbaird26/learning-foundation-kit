@article
Scalars, vectors and covectors follow one pattern: a geometric quantity at a point, together with a rule saying how its components change with the chart. A **tensor** is the general version of that pattern.

### Tensors as multilinear maps

A tensor of **type $(p,q)$** at a point takes $p$ covectors and $q$ vectors and returns a number, and it is linear in each slot separately. Its **components** are its values on basis elements:

$$T^{i_1\dots i_p}{}_{j_1\dots j_q}=T\big(dx^{i_1},\dots,dx^{i_p},\ \partial_{j_1},\dots,\partial_{j_q}\big).$$

A scalar is $(0,0)$. A vector $v^i$ is $(1,0)$, since it eats a covector. A covector $\omega_i$ is $(0,1)$. A matrix $M^i{}_j$ acting as a linear map is $(1,1)$. A bilinear form such as the dot product, $g(u,v)=g_{ij}u^iv^j$, is $(0,2)$. In $n$ dimensions a $(p,q)$ tensor has $n^{p+q}$ components.

### The transformation law

Each upper index transforms like a vector index and each lower index like a covector index: one Jacobian factor per index.

$$T'^{a}{}_{b}=\frac{\partial x'^a}{\partial x^i}\,\frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x'^b}\,T^i{}_j,\qquad T'_{ab}=\frac{\partial x^i}{\partial x'^a}\,\frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x'^b}\,T_{ij}.$$

For a linear change $x'=Ax$ these are matrix products: $T'=A\,T\,A^{-1}$ for type $(1,1)$ and $T'=A^{-T}\,T\,A^{-1}$ for type $(0,2)$.

The law is linear and homogeneous, so a tensor that vanishes in one chart vanishes in every chart. That is why physical laws are written as tensor equations: a statement true in one coordinate system is true in all of them.

### Building new tensors

- **Tensor product:** multiply components, for example $(u\otimes\omega)^i{}_j=u^i\omega_j$, which has type $(1,1)$.
- **Contraction:** set an upper index equal to a lower index and sum. This lowers the type from $(p,q)$ to $(p-1,q-1)$. The pairing $\omega_iv^i$ and the trace $M^i{}_i$ are contractions. They are scalars: $\operatorname{tr}(AMA^{-1})=\operatorname{tr}M$.
- **Symmetry:** $S_{(ij)}=\tfrac12(S_{ij}+S_{ji})$ and $S_{[ij]}=\tfrac12(S_{ij}-S_{ji})$. A symmetric $(0,2)$ tensor has $n(n+1)/2$ independent components and an antisymmetric one has $n(n-1)/2$. Symmetry survives a change of chart.

### Not every array is a tensor

The coordinates $x^i$ are not the components of a vector. The partial derivatives $\partial_jV^i$ of a vector field are not a tensor either: when the Jacobian varies from point to point, differentiating $V'^a=\frac{\partial x'^a}{\partial x^i}V^i$ produces an extra term. Repairing this is the job of connections, in Weeks 7 and 8.

@example
title: Transforming a (1,1) tensor
problem: A tensor of type $(1,1)$ has matrix $T=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\0&3\end{pmatrix}$ (row index up, column index down) in coordinates $(x,y)$. The new chart is $x'=x+y$, $y'=y$. Find its matrix $T'$ in the new chart.
step: The Jacobian is $A=\dfrac{\partial x'}{\partial x}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, and its inverse is $A^{-1}=\dfrac{\partial x}{\partial x'}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$.
step: One upper and one lower index give $T'^a{}_b=A^a{}_i\,T^i{}_j\,(A^{-1})^j{}_b$, that is $T'=A\,T\,A^{-1}$.
step: First $AT=\begin{pmatrix}2&4\\0&3\end{pmatrix}$. Then $T'=\begin{pmatrix}2&4\\0&3\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}2&2\\0&3\end{pmatrix}$.
step: Check: the trace $5$ and the determinant $6$ are unchanged, as they are for any similarity transformation.

@example
title: The dot product in polar coordinates
problem: In Cartesian coordinates the dot product is the $(0,2)$ tensor with components $g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}$. Find its components $g'_{ab}$ in the polar chart.
step: A $(0,2)$ tensor uses two factors $\partial x^i/\partial x'^a$. These are the entries of the polar Jacobian $J=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-r\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta&r\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}$, with row $i$ and column $a$.
step: The law $g'_{ab}=J^i{}_aJ^j{}_b\,\delta_{ij}$ is the matrix product $g'=J^TJ$.
step: Multiply: $J^TJ=\begin{pmatrix}\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta&0\\ 0&r^2\sin^2\theta+r^2\cos^2\theta\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\ 0&r^2\end{pmatrix}$, where the off-diagonal entries $-r\sin\theta\cos\theta+r\sin\theta\cos\theta$ cancel.
step: So $g'_{rr}=1$, $g'_{\theta\theta}=r^2$, $g'_{r\theta}=0$. A small displacement $(dr,d\theta)$ has squared length $dr^2+r^2\,d\theta^2$. This is the polar form of the dot product, and Week 5 builds on it.

@mistakes
- Using $A$ where $A^{-1}$ belongs. Each upper index takes $\partial x'/\partial x$, and each lower index takes $\partial x/\partial x'$.
- Applying the $(0,2)$ rule $A^{-T}TA^{-1}$ to a $(1,1)$ tensor, or the reverse. The index positions decide the rule.
- Contracting two upper indices or two lower indices. Only an upper index paired with a lower index gives a tensor.

@practice num
prompt: How many independent components does a symmetric tensor $S_{ij}$ of type $(0,2)$ have in four dimensions?
answer: 10
check: len([(i, j) for i in range(4) for j in range(i, 4)])
hint: Symmetry means $S_{ij}=S_{ji}$, so only the entries with $i\le j$ are independent.
hint: Count the pairs $(i,j)$ with $1\le i\le j\le4$: there are $4+3+2+1$ of them.
solution: The independent entries are those with $i\le j$: $4+3+2+1=10$, which equals $\tfrac{4\cdot5}{2}$.

@practice tuple
prompt: In one dimension change the chart by $x'=3x$. In the old chart a vector has component $v^x=6$, a covector has component $\omega_x=6$, and a $(0,2)$ tensor has component $g_{xx}=18$. Find the new components $(v'^{x'},\ \omega'_{x'},\ g'_{x'x'})$.
answer: (18, 2, 2)
check: (lambda J: [J*6, 6/J, 18/J**2])(nderiv(lambda x: 3*x, 1))
hint: Here $\partial x'/\partial x=3$. A vector index uses this factor; each lower index uses its inverse, $\partial x/\partial x'=\tfrac13$.
hint: The vector picks up one factor of $3$, the covector one factor of $\tfrac13$, and the $(0,2)$ tensor two factors of $\tfrac13$.
solution: $v'=3\cdot6=18$, $\omega'=\tfrac13\cdot6=2$, $g'=\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac13\cdot18=2$. Check: the pairing $\omega v$ is $36$ in the old chart and $2\cdot18=36$ in the new one.

@practice tuple
prompt: A tensor of type $(1,1)$ has matrix $T=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{pmatrix}$ (row index up, column index down) in coordinates $(x,y)$. The new chart is $x'=2x$, $y'=x+y$. Enter the new components in the order $(T'^1{}_1,\ T'^1{}_2,\ T'^2{}_1,\ T'^2{}_2)$.
answer: (-1, 4, -1, 6)
check: (lambda F: (lambda A, T: list((A @ T @ np.linalg.inv(A)).flatten()))(np.array([[nderiv(lambda s: F(s, 0)[a], 0), nderiv(lambda s: F(0, s)[a], 0)] for a in range(2)]), np.array([[1, 2], [3, 4]])))(lambda x, y: [2*x, x + y])
hint: The Jacobian is $A=\partial x'/\partial x=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$, and $A^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&0\\-1/2&1\end{pmatrix}$.
hint: One upper and one lower index: $T'=A\,T\,A^{-1}$. Multiply $AT$ first.
solution: $AT=\begin{pmatrix}2&4\\4&6\end{pmatrix}$, and $T'=AT\,A^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}2\cdot\frac12-4\cdot\frac12&4\\ 4\cdot\frac12-6\cdot\frac12&6\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&4\\-1&6\end{pmatrix}$. Check: the trace is $5$ before and after, and the determinant is $-2$ before and after.

@practice mc
prompt: Which formula gives the correct transformation law for a tensor $T^i{}_j$ of type $(1,1)$ when passing from coordinates $x$ to coordinates $x'$?
choice*: $T'^a{}_b=\dfrac{\partial x'^a}{\partial x^i}\,\dfrac{\partial x^j}{\partial x'^b}\,T^i{}_j$
choice: $T'^a{}_b=\dfrac{\partial x'^a}{\partial x^i}\,\dfrac{\partial x'^b}{\partial x^j}\,T^i{}_j$
choice: $T'^a{}_b=\dfrac{\partial x^i}{\partial x'^a}\,\dfrac{\partial x'^b}{\partial x^j}\,T^i{}_j$
choice: $T'^a{}_b=\dfrac{\partial x^i}{\partial x'^a}\,\dfrac{\partial x^j}{\partial x'^b}\,T^i{}_j$
hint: The upper index $a$ must appear upstairs on $x'^a$ in a numerator, and the lower index $b$ must appear downstairs in the denominator $\partial x'^b$.
hint: Upper index: $\partial x'/\partial x$. Lower index: $\partial x/\partial x'$. Check which formula obeys both rules.
solution: An upper index transforms like a vector, with $\partial x'^a/\partial x^i$. A lower index transforms like a covector, with $\partial x^j/\partial x'^b$. Only the formula with $\partial x'^a/\partial x^i$ and $\partial x^j/\partial x'^b$ does both. The others put the wrong Jacobian on one or both indices, and they also break the rule that a free index must sit in the same position on both sides of an equation.

@practice self
prompt: Prove that the trace $T^i{}_i$ of a $(1,1)$ tensor is a scalar, that is, $T'^a{}_a=T^i{}_i$ in every chart.
hint: Set $b=a$ in the transformation law and sum over $a$.
hint: The two Jacobian factors that share the index $a$ multiply to a Kronecker delta by the chain rule.
solution: $T'^a{}_a=\dfrac{\partial x'^a}{\partial x^i}\dfrac{\partial x^j}{\partial x'^a}\,T^i{}_j$. Summing over $a$, the chain rule gives $\dfrac{\partial x^j}{\partial x'^a}\dfrac{\partial x'^a}{\partial x^i}=\dfrac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^i}=\delta^j_i$. Hence $T'^a{}_a=\delta^j_i\,T^i{}_j=T^i{}_i$. In matrix language, $\operatorname{tr}(ATA^{-1})=\operatorname{tr}T$.
rubric: Writes the transformation law for $T'^a{}_b$ and sets $b=a$ with a sum over $a$.
rubric: Applies the chain rule to turn the product of the two Jacobian factors into $\delta^j_i$.
rubric: Concludes $T'^a{}_a=T^i{}_i$ and states that this makes the trace a scalar.

@quiz num
prompt: In parabolic coordinates, $x=\tfrac12(\sigma^2-\tau^2)$ and $y=\sigma\tau$, the dot product is a $(0,2)$ tensor with Cartesian components $\delta_{ij}$, so its new components are $g'_{ab}=\dfrac{\partial x^i}{\partial x'^a}\dfrac{\partial x^j}{\partial x'^b}\,\delta_{ij}$. Find $g'_{\sigma\sigma}$ at $(\sigma,\tau)=(3,1)$.
answer: 10
check: sum(nderiv(lambda s: c(s, 1), 3)**2 for c in [lambda s, t: (s**2 - t**2)/2, lambda s, t: s*t])
hint: With $a=b=\sigma$ the sum becomes $\sum_i\big(\partial x^i/\partial\sigma\big)^2$, the squared length of the vector $(x_\sigma,y_\sigma)$.
hint: $x_\sigma=\sigma$ and $y_\sigma=\tau$.
solution: $x_\sigma=\sigma$ and $y_\sigma=\tau$, so $g'_{\sigma\sigma}=\sigma^2+\tau^2=9+1=10$.

@quiz ms
prompt: Which of the following objects are tensors, meaning their components obey the tensor transformation law under every smooth change of coordinates?
choice*: The trace $T^i{}_i$ of a $(1,1)$ tensor.
choice: The array of partial derivatives $\partial_jV^i$ of a vector field $V$.
choice*: The array $u^i\omega_j$ built from a vector $u$ and a covector $\omega$.
choice: The componentwise sum $v^i+\omega_i$ of a vector and a covector.
choice*: The symmetrized array $\tfrac12(S_{ij}+S_{ji})$ of a $(0,2)$ tensor $S$.
hint: Ask whether the object is built only from tensor products, contractions and index permutations of tensors, and whether the indices are used consistently.
solution: The trace is a contraction, so it is a scalar. The array $u^i\omega_j$ is a tensor product, of type $(1,1)$. Symmetrization only permutes indices and adds, which preserves the law. The array $\partial_jV^i$ picks up an extra term from the derivative of the Jacobian, and $v^i+\omega_i$ adds components that transform with opposite Jacobians, so it has no coordinate-independent meaning.
