@article
Start with a Newtonian picture. Take a uniform ball of dust and look at a test particle at radius $R = a\,r$ on its surface. Energy per unit mass is conserved: $\tfrac12\dot R^2 - GM/R = \text{const}$. With $M = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3\rho$ this becomes $H^2 = \tfrac{8\pi G}{3}\rho - \text{const}/R^2$. General relativity keeps this structure, reinterprets the constant as spatial curvature, and adds pressure.

### The equations

Assume a perfect fluid with mass density $\rho$ (so the energy density is $\rho c^2$) and pressure $p$, and define $w \equiv p/(\rho c^2)$. Inserting the FLRW metric of week 2 (signature $(-,+,+,+)$, $a(t_0)=1$) into Einstein's equations gives
$$H^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho - \frac{Kc^2}{a^2} + \frac{\Lambda c^2}{3},\qquad \frac{\ddot a}{a} = -\frac{4\pi G}{3}\left(\rho + \frac{3p}{c^2}\right) + \frac{\Lambda c^2}{3},$$
and the energy-conservation (fluid) equation
$$\dot\rho + 3H\left(\rho + \frac{p}{c^2}\right) = 0 .$$
Only two of the three are independent. It is often convenient to absorb the cosmological constant into the fluid as a component with $w=-1$ and $\rho_\Lambda = \Lambda c^2/(8\pi G)$.

### How densities dilute

For constant $w$, the fluid equation gives $d\rho/\rho = -3(1+w)\,da/a$, so
$$\rho \propto a^{-3(1+w)} .$$
Pressureless matter ($w=0$) dilutes as $a^{-3}$, radiation ($w=1/3$) as $a^{-4}$ (the extra factor is the redshift of each photon), and a cosmological constant ($w=-1$) does not dilute. The curvature term in the Friedmann equation scales as $a^{-2}$, so it mimics a fluid with $w=-1/3$.

### Critical density and density parameters

Define $\rho_c(t) \equiv 3H^2/(8\pi G)$ and $\Omega_i \equiv \rho_i/\rho_c$. Today
$$\rho_{c,0} = 1.878\times10^{-26}\,h^2\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}} = 2.775\times10^{11}\,h^2\ M_\odot\,\mathrm{Mpc^{-3}} .$$
Dividing the Friedmann equation by $H^2$ gives $\sum_i\Omega_i + \Omega_K = 1$ with $\Omega_K \equiv -Kc^2/(a_0H_0)^2$. The total $\Omega_{\rm tot} = \sum_i \Omega_i$ exceeds 1 for a closed universe ($K>0$) and is below 1 for an open one.

### Single-component flat universes

If one fluid dominates and $K=0$, then $H^2 \propto a^{-3(1+w)}$, so $\dot a \propto a^{-(1+3w)/2}$ and integrating gives
$$a \propto t^{2/[3(1+w)]}\quad (w\neq -1),\qquad a\propto e^{H_\Lambda t}\quad (w=-1).$$
Matter: $a\propto t^{2/3}$ with $H = 2/(3t)$. Radiation: $a \propto t^{1/2}$ with $H = 1/(2t)$. A cosmological constant: exponential growth with $H_\Lambda = \sqrt{\Lambda c^2/3}$. The age of a flat matter-only universe is $t_0 = 2/(3H_0)$.

### Acceleration

The acceleration equation shows that $\ddot a>0$ requires $\rho c^2 + 3p < 0$, that is $w<-1/3$ for a single fluid. Gravity is sourced by pressure as well as by energy density. The **deceleration parameter** is $q \equiv -\ddot a\, a/\dot a^2$; for a flat universe with matter and $\Lambda$,
$$q_0 = \frac{\Omega_m}{2} - \Omega_\Lambda .$$

### Several components

In general $H(a) = H_0\sqrt{\Omega_r a^{-4} + \Omega_m a^{-3} + \Omega_K a^{-2} + \Omega_\Lambda}$, and the age follows by integrating $dt = da/(aH)$:
$$t(a) = \int_0^a \frac{da'}{a' H(a')} .$$
For flat matter plus $\Lambda$ the integral has a closed form, $t_0 = \dfrac{2}{3H_0\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}}\sinh^{-1}\!\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda/\Omega_m}$, which is a quick check on a numerical code.

@example
title: The critical density today
problem: Find $\rho_{c,0}$ for $H_0 = 70$ km/s/Mpc, using $G = 6.674\times10^{-11}$ m$^3$ kg$^{-1}$ s$^{-2}$ and $1\ \mathrm{Mpc} = 3.0857\times10^{22}$ m. Express it as a number of proton masses ($1.673\times10^{-27}$ kg) per cubic metre.
step: Convert $H_0$ to SI: $H_0 = 70\times10^{3}\ \mathrm{m/s}\,/\,3.0857\times10^{22}\ \mathrm{m} = 2.269\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$.
step: Square it: $H_0^2 = 5.146\times10^{-36}\ \mathrm{s^{-2}}$.
step: Apply the definition: $\rho_{c,0} = 3H_0^2/(8\pi G) = 1.544\times10^{-35}/1.677\times10^{-9} = 9.20\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$.
step: Divide by the proton mass: $9.20\times10^{-27}/1.673\times10^{-27} \approx 5.5$ protons per cubic metre. The whole universe is a very good vacuum.

@example
title: The age of a matter-only universe
problem: Solve the Friedmann equation for a flat universe containing only pressureless matter, and find its age in terms of $H_0$.
step: With $K=\Lambda=0$ and $\rho = \rho_0 a^{-3}$, the Friedmann equation reads $\dot a^2/a^2 = H_0^2 a^{-3}$, using $\rho_0 = \rho_{c,0}$ for flatness.
step: Take the positive root: $\dot a = H_0 a^{-1/2}$. Separate variables: $a^{1/2}\,da = H_0\,dt$.
step: Integrate from the big bang ($a=0$ at $t=0$): $\tfrac23 a^{3/2} = H_0 t$, so $a(t) = \left(\tfrac32 H_0 t\right)^{2/3}$.
step: Set $a=1$: $t_0 = 2/(3H_0)$. For $H_0 = 70$ km/s/Mpc, $1/H_0 = 13.97$ Gyr so $t_0 = 9.3$ Gyr, younger than the oldest stars. A pure matter model is too young, one reason a cosmological constant is needed.

@mistakes
- Mixing mass density and energy density: with $\rho$ a mass density the pressure enters as $p/c^2$.
- Concluding that acceleration needs negative pressure alone: it needs $w<-1/3$, that is $\rho c^2 + 3p < 0$.
- Using $a\propto t^{2/3}$ when radiation or $\Lambda$ matters, or forgetting that the exponent is $2/[3(1+w)]$ only for a single constant-$w$ fluid.
- Forgetting to convert $H_0$ from km/s/Mpc to s$^{-1}$ before computing densities or ages.

hint: The integral gives $t_0 = 2/(3H_0)$; the Hubble time $1/H_0$ is $13.97$ Gyr.

@practice expr
prompt: A fluid with constant $w = p/(\rho c^2)$ obeys $\dot\rho + 3H(1+w)\rho = 0$. Give $\rho(a)/\rho_0$, where $\rho_0$ is the density at $a=1$.
vars: a, w
lead: $\rho/\rho_0 =$
answer: a^(-3(1+w))
check: lambda a, w: exp(-3*(1 + w)*quad(lambda s: 1/s, 1, a))
domain: a: 0.5..2; w: -0.8..0.6
hint: Use $\dot\rho = (d\rho/da)\,\dot a$ and $H = \dot a/a$ to turn the equation into one for $d\rho/da$.
hint: You get $d\ln\rho = -3(1+w)\,d\ln a$; integrate from $a=1$.
solution: $d\rho/\rho = -3(1+w)\,da/a$, so $\ln(\rho/\rho_0) = -3(1+w)\ln a$ and $\rho/\rho_0 = a^{-3(1+w)}$.

@practice num
prompt: A flat universe has $\Omega_m = 0.3$ and $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.7$ (radiation neglected), so $\dot a = H_0\, a\sqrt{0.3\,a^{-3} + 0.7}$. Find the present deceleration parameter $q_0 = -\ddot a\,a/\dot a^2$.
answer: -0.55
check: -nderiv(lambda a: a*sqrt(0.3/a**3 + 0.7), 1)
tol: 0.005
hint: Write $\ddot a = (d\dot a/da)\,\dot a$.
hint: At $a=1$ we have $\dot a = H_0$, so $q_0 = -(d\dot a/da)/H_0$ evaluated at $a=1$.
solution: Let $f(a) = \dot a/H_0 = \sqrt{0.3/a + 0.7a^2}$. Then $f(1) = 1$ and $f'(1) = (-0.3 + 1.4)/2 = 0.55$, so $q_0 = -0.55$. This agrees with $\Omega_m/2 - \Omega_\Lambda = 0.15 - 0.7$.

@practice num
prompt: In a flat universe with $\Omega_m = 0.3$ and $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.7$ (radiation neglected), the expansion changed from decelerating to accelerating at some redshift $z_{\rm acc}$. Find it, to three decimal places.
answer: 0.671
check: root(lambda z: -0.3*(1 + z)**3/2 + 0.7, 0, 3)
tol: 1%
hint: Set $\ddot a = 0$ in the acceleration equation, which gives $\rho_m = 2\rho_\Lambda$ for dust plus a cosmological constant.
hint: $\rho_m \propto \Omega_m(1+z)^3$ while $\rho_\Lambda$ is constant, so $(1+z)^3 = 2\Omega_\Lambda/\Omega_m$.
solution: $(1+z)^3 = 2(0.7)/0.3 = 4.667$, so $1 + z = 1.671$ and $z_{\rm acc} = 0.671$.

@quiz num
prompt: For a flat universe with $\Omega_m = 0.315$, $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.685$ (radiation neglected) and $H_0 = 67.4$ km/s/Mpc, find the age in Gyr. Use $1\ \mathrm{Mpc} = 3.0857\times10^{19}$ km and $1\ \mathrm{Gyr} = 3.156\times10^{16}$ s. Give two decimal places.
answer: 13.80
check: quad(lambda a: sqrt(a)/sqrt(0.315 + 0.685*a**3), 0, 1)*3.0857e19/67.4/3.156e16
tol: 0.5%
unit: Gyr
hint: $t_0 = \int_0^1 da/(aH)$ with $H = H_0\sqrt{0.315\,a^{-3} + 0.685}$.
hint: Rewrite the integrand as $a^{1/2}/\sqrt{0.315 + 0.685\,a^3}$ to see that it is finite at $a=0$. The closed form $\frac{2}{3\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}}\sinh^{-1}\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda/\Omega_m}$ also works.
solution: $1/H_0 = 14.51$ Gyr. The dimensionless integral equals $\frac{2}{3\sqrt{0.685}}\sinh^{-1}\sqrt{0.685/0.315} = 0.9510$, so $t_0 = 0.9510\times 14.51 = 13.80$ Gyr.

@quiz ms
prompt: Which of the following, if it dominated a flat universe, would give accelerating expansion ($\ddot a>0$)?
choice*: A cosmological constant ($w=-1$).
choice*: A fluid with $w = -2/3$.
choice: Pressureless dust ($w=0$).
choice: Radiation ($w=1/3$).
choice: Spatial curvature alone (an empty open universe, effective $w=-1/3$).
hint: Acceleration needs $\rho c^2 + 3p<0$, that is $w<-1/3$.
solution: The condition is strictly $w<-1/3$. Dust and radiation decelerate. Curvature alone gives $a\propto t$, coasting with $\ddot a=0$, not acceleration.
