@article
About 380,000 years after the big bang the universe became transparent. The photons that last scattered then are the CMB, a snapshot of the universe at $z\approx1090$, and they have travelled almost unimpeded ever since.

### Conventions and inputs

Approximate standard values, to be checked against current data: $T_0 = 2.7255$ K, last scattering at $z_*\approx 1090$ ($T_*\approx 2970$ K, $k_BT_*\approx 0.26$ eV), $\Omega_b h^2 \approx 0.0224$, $\Omega_\gamma h^2\approx 2.47\times10^{-5}$, comoving sound horizon $r_s\approx 144$ Mpc at last scattering, and comoving transverse distance $D_M(z_*)\approx 13.9$ Gpc. Signature $(-,+,+,+)$, flat geometry unless stated.

### From plasma to free streaming

Before recombination the gas is ionized and Thomson scattering ties photons to electrons, and through the Coulomb force to baryons. Recombination, $p + e^-\to {\rm H} + \gamma$, removes the free electrons. In equilibrium the ionization fraction $x_e$ follows the **Saha equation**
$$\frac{x_e^2}{1-x_e} = \frac{1}{n_b}\left(\frac{m_ek_BT}{2\pi\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}e^{-B/k_BT},\qquad n_b = \eta\,n_\gamma,\ \ B = 13.6\ \mathrm{eV}.$$
The same logic as the deuterium bottleneck applies: because photons outnumber baryons by $\sim10^9$, hydrogen stays ionized until $k_BT\approx 0.3$ eV, far below $B$. Saha gives $x_e = 1/2$ near 3760 K ($z\approx1380$). The real process is out of equilibrium (trapped Lyman-$\alpha$ photons and the slow two-photon $2s\to1s$ channel), so last scattering, defined as the peak of the visibility function where the optical depth reaches about 1, occurs later, at $z\approx1090$.

### A blackbody that stays a blackbody

The spectrum is $B_\nu\propto \nu^3/(e^{h\nu/k_BT}-1)$, peaking at $h\nu = 2.821\,k_BT$ (160 GHz today). Free streaming redshifts each photon by $1/(1+z)$ while conserving occupation numbers, so the spectrum remains a blackbody with $T\propto(1+z)$. Measured deviations from a blackbody are below $10^{-4}$ of the peak intensity.

### Anisotropies and the power spectrum

Expand the temperature field on the sky as $\Delta T/T(\hat n) = \sum a_{\ell m}Y_{\ell m}(\hat n)$ and define $C_\ell = \langle|a_{\ell m}|^2\rangle$; an angular scale $\theta$ corresponds to $\ell\approx\pi/\theta$.

- **Dipole** ($\ell=1$): a Doppler effect from our motion, $\Delta T/T = v/c$. A speed near 370 km/s gives $\Delta T\approx 3.4$ mK, about a hundred times larger than the typical intrinsic anisotropy of order $10^{-5}T_0\approx 30\ \mu$K.
- **Large angles** ($\ell\lesssim 30$): the Sachs–Wolfe plateau, $\Delta T/T\approx\Phi/(3c^2)$ for the gravitational potential at last scattering.
- **Acoustic peaks**: before recombination gravity and radiation pressure drive standing sound waves in the photon–baryon fluid with sound speed
$$c_s = \frac{c}{\sqrt{3(1+R)}},\qquad R = \frac{3\rho_b}{4\rho_\gamma} \approx 0.62\ \text{at } z_*.$$
- **Silk damping** suppresses small scales, because photons diffuse out of small perturbations.
- **Polarization** arises from Thomson scattering of a local quadrupole; reionization at $z\sim 8$ (optical depth $\tau\approx0.054$) damps the anisotropy by $e^{-2\tau}$.

### The acoustic scale

The comoving sound horizon is $r_s = \int_{z_*}^\infty c_s\,dz/H(z)$. It is a standard ruler of angular size
$$\theta_* = \frac{r_s}{D_M(z_*)}\approx 0.0104\ \mathrm{rad},\qquad \ell_A \equiv\frac{\pi}{\theta_*}\approx 300 .$$
The first peak sits at $\ell\approx220$ rather than at $\ell_A$ because driving effects shift the oscillation phase, but the peak positions scale with $\ell_A$, so they measure the ratio of $r_s$ to $D_M$. That ratio constrains a combination of the expansion history and spatial curvature, not the curvature alone (the geometric degeneracy).

### What the peaks constrain

| feature | mainly constrains |
|---|---|
| peak positions | $\theta_*$, hence geometry and expansion history |
| odd to even peak height ratio | $\Omega_b h^2$ |
| overall peak heights | $\Omega_m h^2$ (matter–radiation equality) |
| tilt of the spectrum | $n_s$ |
| amplitude $\propto A_se^{-2\tau}$, low-$\ell$ polarization | $A_s$ and $\tau$ |

@example
title: The peak of the CMB spectrum
problem: Find the frequency of the maximum of $B_\nu\propto\nu^3/(e^{h\nu/k_BT}-1)$ for $T = 2.7255$ K, using $k_B = 1.3806\times10^{-23}$ J/K and $h = 6.626\times10^{-34}$ J s.
step: Let $x = h\nu/k_BT$. Setting $d/dx\,[x^3/(e^x-1)] = 0$ gives $3(e^x-1) = x\,e^x$, equivalently $x = 3(1 - e^{-x})$.
step: Iterate from $x=3$: $x\to 3(1-e^{-3}) = 2.851$, then $2.827$, then $2.8225$, converging to $x = 2.821$.
step: Then $\nu_{\rm peak} = x\,k_BT/h = 2.821\times(1.3806\times10^{-23}\times 2.7255)/(6.626\times10^{-34}) = 1.602\times10^{11}$ Hz $= 160$ GHz.
step: This is the peak of the spectrum per unit frequency; the peak per unit wavelength is at a different place ($\lambda_{\rm peak}T = 2.898\times10^{-3}$ m K, about 1.06 mm), because the two quantities are densities in different variables.

@example
title: Sound speed and acoustic scale
problem: Take $\Omega_b h^2 = 0.0224$, $\Omega_\gamma h^2 = 2.47\times10^{-5}$, $z_* = 1090$, $r_s = 144.4$ Mpc and $D_M(z_*) = 13{,}870$ Mpc. Find $R$, $c_s/c$, $\theta_*$ and $\ell_A$.
step: The ratio $\rho_b/\rho_\gamma$ scales as $a$: $\rho_b/\rho_\gamma = (0.0224/2.47\times10^{-5})/(1+z_*) = 906.9/1091 = 0.831$, so $R = \tfrac34(0.831) = 0.623$.
step: Sound speed: $c_s/c = 1/\sqrt{3(1+R)} = 1/\sqrt{3\times1.623} = 0.453$. The fluid is much stiffer than dust but slower than light by a factor of 2.2.
step: Angle: $\theta_* = r_s/D_M = 144.4/13870 = 0.01041$ rad $= 0.597^\circ$.
step: Acoustic scale: $\ell_A = \pi/\theta_* = 302$. The first peak at $\ell\approx 220$ is about $0.73\,\ell_A$ because of the phase shift.

@mistakes
- Assuming recombination happens at $k_BT\approx 13.6$ eV; the photon-to-baryon ratio pushes it down to about 0.3 eV.
- Using the angular-diameter distance for a comoving ruler (use $D_M$ with a comoving $r_s$, or $d_A$ with a proper size).
- Reading the first-peak position as a direct measurement of curvature; it constrains $\theta_*$, and curvature, $H_0$ and $\Omega_m$ are degenerate without other data.
- Interpreting the dipole as primordial; it is dominated by our motion and is removed before the intrinsic anisotropy is analyzed.
