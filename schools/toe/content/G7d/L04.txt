@article
The Friedmann equation says that the expansion rate is set by what is in the universe. Each component dilutes at its own rate, so the cosmic budget changes with time, and the universe passes through eras ruled by one component after another.

### The inventory

Densities scale as $\rho\propto a^{-3(1+w)}$ (week 3). The approximate present-day values below are standard fiducial numbers, to be checked against current data.

| component | $w$ | $\rho(a)$ | present value (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| photons (CMB) | $1/3$ | $a^{-4}$ | $\Omega_\gamma h^2 \approx 2.47\times10^{-5}$ |
| neutrinos | $1/3$ while relativistic | $a^{-4}$ early | $\Omega_\nu h^2 = \sum m_\nu/93.14\ \mathrm{eV}\gtrsim 6\times10^{-4}$ |
| baryons | $0$ | $a^{-3}$ | $\Omega_b h^2 \approx 0.0224$ |
| cold dark matter | $0$ | $a^{-3}$ | $\Omega_c h^2 \approx 0.120$ |
| curvature | $-1/3$ (effective) | $a^{-2}$ | $|\Omega_K|\lesssim 0.01$ |
| cosmological constant / dark energy | $-1$ | constant | $\Omega_\Lambda\approx 0.685$ |

With $h\approx 0.674$ this gives $\Omega_m\approx 0.315$ and $\Omega_\Lambda \approx 0.685$.

### Radiation density

A blackbody has energy density $\rho_\gamma c^2 = \dfrac{\pi^2}{15}\dfrac{(k_BT)^4}{(\hbar c)^3} = a_{\rm rad}T^4$ with $a_{\rm rad} = 7.566\times10^{-16}$ J m$^{-3}$ K$^{-4}$. For $T_0 = 2.7255$ K this gives $\Omega_\gamma h^2 \approx 2.47\times10^{-5}$.

The three neutrino species decouple before electron–positron annihilation and so are colder than the photons by $(4/11)^{1/3}$ (week 5). Treated as massless,
$$\rho_\nu = N_{\rm eff}\,\frac78\left(\frac{4}{11}\right)^{4/3}\rho_\gamma ,\qquad \Omega_r h^2 = \Omega_\gamma h^2\left[1 + 0.2271\,N_{\rm eff}\right]\approx 4.2\times10^{-5},$$
with $N_{\rm eff}\approx 3.046$. Massive neutrinos behave as radiation early and as matter later; we ignore that refinement here.

### The dimensionless expansion rate

Writing $H = H_0E(a)$,
$$E^2(a) = \Omega_r a^{-4} + \Omega_m a^{-3} + \Omega_K a^{-2} + \Omega_\Lambda .$$
Radiation dominates at small $a$, then matter, and (for the fiducial values) $\Lambda$ at late times. The transitions are gradual; "equality" means that the two densities are equal:
$$1+z_{\rm eq} = \frac{\Omega_m}{\Omega_r},\qquad 1+z_{m\Lambda} = \left(\frac{\Omega_\Lambda}{\Omega_m}\right)^{1/3}.$$
In the fiducial model $z_{\rm eq}\approx 3400$ and $z_{m\Lambda}\approx 0.30$.

### Matter and radiation eras: derivations

*Matter domination.* With $E^2 = \Omega_m a^{-3}$, $\dot a = H_0\sqrt{\Omega_m}\,a^{-1/2}$. Separate variables, $a^{1/2}da = H_0\sqrt{\Omega_m}\,dt$, and integrate from $a=0$:
$$a\propto t^{2/3},\qquad H = \frac{2}{3t},\qquad \rho_m = \frac{1}{6\pi G t^2}.$$

*Radiation domination.* With $E^2 = \Omega_r a^{-4}$, $\dot a = H_0\sqrt{\Omega_r}\,a^{-1}$, so $a\,da = H_0\sqrt{\Omega_r}\,dt$ and
$$a\propto t^{1/2},\qquad H = \frac{1}{2t},\qquad \rho_r = \frac{3}{32\pi G t^2}.$$
Since $\rho_r\propto T^4$, this gives $T\propto t^{-1/2}$, the heart of the thermal history in week 5.

*Dark-energy domination.* With $E^2 = \Omega_\Lambda$, $\dot a = H_0\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}\,a$, which gives $a\propto e^{H_0\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}\,t}$, de Sitter expansion.

These power laws are exact only for one component; near equality the full $E(a)$ must be integrated.

@example
title: The photon density parameter
problem: Compute $\rho_\gamma$ and $\Omega_\gamma h^2$ for $T_0 = 2.7255$ K, using $a_{\rm rad} = 7.566\times10^{-16}$ J m$^{-3}$ K$^{-4}$, $c = 2.998\times10^{8}$ m/s and $\rho_{c,0} = 1.878\times10^{-26}\,h^2$ kg m$^{-3}$.
step: Energy density: $u_\gamma = a_{\rm rad}T_0^4 = 7.566\times10^{-16}\times 55.18 = 4.175\times10^{-14}$ J m$^{-3}$.
step: Convert to mass density: $\rho_\gamma = u_\gamma/c^2 = 4.175\times10^{-14}/8.988\times10^{16} = 4.645\times10^{-31}$ kg m$^{-3}$.
step: Divide by the critical density: $\Omega_\gamma = 4.645\times10^{-31}/(1.878\times10^{-26}h^2)$, so $\Omega_\gamma h^2 = 2.47\times10^{-5}$.
step: For $h=0.674$ this is $\Omega_\gamma = 5.4\times10^{-5}$: the photons are a tiny fraction of the critical density today, but they were dominant in the early universe because they dilute as $a^{-4}$.

@example
title: Matter–radiation equality
problem: Take $\Omega_m h^2 = 0.1424$, $\Omega_\gamma h^2 = 2.473\times10^{-5}$ and $N_{\rm eff} = 3.046$. Find $\Omega_r h^2$ and the redshift of matter–radiation equality.
step: The neutrino factor is $0.2271\times 3.046 = 0.6918$, so the total radiation parameter is $\Omega_r h^2 = 2.473\times10^{-5}\times 1.6918 = 4.184\times10^{-5}$.
step: The $h^2$ factors cancel in the ratio: $1+z_{\rm eq} = \Omega_m/\Omega_r = 0.1424/(4.184\times10^{-5}) = 3403$.
step: Therefore $z_{\rm eq}\approx 3400$, well before recombination at $z\approx 1090$ (week 7).
step: Equality is set by physical densities $\Omega_m h^2$ and $\Omega_r h^2$ alone, so it is independent of $\Omega_\Lambda$ and of the Hubble constant taken separately.

@mistakes
- Treating the era boundaries as sharp. At $z_{\rm eq}$ matter and radiation contribute equally, and $a\propto t^{1/2}$ or $t^{2/3}$ is only approximate nearby.
- Forgetting the neutrinos when computing the radiation density; they add about 69% to the photon density.
- Using $\Omega_m$ where the physical density $\Omega_m h^2$ is needed, so that $h$ does not cancel.
- Confusing matter–radiation equality ($z\approx 3400$) with matter–Λ equality ($z\approx 0.3$).

@practice num
prompt: By what factor is the mean matter density larger at redshift $z=2$ than today?
answer: 27
check: (1 + 2)**3
hint: Matter dilutes as the volume grows: $\rho_m\propto a^{-3}$.
solution: $\rho_m(z)/\rho_{m,0} = (1+z)^3 = 3^3 = 27$.

answer: 0.320
check: 4.184e-5/0.1424*(1 + 1089)

@practice num

check: root(lambda z: 0.315*(1 + z)**3 - 0.685, 0, 2)

@practice self
prompt: Starting from the Friedmann equation for a flat universe containing only radiation, derive $a(t)$ up to a constant, then $H(t)$ and $\rho_r(t)$. Then use $\rho_r\propto T^4$ to find how the temperature depends on time.
hint: Use $\rho_r\propto a^{-4}$ in the Friedmann equation, take the positive root for $\dot a$, and separate variables.
solution: Flat, radiation only: $H^2 = (8\pi G/3)\rho_r$ with $\rho_r = \rho_{r,0}a^{-4}$, so $\dot a^2 = H_0^2\Omega_r a^{-2}$ and $\dot a = H_0\sqrt{\Omega_r}/a$. Separate: $a\,da = H_0\sqrt{\Omega_r}\,dt$, so $a^2 = 2H_0\sqrt{\Omega_r}\,t$ and $a\propto t^{1/2}$. Then $H = \dot a/a = 1/(2t)$. From the Friedmann equation, $\rho_r = 3H^2/(8\pi G) = 3/(32\pi G t^2)$. Since $\rho_r\propto T^4$, $T\propto \rho_r^{1/4}\propto t^{-1/2}$, consistent with $T\propto 1/a$.
rubric: Separates variables correctly and integrates to $a\propto t^{1/2}$ with the boundary condition $a(0)=0$.
rubric: Obtains $H = 1/(2t)$ and $\rho_r = 3/(32\pi G t^2)$ from the Friedmann equation.
rubric: Concludes $T\propto t^{-1/2}$ using $\rho_r\propto T^4$ and checks consistency with $T\propto 1/a$.

@quiz num
prompt: In a flat universe with $\Omega_m = 0.3$ and $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.7$ (radiation neglected), at what redshift does matter make up 90% of the total energy density? Give three decimal places.
answer: 1.759
check: root(lambda z: 0.3*(1 + z)**3/(0.3*(1 + z)**3 + 0.7) - 0.9, 0, 5)
tol: 1%
hint: The matter fraction is $\Omega_m(1+z)^3/[\Omega_m(1+z)^3 + \Omega_\Lambda]$.
hint: A 90% fraction means $\rho_m = 9\rho_\Lambda$.
solution: $0.3(1+z)^3 = 9\times 0.7$ gives $(1+z)^3 = 21$, so $1+z = 2.759$ and $z = 1.759$.

@quiz ms
prompt: Select all correct statements about the scaling of cosmic components.
choice*: The photon number density scales as $a^{-3}$.
choice*: The energy of each photon scales as $a^{-1}$.
choice*: A cosmological constant has constant energy density as the universe expands.
choice: The radiation energy density scales as $a^{-3}$ because the photon number density does.
choice: Matter–radiation equality happens after matter–Λ equality.
hint: Combine the number density with the energy per photon to get the energy density.
solution: Photon number is conserved, so $n_\gamma\propto a^{-3}$; each photon's wavelength stretches, so its energy goes as $a^{-1}$, and the energy density scales as $a^{-4}$. A cosmological constant does not dilute. Matter–radiation equality ($z\approx 3400$) is much earlier than matter–Λ equality ($z\approx 0.3$).
