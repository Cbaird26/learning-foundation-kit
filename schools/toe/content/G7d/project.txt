@project
title: A numerical expansion history and a benchmark comparison
overview: You will integrate the Friedmann equation numerically for a flat universe containing radiation, matter and a cosmological constant, test the code against analytic limits, and compute observables such as the age, distances and the CMB acoustic scale. You then compare them with a stated benchmark, propagate parameter uncertainties, and write a short report that says what the comparison does and does not show.
goal: Implement H(a) and solve for a(t) numerically, verifying the code against the matter-only and radiation-only analytic solutions.
goal: Compute at least three observables (for example the age, a distance at one or more redshifts, and the acoustic scale) and compare each with a stated benchmark.
goal: Propagate the uncertainty in H_0 and Ω_m to the observables and report parameters with their uncertainties.
goal: Write conventions, inputs and limitations explicitly, without overclaiming what agreement with a benchmark means.
step: Write a conventions and inputs table: metric signature (-,+,+,+), a(t_0)=1, units (km/s/Mpc, Mpc, Gyr), and the parameter values you use. Suggested approximate standard values, to be checked against current data: H_0 = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc, Ω_m = 0.315 ± 0.007, Ω_r = 9.2×10^-5 (T_0 = 2.7255 K, N_eff = 3.046), Ω_Λ = 1 − Ω_m − Ω_r.
step: Code E(a) = sqrt(Ω_r a^-4 + Ω_m a^-3 + Ω_Λ) and H = H_0 E. Test it: E(1) = 1; for Ω_m = 1 your numerical age must equal 2/(3H_0) and for radiation only a ∝ t^(1/2), each to a stated tolerance such as 10^-6.
step: Solve for a(t) in two independent ways: integrate da/dt = H_0 a E(a) with an ODE solver starting at a tiny a_i with the radiation-era initial condition, and compute t(a) = ∫ da / (a H(a)) by quadrature. Show that the two agree and report the age t_0 in Gyr.
step: Compute χ(z) = c ∫ dz / H(z), the luminosity distance d_L(z), the angular-diameter distance d_A(z) and the distance modulus for z = 0.5, 1 and 2, and compute D_M(z_*) for z_* = 1090 with the acoustic scale ℓ_A = π D_M(z_*) / r_s, taking r_s = 144.4 Mpc as an approximate standard value.
step: Choose a benchmark with stated tolerances (for example t_0 ≈ 13.8 Gyr, D_M(z_*) ≈ 13.9 Gpc, ℓ_A ≈ 302) and report the fractional difference of each observable. Decide, and say in advance, what size of difference you will call agreement.
step: Propagate uncertainty: draw at least 2000 samples of (H_0, Ω_m) from independent Gaussians, recompute t_0 and d_L(z = 0.5), and report mean and standard deviation. Compare with linear error propagation using numerical derivatives. Explain how an anticorrelation between H_0 and Ω_m, as in real CMB fits, would change the result.
step: Vary the model: repeat the main observables for w = −0.9 and for Ω_k = 0.01, and describe how much each parameter change shifts each observable and whether the changes are degenerate with changes in H_0 or Ω_m. Report these as sensitivities, not as detections.
step: Write a one to two page report: conventions, inputs, methods, a results table (value ± uncertainty, benchmark, fractional difference), tests passed, and a short paragraph on limitations such as massless neutrinos, the approximate r_s and the neglect of the transition in g_*.
deliverable: Commented code that reproduces every number in the report, including the tests against analytic limits.
deliverable: A results table listing each observable, your value with its uncertainty, the benchmark and the fractional difference.
deliverable: A one to two page report stating conventions, inputs, methods, results and limitations in the language of the course, without overclaiming.
rubric: Conventions and inputs are stated completely (signature, normalization of a, units, parameter values labelled as approximate and to be checked).
rubric: The code passes the analytic-limit tests at the stated tolerance, and the ODE and quadrature results for the age agree.
rubric: Each observable is compared with a benchmark by a fractional difference, with the agreement criterion stated before the comparison.
rubric: Uncertainty propagation is carried out and explained, including the effect of correlated errors on H_0 and Ω_m.
rubric: The conclusions say what agreement does and does not show, noting degeneracies and neglected physics, and do not overclaim.
tutor: Here is my table of results and my analytic-limit tests. Which sources of numerical error could cause a 0.1% disagreement between my ODE and quadrature ages?
tutor: How should I choose the starting scale factor and initial condition when I integrate a(t) from the radiation era, and how do I show the result does not depend on that choice?
tutor: Check my uncertainty propagation. Is it legitimate to treat H_0 and Ω_m as independent, and how would I report the result if their errors are anticorrelated?

@review
This guide walks through the course week by week, followed by a self-check list and the most common errors. Conventions throughout: signature $(-,+,+,+)$, $a(t_0)=1$, $H_0=100h$ km/s/Mpc, and fiducial values $H_0\approx67.4$, $\Omega_m\approx0.315$, $\Omega_\Lambda\approx0.685$, $T_0\approx2.7255$ K (approximate; check against current data).

### Key results by week

1. **Observations.** Measured versus inferred; $1+z=\lambda_{\rm obs}/\lambda_{\rm emit}$; $cz\simeq H_0d$ at low $z$; $\mu=5\log_{10}(d_L/10\,\mathrm{pc})$; CMB $n_\gamma\approx411\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}$.
2. **FLRW.** The metric, $1+z=1/a_e$, $\chi=c\int dz/H$, $D_M=S_K(\chi)$, $d_L=(1+z)D_M$, $d_A=D_M/(1+z)$.
3. **Friedmann.** The Friedmann, acceleration and fluid equations; $\rho\propto a^{-3(1+w)}$; $\rho_c=3H^2/8\pi G$; $a\propto t^{2/[3(1+w)]}$; $\ddot a>0$ iff $w<-1/3$.
4. **Components.** $E^2(a)=\Omega_ra^{-4}+\Omega_ma^{-3}+\Omega_Ka^{-2}+\Omega_\Lambda$; $1+z_{\rm eq}=\Omega_m/\Omega_r\approx3400$; derivations of the matter and radiation solutions.
5. **Thermal history.** $g_*$ counting, $t\approx2.42\,g_*^{-1/2}(T/\mathrm{MeV})^{-2}$ s, freeze-out at $\Gamma\simeq H$, $T_\nu/T_\gamma=(4/11)^{1/3}$.
6. **Nucleosynthesis.** $n/p=e^{-Q/T}$, freeze-out near $1/6$, decay to $1/7$, $Y_p=2r/(1+r)$, the deuterium bottleneck, and $t=a^2/(2H_0\sqrt{\Omega_r})$ for dating an epoch.
7. **CMB.** Saha recombination, the blackbody and dipole, $c_s$, $\theta_*=r_s/D_M$ and $\ell_A=\pi/\theta_*$.
8. **Perturbations.** Background versus perturbation; $\ddot\delta+2H\dot\delta=4\pi G\bar\rho\delta$; growing mode $\propto a$; $D(a)$ and $k_{\rm eq}$.
9. **Dark matter.** $v_c^2=GM/r$, $\theta_E$, the lines of evidence, $\Omega_\nu h^2=\sum m_\nu/93.14$ eV, halos and the NFW profile; evidence about mass is not evidence about identity.
10. **Dark energy.** $w$, scalar-field $w=(K-V)/(K+V)$, CPL, degeneracies, tension $=\Delta/\sqrt{\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2}$.
11. **Inflation.** $\epsilon<1$, $\epsilon_V$, $\eta_V$, $N$, $n_s-1=-6\epsilon_V+2\eta_V$, $r=16\epsilon_V$, $V^{1/4}$ from $r$.
12. **Particles.** $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$, $m_W=gv/2$, $g_*=106.75$, Sakharov conditions, $\Delta N_{\rm eff}$, and the chain of assumptions from a model to an observable.

### Self-check list

- I can state the metric signature, the normalization of $a$ and the units before doing any calculation.
- I can derive $a\propto t^{2/3}$ and $a\propto t^{1/2}$ and the corresponding $H(t)$.
- I can compute an age, a comoving distance and a luminosity distance by numerical integration.
- I can date an epoch from a temperature and an expansion rate, and say how accurate the estimate is.
- I can say which quantity is background and which is a perturbation, and when linear theory fails.
- I can phrase a result with its parameters, uncertainty, model and data, without claiming more than the data show.

### Common errors

- Mixing proper and comoving lengths, or using the wrong distance ($d_L$ versus $d_A$ versus $D_M$) for an observable.
- Forgetting to convert $H_0$ to s$^{-1}$, or confusing the Planck mass with the reduced Planck mass.
- Dropping neutrinos from the radiation density, or using $T\propto1/a$ across an annihilation epoch.
- Treating $z=1090$ as the epoch where $k_BT=13.6$ eV, or treating a flat rotation curve as the detection of a particle.
- Stating a significance in $\sigma$ without the data, model and priors it assumes, or reading consistency with $w=-1$ as proof of $\Lambda$.

@exam num
prompt: Take a flat universe with $H_0=67.4$ km/s/Mpc, $\Omega_m=0.315$ and $\Omega_\Lambda=0.685$ (radiation neglected), $c=299792.458$ km/s. A Type Ia supernova at $z=0.5$ has absolute magnitude $M=-19.3$. Find its apparent magnitude $m$, to two decimal places.
answer: 23.03
check: -19.3 + 5*log10((1 + 0.5)*299792.458/67.4*quad(lambda z: 1/sqrt(0.315*(1 + z)**3 + 0.685), 0, 0.5)*1e6/10)
tol: 0.02
hint: Compute the comoving distance with the integral $\chi=(c/H_0)\int_0^{0.5}dz/E(z)$ first.
hint: Then $d_L=(1+z)\chi$ for a flat universe and $m=M+5\log_{10}(d_L/10\ \mathrm{pc})$.
solution: The integral $\int_0^{0.5}dz/E(z)=0.4387$ and $c/H_0=4448$ Mpc, so $\chi=1951$ Mpc and $d_L=1.5\chi=2927$ Mpc. Then $\mu=5\log_{10}(2927\times10^{6}/10)=42.33$ and $m=-19.3+42.33=23.03$.

@exam num
prompt: Take a flat universe with $H_0=67.4$ km/s/Mpc, $\Omega_m=0.315$, $\Omega_r=9.2\times10^{-5}$ and $\Omega_\Lambda=1-\Omega_m-\Omega_r$, $c=299792.458$ km/s. A comoving sound horizon $r_s=144.4$ Mpc is seen at last scattering ($z_*=1090$). Compute the acoustic scale $\ell_A=\pi D_M(z_*)/r_s$, to four significant figures.
answer: 301.6
check: pi*299792.458/67.4*quad(lambda z: 1/sqrt(0.315*(1 + z)**3 + 9.2e-5*(1 + z)**4 + (1 - 0.315 - 9.2e-5)), 0, 1090)/144.4
tol: 0.5%
hint: In a flat universe $D_M=\chi=(c/H_0)\int_0^{1090}dz/E(z)$ with $E^2=\Omega_m(1+z)^3+\Omega_r(1+z)^4+\Omega_\Lambda$.
hint: Include the radiation term; it matters at high redshift. Then $\ell_A=\pi D_M/r_s$.
solution: Numerical integration gives $D_M(z_*)=1.386\times10^{4}$ Mpc. Therefore $\theta_*=r_s/D_M=0.01042$ rad and $\ell_A=\pi/\theta_*=301.6$. The first acoustic peak at $\ell\approx220$ lies below this because of the phase shift.

@exam self
prompt: Suppose a new light, decoupled species adds $\Delta N_{\rm eff}=0.5$ to the standard $N_{\rm eff}=3.046$. Trace its effects through the course: (a) the radiation density and expansion rate at nucleosynthesis, and the effect on $Y_p$; (b) the redshift of matter–radiation equality, with a numerical estimate of the fractional change in $1+z_{\rm eq}$ at fixed $\Omega_m h^2$ and $\Omega_\gamma h^2$ (use $\Omega_r h^2=\Omega_\gamma h^2[1+0.2271N_{\rm eff}]$); (c) the consequence for the CMB and the growth of structure; (d) the assumptions you needed, and one sentence stating how you would report a constraint on $\Delta N_{\rm eff}$ without overclaiming.
hint: Work from the radiation density, to the expansion rate, to each observable in turn.
hint: For (b), compare $1+0.2271(3.046)$ with $1+0.2271(3.546)$.
solution: (a) Extra relativistic energy raises $\rho_r$, hence $H$ at a given $T$. Freeze-out of $n/p$ occurs earlier, at higher $T$, so $n/p$ and $Y_p$ increase (by roughly 0.01 per unit of $\Delta N_{\rm eff}$; deuterium also increases). (b) $1+0.2271(3.046)=1.6918$ and $1+0.2271(3.546)=1.8053$, so $\Omega_r$ rises by 6.7% and $1+z_{\rm eq}=\Omega_m/\Omega_r$ falls by about 6.3%. (c) Later equality means a larger radiation fraction at recombination, which raises the early integrated Sachs–Wolfe contribution and changes the acoustic peak heights; the sound horizon $r_s$ shrinks because $H$ is larger; and, at fixed $\theta_*$, small scales are damped relatively more. The matter power spectrum turnover $k_{\rm eq}$ moves to smaller $k$ because $z_{\rm eq}$ is lower. (d) Assumptions: the species is decoupled and relativistic at all times considered, standard thermal history otherwise, no change in $\Omega_b h^2$ or other parameters, and the same $H_0$ and $\Omega_m h^2$. Several parameters ($H_0$, $\Omega_m h^2$, $Y_p$, $n_s$) are degenerate with $N_{\rm eff}$ in CMB fits. Report: "Using CMB and BBN data in flat $\Lambda$CDM with these priors we find $N_{\rm eff}=X\pm Y$, consistent with the standard value" rather than claiming new physics.
rubric: Explains that higher $\rho_r$ raises $H$, freezes out $n/p$ earlier and raises $Y_p$.
rubric: Computes the change in $1+z_{\rm eq}$ correctly (a decrease of about 6%) and states the effect on structure or the CMB.
rubric: Lists assumptions (decoupled relativistic species, standard thermal history, fixed other parameters) and notes degeneracies with $H_0$, $\Omega_m h^2$ and $Y_p$.
rubric: The reporting sentence gives the data, model and priors, and does not overclaim.
