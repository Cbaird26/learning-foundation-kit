@article
Cosmology uses particle physics at three points: the content of the early plasma (which sets $H(T)$), the interactions that decide freeze-out and phase transitions, and the new physics needed for features the Standard Model cannot explain.

### Conventions and inputs

Natural units $\hbar=c=k_B=1$; masses in GeV. Approximate values, to be checked against current data: Higgs vacuum expectation value $v=246.2$ GeV, $m_W=80.4$ GeV, $m_Z=91.19$ GeV, $m_H=125.1$ GeV, SU(2) coupling $g\approx0.653$. The weak mixing angle defined on shell by $\cos\theta_W=m_W/m_Z$ gives $\sin^2\theta_W\approx0.223$; the $\overline{\rm MS}$ value at the $Z$ pole is about $0.231$, so always say which definition is used.

### Gauge structure and matter

The Standard Model is a gauge theory with group $SU(3)_C\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$. It has eight gluons, three $W$ bosons and the $B$ boson, plus a complex Higgs doublet (four real components). Matter comes in three generations of left-handed doublets and right-handed singlets:

| | content per generation |
|---|---|
| quarks | $(u,d)$ in three colours, each with left- and right-handed parts |
| charged leptons | $e,\mu,\tau$ |
| neutrinos | $\nu_e,\nu_\mu,\nu_\tau$ (left-handed only in the minimal model) |

### The Higgs mechanism

The Higgs potential $V=-\mu^2|H|^2+\lambda|H|^4$ is minimized at $|H|=v/\sqrt2$, breaking $SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y\to U(1)_{\rm em}$. The gauge bosons that couple to the broken symmetries acquire mass, while the photon and gluons stay massless:
$$m_W=\frac{gv}{2},\qquad m_Z=\frac{m_W}{\cos\theta_W},\qquad m_H=\sqrt{2\lambda}\,v,\qquad m_f=\frac{y_fv}{\sqrt2}\ \ (\text{Yukawa coupling } y_f).$$
With $m_H=125$ GeV, $\lambda\approx0.13$.

### Where particle physics enters cosmology

- **Degrees of freedom.** Above about 200 GeV, every Standard Model particle is relativistic: $28$ bosonic states (16 from gluons, 8 from the electroweak gauge bosons, 4 from the Higgs doublet) and $90$ fermionic states (72 for quarks, 12 for charged leptons, 6 for neutrinos and antineutrinos), so $g_*=28+\tfrac78\times90=106.75$. Cooling below each mass threshold lowers $g_*$ (week 5). The $Z$ width shows that close to three light neutrino species exist.
- **Phase transitions.** For $m_H=125$ GeV lattice calculations find that the electroweak transition (near $160$ GeV) and the QCD transition (near $155$ MeV at zero chemical potential) are smooth crossovers in the Standard Model, not first-order transitions.
- **Baryogenesis.** The universe has $\eta\approx6\times10^{-10}$ baryons per photon and no primordial antimatter. Sakharov's conditions for generating this from symmetric initial conditions are baryon-number violation, C and CP violation, and departure from thermal equilibrium. The Standard Model contains all three in principle (sphalerons, the CKM phase, the electroweak transition) but not at the strength required, which points to new physics such as leptogenesis.
- **Neutrinos.** Oscillations require nonzero masses ($\sum m_\nu\gtrsim0.06$ eV), outside the minimal model. They affect $N_{\rm eff}$ and structure (week 9).
- **Dark matter.** No Standard Model particle can be the cold dark matter. A thermal relic that freezes out while non-relativistic has $\Omega_\chi h^2\approx3\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}}/\langle\sigma v\rangle$, a rough rule that gives $0.1$ for $\langle\sigma v\rangle=3\times10^{-26}\ \mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}}$.
- **Extra radiation.** A species that decoupled while $g_{*s}=g_d$ has $T_x/T_\nu=(10.75/g_d)^{1/3}$ and contributes $\Delta N_{\rm eff}=\tfrac47g_x(T_x/T_\nu)^4$ if it is a boson, or $\tfrac{g_x}{2}(T_x/T_\nu)^4$ if it is a fermion.
- **Inflation.** A minimally coupled Standard Model Higgs cannot drive slow-roll inflation, since its large quartic coupling would give the wrong $A_s$. Non-minimal couplings or other scalar fields are separate proposals with their own assumptions.

### From model to observable

A prediction is a chain: a particle model, assumptions about initial conditions and the reheating temperature, an interaction rate and a cosmic expansion history, a calculation (freeze-out, nucleosynthesis, a transfer function), and a comparison with data that itself assumes a cosmological model. Each link is an assumption. For example, the relic-density rule above assumes thermal equilibrium at high temperature, radiation domination during freeze-out, no later entropy production, and a single stable species. If any fails, the predicted $\Omega_\chi h^2$ changes. A responsible statement names the chain and says which link a conclusion depends on.

@example
title: Counting g_* above the electroweak scale
problem: Show that $g_*=106.75$ for the Standard Model at $T\gg100$ GeV.
step: Bosons: eight gluons with two polarizations give $16$; the four electroweak gauge bosons (three $W$, one $B$) with two polarizations give $8$; the complex Higgs doublet gives $4$. Total $28$.
step: Quarks: 3 generations $\times$ 2 flavours $\times$ 3 colours $\times$ 2 spin states $\times$ 2 (particle and antiparticle) $=72$.
step: Charged leptons: 3 generations $\times$ 2 spin states $\times$ 2 (particle and antiparticle) $=12$. Neutrinos: 3 species $\times$ 1 helicity $\times$ 2 $=6$. Fermions total $90$.
step: Add with the factor $7/8$ for fermions: $g_*=28+\tfrac78\times90=28+78.75=106.75$.

@example
title: Extra radiation from a decoupled scalar
problem: A real scalar particle ($g_x=1$) decouples while the Standard Model is fully relativistic ($g_{*s}=106.75$). Find its contribution $\Delta N_{\rm eff}$.
step: After decoupling its temperature falls as $1/a$, while the photon and neutrino plasma is reheated as species annihilate. Entropy conservation in the rest of the plasma gives $T_x/T_\nu=(g_{*s}^{\nu\text{-dec}}/g_{*s}^{x\text{-dec}})^{1/3}$ with $g_{*s}=10.75$ at neutrino decoupling.
step: Therefore $T_x/T_\nu=(10.75/106.75)^{1/3}=0.465$ and $(T_x/T_\nu)^4=0.0469$.
step: One neutrino species has energy density $\tfrac78\times2\times\tfrac{\pi^2}{30}T_\nu^4$, so a boson with $g_x=1$ contributes $\Delta N_{\rm eff}=\dfrac{1}{7/4}(T_x/T_\nu)^4=\tfrac47(0.0469)=0.027$.
step: Compare with the measurement precision: $N_{\rm eff}$ is known to a few $\times0.1$ today, so such a species is currently invisible, although future CMB surveys target this level.

@mistakes
- Mixing the two definitions of the weak mixing angle (on-shell versus $\overline{\rm MS}$) and so getting $m_Z$ or $m_W$ wrong by percent-level amounts.
- Forgetting antiparticles, spin states or the factor $7/8$ when counting $g_*$.
- Treating the Standard Model phase transitions as first-order: for $m_H=125$ GeV they are crossovers, so electroweak baryogenesis needs new physics.
- Quoting a relic abundance or a $\Delta N_{\rm eff}$ without stating the assumed thermal history, reheating temperature and decoupling temperature.

@practice self
prompt: A thermal-relic dark matter model predicts $\Omega_\chi h^2\approx3\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{cm^3\,s^{-1}}/\langle\sigma v\rangle$. The CMB infers $\Omega_c h^2\approx0.120$. List the assumptions that connect the model's prediction to that observable, and say what would change if two of them fail.
hint: Walk along the chain: initial conditions, interactions, expansion history, relic calculation, then the comparison with data.
solution: Assumptions: (1) $\chi$ was in thermal equilibrium with the Standard Model plasma at high temperature, which requires a reheating temperature above the freeze-out temperature $T_f\sim m_\chi/20$. (2) Freeze-out occurs during standard radiation domination, so $H(T)$ is given by $g_*$ and the Friedmann equation. (3) $\chi$ is stable and is the only component of the cold dark matter. (4) The annihilation is the only number-changing process (no coannihilations, resonances or thresholds), and $\langle\sigma v\rangle$ is the s-wave value used. (5) No entropy is injected after freeze-out. (6) The observed $\Omega_c h^2$ is inferred assuming a $\Lambda$CDM cosmology with the stated priors. If the expansion was faster at freeze-out (for example an early period dominated by another component), $\chi$ freezes out earlier and its abundance is larger. If late entropy production diluted $\chi$, the abundance is smaller, so the cross-section required to match $0.120$ changes in both cases.
rubric: Lists at least four distinct assumptions, including equilibrium at high temperature, standard radiation-dominated expansion at freeze-out and no later entropy production.
rubric: Includes the assumptions that $\chi$ is stable and is all of the dark matter, and that the observed $\Omega_c h^2$ itself comes from a $\Lambda$CDM fit.
rubric: Explains the direction of the change in the predicted abundance for two failed assumptions.

@quiz num
prompt: A Weyl fermion ($g_x=2$) decouples while $g_{*s}=86.25$ (after the top quark, $W$, $Z$ and Higgs bosons have annihilated) and remains relativistic. Its temperature relative to the neutrinos is $T_x/T_\nu=(10.75/86.25)^{1/3}$, and as a fermion with $g_x=2$ its energy density equals $(T_x/T_\nu)^4$ times that of one neutrino species. Find $\Delta N_{\rm eff}$. Give three significant figures.
answer: 0.0623
check: (7/8*2*(10.75/86.25)**(4/3))/(7/8*2)
tol: 1%
hint: One neutrino species (neutrino plus antineutrino) has $g=2$ with the factor $7/8$.
solution: The energy density of $x$ divided by that of one neutrino species is $[\tfrac78g_xT_x^4]/[\tfrac78\cdot2\,T_\nu^4]=(g_x/2)(T_x/T_\nu)^4=(10.75/86.25)^{4/3}=0.0623$.

@quiz ms
prompt: Select all correct statements about the Standard Model and cosmology.
choice*: The Standard Model contains no particle that can be the cold dark matter.
choice*: For $m_H=125$ GeV the Standard Model electroweak transition is a smooth crossover rather than a first-order transition.
choice*: Neutrino oscillations show that at least two neutrino species have nonzero mass, which goes beyond the minimal model.
choice: The Higgs mechanism gives mass to the photon and the gluons.
choice: The Standard Model alone explains the observed baryon-to-photon ratio quantitatively.
hint: Check each statement against the Higgs mechanism and the Sakharov conditions.
solution: The statements that no Standard Model particle can be the cold dark matter, that the electroweak transition is a crossover for $m_H=125$ GeV, and that oscillations require at least two massive neutrinos are correct. The photon and gluons stay massless because the corresponding symmetries remain unbroken. The Standard Model has the Sakharov ingredients but too little CP violation and a crossover instead of a first-order transition, so it falls short of the observed asymmetry.
