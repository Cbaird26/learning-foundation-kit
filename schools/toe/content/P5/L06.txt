@lesson
title: Central forces
summary: Reduce a two-body central-force problem to one body, and use the cyclic angle to obtain conserved angular momentum, equal areas and the radial equation.
objective: Reduce a two-body problem to the motion of a single particle with the reduced mass.
objective: Show that the angle is cyclic, so the angular momentum $\ell=\mu r^2\dot\phi$ is conserved, and use it to find equal areas in equal times.
objective: Write and use the radial equation and the energy for motion in a central potential.

@article
A **central force** points along the line joining two bodies and depends only on their separation $r$: $\mathbf F=-V'(r)\,\hat{\mathbf r}$. Gravity and the Coulomb force are examples. Symmetry makes these problems almost solvable on their own.

### Step 1: from two bodies to one

Two bodies of masses $m_1,m_2$ interact through $V(|\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2|)$. Use the centre of mass and the separation:

$$\mathbf R=\frac{m_1\mathbf r_1+m_2\mathbf r_2}{m_1+m_2},\qquad \mathbf r=\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2.$$

The Lagrangian becomes

$$L=\tfrac12 M\dot{\mathbf R}^2+\tfrac12\mu\dot{\mathbf r}^2-V(r),\qquad M=m_1+m_2,\quad \mu=\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}.$$

The centre of mass $\mathbf R$ is cyclic, so it moves uniformly. The relative motion is that of **one** particle of mass $\mu$, the **reduced mass**, in the potential $V(r)$. If $m_2\gg m_1$ then $\mu\approx m_1$.

### Step 2: the motion is planar

$L$ for the relative motion depends on $\mathbf r$ and $\dot{\mathbf r}$ only through lengths, so it is invariant under all rotations. Noether's theorem conserves the vector $\mathbf J=\mu\,\mathbf r\times\dot{\mathbf r}$. Both $\mathbf r$ and $\dot{\mathbf r}$ are perpendicular to this fixed vector, so the orbit lies in a plane.

### Step 3: polar coordinates in the plane

$$L=\tfrac12\mu\left(\dot r^2+r^2\dot\phi^2\right)-V(r).$$

The angle $\phi$ is cyclic, so

$$\ell=\mu r^2\dot\phi=\text{constant}.$$

Two consequences follow.

- **Equal areas.** The radius vector sweeps area at the rate $\dot A=\tfrac12 r^2\dot\phi=\dfrac{\ell}{2\mu}$, which is constant for every central force (Kepler's second law).
- **Radial equation.** The Euler–Lagrange equation for $r$ is $\mu\ddot r=\mu r\dot\phi^2-V'(r)$. Eliminating $\dot\phi$ with $\ell$,

$$\mu\ddot r=\frac{\ell^2}{\mu r^3}-V'(r).$$

The first term is the "centrifugal" term. The conserved energy is

$$E=\tfrac12\mu\dot r^2+\frac{\ell^2}{2\mu r^2}+V(r).$$

Note the order of operations: substitute $\dot\phi=\ell/(\mu r^2)$ into the **equation of motion** or into the energy, never into $L$ before varying $r$. For a circular orbit $\ddot r=0$, which gives $\mu r\dot\phi^2=V'(r)$.

@example
title: Reducing a two-body Lagrangian
problem: Show that $T=\tfrac12 m_1\dot{\mathbf r}_1^2+\tfrac12 m_2\dot{\mathbf r}_2^2$ equals $\tfrac12 M\dot{\mathbf R}^2+\tfrac12\mu\dot{\mathbf r}^2$.
step: Invert the definitions of $\mathbf R$ and $\mathbf r$: $\mathbf r_1=\mathbf R+\dfrac{m_2}{M}\mathbf r$ and $\mathbf r_2=\mathbf R-\dfrac{m_1}{M}\mathbf r$.
step: Differentiate and substitute: $T=\tfrac12 m_1\left(\dot{\mathbf R}+\tfrac{m_2}{M}\dot{\mathbf r}\right)^2+\tfrac12 m_2\left(\dot{\mathbf R}-\tfrac{m_1}{M}\dot{\mathbf r}\right)^2$.
step: Cross terms: $\dot{\mathbf R}\cdot\dot{\mathbf r}\left(\dfrac{m_1m_2}{M}-\dfrac{m_2m_1}{M}\right)=0$.
step: Remaining terms: $\tfrac12(m_1+m_2)\dot{\mathbf R}^2+\tfrac12\dot{\mathbf r}^2\,\dfrac{m_1m_2^2+m_2m_1^2}{M^2}=\tfrac12 M\dot{\mathbf R}^2+\tfrac12\dfrac{m_1m_2}{M}\dot{\mathbf r}^2$.
step: Since $\mathbf R$ is absent from $V$, it is cyclic, and the relative coordinate behaves like one particle of mass $\mu=m_1m_2/M$.

@example
title: Angular momentum and areal speed
problem: A particle of mass $0.5\ \text{kg}$ moves in a central field. At $r=2\ \text{m}$ its velocity is purely tangential with speed $3\ \text{m/s}$. Later it is again moving tangentially, at $r=4\ \text{m}$. Find $\ell$, the area swept per second, and the later speed.
step: At a point where the velocity is tangential, $\dot\phi=v/r$, so $\ell=mr^2\dot\phi=mrv=0.5\cdot2\cdot3=3\ \text{kg m}^2/\text{s}$.
step: The areal rate is $\dot A=\ell/(2m)=3/(2\cdot0.5)=3\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}$.
step: Angular momentum is conserved, so at $r=4\ \text{m}$ the tangential speed satisfies $mrv=3$, giving $v=3/(0.5\cdot4)=1.5\ \text{m/s}$.
step: Check with the area rate: $\tfrac12 rv=\tfrac12\cdot4\cdot1.5=3\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}$. It matches.

@mistakes
- Substituting $\dot\phi=\ell/(\mu r^2)$ into $L$ and then varying $r$; this flips the sign of the centrifugal term. Substitute only after deriving the equation of motion.
- Using $m_1$ or $m_2$ instead of the reduced mass $\mu$ for the relative motion.
- Writing the areal rate as $r^2\dot\phi$ and forgetting the factor $\tfrac12$.

@practice self
prompt: Starting from $L=\tfrac12\mu(\dot r^2+r^2\dot\phi^2)-V(r)$, show that $\ell=\mu r^2\dot\phi$ is constant and that the radius vector sweeps out equal areas in equal times.
hint: Is $\phi$ present in $L$? Write the Euler–Lagrange equation for $\phi$.
hint: For the area, find the area of the thin triangle swept in a time $dt$ when the angle changes by $d\phi$.
solution: The angle $\phi$ does not appear in $L$, so $\partial L/\partial\phi=0$ and the Euler–Lagrange equation gives $\frac{d}{dt}\left(\mu r^2\dot\phi\right)=0$, so $\ell=\mu r^2\dot\phi$ is constant. In time $dt$ the radius vector turns through $d\phi=\dot\phi\,dt$ and sweeps a thin triangle of base $r\,d\phi$ and height $r$, so $dA=\tfrac12 r^2\,d\phi$. Then $\dot A=\tfrac12 r^2\dot\phi=\ell/(2\mu)$, which is constant.
rubric: Notes that $\phi$ is absent from $L$ and so is cyclic, with $\partial L/\partial\dot\phi=\mu r^2\dot\phi$.
rubric: Applies the Euler–Lagrange equation to get $\frac{d}{dt}(\mu r^2\dot\phi)=0$.
rubric: Derives $dA=\tfrac12 r^2\,d\phi$ from the triangle geometry, including the factor $\tfrac12$.
rubric: Concludes that $\dot A=\ell/(2\mu)$ is constant.

@quiz num
prompt: A small satellite moves on a circular orbit of radius $7.0\times10^{6}\ \text{m}$ about the Earth, with $V=-GMm/r$ and $GM=3.986\times10^{14}\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}^2$. Neglect the motion of the Earth. Find the orbital period in seconds.
answer: 5829
unit: s
tol: 1%
check: 2*pi/root(lambda w: w**2*7.0e6 - 3.986e14/7.0e6**2, 1e-5, 1e-1)
hint: For a circular orbit $\ddot r=0$, so the radial equation reduces to $mr\dot\phi^2=V'(r)$.
hint: Here $V'(r)=GMm/r^2$, so $\dot\phi^2=GM/r^3$. The period is $2\pi/\dot\phi$.
solution: $\dot\phi=\sqrt{GM/r^3}=\sqrt{3.986\times10^{14}/(3.43\times10^{20})}\approx1.078\times10^{-3}\ \text{rad/s}$, so the period is $2\pi/\dot\phi\approx5829\ \text{s}$.

@quiz ms
prompt: A particle of mass $\mu$ moves in a central potential $V(r)$. Select **all** quantities that are conserved.
choice*: The energy $E=\tfrac12\mu\dot r^2+\tfrac12\mu r^2\dot\phi^2+V(r)$
choice*: The angular momentum $\mu r^2\dot\phi$ about the centre
choice*: The direction of the angular momentum vector
choice: The linear momentum $\mu\dot{\mathbf r}$
choice: The radial momentum $\mu\dot r$
hint: Ask which symmetries the Lagrangian has: shifts in time, rotations, or translations in space.
solution: Time translation gives $E$, and rotation invariance gives the conserved angular momentum vector (so both its size $\mu r^2\dot\phi$ and its direction are constant). The potential is centred on a fixed point, so translations are not a symmetry and $\mu\dot{\mathbf r}$ changes. The radial momentum obeys $\mu\ddot r=\ell^2/(\mu r^3)-V'(r)$, which is not zero in general.
