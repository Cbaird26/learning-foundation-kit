title: Small oscillations of a coupled system by two methods
overview: You will choose a coupled mechanical system with at least two degrees of freedom and derive its small oscillations twice: once by Lagrangian normal-mode analysis and once from the linearised Hamiltonian flow. You will then verify the normal coordinates with Poisson brackets and test the linear theory against a numerical solution of the full nonlinear motion.
goal: Derive exact equations of motion from a Lagrangian and linearise them about a stable equilibrium.
goal: Obtain the normal-mode frequencies and shapes by two independent methods and show that they agree.
goal: Show with Poisson brackets that the normal coordinates and their momenta form a canonical set, and that the Hamiltonian splits into independent oscillators.
goal: Test the linear theory against a numerical simulation of the full nonlinear equations.
step: Choose a system with at least two degrees of freedom: a double pendulum with unequal masses or lengths, two pendulums of equal length joined by a spring, or three masses in a line joined by springs between two walls. Fix numerical values so that the mass matrix is not a multiple of the identity, draw the system, count the degrees of freedom, and choose generalised coordinates that satisfy the constraints automatically.
step: Write $T$ and $V$ exactly, form $L=T-V$, derive the Euler–Lagrange equations, and find the equilibrium from $\partial V/\partial q_i=0$. Decide whether it is stable.
step: Method 1, Lagrangian. Expand to second order to obtain $M$ and $K$, solve $\det(K-\omega^{2}M)=0$, find every mode shape, and check that $\mathbf a^{(s)T}M\mathbf a^{(r)}=0$ for different modes.
step: Method 2, Hamiltonian. Compute the canonical momenta and the Hamiltonian, write Hamilton's equations, and linearise them about the equilibrium as $\dot{\mathbf z}=A\mathbf z$ with $\mathbf z=(\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf p)$. Find the eigenvalues $\pm i\omega_s$ of $A$ and compare them with Method 1.
step: Normalise the modes by $\mathbf a^{(s)T}M\mathbf a^{(s)}=1$, define normal coordinates through $\boldsymbol\eta=\sum_s\xi_s\mathbf a^{(s)}$ with conjugate momenta $\pi_s$, and verify with Poisson brackets that $\{\xi_r,\xi_s\}=0$, $\{\pi_r,\pi_s\}=0$ and $\{\xi_r,\pi_s\}=\delta_{rs}$. Show that the quadratic Hamiltonian becomes $\sum_s\tfrac12(\pi_s^{2}+\omega_s^{2}\xi_s^{2})$.
step: Write a Python script that builds $M$ and $K$ and solves the generalised eigenproblem with `scipy.linalg.eigh`, builds $A$ and finds its eigenvalues with `numpy.linalg.eigvals`, and integrates the full nonlinear equations with `scipy.integrate.solve_ivp` from a small displacement along one normal mode. Measure the oscillation frequency from the simulation and compare it with the prediction.
step: Repeat the simulation with a large amplitude. Describe where the linear theory fails (frequency shift, mixing of modes), and monitor the conservation of $H$ as a check on the numerics.
step: Write the report: setup, both derivations, a table comparing the frequencies and mode shapes from Method 1, Method 2 and the simulation, and a short discussion of what the agreement of the two methods shows.
deliverable: A written report of about four to six pages containing both derivations in full, the comparison table, the equilibrium classification, and the bracket verification.
deliverable: A commented Python script or notebook that reproduces every number in the report, including the plot of the simulated motion along one normal mode.
deliverable: Notes for a five-minute oral explanation of one major result, such as why normal modes are orthogonal with respect to $M$, or why the bracket test confirms the normal-coordinate transformation, written so that you can give the explanation without them.

rubric: Method 1: $M$ and $K$ are symmetric and correct, with positive definiteness checked; the secular equation is solved correctly; mode shapes are found and their $M$-orthogonality is verified.
rubric: Method 2: the canonical momenta and the Hamiltonian are correct; the linearised matrix $A$ is built correctly; its eigenvalues agree with Method 1, and the reason for the agreement is explained.
rubric: Canonical structure: the normal coordinates and momenta satisfy the fundamental bracket relations, and the Hamiltonian is shown to be a sum of independent oscillators.
rubric: Numerical check: the simulated frequencies agree with the predictions within a stated tolerance, the large-amplitude behaviour is described, and energy conservation is monitored.
rubric: Communication: conventions, units and signs are stated, every step is justified, and the report can be followed without reading the code.

@review
### Week by week

- **Weeks 1–2, coordinates and constraints.** A configuration is described by independent generalised coordinates $q_i$. For $N$ particles with $k$ independent holonomic constraints there are $3N-k$ degrees of freedom. Choose coordinates that satisfy the constraints automatically; constraint forces can be recovered with Lagrange multipliers when needed.
- **Weeks 3–4, variational principles.** A functional $\int F(y,y',x)\,dx$ is stationary when $\frac{d}{dx}\frac{\partial F}{\partial y'}-\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}=0$. Hamilton's principle for $S=\int L\,dt$ gives $\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}=0$, with $L=T-V$.
- **Weeks 5–6, symmetry and central forces.** A cyclic coordinate has a conserved momentum $p_i=\partial L/\partial\dot q_i$; continuous symmetries give conserved quantities. For a central force $\ell=mr^{2}\dot\theta$ is conserved and the motion is planar.
- **Week 7, effective potentials.** $U_{\text{eff}}=V+\ell^{2}/(2mr^{2})$. Turning points solve $E=U_{\text{eff}}$; circular orbits have $U_{\text{eff}}'=0$ and are stable when $U_{\text{eff}}''>0$, with $\omega_r=\sqrt{U_{\text{eff}}''/m}$.
- **Week 8, small oscillations.** $M\ddot{\boldsymbol\eta}=-K\boldsymbol\eta$, $\det(K-\omega^{2}M)=0$, modes orthogonal with respect to $M$, and stability read from the signs of $\omega^{2}$.
- **Week 9, rigid bodies.** $T=\frac12MV^{2}+\frac12\boldsymbol\omega^{T}I\boldsymbol\omega$, principal axes, Euler's equations, and stable spin about the largest and smallest moments only.
- **Week 10, Hamiltonian mechanics.** $p_i=\partial L/\partial\dot q_i$, $H=\sum p_i\dot q_i-L$ with velocities eliminated, $\dot q_i=\partial H/\partial p_i$ and $\dot p_i=-\partial H/\partial q_i$.
- **Week 11, Poisson brackets.** $\dot f=\{f,H\}+\partial f/\partial t$, $\{q_i,p_j\}=\delta_{ij}$, antisymmetry, Leibniz and Jacobi, and $\{L_i,L_j\}=\epsilon_{ijk}L_k$.
- **Week 12, canonical transformations.** The test is $\{Q_i,P_j\}=\delta_{ij}$ with $\{Q,Q\}=\{P,P\}=0$; generating functions $F_1$ and $F_2$ give the transformation and $K=H+\partial F/\partial t$.

### Self-check

- Can I count degrees of freedom and defend my choice of coordinates?
- Can I derive the equations of motion of a new system from $L=T-V$ without notes?
- Given a symmetry, can I name and verify the conserved quantity?
- Can I sketch $U_{\text{eff}}$ and read off the types of orbit?
- Can I linearise about an equilibrium, build $M$ and $K$, and find the normal modes?
- Can I go from $L$ to $H$, and decide whether a transformation is canonical?

### Common errors

- Substituting a conserved quantity into $L$ before varying, and getting a wrong sign.
- Testing stability with $V''$ instead of $U_{\text{eff}}''$, or reporting $\omega^{2}$ as a frequency.
- Treating the inertia tensor as a number, or applying Euler's equations outside the body frame.
- Leaving velocities in $H$, or sign slips in Hamilton's equations.
- Accepting $Q=p$, $P=q$ as canonical; the bracket is $-1$.

@exam self
prompt: A spherical pendulum is a particle of mass $m$ on a light rigid rod of length $\ell$ that pivots freely about a fixed point. Let $\theta$ be the angle from the downward vertical and $\phi$ the azimuth. (a) State the number of degrees of freedom and write the Lagrangian. (b) Identify the cyclic coordinate and the conserved quantities. (c) Find the canonical momenta and the Hamiltonian, and use a Poisson bracket to confirm that $p_\phi$ is conserved. (d) Write the effective potential $U_{\text{eff}}(\theta)$ for the $\theta$ motion and show that steady conical motion at angle $\theta_0$ requires $p_\phi^{2}=m^{2}g\ell^{3}\sin^{4}\theta_0/\cos\theta_0$.
hint: Position the particle with spherical coordinates and set $r=\ell$ to remove the constraint, then write $T$ and $V=-mg\ell\cos\theta$.
hint: For (d), hold $p_\phi$ fixed so that $H=p_\theta^{2}/(2m\ell^{2})+U_{\text{eff}}(\theta)$, and impose $U_{\text{eff}}'(\theta_0)=0$.
solution: (a) Three coordinates minus one constraint $r=\ell$ leaves two degrees of freedom, $(\theta,\phi)$. The velocity is $\ell\dot\theta$ along $\hat\theta$ and $\ell\sin\theta\,\dot\phi$ along $\hat\phi$, so $L=\tfrac12m\ell^{2}(\dot\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,\dot\phi^{2})+mg\ell\cos\theta$. (b) $\phi$ does not appear in $L$, so $p_\phi=m\ell^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\,\dot\phi$ (the vertical component of angular momentum) is conserved; $L$ has no explicit time dependence, so the energy is conserved too. (c) $p_\theta=m\ell^{2}\dot\theta$ and $p_\phi=m\ell^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\,\dot\phi$, so $H=\dfrac{p_\theta^{2}}{2m\ell^{2}}+\dfrac{p_\phi^{2}}{2m\ell^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}-mg\ell\cos\theta$. Since $H$ does not contain $\phi$, $\{p_\phi,H\}=-\partial H/\partial\phi=0$. (d) $U_{\text{eff}}(\theta)=\dfrac{p_\phi^{2}}{2m\ell^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}-mg\ell\cos\theta$. Setting $U_{\text{eff}}'(\theta_0)=-\dfrac{p_\phi^{2}\cos\theta_0}{m\ell^{2}\sin^{3}\theta_0}+mg\ell\sin\theta_0=0$ gives $p_\phi^{2}=\dfrac{m^{2}g\ell^{3}\sin^{4}\theta_0}{\cos\theta_0}$.
rubric: Counts two degrees of freedom and writes the correct Lagrangian.
rubric: Identifies $\phi$ as cyclic and names $p_\phi$ and the energy as conserved.
rubric: Obtains the correct momenta and Hamiltonian, and confirms $\{p_\phi,H\}=0$ with the bracket.
rubric: Builds $U_{\text{eff}}(\theta)$ and solves $U_{\text{eff}}'(\theta_0)=0$ for $p_\phi^{2}$.

prompt: A spherical pendulum has a bob of mass $m=2\ \text{kg}$ on a light rod of length $\ell=0.5\ \text{m}$. Take $g=9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$. The bob moves in steady conical motion at $\theta_0=60^\circ$ from the downward vertical. A small kick along the $\theta$ direction, which leaves $p_\phi$ unchanged, makes $\theta$ oscillate slightly about $\theta_0$. Find the angular frequency of these oscillations, to three significant figures.
answer: 8.28
check: (lambda P2: sqrt(nderiv(lambda t: P2/(2*0.5*sin(t)**2) - 2*9.8*0.5*cos(t), pi/3, 2)/0.5))(root(lambda P2: nderiv(lambda t: P2/(2*0.5*sin(t)**2) - 2*9.8*0.5*cos(t), pi/3), 0.01, 1000))
unit: rad/s
tol: 1%
hint: Reduce the $\theta$ motion to one dimension with $U_{\text{eff}}(\theta)=\dfrac{p_\phi^{2}}{2m\ell^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}-mg\ell\cos\theta$, where $p_\phi$ is constant.
hint: The steady conical motion fixes $p_\phi^{2}$ through $U_{\text{eff}}'(\theta_0)=0$. Differentiate a second time with $p_\phi$ held fixed, and only then eliminate $p_\phi^{2}$ using that condition.
hint: The small-oscillation frequency is $\omega^{2}=U_{\text{eff}}''(\theta_0)/(m\ell^{2})$.
solution: $U_{\text{eff}}'(\theta_0)=0$ gives $p_\phi^{2}=m^{2}g\ell^{3}\sin^{4}\theta_0/\cos\theta_0$. Then $U_{\text{eff}}''(\theta_0)=\dfrac{p_\phi^{2}}{m\ell^{2}}\dfrac{\sin^{2}\theta_0+3\cos^{2}\theta_0}{\sin^{4}\theta_0}+mg\ell\cos\theta_0=mg\ell\,\dfrac{1+3\cos^{2}\theta_0}{\cos\theta_0}$. Hence $\omega^{2}=\dfrac{g}{\ell}\,\dfrac{1+3\cos^{2}\theta_0}{\cos\theta_0}=\dfrac{9.8}{0.5}\cdot\dfrac{1.75}{0.5}=68.6\ \text{s}^{-2}$, and $\omega=8.28\ \text{rad/s}$.

prompt: A torque-free axisymmetric body has $I_1=I_2=4\ \text{kg m}^2$ and $I_3=5\ \text{kg m}^2$. In z–x–z Euler angles $(\phi,\theta,\psi)$ its kinetic energy is $T=\tfrac12I_1(\dot\theta^{2}+\dot\phi^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)+\tfrac12I_3(\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta)^{2}$. Its angular momentum has magnitude $L=20\ \text{kg m}^2/\text{s}$ and points along the space-fixed $z$ axis, and the symmetry axis stays at the constant angle $\theta=30^\circ$ from the $z$ axis. Find the spin rate $\dot\psi$.
answer: -sqrt(3)/2
check: np.linalg.solve(np.array([[4*sin(pi/6)**2 + 5*cos(pi/6)**2, 5*cos(pi/6)], [5*cos(pi/6), 5]]), np.array([20.0, 20*cos(pi/6)]))[1]

tol: 1%
hint: $\phi$ and $\psi$ are cyclic, so $p_\phi$ and $p_\psi$ are constant. Here $p_\phi$ is the $z$ component of $\mathbf L$ and $p_\psi=I_3\omega_3$ is its component along the symmetry axis.
hint: With $\mathbf L$ along $z$, $p_\phi=L$ and $p_\psi=L\cos\theta$. Write both as linear expressions in $\dot\phi$ and $\dot\psi$.
hint: Solve the two linear equations for $\dot\phi$ and $\dot\psi$.
solution: The momenta are $p_\psi=I_3(\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta)$ and $p_\phi=I_1\dot\phi\sin^{2}\theta+p_\psi\cos\theta$. Setting $p_\phi=L$ and $p_\psi=L\cos\theta$ gives $I_1\dot\phi\sin^{2}\theta=L\sin^{2}\theta$, so $\dot\phi=L/I_1=5\ \text{rad/s}$. Then $\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta=L\cos\theta/I_3$, so $\dot\psi=L\cos\theta\left(\dfrac1{I_3}-\dfrac1{I_1}\right)=20\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot\left(-\dfrac1{20}\right)=-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\approx-0.866\ \text{rad/s}$.
