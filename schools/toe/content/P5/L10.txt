@article
The Lagrangian uses coordinates and velocities and gives $n$ second-order equations. The Hamiltonian uses coordinates and **momenta** and gives $2n$ first-order equations. The arena is **phase space**, with points $(q_1,\dots,q_n,p_1,\dots,p_n)$. First-order equations are easier to analyse and to integrate numerically, and they are the starting point for statistical and quantum mechanics.

### Canonical momentum

The **canonical momentum** conjugate to $q_i$ is

$$p_i=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}.$$

For $L=\tfrac12m\dot x^{2}-V$ this is $m\dot x$, but in general it is not mass times velocity. In polar coordinates, $p_\theta=mr^{2}\dot\theta$ is an angular momentum.

### The Legendre transform

The **Hamiltonian** is

$$H(q,p,t)=\sum_ip_i\dot q_i-L,$$

with every velocity eliminated in favour of the momenta. The recipe is: (1) compute each $p_i$; (2) solve for $\dot q_i(q,p)$; (3) substitute into $\sum p_i\dot q_i-L$ and check that no dot remains.

### Hamilton's equations

$$\dot q_i=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i},\qquad\dot p_i=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_i},\qquad\frac{dH}{dt}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}.$$

Three consequences follow at once:

- If $H$ has no explicit time dependence, $H$ is conserved.
- If $H$ does not contain $q_i$ (a **cyclic** coordinate), then $\dot p_i=0$ and $p_i$ is conserved. This is the week-5 result in one line. The coordinate $q_i$ itself still moves, at rate $\partial H/\partial p_i$.
- A trajectory stays on the level set $H=E$ of phase space, so the shape of that set shows the possible motions.

### When $H$ is not the energy

If $T$ is a homogeneous quadratic function of the velocities and $V$ does not depend on them, then $H=T+V$, written in terms of momenta. Otherwise $H$ may differ from the energy. For a bead of unit mass on a vertical unit hoop that rotates at constant angular speed $\Omega$, the rotation adds $\tfrac12\Omega^{2}\sin^{2}\theta$ to $L$ with no velocity attached, and

$$H=\tfrac12p^{2}-\tfrac12\Omega^{2}\sin^{2}\theta-g\cos\theta .$$

This $H$ is conserved, but it is not $T+V$, which contains $+\tfrac12\Omega^{2}\sin^{2}\theta$ and is not conserved. The agent that turns the hoop does work on the bead.

@example
title: A particle in a central potential
problem: Find the canonical momenta, the Hamiltonian and Hamilton's equations for a particle of mass $m$ with $L=\tfrac12m(\dot r^{2}+r^{2}\dot\theta^{2})-V(r)$.
step: Differentiate the Lagrangian with respect to each velocity: $p_r=m\dot r$ and $p_\theta=mr^{2}\dot\theta$.
step: Invert: $\dot r=p_r/m$ and $\dot\theta=p_\theta/(mr^{2})$.
step: Form the Legendre transform: $H=p_r\dot r+p_\theta\dot\theta-L=\dfrac{p_r^{2}}{m}+\dfrac{p_\theta^{2}}{mr^{2}}-\dfrac{p_r^{2}}{2m}-\dfrac{p_\theta^{2}}{2mr^{2}}+V$, so $H=\dfrac{p_r^{2}}{2m}+\dfrac{p_\theta^{2}}{2mr^{2}}+V(r)$.
step: Hamilton's equations: $\dot r=\dfrac{p_r}{m}$, $\dot p_r=\dfrac{p_\theta^{2}}{mr^{3}}-V'(r)$, $\dot\theta=\dfrac{p_\theta}{mr^{2}}$ and $\dot p_\theta=0$.
step: The angle $\theta$ is cyclic, so $p_\theta=\ell$ is conserved. Then $H=\dfrac{p_r^{2}}{2m}+U_{\text{eff}}(r)$ with the effective potential of week 7, and $H=E$.

@example
title: The heavy symmetric top
problem: A symmetric top with a fixed pivot has $L=\tfrac12I_1(\dot\theta^{2}+\dot\phi^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)+\tfrac12I_3(\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta)^{2}-Mgl\cos\theta$, where $\theta$ is measured from the upward vertical. Find the canonical momenta, the Hamiltonian and the conserved quantities.
step: The momenta are $p_\theta=I_1\dot\theta$, $p_\psi=I_3(\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta)$ and $p_\phi=I_1\dot\phi\sin^{2}\theta+I_3(\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta)\cos\theta=I_1\dot\phi\sin^{2}\theta+p_\psi\cos\theta$.
step: Invert: $\dot\theta=p_\theta/I_1$, $\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta=p_\psi/I_3$ and $\dot\phi=\dfrac{p_\phi-p_\psi\cos\theta}{I_1\sin^{2}\theta}$.
step: The kinetic energy is quadratic in the velocities and $V$ contains none, so $H=T+V$ with $T$ written through the momenta. Substituting the inversions gives $T=\dfrac{p_\theta^{2}}{2I_1}+\dfrac{(p_\phi-p_\psi\cos\theta)^{2}}{2I_1\sin^{2}\theta}+\dfrac{p_\psi^{2}}{2I_3}$.
step: The Hamiltonian is $H=\dfrac{p_\theta^{2}}{2I_1}+\dfrac{(p_\phi-p_\psi\cos\theta)^{2}}{2I_1\sin^{2}\theta}+\dfrac{p_\psi^{2}}{2I_3}+Mgl\cos\theta$.
step: Neither $\phi$ nor $\psi$ appears in $H$, so $p_\phi$, $p_\psi$ and $H$ are all conserved. What remains is a one-dimensional motion in $\theta$ with an effective potential, exactly as in week 7.

@mistakes
- Leaving velocities in $H$. The Hamiltonian must be a function of $q$, $p$ and $t$ only, so invert the momenta before substituting.
- Writing $p=m\dot q$ for every coordinate. For an angle, $p_\theta=mr^{2}\dot\theta$, and with extra terms in $L$ the momentum differs from mass times velocity.
- Writing $H=T+V$ without checking that $T$ is quadratic in the velocities. In a rotating frame the result can differ from the energy.
- Slipping a sign in Hamilton's equations: $\dot q=+\partial H/\partial p$ but $\dot p=-\partial H/\partial q$.

choice: $H=\dfrac{p^{2}}{2a}-\tfrac12bx^{2}$

With $\theta$ measured from the lowest point, the Lagrangian is $L=\tfrac12\dot\theta^{2}+\tfrac12\Omega^{2}\sin^{2}\theta+g\cos\theta$. Find the Hamiltonian $H(\theta,p)$.
vars: p, theta, Omega, g
lead: $H =$
answer: p^2/2 - Omega^2*sin(theta)^2/2 - g*cos(theta)
check: lambda p, theta, Omega, g: p*p - (0.5*p**2 + 0.5*Omega**2*sin(theta)**2 + g*cos(theta))
hint: Compute $p=\partial L/\partial\dot\theta$ and solve for $\dot\theta$.

@practice self
prompt: (a) Using Hamilton's equations, show that $\dfrac{dH}{dt}=\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial t}$ for $H(q_1,\dots,q_n,p_1,\dots,p_n,t)$. (b) Suppose $T$ is a homogeneous quadratic function of the velocities and $V$ does not depend on them. Show that $H=T+V$.
hint: For (a), apply the chain rule to $H$ and substitute $\dot q_i$ and $\dot p_i$ from Hamilton's equations.
hint: For (b), use Euler's theorem for a homogeneous function of degree 2: $\sum_i\dot q_i\,\partial T/\partial\dot q_i=2T$.
solution: (a) By the chain rule $\dfrac{dH}{dt}=\sum_i\left(\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial q_i}\dot q_i+\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}\dot p_i\right)+\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial t}$. Substituting $\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial q_i}=-\dot p_i$ and $\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}=\dot q_i$ gives $\sum_i(-\dot p_i\dot q_i+\dot q_i\dot p_i)=0$, so $dH/dt=\partial H/\partial t$. (b) Here $p_i=\partial T/\partial\dot q_i$, so $\sum_ip_i\dot q_i=\sum_i\dot q_i\,\partial T/\partial\dot q_i=2T$. Then $H=2T-L=2T-(T-V)=T+V$.
rubric: Applies the chain rule to $H(q,p,t)$ with all three kinds of argument.
rubric: Substitutes Hamilton's equations so the $\dot q_i\dot p_i$ terms cancel and only $\partial H/\partial t$ remains.
rubric: In (b), uses $p_i=\partial T/\partial\dot q_i$ (because $V$ has no velocity dependence) together with Euler's theorem to get $\sum p_i\dot q_i=2T$.
rubric: Concludes $H=2T-(T-V)=T+V$.

@quiz num
prompt: A system with two coordinates has Lagrangian $L=\tfrac12\left(2\dot q_1^{2}+2\dot q_1\dot q_2+\dot q_2^{2}\right)-V(q_1,q_2)$, in units where every quantity is dimensionless. Its Hamiltonian is $H=T(p)+V$. Find the kinetic part $T(p)$ at $p_1=3$, $p_2=2$.
answer: 5/2
check: (lambda qd: 0.5*(2*qd[0]**2 + 2*qd[0]*qd[1] + qd[1]**2))(np.linalg.solve(np.array([[2.0, 1.0], [1.0, 1.0]]), np.array([3.0, 2.0])))
hint: Compute $p_1=\partial L/\partial\dot q_1$ and $p_2=\partial L/\partial\dot q_2$, then solve the two linear equations for $\dot q_1$ and $\dot q_2$.
hint: You should find $p_1=2\dot q_1+\dot q_2$ and $p_2=\dot q_1+\dot q_2$. Insert the velocities into $T$.
solution: $p_1=2\dot q_1+\dot q_2=3$ and $p_2=\dot q_1+\dot q_2=2$ give $\dot q_1=1$ and $\dot q_2=1$. Then $T=\tfrac12(2+2+1)=\tfrac52$. Equivalently $T=\tfrac12\mathbf p^{T}M^{-1}\mathbf p$ with $M=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $M^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}$.

@quiz ms
prompt: A particle has Hamiltonian $H=\dfrac{p_x^{2}+p_y^{2}}{2m}+\tfrac12kx^{2}$, with no explicit time dependence. Which of these quantities are conserved? Select all that apply.
choice*: $H$
choice*: $p_y$
choice: $p_x$
choice: $y$
hint: Check each variable for appearance in $H$, and use $\dot p_i=-\partial H/\partial q_i$ and $\dot q_i=\partial H/\partial p_i$.
hint: A coordinate that does not appear in $H$ has a conserved momentum, but the coordinate itself moves at rate $p/m$.
solution: $H$ is conserved because it has no explicit time dependence. The coordinate $y$ is cyclic, so $\dot p_y=0$ and $p_y$ is conserved. But $\dot p_x=-kx\neq0$, and $\dot y=p_y/m$, so $y$ changes linearly in time unless $p_y=0$.
