@project
title: Constrained model report: the spherical pendulum
overview: Analyze a particle of mass $m$ confined to a smooth sphere of radius $R$ in uniform gravity as a constrained mechanical model. Treat it in three languages (a Lagrangian with a multiplier, a Hamiltonian in angles, and Dirac constraints in Cartesian variables), identify its conserved quantities and equilibria, and count its physical degrees of freedom two independent ways. The report must state and defend every assumption.
goal: Derive the motion with a Lagrange multiplier and identify the constraint force.
goal: Prove with Poisson brackets which quantities are conserved, and use them to reduce the system.
goal: Count the physical degrees of freedom by Dirac's method and by symplectic reduction, and locate and classify the relative equilibria.
step: Fix the model and conventions. Use Cartesian coordinates with $z$ upward, a sphere centered at the origin, and spherical angles with $\theta$ measured from the downward vertical. State that the particle hangs from a rigid massless rod, so the constraint is two-sided, and say what would change for a string that can go slack (a one-sided constraint).
step: Write the Lagrangian $L = \tfrac12 m\dot{\mathbf r}^2 - m g z$ and the extended Lagrangian with the multiplier for $f = \mathbf r^2 - R^2$. Derive the equations of motion, eliminate the accelerations by differentiating the constraint twice, solve for $\lambda$, and express the rod tension in terms of $\theta$, $\dot\theta$ and $\dot\phi$. Compare with the result of eliminating the constraint in angle variables.
step: Form $p_\theta$, $p_\phi$ and the Hamiltonian $H(\theta, \phi, p_\theta, p_\phi)$. Show with brackets that $H$ and $p_\phi = L_z$ are conserved, and check $\{L_z, H\} = 0$ again in Cartesian components using $\{L_i, L_j\} = \epsilon_{ijk}L_k$ and the invariance of $\mathbf p^2$ and $z$.
step: Reduce by $L_z$. Write the effective potential $U_\text{eff}(\theta)$, find the relative equilibria (conical orbits), classify them with $U_\text{eff}''$, and compute the small-oscillation frequency of $\theta$ about a conical orbit. Verify the limit of small cone angle and the limit of a nearly horizontal orbit.
step: Do the Dirac analysis in Cartesian variables $(\mathbf r, \mathbf p)$ with $H_c = \mathbf p^2/(2m) + m g z$ and the primary constraint $\phi_1 = \mathbf r^2 - R^2$. Find the secondary constraint, determine the multiplier $u_1$, classify the constraints, count $n = N - N_1 - \tfrac12 N_2$, and compute one Dirac bracket such as $\{x, p_x\}_D$.
step: Cross-check the count by symplectic reduction: start from the constrained phase space, apply the momentum map $L_z$ of the rotation group about the $z$-axis, and report the dimension of the reduced space. Explain why the system is Liouville integrable and name the two commuting integrals.
step: Assess one small perturbation of your choice, for example an extra potential term $\epsilon\,m g R\sin^2\theta\cos 2\phi$ or a slowly shortened rod. Say which conserved quantities survive, and give a first-order frequency shift or an adiabatic-invariant estimate.
step: Write the report. List the assumptions, define every symbol and sign convention, and finish with a table of the conserved quantities and the constraints with their classes and the degree-of-freedom count from each method.
deliverable: A report of 4 to 6 pages with the derivations from steps 2 to 7, each step doing one thing and each result checked in at least one limit.
deliverable: A summary table: constraints and their classes, conserved quantities, equilibria and their stability, and the degree-of-freedom count by both methods.
deliverable: A one-page statement of assumptions and what changes if they fail (string instead of rod, friction, a non-uniform field, a perturbation that breaks the rotation symmetry).
rubric: Derivations are correct and each step is justified: multiplier, tension, Hamiltonian and effective potential agree with one another.
rubric: Conservation statements are proved with brackets, including the explicit time-derivative term, and consistency with the constraints is checked.
rubric: The Dirac analysis is complete (primary and secondary constraints, the multiplier, and the class of each constraint), and both degree-of-freedom counts agree.
rubric: Equilibria are located and classified correctly, with frequencies that pass the stated limit checks.
rubric: Assumptions, conventions and signs are stated and defended, and the perturbation discussion identifies which symmetries survive.

@review
This guide lists what you must be able to do for the project and the final. Conventions: $L = T - V$, $p_i = \partial L/\partial\dot q^i$, $H = p_i\dot q^i - L$, $\{q^i, p_j\} = \delta^i_j$, $\dot F = \{F, H\} + \partial_t F$, $J = \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint p\,dq$, and $\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH$ with $\omega = dq\wedge dp$.

### Week by week

- **Weeks 1 and 2, action and constraints.** Stationary action with fixed endpoints gives $\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q^i} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q^i} = 0$. A cyclic coordinate gives a conserved $p_i$, and $dh/dt = -\partial L/\partial t$. For holonomic constraints add $\lambda^a\,\partial f_a/\partial q^i$ on the right and differentiate $f_a = 0$ twice; the constraint force is $\lambda\nabla f$. Velocity constraints use $\lambda^a a_{ai}$ (d'Alembert).
- **Weeks 3 and 4, Hamiltonian structure.** The Legendre transform needs a regular Hessian. Know Hamilton's equations, the properties of $\{F, G\}$ (Leibniz, Jacobi), Poisson's theorem and $\{L_i, L_j\} = \epsilon_{ijk}L_k$.
- **Weeks 5 and 6, canonical maps.** Test with $MJM^{T} = J$ or the fundamental brackets. Use the four generating functions, with $K = H + \partial F/\partial t$.
- **Weeks 7 and 8, Hamilton–Jacobi and action-angle.** $\partial_t S + H = 0$; $H(q, W') = E$; separation; $J_k$, $H(J)$, $\omega_k = \partial H/\partial J_k$; Liouville integrability; the Kepler result $H = -mk^2/\left[2(J_r + J_\phi)^2\right]$.
- **Weeks 9 and 10, perturbation and nonlinear dynamics.** $K = H_0 + \epsilon\langle H_1\rangle$, small denominators, adiabatic invariance. Equilibria, $A = J\nabla^2 H$, centers and saddles, bifurcations, area-preserving maps, KAM.
- **Weeks 11 and 12, constraints and geometry.** Primary and secondary constraints, first and second class, $n = N - N_1 - \tfrac12 N_2$, the Dirac bracket. Symplectic form, momentum map, reduction by $\dim M - \dim G - \dim G_\mu$.

### Self-check

- I can derive the Euler–Lagrange equations from $\delta S = 0$ and say where each assumption is used.
- I can compute $\lambda$ and the constraint force for a given constraint.
- Given $L$, I can decide regularity and form $H$.
- I can prove a conservation law with brackets, including explicit time dependence.
- I can test a map for canonicity and build one from $F_1$ or $F_2$.
- I can separate the Hamilton–Jacobi equation and compute an action integral.
- I can find and classify equilibria and give the first-order frequency shift.
- I can run a Dirac analysis and count degrees of freedom in two ways.

### Common errors

- Dropping $\partial F/\partial t$ in $\dot F$ or in $K$.
- Sign slips: $\{p, q\} = -1$, $P = -\partial F_1/\partial Q$, $\partial_t S = -H$.
- Substituting a conserved momentum into $L$ instead of $H$.
- Forgetting the $1/2\pi$ in $J$, or integrating only half a libration.
- Imposing constraints before taking brackets.
- Counting a first-class constraint as removing one dimension, not two.
- Using $K = H$ for a time-dependent transformation.

prompt: A particle of mass $m$ moves on a smooth sphere of radius $R$ centered at the origin in gravity, with $z$ upward. Treat it in Cartesian phase space with $H_c = \mathbf p^2/(2m) + m g z$ and the primary constraint $\phi_1 = \mathbf r^2 - R^2 \approx 0$. (a) Find all constraints and classify them. (b) Count the physical degrees of freedom. (c) Determine the multiplier $u_1$, and show that the constraint force has magnitude $(m v^2 - m g z)/R$. (d) Show with brackets that $L_z = x p_y - y p_x$ is conserved and consistent with the constraints, and give the dimension of the phase space after reduction by $L_z$.
hint: Use $H_T = H_c + u_1\phi_1$ and require $\dot\phi_1 \approx 0$, then $\dot\phi_2 \approx 0$. Remember $\{\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf p, V(\mathbf r)\} = -\mathbf r\cdot\nabla V$.
hint: The bracket $\{\phi_1, \phi_2\}$ equals $2\mathbf r^2$. The force on the particle from the constraint is $-u_1\nabla\phi_1$.
hint: $L_z$ generates rotations about the $z$-axis. Ask whether each of $\phi_1$, $\phi_2$ and $H_c$ is invariant under such rotations.
solution: (a) $\dot\phi_1 \approx \{\phi_1, H_c\} = \frac{2}{m}\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf p$, so there is a secondary constraint $\phi_2 = \mathbf r\cdot\mathbf p \approx 0$. Then $\dot\phi_2 \approx \{\phi_2, H_c\} + u_1\{\phi_2, \phi_1\} = \frac{\mathbf p^2}{m} - m g z - 2R^2 u_1 \approx 0$ fixes $u_1$, so no tertiary constraints arise. Since $\{\phi_1, \phi_2\} = 2\mathbf r^2 \approx 2R^2 \ne 0$, both constraints are second class. (b) $N = 3$, $N_1 = 0$, $N_2 = 2$, so $n = 3 - 1 = 2$ (the angles $\theta, \phi$). (c) $u_1 = \dfrac{\mathbf p^2/m - m g z}{2R^2}$. The constraint force is $-u_1\nabla\phi_1 = -2u_1\mathbf r$, directed toward the center when $u_1 > 0$, with magnitude $2u_1 R = \dfrac{m v^2 - m g z}{R}$ (with $\mathbf p = m\mathbf v$). At the bottom, $z = -R$, this is $m v^2/R + m g$. (d) $\{L_z, \phi_1\} = \{L_z, \phi_2\} = 0$ because $\mathbf r^2$ and $\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf p$ are rotation scalars, and $\{L_z, H_c\} = 0$ because $\mathbf p^2$ and $z$ are invariant under rotations about the $z$-axis. So $\dot L_z \approx 0$ and $L_z$ is consistent with the constraints. The Dirac-reduced phase space is $4$-dimensional ($T^*S^2$). Fixing $L_z$ and dividing by the $SO(2)$ rotations removes $2$ more dimensions, leaving a $2$-dimensional space with coordinates $(\theta, p_\theta)$, i.e. one degree of freedom. With two commuting integrals $H$ and $L_z$ the system is Liouville integrable.

prompt: A particle of mass $m$ moves in the plane under $V(r) = -\dfrac{k}{r} - \dfrac{\epsilon}{r^2}$ with $\epsilon > 0$ small and $E < 0$. (a) Start from the Kepler action-angle Hamiltonian $H_0 = -\dfrac{m k^2}{2(J_r + J_\phi)^2}$ with frequency $\omega_0 = \dfrac{m k^2}{(J_r + J_\phi)^3}$, and show that the first-order perturbation theory of L09 gives $\langle H_1\rangle = -\dfrac{\epsilon\,m\,\omega_0}{J_\phi}$, where $H_1 = -\epsilon/r^2$. (b) Find $\omega_r$ and $\omega_\phi$ to first order in $\epsilon$ and the angle by which the perihelion advances per radial period. (c) Compare with the exact result obtained by absorbing the perturbation into the centrifugal term.
hint: Use $\dot\phi = J_\phi/(m r^2)$ and the fact that the angle average equals the time average over one radial period, during which $\phi$ advances by $2\pi$ at zeroth order.
hint: The radial period is $2\pi/\omega_r$, and the angle $\phi$ advances by $2\pi\,\omega_\phi/\omega_r$ per radial period.
hint: For the exact result, notice that $\dfrac{J_\phi^2}{2 m r^2} - \dfrac{\epsilon}{r^2} = \dfrac{J_\phi^2 - 2 m\epsilon}{2 m r^2}$.
solution: (a) $\langle r^{-2}\rangle = \dfrac{m}{J_\phi}\langle\dot\phi\rangle = \dfrac{m}{J_\phi}\cdot\dfrac{2\pi}{T} = \dfrac{m\omega_0}{J_\phi}$, so $\langle H_1\rangle = -\epsilon\langle r^{-2}\rangle = -\dfrac{\epsilon m\omega_0}{J_\phi} = -\dfrac{\epsilon m^2k^2}{J_\phi (J_r + J_\phi)^3}$. (b) The new Hamiltonian is $K = H_0 + \langle H_1\rangle$. Differentiate: $\omega_r = \omega_0 + \dfrac{3\epsilon m^2k^2}{J_\phi(J_r + J_\phi)^4}$ and $\omega_\phi = \omega_0 + \epsilon m^2k^2\left[\dfrac{1}{J_\phi^2(J_r + J_\phi)^3} + \dfrac{3}{J_\phi(J_r + J_\phi)^4}\right]$. The difference is $\omega_\phi - \omega_r = \dfrac{\epsilon m\omega_0}{J_\phi^2}$. Per radial period $\phi$ advances by $2\pi\omega_\phi/\omega_r \approx 2\pi\left(1 + \dfrac{\epsilon m}{J_\phi^2}\right)$, so the perihelion advances by $\delta\phi = \dfrac{2\pi m\epsilon}{J_\phi^2}$ per orbit, in the direction of motion. (c) With $J_\phi'^2 = J_\phi^2 - 2m\epsilon$ in the radicand, the radial motion is Kepler motion with angular momentum $J_\phi'$, while the orbit integral has $J_\phi$ in the numerator, so $\phi$ advances by $2\pi J_\phi/J_\phi'$ per radial period: $\delta\phi_\text{exact} = 2\pi\left(\dfrac{J_\phi}{\sqrt{J_\phi^2 - 2m\epsilon}} - 1\right) = \dfrac{2\pi m\epsilon}{J_\phi^2} + O(\epsilon^2)$, which agrees with the first-order result.

rubric: Converts the frequency difference to a perihelion advance $2\pi m\epsilon/J_\phi^2$ per orbit.
