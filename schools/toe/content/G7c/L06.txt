@article
Dimensional regularization gave us poles and finite constants. A **renormalization condition** says which finite constants to keep. It is a definition of what we mean by the parameters $m$ and $\lambda$.

### Counterterms

Write the bare Lagrangian in terms of renormalized fields and parameters and split off the extra pieces. With signature $(+,-,-,-)$,

$$\mathcal L = \tfrac12(\partial\phi)^2 - \tfrac12 m^2\phi^2 - \tfrac{\lambda\mu^\epsilon}{4!}\phi^4 + \tfrac12\delta_Z(\partial\phi)^2 - \tfrac12\delta_m\phi^2 - \tfrac{\delta_\lambda}{4!}\phi^4.$$

The counterterms $\delta_Z,\delta_m,\delta_\lambda$ are series in $\lambda$ with coefficients fixed by conditions. The conditions make the Green's functions finite; **which** finite parts remain defines the scheme. In $\phi^4$ theory $\delta_Z=0$ at order $\lambda$, because the one-loop tadpole does not depend on the external momentum; $\delta_Z$ first appears at $O(\lambda^2)$ from the sunset diagram.

### The one-loop four-point function

In dimensional regularization the amplitude, defined so that the tree-level value is $-\lambda$, is

$$\mathcal M = -\lambda - \delta_\lambda + \frac{\lambda^2}{32\pi^2}\sum_{p^2\in\{s,t,u\}}\int_0^1\!dx\Big[\frac2\epsilon-\gamma_E+\ln4\pi - \ln\frac{m^2-x(1-x)p^2}{\mu^2}\Big].$$

The poles of the three channels sum to $\frac{3\lambda^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}$.

### Three schemes for the coupling

1. **$\overline{\rm MS}$.** Subtract only the pole together with $-\gamma_E+\ln4\pi$ (equivalently, pole only in terms of $\bar\mu$): $\delta_\lambda = \frac{3\lambda^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}$. Then
$$\mathcal M = -\lambda(\mu) - \frac{\lambda^2}{32\pi^2}\sum_{s,t,u}\int_0^1dx\,\ln\frac{m^2-x(1-x)p^2}{\mu^2}.$$
2. **Zero momentum.** Define $\lambda_P \equiv -\mathcal M(s=t=u=0)$. Then $\lambda_P = \lambda(\mu) + \frac{3\lambda^2}{32\pi^2}\ln\frac{m^2}{\mu^2}$.
3. **Threshold.** Define $\lambda_S \equiv -\mathcal M(s=4m^2,\ t=u=0)$. The integral for $s=4m^2$ uses $m^2-4m^2x(1-x) = m^2(1-2x)^2$, giving $\lambda_S = \lambda(\mu) + \frac{\lambda^2}{32\pi^2}\big[3\ln\frac{m^2}{\mu^2} - 2\big]$.

These are three different numbers describing the same physics. At one loop they differ by $O(\lambda^2)$ finite terms, so they agree to the order at which the series is truncated.

### The mass

The physical mass is the pole of the propagator. In $\overline{\rm MS}$ the parameter $m(\mu)$ is related to the pole mass by a finite shift. From the tadpole, $\Sigma = \frac\lambda2\,T$, and

$$m_{\rm pole}^2 = m^2(\mu)\Big[1 + \frac{\lambda}{32\pi^2}\Big(\ln\frac{m^2}{\mu^2}-1\Big)\Big] + O(\lambda^2).$$

### What depends on what

| Quantity | Scheme dependent? | Scale $\mu$ dependent? |
|---|---|---|
| Scattering amplitude at fixed momenta, to the order computed | No | No (residual dependence is of higher order) |
| Pole mass | No | No |
| $\lambda_{\overline{\rm MS}}(\mu)$, $m_{\overline{\rm MS}}(\mu)$ | Yes | Yes |
| Finite part of a counterterm | Yes | Yes |
| One-loop coefficient of the beta function | No | No |

If a change of scheme is a redefinition $\lambda' = \lambda + c\lambda^2+\dots$, the one-loop beta-function coefficient is unchanged. For a single coupling in a mass-independent scheme, the two-loop coefficient also survives.

### Using the freedom

Truncating at fixed order leaves a residual $\mu$ dependence of the next order. Choose $\mu$ near the physical scale of the process so that the logarithms $\ln(\Delta/\mu^2)$ are small. A coupling quoted without its scheme and scale is not a complete statement.

@example
title: Converting $\overline{\rm MS}$ to the threshold coupling
problem: Starting from the $\overline{\rm MS}$ amplitude, show that $\lambda_S \equiv -\mathcal M(4m^2,0,0)$ equals $\lambda(\mu) + \frac{\lambda^2}{32\pi^2}\big[3\ln\frac{m^2}{\mu^2}-2\big]$.
step: For $t=u=0$, each integral is $\int_0^1dx\,\ln\frac{m^2}{\mu^2} = \ln\frac{m^2}{\mu^2}$, so those two channels give $2\ln\frac{m^2}{\mu^2}$.
step: For $s=4m^2$, $m^2 - 4m^2x(1-x) = m^2(1-2x)^2$, so the integral is $\ln\frac{m^2}{\mu^2} + 2\int_0^1\ln|1-2x|\,dx$.
step: Substitute $u = 1-2x$: $\int_0^1\ln|1-2x|\,dx = \int_0^1\ln u\,du = -1$, so the $s$-channel integral is $\ln\frac{m^2}{\mu^2}-2$.
step: Sum the channels: $\sum = 3\ln\frac{m^2}{\mu^2} - 2$. Then $\mathcal M = -\lambda - \frac{\lambda^2}{32\pi^2}\big[3\ln\frac{m^2}{\mu^2}-2\big]$, and $\lambda_S=-\mathcal M$.

@example
title: The $\overline{\rm MS}$ counterterm from the poles
problem: Find $\delta_\lambda$ in minimal subtraction so that $\mathcal M$ is finite as $\epsilon\to0$.
step: Each channel contributes the pole $\frac{\lambda^2}{32\pi^2}\cdot\frac{2}{\epsilon}\int_0^1dx = \frac{\lambda^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}$.
step: There are three channels, $s$, $t$ and $u$, so the total pole in $\mathcal M$ is $\frac{3\lambda^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}$.
step: The term $-\delta_\lambda$ must cancel it, so $\delta_\lambda = \frac{3\lambda^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}$, with no finite part in the $\overline{\rm MS}$ convention.
step: The bare coupling is $\lambda_0 = \mu^\epsilon(\lambda + \delta_\lambda)$. It cannot depend on $\mu$, which will give the beta function in week 7.

@mistakes
- Comparing couplings from different schemes or scales as if they were the same number.
- Assuming that $m$ in $\overline{\rm MS}$ is the pole mass; they differ by a finite loop correction.
- Believing that a renormalization condition changes the physics. It relabels the parameters; the amplitude as a function of momenta is the same.
- Adding the three channels incorrectly: the $t$- and $u$-channels at zero momentum each contribute $\ln(m^2/\mu^2)$, not zero.
