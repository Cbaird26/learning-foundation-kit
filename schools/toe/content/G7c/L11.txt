@article
Replace the phase $e^{iq\alpha}$ of week 9 by a matrix $U(x)=e^{i\alpha^a(x)T^a}$ in a compact simple Lie group. The matrices fail to commute, and this changes the structure of the theory.

### Generators and group factors

The Hermitian generators obey $[T^a,T^b]=if^{abc}T^c$ with real, totally antisymmetric structure constants. Fix $\mathrm{tr}_R(T^aT^b)=T(R)\,\delta^{ab}$, with $T_F=\tfrac12$ for the fundamental of $SU(N)$. Then

$$T^aT^a=C_2(R)\,\mathbb 1,\qquad C_F=\frac{N^2-1}{2N},\qquad f^{acd}f^{bcd}=C_A\,\delta^{ab},\quad C_A=N,\qquad \dim G=N^2-1.$$

Taking the trace of $T^aT^a=C_F\mathbb 1$ gives $C_F\dim R=T_F\dim G$. For $SU(2)$, $T^a=\sigma^a/2$ and $f^{abc}=\epsilon^{abc}$.

### Gauge transformation and Lagrangian

With the covariant derivative $D_\mu=\partial_\mu-igA^a_\mu T^a$ and $\psi\to U\psi$, covariance of $D_\mu\psi$ requires

$$A_\mu\equiv A^a_\mu T^a\;\to\;UA_\mu U^{-1}-\frac ig(\partial_\mu U)U^{-1},\qquad \delta A^a_\mu=\frac1g\,\partial_\mu\alpha^a+f^{abc}A^b_\mu\alpha^c.$$

The commutator $[D_\mu,D_\nu]=-igF^a_{\mu\nu}T^a$ defines

$$F^a_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A^a_\nu-\partial_\nu A^a_\mu+g f^{abc}A^b_\mu A^c_\nu,\qquad F_{\mu\nu}\to UF_{\mu\nu}U^{-1}.$$

The Lagrangian is $\mathcal L=-\tfrac14F^a_{\mu\nu}F^{a\mu\nu}+\bar\psi(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu-m)\psi$. The Abelian theory of week 9 is the case $f^{abc}=0$.

### Self-interaction

The commutator term in $F$ makes $-\tfrac14F^2$ contain cubic and quartic terms in $A$:

$$\mathcal L_{\rm YM}\supset-gf^{abc}(\partial_\mu A^a_\nu)A^{b\mu}A^{c\nu}-\frac{g^2}{4}f^{abc}f^{ade}A^b_\mu A^c_\nu A^{d\mu}A^{e\nu}.$$

Gluons carry the charge they couple to, so they interact with each other even without matter. Photons do not. The gauge-fixing and ghost terms follow week 10, with $\mathcal L_{\rm gf}=-\frac1{2\xi}(\partial^\mu A^a_\mu)^2$.

### The one-loop beta function

At one loop in $\overline{\rm MS}$,

$$\mu\frac{dg}{d\mu}=-\frac{b_0}{16\pi^2}\,g^3,\qquad b_0=\frac{11}{3}C_A-\frac43T_Fn_f-\frac13T_sn_s,$$

where $n_f$ is the number of Dirac fermions in a representation with index $T_F$ and $n_s$ the number of complex scalars with index $T_s$. A Weyl fermion counts half. For $SU(N)$ with $n_f$ fundamental flavors, $b_0=\frac{11N-2n_f}{3}$. The gauge-boson term $+\frac{11}{3}C_A$ drives the sign: it makes the coupling decrease at high energy, whereas matter loops screen charge, as in the Abelian case where $C_A=0$ and the sign of the beta function is positive.

With $\alpha_s=g^2/4\pi$ the solution is $\frac{1}{\alpha_s(\mu)}=\frac{1}{\alpha_s(\mu_0)}+\frac{b_0}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\mu}{\mu_0}$. If $b_0>0$ the theory is **asymptotically free**: $\alpha_s\to0$ as $\mu\to\infty$. $SU(3)$ with $n_f$ quark flavors is asymptotically free for $n_f\le16$.

### Dimensional transmutation and its limits

The combination $\Lambda=\mu\,\exp\!\big(-\frac{2\pi}{b_0\alpha_s(\mu)}\big)$ does not depend on $\mu$ at one loop, and $\alpha_s(\mu)=\frac{2\pi}{b_0\ln(\mu/\Lambda)}$. A dimensionless coupling has been traded for a dimensionful scale: **dimensional transmutation**. The value of $\Lambda$ depends on the scheme and on the number of active flavors. The formula diverges at $\mu=\Lambda$, but perturbation theory fails before that, when $\alpha_s\sim1$. So the one-loop pole is not a prediction of confinement; it marks where the weak-coupling description ends.

@example
title: Asymptotic freedom for six flavors
problem: Compute $b_0$ for $SU(3)$ with $n_f=6$ fundamental Dirac flavors and decide whether the theory is asymptotically free.
step: For $SU(3)$, $C_A=3$ and $T_F=\tfrac12$.
step: The gauge term is $\frac{11}{3}\cdot3=11$.
step: The quark term is $\frac43\cdot\frac12\cdot6=4$.
step: Then $b_0=11-4=7>0$, so the coupling decreases with energy: the theory is asymptotically free.

@example
title: Casimir of the fundamental from a trace
problem: Derive $C_F=\frac{N^2-1}{2N}$ for $SU(N)$.
step: Take the trace of $T^aT^a=C_F\mathbb 1$ over the $N$-dimensional fundamental: $\mathrm{tr}(T^aT^a)=C_F\,N$.
step: The normalization $\mathrm{tr}(T^aT^b)=T_F\delta^{ab}$ gives $\mathrm{tr}(T^aT^a)=T_F\,(N^2-1)$ after summing over $a$.
step: Equate: $C_FN=\tfrac12(N^2-1)$, so $C_F=\frac{N^2-1}{2N}$. For $N=3$, $C_F=\frac43$; for $N=2$, $C_F=\frac34$.

@mistakes
- Treating gluons as neutral, like photons. They carry color, so the theory has cubic and quartic self-couplings.
- Confusing $C_F$ (fundamental) with $C_A$ (adjoint) in $b_0$ and in amplitudes.
- Counting a Weyl fermion as a Dirac fermion in $b_0$; a Weyl fermion contributes half.
- Reading the one-loop divergence of $\alpha_s$ at $\mu=\Lambda$ as a derivation of confinement.

prompt: Show that $[D_\mu,D_\nu]=-igF^a_{\mu\nu}T^a$ with $F^a_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A^a_\nu-\partial_\nu A^a_\mu+gf^{abc}A^b_\mu A^c_\nu$, and explain why $F_{\mu\nu}\to UF_{\mu\nu}U^{-1}$ makes $\mathrm{tr}(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})$ gauge invariant. Then explain how the cubic and quartic gluon self-interactions arise and why they have no analogue in QED.
hint: Expand the commutator and use $[A_\mu,A_\nu]=A^b_\mu A^c_\nu\,[T^b,T^c]$.
hint: Cyclicity of the trace turns $UXU^{-1}$ into $X$.
solution: $[D_\mu,D_\nu]=[\partial_\mu-igA_\mu,\partial_\nu-igA_\nu]=-ig(\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu)-g^2[A_\mu,A_\nu]$. With $[A_\mu,A_\nu]=if^{bca}A^b_\mu A^c_\nu T^a$, the last term is $-ig\,(gf^{abc}A^b_\mu A^c_\nu)T^a$, so $[D_\mu,D_\nu]=-igF^a_{\mu\nu}T^a$. Because $D_\mu\psi\to UD_\mu\psi$ for every $\psi$, the commutator transforms as $UF_{\mu\nu}U^{-1}$. Then $\mathrm{tr}(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})\to\mathrm{tr}(UF_{\mu\nu}U^{-1}UF^{\mu\nu}U^{-1})=\mathrm{tr}(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})$, and $\mathrm{tr}(F^2)=\frac12F^aF^a$ for $T=\frac12$. The term $gf^{abc}A^bA^c$ inside $F^a$ produces cubic terms (one factor of $\partial A$ times two factors of $A$) and quartic terms (square of the commutator piece) in $-\frac14F^2$. In QED the structure constants vanish, so $F_{\mu\nu}$ is linear in $A_\mu$ and the photon is free.
