@article
A **generating functional** packs every correlation function into one object, so that computing a correlator means differentiating with respect to a source $J(x)$.

### Sources and correlators

In Minkowski space, with the conventions of week 1,

$$Z[J] = \int\mathcal D\phi\;\exp\Big\{ i\!\int\! d^4x\,\big[\mathcal L(\phi) + J(x)\phi(x)\big]\Big\}.$$

The functional derivative obeys $\delta J(y)/\delta J(x) = \delta^4(x-y)$. Each derivative brings down a factor $i\phi$, so

$$\langle 0|T\{\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n)\}|0\rangle = \frac{1}{Z[0]}\Big(-i\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x_1)}\Big)\cdots\Big(-i\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x_n)}\Big) Z[J]\Big|_{J=0}.$$

In Euclidean signature $Z_E[J] = \int\mathcal D\phi\, e^{-S_E + \int J\phi}$ and the factors of $-i$ disappear.

### The free scalar field

For $\mathcal L_0 = \frac12(\partial_\mu\phi)^2 - \frac12 m^2\phi^2$, integrate by parts to get $S_0 = -\frac12\int\phi\,(\partial^2+m^2)\,\phi$. The exponent $iS_0 + i\int J\phi$ is a Gaussian with operator $i(\partial^2+m^2)$ and source $iJ$. Completing the square, exactly as in week 1, gives

$$Z_0[J] = Z_0[0]\,\exp\Big[-\frac12\int d^4x\,d^4y\; J(x)\,D_F(x-y)\,J(y)\Big],$$

where the **Feynman propagator** is

$$D_F(x-y) = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\,\frac{i\,e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)}}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon},\qquad (\partial^2+m^2)\,D_F(x-y) = -i\,\delta^4(x-y).$$

The $i\epsilon$ selects the vacuum and makes $D_F$ the time-ordered two-point function. Two derivatives of $Z_0$ at $J=0$ give

$$\langle\phi(x)\phi(y)\rangle_0 = (-i)^2\,\frac{\delta^2}{\delta J(x)\,\delta J(y)}\,e^{-\frac12 JD_FJ}\Big|_{0} = D_F(x-y).$$

### Wick's theorem

Odd correlators of the free theory vanish. An even correlator is the sum over all ways of pairing the fields, each pair replaced by $D_F$:

$$\langle\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3\phi_4\rangle_0 = D_{12}D_{34} + D_{13}D_{24} + D_{14}D_{23}.$$

For $n$ fields there are $(n-1)!!$ pairings. Every free correlator is built from the propagator alone.

### Connected and one-particle-irreducible functionals

Write $Z[J] = e^{iW[J]}$ (Euclidean: $Z_E = e^{W_E}$). Then $W$ generates **connected** correlators; disconnected pieces and vacuum bubbles are removed by the logarithm. In the free theory only the two-point function is connected. The mean field in the presence of the source is $\varphi_J = \delta W/\delta J$, and the Legendre transform

$$\Gamma[\varphi] = W[J] - \int d^4x\, J\varphi,\qquad \frac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta\varphi} = -J,$$

is the **effective action**; it generates one-particle-irreducible (1PI) vertices. For the free theory $\Gamma_0 = S_0$ exactly. In general $\Gamma = S + \hbar\,\Gamma_{\rm 1\text{-}loop} + \dots$; in Euclidean signature the one-loop term is $\frac12\,\mathrm{Tr}\ln S_E''[\varphi]$, a functional determinant. We use it in week 12.

### Euclidean propagators

In Euclidean signature the propagator is $G(x-y) = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{e^{ip\cdot(x-y)}}{p^2+m^2}$, and for $m=0$ it equals $\frac{1}{4\pi^2 (x-y)^2}$. In $0+1$ dimensions the same method applied to $S_E = \int d\tau\,[\frac12\dot q^2 + \frac12\omega^2q^2]$ gives

$$\langle q(\tau)\,q(0)\rangle = \int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\frac{e^{i\nu\tau}}{\nu^2+\omega^2} = \frac{e^{-\omega|\tau|}}{2\omega},$$

which agrees with the operator calculation $\langle 0|q(\tau)q(0)|0\rangle$ in the oscillator ground state.

@example
title: Two-point function from the generating functional
problem: Starting from $Z_0[J]/Z_0[0] = \exp[-\frac12\int\!\!\int J D_F J]$ with $D_F(x-y)=D_F(y-x)$, show that $\langle\phi(x)\phi(y)\rangle_0 = D_F(x-y)$.
step: Differentiate the exponent once. Because $D_F$ is symmetric, $\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\big[-\frac12\int\!\!\int JD_FJ\big] = -\int d^4z\,D_F(x-z)J(z)$.
step: Differentiate the exponential: $\frac{\delta Z_0}{\delta J(x)} = -\big[\int d^4z\,D_F(x-z)J(z)\big]\,Z_0[J]$.
step: Differentiate again with respect to $J(y)$. One term contains $-D_F(x-y)\,Z_0[J]$; the other is proportional to $J$ and vanishes at $J=0$.
step: Multiply by $(-i)^2 = -1$ and set $J=0$: $\langle\phi(x)\phi(y)\rangle_0 = -(-D_F(x-y)) = D_F(x-y)$.

@example
title: Why the four-point function has three terms
problem: Show that the fourth functional derivative of $e^{X}$ with $X=-\frac12\int\!\!\int JD_FJ$, taken at $J=0$ with respect to four distinct sources, gives the sum over the three pairings.
step: Expand $e^X = 1 + X + \frac12X^2 + \dots$. At $J=0$, four derivatives kill every term except the one quartic in $J$, namely $\frac12X^2 = \frac18\,(JD_FJ)^2$.
step: Write $(JD_FJ)^2 = J_aD_{ab}J_bJ_cD_{cd}J_d$. The four derivatives $\delta/\delta J_1,\dots,\delta/\delta J_4$ can be assigned to the four $J$ factors in $4! = 24$ ways.
step: For each of the three pairings, such as $\{12\}\{34\}$, there are $2\times2\times2 = 8$ assignments: the order within each pair and which propagator carries which pair.
step: So the derivative equals $\frac18\cdot 8\,(D_{12}D_{34}+D_{13}D_{24}+D_{14}D_{23})$. The factor $(-i)^4=1$ leaves the sum of the three pairings.

@mistakes
- Dropping the factors of $-i$ in Minkowski signature, which flips the sign of the two-point function.
- Using $Z$ and $\ln Z$ interchangeably: $Z$ contains disconnected diagrams, $W$ does not.
- Assuming $\Gamma[\varphi]$ equals the classical action in an interacting theory; that holds only at tree level, while loops add a determinant.
- Counting Wick pairings wrongly: for $2n$ fields there are $(2n-1)!!$ pairings, not $(2n)!/2$.

@practice self
prompt: Derive $Z_0[J]$ for the free real scalar field by completing the square, and use it to recover $\langle\phi(x)\phi(y)\rangle_0 = D_F(x-y)$. State where the $i\epsilon$ enters.
hint: Shift $\phi\to\phi+\phi_c$ with $\phi_c$ chosen to cancel the term linear in $\phi$; $\phi_c$ is fixed by the propagator equation $(\partial^2+m^2)D_F=-i\delta^4$.
hint: After the shift, the $\phi$ integral is independent of $J$ and the exponent is $-\frac12\int\!\!\int JD_FJ$.
solution: Write the exponent as $-\frac12\int\phi\,\mathcal A\,\phi + i\int J\phi$ with $\mathcal A = i(\partial^2+m^2)$. Define $\phi_c(x) = i\int d^4y\,D_F(x-y)J(y)$, which satisfies $\mathcal A\phi_c = iJ$ because $\mathcal A D_F = \delta^4$. Shifting $\phi = \phi' + \phi_c$ removes the linear term and leaves $-\frac12\int\phi'\mathcal A\phi' + \frac12\int (iJ)\,D_F\,(iJ)$, so $Z_0[J] = Z_0[0]\exp[-\frac12\int\!\!\int JD_FJ]$. Two derivatives with the factor $(-i)^2$ give $D_F(x-y)$. The $i\epsilon$ ($m^2\to m^2-i\epsilon$) makes the Gaussian integral converge, selects the vacuum, and fixes the pole structure of $D_F$ in momentum space.
