@article
Loop diagrams contain an integral over an undetermined loop momentum $k$. Two techniques turn nearly every one-loop integral into a standard form: Feynman parameters and Wick rotation.

### Feynman parameters

$$\frac1{AB} = \int_0^1 \frac{dx}{[\,xA + (1-x)B\,]^2},\qquad \frac{1}{A_1\cdots A_n} = (n-1)!\int_0^1\! d^nx\;\delta\Big(1-\sum x_i\Big)\frac{1}{(\sum_i x_iA_i)^n}.$$

For the $\phi^4$ bubble, with propagator denominators $k^2-m^2$ and $(k+p)^2-m^2$, complete the square and shift $\ell = k + xp$:

$$x[(k+p)^2-m^2] + (1-x)[k^2-m^2] = \ell^2 - \Delta,\qquad \Delta = m^2 - x(1-x)\,p^2.$$

### Wick rotation

With signature $(+,-,-,-)$, set $k^0 = i k^0_E$. Then $d^4k = i\,d^4k_E$ and $k^2 = -k_E^2$. The $i\epsilon$ puts the poles in the second and fourth quadrants of the complex $k^0$ plane, so the rotation by $90^\circ$ crosses none of them. The master formula is

$$\int\frac{d^4\ell}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{1}{(\ell^2-\Delta+i\epsilon)^n} = \frac{(-1)^n\, i}{(4\pi)^2}\,\frac{\Gamma(n-2)}{\Gamma(n)}\,\Delta^{2-n}\qquad(n\ge3).$$

For $n\le2$ the integral diverges. It is convenient to work directly in Euclidean space, where $\int\frac{d^4k_E}{(2\pi)^4} = \frac{1}{8\pi^2}\int_0^\infty k^3\,dk$ for a spherically symmetric integrand, because the area of the unit $3$-sphere is $2\pi^2$.

### Cutoff results

Use a hard Euclidean cutoff $|k_E|<\Lambda$. The tadpole and the bubble at zero external momentum are

$$\int^{\Lambda}\!\frac{d^4k_E}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{1}{k^2+m^2} = \frac{1}{16\pi^2}\Big[\Lambda^2 - m^2\ln\frac{\Lambda^2+m^2}{m^2}\Big],$$

$$\int^{\Lambda}\!\frac{d^4k_E}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{1}{(k^2+m^2)^2} = \frac{1}{16\pi^2}\Big[\ln\frac{\Lambda^2+m^2}{m^2} + \frac{m^2}{\Lambda^2+m^2} - 1\Big].$$

The tadpole is **quadratically** divergent and the bubble is **logarithmically** divergent. Two points matter.

- The coefficient of $\ln\Lambda$ in the bubble is regulator independent; the finite constant ($-1$ here) is not.
- A $\Lambda^2$ term has no meaning of its own: it is absorbed into the bare mass, and it does not appear at all in dimensional regularization (week 5).

### Power counting

In $\phi^4$ theory in $d=4$, a connected diagram with $L$ loops, $I$ internal lines, $V$ vertices and $E$ external legs satisfies $4V = 2I+E$ and $L = I-V+1$. The integrand scales like $k^{-2}$ per line and the measure like $k^{4}$ per loop, so the **superficial degree of divergence** is

$$D = 4L - 2I = 4 - E.$$

It does not grow with the order in $\lambda$: this is the signature of a **renormalizable** theory. Only $E=2$ ($D=2$) and $E=4$ ($D=0$) can diverge superficially. In general $D$ depends on the dimensions of the couplings: a coupling of negative mass dimension raises $D$ at each order. For $\phi^3$ in $d=6$, $D = 6-2E$.

$D$ is only a guide. A diagram with $D<0$ can contain a divergent subdiagram, and symmetry can reduce the true divergence below $D$. In QED the photon self-energy has $D=2$ but diverges only logarithmically because of gauge invariance.

### Shifting momenta

Shifting $k\to k+xp$ is safe when the integral converges absolutely, and it is safe in dimensional regularization. With a hard cutoff a shift changes the cut region and can change finite terms of linearly and quadratically divergent integrals.

@example
title: Feynman parameters for the Euclidean bubble
problem: Combine $\frac{1}{(k^2+m^2)\,((k+p)^2+m^2)}$ using a Feynman parameter and identify the effective mass-squared $\Delta$ after shifting the loop momentum.
step: Apply $\frac{1}{AB} = \int_0^1 dx\,[xA+(1-x)B]^{-2}$ with $A=(k+p)^2+m^2$ and $B=k^2+m^2$.
step: Expand the combination: $xA+(1-x)B = k^2 + 2x\,k\cdot p + x p^2 + m^2$.
step: Complete the square: $= (k+xp)^2 + m^2 + x(1-x)p^2$.
step: With $\ell = k+xp$ the integrand is $[\ell^2+\Delta]^{-2}$ and $\Delta = m^2 + x(1-x)p^2$. For spacelike (Euclidean) $p$ this is positive for every $x$, so no singularity appears.

@example
title: Superficial degree of divergence in $\phi^4$ theory
problem: Show that $D = 4 - E$ for any connected diagram in $\phi^4$ theory in four dimensions.
step: Each internal line carries a propagator $\sim k^{-2}$ and each loop a measure $d^4k\sim k^4$, so $D = 4L - 2I$.
step: Each vertex has four legs, and each internal line uses two of them: $4V = 2I + E$, so $I = (4V-E)/2$.
step: The loop count is $L = I - V + 1 = V - E/2 + 1$.
step: Substitute: $D = 4(V - E/2 + 1) - (4V - E) = 4 - E$. The vertex number $V$ cancels, so higher orders are no worse than lower ones.

@mistakes
- Dropping the factor $i$ from $d^4k = i\,d^4k_E$, or the sign $(-1)^n$ from $k^2 = -k_E^2$, which flips the overall sign or phase of a loop.
- Treating the finite constant that remains after discarding $\Lambda$ as physical; it depends on the regulator.
- Reading $D<0$ as "this diagram is finite" without checking subdiagrams.
- Shifting the loop momentum inside a divergent integral with a hard cutoff as if the shift were harmless.

@practice self
prompt: With a hard Euclidean cutoff $\Lambda$, derive the large-$\Lambda$ behavior of the tadpole $T(\Lambda)=\int^\Lambda\frac{d^4k_E}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{1}{k^2+m^2}$ and of the zero-momentum bubble $B(\Lambda)=\int^\Lambda\frac{d^4k_E}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{1}{(k^2+m^2)^2}$. List the divergent terms, state which coefficients are regulator independent, and say how each divergence would be absorbed.
hint: Reduce to $\frac{1}{8\pi^2}\int_0^\Lambda k^3dk\,(\dots)$ and substitute $u=k^2$.

solution: With $u=k^2$: $T = \frac{1}{16\pi^2}\int_0^{\Lambda^2}\frac{u\,du}{u+m^2} = \frac{1}{16\pi^2}\big[\Lambda^2 - m^2\ln\frac{\Lambda^2+m^2}{m^2}\big]$, and $B = \frac{1}{16\pi^2}\int_0^{\Lambda^2}\frac{u\,du}{(u+m^2)^2} = \frac{1}{16\pi^2}\big[\ln\frac{\Lambda^2+m^2}{m^2}+\frac{m^2}{\Lambda^2+m^2}-1\big]$.
