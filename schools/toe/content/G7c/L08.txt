@article
Low-energy physics does not require knowing every detail of high-energy physics. An **effective field theory** (EFT) keeps the light fields and replaces heavy ones by local interactions, organized in a series in $E/\Lambda$, where $E$ is the energy of the process and $\Lambda$ the heavy scale.

### Tree-level matching

Take a light scalar $\phi$ and a heavy scalar $\Phi$ of mass $M$, with $\mathcal L_{\rm int} = -\frac g2\Phi\phi^2 - \frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$ in signature $(+,-,-,-)$. The equation of motion for $\Phi$ is $(\partial^2+M^2)\Phi = -\frac g2\phi^2$. For momenta far below $M$ we invert the operator as a series:

$$\Phi = -\frac{g}{2M^2}\Big(1 - \frac{\partial^2}{M^2}+\dots\Big)\phi^2.$$

Substituting back, the heavy-field terms in $\mathcal L$ collapse to $-\frac g4\Phi\phi^2$, so

$$\mathcal L_{\rm eff} = \tfrac12(\partial\phi)^2 - \tfrac12 m^2\phi^2 - \frac{\lambda_{\rm eff}}{4!}\phi^4 + \frac{g^2}{2M^4}\,\phi^2(\partial_\mu\phi)^2+\dots,\qquad \lambda_{\rm eff} = \lambda - \frac{3g^2}{M^2}.$$

Check with amplitudes. Exchanging $\Phi$ in the $s$-channel gives $\mathcal M_s = \frac{g^2}{M^2-s} = \frac{g^2}{M^2}\big(1+\frac{s}{M^2}+\dots\big)$. Adding the $t$ and $u$ channels, the constant part is $3g^2/M^2$, which equals $-(\lambda_{\rm eff}-\lambda)$: the heavy particle's effect at low energy is a contact interaction.

### Power counting

An operator of mass dimension $\Delta>4$ enters with a coefficient $c/\Lambda^{\Delta-4}$. At energy $E$ its effect relative to the renormalizable terms is of order $(E/\Lambda)^{\Delta-4}$. Dimension-6 effects at $E/\Lambda=0.1$ are therefore about $10^{-2}$. The classic example is Fermi's four-fermion theory, a dimension-6 interaction with $\frac{G_F}{\sqrt2}=\frac{g^2}{8M_W^2}$, obtained by integrating out the $W$ boson.

Because the coefficient grows with $E^{\Delta-4}$ relative to the leading terms, **the EFT signals its own breakdown**: amplitudes grow with energy until they violate unitarity at $E\sim\Lambda$. For the Fermi theory this happens at a few hundred GeV, where the $W$ must be included explicitly.

### Thresholds and decoupling

In a mass-independent scheme such as $\overline{\rm MS}$, a heavy particle contributes to the beta function at every $\mu$ if kept in the theory. The practical procedure is:

1. For $\mu>M$ run with the heavy field included.
2. At $\mu\approx M$ **match**: choose the EFT parameters so that low-energy amplitudes agree.
3. For $\mu<M$ run with the heavy field removed.

The slope of the running changes at each threshold. For a QED coupling with $n$ active unit-charge Dirac fermions, $\frac{1}{\alpha(\mu)} = \frac{1}{\alpha(\mu_0)} - \frac{2n}{3\pi}\ln\frac{\mu}{\mu_0}$ between thresholds.

### Sensitivity to heavy physics

Dimensional regularization hides power divergences, but not the physics behind them. Corrections to a light mass from a heavy particle go like (coupling) $\times\,M^2/16\pi^2$ in the matching condition, so a small light mass requires a cancellation between the high-scale and low-scale parameters. This is the naturalness problem. It is a statement about the relation between UV and IR parameters of an assumed theory, and an EFT is built to tell you precisely that.

### What an EFT can and cannot claim

Truncating at dimension $\Delta_{\max}$ leaves errors of order $(E/\Lambda)^{\Delta_{\max}-3}$. The EFT predicts low-energy observables in terms of a finite number of coefficients, and it predicts the size of its own errors. It does not determine the ultraviolet completion: many different heavy theories give the same low-energy coefficients. A Landau pole or a unitarity bound only tells you $\Lambda$.

@example
title: Integrating out a heavy scalar
problem: For $\mathcal L\supset\frac12(\partial\Phi)^2-\frac12M^2\Phi^2-\frac g2\Phi\phi^2$, find the induced $\phi^4$ interaction at energies far below $M$.
step: Ignore the kinetic term of $\Phi$ at low energy; the equation of motion is $M^2\Phi = -\frac g2\phi^2$, so $\Phi = -\frac{g}{2M^2}\phi^2$.
step: Substitute into the Lagrangian: $-\frac12M^2\Phi^2 = -\frac{g^2}{8M^2}\phi^4$ and $-\frac g2\Phi\phi^2 = +\frac{g^2}{4M^2}\phi^4$.
step: Add them: $\Delta\mathcal L = +\frac{g^2}{8M^2}\phi^4$.
step: Compare with $-\frac{\Delta\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$: $\Delta\lambda = -\frac{24g^2}{8M^2} = -\frac{3g^2}{M^2}$. The heavy exchange lowers the quartic coupling.

@example
title: Running across thresholds
problem: Take $1/\alpha = 135$ at $\mu=1$ GeV, with two active unit-charge fermions below $10$ GeV and a third one above it. Find $1/\alpha$ at $100$ GeV.
step: From $1$ to $10$ GeV, $n=2$, so the slope of $1/\alpha$ is $-\frac{2\cdot2}{3\pi}\approx-0.4244$ per unit of $\ln\mu$.
step: $\ln10 = 2.3026$, so $1/\alpha(10\ {\rm GeV}) = 135 - 0.4244\times2.3026 = 134.02$.
step: Matching at $10$ GeV is continuous at this order: $\alpha$ is the same on both sides.
step: From $10$ to $100$ GeV, $n=3$ and the slope is $-0.6366$, giving $1/\alpha(100\ {\rm GeV}) = 134.02 - 0.6366\times2.3026 = 132.56$.

@mistakes
- Using the EFT above the mass of the particle that was integrated out.
- Running a coupling through a threshold with the same beta function on both sides instead of changing the number of active fields.
- Getting the sign wrong in tree-level matching: exchange of a heavy scalar with a cubic coupling lowers the quartic coupling, $\lambda_{\rm eff}=\lambda-3g^2/M^2$.
- Estimating higher-dimension effects with the heavy scale replaced by the experimental energy; the suppression is $(E/\Lambda)^{\Delta-4}$.
