### Week-by-week key results

**Weeks 1 and 2: path integrals, generating functionals.** $\langle q_f|e^{-iHT}|q_i\rangle=\int\mathcal Dq\,e^{iS}$, and $t=-i\tau$ turns $e^{iS}$ into $e^{-S_E}$ with $S_E=T_E+V$. Gaussian integrals: $\int d^Nx\,e^{-\frac12x^TAx+J^Tx}=(2\pi)^{N/2}(\det A)^{-1/2}e^{\frac12J^TA^{-1}J}$. For the free field, $Z_0[J]=Z_0[0]\exp[-\frac12\int JD_FJ]$, $\langle\phi\phi\rangle=D_F$, Wick's theorem sums pairings; $W$ is connected, $\Gamma$ is 1PI.

**Weeks 3 and 4: perturbation theory, loops.** $Z[J]=e^{-S_I[\delta/\delta J]}Z_0[J]$, symmetry factors, cancellation of vacuum bubbles, asymptotic series. Feynman parameters, Wick rotation ($d^4k=i\,d^4k_E$), $D=4-E$ in $\phi^4$.

**Weeks 5 and 6: regularization, renormalization conditions.** $d=4-\epsilon$, $\Gamma(\epsilon/2)=\frac2\epsilon-\gamma_E+O(\epsilon)$, the bubble $\frac{1}{16\pi^2}[\frac2\epsilon-\gamma_E+\ln4\pi-\int_0^1dx\ln\frac{\Delta}{\mu^2}]$. In $\overline{\rm MS}$, $\delta_\lambda=\frac{3\lambda^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}$. Other schemes differ by finite terms, such as $\lambda_P=\lambda(\mu)+\frac{3\lambda^2}{32\pi^2}\ln\frac{m^2}{\mu^2}$.

**Weeks 7 and 8: renormalization group, effective field theory.** $\beta=\frac{3\lambda^2}{16\pi^2}$, $\lambda(\mu)=\frac{\lambda_0}{1-b\lambda_0\ln(\mu/\mu_0)}$, $\mu\frac{dm^2}{d\mu}=\frac{\lambda}{16\pi^2}m^2$, QED $\mu\frac{d\alpha}{d\mu}=\frac{2\alpha^2}{3\pi}$. Matching $\lambda_{\rm eff}=\lambda-3g^2/M^2$, corrections $(E/\Lambda)^{\Delta-4}$, slope changes at thresholds.

**Weeks 9 and 10: Abelian gauge theory, gauge fixing.** $D_\mu=\partial_\mu-iqA_\mu$ with $\psi\to e^{iq\alpha}\psi$ and $A_\mu\to A_\mu+\partial_\mu\alpha$; two physical polarizations; $k_\mu\Pi^{\mu\nu}=0$. Faddeev–Popov gives $-\frac1{2\xi}(\partial\cdot A)^2$ and the propagator $\frac{-i}{k^2}[g_{\mu\nu}-(1-\xi)\frac{k_\mu k_\nu}{k^2}]$.

**Weeks 11 and 12: non-Abelian theory, symmetry breaking.** $F^a_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A^a_\nu-\partial_\nu A^a_\mu+gf^{abc}A^b_\mu A^c_\nu$, $b_0=\frac{11}{3}C_A-\frac43T_Fn_f-\frac13T_sn_s$, $\Lambda=\mu e^{-2\pi/(b_0\alpha_s)}$. Goldstone modes number $\dim G-\dim H$; in the Abelian Higgs model $m_A=|q|v$, $m_h^2=2\lambda v^2$, and the count of degrees of freedom is $4\to4$.

### Most common errors

- Dropping the $i$ in $d^4k=i\,d^4k_E$, or the factors of $-i$ in Minkowski functional derivatives.
- Forgetting $\mu^\epsilon$, or the finite term left by (pole) $\times\,O(\epsilon)$.
- Comparing couplings from different schemes or scales as if they were one number.
- Treating a Landau pole as a prediction rather than a validity limit.
- Inconsistent signs between $D_\mu$ and the gauge transformation of $A_\mu$.
- Counting the eaten Goldstone as a physical particle.

prompt: In $\phi^4$ theory the $\overline{\rm MS}$ coupling is $\lambda(\mu=m)=0.8$. Run it to $\mu=5m$ with the one-loop flow $\mu\,d\lambda/d\mu=\frac{3\lambda^2}{16\pi^2}$. Then evaluate the zero-momentum coupling $\lambda_P=\lambda(\mu)+\frac{3\lambda(\mu)^2}{32\pi^2}\ln\frac{m^2}{\mu^2}$ at $\mu=5m$. Give $\lambda_P$ to four decimal places. (At $\mu=m$ the formula returns exactly $0.8$.)
answer: 0.7995
check: (lambda l: l + 3*l**2/(32*pi**2)*log(1/25))(ivp(lambda t, y: 3*y**2/(16*pi**2), 0.8, 0, log(5)))
tol: 0.0002
hint: Solve the flow in terms of $t=\ln(\mu/m)$: $\frac1{\lambda(t)}=\frac1{0.8}-\frac{3}{16\pi^2}t$.
hint: Here $t=\ln5\approx1.609$, and $\ln(m^2/\mu^2)=-2\ln5$.
solution: The flow gives $\lambda(5m)=\frac{0.8}{1-0.8\times0.018997\times1.6094}=0.8201$. Then $\lambda_P=0.8201+\frac{3\times0.8201^2}{32\pi^2}\times(-3.2189)=0.8201-0.0206=0.7995$. The zero-momentum coupling is a physical amplitude, so it should not depend on $\mu$; the result differs from $0.8$ by $5\times10^{-4}$, which is of order $\lambda^3$, the size of the neglected higher-order terms.

@exam self
prompt: A light real scalar $\phi$ with $\mathcal L\supset-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$ couples to a heavy real scalar $\Phi$ of mass $M$ through $-\frac g2\Phi\phi^2$, with $g^2/M^2=0.1$ and $\lambda=0.9$ at $\mu=M$ in $\overline{\rm MS}$. (a) Integrate out $\Phi$ at tree level and give $\lambda_{\rm eff}(M)$. (b) Run $\lambda_{\rm eff}$ down to $\mu=M/100$ with the one-loop beta function of the effective theory and give the number. (c) State the validity limits of this calculation: why the effective theory cannot be used above $M$, how to read the formal Landau pole of the effective theory's running, and what could change at one loop in the matching.
hint: Use $\lambda_{\rm eff}=\lambda-3g^2/M^2$ and $\frac{1}{\lambda(\mu)}=\frac1{\lambda_{\rm eff}}-\frac{3}{16\pi^2}\ln\frac\mu M$.
hint: For part (c), compare $\ln(\mu_L/M)=\frac{16\pi^2}{3\lambda_{\rm eff}}$ with the range of $\mu$ where the effective theory exists.
solution: (a) $\lambda_{\rm eff}(M)=0.9-3(0.1)=0.6$. (b) $\frac1{\lambda}=\frac1{0.6}+\frac{3}{16\pi^2}\ln100=1.6667+0.0875=1.7542$, so $\lambda(M/100)\approx0.570$. (c) The effective theory exists only for $E,\mu\ll M$: near $E\sim M$ the heavy particle is produced and the expansion in $E^2/M^2$ fails, so the running above $M$ must use the full theory. The formal pole of the effective running, at $\ln(\mu_L/M)=\frac{16\pi^2}{3\times0.6}\approx88$, lies far above $M$ where the effective theory does not apply, so it is not a prediction. At one loop the matching condition acquires finite, scheme-dependent terms of order $\frac{\lambda^2}{16\pi^2}$ and $\frac{g^2}{16\pi^2M^2}\times(\text{couplings})$, so $\lambda_{\rm eff}(M)$ is only reliable to the order computed and depends on the scheme and on the choice of matching scale.
