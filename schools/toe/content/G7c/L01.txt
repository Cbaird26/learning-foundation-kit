@article
This course uses one fixed set of conventions, so state them before anything else.

### Conventions for the whole course

- **Units:** natural units $\hbar = c = 1$.
- **Metric:** signature $(+,-,-,-)$, as in QFT I, so $p^2 = E^2 - \vec p^{\,2}$, $\partial^2 = \partial_t^2 - \nabla^2$, and the Klein–Gordon equation is $(\partial^2 + m^2)\phi = 0$.
- **Fourier transform:** $\phi(x) = \int \frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\, e^{-ip\cdot x}\,\tilde\phi(p)$.
- **Covariant derivative:** $D_\mu = \partial_\mu - i q A_\mu$ for an Abelian field of charge $q$, and $D_\mu = \partial_\mu - i g A_\mu^a T^a$ for a non-Abelian field. The overall sign of $q$ or $g$ is a convention; physical results depend on $q^2$ and on relative signs.
- **Scheme:** dimensional regularization in $d = 4-\epsilon$ dimensions with modified minimal subtraction ($\overline{\rm MS}$) and reference scale $\mu$, unless another scheme is named.

### From amplitude to sum over paths

For $H = \frac{p^2}{2m} + V(q)$ the amplitude to go from $q_i$ to $q_f$ in time $T$ is $\langle q_f|e^{-iHT}|q_i\rangle$. Cut $T = N\delta$ into $N$ slices and insert the completeness relation $\int dq_k\,|q_k\rangle\langle q_k| = 1$ at each of the $N-1$ intermediate times. For small $\delta$ each factor is a Gaussian integral over momentum, which leaves

$$\langle q_f|e^{-iHT}|q_i\rangle = \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\mathcal N}\int \prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{dq_k}{\mathcal N}\; \exp\Big\{ i\delta \sum_{k}\Big[\tfrac{m}{2}\Big(\tfrac{q_{k+1}-q_k}{\delta}\Big)^2 - V(q_k)\Big]\Big\} \equiv \int \mathcal Dq\; e^{iS[q]},$$

with $\mathcal N = \sqrt{2\pi i\delta/m}$ for each step. Every path is weighted by the **phase** $e^{iS}$, $S = \int dt\,(\frac m2\dot q^2 - V)$. Restoring $\hbar$, the phase is $e^{iS/\hbar}$; as $\hbar\to 0$ the phases cancel except near paths with $\delta S = 0$, which are the classical paths.

### Free particle and oscillator

For $V=0$ the integrals are Gaussian and give

$$K(q_f,T;q_i,0) = \sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi i T}}\;\exp\Big[\frac{i m (q_f-q_i)^2}{2T}\Big],\qquad S_{\rm cl}=\frac{m(q_f-q_i)^2}{2T}.$$

For a quadratic action the result is exactly $A(T)\,e^{iS_{\rm cl}}$. For the oscillator, $V=\frac12 m\omega^2q^2$,

$$S_{\rm cl} = \frac{m\omega}{2\sin\omega T}\Big[(q_f^2+q_i^2)\cos\omega T - 2q_fq_i\Big].$$

### Euclidean time

Set $t = -i\tau$. Then $dt = -i\,d\tau$ and $\dot q^2 = -(dq/d\tau)^2$, so $e^{iS}\to e^{-S_E}$ with

$$S_E = \int d\tau\Big[\tfrac m2 \Big(\tfrac{dq}{d\tau}\Big)^2 + V(q)\Big].$$

The weight is real and positive, like a Boltzmann factor. For a scalar field, $Z = \int\mathcal D\phi\, e^{-S_E[\phi]}$ with $S_E = \int d^4x_E\,[\frac12(\partial_\mu\phi)^2 + \frac12 m^2\phi^2 + \dots]$. In Minkowski signature the $i\epsilon$ prescription $m^2\to m^2 - i\epsilon$ damps $e^{iS}$ and projects onto the vacuum at large times.

### The one tool: Gaussian integrals

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\, e^{-\frac12 a x^2 + Jx} = \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{a}}\; e^{J^2/2a},\qquad \int d^Nx\, e^{-\frac12 x^TAx + J^Tx} = \frac{(2\pi)^{N/2}}{\sqrt{\det A}}\; e^{\frac12 J^TA^{-1}J}.$$

A discretized free field is exactly such an integral with $A$ the lattice version of $-\partial^2+m^2$. So $\int\mathcal D\phi\,e^{-S_E}\propto[\det(-\partial^2+m^2)]^{-1/2}$, and $A^{-1}$ is the propagator.

### What is and is not rigorous

$\mathcal Dq$ is not a Lebesgue measure on function space; it is defined by the sliced limit. Typical paths are continuous but nowhere differentiable, so $\dot q$ means a difference quotient. In field theory the continuum limit needs regularization and renormalization, which is the subject of weeks 4 to 6.

@example
title: Euclidean action of the oscillator
problem: Starting from $L = \frac12\dot q^2 - \frac12\omega^2 q^2$, find $S_E$ such that $e^{iS} = e^{-S_E}$ under $t = -i\tau$.
step: Substitute $dt = -i\,d\tau$. The factor $i\,dt$ in $iS = i\int dt\,L$ becomes $i(-i)\,d\tau = d\tau$.
step: Because $\tau = it$, $\frac{dq}{dt} = i\frac{dq}{d\tau}$, so $\dot q^2 = -\big(\frac{dq}{d\tau}\big)^2$. The potential term has no derivative and does not change sign.
step: Then $iS = \int d\tau\,\big[-\frac12 (q')^2 - \frac12\omega^2q^2\big]$ with $q' = dq/d\tau$.
step: Identify $iS = -S_E$, so $S_E = \int d\tau\,\big[\frac12 (q')^2 + \frac12\omega^2q^2\big]$: kinetic plus potential, both positive.

@example
title: A two-variable Gaussian and its determinant
problem: Evaluate $\int dx_1\,dx_2\; \exp\!\big[-\tfrac12(2x_1^2 + 2x_1x_2 + 2x_2^2)\big]$.
step: Write the exponent as $-\frac12 x^TAx$ with $A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$.
step: The eigenvalues of $A$ are $1$ and $3$, with eigenvectors $(1,-1)/\sqrt2$ and $(1,1)/\sqrt2$. A rotation of the variables has unit Jacobian.
step: In the rotated variables the integral factorizes: $\sqrt{2\pi/1}\,\sqrt{2\pi/3} = 2\pi/\sqrt3$.
step: This matches $(2\pi)^{N/2}/\sqrt{\det A}$ with $N=2$ and $\det A = 3$.

@mistakes
- Treating $\mathcal Dq$ as an honest infinite-dimensional Lebesgue measure with a meaningful absolute normalization; only ratios, or the sliced definition, are meaningful.
- Changing the sign of the kinetic term but also of the potential when going to Euclidean time. The Euclidean action is kinetic plus potential, $T_E + V$.
- Forgetting the $i\epsilon$ or the Euclidean rotation, so that oscillatory Gaussians are treated as if they converged absolutely.
- Writing $\frac12(\partial_\mu\phi)^2$ as if it were positive-definite: in signature $(+,-,-,-)$ it equals $\frac12\dot\phi^2 - \frac12(\nabla\phi)^2$.

@quiz self
prompt: Starting from $\langle q_f|e^{-iHT}|q_i\rangle$ with $H=p^2/2m+V(q)$, outline the steps that lead to $\int\mathcal Dq\,e^{iS[q]}$ and explain the role of the Gaussian momentum integral.
hint: Split $e^{-iHT}$ into $N$ equal factors and insert $\int dq_k|q_k\rangle\langle q_k|$ between them.
hint: For small $\delta$, $\langle q'|e^{-i\delta H}|q\rangle\approx\int\frac{dp}{2\pi}e^{ip(q'-q)-i\delta H(p,q)}$.
solution: Write $e^{-iHT}=(e^{-i\delta H})^N$ with $\delta=T/N$ and insert position completeness between factors. To first order in $\delta$, $\langle q_{k+1}|e^{-i\delta H}|q_k\rangle\approx\int\frac{dp}{2\pi}\exp[ip(q_{k+1}-q_k)-i\delta(\frac{p^2}{2m}+V(q_k))]$. The momentum integral is Gaussian and gives $\sqrt{m/2\pi i\delta}\,\exp\{i\delta[\frac m2(\frac{q_{k+1}-q_k}{\delta})^2 - V(q_k)]\}$. Multiplying the $N$ factors and integrating over the $N-1$ intermediate positions gives the sliced integral, whose exponent is the discretized action; the limit $N\to\infty$ defines $\int\mathcal Dq\,e^{iS}$.
