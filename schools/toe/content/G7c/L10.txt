@article
The integral $\int\mathcal DA\,e^{iS[A]}$ runs over all field configurations, but $S$ is constant along each gauge orbit $\{A^\alpha\}$. Integrating along orbits gives the infinite volume of the gauge group, a divergent constant that multiplies the answer. This is the path-integral face of the zero mode found in week 9. We want one representative of each orbit.

### A toy model

Let rotations of the plane be the "gauge group" acting on $(x,y)$, and let $f(r)$, $r^2=x^2+y^2$, be gauge invariant. Then $\int d^2x\,f = 2\pi\int_0^\infty r f(r)\,dr$, where $2\pi$ is the group volume. The condition $y=0$ with $x>0$ selects one point per orbit, and the integral over that slice must be weighted by $\Delta_{\rm FP}=x$, the rate at which the condition $y$ changes under a rotation. The weight is the Faddeev–Popov determinant.

### The Faddeev–Popov procedure

Choose a gauge condition $G(A)=0$ and define

$$1 = \Delta_{\rm FP}[A]\int\mathcal D\alpha\;\delta\big(G(A^\alpha)\big),\qquad \Delta_{\rm FP}[A]=\det\frac{\delta G(A^\alpha)}{\delta\alpha}\Big|_{G=0}.$$

Insert this into $Z$. Since $S$, $\mathcal DA$ and $\Delta_{\rm FP}$ are gauge invariant, shifting $A\to A^{-\alpha}$ factors out $\int\mathcal D\alpha$, the divergent group volume, which is dropped:

$$Z = \int\mathcal DA\;\Delta_{\rm FP}[A]\;\delta\big(G(A)\big)\,e^{iS[A]}.$$

### Abelian case

Take $G=\partial^\mu A_\mu-\omega(x)$ with arbitrary $\omega$. Under $A_\mu\to A_\mu+\partial_\mu\alpha$, $\delta G/\delta\alpha=\partial^2$, independent of $A$. So $\Delta_{\rm FP}=\det\partial^2$ is a constant that decouples. Because the original integral does not depend on $\omega$, average over it with a Gaussian weight $e^{-\frac{i}{2\xi}\int\omega^2}$:

$$\int\mathcal D\omega\;e^{-\frac{i}{2\xi}\int\omega^2}\,\delta(\partial\cdot A-\omega) = e^{-\frac{i}{2\xi}\int(\partial\cdot A)^2}\;\Longrightarrow\;\mathcal L_{\rm gf} = -\frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial_\mu A^\mu)^2.$$

### The photon propagator

With $\mathcal L=-\frac14F^2-\frac1{2\xi}(\partial\cdot A)^2$ the quadratic operator becomes $K^{\mu\nu}=-k^2g^{\mu\nu}+\big(1-\tfrac1\xi\big)k^\mu k^\nu$, which is invertible, and

$$\langle A_\mu A_\nu\rangle(k) = \frac{-i}{k^2+i\epsilon}\Big[g_{\mu\nu}-(1-\xi)\frac{k_\mu k_\nu}{k^2}\Big].$$

Feynman gauge is $\xi=1$ and Landau gauge is $\xi\to0$ (a transverse propagator). The $\xi$-dependent part is proportional to $k_\mu k_\nu$ and drops out of any amplitude built from conserved currents, $k_\mu J^\mu=0$. Hence gauge-invariant observables (S-matrix elements, correlators of $F_{\mu\nu}$) are independent of $\xi$, while the propagator itself and quantities such as the electron wave-function renormalization $Z_2$ are not.

### Non-Abelian case: ghosts

For $SU(N)$, $\delta A^a_\mu=\frac1g(D_\mu\alpha)^a$ with $(D_\mu\alpha)^a=\partial_\mu\alpha^a+gf^{abc}A^b_\mu\alpha^c$. With $G^a=\partial^\mu A^a_\mu-\omega^a$,

$$\frac{\delta G^a}{\delta\alpha^b}=\frac1g\,\partial^\mu D^{ab}_\mu,$$

which depends on $A$. The determinant no longer decouples. It is exponentiated with anticommuting scalar **ghost** fields: $\det M=\int\mathcal D\bar c\,\mathcal Dc\;e^{i\int\bar c M c}$, giving

$$\mathcal L_{\rm gh} = \bar c^a\big(-\partial^\mu D^{ab}_\mu\big)c^b.$$

Ghosts have the wrong spin-statistics for scalars and never appear as asymptotic states. In loops they cancel the unphysical polarizations of the gauge field: a gluon loop in a covariant gauge counts $4$ polarizations per color, and the ghost loop (with its minus sign) removes $2$, leaving $2$. In QED the ghosts decouple.

### BRST symmetry and Gribov copies

The gauge-fixed action keeps a global fermionic symmetry, **BRST**, generated by a nilpotent charge $Q_B^2=0$. Physical states are the $Q_B$-closed states modulo $Q_B$-exact ones; this implies that physical S-matrix elements do not depend on the gauge condition, and it yields the Slavnov–Taylor identities, the non-Abelian generalization of the Ward identity.

The condition $\partial\cdot A=0$ does not select a unique point on each orbit for large non-Abelian fields: there are **Gribov copies**. Perturbation theory is blind to this, but non-perturbative gauge fixing is not simple. Other gauges exist: Coulomb gauge $\nabla\cdot\vec A=0$ and axial gauge $A_3=0$ (no ghosts, but spurious poles in the propagator).

@example
title: A gauge-fixed integral in the toy model
problem: With rotations of the plane as gauge group and $\Delta_{\rm FP}=x$ on the slice $y=0$, $x>0$, show that $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^2x\;e^{-r^2/2}$ equals the gauge-fixed integral $\int_0^\infty dx\,\Delta_{\rm FP}\,e^{-x^2/2}$.
step: The left side is $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta\int_0^\infty r\,dr\,e^{-r^2/2}$. The factor $2\pi$ from the angle is the group volume and cancels.
step: What remains is $\int_0^\infty r\,e^{-r^2/2}\,dr$.
step: On the slice $y=0$, $x>0$ we have $r=x$ and the weight is $\Delta_{\rm FP}=x$, so the gauge-fixed integral is $\int_0^\infty x\,e^{-x^2/2}\,dx$.
step: Both equal $1$. The Faddeev–Popov weight $x$ is exactly the Jacobian of polar coordinates.

@example
title: The propagator inverts the gauge-fixed operator
problem: Verify that $D_{\nu\rho}=-\frac1{k^2}\big[g_{\nu\rho}-(1-\xi)\frac{k_\nu k_\rho}{k^2}\big]$ is the inverse of $K^{\mu\nu}=-k^2g^{\mu\nu}+(1-\frac1\xi)k^\mu k^\nu$.
step: Write $K^{\mu\nu}=-k^2g^{\mu\nu}+a\,k^\mu k^\nu$ with $a=1-\frac1\xi$, and $D_{\nu\rho}=-\frac{1}{k^2}\big[g_{\nu\rho}-b\,\frac{k_\nu k_\rho}{k^2}\big]$ with $b=1-\xi$.
step: Multiply the four pairs of terms, using $k^\nu k_\nu=k^2$: $K^{\mu\nu}D_{\nu\rho}=-\frac{1}{k^2}\big[-k^2\,\delta^\mu_\rho+(a+b-ab)\,k^\mu k_\rho\big]$.
step: Compute the coefficient: $(1-\tfrac1\xi)(1-\xi)=2-\xi-\tfrac1\xi$, so $a+b-ab=\big(2-\tfrac1\xi-\xi\big)-\big(2-\xi-\tfrac1\xi\big)=0$.
step: Only $-\frac{1}{k^2}\cdot(-k^2)\,\delta^\mu_\rho=\delta^\mu_\rho$ survives, so $KD=1$. The Feynman propagator is $i$ times this inverse.

@mistakes
- Dropping the Faddeev–Popov determinant for non-Abelian groups; it depends on $A$ and produces the ghost loops.
- Writing the gauge-fixing term with the wrong sign or with $\xi$ in the numerator: it is $-\frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial\cdot A)^2$.
- Concluding that a gauge-dependent quantity (the propagator, $Z_2$) is physical because it is finite.
- Treating ghosts as physical particles; they are bookkeeping fields for the determinant and never appear in external states.
