@article
Interacting theories are not Gaussian, so the exact methods of week 2 no longer apply. The standard strategy is to expand in the coupling and evaluate each term with Gaussian integrals.

### The master formula

We work in Euclidean signature, obtained from the $(+,-,-,-)$ theory by $t=-i\tau$:

$$S_E = \int d^4x\,\Big[\tfrac12(\partial_\mu\phi)^2 + \tfrac12 m^2\phi^2 + \tfrac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4\Big].$$

Since $\phi(x)\,e^{\int J\phi} = \frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\,e^{\int J\phi}$, every factor of $\phi$ in the integrand may be replaced by a functional derivative outside the integral:

$$Z[J] = \exp\Big(-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\int d^4x\,\Big(\frac{\delta}{\delta J(x)}\Big)^4\Big)\,Z_0[J],\qquad Z_0[J] = Z_0[0]\,\exp\Big(\tfrac12\int\!\!\int J\,G\,J\Big).$$

Expand the exponential in $\lambda$. Each order is a finite number of derivatives of a Gaussian, which is a sum over Wick contractions. In Minkowski signature the rules are the vertex $-i\lambda$ and the propagator $\frac{i}{p^2-m^2+i\epsilon}$; in Euclidean signature they are $-\lambda$ and $\frac{1}{p^2+m^2}$.

### A zero-dimensional laboratory

Replace spacetime by a single point. Then $Z(g) = \int d\phi\; e^{-\frac12\phi^2 - \frac{g}{4!}\phi^4}$ and $\langle\phi^{2n}\rangle_0 = (2n-1)!!$. Expanding in $g$,

$$\frac{Z(g)}{\sqrt{2\pi}} = \sum_{n}\frac{(-g/24)^n}{n!}\,(4n-1)!! = 1 - \frac{g}{8} + \frac{35}{384}g^2 - \dots$$

The coefficients grow like $n!$, so the series has **zero radius of convergence**: for $g<0$ the integral diverges. Perturbation series in field theory are **asymptotic**: useful at small coupling, not exact sums.

### Diagrams and symmetry factors

A Wick contraction pattern is a Feynman diagram. Each vertex carries $-\lambda$, each line the propagator, and the diagram is divided by its **symmetry factor** $S$, the number of ways to relabel lines and vertices that leave the diagram unchanged with external legs fixed. The order-$g$ vacuum term $-g/8$ is the figure-eight diagram with $S=8$.

### Vacuum bubbles cancel

Normalized correlators are ratios, $\langle\phi\cdots\rangle = \int\phi\cdots e^{-S}/\int e^{-S}$. Disconnected vacuum diagrams factor out of the numerator as the same exponential that appears in the denominator, and they cancel. Example:

$$\langle\phi^2\rangle = \frac{1 - \frac58 g + \dots}{1 - \frac18 g + \dots} = 1 - \frac{g}{2} + \frac23 g^2 - \dots$$

The $-\frac g2$ is the tadpole: vertex $-g$ divided by symmetry factor $S=2$. At order $g^2$ three connected diagrams contribute: two successive tadpoles on a line ($S=4$), a tadpole inside a tadpole ($S=4$), and the sunset with three lines between two vertices ($S=3!=6$). Their weights $\frac14+\frac14+\frac16=\frac23$ reproduce the coefficient above. This kind of check on symmetry factors is worth doing whenever you can.

### Connected and 1PI diagrams

$W=\ln Z$ is the sum of connected diagrams. Cutting one internal line of a 1PI diagram never disconnects it, and $\Gamma$ is the sum of 1PI diagrams. The connected four-point function at order $g$ is a single vertex: in the zero-dimensional model $\langle\phi^4\rangle_c = \langle\phi^4\rangle - 3\langle\phi^2\rangle^2 = -g + \dots$

### Schwinger–Dyson equations

The integral of a total functional derivative vanishes, $\int\mathcal D\phi\,\frac{\delta}{\delta\phi(x)}\big(e^{-S+\int J\phi}\big) = 0$, which gives

$$\Big[-\frac{\delta S_E}{\delta\phi(x)}\Big(\frac{\delta}{\delta J}\Big) + J(x)\Big]Z[J] = 0.$$

For the free theory this reproduces $(-\partial^2+m^2)\langle\phi(x)\phi(y)\rangle = \delta^4(x-y)$. These exact equations relate correlators of different orders and are a non-perturbative starting point.

@example
title: The figure-eight by counting contractions
problem: Show that the order-$g$ term of $Z(g)/\sqrt{2\pi}$ in the zero-dimensional model is $-g/8$ and identify its symmetry factor.
step: The first-order term is $-\frac{g}{4!}\langle\phi^4\rangle_0$, where $\langle\ \rangle_0$ uses $e^{-\phi^2/2}$ and a unit propagator.
step: Pair the four fields of the single vertex: field $1$ can pair with $3$ others and the remaining two pair together, so $\langle\phi^4\rangle_0 = 3$.
step: The term is $-\frac{g}{24}\cdot 3 = -\frac g8$.
step: Hence $S=8$: each of the two loops can be reversed (factor $2\times2$) and the two loops can be exchanged (factor $2$).

@example
title: The tadpole correction to the two-point function
problem: Count the connected order-$g$ contractions in $\langle\phi\,\phi\,(-\frac{g}{4!}\phi^4)\rangle_0$ and show that they give $-g/2$.
step: Call the external fields $\phi_a$ and $\phi_b$. A connected contraction must pair each external field with a leg of the vertex.
step: $\phi_a$ has $4$ choices of leg, then $\phi_b$ has $3$ choices among the remaining legs, and the last two legs contract with each other: $4\cdot3\cdot1 = 12$ contractions.
step: The coefficient is $-\frac{g}{24}\cdot 12 = -\frac g2$, so the symmetry factor is $S=2$ (the closed loop can be reversed).
step: The disconnected contractions, $\phi_a\phi_b$ paired together times a figure-eight, are removed by the denominator.

@mistakes
- Omitting the symmetry factor, or using the vertex factor $-\lambda/4!$ instead of $-\lambda$ once the $4!$ from labeling the legs has been counted.
- Keeping disconnected vacuum diagrams in a normalized correlator instead of dividing by $Z$.
- Treating the perturbation series as convergent and quoting a "radius of convergence" in the coupling.
- Mixing Minkowski and Euclidean rules: the vertex is $-i\lambda$ with propagator $\frac{i}{p^2-m^2}$, but $-\lambda$ with $\frac{1}{p^2+m^2}$.
