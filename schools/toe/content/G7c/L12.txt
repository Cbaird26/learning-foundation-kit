@article
A symmetry of the Lagrangian need not be a symmetry of the vacuum. When the ground state does not respect a symmetry, it is **spontaneously broken**. The Lagrangian keeps the symmetry; the vacuum does not.

### A potential with a flat direction

Take a complex scalar with global $U(1)$ symmetry $\phi\to e^{i\theta}\phi$ and, in signature $(+,-,-,-)$,

$$\mathcal L=\partial_\mu\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi-V(\phi),\qquad V=-\mu^2|\phi|^2+\lambda|\phi|^4,\quad\mu^2>0,\ \lambda>0.$$

Writing $\phi=\rho\,e^{i\theta}/\sqrt2$, $V=-\frac{\mu^2}{2}\rho^2+\frac\lambda4\rho^4$. Then $V'(\rho)=0$ gives $\rho=v$ with $v^2=\mu^2/\lambda$: a whole circle of degenerate minima, the **vacuum manifold**. Choose the point $\langle\phi\rangle=v/\sqrt2$ and expand, $\phi=\frac1{\sqrt2}(v+h+i\chi)$:

$$\mathcal L=\tfrac12(\partial h)^2+\tfrac12(\partial\chi)^2-\tfrac12m_h^2h^2-\lambda v\,h(h^2+\chi^2)-\tfrac\lambda4(h^2+\chi^2)^2+\text{const},\qquad m_h^2=V''(v)=2\lambda v^2=2\mu^2.$$

The radial mode $h$ is massive. The angular mode $\chi$ has no mass term: it is the **Goldstone boson**, the excitation along the flat direction.

**Goldstone's theorem:** a spontaneously broken continuous global symmetry $G\to H$ produces $\dim G-\dim H$ massless scalars. For $O(N)\to O(N-1)$ this is $\frac{N(N-1)}2-\frac{(N-1)(N-2)}2=N-1$.

### Gauging the symmetry: the Abelian Higgs model

Now make the symmetry local with $D_\mu\phi=(\partial_\mu-iqA_\mu)\phi$, $\phi\to e^{iq\alpha}\phi$, as in week 9:

$$\mathcal L=-\tfrac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+(D_\mu\phi)^*(D^\mu\phi)-V(\phi).$$

Write $\phi=\frac1{\sqrt2}(v+h)\,e^{i\theta/v}$. A gauge transformation with $\alpha=-\theta/(qv)$ removes $\theta$: this is **unitary gauge**. The kinetic term becomes

$$|D_\mu\phi|^2=\tfrac12(\partial h)^2+\tfrac12q^2(v+h)^2A_\mu A^\mu\;\Longrightarrow\; m_A^2=q^2v^2.$$

The vector acquires a mass term $\frac12m_A^2A_\mu A^\mu$ even though the theory is gauge invariant: the apparent violation is the Goldstone field $\theta$ in disguise (the invariant combination is $A_\mu-\frac{1}{qv}\partial_\mu\theta$).

**Counting degrees of freedom:**

| | Before (symmetric phase) | After (unitary gauge) |
|---|---|---|
| Scalar | $2$ (complex $\phi$) | $1$ ($h$) |
| Vector | $2$ (massless) | $3$ (massive) |
| Total | $4$ | $4$ |

The Goldstone mode is not a physical particle: it is "eaten" and becomes the longitudinal polarization. The same mechanism in the Standard Model, $SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y\to U(1)_{\rm em}$ with a doublet of vev $v\approx246$ GeV, gives $m_W=\frac12gv$, $m_Z=\frac12\sqrt{g^2+g'^2}\,v$, a massless photon, and $m_h^2=2\lambda v^2$ for $V=-\mu^2|H|^2+\lambda|H|^4$; three broken generators supply the three longitudinal modes.

### What is physical

The value $\langle\phi\rangle\neq0$ is not gauge invariant, and a local gauge symmetry cannot be broken in the sense of Goldstone: gauge-variant order parameters vanish (Elitzur's theorem). "Broken" is a description in a fixed gauge. The physical content is gauge invariant: the spectrum (massive vector, Higgs scalar, no Goldstone) and correlators of gauge-invariant operators.

### Loop corrections: the effective potential

By week 2, the Euclidean one-loop effective action is $\Gamma=S_E+\frac12\mathrm{Tr}\ln S_E''$. For a constant background field $\varphi$ and a single real scalar with $V''(\varphi)>0$, in $\overline{\rm MS}$,

$$V_{\rm eff}(\varphi)=V(\varphi)+\frac{[V''(\varphi)]^2}{64\pi^2}\Big[\ln\frac{V''(\varphi)}{\mu^2}-\frac32\Big].$$

Each species $i$ with field-dependent mass $M_i(\varphi)$ contributes a similar term weighted by its number of degrees of freedom $n_i$ (negative for fermions), with $\frac{(V'')^2}{64\pi^2}$ replaced by $\frac{n_iM_i^4}{64\pi^2}$; the constant $\frac32$ becomes $\frac56$ for massive vector bosons in Landau gauge. Loops shift the minimum and can even trigger breaking in a classically scale-invariant theory. The location of the minimum is gauge dependent in gauge theories; the masses and the value of $V_{\rm eff}$ at the minimum are not. The one-loop expression is reliable only at weak coupling and when the logarithms $\ln(M^2/\mu^2)$ are not large.

@example
title: Vacuum and spectrum
problem: For $V=-\frac{\mu^2}{2}\rho^2+\frac\lambda4\rho^4$ find the minimum $v$, the radial mass, and the Goldstone mass.
step: Set $V'(\rho)=-\mu^2\rho+\lambda\rho^3=0$. The solutions are $\rho=0$ and $\rho^2=\mu^2/\lambda\equiv v^2$. Since $V''(0)=-\mu^2<0$ the origin is a maximum, so the minima are at $\rho=v$.
step: The radial mass squared is $V''(v)=-\mu^2+3\lambda v^2=2\mu^2$, so $m_h^2=2\lambda v^2$.
step: The angular direction leaves $V$ unchanged because $V$ depends only on $\rho$: the curvature is $0$, so $m_\chi=0$.
step: The massless $\chi$ is the Goldstone boson of the broken $U(1)$.

@example
title: The vector mass in unitary gauge
problem: Show that $|D_\mu\phi|^2$ contains $\frac12q^2v^2A_\mu A^\mu$ when $\phi=\frac1{\sqrt2}(v+h)$ is real.
step: With real $\phi=\frac1{\sqrt2}(v+h)$, $D_\mu\phi=\frac1{\sqrt2}\big[\partial_\mu h-iqA_\mu(v+h)\big]$.
step: Multiply by the complex conjugate: $|D_\mu\phi|^2=\frac12(\partial_\mu h)^2+\frac12q^2A_\mu A^\mu(v+h)^2$, since the cross terms are imaginary and cancel.
step: At $h=0$ the second term is $\frac12q^2v^2A_\mu A^\mu$, which has the form $\frac12m_A^2A_\mu A^\mu$ of a vector mass term.
step: Therefore $m_A=|q|v$.

@mistakes
- Saying that the Lagrangian symmetry is broken; it is the vacuum that does not respect it.
- Counting the Goldstone boson as a physical massless particle in a gauged theory; it is absorbed into the longitudinal polarization.
- Claiming that $\langle\phi\rangle\neq0$ is a gauge-invariant order parameter for a local symmetry.
- Using the tree-level potential where large logarithms $\ln(M^2/\mu^2)$ make loop corrections comparable to the tree terms.
