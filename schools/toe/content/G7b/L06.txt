@article
We now solve the free Dirac equation $(i\not\partial-m)\psi=0$ with plane waves. The solutions are the spinors that appear on every external fermion line of a Feynman diagram.

### Plane-wave solutions

Positive-frequency solutions are $\psi=u(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot x}$ and negative-frequency ones are $\psi=v(p)\,e^{+ip\cdot x}$, with $p^0=E_{\mathbf p}>0$ in both. Since $i\partial_\mu e^{\mp ip\cdot x}=\pm p_\mu e^{\mp ip\cdot x}$, the Dirac equation becomes
$$(\not p-m)\,u(p)=0,\qquad(\not p+m)\,v(p)=0.$$
In the Weyl representation $\not p=\begin{pmatrix}0&p\cdot\sigma\\p\cdot\bar\sigma&0\end{pmatrix}$ with $p\cdot\sigma=p^0-\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ and $p\cdot\bar\sigma=p^0+\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$. Writing $u=(u_L,u_R)^T$, the first equation says $(p\cdot\sigma)u_R=m\,u_L$ and $(p\cdot\bar\sigma)u_L=m\,u_R$. Using $(p\cdot\sigma)(p\cdot\bar\sigma)=p^2=m^2$ the solutions are
$$u^s(p)=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\;\xi^s\\\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\;\xi^s\end{pmatrix},\qquad v^s(p)=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\;\eta^s\\-\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\;\eta^s\end{pmatrix}.$$
Here $\xi^s,\eta^s$ are two-component spinors with $\xi^{r\dagger}\xi^s=\delta^{rs}$ (and likewise for $\eta$), and the square roots are matrix square roots of positive matrices. At rest $u=\sqrt m\,(\xi,\xi)^T$. For $\mathbf p=p\hat z$ and spin up, $\xi=(1,0)^T$, one finds $u^\uparrow=(\sqrt{E-p}\,\xi,\ \sqrt{E+p}\,\xi)^T$, and for spin down $u^\downarrow=(\sqrt{E+p}\,\xi',\ \sqrt{E-p}\,\xi')^T$ with $\xi'=(0,1)^T$.

### Normalization and bilinears

With these conventions
$$\bar u^ru^s=2m\,\delta^{rs},\qquad\bar v^rv^s=-2m\,\delta^{rs},\qquad u^{r\dagger}u^s=v^{r\dagger}v^s=2E_{\mathbf p}\,\delta^{rs},\qquad\bar u^r\gamma^\mu u^s=\bar v^r\gamma^\mu v^s=2p^\mu\delta^{rs}.$$
The factor $2E_{\mathbf p}$ is the relativistic normalization again: a plane wave of unit amplitude has $j^0=\psi^\dagger\psi=2E_{\mathbf p}$, just as for the complex scalar in week 2. The **completeness relations** turn spin sums into traces:
$$\sum_su^s(p)\bar u^s(p)=\not p+m,\qquad\sum_sv^s(p)\bar v^s(p)=\not p-m.$$
For example $\sum_s\bar u^s\gamma^\mu u^s=\mathrm{tr}[(\not p+m)\gamma^\mu]=4p^\mu$, which is two spin states times $2p^\mu$.

### Checking a solution and its current

To check a candidate solution: (i) confirm $p^2=m^2$; (ii) multiply out $(\not p-m)u$ block by block; (iii) compute $\bar u\gamma^\mu u$ and compare with $2p^\mu$; (iv) for a superposition $\psi=\sum_ic_iu_ie^{-ip_i\cdot x}$ test $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$. The diagonal terms of $j^\mu=\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ are constants. A cross term between waves $i$ and $j$ carries the phase $e^{i(p_j-p_i)\cdot x}$, and
$$\partial_\mu\Big(\bar u_j\gamma^\mu u_i\,e^{i(p_j-p_i)\cdot x}\Big)=i\,\bar u_j(\not p_j-\not p_i)\,u_i\,e^{i(p_j-p_i)\cdot x}=i(m-m)\,\bar u_ju_i\,e^{i(p_j-p_i)\cdot x}=0,$$
using $\bar u_j\not p_j=m\bar u_j$ and $\not p_iu_i=mu_i$. So the current is conserved only because both waves obey the Dirac equation with the same mass.

### Massless spinors: chirality and helicity

For $m=0$ the blocks decouple: $(p\cdot\bar\sigma)u_L=0$ and $(p\cdot\sigma)u_R=0$. For $\mathbf p=p\hat z$, $p\cdot\sigma=E(1-\sigma_z)$ annihilates spin up. A massless particle with spin along its momentum (positive helicity) therefore has $u_L=0$: it is purely right-chiral.

### Quantization with anticommutators

The quantized field is
$$\psi(x)=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_s\Big(b^s_{\mathbf p}\,u^s(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot x}+d^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,v^s(p)\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Big),$$
with $\{b^r_{\mathbf p},b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q}\}=(2\pi)^3\delta^{rs}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$ and the same for $d$; all other anticommutators vanish. This gives
$$:\!H\!:\;=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}\sum_s\big(b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}b^s_{\mathbf p}+d^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}d^s_{\mathbf p}\big),\qquad Q=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\sum_s\big(b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}b^s_{\mathbf p}-d^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}d^s_{\mathbf p}\big).$$
With commutators instead, the Hamiltonian would contain $-E_{\mathbf p}d^\dagger d$ and be unbounded below. Anticommutators also make multi-fermion states antisymmetric: this is the spin–statistics connection. $b^\dagger$ creates a particle of charge $+1$ and $d^\dagger$ an antiparticle of charge $-1$.

@example
title: An explicit spinor, checked
problem: For $m=3$ and $\mathbf p=(0,0,4)$ construct the spin-up spinor $u$, verify the Dirac equation, and compute $\bar uu$, $u^\dagger u$ and $\bar u\gamma^3u$.
step: $E=\sqrt{4^2+3^2}=5$. For spin up, $\sigma_z\xi=\xi$, so $p\cdot\sigma=E-p\sigma_z=\mathrm{diag}(1,9)$ and $p\cdot\bar\sigma=E+p\sigma_z=\mathrm{diag}(9,1)$.
step: $u=(\sqrt1\,\xi,\ \sqrt9\,\xi)^T=(1,0,3,0)^T$, i.e. $u_L=(1,0)^T$ and $u_R=(3,0)^T$.
step: Dirac equation: $(p\cdot\sigma)u_R=\mathrm{diag}(1,9)(3,0)^T=(3,0)^T=3\,u_L$ and $(p\cdot\bar\sigma)u_L=\mathrm{diag}(9,1)(1,0)^T=(9,0)^T=3\,u_R$. Both hold with $m=3$.
step: Scalar and density: $\bar uu=2u_L^\dagger u_R=6=2m$ and $u^\dagger u=1+9=10=2E$.
step: Current component: $\gamma^0\gamma^3=\mathrm{diag}(-\sigma_z,\sigma_z)$, so $\bar u\gamma^3u=-u_L^\dagger\sigma_zu_L+u_R^\dagger\sigma_zu_R=-1+9=8=2p^3$.

@example
title: Conservation of the current of a two-wave superposition
problem: Let $\psi=u_1e^{-ip_1\cdot x}+u_2e^{-ip_2\cdot x}$ with both waves solving the Dirac equation with mass $m$. Show that $\partial_\mu(\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi)=0$.
step: Write $\bar\psi=\bar u_1e^{ip_1\cdot x}+\bar u_2e^{ip_2\cdot x}$. Then $j^\mu$ has two diagonal terms $\bar u_i\gamma^\mu u_i$, which are constant, and two cross terms $\bar u_j\gamma^\mu u_i\,e^{i(p_j-p_i)\cdot x}$ for $i\neq j$.
step: Constants have zero divergence, so only the cross terms matter. Differentiating, $\partial_\mu e^{i(p_j-p_i)\cdot x}=i(p_j-p_i)_\mu e^{i(p_j-p_i)\cdot x}$.
step: The divergence of a cross term is $i\,\bar u_j(\not p_j-\not p_i)\,u_i\,e^{i(p_j-p_i)\cdot x}$.
step: Use $\bar u_j\not p_j=m\bar u_j$ (the Dirac adjoint of $(\not p_j-m)u_j=0$) and $\not p_iu_i=mu_i$: the bracket becomes $m\bar u_ju_i-m\bar u_ju_i=0$. Hence $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$.

@mistakes
- Using $(\not p-m)$ for $v(p)$. The wave $v\,e^{+ip\cdot x}$ flips the sign of the derivative term, so $(\not p+m)v=0$.
- Forgetting the sign difference $\bar uu=+2m$ but $\bar vv=-2m$.
- Treating $\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}$ as a number. It is the matrix square root of $E-\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$, whose eigenvalues are $E\mp|\mathbf p|$.
- Normalizing $u$ to 1. With this convention $u^\dagger u=2E_{\mathbf p}$.

@quiz mc
prompt: Why is the quantized Dirac field expanded with anticommutators $\{b,b^\dagger\}$ and $\{d,d^\dagger\}$ rather than commutators?
choice*: With commutators the Hamiltonian would contain $-E_{\mathbf p}d^\dagger d$ and be unbounded below; anticommutators make it positive and give antisymmetric multi-fermion states.
choice: Anticommutators are needed so that $\bar uu=2m$ holds.
choice: Anticommutators make the field operator Hermitian.
choice: With commutators the charge $Q$ would be zero.
hint: Compute $H$ with commutators and with anticommutators, and look at the sign of the antiparticle term.
hint: Normal ordering fermion operators introduces a minus sign when two operators are swapped.
solution: The spinor normalizations are fixed by the Dirac equation and do not depend on operator statistics, and the Dirac field is not Hermitian with either commutators or anticommutators. With commutators the Hamiltonian would be $H=\int E_{\mathbf p}(b^\dagger b-d^\dagger d)$, with a spectrum unbounded below, and the charge would be $b^\dagger b+d^\dagger d$, which would not distinguish particles from antiparticles (it would not vanish). Anticommutators give $H=\int E_{\mathbf p}(b^\dagger b+d^\dagger d)\ge0$ after normal ordering, $Q=\int(b^\dagger b-d^\dagger d)$, and antisymmetric (Pauli-exclusion) multi-fermion states.
