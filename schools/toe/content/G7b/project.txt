title: Tree-level scalar scattering with every factor tracked
overview: You will derive the lowest-order amplitude for $\phi\phi\to\phi\phi$ in a real scalar theory with cubic and quartic self-interactions, starting from the Lagrangian and ending at a cross section. At every stage you record the conventions, the normalization of states, each combinatorial and symmetry factor, and a dimensional check. The deliverable is a short write-up in which every number can be traced to a stated rule or count.
goal: Derive the order-$\lambda$ and order-$g^2$ amplitudes for $\phi\phi\to\phi\phi$ from the Dyson series and Wick's theorem, with each combinatorial factor traced to an explicit count.
goal: State and apply conventions consistently: units, signature, mode expansion, state normalization and the definition of $\mathcal M$.
goal: Convert the amplitude into a cross section with the correct phase space and identical-particle factor, and test the result by dimensional analysis and limiting cases.
step: Write $\mathcal L=\frac12(\partial\phi)^2-\frac12m^2\phi^2-\frac{g}{3!}\phi^3-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$. List the conventions ($\hbar=c=1$, signature $(+,-,-,-)$, the mode expansion, $[a_p,a_q^\dagger]=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$, $|p\rangle=\sqrt{2E_p}\,a_p^\dagger|0\rangle$) and the mass dimensions of $\phi$, $g$ and $\lambda$ in four dimensions.
step: Expand $S=T\exp\!\big(-i\int d^4x\,H_I\big)$ to first order in $\lambda$. Write $\langle p_3p_4|\phi^4(x)|p_1p_2\rangle$ and count the Wick contractions explicitly. Show how the $4!$ contractions cancel the $\frac1{4!}$, how the $x$-integral produces $(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}$, and read off $i\mathcal M$.
step: Expand to second order in $g$. Count the contractions that produce the $s$-channel diagram and show how the $\frac1{2!}$ of the Dyson series and the two factors $\frac1{3!}$ are cancelled. Repeat for the $t$ and $u$ channels and insert $D_F$ in momentum space.
step: Write $\mathcal M(s,t,u)$, express $t$ and $u$ through $s$ and the CM angle, and verify $s+t+u=4m^2$, symmetry under exchange of identical particles, the signs of $t$ and $u$ in the physical region, and $[\mathcal M]=0$.
step: Compute $d\sigma/d\Omega$ and the total $\sigma$ for two or three parameter sets, including the factor $\frac12$ for identical final particles, and check $[\sigma]=-2$.
step: Verify the counting independently. Write a short program that enumerates the Wick pairings (for example with a recursive pairing generator), confirms the counts from steps 2 and 3, and evaluates $\mathcal M$ and $\sigma$ numerically for comparison with your hand results.
step: Write the report: a conventions table, the derivation, the list of diagrams with their symmetry factors, the checks, and a paragraph on what you did not verify. Practice explaining why each factor appears, aloud and without notes.

@review
### Conventions to restate on every page
Units $\hbar=c=1$ and metric $(+,-,-,-)$. The field is $\phi(x)=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\sqrt{2E_p}}\big(a_pe^{-ip\cdot x}+a_p^\dagger e^{ip\cdot x}\big)$ with $[a_p,a_q^\dagger]=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$. States are $|p\rangle=\sqrt{2E_p}\,a_p^\dagger|0\rangle$, so $\langle p|q\rangle=2E_p(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$.

### Week by week
1. **Classical fields:** the Euler-Lagrange equation $\partial_\mu\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi}$, the Klein-Gordon equation, and dimension counting $[\phi]=(d-2)/2$.
2. **Noether's theorem:** a continuous symmetry gives a conserved current; translations give $T^{\mu\nu}$; the $U(1)$ phase symmetry gives the charge current.
3. **Scalar quantization:** $\pi=\dot\phi$, the equal-time commutator, the mode algebra, and $H=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_p\,a_p^\dagger a_p$ after normal ordering.
4. **Propagators:** the commutator vanishes at spacelike separation, and $D_F$ is the time-ordered vacuum expectation value, with the $i\epsilon$ prescription.
5. **Spinors:** $\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}$, the Lorentz transformation $S(\Lambda)$, and covariant bilinears.
6. **Dirac field:** $u(p)$, $v(p)$, spin sums $\not p\pm m$, the current $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$, and why fermions need anticommutators.
7. **Interaction picture:** fields evolve with $H_0$, and the states carry the effect of $H_I$.
8. **Dyson series:** $U=T\exp(-i\int H_I\,dt)$ and the order-by-order expansion of the $S$-matrix.
9. **Wick's theorem:** a time-ordered product is the normal-ordered product plus all contractions, each contraction being $D_F$; counting pairings.
10. **Feynman rules:** vertices $-ig$ and $-i\lambda$, propagator $i/(p^2-m^2+i\epsilon)$, symmetry factors, and $L=I-V+1$.
11. **Amplitudes:** $\langle f|iT|i\rangle=(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}\,i\mathcal M$, $s+t+u=\sum m^2$, and $[\mathcal M]=4-n$.
12. **Rates:** $\Gamma=\frac1{2M}\int d\Pi\,|\mathcal M|^2$, $d\sigma/d\Omega=|\mathcal M|^2/(64\pi^2s)$ for elastic CM scattering, identical-particle factors and unit conversion.

### Self-check list
- I can derive a field equation and a Noether current from a Lagrangian.
- I can move between the mode algebra and the field commutators.
- I can count Wick contractions for a diagram and show where each $n!$ cancels.
- I can evaluate $\mathcal M$ at a physical point and check its dimension.
- I can turn $|\mathcal M|^2$ into $\Gamma$ or $\sigma$ with the right flux and identical-particle factors.

### Most common errors
- Switching signature mid-calculation, which flips the sign of $p^2-m^2$ and of $t$ and $u$.
- Dropping $2E_p$ in the state normalization or $(2\pi)^3$ in the commutator.
- Applying the symmetry factor twice, or not at all.
- Using $\sum v\bar v=\not p+m$ instead of $\not p-m$.
- Treating the $i\epsilon$ as optional when closing contours.
- Quoting a cross section in GeV$^{-2}$ without converting to a measurable unit.

prompt: A real scalar field of mass $m=1$ has $\mathcal L_{\rm int}=-\frac{g}{3!}\phi^3-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$ with $g=1.5$ and $\lambda=2$ (all in units of $m$). At tree level, compute the total cross section $\sigma(\phi\phi\to\phi\phi)$ at $\sqrt s=6$, including the correct treatment of the identical final particles. Give the value in units of $m^{-2}$ to three significant figures.
answer: 7.19e-4
check: (lambda mm, g, lam, rs, dot: (lambda p: 0.5*quad(lambda th: 2*pi*sin(th)*(lam + g**2*sum(1/(dot(q, q) - mm**2) for q in [np.array([rs, 0, 0, 0]), np.array([0, -p*sin(th), 0, p - p*cos(th)]), np.array([0, p*sin(th), 0, p + p*cos(th)])]))**2/(64*pi**2*rs**2), 0, pi))(sqrt(rs**2/4 - mm**2)))(1, 1.5, 2, 6, lambda a, b: a[0]*b[0] - a[1]*b[1] - a[2]*b[2] - a[3]*b[3])
tol: 1%
hint: In the CM frame $|\mathbf p|^2=s/4-m^2$, with $t=-2|\mathbf p|^2(1-\cos\theta)$ and $u=-2|\mathbf p|^2(1+\cos\theta)$.
hint: The Feynman rules give $\mathcal M=-\lambda-g^2\big[\frac1{s-m^2}+\frac1{t-m^2}+\frac1{u-m^2}\big]$, which depends on $\cos\theta$ through $t$ and $u$.
hint: The scattering is elastic and the final particles are identical, so $\sigma=\frac12\int d\Omega\,\frac{|\mathcal M|^2}{64\pi^2s}$. Integrate over $x=\cos\theta$ numerically, or use $\int_{-1}^1\frac{dx}{c^2-d^2x^2}=\frac1{cd}\ln\frac{c+d}{c-d}$.
solution: Here $|\mathbf p|^2=8$, so $t=-16(1-x)$, $u=-16(1+x)$ and $s+t+u=4$. Then $\mathcal M=-\lambda-\frac{g^2}{s-m^2}+\frac{2g^2c}{c^2-d^2x^2}=-\frac{289}{140}+\frac{76.5}{289-256x^2}$ with $c=17$, $d=16$. Since $\mathcal M$ is real and depends only on $x$, $\sigma=\frac12\cdot2\pi\int_{-1}^1dx\,\frac{\mathcal M^2}{64\pi^2s}=\frac1{64\pi s}\int_{-1}^1\mathcal M^2dx$. The integral is $2A^2+2AB\,I_2+B^2I_3$ with $A=-\frac{289}{140}$, $B=76.5$, $I_2=\frac{\ln33}{272}$ and $I_3=\frac1{c^2(c^2-d^2)}+\frac{\ln33}{2c^3d}$, which evaluates to about $5.206$. Therefore $\sigma\approx\frac{5.206}{64\pi\cdot36}\approx7.19\times10^{-4}\,m^{-2}$. The dimension is $[\sigma]=-2$ because $g/m$ and $\lambda$ are dimensionless.

prompt: A heavy real scalar $\Phi$ (mass $M=26$) and a light real scalar $\phi$ (mass $m=5$) interact only through $\mathcal L_{\rm int}=-\frac{\mu}{2}\Phi\phi^2$ with $\mu=13$ (one common mass unit). Work at tree level and enter the ordered triple of (i) the width $\Gamma(\Phi\to\phi\phi)$, (ii) the amplitude $\mathcal M(\phi\phi\to\phi\phi)$ at threshold, $s=4m^2$ and $t=u=0$, from $\Phi$ exchange in the $s$, $t$ and $u$ channels, and (iii) the coefficient $\lambda_{\rm eff}$ in $\mathcal L_{\rm eff}=-\frac{\lambda_{\rm eff}}{4!}\phi^4$, found by solving the $\Phi$ field equation for constant fields and substituting the solution back into $\mathcal L$. Give each value to three significant figures.
answer: (3/(16pi), 457/576, -3/4)
check: (lambda M, m, mu, dot, v: (1/(2*M)*0.5*mu**2*quad(lambda th: 2*pi*sin(th)/(16*pi**2)*sqrt(M**2/4 - m**2)/M, 0, pi), -mu**2*sum(1/(dot(q, q) - M**2) for q in [v + v, v - v, v - v]), -24*(lambda f: f(root(lambda x: nderiv(f, x), -10, 10)))(lambda x: -0.5*M**2*x**2 - 0.5*mu*x)))(26, 5, 13, lambda a, b: a[0]*b[0] - a[1]*b[1] - a[2]*b[2] - a[3]*b[3], np.array([5, 0, 0, 0]))
tol: 1%
hint: The vertex is $-i\mu$ because the $\frac12$ cancels the $2!$ ways of attaching two identical $\phi$ lines. For (i) use $\Gamma=\frac1{2M}\cdot\frac12\int d\Pi_2\,|\mathcal M|^2$ with $\int d\Pi_2=\frac{|\mathbf p_f|}{4\pi M}$.
hint: For (ii) each channel contributes $-\mu^2/(q^2-M^2)$ with $q^2=s$, $t$ or $u$. At threshold both $\phi$ particles are at rest, so $s=4m^2$ and $t=u=0$.
hint: For (iii) at constant fields $\mathcal L=-\frac12M^2\Phi^2-\frac\mu2\Phi\phi^2$. Set $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\Phi=0$, substitute the solution back, and match the $\phi^4$ term to $-\frac{\lambda_{\rm eff}}{4!}\phi^4$.
solution: (i) $|\mathbf p_f|=\sqrt{M^2/4-m^2}=12$, so $\Gamma=\frac{\mu^2|\mathbf p_f|}{16\pi M^2}=\frac{169\cdot12}{16\pi\cdot676}=\frac3{16\pi}\approx0.0597$. (ii) $\mathcal M=-\mu^2\Big[\frac1{4m^2-M^2}+\frac2{-M^2}\Big]=169\Big[\frac1{576}+\frac2{676}\Big]=\frac{457}{576}\approx0.793$. (iii) The field equation gives $\Phi=-\mu\phi^2/(2M^2)$. Substituting, $\mathcal L_{\rm eff}=-\frac{\mu^2}{8M^2}\phi^4\cdot(-1)=+\frac{\mu^2}{8M^2}\phi^4=-\frac{\lambda_{\rm eff}}{4!}\phi^4$, so $\lambda_{\rm eff}=-\frac{3\mu^2}{M^2}=-\frac34$. @exam self
prompt: A complex scalar field has $\mathcal L=\partial_\mu\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi-m^2\phi^*\phi-\frac{\lambda}{4}(\phi^*\phi)^2$ and the mode expansion $\phi(x)=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\sqrt{2E_p}}\big(a_pe^{-ip\cdot x}+b_p^\dagger e^{ip\cdot x}\big)$, with $[a_p,a_q^\dagger]=[b_p,b_q^\dagger]=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$ and all other commutators zero. (a) For $\delta\phi=-i\alpha\phi$ find the Noether current and show that its charge is $Q=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,(a_p^\dagger a_p-b_p^\dagger b_p)$. (b) Call the quanta created by $a^\dagger$ and $b^\dagger$ particles $a$ and $b$. Count the Wick contractions at first order in $\lambda$ to find the tree amplitudes for $aa\to aa$ and $ab\to ab$. (c) Give the total CM cross sections of both processes, including any identical-particle factor, and explain their ratio. (d) Check the dimensions of $\lambda$ and of each cross section in four spacetime dimensions.
solution: (a) The Noether formula $j^\mu=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\frac{\delta\phi}{\alpha}+\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^*)}\frac{\delta\phi^*}{\alpha}$ gives $j^\mu=i(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi-\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi^*)$, since the interaction has no derivatives. Insert the expansions into $Q=\int d^3x\,j^0$ and use $\int d^3x\,e^{i(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)\cdot\mathbf x}=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$. The $ab$ and $a^\dagger b^\dagger$ terms cancel between the two pieces of $j^0$, leaving $\frac12\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\big(a^\dagger a+aa^\dagger-bb^\dagger-b^\dagger b\big)$. The reordering constants cancel between $a$ and $b$, so $Q=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}(a_p^\dagger a_p-b_p^\dagger b_p)$, hence $[Q,a_p^\dagger]=a_p^\dagger$ and $[Q,b_p^\dagger]=-b_p^\dagger$: the two kinds of quanta carry opposite charge. (b) The first-order term is $-i\frac\lambda4\int d^4x\,\phi^*\phi^*\phi\phi$. For $aa\to aa$ the two incoming $a$ are annihilated by the two $\phi$ (2 ways) and the two outgoing $a$ are created by the two $\phi^*$ (2 ways): 4 contractions. For $ab\to ab$ the incoming $a$ is annihilated by one of the two $\phi$ (2 ways), the incoming $b$ by one of the two $\phi^*$ (2 ways), and the remaining $\phi^*$ and $\phi$ create the outgoing $a$ and $b$: again 4. Each count cancels the $\frac14$, so $i\mathcal M=-i\lambda$ and $\mathcal M=-\lambda$ for both processes. (c) The amplitude is isotropic, so $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{\lambda^2}{64\pi^2s}$. For $ab\to ab$ the final particles are distinguishable, so $\sigma=4\pi\frac{\lambda^2}{64\pi^2s}=\frac{\lambda^2}{16\pi s}$. For $aa\to aa$ the final particles are identical, so $\sigma=\frac12\cdot\frac{\lambda^2}{16\pi s}=\frac{\lambda^2}{32\pi s}$. The ratio of 2 comes entirely from the identical-particle factor, since the amplitudes are equal. (d) $[\phi]=1$, so $[\lambda]=0$, and $\lambda^2/s$ has dimension $-2$, as a cross section must.
hint: For (a) form $j^0=i(\phi^*\dot\phi-\phi\,\dot\phi^*)$ from the Noether formula, insert both expansions, and keep only the terms with one $\mathbf p$ integral left after the $x$-integration.
hint: For (b) the vertex is $-i\frac\lambda4\phi^*\phi^*\phi\phi$. Decide which fields annihilate the incoming quanta and which create the outgoing ones (recall $\phi$ contains $a$ and $b^\dagger$, while $\phi^*$ contains $a^\dagger$ and $b$), then count the choices.
hint: For (c) use $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{|\mathcal M|^2}{64\pi^2s}$ and ask in each process whether the two final particles are identical.

rubric: Counts four contractions for each of $aa\to aa$ and $ab\to ab$ and obtains $\mathcal M=-\lambda$ for both.
rubric: Obtains $\sigma(ab\to ab)=\lambda^2/(16\pi s)$ and $\sigma(aa\to aa)=\lambda^2/(32\pi s)$, attributing the ratio 2 to the identical-particle factor.
