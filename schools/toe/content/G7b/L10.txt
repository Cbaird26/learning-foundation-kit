@article
Wick's theorem turns every term of the S-matrix expansion into propagators, plane-wave factors and integrals. **Feynman diagrams** keep track of these terms, and the **Feynman rules** give the value of each piece so that you do not repeat the contraction counting every time.

### From contractions to diagrams

Take $\mathcal L_{\rm int}=-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$. Each factor $-i\frac{\lambda}{4!}\int d^4x\,\phi^4(x)$ is a **vertex**, drawn as a point with four legs, one per field. Each contraction $D_F(x-y)$ is an **internal line** joining two legs. Each leg contracted with an external particle is an **external line**. A diagram is *connected* if one can travel between any two vertices along lines. Scattering amplitudes come from connected diagrams; disconnected pieces describe particles that miss each other, and vacuum bubbles cancel against the normalization of the vacuum.

### Momentum-space rules

Insert $D_F(x-y)=\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{i}{k^2-m^2+i\epsilon}e^{-ik\cdot(x-y)}$ and integrate over $x$ at each vertex. Each integral gives $(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}$, which is momentum conservation at that vertex, and one overall $(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}$ is left. Define the amplitude by
$$\langle f|S-1|i\rangle=(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}\Big(\sum p_f-\sum p_i\Big)\;i\mathcal M.$$
For $\mathcal L_{\rm int}=-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4-\frac{g}{3!}\phi^3$ the rules for $i\mathcal M$ are:

| Element | Factor |
|---|---|
| Internal line with momentum $k$ | $\dfrac{i}{k^2-m^2+i\epsilon}$ |
| $\phi^4$ vertex | $-i\lambda$ |
| $\phi^3$ vertex | $-ig$ |
| External line (relativistic normalization) | $1$ |
| Each vertex | momentum conserved |
| Each undetermined loop momentum | $\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}$ |
| Overall | divide by symmetry factor $S$ |

$i\mathcal M$ is the sum of all connected, amputated diagrams (no propagators on external legs).

### Examples

At tree level in $\phi^4$ theory, $i\mathcal M=-i\lambda$. In $\phi^3$ theory the tree-level $2\to2$ amplitude is the sum of three exchange diagrams. With $s=(p_1+p_2)^2$, $t=(p_1-p_1')^2$, $u=(p_1-p_2')^2$ and $s+t+u=4m^2$,
$$i\mathcal M=(-ig)^2\Big[\frac{i}{s-m^2}+\frac{i}{t-m^2}+\frac{i}{u-m^2}\Big],\qquad\mathcal M=-g^2\Big[\frac{1}{s-m^2}+\frac{1}{t-m^2}+\frac{1}{u-m^2}\Big].$$
At order $\lambda^2$ in $\phi^4$ theory there are three one-loop bubbles. The $s$-channel one is
$$\frac{(-i\lambda)^2}{2}\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{i}{k^2-m^2+i\epsilon}\,\frac{i}{(P-k)^2-m^2+i\epsilon},\qquad P=p_1+p_2,$$
and the $t$ and $u$ channels have $P=p_1-p_1'$ and $P=p_1-p_2'$.

### Symmetry factors

The weight $\frac{1}{n!}\big(\frac{1}{4!}\big)^n\times(\text{number of Wick contractions})$ equals $1/S$. Equivalently $S$ is the number of symmetry operations that leave the diagram unchanged with the external legs held fixed: exchanging identical internal lines between the same two vertices, flipping a line that starts and ends at the same vertex, and exchanging vertices when that maps the diagram to itself. In $\phi^4$ theory: the figure-eight vacuum bubble has $S=8$; the $s$-channel bubble has $S=2$; the sunset self-energy has $S=3!=6$; a tadpole loop on a line has $S=2$. When in doubt, count the contractions as in week 9.

### Translating an interaction into diagrams

1. **Read off the vertices.** A term $-\frac{c}{n_1!\,n_2!}\phi_1^{n_1}\phi_2^{n_2}$ gives a vertex $-ic$ with $n_1$ legs of type 1 and $n_2$ of type 2.
2. **Fix the order.** With $V$ vertices of valence $n$ and $E$ external legs, $nV=2I+E$, where $I$ is the number of internal lines. The number of loops is $L=I-V+1$.
3. **Enumerate topologies.** Connect the legs in all inequivalent ways, discarding disconnected diagrams and corrections on external legs.
4. **Write each diagram with the rules** and compute $S$.

For trees in $\phi^3$ theory with $n$ external legs there are $n-2$ vertices, $n-3$ propagators and $(2n-5)!!$ distinct diagrams: $3$ for $n=4$ (the $s$, $t$, $u$ channels) and $15$ for $n=5$.

@example
title: Diagrams for $\phi\phi\to\phi\phi$ in $\phi^4$ theory
problem: List the connected, amputated diagrams for $\phi\phi\to\phi\phi$ in $\mathcal L_{\rm int}=-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$ up to order $\lambda^2$, and give the value of each.
step: Each vertex has four legs, so $4V=2I+4$, which gives $I=2V-2$ and $L=I-V+1=V-1$.
step: Order $\lambda$ ($V=1$): $I=0$, $L=0$. There is a single contact diagram, $i\mathcal M=-i\lambda$, with $S=1$.
step: Order $\lambda^2$ ($V=2$): $I=2$, $L=1$. Two vertices joined by two lines, each vertex carrying two external legs. The external legs can be split between the vertices in three ways, $\{p_1p_2|p_1'p_2'\}$, $\{p_1p_1'|p_2p_2'\}$, $\{p_1p_2'|p_2p_1'\}$: the $s$, $t$ and $u$ bubbles.
step: Each bubble has $S=2$ because its two internal lines can be exchanged, so each equals $\frac{(-i\lambda)^2}{2}\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{i}{k^2-m^2}\frac{i}{(P-k)^2-m^2}$ with $P^2=s,t,u$.
step: Other order-$\lambda^2$ connected diagrams, such as a tadpole loop attached to an external line, are corrections to external legs. They are removed by amputation and absorbed into the mass and field normalization.

@example
title: A symmetry factor from counting contractions
problem: Find the symmetry factor of the order-$\lambda$ diagram for $\mathbf p\to\mathbf p'$ in which one vertex has a closed loop (the tadpole), by counting Wick contractions of $-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4x\,\langle\mathbf p'|T\{\phi^4(x)\}|\mathbf p\rangle$.
step: There are $4$ choices for the field that absorbs the incoming particle $\mathbf p$.
step: There are $3$ remaining choices for the field that emits $\mathbf p'$.
step: The last two fields contract with each other in $1$ way, giving $D_F(0)$.
step: The count is $4\cdot3\cdot1=12$ and the prefactor has $4!=24$ in the denominator, so the weight is $\frac{12}{24}=\frac12$ and $S=2$. The loop can be flipped.
step: Hence $i\mathcal M=\frac{-i\lambda}{2}\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{i}{k^2-m^2+i\epsilon}$. It diverges and is removed by normal ordering the interaction or by mass renormalization.

@mistakes
- Putting a propagator on each external line. The amplitude is amputated, and external scalar lines contribute $1$.
- Multiplying by the symmetry factor instead of dividing by it.
- Forgetting that vertex factors for $\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$ are $-i\lambda$ (the $4!$ cancels), not $-i\lambda/4!$.
- Counting the same $\phi^3$ exchange diagram twice, or missing the $u$ channel when the external legs are identical.

@quiz num
prompt: How many distinct tree-level diagrams contribute to the amplitude for six distinguishable external legs in $\phi^3$ theory?
answer: 105
check: np.prod([2*k - 5 for k in range(4, 7)])
hint: Such a tree has $4$ vertices and $3$ internal lines. Build it up leg by leg instead of drawing every diagram.
hint: A tree with $n$ legs arises from one with $n-1$ legs by attaching the new leg to any of its $2n-5$ lines (including external ones).
solution: The number of labeled trivalent trees with $n$ external legs is $(2n-5)!!$. For $n=4$ this is $3$ ($s$, $t$, $u$). A four-leg tree has $5$ lines, so there are $3\cdot5=15$ five-leg trees. A five-leg tree has $5$ external and $2$ internal lines, $7$ in all, and attaching the sixth leg to any one of them gives a distinct six-leg tree. So there are $15\cdot7=105=7!!$ diagrams.

@quiz ms
prompt: Which statements about the momentum-space Feynman rules for $\mathcal L_{\rm int}=-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$ are correct?
choice*: Each vertex contributes $-i\lambda$, because the $4!$ ways of attaching the lines cancel the $\frac{1}{4!}$.
choice*: Each internal line contributes $\frac{i}{k^2-m^2+i\epsilon}$, and the momentum $k$ is integrated over only if it is not fixed by momentum conservation.
choice: Each external line contributes a propagator $\frac{i}{p^2-m^2}$.
choice: The symmetry factor multiplies the value of the diagram.
hint: Recall that $i\mathcal M$ is built from amputated diagrams.
hint: Symmetry factors correct for overcounting.
solution: The vertex factor $-i\lambda$ and the internal-line factor $\frac{i}{k^2-m^2+i\epsilon}$, integrated over only when $k$ is undetermined, are the vertex and propagator rules. The claim that external lines carry propagators is false because amputated external legs carry the factor $1$. The claim that the symmetry factor multiplies the diagram is false: you divide by $S$.
