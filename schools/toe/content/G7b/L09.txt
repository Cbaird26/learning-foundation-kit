@article
Evaluating $\langle f|T\{\mathcal H_I(x_1)\cdots\mathcal H_I(x_n)\}|i\rangle$ by inserting mode expansions directly would be hopeless. **Wick's theorem** reduces any time-ordered product of free fields to propagators and normal-ordered products.

### Normal ordering and contractions

Split the free field as $\phi=\phi^++\phi^-$ with
$$\phi^+(x)=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{a_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}e^{-ip\cdot x},\qquad\phi^-(x)=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}e^{ip\cdot x}.$$
The **normal-ordered** product ${:}\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n){:}$ places every $\phi^-$ to the left of every $\phi^+$, so $\langle0|{:}\cdots{:}|0\rangle=0$. The **contraction** of two fields is the difference between the time-ordered and the normal-ordered product:
$$\overline{\phi(x)\phi(y)}\equiv T\phi(x)\phi(y)-{:}\phi(x)\phi(y){:}=D_F(x-y).$$
To see this, note that for $x^0>y^0$ the difference is $\phi^+(x)\phi^-(y)-\phi^-(y)\phi^+(x)=[\phi^+(x),\phi^-(y)]=D(x-y)$, and for $x^0<y^0$ it is $D(y-x)$. Both equal $D_F(x-y)$. The contraction is a c-number.

### Wick's theorem

$$T[\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n)]={:}\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n){:}+\big(\text{all single contractions}\big)+\big(\text{all double contractions}\big)+\cdots$$
In each term the contracted pairs are replaced by $D_F$ and the uncontracted fields remain inside a normal-ordered product. Writing $D_{ij}\equiv D_F(x_i-x_j)$:
$$T[\phi_1\phi_2]={:}\phi_1\phi_2{:}+D_{12},$$
$$T[\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3\phi_4]={:}\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3\phi_4{:}+D_{12}{:}\phi_3\phi_4{:}+(5\ \text{more single contractions})+D_{12}D_{34}+D_{13}D_{24}+D_{14}D_{23}.$$

### Vacuum expectation values

Only completely contracted terms survive in $\langle0|\cdots|0\rangle$, so
$$\langle0|T[\phi_1\cdots\phi_{2n}]|0\rangle=\sum_{\text{pairings}}\ \prod_{\text{pairs}}D_{ij},$$
and the expectation value of an odd number of fields vanishes. The number of pairings of $2n$ fields is $(2n-1)!!=\dfrac{(2n)!}{2^n\,n!}$, which is $1,3,15,105$ for $2n=2,4,6,8$. When the fields sit at a few spacetime points, group the pairings by how many propagators join each pair of points.

### Contractions with external states

A field that is not contracted with another field must absorb or create an external particle. With relativistic normalization,
$$\phi^+(x)\,|\mathbf p\rangle=e^{-ip\cdot x}\,|0\rangle,\qquad\langle\mathbf p|\,\phi^-(x)=\langle0|\,e^{ip\cdot x}.$$
(The first follows from $|\mathbf p\rangle=\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$ and $[a_{\mathbf q},a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf q-\mathbf p)$.) An incoming particle attached at $x$ contributes $e^{-ip\cdot x}$ and an outgoing one $e^{+ip'\cdot x}$.

### Self-contractions

Fields at the same point contract to $D_F(0)=\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{i}{k^2-m^2+i\epsilon}$, which diverges. Writing the interaction as ${:}\phi^4{:}$ removes such self-contractions; this amounts to a redefinition of the mass, treated properly in later courses.

### Recipe

To evaluate $\langle f|T\{\cdots\}|i\rangle$:

1. Apply Wick's theorem.
2. Keep terms in which the uncontracted fields are exactly those that annihilate or create the external particles.
3. Replace each contraction by $D_F$ and each external particle by its plane-wave factor.
4. Count equivalent terms by choosing which fields at each vertex attach where.
5. Multiply by the prefactor $\frac{1}{n!}\big(\frac{-i\lambda}{4!}\big)^n$. The ratio (number of equivalent terms)/(the factorials in the prefactor) is $1/S$, with $S$ the symmetry factor of the resulting diagram (week 10).

@example
title: A three-point function from Wick's theorem
problem: Evaluate $\langle0|\,T\{\phi(x)\,\phi(y)\,{:}\phi^2(z){:}\}\,|0\rangle$.
step: Wick's theorem applies to the four fields $\phi(x)$, $\phi(y)$ and the two copies of $\phi(z)$. Only complete pairings survive in the vacuum expectation value.
step: The normal ordering of ${:}\phi^2(z){:}$ forbids contracting the two fields at $z$ with each other.
step: That leaves pairings in which $\phi(x)$ contracts with one of the two $\phi(z)$ and $\phi(y)$ with the other. There are $2$ such pairings. The pairing $\phi(x)\phi(y)$ with the two fields at $z$ contracted together is not allowed.
step: Each allowed pairing equals $D_F(x-z)D_F(y-z)$, so the result is $2D_F(x-z)\,D_F(y-z)$. The factor $2$ is why a vertex $\frac{g}{2}\phi^2\chi$ carries no extra factor in the Feynman rules.

@example
title: Counting the contractions of an $s$-channel bubble
problem: In $\phi^4$ theory, find the weight of the contractions in $\frac{1}{2!}\big(\frac{-i\lambda}{4!}\big)^2\int d^4x\,d^4y\,\langle\mathbf p_1'\mathbf p_2'|T\{\phi^4(x)\phi^4(y)\}|\mathbf p_1\mathbf p_2\rangle$ in which $\mathbf p_1,\mathbf p_2$ attach to one vertex, $\mathbf p_1',\mathbf p_2'$ to the other, and the two remaining fields at each vertex are contracted with those of the other vertex.
step: Choose which vertex absorbs the two incoming particles: $2$ ways. This cancels the $\frac{1}{2!}$.
step: At that vertex choose which two of its four fields absorb $\mathbf p_1$ and $\mathbf p_2$: $4\cdot3=12$ ways.
step: At the other vertex choose which two fields emit $\mathbf p_1'$ and $\mathbf p_2'$: $4\cdot3=12$ ways.
step: Contract the two remaining fields at $x$ with the two remaining at $y$: $2$ ways.
step: The total count is $2\cdot12\cdot12\cdot2=576$, and the prefactor has $2!\,(4!)^2=1152$ in the denominator, so the weight is $\frac{576}{1152}=\frac12$.
step: This $\frac12$ is the symmetry factor of the $s$-channel bubble, since its two internal lines can be exchanged. The $t$ and $u$ channels have the same weight.

@mistakes
- Contracting two fields inside a normal-ordered product or, for a normal-ordered vertex, two fields at the same vertex. Such contractions are zero.
- Using the Wightman function $D(x-y)$ instead of $D_F(x-y)$ as the contraction in a time-ordered product. They agree only for $x^0>y^0$.
- Miscounting pairings: the number of pairings of $2n$ fields is $(2n)!/(2^nn!)$, not $(2n)!/2^n$.
- Forgetting that the vacuum expectation value of an odd number of free fields vanishes, or keeping partially contracted terms in a vacuum expectation value.

@quiz ms
prompt: Which statements about Wick's theorem for free real scalar fields are correct?
choice*: $\langle0|{:}\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n){:}|0\rangle=0$ for $n\ge1$.
choice*: $\langle0|T[\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3\phi_4]|0\rangle=D_{12}D_{34}+D_{13}D_{24}+D_{14}D_{23}$.
choice: $\langle0|T[\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3]|0\rangle\neq0$ because there are three ways to contract two of the fields.
choice: The contraction $\overline{\phi(x)\phi(y)}$ is an operator that depends on the state it acts on.
hint: Which terms of the Wick expansion survive when sandwiched between vacuum states?
hint: A complete pairing needs an even number of fields.
solution: The vanishing of $\langle0|{:}\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n){:}|0\rangle$ holds because every normal-ordered term has an annihilation operator on the right. The four-field formula lists the three complete pairings of four fields. The claim that $\langle0|T[\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3]|0\rangle$ is nonzero is false: with three fields one is always left uncontracted, and the remaining normal-ordered field has zero vacuum expectation value. The claim that the contraction is a state-dependent operator is false: the contraction is the c-number $D_F(x-y)$.
