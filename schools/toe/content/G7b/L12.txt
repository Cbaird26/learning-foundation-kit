@article
An amplitude is not yet a measurable number. Experiments measure decay rates and cross sections, which require squaring $\mathcal M$ and integrating over the final-state **phase space**.

### From $|\mathcal M|^2$ to rates

In a box of volume $V$ over a long time $T$, the square of the momentum-conserving delta function becomes $VT$, and each particle contributes a state normalization $2EV$. Dividing by $T$ and by the incident flux gives the standard results. With the **Lorentz-invariant phase space**
$$d\Pi_n=\prod_{f=1}^n\frac{d^3p_f}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_f}\;(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}\Big(P-\sum_fp_f\Big),$$
the decay rate of a particle of mass $M$ at rest and the cross section for $2\to n$ are
$$\Gamma=\frac{1}{2M}\int d\Pi_n\,|\mathcal M|^2,\qquad\sigma=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{(p_1\cdot p_2)^2-m_1^2m_2^2}}\int d\Pi_n\,|\mathcal M|^2.$$
For particles with spin, average $|\mathcal M|^2$ over initial spins and sum over final spins. If the final state contains $n_j$ identical particles of type $j$, divide by $\prod_jn_j!$, because integrating over all momenta counts each physical final state $n_j!$ times. In the CM frame the flux factor is $4|\mathbf p_i|\sqrt s$.

### Two-body phase space

In the CM frame with total energy $\sqrt s$ and final masses $m_a,m_b$, the delta functions leave only an angular integral:
$$\int d\Pi_2=\int\frac{d\Omega}{16\pi^2}\,\frac{|\mathbf p_f|}{\sqrt s},\qquad|\mathbf p_f|=\frac{\sqrt{\big[s-(m_a+m_b)^2\big]\big[s-(m_a-m_b)^2\big]}}{2\sqrt s}.$$
Integrating over the full solid angle gives $\frac{|\mathbf p_f|}{4\pi\sqrt s}$; for equal masses this is $\beta/8\pi$ with $\beta=\sqrt{1-4m^2/s}$. Two consequences follow:
$$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\bigg|_{\rm CM}=\frac{1}{64\pi^2s}\,\frac{|\mathbf p_f|}{|\mathbf p_i|}\,|\mathcal M|^2,\qquad\Gamma_{1\to2}=\frac{|\mathbf p_f|}{8\pi M^2}\,|\mathcal M|^2.$$
The second form holds when $|\mathcal M|^2$ is constant over the angles, which is automatic for a decay of a spin-zero particle. Multiply by $\frac12$ if the two final particles are identical.

### Worked structure: the $\phi^4$ cross section

For $\phi\phi\to\phi\phi$ in $\phi^4$ theory, $\mathcal M=-\lambda$ and $|\mathbf p_f|=|\mathbf p_i|$, so $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{\lambda^2}{64\pi^2s}$ is isotropic. Because the final particles are identical,
$$\sigma=\frac12\cdot4\pi\cdot\frac{\lambda^2}{64\pi^2s}=\frac{\lambda^2}{32\pi\,s}.$$
It falls like $1/s$, as dimensional analysis requires for a dimensionless coupling.

### Worked structure: the decay $\phi\to\chi\chi$

For $\mathcal L_{\rm int}=-\frac g2\phi\chi^2$ the vertex is $-ig$, because the $2!$ cancels the $\frac12$, so $\mathcal M=-g$ with $[g]=1$. The final particles are identical and $|\mathbf p_f|=\frac M2\sqrt{1-4m_\chi^2/M^2}$, so
$$\Gamma=\frac12\cdot\frac{|\mathbf p_f|}{8\pi M^2}\,g^2=\frac{g^2}{32\pi M}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_\chi^2}{M^2}}.$$
The rate vanishes at the threshold $M=2m_\chi$ and is zero below it, as it must be. For decays to fermions, insert the spin-summed $\sum|\mathcal M|^2$ of week 11 into the same two-body formula, with no identical-particle factor if the particles are distinct.

### Units and checks

In natural units $[\sigma]=[\text{mass}]^{-2}$ and $[\Gamma]=[\text{mass}]$. To convert, use $(\hbar c)^2=0.3894\ \text{GeV}^2\,\text{mb}$, so $1\ \text{GeV}^{-2}=0.3894\ \text{mb}$ and $1\ \text{mb}=10^6\ \text{nb}$. A width in GeV gives a mean lifetime $\tau=\hbar/\Gamma$ with $\hbar=6.582\times10^{-25}\ \text{GeV s}$. Before trusting a result, check:

1. Dimension: $[\sigma]=-2$, $[\Gamma]=+1$.
2. Positivity: $\sigma\ge0$ and $\Gamma\ge0$.
3. Thresholds: no decay below $M=\sum m_f$, and $\sigma$ vanishes below the threshold $s=(\sum m_f)^2$.
4. High-energy behaviour: for dimensionless couplings $\sigma\sim1/s$.
5. Identical-particle and spin-averaging factors applied exactly once.

@example
title: Total cross section in $\phi^4$ theory
problem: Compute $\sigma(\phi\phi\to\phi\phi)$ at leading order for $\lambda=2$ and $\sqrt s=5\ \text{GeV}$, and check its dimension.
step: At tree level $\mathcal M=-\lambda$, and the scattering is elastic, so $|\mathbf p_f|=|\mathbf p_i|$.
step: Then $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{\lambda^2}{64\pi^2s}$, independent of angle.
step: Integrate over $4\pi$ to get $\frac{\lambda^2}{16\pi s}$ and multiply by $\frac12$ for the two identical final particles: $\sigma=\frac{\lambda^2}{32\pi s}$.
step: Numerically, $\sigma=\frac{4}{32\pi\cdot25}=\frac{1}{200\pi}\approx1.59\times10^{-3}\ \text{GeV}^{-2}$.
step: Dimension: $\lambda$ is dimensionless and $[s]=2$, so $[\sigma]=-2$, an area, as required.

@example
title: A decay width with a dimension check
problem: Compute $\Gamma(\phi\to\chi\chi)$ for $\mathcal L_{\rm int}=-\frac g2\phi\chi^2$ with $g=1\ \text{GeV}$, $M=8\ \text{GeV}$ and $m_\chi=2\ \text{GeV}$.
step: The vertex is $-ig$, so $\mathcal M=-g$. Dimension check: $[g]=1=4-3$, as $[\mathcal M]=4-n$ with $n=3$ requires.
step: The final momentum is $|\mathbf p_f|=\sqrt{M^2/4-m_\chi^2}=\sqrt{16-4}=\sqrt{12}\approx3.464\ \text{GeV}$.
step: The final particles are identical, so $\Gamma=\frac12\cdot\frac{|\mathbf p_f|}{8\pi M^2}g^2=\frac{3.464}{2\cdot8\pi\cdot64}\ \text{GeV}$.
step: This is $\Gamma\approx1.08\times10^{-3}\ \text{GeV}$. The same value follows from $\frac{g^2}{32\pi M}\sqrt{1-4m_\chi^2/M^2}$.
step: Dimension: $g^2\,|\mathbf p_f|/M^2$ has dimension $2+1-2=1$, a mass, as a rate should. The lifetime is $\tau=\hbar/\Gamma\approx6.1\times10^{-22}\ \text{s}$.

@mistakes
- Forgetting the identical-particle factor $\frac12$ for identical final particles, or applying it when the final particles are distinct.
- Using the elastic formula $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{|\mathcal M|^2}{64\pi^2s}$ for inelastic processes without the ratio $|\mathbf p_f|/|\mathbf p_i|$.
- Using the wrong flux factor, or omitting the $\frac{1}{2M}$ in the decay formula.
- Leaving a cross section in GeV$^{-2}$ when a measurable unit (mb, nb) is requested, or failing to notice that $\sigma$ must have dimension $[\text{mass}]^{-2}$.
