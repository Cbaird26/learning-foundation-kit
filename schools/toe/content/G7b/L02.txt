@article
A **continuous symmetry** is a family of field transformations that leaves the equations of motion unchanged. **Noether's theorem** says that each one gives a conserved current. After quantization these currents become the operators for charge, energy and momentum, which are the labels you will attach to particles.

### The theorem

Consider an infinitesimal change $\phi\to\phi+\delta\phi$ with $\delta\phi=\epsilon\,\Delta\phi$, where $\epsilon$ is a small constant. It is a **symmetry** if the Lagrangian density changes at most by a total derivative:
$$\delta\mathcal L=\epsilon\,\partial_\mu K^\mu.$$
(For internal symmetries usually $K^\mu=0$.) Now compute $\delta\mathcal L$ from its dependence on $\phi$ and $\partial_\mu\phi$ and regroup:
$$\delta\mathcal L=\left[\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi}-\partial_\mu\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\right]\delta\phi+\partial_\mu\!\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\,\delta\phi\right).$$
The bracket vanishes on solutions of the Euler–Lagrange equation ("on-shell"). Equating the two expressions for $\delta\mathcal L$ and removing $\epsilon$:
$$\partial_\mu j^\mu=0,\qquad j^\mu=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\,\Delta\phi-K^\mu,$$
summed over all fields. The charge $Q=\int d^3x\,j^0$ is constant: $\dot Q=-\int d^3x\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf j=0$ if $\mathbf j$ falls off fast enough at infinity. The overall sign and normalization of $j^\mu$ are conventions; conservation is not.

### The U(1) current

The complex scalar $\mathcal L=\partial_\mu\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi-m^2\phi^*\phi$ is invariant under $\phi\to e^{-i\alpha}\phi$, $\phi^*\to e^{+i\alpha}\phi^*$. Infinitesimally $\Delta\phi=-i\phi$, $\Delta\phi^*=+i\phi^*$ and $K^\mu=0$, and the theorem gives
$$j^\mu=i\left(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi-\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi^*\right).$$
We chose the overall sign so that a positive-frequency wave $\phi=Ne^{-ip\cdot x}$, for which $\partial^\mu\phi=-ip^\mu\phi$, has
$$j^\mu=2p^\mu|N|^2,$$
a positive charge density $2E|N|^2$. In a box of volume $V$ the charge is $Q=2E|N|^2V$, so $Q=1$ requires $N=1/\sqrt{2EV}$. That is the origin of the factor $1/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$ you will meet in the mode expansion.

For a real field the same expression vanishes identically: a real scalar has no U(1) symmetry and carries no such charge.

### Translations and the energy–momentum tensor

A translation $x^\mu\to x^\mu-a^\mu$ shifts the field by $\delta\phi=-a^\nu\partial_\nu\phi$, and $\mathcal L$ changes by the total derivative $\delta\mathcal L=-a^\nu\partial_\nu\mathcal L=\partial_\mu(-a^\nu\delta^\mu_\nu\mathcal L)$, so now $K^\mu\neq0$. There is one conserved current for each direction $\nu$, collected in the canonical **energy–momentum tensor**
$$T^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\,\partial^\nu\phi-\eta^{\mu\nu}\mathcal L,\qquad \partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0.$$
The term $-\eta^{\mu\nu}\mathcal L$ is exactly the contribution of $K^\mu$. For the real scalar field this is $T^{\mu\nu}=\partial^\mu\phi\,\partial^\nu\phi-\eta^{\mu\nu}\mathcal L$, with components
$$T^{00}=\dot\phi^2-\mathcal L=\mathcal H,\qquad T^{0i}=\dot\phi\,\partial^i\phi=-\dot\phi\,\partial_i\phi.$$
$T^{00}$ is the energy density and $T^{0i}$ the momentum density. The conserved four-momentum is
$$P^\nu=\int d^3x\,T^{0\nu},\qquad H=P^0=\int d^3x\,\mathcal H,\qquad \mathbf P=-\int d^3x\,\pi\,\nabla\phi.$$
Note the minus sign in $\mathbf P$: it comes from raising the spatial index with the metric $(+,-,-,-)$. In week 3 you will check that these operators generate time and space translations.

The canonical tensor need not be symmetric for fields with spin; one can add a divergence-free improvement term that symmetrizes it without changing $P^\nu$. We do not need this for scalars.

### Strategy for a Noether problem

1. Find the infinitesimal $\Delta\phi$ for every field.
2. Compute $\delta\mathcal L$ and check that it is $\epsilon\,\partial_\mu K^\mu$.
3. Form $j^\mu$ and verify $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$ using the field equations. If the divergence does not vanish on-shell, a sign or a missing field is the usual culprit.

@example
title: Energy and momentum densities of a Klein–Gordon wave
problem: In one space dimension, $\phi=A\cos(\omega t-kx)$ solves $(\Box+m^2)\phi=0$. Find the period averages $\langle T^{00}\rangle$ and $\langle T^{01}\rangle$ and show that $\langle T^{01}\rangle/\langle T^{00}\rangle=k/\omega$.
step: The dispersion relation is $\omega^2=k^2+m^2$. With $\theta=\omega t-kx$: $\dot\phi=-A\omega\sin\theta$, $\phi'=\partial_x\phi=Ak\sin\theta$.
step: $T^{00}=\mathcal H=\frac12\dot\phi^2+\frac12\phi'^2+\frac12m^2\phi^2=\frac12A^2\left[(\omega^2+k^2)\sin^2\theta+m^2\cos^2\theta\right]$.
step: Use $\langle\sin^2\theta\rangle=\langle\cos^2\theta\rangle=\frac12$: $\langle T^{00}\rangle=\frac14A^2(\omega^2+k^2+m^2)=\frac12A^2\omega^2$, since $k^2+m^2=\omega^2$.
step: $T^{01}=-\dot\phi\,\partial_x\phi=A^2\omega k\sin^2\theta$, so $\langle T^{01}\rangle=\frac12A^2\omega k$.
step: The ratio is $k/\omega=|\mathbf p|/E$, the velocity of a particle with energy $\omega$ and momentum $k$, as it should be.

@example
title: On-shell conservation of the U(1) current
problem: Show that $j^\mu=i(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi-\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi^*)$ satisfies $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$ when $\phi$ obeys the Klein–Gordon equation.
step: Differentiate with the product rule: $\partial_\mu j^\mu=i\left(\partial_\mu\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi+\phi^*\Box\phi-\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi^*-\phi\,\Box\phi^*\right)$.
step: The first and third terms are equal and cancel, leaving $\partial_\mu j^\mu=i(\phi^*\Box\phi-\phi\,\Box\phi^*)$.
step: Use $\Box\phi=-m^2\phi$ and, from the complex conjugate equation, $\Box\phi^*=-m^2\phi^*$.
step: Then $\partial_\mu j^\mu=i(-m^2\phi^*\phi+m^2\phi\,\phi^*)=0$. The cancellation used the field equations: off-shell the divergence is not zero.

@mistakes
- Treating $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$ as an identity. It holds only on-shell, after using the Euler–Lagrange equations.
- Forgetting the term $K^\mu$ for spacetime symmetries; the $-\eta^{\mu\nu}\mathcal L$ in $T^{\mu\nu}$ is exactly that term.
- Sign slips with indices: $T^{0i}=-\dot\phi\,\partial_i\phi$ because $\partial^i=-\partial_i$, so $\mathbf P=-\int\pi\nabla\phi$.
- Treating the complex field as one real variable. It has two real components, and U(1) rotates one into the other.

@practice self
prompt: Consider $\mathcal L=\partial_\mu\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi-m^2\phi^*\phi-\frac{\lambda}{4}(\phi^*\phi)^2$. Derive the field equation for $\phi$, find the Noether current of the symmetry $\phi\to e^{-i\alpha}\phi$, and prove that it is conserved on-shell.
hint: Vary $\phi^*$ with $\phi$ held fixed. The potential term contributes $-\frac{\lambda}{4}\cdot2(\phi^*\phi)\phi$ to $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\phi^*$.
hint: Use the infinitesimal transformation $\Delta\phi=-i\phi$, $\Delta\phi^*=+i\phi^*$ in $j^\mu=\sum\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\varphi)}\Delta\varphi-K^\mu$ and check whether $\delta\mathcal L=0$.
hint: To show $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$, compute $i(\phi^*\Box\phi-\phi\,\Box\phi^*)$ and substitute both interacting field equations.
solution: Varying $\phi^*$: $\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^*)=\partial^\mu\phi$ and $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\phi^*=-m^2\phi-\frac{\lambda}{2}(\phi^*\phi)\phi$, so $\Box\phi+m^2\phi+\frac{\lambda}{2}(\phi^*\phi)\phi=0$ and its conjugate $\Box\phi^*+m^2\phi^*+\frac{\lambda}{2}(\phi^*\phi)\phi^*=0$. The transformation has $\Delta\phi=-i\phi$, $\Delta\phi^*=i\phi^*$ and $\delta\mathcal L=0$ because $\phi^*\phi$ is invariant, so $K^\mu=0$ and $j^\mu=\partial^\mu\phi^*(-i\phi)+\partial^\mu\phi\,(i\phi^*)=i(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi-\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi^*)$. The potential contains no derivatives and so does not change $j^\mu$. Then $\partial_\mu j^\mu=i(\phi^*\Box\phi-\phi\,\Box\phi^*)=i\left[\phi^*(-m^2\phi-\frac{\lambda}{2}|\phi|^2\phi)-\phi(-m^2\phi^*-\frac{\lambda}{2}|\phi|^2\phi^*)\right]=0$, because every term appears with its mirror image.
rubric: The field equation $\Box\phi+m^2\phi+\frac{\lambda}{2}(\phi^*\phi)\phi=0$ is obtained with the correct factor $\lambda/2$.
rubric: The infinitesimal transformation, $K^\mu=0$ and the resulting current are stated correctly.
rubric: The divergence is evaluated using the interacting field equations, not the free ones.
rubric: The interaction terms are shown to cancel in $\partial_\mu j^\mu$, giving zero.
