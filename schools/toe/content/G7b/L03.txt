@article
The classical Klein–Gordon field is an infinite collection of oscillators, one for each momentum. **Canonical quantization** treats it exactly like harmonic oscillators, and particles emerge as the oscillator excitations.

### Canonical commutation relations

Promote $\phi(\mathbf x)$ and its conjugate momentum $\pi(\mathbf x)=\dot\phi(\mathbf x)$ to operators obeying
$$[\phi(\mathbf x,t),\pi(\mathbf y,t)]=i\,\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x-\mathbf y),\qquad[\phi,\phi]=[\pi,\pi]=0$$
at equal times. Time dependence is in the Heisenberg picture: $\phi(x)=e^{iHt}\phi(\mathbf x,0)e^{-iHt}$.

### Mode expansion

The general Hermitian solution of the Klein–Gordon equation is
$$\phi(x)=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Big(a_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x}+a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\Big),\qquad p^0=E_{\mathbf p}=\sqrt{|\mathbf p|^2+m^2},$$
$$\pi(x)=\dot\phi(x)=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}(-i)\sqrt{\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}}\Big(a_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x}-a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\Big).$$
At $t=0$ these invert to $a_{\mathbf p}=\int d^3x\,e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}\big(\sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}/2}\,\phi+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\,\pi\big)$, and the field commutators become equivalent to
$$[a_{\mathbf p},a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}]=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q),\qquad[a_{\mathbf p},a_{\mathbf q}]=0=[a^\dagger_{\mathbf p},a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}].$$
Each momentum is an independent oscillator: $a_{\mathbf p}$ lowers and $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ raises its occupation number.

### Hamiltonian and normal ordering

Insert the expansions into $H=\int d^3x\,\mathcal H$. The $aa$ and $a^\dagger a^\dagger$ terms cancel because $E_{\mathbf p}^2=|\mathbf p|^2+m^2$, leaving
$$H=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,E_{\mathbf p}\Big(a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p}+\tfrac12[a_{\mathbf p},a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]\Big),\qquad[a_{\mathbf p},a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(0).$$
The second term is the zero-point energy $\sum\frac12\omega$ of infinitely many oscillators. In a box of volume $V$, $(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(0)=V$, so the energy density $\frac12\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}$ diverges as the fourth power of a momentum cutoff. Only energy differences matter here, so we use the **normal-ordered** operator, with every $a^\dagger$ to the left of every $a$:
$$:\!H\!:\;=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p},\qquad\mathbf P=-\int d^3x\,\pi\nabla\phi=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,\mathbf p\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p}.$$
The zero-point term in $\mathbf P$ cancels by itself, since its integrand is odd in $\mathbf p$.

### States

The **vacuum** satisfies $a_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle=0$ and $\langle0|0\rangle=1$. From
$$[:\!H\!:,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]=E_{\mathbf p}\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p},\qquad[\mathbf P,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]=\mathbf p\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$$
the state $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$ has energy $E_{\mathbf p}$ and momentum $\mathbf p$: one particle of mass $m$. The convention for **relativistically normalized** states is
$$|\mathbf p\rangle=\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\;a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle,\qquad\langle\mathbf p|\mathbf q\rangle=2E_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q).$$
The factor $2E_{\mathbf p}\,\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$ and the measure $\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_{\mathbf p}}$ are Lorentz invariant, because $\frac{d^3p}{2E_{\mathbf p}}=d^4p\,\delta(p^2-m^2)\theta(p^0)$. Hence boosts act simply, $U(\Lambda)|\mathbf p\rangle=|\Lambda\mathbf p\rangle$. The one-particle completeness relation is
$$\mathbb 1_{1\text{-particle}}=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_{\mathbf p}}\,|\mathbf p\rangle\langle\mathbf p|.$$
Since the $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ commute, multiparticle states are symmetric under exchange: the particles are bosons. Finally $\langle0|\phi(x)|\mathbf p\rangle=e^{-ip\cdot x}$, which fixes the meaning of the prefactor $1/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$: the field annihilates a particle with unit-amplitude plane-wave wavefunction.

Note that $\langle\mathbf p|\mathbf p\rangle=2E_{\mathbf p}V$ in a box, not $1$. Whenever you meet an unfamiliar convention, rederive the commutator and the state norm before trusting a formula.

@example
title: The energy of a one-particle state
problem: Using the normal-ordered Hamiltonian $:\!H\!:\,=\int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf q}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}a_{\mathbf q}$, show that $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$ is an energy eigenstate with eigenvalue $E_{\mathbf p}$.
step: Use $[AB,C]=A[B,C]+[A,C]B$ with $A=a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}$, $B=a_{\mathbf q}$, $C=a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$.
step: $[a_{\mathbf q},a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf q-\mathbf p)$ and $[a^\dagger_{\mathbf q},a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]=0$, so $[a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}a_{\mathbf q},a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf q-\mathbf p)\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}$.
step: Integrate over $\mathbf q$ with weight $E_{\mathbf q}/(2\pi)^3$: $[:\!H\!:,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]=E_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$.
step: Apply to the vacuum: $:\!H\!:a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle=a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}:\!H\!:|0\rangle+E_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle=E_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$, because every term of $:\!H\!:$ has an $a$ on the right and $a|0\rangle=0$.
step: So the state has energy $E_{\mathbf p}=\sqrt{|\mathbf p|^2+m^2}$, the energy of a free relativistic particle.

@example
title: Overlap of two-particle states
problem: Compute $\langle0|a_{\mathbf k}a_{\mathbf l}\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle$ and say what it implies for identical particles.
step: Commute $a_{\mathbf l}$ to the right: $a_{\mathbf l}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}=a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf l}+(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf l-\mathbf p)$.
step: Then $a_{\mathbf l}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf l-\mathbf p)\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle+(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf l-\mathbf q)\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$, using $a_{\mathbf l}|0\rangle=0$ for the leftover term.
step: Apply $\langle0|a_{\mathbf k}$ and use $\langle0|a_{\mathbf k}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf k-\mathbf q)$.
step: The result is $(2\pi)^6\big[\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf l-\mathbf p)\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf k-\mathbf q)+\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf l-\mathbf q)\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf k-\mathbf p)\big]$.
step: It is symmetric under $\mathbf p\leftrightarrow\mathbf q$ because $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}=a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$. The two particles are identical bosons.

@mistakes
- Dropping the $(2\pi)^3$ in $[a_{\mathbf p},a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}]$, or using it together with a mode expansion that has a different measure. The commutator, the expansion and the state normalization must be a matched set.
- Expecting $\langle\mathbf p|\mathbf p\rangle=1$. With relativistic normalization it is $2E_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(0)$, which is $2E_{\mathbf p}V$ in a box.
- Mixing $a_{\mathbf p}$ with $a_{-\mathbf p}$ when relabeling integration variables. The sign matters for $\mathbf P$.
- Treating the zero-point energy as physical here. It is a constant that cancels in energy differences, which is why we normal order, although it matters once gravity is included.

prompt: In this course the field is expanded as $\phi(x)=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\big(a_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x}+a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\big)$. Which commutator then reproduces $[\phi(\mathbf x),\pi(\mathbf y)]=i\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)$?
choice*: $[a_{\mathbf p},a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}]=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$
choice: $[a_{\mathbf p},a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}]=\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$
choice: $[a_{\mathbf p},a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}]=2E_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$
choice: $\{a_{\mathbf p},a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}\}=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$
hint: Compute $[\phi(\mathbf x),\pi(\mathbf y)]$ with the expansions and look at which constants multiply the double integral.
hint: The prefactors $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E}}$ and $\sqrt{E/2}$ combine to $\frac12$, so the $(2\pi)^3$ from the commutator must cancel one of the two $(2\pi)^{-3}$ measure factors to leave $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}e^{i\mathbf p\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)}=\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)$.
solution: The commutator is $\int\frac{d^3p\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^6}\frac{i}{2}\sqrt{E_{\mathbf q}/E_{\mathbf p}}\,[\ldots]$ and only $[a,a^\dagger]$ terms survive. With $[a_{\mathbf p},a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}]=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$ one factor $(2\pi)^{-3}$ remains, giving $\frac{i}{2}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\big(e^{i\mathbf p\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)}+e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)}\big)=i\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)$. The other normalizations give extra factors; an anticommutator would give the wrong statistics and no $\delta$-function.

@practice num
prompt: A boost along $x$ with rapidity $\eta=0.3$ maps $p^x\to p'^x=p^x\cosh\eta+E\sinh\eta$, where $E=\sqrt{(p^x)^2+m^2}$. For $m=1$ and $p^x=0.5$ (other components fixed), compute the Jacobian $\partial p'^x/\partial p^x$ to four decimal places. This equals $E'/E$, which is why $d^3p/E_{\mathbf p}$ is invariant.
answer: 1.1815
check: nderiv(lambda px: px*cosh(0.3) + sqrt(px**2 + 1)*sinh(0.3), 0.5)
tol: 0.001
hint: Differentiate $p'^x=p^x\cosh\eta+\sqrt{(p^x)^2+m^2}\,\sinh\eta$ with respect to $p^x$.
hint: $\partial E/\partial p^x=p^x/E$. Evaluate at $p^x=0.5$ where $E=\sqrt{1.25}$.

@practice self
prompt: Using the mode expansions of $\phi$ and $\pi$ at $t=0$, show that $H=\int d^3x\,\frac12\big(\pi^2+(\nabla\phi)^2+m^2\phi^2\big)$ equals $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}\big(a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p}+\frac12[a_{\mathbf p},a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]\big)$. Then show $[:\!H\!:,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]=E_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ and interpret it.
hint: At $t=0$ write $\phi$ and $\pi$ as single momentum integrals (relabel $\mathbf p\to-\mathbf p$ in the $a^\dagger$ terms) so that $\int d^3x\,e^{i(\mathbf p+\mathbf q)\cdot\mathbf x}=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p+\mathbf q)$ collapses each product.
hint: Compare the coefficient of $E_{\mathbf p}/2$ in the $\pi^2$ term and in the $(\nabla\phi)^2+m^2\phi^2$ term, and watch which products of $a$'s cancel.
hint: For the commutator use $[AB,C]=A[B,C]+[A,C]B$ with $A=a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}$, $B=a_{\mathbf q}$.
solution: Write $\int_p=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}$. At $t=0$, relabeling $\mathbf p\to-\mathbf p$ in the $a^\dagger$ terms, $\phi(\mathbf x)=\int_p\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}(a_{\mathbf p}+a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p})e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}$ and $\pi(\mathbf x)=\int_p(-i)\sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}/2}\,(a_{\mathbf p}-a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p})e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}$. The integral $\int d^3x\,e^{i(\mathbf p+\mathbf q)\cdot\mathbf x}=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p+\mathbf q)$ collapses each quadratic term to one momentum integral: $\int d^3x\,\pi^2=-\int_p\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}(a_{\mathbf p}-a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p})(a_{-\mathbf p}-a^\dagger_{\mathbf p})$ and $\int d^3x\,[(\nabla\phi)^2+m^2\phi^2]=\int_p\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}(a_{\mathbf p}+a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p})(a_{-\mathbf p}+a^\dagger_{\mathbf p})$, using $|\mathbf p|^2+m^2=E_{\mathbf p}^2$. In the sum the $aa$ and $a^\dagger a^\dagger$ terms cancel, leaving $H=\frac12\int_pE_{\mathbf p}(a_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}+a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}a_{-\mathbf p})=\frac12\int_pE_{\mathbf p}(a_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}+a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p})$. Writing $a_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}=a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p}+[a_{\mathbf p},a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]$ gives the claimed form, and normal ordering drops the constant. Finally $[a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}a_{\mathbf q},a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf q-\mathbf p)a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}$, so $[:\!H\!:,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]=E_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$: acting with $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ raises the energy by exactly $E_{\mathbf p}$, so it creates a quantum of energy $E_{\mathbf p}$.
rubric: Both fields are written at $t=0$ with correct prefactors and the spatial integral is used to collapse the double momentum integral.
rubric: The $aa$ and $a^\dagger a^\dagger$ terms are shown to cancel using $E_{\mathbf p}^2=|\mathbf p|^2+m^2$.
rubric: The zero-point term $\frac12 E_{\mathbf p}[a_{\mathbf p},a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]$ is identified and removed by normal ordering.
rubric: The commutator $[:\!H\!:,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]=E_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ is derived and interpreted as creating energy $E_{\mathbf p}$.

@quiz num
prompt: For a massless field the vacuum energy density is $\rho=\frac12\int^{\Lambda}\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}$ with a hard cutoff $|\mathbf p|<\Lambda$. Evaluate $\rho$ for $\Lambda=2$ in natural units.
answer: 1/pi^2
check: quad(lambda p: 0.5*4*pi/(2*pi)**3*p**2*p, 0, 2)
hint: Use spherical coordinates, $d^3p=4\pi p^2dp$, and $E_{\mathbf p}=p$ for a massless particle.
hint: The integral reduces to $\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\int_0^\Lambda p^3\,dp$.
solution: $\rho=\frac12\cdot\frac{4\pi}{(2\pi)^3}\int_0^\Lambda p^3\,dp=\frac{\Lambda^4}{16\pi^2}$. For $\Lambda=2$ this is $1/\pi^2\approx0.101$. It grows as $\Lambda^4$, one reason normal ordering is used.

@quiz ms
prompt: Which of the following are Lorentz invariant under proper orthochronous boosts of on-shell momenta $p^\mu=(E_{\mathbf p},\mathbf p)$?
choice*: The measure $\dfrac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_{\mathbf p}}$
choice*: The combination $2E_{\mathbf p}\,\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$
choice: The measure $d^3p$
choice: The distribution $\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$
hint: $\frac{d^3p}{2E_{\mathbf p}}$ can be written as $d^4p\,\delta(p^2-m^2)\theta(p^0)$.
hint: A Jacobian of $E'/E$ multiplies $d^3p$ under a boost, so any invariant must compensate for it.
solution: $d^3p$ picks up a factor $E'/E$ under a boost, so $d^3p/E_{\mathbf p}$ is invariant. Since $\int d^3p\,\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)f(\mathbf p)=f(\mathbf q)$ for any $f$, the delta function scales inversely with $d^3p$; therefore $E_{\mathbf p}\,\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$ is invariant. Neither $d^3p$ nor $\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$ alone is invariant.
