@article
Free fields do not scatter. To describe particles that collide, decay or exchange forces we add an **interaction** to the Lagrangian, and then need a way to calculate with it. The tool is the interaction picture.

### Interaction Lagrangians

Write $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_0+\mathcal L_{\rm int}$, where $\mathcal L_0$ is the free Klein–Gordon or Dirac Lagrangian. Standard examples are
$$\mathcal L_{\rm int}=-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4,\qquad-\frac{g}{3!}\phi^3,\qquad-g\,\phi\,\bar\psi\psi\quad(\text{Yukawa}).$$
The numerical factors are chosen so that the vertices of week 10 carry no extra combinatorial factor. In $d=4$, $[\phi]=1$ and $[\psi]=\frac32$, so $[\lambda]=0$, $[g_{\phi^3}]=1$ and $[g_{\rm Yukawa}]=4-1-3=0$. A coupling of positive mass dimension is called *super-renormalizable*, zero *renormalizable* (as for $\phi^4$ and Yukawa), and negative *non-renormalizable*. The $\phi^4$ theory has the symmetry $\phi\to-\phi$; the $\phi^3$ theory does not, and its potential is unbounded below, so it is only a perturbative toy model.

### From Lagrangian to Hamiltonian

If $\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ contains no derivatives of the fields, the conjugate momentum is still $\pi=\dot\phi$ and
$$\mathcal H=\mathcal H_0+\mathcal H_{\rm int},\qquad\mathcal H_{\rm int}=-\mathcal L_{\rm int},\qquad H_{\rm int}=\int d^3x\,\mathcal H_{\rm int}.$$
For $\phi^4$, $H_{\rm int}=\int d^3x\,\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$, bounded below for $\lambda>0$. Derivative couplings need extra care that we skip.

### The interaction picture

The full Heisenberg field $\phi_H(t,\mathbf x)=e^{iH(t-t_0)}\phi(t_0,\mathbf x)e^{-iH(t-t_0)}$ obeys a nonlinear equation we cannot solve. The **interaction picture** splits the evolution between operators and states. Choose a reference time $t_0$ at which all pictures coincide, and define
$$\phi_I(t,\mathbf x)=e^{iH_0(t-t_0)}\,\phi(t_0,\mathbf x)\,e^{-iH_0(t-t_0)},\qquad|\psi_I(t)\rangle=e^{iH_0(t-t_0)}e^{-iH(t-t_0)}\,|\psi_I(t_0)\rangle.$$
Operators evolve with $H_0$ only, so $\phi_I$ is a **free field**: it obeys $(\Box+m^2)\phi_I=0$ and has exactly the mode expansion of week 3, with the same $a_{\mathbf p}$ and $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$. States evolve with $H_I(t)=e^{iH_0(t-t_0)}H_{\rm int}\,e^{-iH_0(t-t_0)}$, the interaction Hamiltonian written in terms of $\phi_I$:
$$i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\psi_I(t)\rangle=H_I(t)\,|\psi_I(t)\rangle,\qquad|\psi_I(t)\rangle=U(t,t_0)|\psi_I(t_0)\rangle,\qquad i\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}=H_I(t)\,U,\quad U(t_0,t_0)=1.$$
The operator $U=e^{iH_0(t-t_0)}e^{-iH(t-t_0)}$ is unitary and obeys $U(t,t_1)U(t_1,t_0)=U(t,t_0)$. Note that $H_I(t)$ depends on time even though $H_{\rm int}$ does not, because of the factors $e^{\pm iH_0t}$.

### Asymptotic states and the S-matrix

In a scattering experiment the particles are far apart at $t\to\pm\infty$ and behave as free particles. The interacting vacuum $|\Omega\rangle$ differs from the free vacuum $|0\rangle$, and the interaction shifts masses; a careful treatment (switching the coupling on and off adiabatically, as in the Gell-Mann–Low theorem) shows that for scattering amplitudes one can use free asymptotic states built with the interaction-picture $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ and define
$$S_{fi}=\langle f|\,U(+\infty,-\infty)\,|i\rangle\equiv\langle f|S|i\rangle.$$
Unitarity of $U$ gives $S^\dagger S=1$, which is conservation of probability. Writing $S=1+iT$, the coming lessons compute $T$ order by order in the coupling.

### A solvable warm-up

For one oscillator mode, $H_0=\omega a^\dagger a$ and $\phi=(a+a^\dagger)/\sqrt{2\omega}$. First-order perturbation theory gives $\Delta E_n=\langle n|H_{\rm int}|n\rangle$. For $H_{\rm int}=\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$ one finds $\langle0|(a+a^\dagger)^4|0\rangle=3$, so $\Delta E_0=\frac{\lambda}{32\omega^2}$. The same combinatorics, three pairings of four fields, drives the field-theory calculations of the next weeks.

@example
title: Quartic shift of the ground-state energy
problem: For one mode with $H_0=\omega a^\dagger a$ and $H_{\rm int}=\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$, $\phi=(a+a^\dagger)/\sqrt{2\omega}$, find the first-order shift of the ground-state energy.
step: First-order perturbation theory gives $\Delta E_0=\langle0|H_{\rm int}|0\rangle$.
step: $\phi^4=\frac{1}{(2\omega)^2}(a+a^\dagger)^4$, so we need $\langle0|(a+a^\dagger)^4|0\rangle=\|(a+a^\dagger)^2|0\rangle\|^2$.
step: $(a+a^\dagger)^2|0\rangle=(aa^\dagger+a^{\dagger2})|0\rangle=|0\rangle+\sqrt2\,|2\rangle$.
step: Its squared norm is $1+2=3$. This equals the number of ways to pair four fields into two pairs, each pair giving $\langle0|aa^\dagger|0\rangle=1$.
step: $\Delta E_0=\frac{\lambda}{4!}\cdot\frac{3}{4\omega^2}=\frac{\lambda}{32\omega^2}$, positive for $\lambda>0$ as expected for a stiffer potential.

@example
title: Deriving the interaction-picture equation
problem: Show that $U(t,t_0)=e^{iH_0(t-t_0)}e^{-iH(t-t_0)}$ satisfies $i\,\partial_tU=H_I(t)\,U$ with $H_I(t)=e^{iH_0(t-t_0)}H_{\rm int}\,e^{-iH_0(t-t_0)}$.
step: Let $\tau=t-t_0$ and differentiate with the product rule: $i\partial_tU=e^{iH_0\tau}(-H_0+H)\,e^{-iH\tau}$.
step: Use $H-H_0=H_{\rm int}$: $i\partial_tU=e^{iH_0\tau}H_{\rm int}\,e^{-iH\tau}$.
step: Insert $1=e^{-iH_0\tau}e^{iH_0\tau}$ between $H_{\rm int}$ and $e^{-iH\tau}$: $i\partial_tU=\big(e^{iH_0\tau}H_{\rm int}e^{-iH_0\tau}\big)\big(e^{iH_0\tau}e^{-iH\tau}\big)$.
step: The first bracket is $H_I(t)$ and the second is $U$, so $i\partial_tU=H_I(t)\,U$. Also $U(t_0,t_0)=1$, and $U=1$ when $H_{\rm int}=0$, as it should.

@mistakes
- Using $\mathcal H_{\rm int}=+\mathcal L_{\rm int}$. For non-derivative interactions $\mathcal H_{\rm int}=-\mathcal L_{\rm int}$.
- Believing $\phi_I$ obeys the interacting field equation. It is a free field even in an interacting theory.
- Treating $H_I(t)$ as time independent. The factors $e^{\pm iH_0t}$ make it depend on $t$.
- Identifying the interacting vacuum with $|0\rangle$ without the adiabatic-switching argument, or ignoring mass shifts.

@quiz ms
prompt: Which statements about the interaction picture are correct?
choice*: Interaction-picture fields obey the free Klein–Gordon equation even when the full theory is interacting.
choice*: States evolve by $i\partial_t|\psi_I\rangle=H_I(t)|\psi_I\rangle$ with $H_I(t)=e^{iH_0(t-t_0)}H_{\rm int}e^{-iH_0(t-t_0)}$.
choice: $H_I(t)$ is independent of time because $H_{\rm int}$ is.
choice: $U(t,t_0)$ fails to be unitary when $H_{\rm int}\neq0$.
hint: Operators in this picture evolve with $H_0$ alone.
hint: $U$ is a product of exponentials of Hermitian operators.
solution: The statements that interaction-picture fields are free and that states obey $i\partial_t|\psi_I\rangle=H_I(t)|\psi_I\rangle$ restate the definitions: operators evolve with $H_0$, states with $H_I(t)$. The claim that $H_I(t)$ is independent of time is false: conjugation by $e^{iH_0t}$ introduces explicit time dependence. The claim that $U$ fails to be unitary is false: $U=e^{iH_0\tau}e^{-iH\tau}$ is unitary for any Hermitian $H_{\rm int}$.
