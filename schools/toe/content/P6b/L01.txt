@article
In P6a you met the electric and magnetic fields one source at a time. Here we put everything together. All of classical electromagnetism is the four **Maxwell equations** plus the Lorentz force law. In SI units, with charge density $\rho$ and current density $\mathbf{J}$:

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E} = \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0},\qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0,$$
$$\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t},\qquad \nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{J} + \mu_0\varepsilon_0\frac{\partial\mathbf{E}}{\partial t}.$$

### What each equation says

- **Gauss's law**: electric charge is the source of $\mathbf{E}$. Integral form: $\oint \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{A} = Q_{\text{enc}}/\varepsilon_0$.
- **No magnetic monopoles**: $\oint \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{A} = 0$ for every closed surface.
- **Faraday's law**: a changing magnetic flux drives circulating $\mathbf{E}$. Integral form: $\oint \mathbf{E}\cdot d\boldsymbol{\ell} = -\,d\Phi_B/dt$.
- **Ampère–Maxwell law**: currents *and* changing electric flux drive circulating $\mathbf{B}$. Integral form: $\oint \mathbf{B}\cdot d\boldsymbol{\ell} = \mu_0 I_{\text{enc}} + \mu_0\varepsilon_0\, d\Phi_E/dt$.

The curl equations are where the dynamics live: each field's time derivative feeds the other field's curl.

### Why the displacement current is forced on us

Without the last term, Ampère's law reads $\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{J}$. Take the divergence. The divergence of a curl is always zero, so $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$, which is false whenever charge piles up somewhere (a charging capacitor). With the extra term, use Gauss's law to replace $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}$:

$$0 = \mu_0\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} + \mu_0\varepsilon_0\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0.$$

This is the **continuity equation**: local charge conservation is built into Maxwell's equations. The quantity $\mathbf{J}_d = \varepsilon_0\,\partial\mathbf{E}/\partial t$ is the **displacement current density**.

### Waves from the equations

In empty space ($\rho = 0$, $\mathbf{J} = 0$) take the curl of Faraday's law and use $\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{E}) = \nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}) - \nabla^2\mathbf{E} = -\nabla^2\mathbf{E}$:

$$\nabla^2\mathbf{E} = \mu_0\varepsilon_0\frac{\partial^2\mathbf{E}}{\partial t^2},\qquad c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}}\approx 3.00\times10^{8}\ \text{m/s}.$$

The same equation holds for $\mathbf{B}$. Light is an electromagnetic wave. We will solve this equation in Week 3.

@example
title: The magnetic field inside a charging capacitor
problem: A circular parallel-plate capacitor has plate radius $R$. Wires carry a current $I(t)$ that charges it, and the field between the plates is uniform. Find $B$ at distance $r < R$ from the axis, then evaluate it for $I = 2.0\ \text{A}$, $R = 5.0\ \text{cm}$, $r = 2.5\ \text{cm}$.
step: Between the plates there is no conduction current, so the Ampère–Maxwell law on a circle of radius $r$ about the axis reads $B(2\pi r) = \mu_0\varepsilon_0\, d\Phi_E/dt$, where $\Phi_E$ is the electric flux through the flat disc the circle bounds. By symmetry $\mathbf{B}$ circles the axis with uniform magnitude on the circle.
step: The field is uniform, $E = Q/(\varepsilon_0\pi R^2)$, so the flux through the disc of radius $r$ is $\Phi_E = E\,\pi r^2 = Q\,r^2/(\varepsilon_0 R^2)$.
step: Differentiate, using $dQ/dt = I$: $\dfrac{d\Phi_E}{dt} = \dfrac{I\,r^2}{\varepsilon_0 R^2}$.
step: Substitute: $B(2\pi r) = \mu_0 I r^2/R^2$, so $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I\, r}{2\pi R^2}$.
step: Numbers: $\dfrac{\mu_0}{2\pi} = 2\times10^{-7}\ \text{T·m/A}$, so $B = (2\times10^{-7})(2.0)(0.025)/(0.050)^2 = 4.0\times10^{-6}\ \text{T}$.
step: Check: at $r = R$ this becomes $\mu_0 I/(2\pi R)$, the field of a long straight wire carrying the same current. The displacement current continues the wire's current across the gap.

@mistakes
- Saying there is no magnetic field between capacitor plates because no charge flows through the gap. The changing electric flux plays the role of a current.
- Using $\nabla\times\mathbf{E} = 0$ outside electrostatics. In general Faraday's law adds $-\partial\mathbf{B}/\partial t$.
- Putting $\mu_0\varepsilon_0$ on the wrong curl equation: the curl of $\mathbf{E}$ carries no constants, while the displacement term in the curl of $\mathbf{B}$ carries $\mu_0\varepsilon_0$.
- Forgetting the sign in Faraday's law, which sets the direction of the induced field.

choice*: The changing electric flux, through the term $\mu_0\varepsilon_0\,\partial\mathbf{E}/\partial t$ in the Ampère–Maxwell law.
choice: The static charge stored on the plates.

@practice ms
prompt: Which of the following follow directly from Maxwell's equations (with sources)? Select all that apply.
choice*: Local charge conservation, $\partial\rho/\partial t + \nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$.
choice*: In empty space each Cartesian component of $\mathbf{E}$ obeys a wave equation with speed $1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}$.

choice*: Magnetic field lines never begin or end on a source.
