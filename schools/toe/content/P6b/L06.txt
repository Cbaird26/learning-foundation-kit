@article
The boundary conditions fix not only the directions of the reflected and transmitted waves (last lesson) but also their amplitudes.

### Normal incidence

Let a wave travel along $+z$ from medium 1 (index $n_1$) into medium 2 (index $n_2$), polarized along $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$. With $\mathbf{B} = (n/c)\,\hat{\mathbf{k}}\times\mathbf{E}$ for each wave, the amplitudes are

$$\text{incident: } E_I,\ B_I = \frac{n_1E_I}{c};\qquad \text{reflected: } E_R,\ B_R = -\frac{n_1E_R}{c};\qquad \text{transmitted: } E_T,\ B_T = \frac{n_2E_T}{c},$$

all with $\mathbf{B}$ along $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$. The sign in $B_R$ appears because the reflected wave travels along $-\hat{\mathbf{z}}$. At $z = 0$ both fields are tangential, so $E$ and $B$ (for nonmagnetic media) are continuous:

$$E_I + E_R = E_T,\qquad n_1(E_I - E_R) = n_2E_T.$$

Solving gives the **Fresnel coefficients** at normal incidence:

$$r = \frac{E_R}{E_I} = \frac{n_1 - n_2}{n_1 + n_2},\qquad t = \frac{E_T}{E_I} = \frac{2n_1}{n_1 + n_2},\qquad 1 + r = t.$$

If $n_2 > n_1$ then $r < 0$: the reflected field is reversed (a phase shift of $\pi$). If $n_2 < n_1$ then $r > 0$.

### Energy conservation

The intensity in a medium of index $n$ is $I = \tfrac12 n\varepsilon_0 c\,E_0^2$, so the fractions of incident intensity that are reflected and transmitted are

$$R = r^2,\qquad T = \frac{n_2}{n_1}\,t^2.$$

Then

$$R + T = \frac{(n_1 - n_2)^2 + 4n_1n_2}{(n_1 + n_2)^2} = 1.$$

The time-averaged Poynting flux normal to the surface is continuous, $S_I - S_R = S_T$, which is the same statement. Note that $t$ can exceed $1$ (when $n_1 > n_2$): $t$ is an amplitude ratio, while $T$ is an intensity ratio and never exceeds $1$ without gain.

### Oblique incidence

For general $\theta_i$ the answers depend on the polarization relative to the plane of incidence: **s** (E perpendicular to the plane) or **p** (E in the plane). The reflectances are $R = |r|^2$ with

$$r_s = \frac{n_1\cos\theta_i - n_2\cos\theta_t}{n_1\cos\theta_i + n_2\cos\theta_t},\qquad r_p = \frac{n_2\cos\theta_i - n_1\cos\theta_t}{n_2\cos\theta_i + n_1\cos\theta_t}$$

(the sign of $r_p$ depends on how positive directions are chosen; $R$ does not). For lossless media $T = 1 - R$.

- **Brewster's angle**: $r_p = 0$ when $\theta_i + \theta_t = 90^\circ$, i.e. $\tan\theta_B = n_2/n_1$. Reflected light is then purely s-polarized.
- **Total internal reflection**: beyond the critical angle $|r| = 1$ for both polarizations, with a phase shift.

@example
title: Glass to water at normal incidence
problem: Light passes from glass ($n_1 = 1.50$) into water ($n_2 = 1.33$) at normal incidence. Find $r$, $t$, $R$ and $T$, and check energy conservation.
step: The reflection coefficient is $r = \dfrac{n_1 - n_2}{n_1 + n_2} = \dfrac{0.17}{2.83} = +0.0601$. It is positive because $n_2 < n_1$, so there is no phase flip.
step: The transmission coefficient is $t = \dfrac{2n_1}{n_1 + n_2} = \dfrac{3.00}{2.83} = 1.060$. Check: $1 + r = 1.060$.
step: The reflectance is $R = r^2 = 0.00361$, or $0.36\%$.
step: The transmittance is $T = \dfrac{n_2}{n_1}\,t^2 = \dfrac{1.33}{1.50}(1.124) = 0.9964$. Although $t > 1$, $T < 1$.
step: Check: $R + T = 0.00361 + 0.99639 = 1.0000$. All the incident power is accounted for.

@mistakes
- Using $T = t^2$. The transmitted intensity also involves the factor $n_2/n_1$ (more generally $n_2\cos\theta_t/n_1\cos\theta_i$), so $T = t^2$ breaks $R + T = 1$ (it gives totals above $1$ when $n_1 > n_2$).
- Thinking $t > 1$ violates energy conservation. It is an amplitude ratio.
- Forgetting that $r < 0$ when $n_2 > n_1$ (a phase flip of $\pi$ for the reflected $\mathbf{E}$).
- Applying $\tan\theta_B = n_2/n_1$ to s-polarized light. Only the p component of the reflection vanishes at that angle.

solution: $r = \dfrac{n_1 - n_2}{n_1 + n_2} = \dfrac{1.00 - 1.50}{2.50} = -0.20$. The negative sign means the reflected electric field is reversed.

@practice self
prompt: Derive the amplitude coefficients $r$ and $t$ for a plane wave at normal incidence on an interface between nonmagnetic media of indices $n_1$ and $n_2$. Then compute the reflectance $R$ and transmittance $T$ and show that $R + T = 1$.
hint: Write the three waves with $B = (n/c)\hat{\mathbf{k}}\times\mathbf{E}$; for the reflected wave $\hat{\mathbf{k}} = -\hat{\mathbf{z}}$.
hint: Impose continuity of tangential $E$ and tangential $B$ at $z = 0$, solve the two linear equations, then use $I \propto nE_0^2$.
solution: Take $\mathbf{E}$ along $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ and wave directions $\pm\hat{\mathbf{z}}$. Then $B_I = n_1E_I/c$, $B_R = -n_1E_R/c$, $B_T = n_2E_T/c$, all along $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$. Continuity of tangential $E$ at $z = 0$: $E_I + E_R = E_T$. Continuity of tangential $B$ (nonmagnetic media): $n_1(E_I - E_R) = n_2E_T$. Substituting $E_T$ from the first into the second gives $n_1E_I - n_1E_R = n_2E_I + n_2E_R$, so $r = E_R/E_I = (n_1 - n_2)/(n_1 + n_2)$ and $t = 1 + r = 2n_1/(n_1 + n_2)$. The intensity in a medium is $\tfrac12n\varepsilon_0cE_0^2$, so $R = r^2$ and $T = (n_2/n_1)t^2$. Then $R + T = \dfrac{(n_1 - n_2)^2 + 4n_1n_2}{(n_1 + n_2)^2} = \dfrac{(n_1 + n_2)^2}{(n_1 + n_2)^2} = 1$.
rubric: Writes the magnetic amplitudes of the three waves correctly, including the minus sign for the reflected wave.
rubric: Imposes continuity of tangential $E$ and tangential $B$ and solves correctly for $r$ and $t$.
rubric: Uses $I \propto nE_0^2$ to obtain $R = r^2$ and $T = (n_2/n_1)t^2$, explaining the factor $n_2/n_1$.
rubric: Shows algebraically that $R + T = 1$.
