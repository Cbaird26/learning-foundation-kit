@article
Until now the sources were static, or we studied free waves. To ask how a changing source *makes* radiation, we work with potentials.

### Potentials

Since $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$ we can write $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$. Faraday's law then says $\nabla\times(\mathbf{E} + \partial_t\mathbf{A}) = 0$, so the bracket is a gradient:

$$\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A},\qquad \mathbf{E} = -\nabla\varphi - \frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial t}.$$

These two homogeneous Maxwell equations are then satisfied automatically. Gauss's law and the Ampère–Maxwell law remain, and they become equations for $\varphi$ and $\mathbf{A}$.

### The Lorenz gauge

The potentials are not unique (the topic of Week 12). The **Lorenz gauge condition**

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} + \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t} = 0$$

decouples the equations. Substituting into Gauss's law gives $-\nabla^2\varphi - \partial_t(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}) = \rho/\varepsilon_0$, and using the condition leaves

$$\nabla^2\varphi - \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2\varphi}{\partial t^2} = -\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0},\qquad \nabla^2\mathbf{A} - \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2\mathbf{A}}{\partial t^2} = -\mu_0\mathbf{J}.$$

The Ampère–Maxwell law gives the second equation in the same way. Each is the wave equation with a source.

### Retarded solutions

The solution consistent with causality is

$$\varphi(\mathbf{r},t) = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\int\frac{\rho(\mathbf{r}',t_r)}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}\,d^3r',\qquad \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r},t) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int\frac{\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}',t_r)}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}\,d^3r',$$

with the **retarded time**

$$t_r = t - \frac{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}{c}.$$

Each source element contributes as it was a time $|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|/c$ ago, because information needs that long to travel from $\mathbf{r}'$ to $\mathbf{r}$. The fields at $(\mathbf{r},t)$ depend only on sources on the **past light cone**. For static sources $t_r$ drops out and we recover Coulomb's law and the Biot–Savart law. The mathematically allowed *advanced* solution, with $t + |\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|/c$, would let effects precede their causes, so it is discarded. The Lorenz condition is consistent with these solutions because charge is conserved.

### Moving point charge

For a point charge on a trajectory $\mathbf{r}_q(t)$ the retarded time is defined implicitly:

$$c\,(t - t_r) = |\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_q(t_r)|.$$

If the speed is below $c$, this has exactly one solution with $t_r < t$. The charge's position and velocity must be evaluated at $t_r$, not at $t$: you see where the charge was, not where it is.

@example
title: Retarded time for a moving charge
problem: A point charge moves along the $x$-axis with $x_q(t) = 0.60\,c\,t$. An observer sits at $(0, d, 0)$ with $d = 1.0$ light-second ($3.00\times10^{8}\ \text{m}$). Find the retarded time $t_r$ for the observation time $t = 2.0\ \text{s}$.
step: Use units where $c = 1\ \text{ls/s}$. The distance from the charge at time $t_r$ to the observer is $\sqrt{(0.60\,t_r)^2 + 1}$ light-seconds, so the retarded-time condition is $2.0 - t_r = \sqrt{0.36\,t_r^2 + 1}$.
step: Square both sides: $4 - 4t_r + t_r^2 = 0.36\,t_r^2 + 1$, which simplifies to $0.64\,t_r^2 - 4t_r + 3 = 0$.
step: The quadratic formula gives $t_r = \dfrac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 7.68}}{1.28} = \dfrac{4 \pm 2.884}{1.28}$, i.e. $0.872\ \text{s}$ or $5.38\ \text{s}$.
step: The second root is later than $t = 2.0\ \text{s}$ (an *advanced* time) and is rejected. So $t_r = 0.872\ \text{s}$.
step: Check: at $t_r$ the charge was at $x_q = 0.523$ ls, so its distance from the observer was $\sqrt{0.523^2 + 1} = 1.129$ ls. Light needs $1.129\ \text{s}$, and $2.0 - 0.872 = 1.128\ \text{s}$. The observer sees the charge where it was $1.13\ \text{s}$ ago.

@mistakes
- Evaluating $\rho$ and $\mathbf{J}$ at the present time $t$ instead of the retarded time $t_r$.
- Using the observer–charge distance at time $t$ in the retarded-time condition; it must be the distance at $t_r$, which makes the equation implicit.
- Thinking retardation changes the static limit. For time-independent sources the retarded potentials reduce to the Coulomb and Biot–Savart results.
- Keeping the root with $t_r > t$. Only the retarded root is physical.

@practice self
prompt: Show that in the Lorenz gauge, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} + c^{-2}\partial\varphi/\partial t = 0$, Gauss's law and the Ampère–Maxwell law become the wave equations $\nabla^2\varphi - c^{-2}\partial_t^2\varphi = -\rho/\varepsilon_0$ and $\nabla^2\mathbf{A} - c^{-2}\partial_t^2\mathbf{A} = -\mu_0\mathbf{J}$.
hint: Substitute $\mathbf{E} = -\nabla\varphi - \partial_t\mathbf{A}$ into $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E} = \rho/\varepsilon_0$.
hint: For the second equation use $\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{A}) = \nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}) - \nabla^2\mathbf{A}$ and collect the gradient terms.
solution: Gauss's law: $\nabla\cdot(-\nabla\varphi - \partial_t\mathbf{A}) = \rho/\varepsilon_0$ gives $-\nabla^2\varphi - \partial_t(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}) = \rho/\varepsilon_0$. The Lorenz condition gives $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} = -c^{-2}\partial_t\varphi$, so $-\nabla^2\varphi + c^{-2}\partial_t^2\varphi = \rho/\varepsilon_0$, i.e. $\nabla^2\varphi - c^{-2}\partial_t^2\varphi = -\rho/\varepsilon_0$. Ampère–Maxwell: $\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{J} + c^{-2}\partial_t\mathbf{E}$ with $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$ gives $\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}) - \nabla^2\mathbf{A} = \mu_0\mathbf{J} - c^{-2}\nabla\partial_t\varphi - c^{-2}\partial_t^2\mathbf{A}$. Moving terms: $-\nabla^2\mathbf{A} + c^{-2}\partial_t^2\mathbf{A} + \nabla\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} + c^{-2}\partial_t\varphi\right) = \mu_0\mathbf{J}$. The bracket vanishes in the Lorenz gauge, leaving $\nabla^2\mathbf{A} - c^{-2}\partial_t^2\mathbf{A} = -\mu_0\mathbf{J}$.
rubric: Substitutes $\mathbf{E} = -\nabla\varphi - \partial_t\mathbf{A}$ into Gauss's law and obtains an equation containing $\nabla^2\varphi$ and $\partial_t(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A})$.
rubric: Applies the Lorenz condition to replace $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}$ and reaches the wave equation for $\varphi$ with source $-\rho/\varepsilon_0$.
rubric: Substitutes $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{E}$ into the Ampère–Maxwell law and uses the curl-of-curl identity.
rubric: Shows that the leftover gradient term vanishes in the Lorenz gauge and obtains the wave equation for $\mathbf{A}$ with source $-\mu_0\mathbf{J}$.

@quiz num
prompt: A point charge moves directly toward an observer along the $x$-axis with $x_q(t) = 0.50\,c\,t$, and the observer is at $x = 2.0$ light-seconds. Using units where $c = 1\ \text{ls/s}$, find the retarded time $t_r$ in seconds for observation time $t = 3.0\ \text{s}$.
answer: 2.0
check: root(lambda tp: (3.0 - tp) - abs(2.0 - 0.5*tp), -10, 3)
tol: 1%
unit: s
hint: The distance from the charge at time $t_r$ to the observer is $2.0 - 0.50\,t_r$ light-seconds, while light needs $t - t_r$ to cover it.
hint: Solve $3.0 - t_r = 2.0 - 0.50\,t_r$, which is linear.
solution: $3.0 - t_r = 2.0 - 0.5\,t_r$ gives $1.0 = 0.5\,t_r$, so $t_r = 2.0\ \text{s}$. At that time the charge was at $x = 1.0$ ls, a light-second from the observer, which matches $t - t_r = 1.0\ \text{s}$. Because the charge approaches, the retarded time is later than for a receding charge.

@quiz ms
prompt: Which statements about the retarded potentials are correct? Select all that apply.
choice*: For static sources they reduce to Coulomb's law and the Biot–Savart law.
choice*: The potentials at $(\mathbf{r}, t)$ depend only on the sources on the past light cone of that event.
choice: If a distant charge is suddenly displaced, the potential at $\mathbf{r}$ changes immediately.
choice*: The Lorenz gauge condition is consistent with the sourced wave equations because charge is conserved.
choice: The advanced solution, with $t + |\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|/c$, is the one chosen to respect causality.
hint: Think about what the retarded time says about the earliest moment a disturbance can be felt.
solution: With a time-independent source, $t_r$ does not matter, giving the static results. The effect of a displacement travels outward at $c$, so the potential at distance $R$ only changes after a delay $R/c$. Charge conservation (the continuity equation) is what makes the Lorenz condition compatible with the two wave equations. The advanced solution violates causality.
