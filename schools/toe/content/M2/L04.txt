objective: Divide first when the numerator's degree is at least the denominator's, then factor the denominator.
objective: Write the correct decomposition form for distinct, repeated and irreducible quadratic factors.
objective: Solve for the constants, integrate term by term, and verify the antiderivative by differentiating.

@article
Adding fractions is routine: $\frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{1}{x+1}=\frac{(x+1)-(x-1)}{x^2-1}=\frac{2}{x^2-1}$. **Partial fractions** runs this in reverse. The single fraction $\frac{1}{x^2-1}$ is awkward to integrate, but the two pieces in $\frac12\left(\frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{1}{x+1}\right)$ are easy, because each one is a logarithm:

$$\int\frac{dx}{x^2-1}=\frac12\ln|x-1|-\frac12\ln|x+1|+C.$$

### Step 0: make the fraction proper

A rational function $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$ is **proper** when $\deg P<\deg Q$. If it is not, divide first: $\frac{x^3}{x^2-1}=x+\frac{x}{x^2-1}$. The polynomial part integrates directly, and only the proper remainder needs decomposing.

### Step 1: factor the denominator

Factor $Q(x)$ over the real numbers into linear factors $(x-a)$ and **irreducible quadratics** $x^2+bx+c$, which are the ones with $b^2-4c<0$ and no real roots.

### Step 2: write the form

Each factor of $Q$ contributes terms with unknown constants:

| Factor in $Q$ | Terms it contributes |
|---|---|
| $(x-a)$ | $\frac{A}{x-a}$ |
| $(x-a)^k$ | $\frac{A_1}{x-a}+\frac{A_2}{(x-a)^2}+\cdots+\frac{A_k}{(x-a)^k}$ |
| $x^2+bx+c$ irreducible | $\frac{Ax+B}{x^2+bx+c}$ |

A repeated factor needs one term for **every** power up to $k$, and an irreducible quadratic needs a linear numerator.

### Step 3: find the constants

Multiply both sides by $Q(x)$ to clear the denominators. Then substitute convenient values of $x$: a root of a linear factor wipes out every term except one, which isolates that constant at once. Constants that remain can be found by comparing coefficients of like powers.

### Step 4: integrate

$$\int\frac{A}{x-a}\,dx=A\ln|x-a|,\qquad \int\frac{A}{(x-a)^k}\,dx=\frac{A}{(1-k)(x-a)^{k-1}}\quad(k\ge2).$$

For $\frac{Ax+B}{x^2+c^2}$, split it in two: $\frac{Ax}{x^2+c^2}$ is a logarithm (use $u=x^2+c^2$), and $\frac{B}{x^2+c^2}$ is an arctangent, since $\int\frac{dx}{x^2+c^2}=\frac1c\arctan\frac{x}{c}$.

### Always check

Arithmetic slips in the constants are common. Recombine the fractions over a common denominator, or differentiate the final antiderivative; either one tells you immediately whether you are right.

@example
title: Distinct linear factors
problem: Find $\int\frac{x+7}{x^2-x-6}\,dx$.
step: Factor the denominator: $x^2-x-6=(x-3)(x+2)$. The fraction is proper and the factors are distinct and linear, so write $\frac{x+7}{(x-3)(x+2)}=\frac{A}{x-3}+\frac{B}{x+2}$.
step: Clear the denominators: $x+7=A(x+2)+B(x-3)$.
step: Set $x=3$: $10=5A$, so $A=2$. Set $x=-2$: $5=-5B$, so $B=-1$.
step: Integrate term by term: $\int\left(\frac{2}{x-3}-\frac{1}{x+2}\right)dx=2\ln|x-3|-\ln|x+2|+C$.
step: Check by recombining: $\frac{2}{x-3}-\frac{1}{x+2}=\frac{2(x+2)-(x-3)}{(x-3)(x+2)}=\frac{x+7}{x^2-x-6}$, the original integrand.

@example
title: A repeated factor
problem: Find $\int\frac{2x^2+3x+3}{x(x+1)^2}\,dx$.
step: The factor $(x+1)$ is repeated, so the form needs a term for each power: $\frac{2x^2+3x+3}{x(x+1)^2}=\frac{A}{x}+\frac{B}{x+1}+\frac{C}{(x+1)^2}$.
step: Clear the denominators: $2x^2+3x+3=A(x+1)^2+Bx(x+1)+Cx$.
step: Set $x=0$: $3=A$. Set $x=-1$: $2-3+3=-C$, so $C=-2$.
step: Compare the $x^2$ coefficients: $2=A+B$, so $B=-1$.
step: Integrate, using $\int\frac{-2}{(x+1)^2}\,dx=\frac{2}{x+1}$: the result is $3\ln|x|-\ln|x+1|+\frac{2}{x+1}+C$.
step: Check by differentiating: the derivative is $\frac3x-\frac{1}{x+1}-\frac{2}{(x+1)^2}=\frac{3(x+1)^2-x(x+1)-2x}{x(x+1)^2}=\frac{2x^2+3x+3}{x(x+1)^2}$.

@mistakes
- Skipping polynomial division when the numerator's degree is at least the denominator's.
- Writing only $\frac{A}{(x-a)^2}$ for a repeated factor and leaving out the $\frac{A_1}{x-a}$ term, or using a constant numerator over an irreducible quadratic.
- Integrating $\frac{1}{(x-a)^2}$ as a logarithm. Only the first power gives $\ln|x-a|$; higher powers give a power of $\frac{1}{x-a}$.
- Trusting the constants without a check. Recombine the fractions or differentiate.

@practice num
prompt: Evaluate $\int_1^2\frac{dx}{x(x+1)}$. Give an exact answer.
answer: ln(4/3)
check: quad(lambda x: 1/(x*(x+1)), 1, 2)
hint: Write $\frac{1}{x(x+1)}=\frac{A}{x}+\frac{B}{x+1}$ and clear denominators: $1=A(x+1)+Bx$.
hint: Setting $x=0$ gives $A$ and setting $x=-1$ gives $B$. Integrate each term and combine the logarithms.
solution: $A=1$ and $B=-1$, so $\int_1^2\left(\frac1x-\frac{1}{x+1}\right)dx=\left[\ln\frac{x}{x+1}\right]_1^2=\ln\frac23-\ln\frac12=\ln\frac43$.

@practice mc
prompt: Which is the correct form of the partial fraction decomposition of $\frac{x+1}{x(x-2)^2(x^2+1)}$?
choice*: $\frac{A}{x}+\frac{B}{x-2}+\frac{C}{(x-2)^2}+\frac{Dx+E}{x^2+1}$
choice: $\frac{A}{x}+\frac{B}{(x-2)^2}+\frac{Cx+D}{x^2+1}$
choice: $\frac{A}{x}+\frac{B}{x-2}+\frac{C}{(x-2)^2}+\frac{D}{x^2+1}$
choice: $\frac{A}{x}+\frac{B}{x-2}+\frac{Cx+D}{x^2+1}$
hint: Count the terms: a factor repeated twice needs two terms, and an irreducible quadratic needs a linear numerator.
solution: The linear factor $x$ gives $\frac{A}{x}$; the repeated factor $(x-2)^2$ gives both $\frac{B}{x-2}$ and $\frac{C}{(x-2)^2}$; the irreducible quadratic $x^2+1$ gives $\frac{Dx+E}{x^2+1}$. The other forms omit the $\frac{1}{x-2}$ term, use a constant numerator over $x^2+1$, or ignore the multiplicity of $(x-2)$.

@practice num
prompt: Evaluate $\int_2^3\frac{x^2}{x^2-1}\,dx$. Give an exact answer.
answer: 1 + ln(3/2)/2
check: quad(lambda x: x**2/(x**2-1), 2, 3)
hint: The numerator and denominator have the same degree, so divide first: $\frac{x^2}{x^2-1}=1+\frac{1}{x^2-1}$.
hint: Decompose $\frac{1}{x^2-1}=\frac12\left(\frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{1}{x+1}\right)$ and integrate from $2$ to $3$.
solution: $\int_2^3 1\,dx=1$, and $\int_2^3\frac12\left(\frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{1}{x+1}\right)dx=\frac12\left[\ln\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right]_2^3=\frac12\left(\ln\frac12-\ln\frac13\right)=\frac12\ln\frac32$. The total is $1+\frac12\ln\frac32$.

@practice expr
prompt: Find an antiderivative of $\frac{2x^2+x+3}{x(x^2+1)}$ for $x>0$. Omit the $+C$.
vars: x
lead: $\int\frac{2x^2+x+3}{x(x^2+1)}\,dx =$
answer: 3 ln(x) - ln(x^2+1)/2 + atan(x)
check: lambda x: quad(lambda t: (2*t**2+t+3)/(t*(t**2+1)), 1, x)
const: yes
hint: The denominator has a linear factor and an irreducible quadratic, so use $\frac{A}{x}+\frac{Bx+C}{x^2+1}$.
hint: Clear denominators: $2x^2+x+3=A(x^2+1)+(Bx+C)x$. Setting $x=0$ gives $A$; comparing the $x^2$ and $x$ coefficients gives $B$ and $C$.
solution: $A=3$, $B=-1$, $C=1$, so the integrand is $\frac3x-\frac{x}{x^2+1}+\frac{1}{x^2+1}$. Integrating gives $3\ln x-\frac12\ln(x^2+1)+\arctan x+C$.

@practice self
prompt: Solve each integral, name the method you use, and check each answer by differentiating it. (a) $\int x\ln x\,dx$ for $x>0$. (b) $\int\frac{dx}{x^2-4}$ for $x>2$. (c) $\int\frac{dx}{x^2\sqrt{x^2+1}}$ for $x>0$.
hint: Look at the form of each integrand. A product of $x$ and a logarithm, a rational function, and a root of $x^2+1$ call for three different methods.
hint: For (a) take $u=\ln x$ and $dv=x\,dx$. For (b) factor $x^2-4$. For (c) let $x=\tan\theta$.
solution: (a) Integration by parts with $u=\ln x$, $dv=x\,dx$ gives $\frac{x^2}{2}\ln x-\int\frac{x}{2}\,dx=\frac{x^2}{2}\ln x-\frac{x^2}{4}+C$. Check: $x\ln x+\frac{x}{2}-\frac{x}{2}=x\ln x$. (b) Partial fractions: $\frac{1}{x^2-4}=\frac14\left(\frac{1}{x-2}-\frac{1}{x+2}\right)$, so the integral is $\frac14\ln\frac{x-2}{x+2}+C$. Check: $\frac14\left(\frac{1}{x-2}-\frac{1}{x+2}\right)=\frac14\cdot\frac{4}{x^2-4}$. (c) Trigonometric substitution $x=\tan\theta$ turns the integrand into $\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta}\,d\theta$, which integrates to $-\frac{1}{\sin\theta}=-\frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{x}+C$. Check by the quotient rule: $-\frac{x\cdot\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}-\sqrt{x^2+1}}{x^2}=\frac{1}{x^2\sqrt{x^2+1}}$.
rubric: Chooses a suitable method for each integral: parts for (a), partial fractions for (b), a tangent substitution for (c).
rubric: Obtains the antiderivatives $\frac{x^2}{2}\ln x-\frac{x^2}{4}$, $\frac14\ln\frac{x-2}{x+2}$ and $-\frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{x}$, each plus a constant.
rubric: Differentiates each answer and shows that it returns the original integrand.

@quiz num
prompt: Evaluate $\int_1^2\frac{dx}{x^2(x+1)}$. Give an exact answer.
answer: ln(3/4) + 1/2
check: quad(lambda x: 1/(x**2*(x+1)), 1, 2)
hint: The factor $x^2$ is repeated: $\frac{1}{x^2(x+1)}=\frac{A}{x}+\frac{B}{x^2}+\frac{C}{x+1}$.
hint: Clear denominators, set $x=0$ and $x=-1$ to find $B$ and $C$, then compare $x^2$ coefficients to find $A$.
solution: $1=Ax(x+1)+B(x+1)+Cx^2$ gives $B=1$, $C=1$ and $A=-1$. Then $\int_1^2\left(-\frac1x+\frac{1}{x^2}+\frac{1}{x+1}\right)dx=\left[-\ln x-\frac1x+\ln(x+1)\right]_1^2=\left(\ln\frac32-\frac12\right)-\left(\ln 2-1\right)=\ln\frac34+\frac12$.

@quiz tuple
prompt: Find the constants $A$, $B$, $C$, in that order, in $\frac{x^2+1}{(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)}=\frac{A}{x-1}+\frac{B}{x-2}+\frac{C}{x-3}$.
answer: (1, -5, 5)
check: lin([[6, 3, 2], [2, 3, 6], [6, 8, 12]], [1, 17, 26])
hint: Multiply both sides by $(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)$ to get $x^2+1=A(x-2)(x-3)+B(x-1)(x-3)+C(x-1)(x-2)$.
hint: Substituting each root of the denominator in turn ($x=1$, $x=2$, $x=3$) kills two of the three terms and leaves one constant.
solution: At $x=1$: $2=A(-1)(-2)=2A$, so $A=1$. At $x=2$: $5=B(1)(-1)=-B$, so $B=-5$. At $x=3$: $10=C(2)(1)=2C$, so $C=5$.
