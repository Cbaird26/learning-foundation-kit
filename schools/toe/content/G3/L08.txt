@article
**Setting.** SI units. A hollow guide has uniform cross-section along $z$, perfectly conducting walls and vacuum inside. Fields vary as $e^{i(\beta z-\omega t)}$, the convention of week 7. On a perfect conductor $\hat n\times\mathbf E=0$ and $\hat n\cdot\mathbf B=0$.

### Reduction to a scalar eigenproblem

Write $\gamma^2=\omega^2/c^2-\beta^2$. The longitudinal components obey a two-dimensional Helmholtz equation, and the transverse fields follow from them:

$$(\nabla_t^2+\gamma^2)\begin{Bmatrix}E_z\\B_z\end{Bmatrix}=0,\qquad \mathbf E_t=\frac{i}{\gamma^2}\left[\beta\nabla_tE_z-\omega\,\hat z\times\nabla_tB_z\right],\qquad \mathbf B_t=\frac{i}{\gamma^2}\left[\beta\nabla_tB_z+\frac{\omega}{c^2}\,\hat z\times\nabla_tE_z\right].$$

The modes split into two families:

- **TM** ($B_z=0$): $E_z$ is tangential to the wall, so $E_z=0$ on the boundary (Dirichlet).
- **TE** ($E_z=0$): $\hat n\cdot\mathbf B=0$ becomes $\partial_nB_z=0$ (Neumann).

Each condition gives a discrete set of eigenvalues $\gamma_\lambda^2$. A hollow single-conductor guide has no TEM mode: that would need $\gamma=0$ and a transverse field $-\nabla_t\varphi$ with $\varphi$ harmonic and constant on the one boundary, hence zero.

### Cutoff and dispersion

For each eigenvalue, $\beta=\sqrt{\omega^2-\omega_\lambda^2}/c$ with cutoff $\omega_\lambda=c\gamma_\lambda$. Below cutoff $\beta$ is imaginary and the mode decays like $e^{-|\beta|z}$. Above cutoff,

$$v_p=\frac{c}{\sqrt{1-\omega_\lambda^2/\omega^2}},\qquad v_g=c\sqrt{1-\omega_\lambda^2/\omega^2},\qquad \lambda_g=\frac{2\pi}{\beta}=\frac{\lambda_0}{\sqrt{1-\omega_\lambda^2/\omega^2}},$$

the same structure as a plasma with $\omega_\lambda$ in place of $\omega_p$.

### Rectangular guide ($0<x<a$, $0<y<b$)

The eigenvalues are $\gamma_{mn}=\pi\sqrt{(m/a)^2+(n/b)^2}$. TM$_{mn}$ has $E_z\propto\sin(m\pi x/a)\sin(n\pi y/b)$ with $m,n\ge1$. TE$_{mn}$ has $B_z\propto\cos(m\pi x/a)\cos(n\pi y/b)$ with $m,n\ge0$ not both zero. For $a>b$ the lowest mode is TE$_{10}$, with $f_c=c/(2a)$, so there is a band in which only it propagates.

### Circular guide (radius $a$)

$E_z$ or $B_z\propto J_m(\gamma\rho)\,e^{\pm im\phi}$. TM modes need $J_m(\gamma a)=0$, so $\gamma=x_{mn}/a$ with $x_{mn}$ the $n$th zero of $J_m$ ($x_{01}\approx2.405$). TE modes need $J_m'(\gamma a)=0$, so $\gamma=x'_{mn}/a$ ($x'_{11}\approx1.841$). TE$_{11}$ has the lowest cutoff and is dominant.

### Cavities

Closing the guide with plates at $z=0$ and $z=d$ forces standing waves with $\beta=p\pi/d$, so $\omega^2=c^2(\gamma_\lambda^2+p^2\pi^2/d^2)$. For a rectangular box

$$\omega_{mnp}=c\pi\sqrt{(m/a)^2+(n/b)^2+(p/d)^2}.$$

TM$_{mnp}$ needs $m,n\ge1$ and allows $p=0$; TE$_{mnp}$ needs $p\ge1$.

### Losses

Real walls have finite conductivity. Currents flow within a skin depth $\delta$ and dissipate power in the walls, which gives each propagating mode a small attenuation constant. The lossless modes above are the leading approximation, valid when $\delta$ is much smaller than the guide dimensions.

@example
title: Cutoff and velocities in a standard guide
problem: A hollow rectangular guide has $a=22.86\ \text{mm}$ and $b=10.16\ \text{mm}$. For the TE$_{10}$ mode at $10\ \text{GHz}$ find the cutoff frequency, the phase and group velocities, the guide wavelength, and the single-mode band. Use $c=2.998\times10^8\ \text{m/s}$.
step: The TE$_{10}$ eigenvalue is $\gamma_{10}=\pi/a$, so $f_c=c/(2a)=2.998\times10^8/0.04572=6.557\ \text{GHz}$.
step: At $10\ \text{GHz}$, $f_c/f=0.6557$ and $\sqrt{1-0.6557^2}=0.7550$.
step: Then $v_g=0.7550\,c=2.264\times10^8\ \text{m/s}$ and $v_p=c/0.7550=3.971\times10^8\ \text{m/s}$. Their product is $c^2$, as it must be.
step: The free-space wavelength is $\lambda_0=c/f=2.998\ \text{cm}$, so $\lambda_g=\lambda_0/0.7550=3.971\ \text{cm}$, longer than $\lambda_0$.
step: The next cutoffs are TE$_{20}$ at $c/a=13.11\ \text{GHz}$ and TE$_{01}$ at $c/(2b)=14.75\ \text{GHz}$, so only TE$_{10}$ propagates between $6.56$ and $13.11\ \text{GHz}$.

@example
title: Fields and power of the TE10 mode
problem: For the TE$_{10}$ mode of a rectangular guide, find the fields in terms of the peak transverse electric field $E_0$, and the time-averaged power $P$ carried along $+z$.
step: Take $E_y=E_0\sin(\pi x/a)\,e^{i(\beta z-\omega t)}$ and $E_x=E_z=0$. It vanishes on the walls $x=0,a$ and is normal to the walls $y=0,b$, so the conditions hold. The Helmholtz equation gives $\gamma=\pi/a$, so $\beta^2=\omega^2/c^2-\pi^2/a^2$ and $\omega_c=\pi c/a$.
step: Faraday's law $\nabla\times\mathbf E=i\omega\mathbf B$ gives $B_x=-(\beta/\omega)E_y$ and $B_z=-\dfrac{i\pi}{a\omega}E_0\cos(\pi x/a)\,e^{i(\beta z-\omega t)}$.
step: The time-averaged flux along $z$ is $\langle S_z\rangle=\dfrac{1}{2\mu_0}\mathrm{Re}(E_xB_y^*-E_yB_x^*)=\dfrac{\beta}{2\mu_0\omega}|E_0|^2\sin^2(\pi x/a)$.
step: Integrating over the cross-section, $\displaystyle\int_0^a\!\!\int_0^b\sin^2(\pi x/a)\,dy\,dx=ab/2$, so $P=\dfrac{\beta\,ab}{4\mu_0\omega}|E_0|^2$.
step: Since $\mu_0c=Z_0$, $P=\dfrac{|E_0|^2ab}{4Z_0}\sqrt{1-\omega_c^2/\omega^2}$. The power vanishes at cutoff and approaches the free-space value $|E_0|^2ab/(4Z_0)$ at high frequency.

@mistakes
- Using Dirichlet conditions for TE modes or Neumann conditions for TM modes. TM has $E_z=0$ at the wall; TE has $\partial_nB_z=0$.
- Allowing TM$_{m0}$, TM$_{0n}$ or TE$_{00}$ in a rectangular guide. TM modes need $m,n\ge1$ and TE modes need $(m,n)\ne(0,0)$.
- Using $\lambda_0$ for the wavelength along the guide. The guide wavelength is $\lambda_0/\sqrt{1-\omega_c^2/\omega^2}>\lambda_0$.
- Expecting a TEM mode in a hollow guide with a single conductor. It needs at least two separate conductors.

@practice self
prompt: Find the TE$_{101}$ mode of a rectangular cavity $0<x<a$, $0<y<b$, $0<z<d$ with perfectly conducting walls. Give the nonzero field components, show that the boundary conditions fix the resonant frequency, and relate the standing wave to two guide waves.
hint: Look for $E_y=E_0\sin(\pi x/a)\sin(p\pi z/d)\,e^{-i\omega t}$ and check which walls impose which conditions.
hint: Get $\mathbf B$ from Faraday's law $\nabla\times\mathbf E=i\omega\mathbf B$ and check $\hat n\cdot\mathbf B=0$ on every wall.
solution: Take $E_y=E_0\sin(\pi x/a)\sin(\pi z/d)\,e^{-i\omega t}$ (the TE$_{101}$ case, $p=1$). It is normal to the walls $y=0,b$ and vanishes on $x=0,a$ and on $z=0,d$, so $\hat n\times\mathbf E=0$ everywhere. The wave equation requires $\omega^2/c^2=(\pi/a)^2+(\pi/d)^2$, so $\omega_{101}=c\pi\sqrt{1/a^2+1/d^2}$. Faraday's law gives $B_x=\dfrac{1}{i\omega}\left(-\partial_zE_y\right)=\dfrac{i\pi}{\omega d}E_0\sin(\pi x/a)\cos(\pi z/d)\,e^{-i\omega t}$ and $B_z=\dfrac{1}{i\omega}\partial_xE_y=-\dfrac{i\pi}{\omega a}E_0\cos(\pi x/a)\sin(\pi z/d)\,e^{-i\omega t}$, with $B_y=0$. The normal component $B_x$ vanishes at $x=0,a$ and $B_z$ at $z=0,d$, as required. Writing $\sin(\pi z/d)=\left(e^{i\pi z/d}-e^{-i\pi z/d}\right)/(2i)$ shows the field is a TE$_{10}$ guide wave with $\beta=\pi/d$ travelling along $+z$ plus an equal and opposite wave along $-z$, reflected from the end plates with a sign change of $E_y$.
