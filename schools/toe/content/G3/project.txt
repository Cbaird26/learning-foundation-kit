## Course map

**Weeks 1-2: boundary data and Green identities.** Poisson's equation $\nabla^2\Phi=-\rho/\epsilon_0$; Dirichlet, Neumann and mixed data; uniqueness from $\int_V|\nabla\psi|^2=\oint\psi\,\partial_n\psi$; conductor conditions ($\sigma=\epsilon_0E_n$); images for a plane ($-q$) and a grounded sphere ($-qa/d$ at $a^2/d$). Green's identities, $\nabla'^2G=-\delta$, the representation formula, the kernel $-\partial_{n'}G_D$, and the half-space and ball kernels.

**Weeks 3-4: separation and multipoles.** Sines, Legendre polynomials ($r^l$, $r^{-l-1}$) and Bessel zeros $j_{0n}$, each with the correct orthogonality weight; regularity selects the radial functions. Moments $q$, $\mathbf p$, $Q_{ij}$; convergence for $r>r'_{\max}$; the truncation bound $\frac{Q_{\rm abs}}{r-R}(R/r)^{L+1}$; dependence on the origin.

**Weeks 5-6: media, energy, momentum.** Bound sources, $\mathbf D$ and $\mathbf H$, interface conditions, depolarization ($3/(\epsilon_r+2)$ for a sphere), the magnetized sphere ($\tfrac23\mu_0\mathbf M$). Poynting's theorem and the assumptions behind $u=\tfrac12(\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf D+\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf H)$; the stress tensor; radiation pressure $I/c$ and $2I/c$, with a factor $\cos^2\theta$ at oblique incidence.

**Weeks 7-8: waves and guides.** The $e^{-i\omega t}$ convention, $k^2=\mu\epsilon(\omega)\omega^2$, $v_p$ and $v_g$, plasma, skin depth, Fresnel coefficients, Brewster's angle, total internal reflection. TM modes have $E_z=0$ at the wall and TE modes $\partial_nB_z=0$; cutoff, guide wavelength, TE$_{10}$ power, cavity resonances.

**Weeks 9-10: radiation and scattering.** Retarded Green function, far-zone integral, $P=\mu_0\omega^4p^2/(12\pi c)$, Larmor's formula, magnetic dipole and quadrupole, corrections of order $(kd)^2$. Cross sections from the induced dipole: Thomson, Rayleigh, absorption $\propto a^3$, validity $ka\ll1$, optical theorem.

**Weeks 11-12: covariance and gauge.** Signature $(+,-,-,-)$, $F^{\mu\nu}$, $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=\mu_0J^\nu$, boosts of $\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf B$, invariants, $T^{\mu\nu}$. Gauge transformations, Lorenz gauge (residual $\Box\chi=0$), Coulomb and temporal gauges, loop integrals of $\mathbf A$.

## Self-check

- I state my conventions (units, signature, time convention, normalization of $G$) before computing.
- I can prove uniqueness and say exactly which boundary data suffice.
- I choose the series (sine, Legendre, Bessel) and the radial or axial functions from the geometry.
- I can say where a multipole or Rayleigh formula stops being valid and estimate its error.
- I can name the material assumptions in an energy balance.
- I can derive the cutoff and dispersion of a mode and the power it carries.
- I can move a calculation between three-vector and tensor notation and check gauge invariance.

## Common errors

- The wrong sign of the outward normal in surface terms; image charges placed inside the region.
- A mismatch in the normalization of the Green function (a factor of $4\pi$).
- Missing weights or normalization factors in orthogonality integrals.
- Using a series outside its convergence domain, or quoting more digits than the error allows.
- Dropping the factor $1/2$ in time averages; mixing $e^{-i\omega t}$ with $e^{+i\omega t}$.
- Treating $v_p$, or the potentials themselves, as physical.
- Confusing TE and TM boundary conditions.

prompt: A small vertical oscillating electric dipole (real amplitude $p_0$, angular frequency $\omega$, size much smaller than $\lambda$) sits at height $h=\lambda/10$ above an infinite perfectly conducting plane $z=0$ and points along $\hat z$. Use images and the dipole radiation formulas to find the ratio of the total power radiated into the upper half-space $z>0$ to the power the same dipole radiates in free space.
answer: 1.851
check: quad(lambda x: 4*(1 - x**2)*cos(2*pi/10*x)**2, 0, 1)/(4/3)
tol: 1%
hint: For a vertical dipole the image is a dipole of the same sign and direction at $z=-h$.
hint: In a direction at angle $\theta$ from $\hat z$, the two dipoles differ in path by $2h\cos\theta$, so the field is multiplied by $2\cos(kh\cos\theta)$.
hint: Integrate $\sin^2\theta\cdot4\cos^2(kh\cos\theta)$ over the upper hemisphere and divide by $\int\sin^2\theta\,d\Omega=8\pi/3$ for the full sphere. With $x=\cos\theta$ the numerator is $2\pi\int_0^1(1-x^2)\,4\cos^2(khx)\,dx$.
solution: The image dipole $p_0\hat z$ at $-h$ has the same sign. The far field is that of a single dipole times the array factor $2\cos(kh\cos\theta)$, so $dP/d\Omega=\frac{\mu_0\omega^4p_0^2}{32\pi^2c}\sin^2\theta\cdot4\cos^2(kh\cos\theta)$ for $z>0$ (no radiation below the plane). Integrating, with $b=2kh=4\pi h/\lambda$, the ratio to the free-space power is $1+3(\sin b-b\cos b)/b^3$. For $h=\lambda/10$, $b=0.4\pi$ and the ratio is $1.851$. For $h\to0$ it tends to $2$: the dipole and image act as a dipole of twice the moment radiating into half the space.

@exam num
prompt: A plane wave of intensity $1.0\ \text{kW/m}^2$ and frequency $3.0\ \text{GHz}$ in vacuum is normally incident on a thick copper block with $\sigma=5.8\times10^{7}\ \text{S/m}$ and $\mu=\mu_0$. Take $\epsilon_0=8.854\times10^{-12}\ \text{F/m}$ and $c=2.998\times10^8\ \text{m/s}$. Find the power absorbed per unit area of the block, which equals the Joule heating in the metal.
answer: 0.1517
check: (lambda n: 1000.0*(1 - abs((1 - n)/(1 + n))**2))(cmath.sqrt(1 + 1j*5.8e7/(8.854e-12*2*pi*3.0e9)))
tol: 1%
unit: W/m^2
hint: By energy conservation (Poynting's theorem), the absorbed power per area is the incident intensity times $1-R$.
hint: With $e^{-i\omega t}$, the metal has $\epsilon_{\rm eff}=\epsilon_0+i\sigma/\omega$, so $n=\sqrt{1+i\sigma/(\epsilon_0\omega)}\approx(1+i)/(k_0\delta)$ with $\delta=\sqrt{2/(\mu_0\sigma\omega)}$.
hint: For $|n|\gg1$ the absorbed fraction is $1-R\approx4\,\mathrm{Re}(1/n)=2k_0\delta=4\pi\delta/\lambda$.
solution: Here $\delta=\sqrt{2/(\mu_0\sigma\omega)}=1.21\ \mu\text{m}$ and $\lambda=9.99$ cm, so $2k_0\delta=4\pi\delta/\lambda=1.517\times10^{-4}$. The exact complex Fresnel reflectance $R=|(1-n)/(1+n)|^2$ gives the same fraction. The absorbed power is $(1.0\times10^3)(1.517\times10^{-4})\approx0.152\ \text{W/m}^2$. The Joule route agrees: $\int_0^\infty\tfrac12\sigma|tE_0|^2e^{-2z/\delta}dz=\tfrac14\sigma\delta|t|^2E_0^2$ with $|t|^2\approx2(k_0\delta)^2$ and $I=\tfrac12\epsilon_0cE_0^2$. The leading estimate has relative error of order $k_0\delta\approx8\times10^{-5}$ plus the neglect of $\omega\epsilon_0/\sigma\sim3\times10^{-9}$.

@exam self
prompt: A plane wave is described in Lorenz gauge by $A^\mu=a\,\epsilon^\mu\cos(k_\nu x^\nu)$ with $k^\mu=(\omega/c,\mathbf k)$, $k_\mu k^\mu=0$, and a constant real polarization four-vector $\epsilon^\mu=(0,\hat\epsilon)$ with $\hat\epsilon\cdot\mathbf k=0$ and $|\hat\epsilon|=1$ (signature $(+,-,-,-)$). (a) Verify the Lorenz condition and the wave equation. (b) Compute $F^{\mu\nu}$ in terms of $k^\mu$ and $\epsilon^\mu$ and show that both Lorentz invariants vanish. (c) Compute $T^{\mu\nu}$ and read off the energy density, the Poynting vector and the momentum flux, and verify $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0$. (d) Show that $\chi=b\sin(k_\nu x^\nu)$ preserves the Lorenz condition, changes $\epsilon^\mu$ by a multiple of $k^\mu$, and leaves $F^{\mu\nu}$ unchanged.
hint: Use $\partial_\mu\cos(k\cdot x)=-k_\mu\sin(k\cdot x)$, $k\cdot k=0$ and $k\cdot\epsilon=0$, with $\epsilon\cdot\epsilon=-1$.
hint: In $T^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{\mu_0}\left[F^{\mu\lambda}F_\lambda{}^\nu+\frac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}\right]$ the second term vanishes by part (b).
hint: Under $A^\mu\to A^\mu+\partial^\mu\chi$ the Lorenz condition changes by $\Box\chi$.
solution: (a) $\partial_\mu A^\mu=-a(\epsilon^\mu k_\mu)\sin(k\cdot x)=0$ because $\epsilon^\mu k_\mu=0-\hat\epsilon\cdot\mathbf k=0$, and $\Box A^\mu=-(k\cdot k)A^\mu=0$. (b) $F^{\mu\nu}=\partial^\mu A^\nu-\partial^\nu A^\mu=-a\,(k^\mu\epsilon^\nu-k^\nu\epsilon^\mu)\sin(k\cdot x)$. Then $F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=2a^2\left[(k\cdot k)(\epsilon\cdot\epsilon)-(k\cdot\epsilon)^2\right]\sin^2(k\cdot x)=0$, and the dual invariant $\propto\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B$ vanishes because $\mathbf E\perp\mathbf B$ (equivalently, its contraction contains $k$ twice). So $E=cB$ and $\mathbf E\perp\mathbf B$. (c) The second term of $T^{\mu\nu}$ vanishes, and $F^{\mu\lambda}F_\lambda{}^\nu=a^2\sin^2(k\cdot x)\left[-(\epsilon\cdot\epsilon)\,k^\mu k^\nu\right]=a^2\sin^2(k\cdot x)\,k^\mu k^\nu$ since the other terms contain $k\cdot k$ or $k\cdot\epsilon$. Hence $T^{\mu\nu}=\dfrac{a^2}{\mu_0}\sin^2(k\cdot x)\,k^\mu k^\nu$. Then $u=T^{00}=\epsilon_0a^2\omega^2\sin^2(k\cdot x)$, $\mathbf S=c\,T^{0i}=cu\,\hat k$ and the momentum flux is $T^{ij}=u\,\hat k^i\hat k^j$: energy and momentum travel at $c$ along $\hat k$. The divergence is $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=\dfrac{a^2}{\mu_0}k^\nu k^\mu\,2\sin\cos\,k_\mu=0$ since $k\cdot k=0$, as it must be with no sources. (d) With $\chi=b\sin(k\cdot x)$, $\Box\chi=-(k\cdot k)\chi=0$, so the condition is preserved. Also $A'^\mu=(a\epsilon^\mu+bk^\mu)\cos(k\cdot x)$, so $\epsilon^\mu\to\epsilon^\mu+(b/a)k^\mu$, and $F^{\mu\nu}$ is unchanged because $k^\mu k^\nu-k^\nu k^\mu=0$. Only the two components of $\epsilon$ transverse to $\mathbf k$ are physical.
rubric: Verifies $\partial_\mu A^\mu=0$ and $\Box A^\mu=0$ using $k\cdot\epsilon=0$ and $k\cdot k=0$.
rubric: Obtains $F^{\mu\nu}\propto k^\mu\epsilon^\nu-k^\nu\epsilon^\mu$ and shows both invariants vanish.
rubric: Obtains $T^{\mu\nu}\propto k^\mu k^\nu\sin^2(k\cdot x)$ with the correct energy density and flux, and verifies its divergence is zero.
rubric: Shows the gauge shift $\epsilon^\mu\to\epsilon^\mu+\lambda k^\mu$ preserves the Lorenz condition and $F^{\mu\nu}$.
