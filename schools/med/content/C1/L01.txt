@article
Living cells are mostly water, and almost all of the rest is built on carbon. Carbon forms four covalent bonds, so it can make chains, branches and rings that carry functional groups such as hydroxyl (-OH), carboxyl (-COOH), amino (-NH$_2$) and phosphate. Those groups give each molecule its polarity, charge and reactivity. Water matters just as much: its polarity lets it dissolve ions and polar molecules (**hydrophilic**) while pushing nonpolar molecules together (**hydrophobic** effect). That one idea explains how membranes form and how proteins fold.

### Polymers from monomers
Most macromolecules are **polymers**: long chains of similar **monomers**. Cells join monomers by **dehydration synthesis** (condensation), which removes one water molecule for every bond made. They break polymers apart by **hydrolysis**, which uses one water per bond broken. Both reactions are catalyzed by enzymes; building polymers costs energy, supplied by ATP or other activated intermediates, whereas hydrolysis is energetically favorable.

### Carbohydrates
Monosaccharides such as glucose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$) have the general formula $(\text{CH}_2\text{O})_n$. Two monosaccharides joined by a **glycosidic bond** make a disaccharide (sucrose, lactose, maltose). Polysaccharides store energy (starch in plants, **glycogen** in animals) or give structure (**cellulose** in plant walls, chitin in fungi and arthropods). Starch and glycogen use $\alpha$-glycosidic linkages that human enzymes can cut; cellulose uses $\beta$ linkages that they cannot, which is why cellulose is dietary fiber for us.

### Lipids
Lipids are defined by being mostly nonpolar, not by a shared monomer. A **triglyceride** is glycerol joined by ester bonds to three fatty acids. **Saturated** fatty acids have no carbon-carbon double bonds and pack tightly (solid at room temperature); **unsaturated** ones have double bonds, usually cis, that kink the chain. **Phospholipids** replace one fatty acid with a polar phosphate head, so they are **amphipathic** and assemble into bilayers. Steroids, including cholesterol, have four fused rings.

### Proteins
A protein is a chain of amino acids joined by **peptide bonds** (an amide bond between the carboxyl carbon of one residue and the amino nitrogen of the next). A chain of $n$ residues has $n-1$ peptide bonds, with a free amino (N) terminus and a free carboxyl (C) terminus.
- **Primary:** the amino acid sequence.
- **Secondary:** local $\alpha$-helices and $\beta$-sheets held by hydrogen bonds between backbone C=O and N-H groups.
- **Tertiary:** the overall 3-D fold, stabilized by hydrophobic packing, ionic bonds, hydrogen bonds and disulfide bonds between cysteines.
- **Quaternary:** association of more than one chain, as in hemoglobin ($\alpha_2\beta_2$).

Because sequence determines folding, a single substitution can change function, as in sickle cell disease, where valine replaces glutamate at position 6 of the $\beta$-globin chain.

### Nucleic acids
Nucleotides contain a pentose sugar, a phosphate and a nitrogenous base. DNA uses deoxyribose and the bases A, G, C, T; RNA uses ribose and A, G, C, U. Nucleotides are linked by **phosphodiester bonds** between the 3' hydroxyl of one sugar and the 5' phosphate of the next, so every strand has 5' and 3' ends. In DNA the two strands run **antiparallel** and pair A with T (two hydrogen bonds) and G with C (three). **Chargaff's rules** follow: in double-stranded DNA, $\%A = \%T$ and $\%G = \%C$, so $\%A + \%G = 50\%$.

@example
title: Bond and water bookkeeping in a polypeptide
problem: A protein chain contains 120 amino acids. How many peptide bonds does it have, how many water molecules were released when it was made, and how many are used if it is completely hydrolyzed into free amino acids?
step: Joining $n$ residues in one chain needs $n - 1$ bonds, because the first residue makes no bond on its free end. So there are $120 - 1 = 119$ peptide bonds.
step: Each dehydration synthesis releases one water molecule, so making the chain released $119$ water molecules.
step: Hydrolysis reverses each bond using one water, so complete hydrolysis consumes $119$ water molecules and gives $120$ free amino acids.
step: Check the termini: a single unbranched chain has exactly one free amino end and one free carboxyl end, regardless of length.
@example
title: Building a disaccharide
problem: Two glucose molecules ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$) join by a glycosidic bond to form maltose. What is the molecular formula of maltose?
step: Add the two molecules: $\text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{24}\text{O}_{12}$.
step: Dehydration synthesis removes one water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$), so subtract $2$ H and $1$ O.
step: Maltose is $\text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{22}\text{O}_{11}$. The same bookkeeping applies to every condensation reaction.
@mistakes
- Saying a chain of $n$ monomers has $n$ bonds; a linear polymer has $n-1$.
- Thinking hydrolysis releases water; it consumes water, and dehydration synthesis releases it.
- Confusing the secondary structure (backbone hydrogen bonds) with the tertiary structure (interactions among side chains).
- Forgetting that DNA strands are antiparallel, so a 5' end pairs with a 3' end.
@practice num
prompt: A polypeptide is made of 50 amino acids joined in a single unbranched chain. How many peptide bonds does it contain?
answer: 49
check: 50 - 1
hint: Count the links between neighbors, not the residues.
hint: A chain of $n$ residues has $n - 1$ links.
solution: Fifty residues in one chain are joined by $50 - 1 = 49$ peptide bonds.
@practice num
prompt: In a sample of double-stranded DNA, 30% of the bases are adenine. What percentage of the bases are guanine?
answer: 20
check: (100 - 2 * 30) / 2
unit: %
hint: Chargaff's rules say $\%T = \%A$.
hint: A and T together make up $60\%$; the remainder is split equally between G and C.
solution: $\%T = 30\%$, so $\%A + \%T = 60\%$. The remaining $40\%$ is G plus C, split equally, so $\%G = 20\%$.

@quiz mc
prompt: Which level of protein structure is defined by hydrogen bonds between backbone carbonyl and amide groups that produce $\alpha$-helices and $\beta$-sheets?
choice: Primary structure
choice*: Secondary structure
choice: Tertiary structure
choice: Quaternary structure
hint: Think about which level involves only the backbone and not the side chains.
solution: Local backbone hydrogen bonding that forms helices and sheets is secondary structure. Primary is the sequence, tertiary is the full fold stabilized largely by side-chain interactions, and quaternary is subunit assembly.
