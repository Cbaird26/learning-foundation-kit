@article
**Photosynthesis** is the reverse of respiration in terms of overall chemistry:
$$6\,\text{CO}_2 + 6\,\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{light} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\,\text{O}_2.$$
It occurs in **chloroplasts**. The **thylakoid membranes** (stacked into grana) carry the light reactions; the **stroma** is the fluid in which the Calvin cycle runs. Chlorophyll a and accessory pigments absorb mostly red and blue light and reflect green.

### Light reactions (thylakoid membrane)
Light energy is $E = hc/\lambda$, with $h = 6.626\times10^{-34}$ J s and $c = 2.998\times10^{8}$ m/s; shorter wavelengths carry more energy per photon.

In **noncyclic electron flow**:
1. **Photosystem II** (reaction center P680) absorbs light and loses an electron. Water is split to replace it, releasing **O$_2$** and protons into the thylakoid lumen. All the O$_2$ comes from water.
2. Electrons pass through plastoquinone, the cytochrome $b_6f$ complex and plastocyanin, pumping protons into the lumen.
3. **Photosystem I** (P700) absorbs light and re-energizes the electrons, which reduce NADP$^+$ to **NADPH**.
4. The proton gradient drives **ATP synthase**, making ATP (**photophosphorylation**, as in the chemiosmosis of week 5 but with lumen as the high-proton side).

**Cyclic electron flow** around photosystem I makes only ATP, adjusting the ATP:NADPH ratio.

### The Calvin cycle (stroma)
The light reactions supply ATP and NADPH; the Calvin cycle uses them to fix carbon. It has three phases:
1. **Carbon fixation:** **rubisco** attaches CO$_2$ to ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP, 5 C), giving two 3-phosphoglycerate (3 C) per CO$_2$.
2. **Reduction:** 3-phosphoglycerate is phosphorylated (ATP) and reduced (NADPH) to glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P).
3. **Regeneration:** most G3P is used to regenerate RuBP, using more ATP.

For every **3 CO$_2$** fixed, the cycle makes one net G3P and uses **9 ATP and 6 NADPH**, that is 3 ATP and 2 NADPH per CO$_2$. Two G3P can be joined to make a glucose, so one glucose needs 6 CO$_2$, **18 ATP and 12 NADPH**.

### Photorespiration, C$_4$ and CAM
Rubisco can also use O$_2$, which wastes carbon and energy (**photorespiration**), worse when stomata close in hot, dry air. **C$_4$ plants** (maize, sugarcane) first fix CO$_2$ in mesophyll cells with PEP carboxylase into a four-carbon acid, then release it near rubisco in bundle-sheath cells. **CAM plants** (cacti) open stomata at night, store the four-carbon acid, and run the Calvin cycle by day with stomata closed.

### Comparing the two pathways
Photosynthesis stores light energy in sugar; respiration releases it as ATP. The chloroplast and mitochondrion both use electron transport and chemiosmosis, but photosynthesis builds a gradient across the thylakoid membrane using light, while respiration builds one across the inner mitochondrial membrane using NADH and FADH$_2$.

When accounting for ATP yield in respiration, remember that cytosolic NADH from glycolysis cannot cross the inner membrane. The malate-aspartate shuttle (heart, liver) keeps the electrons as NADH in the matrix (2.5 ATP each). The glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle (skeletal muscle, brain) passes them to FADH$_2$ (1.5 ATP each), so the yield is lower.

@example
title: Energy cost of making one triose phosphate
problem: How many ATP and NADPH does the Calvin cycle consume to fix 3 CO$_2$ into one net G3P?
step: Each CO$_2$ fixed needs one turn of the cycle, so there are 3 turns.
step: Each turn uses 2 ATP in the reduction phase for the 2 molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate formed and 1 ATP in regeneration, for 3 ATP per turn. It uses 2 NADPH per turn in the reduction phase.
step: Over 3 turns: $3 \times 3 = 9$ ATP and $3 \times 2 = 6$ NADPH.
@mistakes
- Saying the O$_2$ released comes from CO$_2$; it comes from the splitting of water in photosystem II.
- Calling the Calvin cycle "light-independent" and assuming it can run for long in the dark; it depends on ATP and NADPH made in the light.
- Thinking rubisco only fixes CO$_2$; it can also react with O$_2$.
- Counting all cytosolic NADH as worth 2.5 ATP regardless of the shuttle used.
