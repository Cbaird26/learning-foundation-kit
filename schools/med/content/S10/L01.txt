@article
Psychiatry has no blood test that confirms most diagnoses, so the **interview** and the **mental status examination (MSE)** are the core of the physical examination. The history tells you how the illness unfolded over time. The MSE is a structured snapshot of the patient's mental functioning during this encounter, like a set of vital signs for the mind. Both are written in precise language so another clinician can picture the patient.

### The psychiatric history

A complete assessment includes: identifying data, **chief complaint** (ideally in the patient's own words), **history of present illness** (onset, course, stressors, symptom clusters, functional impact), **past psychiatric history** (diagnoses, hospitalizations, medication trials, prior suicide attempts or self-harm, violence), **substance use**, **medical history and medications**, **family history** (psychiatric illness, suicide, substance use), and **social and developmental history** (education, work, relationships, trauma, legal problems, housing, supports). Always ask about suicidal and homicidal thoughts; asking does not plant the idea or raise risk. Seek **collateral information** from family, records or other clinicians when a patient is psychotic, confused, a child or unable to give a reliable account, and when safety is in question.

Rule out medical contributors in every new presentation: thyroid disease, B12 deficiency, anemia, electrolyte and glucose abnormalities, infection, seizure, medication effects and intoxication or withdrawal.

### The MSE domains

| Domain | What you describe |
|---|---|
| Appearance | Grooming, dress, apparent age, hygiene, eye contact |
| Behavior | Psychomotor agitation or retardation, tremor, tics, catatonia, cooperation |
| Speech | Rate, rhythm, volume, amount; pressured, poverty of speech, dysarthria |
| Mood | The patient's **subjective** sustained emotional state, quoted ("I feel empty") |
| Affect | The clinician's **objective** observation of emotional expression: range (full, restricted, blunted, flat), intensity, reactivity, congruence with mood and content |
| Thought process | Form and flow: linear, circumstantial, tangential, loosening of associations, flight of ideas, thought blocking, perseveration |
| Thought content | What the patient thinks about: delusions, obsessions, phobias, preoccupations, suicidal or homicidal ideation |
| Perception | Hallucinations (perception without a stimulus), illusions (misperception of a real stimulus), depersonalization, derealization |
| Cognition | Alertness, orientation, attention, memory, abstraction; screened with the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) or Mini-Mental State Examination |
| Insight and judgment | Understanding of illness; capacity to make sound decisions |

**Mood is what the patient says; affect is what you see.** A patient who says "I'm fine" while crying has an affect incongruent with the stated mood. **Process versus content:** a patient with linear thoughts can still hold a delusion, and a patient with disorganized process may have no delusion at all.

Process terms worth memorizing: **circumstantial** thinking includes excessive detail but eventually reaches the point; **tangential** thinking drifts away and never returns; **loosening of associations** has illogical connections between ideas; **flight of ideas** is rapid, goal-directed-seeming shifts linked by sounds or themes, typical of mania; **word salad** is incoherent speech; **thought blocking** is an abrupt halt in the stream of speech.

### Delirium, dementia or psychiatric illness?

**Delirium** is acute, fluctuating and defined by disturbed attention and awareness with a medical cause, so inattention (for example, difficulty reciting months backward) is the key MSE sign. Visual hallucinations are common. **Dementia** is gradual, with attention preserved until late and memory predominantly affected. Primary psychiatric illness usually leaves alertness and orientation intact.

### Screening tools

Common instruments are the **PHQ-9** (depression), **GAD-7** (anxiety), **AUDIT-C** (alcohol) and the **Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale** screener. A screen is not a diagnosis. For a screen, **sensitivity** is $\frac{TP}{TP+FN}$, **specificity** is $\frac{TN}{TN+FP}$ and **positive predictive value** is $\frac{TP}{TP+FP}$, which depends strongly on prevalence.

@example
title: Mood, affect, process and content from one excerpt
problem: A 34-year-old woman says, slowly and quietly, "I'm okay, just tired of everything." She smiles briefly when you ask about her daughter, then looks away and tears up. Asked how she got to clinic, she describes the bus route in great detail, each transfer and each stop, before finally saying she took bus 12. Which mood, affect and thought-process terms apply?
step: Mood is the patient's own word: "okay, just tired of everything." Record it in quotes.
step: Affect is your observation: she shows a brief reactive smile and then tearfulness, so the range is not flat; describe it as constricted or dysphoric and note it is mood-congruent with "tired of everything" more than with "okay."
step: Speech is slow and quiet (decreased rate and volume).
step: Her description of the bus route included excessive detail but she reached the answer. That is circumstantial thought process, not tangential.

@mistakes
- Writing a diagnosis ("depressed") in the mood field instead of the patient's own words.
- Calling thought process "delusional" or thought content "disorganized"; process is form, content is what the thoughts are about.
- Skipping suicide and violence inquiry because the patient seems calm, or forgetting collateral for a patient who is confused.
- Treating a positive screen as a diagnosis, or reading predictive value without regard to how common the disorder is.

check: 54 / 60

prompt: A 76-year-old woman admitted with pneumonia becomes confused over two days. She is drowsy in the morning and alert but disoriented in the afternoon, cannot repeat the months backward, and sees insects on the bed. Which MSE finding best supports delirium rather than primary psychosis?
