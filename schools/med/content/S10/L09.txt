@article
Neurodevelopmental disorders begin in the early developmental period and impair personal, social, academic or occupational functioning. Genetic and prenatal factors are major contributors, and affected children often have more than one condition. Assessment uses information from parents, teachers and the child, developmental history, a physical and neurologic examination, and standardized rating scales. Pediatric psychiatry also pays special attention to safety, family systems and the school.

### ADHD

**Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder** requires a persistent pattern of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity. Children need **six or more** symptoms in a domain (adolescents 17 and older and adults need five), lasting **at least 6 months**, with several symptoms present **before age 12**, present in **two or more settings** (home, school), and interfering with functioning. Presentations: predominantly inattentive, predominantly hyperactive-impulsive, or combined. Rule out hearing or vision problems, sleep disorders, anxiety, learning disorders, lead exposure, thyroid disease and substance use.

**Treatment:** stimulants (methylphenidate and amphetamine formulations) are first-line for school-aged children, adolescents and adults and have large effect sizes; they increase dopamine and norepinephrine signaling in prefrontal circuits. Adverse effects: decreased appetite, weight loss, insomnia, irritability, increased heart rate and blood pressure, tics in a minority, and potential for misuse. Ask about syncope, exertional chest pain and family history of sudden death before starting; monitor growth, blood pressure and heart rate. Non-stimulants are **atomoxetine** (norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor) and the alpha-2 agonists **guanfacine** and **clonidine** (sedation, hypotension, rebound hypertension if abruptly stopped). For preschool children (ages 4 to 5), **behavioral parent training** is first-line.

### Autism spectrum disorder

Persistent deficits in **social communication and social interaction** across three areas (social-emotional reciprocity, nonverbal communication, developing and maintaining relationships) plus **restricted, repetitive patterns** of behavior, interests or activities (at least two of four: stereotyped movements or speech, insistence on sameness, highly restricted fixated interests, sensory hyper- or hyporeactivity). Symptoms begin in early development. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends developmental surveillance at every visit and autism screening with a tool such as the **M-CHAT-R/F at 18 and 24 months**. Evaluate hearing, consider genetic testing, and refer promptly for early intensive behavioral and developmental intervention, speech and occupational therapy. Vaccines do not cause autism. Risperidone and aripiprazole are approved for irritability associated with autism, with metabolic monitoring.

### Intellectual disability and learning disorders

**Intellectual disability** involves deficits in intellectual functioning (reasoning, learning, abstract thinking, confirmed by clinical assessment and standardized testing, with IQ approximately two standard deviations below the mean, about 70 or below) and **adaptive functioning** deficits, with onset in the developmental period. Severity (mild, moderate, severe, profound) is graded by adaptive functioning rather than IQ alone. Common causes include Down syndrome, fragile X syndrome and fetal alcohol spectrum disorder. **Specific learning disorder** involves persistent difficulty with reading, written expression or mathematics despite intervention.

### Tic disorders

**Tourette disorder:** at least two motor tics and at least one vocal tic, present for more than 1 year (not necessarily together), onset before age 18. Comorbid ADHD and OCD are common. Treatment begins with education and **comprehensive behavioral intervention for tics (CBIT)**; alpha-2 agonists, then antipsychotics such as aripiprazole or risperidone for severe tics.

### Disruptive, impulse-control and mood disorders in children

- **Oppositional defiant disorder:** angry or irritable mood, argumentative or defiant behavior, or vindictiveness lasting at least 6 months.
- **Conduct disorder:** repetitive violation of the rights of others or major societal norms, with at least three of 15 criteria in the past 12 months and one in the past 6 months (aggression to people and animals, destruction of property, deceitfulness or theft, serious rule violations). Conduct disorder before age 15 is required for later antisocial personality disorder.
- **Disruptive mood dysregulation disorder (DMDD):** severe recurrent temper outbursts (at least three per week) out of proportion to the situation, with persistently irritable or angry mood between outbursts, for at least 12 months, ages 6 to 18, onset before age 10. Children with DMDD are more likely to develop depressive and anxiety disorders than bipolar disorder.
- **Separation anxiety disorder** and **selective mutism** are childhood anxiety disorders. **Enuresis** after age 5 is treated with alarms and, secondarily, desmopressin.
- **Depression in youth:** fluoxetine and escitalopram have FDA approval for adolescents, with boxed-warning monitoring for suicidality.

### Child maltreatment

Physicians are **mandated reporters**: report reasonable suspicion of abuse or neglect to child protective services; certainty is not required. Suspicious features include injuries inconsistent with history or developmental stage, patterned bruising, bruising in infants who do not yet cruise, and delay in seeking care. Document objectively, ensure safety and involve a child protection team.

### Number needed to treat

$NNT = \frac{1}{\text{ARR}}$, where ARR is the absolute difference in event rates (for example, response in treatment minus response in control).

@example
title: ADHD or something else?
problem: A 9-year-old boy is described by his teacher as inattentive and fidgety since first grade. His parents note the same at home. He snores loudly, sleeps poorly, and his grades are declining. What should be done before prescribing a stimulant?
step: Symptoms for more than 6 months, in two settings, with onset before 12, support ADHD.
step: Snoring and poor sleep suggest a sleep disorder, which can mimic ADHD; consider adenotonsillar enlargement and obstructive sleep apnea.
step: Screen for hearing and vision problems, anxiety, learning problems and family stressors, and check cardiac history and vital signs.
step: Treat contributing conditions, then use behavioral therapy and a stimulant if ADHD persists.

@mistakes
- Diagnosing ADHD from a single setting or without teacher input.
- Starting a stimulant without asking about syncope, chest pain with exertion or family sudden death.
- Mistaking DMDD for bipolar disorder; bipolar requires distinct episodes, not chronic irritability.
- Delaying a report of suspected abuse until certainty is achieved.

@practice num
prompt: In a hypothetical trial, 45% of children improved with parent training and 30% improved with waitlist control. What is the number needed to treat, rounded to one decimal place?
answer: 6.7
check: 1 / (0.45 - 0.30)
tol: 0.05
hint: Compute the absolute risk reduction (as a decimal), then take its reciprocal.
solution: ARR = 0.45 - 0.30 = 0.15, NNT = 1/0.15 = 6.7.

@practice self
prompt: A 5-year-old girl is brought by her mother, who reports a bruise in the shape of a hand on the girl's upper arm and says the child fell off a bed. The girl is withdrawn and the explanation changes. Explain your obligations and the steps you would take.
hint: Think of safety, documentation, reporting and the team.
solution: Examine the child fully with a chaperone, document the injury objectively with photographs and quotes, and compare the history with the developmental stage and the injury pattern. Obtain history separately from each caregiver if possible. A physician who has reasonable suspicion of abuse or neglect is a mandated reporter and must report to child protective services without proof, even if the parent objects. Involve the child protection team, ensure the child's immediate safety (hospital admission if the home is unsafe), consider imaging such as a skeletal survey for young children, and explain the report to the family honestly and nonjudgmentally.
rubric: States that reasonable suspicion, not certainty, triggers a mandated report.
rubric: Includes objective documentation, a safety plan and the team approach.
rubric: Addresses communication with the family respectfully.
