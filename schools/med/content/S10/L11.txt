@article
Psychotropic drugs alter neurotransmission in receptors, transporters and enzymes. Predicting adverse effects from receptor pharmacology (the **receptor profile**) is more useful than memorizing lists. Doses given here are not needed; check current references before prescribing.

### Antidepressants

- **SSRIs** (sertraline, escitalopram, fluoxetine, paroxetine, citalopram, fluvoxamine) block the serotonin transporter (SERT). Adverse effects: GI upset, sexual dysfunction, hyponatremia (SIADH), bleeding risk, QT prolongation (citalopram), discontinuation syndrome, **serotonin syndrome**. Paroxetine has the most anticholinergic effect and shortest half-life; fluoxetine has the longest (active metabolite norfluoxetine) and inhibits CYP2D6 strongly, as does paroxetine.
- **SNRIs** (venlafaxine, duloxetine): also block norepinephrine reuptake; can raise blood pressure; useful in neuropathic pain.
- **Bupropion** (norepinephrine–dopamine reuptake inhibitor): no sexual dysfunction, weight neutral; lowers the seizure threshold, so it is contraindicated in seizure disorder, bulimia nervosa/anorexia nervosa and abrupt alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal; aids smoking cessation.
- **Mirtazapine** (alpha-2 and 5-HT2/5-HT3 antagonist, strong H1 antagonist): sedation, increased appetite and weight gain; helpful in insomnia and low appetite.
- **Trazodone:** sedating; priapism rarely.
- **Tricyclic antidepressants** (amitriptyline, nortriptyline, clomipramine): block reuptake of serotonin and norepinephrine plus muscarinic, H1 and alpha-1 receptors, causing anticholinergic effects, sedation, orthostasis. **Overdose is dangerous**: sodium-channel blockade causes QRS widening, arrhythmia and seizures, treated with **sodium bicarbonate**.
- **MAOIs** (phenelzine, tranylcypromine, selegiline): block monoamine oxidase. **Hypertensive crisis** with tyramine-rich foods (aged cheese, cured meats) or sympathomimetics, and **serotonin syndrome** with serotonergic drugs. Allow a washout of **2 weeks** after stopping an MAOI before a serotonergic drug and 2 weeks the other way around, and **5 weeks after fluoxetine** because of its long half-life.

### Antipsychotics

All block D2 receptors; side effects reflect other receptors: **H1** (sedation, weight gain), **M1** (dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision), **alpha-1** (orthostasis), **5-HT2C** (weight gain). **Low-potency** first-generation drugs (chlorpromazine) are more sedating and anticholinergic; **high-potency** agents (haloperidol) cause more extrapyramidal symptoms. Second-generation agents combine D2 and 5-HT2A blockade and cause fewer movement disorders but more metabolic effects; aripiprazole is a D2 partial agonist (akathisia possible). Serious: neuroleptic malignant syndrome, tardive dyskinesia, QT prolongation, agranulocytosis and myocarditis (clozapine), metabolic syndrome, and increased mortality in older adults with dementia (boxed warning).

### Mood stabilizers

Lithium (narrow index; nephrogenic DI, hypothyroidism, tremor, teratogenic), valproate (hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis, neural tube defects), carbamazepine (agranulocytosis, Stevens–Johnson syndrome, CYP3A4 induction, SIADH), lamotrigine (Stevens–Johnson syndrome). See Lesson 3.

### Anxiolytics and hypnotics

- **Benzodiazepines** (lorazepam, diazepam, alprazolam, clonazepam) bind GABA-A receptors and **increase the frequency of chloride-channel opening**; **barbiturates** increase the duration of opening and are more dangerous in overdose. Benzodiazepine adverse effects: sedation, falls, anterograde amnesia, paradoxical disinhibition, respiratory depression especially with opioids or alcohol, dependence and **withdrawal seizures**. **Lorazepam, oxazepam and temazepam** are glucuronidated and have no active metabolites, so they are preferred in liver disease and older adults. **Flumazenil** reverses benzodiazepines but can precipitate seizures in dependent patients.
- **Buspirone:** 5-HT1A partial agonist; non-sedating, no dependence, delayed onset.
- **Z-drugs** (zolpidem) act at GABA-A; complex sleep behaviors; **orexin antagonists** (suvorexant); **ramelteon** (melatonin agonist).

### Stimulants and others

Methylphenidate and amphetamine increase synaptic dopamine and norepinephrine: appetite loss, insomnia, tachycardia, misuse. **Atomoxetine** and alpha-2 agonists (guanfacine) are non-stimulants. **Naltrexone** and **acamprosate** treat alcohol use disorder; **buprenorphine** and **methadone** treat opioid use disorder. **Esketamine** (nasal NMDA antagonist) is used for treatment-resistant depression under supervision.

### Half-life and washout

For first-order elimination, the fraction remaining after $n$ half-lives is $\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^n$. Steady state is reached after roughly 4 to 5 half-lives and most of a drug is eliminated after the same interval.

@example
title: Switching from fluoxetine
problem: A patient taking fluoxetine does not respond and the physician wants to switch to phenelzine. Why can't phenelzine be started the next day, and what washout is required?
step: Fluoxetine and its active metabolite norfluoxetine have long half-lives, so serotonergic activity persists for weeks.
step: Combining a serotonin reuptake inhibitor with an MAOI risks serotonin syndrome (hyperthermia, clonus, autonomic instability).
step: The standard washout is 5 weeks after fluoxetine before starting an MAOI (2 weeks for most other antidepressants).
step: Alternatively, choose a non-MAOI option to avoid a long gap; counsel the patient about tyramine dietary restrictions if an MAOI is used.

@mistakes
- Combining an MAOI with an SSRI, SNRI, meperidine, tramadol or sympathomimetics.
- Giving bupropion to someone with an eating disorder or seizure risk.
- Stopping benzodiazepines abruptly in a dependent patient.
- Using tricyclic antidepressants in a patient at risk of overdose without considering lethality.

@practice num
prompt: A medication has a half-life of 12 hours. If a dose is stopped, what percentage of the drug remains after 36 hours? Assume first-order elimination.
answer: 12.5
check: 100 * 0.5 ** (36 / 12)
tol: 0.1
unit: %
hint: 36 hours is how many half-lives?
solution: 36/12 = 3 half-lives; (1/2)^3 = 1/8 = 12.5 percent.
