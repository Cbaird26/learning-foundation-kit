@review
### Key ideas by week

1. **Evolution.** Evolution is change in allele frequency in populations. Selection needs heritable variation and differential reproduction. Relative fitness $w$ and $s = 1 - w$ measure selection.
2. **Population genetics.** Hardy–Weinberg: $p + q = 1$, $p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$. Incidence is $q^2$, carriers are $2pq$. Drift, gene flow, mutation and selection break equilibrium. Prezygotic and postzygotic barriers produce species.
3. **Microbes.** Prokaryotes have $70$S ribosomes and peptidoglycan; Gram stain reflects wall structure. Growth is $N = N_0 2^{t/g}$. Gene transfer: transformation, transduction, conjugation. Fungi have chitin and ergosterol.
4. **Plants and animals.** $\Psi = \Psi_s + \Psi_p$; water moves to lower $\Psi$. Animals are classified by symmetry, germ layers, coelom and development. Selective toxicity depends on features absent from human cells.
5. **Tissues and homeostasis.** Four tissue types. Negative feedback has a sensor, control center, effector and response. Fever is a raised set point.
6. **Nervous system.** Nernst potential, action potential phases, myelin, synapses, reflex arcs.
7. **Endocrine.** Peptide versus steroid hormones, pituitary axes, the thyroid loop (TSH and free T$_4$ patterns), insulin and glucagon.
8. **Musculoskeletal.** Bone remodeling, PTH and calcium, corrected calcium, sliding-filament contraction and the role of ATP.
9. **Circulation and respiration.** Path of blood, $\text{CO} = \text{HR}\times\text{SV}$, MAP, A–a gradient, right and left shifts of the oxygen-hemoglobin curve.
10. **Digestion and excretion.** Enzymes and sites, absorption routes, nephron segments, clearance $UV/P$, filtered load.
11. **Immunity.** Innate versus adaptive, MHC I with CD8 and MHC II with CD4, antibody classes, herd immunity $1 - 1/R_0$.
12. **Reproduction.** Gametogenesis, the menstrual cycle, ovulation about cycle length minus $14$, germ layers.

### Common errors

Using incidence as $q$; forgetting the $2$ in $2pq$; saying pulmonary veins carry deoxygenated blood; mixing up afferent and efferent; confusing osteoblasts and osteoclasts; forgetting to convert units in clearance; saying innate immunity has memory; assuming ovulation is always on day $14$.

@exam num
prompt: A recessive disease affects $1$ in $10{,}000$ births in a population in Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium. Two unrelated, unaffected people from this population have a child. Assuming each is a carrier with probability $2pq$, what is the probability that the child is affected, that is, that both are carriers and both transmit the recessive allele? Give the answer as a decimal rounded to $3$ significant figures.
answer: 9.8e-5
check: (2 * 0.01 * 0.99)**2 / 4
tol: 1%
hint: Find $q$ from $q^2 = 1/10{,}000$.
hint: Carrier probability is $2pq = 0.0198$ for each person. Both must be carriers.
hint: If both are carriers, the chance of an affected child is $1/4$.
solution: $q = 0.01$, $p = 0.99$, and $2pq = 0.0198$. Probability both are carriers is $0.0198^2 = 3.92 \times 10^{-4}$. Multiply by $1/4$: $9.80 \times 10^{-5}$.

prompt: A healthy adult has a cardiac output of $5400$ mL/min. The kidneys receive $20\%$ of cardiac output as blood flow, the hematocrit is $0.45$, and the GFR is $120$ mL/min. Calculate the filtration fraction (GFR divided by renal plasma flow) as a decimal, rounded to $3$ decimal places.
answer: 0.202
check: 120 / (5400 * 0.20 * (1 - 0.45))
tol: 2%
hint: Renal blood flow is $20\%$ of cardiac output.
hint: Plasma is the fraction $1 - \text{hematocrit}$ of blood.
solution: Renal blood flow $= 0.20 \times 5400 = 1080$ mL/min. Renal plasma flow $= 1080 \times 0.55 = 594$ mL/min. Filtration fraction $= 120/594 \approx 0.202$.

prompt: A $55$-year-old man with poorly controlled type 2 diabetes (glucose $320$ mg/dL) has white plaques in his mouth that scrape off to leave red mucosa. A scraping shows budding yeast and pseudohyphae. Which statement best explains why an azole such as fluconazole can treat this infection with fewer effects on his own cells than amphotericin B?
choice: Azoles inhibit peptidoglycan cross-linking, which the fungus needs for its wall.
choice*: Azoles inhibit synthesis of ergosterol, the fungal membrane sterol, so the target differs from human cholesterol synthesis.
choice: Azoles bind the $30$S ribosomal subunit, which humans lack.
choice: Azoles increase insulin secretion, which restores immunity.
hint: Which sterol is in fungal membranes?
solution: Fungi are eukaryotes with chitin walls and ergosterol membranes. Azoles inhibit the fungal enzymes that make ergosterol, a more selective target than binding the sterol itself, which also affects cholesterol-containing human membranes.
