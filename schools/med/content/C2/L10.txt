@article
The digestive system breaks food into molecules small enough to absorb, and the excretory system maintains fluid, electrolyte and acid-base balance while eliminating metabolic wastes.

### Digestion

Digestion is **mechanical** (chewing, churning) and **chemical** (enzymes).

- **Mouth**: salivary amylase begins starch digestion; lingual lipase begins fat digestion.
- **Stomach**: parietal cells secrete HCl and intrinsic factor (needed for vitamin B$_{12}$ absorption); chief cells secrete pepsinogen, activated to **pepsin** by acid, which digests proteins. Gastrin stimulates acid secretion. Mucus protects the lining.
- **Small intestine** (duodenum, jejunum, ileum): most digestion and absorption occur here. The pancreas supplies bicarbonate, which neutralizes acid, and enzymes (amylase, lipase, proteases such as trypsin). The liver makes **bile**, stored in the gallbladder, which **emulsifies** fat to increase the surface area for lipase. Brush-border enzymes finish the job. **Secretin** (acid in duodenum) stimulates bicarbonate release, and **cholecystokinin (CCK)** (fat and protein) stimulates gallbladder contraction and pancreatic enzymes.
- **Absorption**: villi and microvilli greatly increase the surface area. Carbohydrates are absorbed as monosaccharides and amino acids go into capillaries, which drain through the **hepatic portal vein** to the liver. Fats are absorbed as micelles, reassembled as chylomicrons and enter lymphatic **lacteals**. The terminal ileum absorbs bile salts and B$_{12}$.
- **Large intestine**: absorbs water and electrolytes and houses the gut microbiota.

### The kidney and nephron

Each kidney has about a million **nephrons**. Blood is filtered in the **glomerulus** into Bowman's capsule, forming a protein-free filtrate. The **glomerular filtration rate (GFR)** is normally about $90$–$120$ mL/min. The filtrate then passes through the **proximal tubule** (reabsorbs about two-thirds of filtered sodium and water, nearly all glucose and amino acids), the **loop of Henle** (descending limb permeable to water; thick ascending limb pumps out Na$^+$, K$^+$ and Cl$^-$ and is the site of loop diuretics), the **distal tubule** and the **collecting duct**. **ADH** inserts aquaporins in the collecting duct so water can be reabsorbed into the hypertonic medulla, concentrating urine. **Aldosterone** increases Na$^+$ reabsorption and K$^+$ secretion. The kidney also makes **erythropoietin** and activates vitamin D.

### Clearance

The **clearance** of a substance is the volume of plasma cleared of it per unit time:
$$C = \frac{U \times V}{P},$$
where $U$ and $P$ are urine and plasma concentrations and $V$ is the urine flow rate. Creatinine, which is filtered and only slightly secreted, approximates GFR. The **filtered load** of a substance is GFR times its plasma concentration. Glucose appears in urine only when its filtered load exceeds the maximum transport capacity of the proximal tubule.

### One meal, one pathway

A carbohydrate meal is digested to glucose, absorbed in the small intestine, carried by the portal vein to the liver, and distributed in the blood; insulin promotes uptake. Glucose is filtered at the glomerulus but is normally fully reabsorbed in the proximal tubule. Seeing this chain is the basis of the project.

@example
title: Calculating clearance
problem: A patient's urine creatinine concentration is $48$ mg/dL, urine flow is $2.5$ mL/min, and plasma creatinine is $1.0$ mg/dL. Estimate GFR.
step: Use $C = U \times V / P$. The units of $U$ and $P$ are the same, so they cancel.
step: $C = \dfrac{48 \times 2.5}{1.0}$.
step: $C = 120$ mL/min.
step: This is a normal GFR, assuming creatinine is freely filtered and not secreted.

@mistakes
- Saying that most digestion and absorption occur in the stomach; they occur in the small intestine.
- Saying that fats are absorbed into the portal blood; chylomicrons enter lymphatics first.
- Confusing filtration with excretion: most filtered water and solutes are reabsorbed.
- Mixing up units in clearance; keep $U$ and $P$ in the same units and $V$ in mL/min.

@practice self
prompt: Trace one red blood cell through the heart and lungs, starting as it returns from the body and ending as it leaves the heart. Name each chamber, valve and vessel in order, and state where the cell picks up oxygen and gives up carbon dioxide.
solution: The cell enters the right atrium from the superior or inferior vena cava, passes the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle, then crosses the pulmonary valve into the pulmonary artery. It travels through pulmonary capillaries around the alveoli, where CO$_2$ leaves and O$_2$ binds hemoglobin. It returns by the pulmonary veins to the left atrium, crosses the mitral valve into the left ventricle, then the aortic valve into the aorta, and leaves the heart.
