@article
The **nervous system** senses the environment, integrates information and commands effectors. The **central nervous system** (CNS) is the brain and spinal cord; the **peripheral nervous system** (PNS) carries information to and from it. The PNS has an afferent (sensory) division and an efferent (motor) division; the efferent side is somatic (skeletal muscle) or autonomic (sympathetic and parasympathetic).

### Membrane potential

Neurons maintain a **resting membrane potential** of about $-70$ mV, set by the Na$^+$/K$^+$-ATPase, which pumps $3$ Na$^+$ out and $2$ K$^+$ in, and by leak channels that make the membrane much more permeable to K$^+$ than Na$^+$ at rest. The **equilibrium potential** for an ion is the voltage at which its electrical and chemical gradients balance. For a monovalent cation at body temperature (about $37\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$),
$$E_{\text{ion}} \approx 61.5\,\text{mV} \cdot \log_{10}\frac{[\text{ion}]_{\text{out}}}{[\text{ion}]_{\text{in}}}.$$
Because K$^+$ is concentrated inside, $E_K$ is strongly negative (about $-90$ mV); Na$^+$ is concentrated outside, so $E_{Na}$ is positive (about $+60$ mV).

### The action potential

A stimulus that depolarizes the membrane to **threshold** (about $-55$ mV) opens **voltage-gated Na$^+$ channels**. Na$^+$ rushes in, and the cell depolarizes (rising phase). The Na$^+$ channels then inactivate, and **voltage-gated K$^+$ channels** open, so K$^+$ leaves and the membrane **repolarizes**. A brief **hyperpolarization** follows. During the **absolute refractory period**, a new action potential cannot be triggered, which makes conduction one-directional. Action potentials are **all-or-none**; stimulus strength is encoded by firing frequency. **Myelin**, produced by oligodendrocytes in the CNS and Schwann cells in the PNS, insulates axons; the impulse jumps between gaps called **nodes of Ranvier** (**saltatory conduction**), which greatly increases speed. Demyelination, as in multiple sclerosis (CNS) and Guillain–Barré syndrome (PNS), slows or blocks conduction.

### Synapses

At a chemical synapse, the action potential opens voltage-gated Ca$^{2+}$ channels in the presynaptic terminal. Ca$^{2+}$ entry triggers vesicle fusion and neurotransmitter release into the cleft. The transmitter binds postsynaptic receptors, producing an **excitatory** (EPSP) or **inhibitory** (IPSP) postsynaptic potential; a neuron sums these inputs. Acetylcholine acts at the neuromuscular junction. Neurotransmitter action ends by reuptake, enzymatic breakdown (acetylcholinesterase) or diffusion.

### Reflexes and sensation

A **reflex arc** has a receptor, a sensory (afferent) neuron, an integrating center (often the spinal cord), a motor (efferent) neuron and an effector. The knee-jerk reflex is monosynaptic. Sensory receptors **transduce** a stimulus into a change in membrane potential. Types include mechanoreceptors, thermoreceptors, nociceptors, chemoreceptors and photoreceptors. In the retina, **rods** work in dim light and **cones** mediate color vision and acuity; hair cells in the cochlea transduce sound.

### Drawing the temperature loop

Combine this week and last: thermoreceptors (sensors) send afferent signals to the hypothalamus (control center), which sends efferent signals through the autonomic and somatic nervous systems to effectors such as sweat glands, skin arterioles and skeletal muscle. A good diagram labels the set point, stimulus, sensor, control center, effector and response, and uses arrows to show that the response reduces the stimulus.

@example
title: Equilibrium potential for potassium
problem: A neuron has $[K^+]_{out} = 4$ mM and $[K^+]_{in} = 150$ mM. Find $E_K$ at $37\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ using the $61.5$ mV constant.
step: Form the concentration ratio: $\dfrac{[K^+]_{out}}{[K^+]_{in}} = \dfrac{4}{150} = 0.02667$.
step: Take the base-10 logarithm: $\log_{10}(0.02667) = -1.574$.
step: Multiply: $E_K = 61.5 \times (-1.574) \approx -96.8$ mV.
step: The result is negative because K$^+$ is concentrated inside; opening K$^+$ channels pushes the membrane toward this value.

@mistakes
- Putting the concentration ratio upside down in the Nernst equation, which flips the sign.
- Saying that the Na$^+$/K$^+$ pump alone creates the resting potential; leak K$^+$ channels carry most of the influence.
- Thinking a stronger stimulus makes a bigger action potential; it increases the frequency, not the size.
- Confusing afferent and efferent: afferent neurons carry signals toward the CNS.
