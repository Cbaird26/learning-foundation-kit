@article
Outpatient medicine is about prevention and long-term control: the same few diseases, managed over years. The questions are who needs treatment, which treatment, and what to monitor.

### Hypertension

The ACC/AHA classification uses average office readings on at least two occasions: **elevated** 120-129 systolic and below 80 diastolic; **stage 1** 130-139 or 80-89 mm Hg; **stage 2** 140 or higher or 90 or higher. (Some other organizations still use 140/90 for diagnosis; the threshold differs between guidelines.) Confirm with home or ambulatory monitoring, which also identifies white-coat and masked hypertension. A typical target is below 130/80 mm Hg. Look for secondary causes if hypertension is severe, resistant or has a sudden onset: primary aldosteronism (hypokalemia, suppressed renin), renal artery stenosis, obstructive sleep apnea, pheochromocytoma, Cushing syndrome, coarctation and drugs.

Start lifestyle changes (weight loss, DASH diet, sodium restriction, exercise, limiting alcohol). **First-line drugs** are a thiazide-type diuretic (chlorthalidone), an ACE inhibitor or ARB, or a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker. In Black patients without heart failure or CKD, a thiazide or calcium channel blocker is preferred initially. Use an ACE inhibitor or ARB with diabetes and albuminuria, CKD or heart failure. Avoid ACE inhibitors and ARBs in pregnancy (use labetalol, nifedipine or methyldopa) and never combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB.

### Dyslipidemia and statins

Statins reduce cardiovascular events. Indications (ACC/AHA): clinical atherosclerotic disease (high-intensity statin); LDL cholesterol of 190 mg/dL or higher; diabetes at age 40-75 (at least moderate-intensity); and primary prevention in ages 40-75 with elevated 10-year risk by the pooled cohort equations (intermediate risk of 7.5% to below 20% favors a statin after a risk discussion; high risk of 20% or higher, high-intensity; USPSTF uses a 10% risk threshold with at least one risk factor, so guidelines differ). Check liver tests and lipids at baseline, myalgias are common but true myopathy and rhabdomyolysis are rare. Add ezetimibe or a PCSK9 inhibitor if LDL goals are not met. (The 2026 ACC/AHA guideline instead uses PREVENT-ASCVD risk, with lower primary-prevention categories of about 3% to under 5% borderline, 5% to under 10% intermediate and 10% or more high, and LDL-C goals of below 100, 70 or 55 mg/dL; it also advises measuring lipoprotein(a) at least once in every adult.)

### Type 2 diabetes in primary care

Diagnosis: HbA1c 6.5% or higher, fasting glucose 126 mg/dL or higher, 2-hour glucose 200 mg/dL or higher during an oral glucose tolerance test, or random glucose 200 or higher with symptoms. A typical HbA1c goal is below 7%, individualized. **Metformin** and lifestyle change are initial therapy, with an **SGLT2 inhibitor or GLP-1 receptor agonist** added early for established cardiovascular disease, heart failure (SGLT2) or chronic kidney disease. Screen annually: urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and eGFR, dilated eye examination, foot examination, blood pressure and lipids; vaccinate.

### Screening (USPSTF-based; recommendations change)

- **Colorectal cancer:** ages 45-75 (colonoscopy every 10 years, FIT yearly, or other tests).
- **Breast cancer:** mammography every 2 years, ages 40-74.
- **Cervical cancer:** ages 21-65 (cytology every 3 years, or HPV-based testing from age 30).
- **Lung cancer:** low-dose CT yearly, ages 50-80, at least 20 pack-years, currently smoking or quit within the past 15 years.
- **Abdominal aortic aneurysm:** one-time ultrasound in men aged 65-75 who have ever smoked.
- **Osteoporosis:** bone density testing in women 65 and older, and younger postmenopausal women at increased risk.
- **Hepatitis C:** once in adults 18-79. **HIV:** ages 15-65.

**Pack-years** = packs per day times years smoked.

### Low back pain

Most acute low back pain is mechanical and improves within 4-6 weeks. Look for red flags: age over 50 with new onset, cancer history, fever or injection drug use (infection), trauma, progressive neurologic deficit, **saddle anesthesia, urinary retention or incontinence (cauda equina syndrome)**. Without red flags, do not image: advise staying active, heat, NSAIDs and physical therapy. Urgent MRI is needed for cauda equina syndrome, suspected epidural abscess or progressive deficits.

@example
title: Pack-years and screening eligibility
problem: A 62-year-old man smoked 2 packs daily for 25 years and quit 8 years ago. Does he qualify for lung cancer screening?
step: Pack-years $= 2 \times 25 = 50$.
step: He is aged 50-80, has at least 20 pack-years and quit within 15 years.
step: Yes, he qualifies for yearly low-dose CT.

@example
title: Number needed to treat
problem: In a trial, 12% of control patients and 8% of treated patients had a stroke over 5 years. Find the absolute risk reduction and the number needed to treat.
step: $\text{ARR} = 0.12 - 0.08 = 0.04$.
step: $\text{NNT} = 1/\text{ARR} = 1/0.04 = 25$.
step: Twenty-five patients must be treated for 5 years to prevent one stroke.

@mistakes
- Diagnosing hypertension from a single reading, or treating white-coat hypertension without out-of-office confirmation.
- Combining an ACE inhibitor with an ARB, or giving an ACE inhibitor in pregnancy.
- Ordering lumbar spine imaging for acute back pain without red flags.
- Screening beyond or short of the age ranges, or using the wrong test (screening mammography for a man, AAA ultrasound for a nonsmoking woman).
