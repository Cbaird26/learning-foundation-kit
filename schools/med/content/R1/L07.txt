@article
**Upper GI bleeding** arises proximal to the ligament of Treitz: peptic ulcer disease (most common), gastroesophageal varices, erosive gastritis or esophagitis, Mallory-Weiss tear, angiodysplasia and malignancy. It presents with hematemesis, coffee-ground emesis or melena; a high BUN-to-creatinine ratio supports an upper source. **Lower GI bleeding** (diverticulosis, angiodysplasia, colitis, hemorrhoids, colorectal cancer) presents with hematochezia, although brisk upper bleeding can too.

### Resuscitation and risk

Assess airway and hemodynamics first. Place two large-bore IV lines, send type and screen, CBC, coagulation tests, BUN and creatinine, liver tests. Give isotonic crystalloid for hypovolemia. Use a **restrictive transfusion strategy**: transfuse for hemoglobin below 7 g/dL (below 8 g/dL with cardiovascular disease), because over-transfusion raises portal pressure and rebleeding in variceal disease. Correct coagulopathy only when severe or life-threatening. The **Glasgow-Blatchford score** (BUN, hemoglobin, systolic pressure, heart rate, melena, syncope, liver disease, heart failure) identifies very low risk patients (score 0-1) for outpatient care. Give intravenous **proton pump inhibitor** (reasonable before endoscopy); erythromycin before endoscopy improves visualization. Perform **upper endoscopy within 24 hours** after resuscitation; for ulcers with high-risk stigmata (active bleeding, visible vessel), treat endoscopically and then continue high-dose PPI for 72 hours. Test for *H. pylori* and treat; stop NSAIDs.

### Variceal bleeding: the first hour

Portal hypertension from cirrhosis produces esophageal and gastric varices, and a first bleed carries substantial mortality. In the first hour:

1. Secure the airway if encephalopathic or vomiting blood, then establish large-bore access.
2. Start a **vasoactive drug**: octreotide infusion (typical adult: 50 microgram bolus, then 50 microgram/h) before endoscopy.
3. Give **prophylactic antibiotics**, typically ceftriaxone 1 g daily (typical adult dose for study) for up to 7 days, which reduces infection, rebleeding and death.
4. Transfuse restrictively (hemoglobin target 7-8 g/dL).
5. **Endoscopy within 12 hours** with endoscopic **band ligation**; add a PPI because post-banding ulcers occur.
6. Hold beta-blockers and diuretics during the acute bleed. For failure of control, use balloon tamponade as a bridge and rescue or early **TIPS** (transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt); early pre-emptive TIPS is considered in high-risk Child-Pugh C patients.

Secondary prophylaxis: nonselective beta-blocker (propranolol, nadolol or carvedilol) plus repeated band ligation. Lactulose prevents hepatic encephalopathy that a GI bleed can trigger.

### Ascites and its complications

New ascites needs **diagnostic paracentesis**. The **serum-ascites albumin gradient** is

$$\text{SAAG} = \text{serum albumin} - \text{ascitic fluid albumin}.$$

A SAAG of 1.1 g/dL or more indicates portal hypertension (cirrhosis, heart failure, Budd-Chiari); below 1.1 indicates nodular peritoneal disease, nephrotic syndrome or pancreatitis. Treat with sodium restriction and spironolactone plus furosemide (about 100:40 mg ratio), large-volume paracentesis with albumin for tense ascites, and avoid NSAIDs.

**Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP)** is infection of ascites without a surgical source, with an ascitic **neutrophil count of 250 cells/mm³ or more** (absolute neutrophils = total white cells times percent neutrophils). Treat with a third-generation cephalosporin (cefotaxime or ceftriaxone) plus albumin (1.5 g/kg on day 1, 1 g/kg on day 3) to prevent hepatorenal syndrome. Secondary prophylaxis follows.

**Hepatic encephalopathy**: look for a precipitant (infection, bleeding, constipation, dehydration, sedatives, hypokalemia), and treat with lactulose, adding rifaximin for recurrence. **Hepatorenal syndrome** is kidney failure from profound renal vasoconstriction in cirrhosis, diagnosed after volume expansion with albumin fails and other causes are excluded; treat with albumin plus a vasoconstrictor (terlipressin, or norepinephrine) and consider transplant.

### Alcohol-associated hepatitis

AST is elevated but usually below 300 U/L with an AST:ALT ratio above 2, with fever, jaundice and leukocytosis. The **Maddrey discriminant function**:

$$\text{DF} = 4.6 \times (\text{PT} - \text{control PT}) + \text{bilirubin (mg/dL)}.$$

A DF of 32 or more indicates severe disease; consider prednisolone after excluding infection and GI bleeding. Always check acetaminophen level in unexplained hepatitis; treat acetaminophen toxicity with N-acetylcysteine.

@example
title: SAAG
problem: A patient with new ascites has serum albumin 3.4 g/dL and ascitic fluid albumin 1.0 g/dL. Calculate SAAG and interpret it.
step: $\text{SAAG} = 3.4 - 1.0 = 2.4$ g/dL.
step: A value of 1.1 or more indicates portal hypertension.
step: The leading diagnoses are cirrhosis, right heart failure and hepatic venous outflow obstruction.

@example
title: Ascitic neutrophil count
problem: Paracentesis fluid has 900 white cells/mm³, of which 40% are neutrophils. Does the patient meet the criterion for SBP?
step: Neutrophils $= 900 \times 0.40 = 360$ cells/mm³.
step: The criterion is 250 or more.
step: Yes: begin antibiotics (third-generation cephalosporin) with albumin after sending cultures.

@mistakes
- Transfusing every patient with a GI bleed to a normal hemoglobin, which raises portal pressure and rebleeding in varices.
- Omitting antibiotic prophylaxis in a cirrhotic patient with GI bleeding.
- Skipping paracentesis in a cirrhotic patient with fever, abdominal pain, encephalopathy or kidney injury and calling it "just cirrhosis."
- Giving NSAIDs for pain in cirrhosis, or large-volume paracentesis without albumin.
