@article
**Acute coronary syndrome (ACS)** results from rupture or erosion of an atherosclerotic plaque with thrombus formation. Complete occlusion produces **STEMI**; partial or transient occlusion produces **unstable angina** or **NSTEMI**. The difference between the last two is troponin: it is released in NSTEMI (myocyte necrosis) and not in unstable angina.

### Approach to chest pain

First exclude the killers: ACS, pulmonary embolism, aortic dissection, tension pneumothorax, esophageal rupture and cardiac tamponade. Pericarditis, pneumonia, GERD and musculoskeletal pain are common but less urgent. Clues: pressure radiating to the arm or jaw with exertion and diaphoresis suggests ACS; tearing pain radiating to the back with unequal arm pressures suggests dissection; pleuritic pain with dyspnea, tachycardia or risk factors suggests PE; positional pain relieved by leaning forward with a friction rub suggests pericarditis.

Obtain a 12-lead **ECG within 10 minutes** of arrival.

### The ECG

STEMI requires new ST elevation at the J point in two contiguous leads: at least 1 mm in all leads except V2-V3, where the thresholds are at least 2 mm in men aged 40 or older, 2.5 mm in men under 40, and 1.5 mm in women. A new left bundle branch block with suggestive features, and posterior infarction (ST depression V1-V3 with tall R waves), are STEMI equivalents.

| Leads | Territory | Usual artery |
|---|---|---|
| II, III, aVF | Inferior | Right coronary |
| V1-V4 | Anterior or septal | Left anterior descending |
| I, aVL, V5-V6 | Lateral | Left circumflex |

Inferior STEMI may involve the right ventricle: check right-sided leads. These patients are preload dependent, so avoid nitrates.

### Troponin and risk stratification

Cardiac troponin rises within hours and stays elevated for days. High-sensitivity assays allow rapid rule-out with serial values (for example at 0 and 1-3 hours). Troponin also rises in myocarditis, PE, sepsis, heart failure and kidney disease, so interpret it in context.

The **HEART score** (0-10) adds 0-2 points each for History, ECG, Age (under 45: 0; 45-64: 1; 65 or older: 2), Risk factors (none: 0; 1-2: 1; 3 or more, or known atherosclerotic disease: 2) and Troponin (normal: 0; 1-3 times normal: 1; more than 3 times: 2). A score of 0-3 is low risk, 4-6 moderate, 7 or more high.

### Management

**STEMI:** immediate reperfusion. Primary PCI with **first medical contact to device within 90 minutes** (120 minutes if transferred). If PCI cannot be done within 120 minutes of first contact, give fibrinolysis, ideally within 30 minutes of arrival (door-to-needle).

**All ACS** (typical adult regimen for study; verify against current references): aspirin 162-325 mg chewed, then 81 mg daily; a P2Y12 inhibitor (ticagrelor or prasugrel, or clopidogrel); parenteral anticoagulation (unfractionated heparin, enoxaparin or bivalirudin); a high-intensity statin; an oral beta-blocker within 24 hours unless there is heart failure, low output, risk of shock, or heart block. Nitroglycerin relieves ischemic pain but does not change outcomes, and is contraindicated with systolic pressure under 90 mm Hg, right ventricular infarction, or recent phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor use. Oxygen is given only if saturation is below about 90%. NSTE-ACS is managed with an early invasive strategy (angiography within 24 hours) for high-risk features, or immediately for refractory pain or instability.

Later: ACE inhibitor or ARB (especially with LV dysfunction), cardiac rehabilitation, risk-factor control.

**Complications:** arrhythmias (ventricular fibrillation in the first hours), cardiogenic shock, papillary muscle rupture (new murmur, 2-7 days), free wall rupture, ventricular septal rupture, and Dressler syndrome (weeks later).

title: A HEART score
problem: A 52-year-old man with hypertension and hyperlipidemia (two risk factors) has moderately suspicious chest pain, a nonspecific repolarization abnormality on ECG, and a troponin twice the upper limit of normal. Calculate the HEART score and risk group.
step: History: moderately suspicious scores 1. ECG: nonspecific repolarization scores 1.
step: Age 52 is in the 45-64 band: 1. Two risk factors: 1. Troponin 2 times normal: 1.
step: Total $1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 5$.
step: A score of 4-6 is moderate risk: observe, serial troponins and further testing rather than discharge.

@example
title: Door-to-needle decision
problem: A rural hospital sees a patient with anterior STEMI. The nearest PCI center would achieve first medical contact to device time of 150 minutes. What is the best reperfusion strategy?
step: The goal for transfer PCI is 120 minutes from first contact.
step: Predicted 150 minutes exceeds 120, so PCI will not be timely.
step: Give fibrinolysis, aiming for door-to-needle within 30 minutes, if no contraindication, then transfer to a PCI-capable center.

@mistakes
- Waiting for troponin before treating a patient with ST elevation. STEMI is a clinical and ECG diagnosis.
- Giving nitroglycerin in inferior STEMI with right ventricular involvement or to a patient who recently took sildenafil.
- Calling normal first troponin a rule-out for a patient with ongoing pain, instead of repeating it serially.
- Giving a beta-blocker to a patient with acute decompensated heart failure or cardiogenic shock.
