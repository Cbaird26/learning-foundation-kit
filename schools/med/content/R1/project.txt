step: Case C. A 21-year-old woman with type 1 diabetes has 2 days of vomiting and abdominal pain after missing insulin. Weight 60 kg. Write the DKA plan with fluid, potassium and insulin decisions, anion gap and corrected sodium, a search for the precipitant, and the transition to subcutaneous basal-bolus insulin.
step: Case D. A 56-year-old man with alcohol-related cirrhosis presents with fever, abdominal pain and increasing confusion. Blood pressure 98/58 mm Hg, heart rate 112/min, temperature 38.4 C. ### Week by week

**Week 1, admission.** History with OLDCARTS, medication reconciliation, one-liner, problem-based plan and the ADC VAAN DIMLS orders. Cockcroft-Gault clearance and the 4-2-1 fluid rule.

**Week 2, ACS.** ECG within 10 minutes, STEMI criteria, territories, troponin, HEART score. PCI within 90 minutes of first medical contact (120 if transferred), otherwise fibrinolysis. Aspirin, P2Y12 inhibitor, anticoagulant, statin; no nitrates in right ventricular infarction.

**Week 3, dyspnea and heart failure.** HFrEF, HFmrEF and HFpEF by ejection fraction; BNP caveats; A-a gradient; warm and wet means IV loop diuretic; the four pillars of GDMT.

**Week 4, pneumonia and sepsis.** CURB-65, empiric regimens by setting, sepsis definitions, at least 30 mL/kg crystalloid (a weak recommendation, reassess after each bolus), norepinephrine first.

**Week 5, AKI and electrolytes.** KDIGO, prerenal versus ATN versus postrenal, FeNa, hyperkalemia treatment order (calcium, shift, remove), hyponatremia correction no faster than 8 mEq/L in 24 hours when osmotic demyelination risk is high (10-12 otherwise), anion gap.

**Week 6, inpatient diabetes.** Target 140-180 mg/dL, hold metformin and SGLT2 inhibitors, basal-bolus insulin from 0.4 units/kg, 1800 rule, DKA with potassium before insulin.

**Week 7, GI bleeding and liver disease.** Restrictive transfusion, octreotide and ceftriaxone for varices, banding within 12 hours, SAAG, SBP at 250 neutrophils, Maddrey function.

**Week 8, anemia and VTE.** MCV with reticulocytes and iron studies, B12 versus folate, Wells score, D-dimer rules, anticoagulation.

**Week 9, antibiotics.** Match drug to syndrome, MSSA with cefazolin, meningitis regimen with dexamethasone, C. difficile therapy, 48-hour time-out.

**Week 10, delirium.** CAM, DELIRIUMS causes, glucose first, medication review, nonpharmacologic care, avoid benzodiazepines except withdrawal.

**Week 11, outpatient care.** Blood pressure classes and first-line drugs, statin indications, screening ages, back pain red flags, pack-years, BMI, NNT.

**Week 12, discharge.** Reconciliation with reasons, LACE index, pending results, follow-up within 7 days for heart failure.

### Self-check

- Can I rank a problem list by danger, not by chart order?
- Can I calculate CrCl, anion gap, A-a gradient, SAAG, corrected calcium and a weight-based dose from raw numbers?
- Can I name the first drug and the drug to avoid for each emergency in this course?
- Can I explain one major idea aloud without notes?

### Common errors

- Treating a lab value instead of the patient (for example, calcium for a normal-ECG potassium of 5.5).
- Continuing nephrotoxic or hypotensive home drugs in acute illness.
- Over-transfusing in variceal bleeding; correcting sodium too fast.
- Starting insulin in DKA before checking potassium.
- Delaying antibiotics in septic shock or meningitis for tests.
- Writing a discharge summary with no reasons for medication changes and no pending results.

@exam tuple
prompt: A 70 kg patient with DKA has glucose 700 mg/dL, sodium 126 mEq/L, chloride 90 mEq/L and bicarbonate 8 mEq/L. Calculate, in order: the anion gap (mEq/L), the glucose-corrected sodium (mEq/L) using 1.6 mEq/L per 100 mg/dL of glucose above 100, and the insulin infusion rate in units/h at 0.1 units/kg/h.
answer: (28, 135.6, 7)
check: (126 - (90 + 8), 126 + 1.6 * (700 - 100) / 100, 0.1 * 70)
tol: 0.1
hint: Anion gap is sodium minus the sum of chloride and bicarbonate.
hint: Glucose is 600 above 100, so the sodium correction is $1.6 \times 6$.
hint: Insulin rate is 0.1 units per kilogram per hour.
solution: Anion gap $= 126 - 98 = 28$. Corrected sodium $= 126 + 1.6 \times 6 = 135.6$. Insulin $= 0.1 \times 70 = 7$ units/h, started once potassium is at least 3.3 mEq/L (3.5 in the 2024 consensus).

prompt: A 85 kg patient has septic shock from pneumonia with a lactate of 5.2 mmol/L. The team gives 30 mL/kg of crystalloid over the first 3 hours. What pump rate in mL/h is needed?
answer: 850
check: 30 * 85 / 3
unit: mL/h
tol: 1
hint: Find the total volume first.
hint: $30 \times 85 = 2550$ mL.
solution: $2550 \text{ mL} / 3 \text{ h} = 850$ mL/h, with reassessment of perfusion and lung function during the infusion.

@exam mc
prompt: A 60-year-old man with cirrhosis and ascites has creatinine rising from 1.0 to 2.4 mg/dL after 2 days of diuretics. Diuretics are stopped and albumin 1 g/kg is given on two consecutive days, but creatinine is 2.6 mg/dL. Urine sodium is below 10 mEq/L, sediment is bland, there is no proteinuria or shock, and he has taken no nephrotoxic drugs. What is the best next treatment?
choice: Resume furosemide at a higher dose
choice: Intravenous contrast to look for renal artery stenosis
choice*: A vasoconstrictor such as terlipressin (or norepinephrine) together with albumin
choice: Renal biopsy
choice: Normal saline boluses without albumin
hint: Think of the diagnosis when kidney failure persists after volume expansion in cirrhosis.
solution: This is hepatorenal syndrome. After stopping diuretics and volume expansion with albumin, treat with albumin plus a vasoconstrictor (terlipressin or norepinephrine) and assess for liver transplantation.
