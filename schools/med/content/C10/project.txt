@review
## Course review: key ideas by week

**Week 1, Mendelian genetics and pedigrees.** Segregation and independent assortment; product and sum rules; clues for autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, X-linked and mitochondrial inheritance; penetrance and expressivity; the conditional carrier probability $\frac{2}{3}$ for an unaffected sibling of a patient.

**Week 2, linkage and recombination.** Recombinants are the rare classes; $1\%$ recombination is $1$ cM; recombination frequency tops out at $0.5$; in three-point crosses the double crossovers reveal the middle gene; interference is $1-\text{CoC}$; a LOD score of $3$ indicates linkage.

**Week 3, chromosomes.** Karyotype notation, nondisjunction in meiosis I and II, trisomies, Turner and Klinefelter syndromes, Barr bodies equal X chromosomes minus one, balanced and Robertsonian translocations.

**Week 4, mutation and repair.** Silent, missense, nonsense, frameshift; deamination of methylcytosine; BER, NER, MMR, HR and NHEJ; Lynch syndrome, xeroderma pigmentosum, BRCA; Knudson's two hits.

**Week 5, transcription and RNA processing.** Template versus coding strand; cap, splicing, poly(A); invariant GU and AG; effects of splice mutations depend on exon length.

**Week 6, translation.** Codon table logic, wobble, three steps of translation, nonsense-mediated decay, antibiotics by ribosomal subunit.

**Week 7, bacterial regulation.** lac operon needs lactose present and glucose absent; repressor acts in trans, operator in cis; trp operon is repressible with tryptophan as corepressor.

**Week 8, eukaryotic regulation.** Enhancers and combinatorial control; histone acetylation opens chromatin; promoter methylation silences; imprinting and X inactivation.

**Week 9, molecular tools.** PCR doubles per cycle; reverse primer is the reverse complement; Sanger reads the new strand; CRISPR needs a PAM.

**Week 10, genomics.** Exome versus genome; percent identity and scores; E-values; ACMG variant classes; do not act on a VUS.

**Week 11, population genetics.** $p^2+2pq+q^2=1$; incidence is $q^2$; carrier frequency is $2pq$; the evolutionary forces.

**Week 12, complex traits and cancer.** Heritability from twins; GWAS threshold $5\times10^{-8}$; PRS versus monogenic test; oncogenes versus tumor suppressors.

### Self-check

- Can you state the pattern clues for each inheritance type without notes?
- Can you derive an mRNA from a template strand and translate it?
- Can you predict whether a splice or deletion change preserves the reading frame?
- Can you compute a carrier risk from incidence?

### Common errors

Using $\frac{1}{2}$ for the carrier risk of an unaffected sibling; mixing up the template and coding strands; forgetting double crossovers when counting recombinants; reporting the incidence as $q$; treating a VUS as pathogenic; interpreting heritability as applying to an individual.

@exam num
prompt: A severe autosomal recessive disease has an incidence of 1 in 10000 in a population at Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium. An unaffected woman has a brother with the disease. She plans a child with an unrelated man from the same population who has no family history. What is the probability, as a decimal rounded to four decimal places, that the child is affected?
answer: 0.0033
tol: 0.0002
check: (2/3) * (2 * (1 - 0.01) * 0.01) * (1/4)
hint: Find $q$ from the incidence $q^2$, then the population carrier frequency $2pq$.
hint: Her carrier probability is conditional: she is unaffected with a carrier-parent pair, so use $\frac{2}{3}$.
hint: Multiply her carrier probability, his carrier frequency and $\frac{1}{4}$.
solution: $q=\sqrt{0.0001}=0.01$, so $2pq=2(0.99)(0.01)=0.0198$. Risk $=\frac{2}{3}\times0.0198\times\frac{1}{4}=0.0033$.

@exam mc
prompt: A boy has a progressive neuromuscular disorder. Sequencing finds a G to A change at the first base of the donor splice site of intron 6 of the gene (GU becomes AU). Exon 6 is 87 nucleotides long and is within the coding region. RNA studies show that exon 6 is skipped. Which is the most likely consequence for the protein?
choice*: An in-frame deletion of 29 amino acids
choice: A frameshift with a premature stop codon and nonsense-mediated decay
choice: A silent change with a normal protein
choice: A nonsense mutation in exon 6
hint: Divide the length of the skipped exon by three.
solution: Skipping an exon of 87 nucleotides removes $87/3=29$ codons without changing the frame, so the protein loses 29 amino acids but stays in frame. The effect on function depends on the importance of the region lost.

@exam self
prompt: A 6-year-old boy with a severe disease has a homozygous nonsense variant (c.610C>T, p.Arg204Ter, exon 5 of 12) in a gene. His unaffected sister, aged 24, is pregnant. Outline a plan that (a) confirms the variant using PCR and Sanger sequencing, (b) states the sister's carrier risk and why, and (c) explains what a nonsense variant does to the mRNA and protein.
solution: (a) Design primers in the introns flanking exon 5, about 20 nucleotides each with similar melting temperatures (the forward primer identical to the top strand and the reverse primer the reverse complement), amplify a product of a few hundred base pairs from the sister's DNA, and sequence it. A noncarrier shows a single C peak at the site, a carrier shows overlapping C and T peaks, and the affected boy shows a single T peak. (b) Her parents are carriers, since the boy is affected and they are unaffected. She is unaffected, so her carrier probability is two thirds, not one half. Testing her directly gives a definite answer. If she is a carrier, her partner should be tested, and if he is also a carrier the risk is one quarter for each pregnancy. (c) The change turns an arginine codon (CGA) into a stop codon (UGA) in the mRNA, a premature stop. Because exon 5 is not the last exon, nonsense-mediated decay is expected to remove most of the transcript, so little or no protein is made, which fits a loss-of-function recessive disease.
rubric: Gives a PCR design with flanking primers and the three expected Sanger results.
rubric: States the carrier probability as two thirds with a reason and proposes partner testing.
rubric: Explains premature stop, nonsense-mediated decay and loss of function.
hint: Consider what peaks a Sanger trace shows at a heterozygous site.
hint: Recall the conditional probability for an unaffected sibling of an affected patient.
