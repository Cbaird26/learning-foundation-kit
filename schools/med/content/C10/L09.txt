@article
Molecular genetics rests on a toolbox that lets you copy, read, cut and rewrite DNA.

### Polymerase chain reaction

PCR amplifies a chosen segment from a tiny amount of template. Each cycle has three steps:

1. **Denaturation** (about $95\,^\circ\text{C}$) separates the strands.
2. **Annealing** (about $50$ to $65\,^\circ\text{C}$) lets two short **primers** bind the flanking sequences.
3. **Extension** (about $72\,^\circ\text{C}$) lets a heat-stable polymerase (*Taq*) extend from each primer, $5'\to3'$.

Since each cycle doubles the target, $n$ cycles of ideal PCR give $2^n$ copies per starting target molecule.

**Primer design.** The **forward primer** matches the sequence of the top (coding) strand at the left end of the target, written $5'\to3'$. The **reverse primer** is the **reverse complement** of the top strand at the right end of the target. A good primer is $18$ to $25$ nucleotides long, has $40$ to $60\%$ G+C, ends in one or two G or C at the $3'$ end (a "clamp"), avoids long runs of one base, and does not form hairpins or pair with the other primer (primer dimers). The two primers should have similar melting temperatures, within about $5\,^\circ\text{C}$. For short primers the Wallace rule estimates

$$T_m\approx 2(A+T)+4(G+C)\ {}^\circ\text{C},$$

and the annealing temperature is set a few degrees below $T_m$. The product size is the distance between the outer ends of the two primers.

**Variants.** **RT-PCR** first converts RNA to cDNA with reverse transcriptase. **Quantitative PCR (qPCR)** measures fluorescence each cycle; the **threshold cycle ($C_t$)** is the cycle at which the signal crosses a threshold. A lower $C_t$ means more starting template, and with $100\%$ efficiency each cycle of difference means a twofold difference.

### Sanger sequencing

Sanger sequencing runs a primer extension in the presence of normal dNTPs and a small amount of fluorescent **dideoxynucleotides (ddNTPs)**, which lack the $3'$-OH and terminate the chain. This yields fragments ending at every position; capillary electrophoresis orders them by size, shortest first, and the color of the terminal ddNTP gives the sequence of the **newly synthesized** strand in the $5'\to3'$ direction. **Next-generation sequencing** reads millions of short fragments in parallel.

### Restriction enzymes and cloning

**Restriction endonucleases** cut DNA at specific, often palindromic, sites: EcoRI cuts $5'\text{-G}\downarrow\text{AATTC-}3'$, leaving sticky ends. To clone, cut a **vector** (plasmid with an origin of replication, a selectable marker such as an antibiotic resistance gene, and a multiple cloning site) and the insert with the same enzyme, join them with **DNA ligase**, and transform bacteria. Selection on antibiotic keeps cells with the plasmid; blue-white screening (lacZ disruption) identifies those with an insert.

For circular plasmid DNA, $k$ cuts give $k$ fragments; for linear DNA, $k$ cuts give $k+1$.

### Blotting

**Southern blot** detects DNA, **Northern blot** RNA, and **Western blot** protein (with antibodies). (Only "Southern" is an eponym, after its inventor; Northern and Western are jokes on that name.)

### CRISPR-Cas9

A **guide RNA** directs Cas9 to a matching DNA sequence next to a **protospacer adjacent motif (PAM)**, which is $5'\text{-NGG-}3'$ for *Streptococcus pyogenes* Cas9. Cas9 makes a **double-strand break**. Repair by **non-homologous end joining** introduces small insertions or deletions that disrupt the gene, while **homology-directed repair** with a supplied template can introduce a precise change. Base editors change single bases without a double-strand break. Edited therapies exist, for example for sickle cell disease.

@example
title: How many copies from PCR?
problem: A reaction starts with a single double-stranded template and runs 25 ideal cycles. How many copies of the target are expected, and what is the size of the product if the forward primer begins at position 101 and the reverse primer's 5' end pairs at position 480 of the top strand?
step: Each cycle doubles the number of copies, so after 25 cycles there are $2^{25}=33{,}554{,}432$ copies (the exponential count ignores the early longer products).
step: The product spans from position 101 to 480, inclusive. Its length is $480-101+1=380$ base pairs.

@example
title: Reading a Sanger ladder
problem: In a Sanger reaction, the terminating ddNTPs for fragments ordered from shortest to longest are G, A, T, C, C, A. What is the sequence of the synthesized strand, and what sequence is on the template?
step: The shortest fragment ends at the first nucleotide added after the primer, so the new strand reads $5'\text{-GATCCA-}3'$.
step: The template is complementary and antiparallel: $3'\text{-CTAGGT-}5'$, which is $5'\text{-TGGATC-}3'$.

@mistakes
- Making the reverse primer identical to the top strand rather than its reverse complement.
- Reading the Sanger result as the template sequence rather than the newly synthesized strand.
- Counting $2^n$ copies without stating that this assumes $100\%$ efficiency.
- Predicting $k+1$ fragments from $k$ cuts in a circular plasmid; circular DNA gives $k$ fragments.

@practice self
prompt: A hospital laboratory wants to detect a bacterial gene in a patient sample. List the steps of PCR, explain why a heat-stable polymerase is needed, and describe two controls that you would run to interpret the result.
solution: Each cycle has denaturation at about 95 degrees Celsius to separate the strands, annealing at a temperature just under the primer melting temperature so primers bind the flanking sequences, and extension at about 72 degrees Celsius so the polymerase builds the new strands. Taq polymerase, from a thermophilic bacterium, survives the repeated 95 degree denaturation steps; an ordinary polymerase would be destroyed. A positive control with known template shows the reaction works; a no-template (negative) control containing water shows that reagents are not contaminated, since a band there signals false positives. An internal control, such as a human gene amplified from the sample, shows that there is no inhibition and the DNA was extracted.

rubric: Names a positive control and a no-template negative control.
hint: Think of what a failed reaction and a contaminated reaction would each look like.
