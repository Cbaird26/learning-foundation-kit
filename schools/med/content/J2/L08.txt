@lesson
title: Circles: circumference and area
summary: Compute the circumference and area of a circle from its radius or diameter, in exact form with $\pi$ and as a rounded decimal.
objective: Use $C = 2\pi r = \pi d$ to find circumference.
objective: Use $A = \pi r^2$ to find area.
objective: Switch between radius and diameter and explain how doubling the radius changes area.
reading: Khan Academy 7th grade math, unit "Circles" (area and circumference of circles, and problems with circles).

@article
A **circle** is the set of all points at the same distance from a center. That distance is the **radius** $r$. A segment across the circle through the center is a **diameter** $d$, and $d = 2r$.

### Where $\pi$ comes from

If you measure around any circle (the **circumference** $C$) and divide by its diameter, you always get the same number, about $3.14159$. That number is $\pi$:

$$\frac{C}{d} = \pi \qquad\text{so}\qquad C = \pi d = 2\pi r.$$

It is another proportional relationship: the circumference is proportional to the diameter, with constant of proportionality $\pi$.

### Area

The **area** of a circle is

$$A = \pi r^2.$$

Notice that area uses the radius **squared**. A circle with $r = 3$ has $A = \pi \cdot 3^2 = 9\pi$ square units. The units are square units, while circumference is in ordinary length units.

### Exact and approximate answers

The number $\pi$ never ends, so $9\pi$ is the exact area. For a decimal, use a calculator's $\pi$ key (about $3.14159$), or $3.14$ if the problem says so. Round only at the end: $9\pi \approx 28.27$.

### Radius or diameter?

Read carefully. A problem that gives a diameter of $14$ means $r = 7$, so $A = \pi \cdot 7^2 = 49\pi$. Using $14$ directly in $\pi r^2$ is a common error.

### Working backward

If $C = 18\pi$, then $2\pi r = 18\pi$, so $r = 9$. Divide both sides by $2\pi$.

### Changing the size

If you double the radius, the circumference doubles but the area becomes $\pi(2r)^2 = 4\pi r^2$, which is **four times** as big. This matches the rule for scale drawings: lengths scale by $k$, areas by $k^2$.

### Scale and circles together

To find the real area of a circle in a scale drawing, use the scale to convert the drawn radius to the actual radius first, then use $A = \pi r^2$.

@example
title: A circular table
problem: A round table has a diameter of $8$ ft. Find its exact area and its area to the nearest tenth, and then its circumference as an exact value.
step: The radius is half the diameter: $r = 4$ ft.
step: Area: $A = \pi r^2 = \pi \cdot 16 = 16\pi$ square feet.
step: As a decimal: $16\pi \approx 50.3$ square feet.
step: Circumference: $C = \pi d = 8\pi$ feet.

@mistakes
- Using the diameter in place of the radius in $A = \pi r^2$.
- Forgetting to square the radius, or squaring $\pi$ as well.
- Giving the area in plain units instead of square units.

@practice num
prompt: A circle has radius $5$. What is its circumference? Give an exact answer (use pi).
answer: 10pi
check: 2 * pi * 5
hint: Use $C = 2\pi r$.
solution: $C = 2\pi \cdot 5 = 10\pi$.

@practice num
prompt: On a scale drawing with the scale $1\text{ cm} = 2\text{ m}$, a circular fountain is drawn with a diameter of $3$ cm. What is the actual area of the fountain in square meters? Give an exact answer (use pi).
answer: 9pi
unit: square m
check: pi * (3 * 2 / 2)**2
hint: Convert the drawn diameter to the actual diameter with the scale.
hint: The actual diameter is $6$ m, so the radius is $3$ m.
solution: Actual diameter is $3 \cdot 2 = 6$ m, so $r = 3$ m and $A = \pi \cdot 3^2 = 9\pi$ square meters.

@quiz num

answer: 113.1
unit: square in
tol: 0.05
check: pi * 6**2
hint: Find the radius, then use $A = \pi r^2$.
solution: $r = 6$, so $A = 36\pi \approx 113.1$ square inches.

@quiz num
prompt: A bicycle wheel has a diameter of $70$ cm. How far does the bike travel in $10$ full turns of the wheel, in meters, rounded to the nearest tenth?
answer: 22
unit: m
tol: 0.05
check: 10 * pi * 0.7
hint: One full turn covers one circumference.
hint: The circumference is $\pi \cdot 70$ cm, or $0.7\pi$ m.
solution: One turn covers $0.7\pi$ m, and $10$ turns cover $7\pi \approx 22.0$ meters.
