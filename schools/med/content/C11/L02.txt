@article
**Learning** is a relatively lasting change in behavior or knowledge produced by experience. The simplest forms are **nonassociative**: **habituation** is a decreased response to a repeated harmless stimulus, and **sensitization** is an increased response after an intense or noxious stimulus.

### Classical conditioning

In **classical conditioning** a neutral stimulus acquires the power to trigger a response because it predicts another stimulus. The classic experiments with dogs and salivation established the vocabulary:

- **Unconditioned stimulus (UCS)**: naturally triggers a response (food).
- **Unconditioned response (UCR)**: the natural reaction (salivation to food).
- **Conditioned stimulus (CS)**: initially neutral, then paired with the UCS (a tone).
- **Conditioned response (CR)**: the learned reaction to the CS alone (salivation to the tone).

Key phenomena: **acquisition** (CS-UCS pairing strengthens the CR; the CS must precede the UCS and predict it), **extinction** (CS presented repeatedly without the UCS weakens the CR), **spontaneous recovery** (a weakened CR reappears after a rest), **generalization** (similar stimuli also trigger the CR) and **discrimination** (only the CS does). **Higher-order conditioning** uses an established CS as if it were a UCS. A famous demonstration (Watson and Rayner's "Little Albert" study) showed learned fear of a neutral object; it is now cited as an ethics lesson as well as a learning example. **Taste-aversion learning** (Garcia) is a case of **biological preparedness**: nausea can be conditioned to a taste after a single pairing and a delay of hours, because evolution prepared organisms to link food with illness.

### Operant conditioning

In **operant conditioning** behavior is shaped by its consequences (Thorndike's law of effect; Skinner's operant chamber). Reinforcement increases a behavior; punishment decreases it. "Positive" means a stimulus is added, "negative" means one is removed.

| | Stimulus added | Stimulus removed |
|---|---|---|
| Behavior increases | Positive reinforcement | Negative reinforcement |
| Behavior decreases | Positive punishment | Negative punishment |

Negative reinforcement is not punishment: taking an analgesic removes pain and makes taking the analgesic more likely. **Shaping** reinforces successive approximations to a target behavior.

**Reinforcement schedules** govern how often reward follows the behavior:

- **Fixed ratio (FR)**: after a set number of responses; high rate with a pause after reinforcement.
- **Variable ratio (VR)**: after an unpredictable number averaging a set value; the highest and steadiest response rate and the most resistance to extinction (slot machines).
- **Fixed interval (FI)**: first response after a fixed time; response rate rises toward the deadline (scalloped pattern).
- **Variable interval (VI)**: first response after an unpredictable time; slow, steady responding.

Partial (intermittent) reinforcement produces slower acquisition but greater resistance to extinction than continuous reinforcement.

### Observational and cognitive learning

**Observational learning** (Bandura's Bobo doll studies) occurs by watching a model, and depends on attention, retention, reproduction and motivation. **Latent learning** (Tolman) is learning without reinforcement that shows itself once a reward appears, evidence for cognitive maps. In medicine, classical conditioning explains anticipatory nausea before chemotherapy, operant conditioning underlies contingency management for substance use and token systems for adherence, and observational learning shapes how trainees and patients adopt behaviors.

@example
title: Classical conditioning in chemotherapy
problem: A patient receiving a nausea-inducing drug in the infusion clinic begins to feel nauseated when she smells the clinic's antiseptic soap, even before treatment. Identify the UCS, UCR, CS and CR.
step: The drug naturally causes nausea, so the drug is the UCS and the nausea it causes is the UCR.
step: The antiseptic smell was initially neutral and was repeatedly paired with the drug, so it is the CS.
step: Nausea to the smell alone is learned, so it is the CR.
step: Treatment: antiemetics, relaxation training or visiting without infusion (extinction) can reduce the CR.

@example
title: Expected reinforcers on a variable-ratio schedule
problem: A token program rewards a patient on a VR-5 schedule for completing home exercises: on average every 5th session earns a reward. About how many rewards does the patient earn over 200 sessions?
step: A VR-5 schedule delivers one reinforcer per 5 responses on average.
step: Divide responses by the ratio: $200 / 5 = 40$.
step: So about $40$ rewards are expected; the exact number varies because the ratio is unpredictable.

@mistakes
- Calling negative reinforcement "punishment"; reinforcement of either kind increases behavior.
- Mixing up the UCR and the CR: the UCR is to the UCS, the CR is to the CS.
- Treating "positive" as meaning "good" and "negative" as meaning "bad".
- Forgetting that variable schedules, not fixed ones, are the most resistant to extinction.

@practice ms
prompt: Which statements about extinction in classical conditioning are correct?
choice*: The CS is presented repeatedly without the UCS.
choice*: The CR weakens, but spontaneous recovery can occur later.
choice: The UCR to the UCS is permanently erased.
choice: The CS is now presented after the UCS.
choice: Extinction occurs only for punishment, not for classical conditioning.
hint: Extinction is a procedure (CS alone) and an outcome (a weakened CR).
hint: Recall what happens after a rest period.
solution: Extinction means presenting the CS without the UCS, weakening the CR; the response can return by spontaneous recovery, so extinction is not erasure. The UCR to the UCS remains, and placing the CS after the UCS is backward pairing, which is weak conditioning.

@practice self
prompt: A child develops a strong fear of the dental office after a painful procedure. (a) Explain this with classical conditioning terms. (b) Explain how a parent who allows the child to avoid dental visits maintains the fear by operant conditioning. (c) Name one approach to reduce the fear.
solution: (a) The painful drilling is the UCS and pain/fear is the UCR. The office (sights, smells, sounds) was neutral and became the CS, and fear of the office is the CR. (b) Avoiding the visit removes anxiety, which is negative reinforcement, so avoidance becomes more likely and the CS is never presented without the UCS, preventing extinction. (c) Graded exposure to the office without pain allows extinction; relaxation training and distraction also help.
rubric: Correctly labels UCS, UCR, CS and CR.
rubric: Identifies avoidance as negative reinforcement and explains why it blocks extinction.
rubric: Proposes a reasonable approach such as graded exposure.
hint: List the four classical terms first, then ask what happens to the child's anxiety when the visit is skipped.
hint: Relief from anxiety is a removed aversive stimulus.
