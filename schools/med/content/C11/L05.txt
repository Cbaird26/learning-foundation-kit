@article
**Consciousness** is awareness of internal states and the external world. **Selective attention** filters what we process (the cocktail-party effect); **inattentional blindness** shows that unattended stimuli can go unnoticed even when they are in view. **Divided attention** (multitasking) reduces performance on demanding tasks, which is why distracted driving is dangerous.

### Circadian rhythm

The **suprachiasmatic nucleus** (SCN) of the hypothalamus is the body's master clock, with a period near 24 hours. Light detected by retinal ganglion cells synchronizes it, and the **pineal gland** secretes **melatonin** at night. Shift work and jet lag cause **circadian misalignment**, with effects on mood, cognition and metabolism.

### Stages of sleep

EEG waves differ by state: **beta** (alert, 13 to 30 Hz), **alpha** (relaxed, eyes closed, 8 to 12 Hz), **theta** (4 to 7 Hz) and **delta** (under 4 Hz, deep sleep). Sleep alternates between non-REM (NREM) and REM:

- **N1**: light sleep, theta waves, hypnic jerks.
- **N2**: **sleep spindles** and **K complexes**; the largest share of the night.
- **N3**: slow-wave sleep with high-amplitude delta waves; most growth hormone release; sleepwalking, night terrors and bedwetting arise here.
- **REM**: rapid eye movements, vivid dreams, an EEG similar to waking, loss of skeletal muscle tone (atonia) and irregular autonomic activity.

A full cycle lasts about 90 minutes, and a night contains four to six cycles. Early cycles have more N3 and later cycles have more REM. Adults need about 7 to 9 hours of sleep, and REM typically makes up about 20% to 25% of the night. Sleep supports memory consolidation and restoration. Major dream theories include Freud's wish fulfillment, the **activation-synthesis** hypothesis (the cortex interprets random brainstem activity) and memory-consolidation accounts.

### Sleep disorders

- **Insomnia**: difficulty falling or staying asleep; cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia is first-line treatment.
- **Obstructive sleep apnea**: repeated upper-airway collapse, snoring, daytime sleepiness, associated with hypertension and obesity; treated with continuous positive airway pressure.
- **Narcolepsy**: loss of hypocretin (orexin)-producing hypothalamic neurons; sudden sleep attacks, **cataplexy** (emotion-triggered loss of muscle tone), sleep paralysis and early REM onset.
- **REM sleep behavior disorder**: loss of REM atonia so patients act out dreams; it can precede synucleinopathies such as Parkinson disease.

### Psychoactive substances

Most drugs of abuse increase dopamine in the **mesolimbic reward pathway** (ventral tegmental area to nucleus accumbens).

| Class | Examples | Main mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Depressants | Alcohol, benzodiazepines, barbiturates | Enhance GABA-A inhibition |
| Stimulants | Cocaine, amphetamine, nicotine, caffeine | Raise dopamine and norepinephrine (cocaine blocks reuptake; amphetamine promotes release); nicotine activates nicotinic receptors; caffeine blocks adenosine receptors |
| Opioids | Morphine, heroin, fentanyl | Activate mu opioid receptors |
| Hallucinogens | LSD, psilocybin | Activate 5-HT2A serotonin receptors |
| Cannabinoids | Cannabis (THC) | Activate CB1 receptors |

**Tolerance** means a larger dose is needed for the same effect. **Physical dependence** means withdrawal symptoms appear when the drug stops; alcohol and benzodiazepine withdrawal can be life-threatening (seizures), while opioid withdrawal is intensely unpleasant but rarely fatal. **Substance use disorder** is defined by impaired control, craving, continued use despite harm and other criteria, and **harm reduction** (naloxone, needle exchange) and **medications** (such as buprenorphine and methadone for opioid use disorder) are evidence-based. Hypnosis and meditation are altered states studied for their effects on attention and pain.

@example
title: Counting sleep cycles
problem: A student sleeps 7.5 hours. If a cycle lasts 90 minutes, how many complete cycles does she get, and if REM makes up 22% of the night, how many minutes of REM is that?
step: Convert to minutes: $7.5 \times 60 = 450$ minutes.
step: Divide by cycle length: $450/90 = 5$ complete cycles.
step: REM minutes: $0.22 \times 450 = 99$ minutes.
step: Because REM lengthens in later cycles, most of this REM occurs in the second half of the night, so waking her early cuts REM disproportionately.

@example
title: Matching a withdrawal syndrome to a drug class
problem: A 45-year-old man admitted for a hernia repair becomes tremulous, tachycardic and diaphoretic on hospital day 2 and then has a seizure. He drinks heavily every day. What is the mechanism?
step: Chronic alcohol enhances GABA-A inhibition, so the brain adapts by reducing GABA tone and upregulating excitatory (glutamate) signaling.
step: When alcohol is stopped, the adapted brain is unopposed and hyperexcitable.
step: This produces tremor, autonomic hyperactivity and seizures, so alcohol withdrawal is a medical emergency.
step: Cross-tolerant GABAergic drugs (benzodiazepines) are the standard treatment.

@mistakes
- Believing REM is the deepest sleep; slow-wave N3 is the deepest NREM sleep and REM is physiologically close to waking.
- Treating tolerance and physical dependence as the same thing.
- Assuming sleepwalking occurs in REM; it occurs in N3.
- Saying drugs "give" dopamine; most block reuptake or promote release of dopamine already in neurons.

solution: (a) Obstructive sleep apnea: repeated collapse of the upper airway during sleep causes apneas and hypopneas. (b) Diagnosis by polysomnography (or home sleep apnea testing); treatment with continuous positive airway pressure, along with weight loss. (c) Each event causes hypoxemia and arousal, fragmenting sleep so that N3 and REM are reduced and he is sleepy by day. Repeated hypoxemia and sympathetic surges contribute to hypertension, arrhythmia and cardiovascular disease.

rubric: Links arousals and sleep fragmentation to sleepiness.
rubric: Links hypoxemia and sympathetic activation to cardiovascular risk.
