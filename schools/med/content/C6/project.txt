### Week by week

- **Week 1, NMR:** $\delta = \text{Hz}/\text{MHz}$; DoU $= (2c+2+n-h-x)/2$; integration gives H counts; $n+1$ rule gives neighbors; aldehyde H 9 to 10, aromatic 6.5 to 8, O-CH 3.3 to 4.5.
- **Week 2, conjugation and aromaticity:** resonance lowers energy; aromatic means cyclic, planar, fully conjugated, $4n+2$ $\pi$ electrons (pyridine 6, pyrrole 6, cyclopentadienyl anion 6).
- **Week 3, EAS:** attack (slow) then loss of H$^+$; electrophiles NO$_2^+$, acylium, carbocations; acylation avoids rearrangement.
- **Week 4, aldehydes and ketones:** nucleophilic addition; hydride and Grignard give alcohols; acetals protect carbonyls; directing effects: OH, NH$_2$, alkyl, halogens ortho/para; NO$_2$, C=O meta.
- **Week 5, carboxylic acids:** carboxylate resonance explains acidity; electron-withdrawing groups raise acidity; $\text{pH} = \text{pK}_a + \log([\text{A}^-]/[\text{HA}])$.
- **Week 6, derivatives:** acid chloride > anhydride > thioester > ester > amide; addition-elimination; saponification is irreversible, acid hydrolysis reversible.
- **Week 7, enolates:** alpha H acidity (ketone 19, ester 25, 1,3-dicarbonyl 9 to 13); aldol, Claisen, malonic ester, Michael.
- **Week 8, amines:** basicity by pK$_{aH}$; anilines, amides and pyridine are weaker bases; reductive amination and Gabriel give clean amines.
- **Week 9, carbohydrates:** D/L, epimers, hemiacetal ring, anomers, mutarotation, reducing sugars.
- **Week 10, lipids:** cis double bonds lower melting point; saponification uses 3 NaOH per triglyceride; phospholipids are amphiphilic; cholesterol is a steroid.
- **Week 11, amino acids:** zwitterions, pI as the average of the two pK$_a$ values around the neutral form, planar peptide bond, four structure levels.
- **Week 12, nucleic acids and drugs:** A-T two hydrogen bonds, G-C three; phosphodiester backbone; ionization and Lipinski's rules; phase I and II metabolism.

### Common errors

- Using O-H or N-H protons in the $n+1$ rule.
- Calling halogens meta directors, or Friedel-Crafts alkylation clean for primary chains.
- Writing the Henderson-Hasselbalch ratio upside down.
- Forgetting that saponification consumes a full equivalent of base.
- Putting the aldol bond between two carbonyl carbons.
- Placing right-hand OH groups up in a Haworth projection.

@exam num
prompt: Aspirin has pK$_a$ 3.5. What fraction of the dissolved drug is in the neutral form in stomach fluid at pH 1.5?
answer: 0.99
check: 1 / (1 + 10 ** (1.5 - 3.5))
tol: 0.002
hint: Find the ratio [A$^-$]/[HA] from Henderson-Hasselbalch.
hint: The ratio is $10^{-2}$, so the neutral form is 100 times more abundant.
solution: $[\text{A}^-]/[\text{HA}] = 10^{1.5 - 3.5} = 0.01$, so the neutral fraction is $1/1.01 = 0.990$.

@exam self
prompt: Analyze ibuprofen (a para-disubstituted benzene bearing an isobutyl group and a CH(CH$_3$)CO$_2$H group; pK$_a$ about 4.5). Identify its functional groups and stereocenter, state the ratio of ionized to neutral drug at pH 7.4, propose one phase I and one phase II metabolic reaction, and predict the directing effect of its two ring substituents.
solution: Functional groups: a benzene ring, a carboxylic acid, and alkyl groups (isobutyl and methyl). The carbon bearing the CH$_3$, H, the ring and CO$_2$H is the single stereocenter, so ibuprofen is chiral and is sold as a racemate. At pH 7.4, $[\text{A}^-]/[\text{HA}] = 10^{7.4 - 4.5} = 10^{2.9} \approx 800$, so about 99.9% is ionized in blood (but mostly neutral in the stomach, which favors absorption there). Phase I: CYP2C9 hydroxylation of the isobutyl chain (to a hydroxy and then carboxy metabolite). Phase II: glucuronidation of the carboxylic acid to an acyl glucuronide. Both ring substituents are alkyl-type groups (the CH(CH$_3$)CO$_2$H group attaches through carbon), weakly activating and ortho/para directing, and because the ring is para-substituted any new group enters ortho to one of them.

rubric: Calculates an ionized to neutral ratio near $10^{2.9}$ at pH 7.4.
rubric: Gives one reasonable phase I and one phase II reaction and labels them correctly.
rubric: States that the alkyl-type substituents are weakly activating ortho/para directors.
hint: Find the carbon with four different groups, then use $10^{\text{pH} - \text{pK}_a}$ for the ratio.
