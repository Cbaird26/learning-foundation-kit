@article
Benzene is an electron-rich but unusually stable ring. It does not undergo addition (which would destroy aromaticity); instead, strong electrophiles replace a hydrogen. This is **electrophilic aromatic substitution (EAS)**, the main way aromatic rings are functionalized in synthesis and in drug manufacture.

### The general mechanism

1. **Attack.** The ring $\pi$ electrons attack the electrophile $\text{E}^+$, forming a resonance-stabilized carbocation called the **arenium ion** (sigma complex). Aromaticity is lost. This is the **slow, rate-determining step**.
2. **Loss of H$^+$.** A base removes the proton on the sp$^3$ carbon, restoring the aromatic ring. This step is fast.

The overall result is $\text{Ar-H} + \text{E}^+ \rightarrow \text{Ar-E} + \text{H}^+$. The positive charge in the arenium ion is delocalized onto the carbons ortho and para to the new bond, which is the key to the directing effects covered next week.

### Common reactions and their electrophiles

| Reaction | Reagents | Electrophile | Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bromination or chlorination | Br$_2$ or Cl$_2$ with FeBr$_3$ or FeCl$_3$ | Polarized Br-Br...FeBr$_3$ (acts as Br$^+$) | Ar-Br, Ar-Cl |
| Nitration | HNO$_3$ with H$_2$SO$_4$ | Nitronium ion, NO$_2^+$ | Ar-NO$_2$ |
| Sulfonation | Fuming H$_2$SO$_4$ (SO$_3$) | SO$_3$ or HSO$_3^+$ | Ar-SO$_3$H (reversible) |
| Friedel-Crafts alkylation | R-Cl with AlCl$_3$ | Carbocation R$^+$ | Ar-R |
| Friedel-Crafts acylation | RCOCl with AlCl$_3$ | Acylium ion, RC$\equiv$O$^+$ | Ar-COR |

Sulfuric acid in nitration protonates nitric acid, which loses water to give NO$_2^+$. A nitro group can be reduced to an amine with H$_2$ over Pd, or with Fe or Sn and HCl, a common route to anilines.

### Friedel-Crafts limits

**Alkylation** has three problems: carbocations can **rearrange** (1-chloropropane with AlCl$_3$ gives mostly isopropylbenzene), the product is more reactive than benzene so **polyalkylation** occurs, and the reaction fails on rings with strongly deactivating groups or with an amino group (which complexes the catalyst). **Acylation** avoids rearrangement because the acylium ion is resonance-stabilized, and it stops after one substitution because the acyl group deactivates the ring. A ketone can then be reduced to an alkyl group (Clemmensen: Zn(Hg), HCl; or Wolff-Kishner: H$_2$NNH$_2$, KOH), giving a straight-chain alkylbenzene without rearrangement.

### Practical points

Sulfonation is reversible: heating with dilute aqueous acid removes the sulfonic acid group, so it can be used as a temporary blocking group. Halogenation of benzene needs a Lewis acid, but highly activated rings (phenol, aniline) react with Br$_2$ without catalyst, often at several positions at once.
@example
title: Making propylbenzene without rearrangement
problem: Propose a two-step synthesis of propylbenzene from benzene and a three-carbon reagent, and explain why direct alkylation with 1-chloropropane is a poor choice.
step: Direct alkylation with 1-chloropropane and AlCl$_3$ generates a primary carbocation that undergoes a hydride shift to the more stable secondary cation. The main product would be isopropylbenzene, not propylbenzene.
step: Instead, acylate: benzene plus propanoyl chloride (CH$_3$CH$_2$COCl) with AlCl$_3$ gives propiophenone, because the acylium ion does not rearrange.
step: The ketone deactivates the ring, so only one acyl group is added.
step: Reduce C=O to CH$_2$ by Clemmensen (Zn(Hg), HCl) or Wolff-Kishner (H$_2$NNH$_2$, KOH, heat). The product is propylbenzene.
@mistakes
- Drawing the arenium ion's positive charge on the carbon bearing the electrophile; it sits on the ortho and para carbons relative to the new sp$^3$ carbon.
- Forgetting that the first step (attack) is rate-determining, not deprotonation.
- Using Friedel-Crafts alkylation to put a straight-chain primary alkyl group on a ring.
- Trying a Friedel-Crafts reaction on nitrobenzene or aniline; both fail.

@practice self
prompt: Explain, in terms of the arenium ion, why EAS regenerates an aromatic ring rather than ending with addition of a nucleophile to the carbocation.
hint: Compare the stability of a non-aromatic cyclohexadiene with that of the product when the proton is lost.
solution: The arenium ion has lost aromatic stabilization (roughly 150 kJ/mol for benzene). If a nucleophile added to the cation, the product would be a non-aromatic cyclohexadiene. Losing the proton from the sp$^3$ carbon instead restores the cyclic six-$\pi$-electron aromatic system, which is much more stable, so substitution rather than addition is the favored pathway.
rubric: States that the arenium ion is non-aromatic (aromaticity lost in the attack step).
rubric: States that deprotonation restores the aromatic sextet and is energetically favored over nucleophilic addition.
rubric: Concludes that the net result is substitution, not addition.
