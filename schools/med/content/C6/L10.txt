@article
**Lipids** are defined by solubility, not by a shared functional group: they dissolve in nonpolar solvents and are largely insoluble in water. They include fats and oils, phospholipids, steroids, waxes, fat-soluble vitamins and terpenes.

### Fatty acids

A **fatty acid** is a long unbranched carboxylic acid, usually with an even number of carbons (12 to 22), because biosynthesis adds two carbons at a time from malonyl-CoA. A shorthand $\text{C}n{:}m$ gives the carbon number $n$ and the number of C=C bonds $m$.

| Fatty acid | Shorthand | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Palmitic | 16:0 | saturated |
| Stearic | 18:0 | saturated |
| Oleic | 18:1 ($\Delta^9$, cis) | omega-9 |
| Linoleic | 18:2 ($\Delta^{9,12}$) | omega-6, essential |
| $\alpha$-Linolenic | 18:3 | omega-3, essential |
| Arachidonic | 20:4 | precursor of prostaglandins |

Natural double bonds are almost always **cis**, creating a bend that prevents tight packing, so unsaturated fatty acids and oils have lower melting points than saturated ones of the same length. **Trans** fats (formed by partial hydrogenation) pack like saturated chains and raise LDL cholesterol. Each C=C removes two hydrogens: stearic acid is C$_{18}$H$_{36}$O$_2$, oleic acid C$_{18}$H$_{34}$O$_2$, linoleic acid C$_{18}$H$_{32}$O$_2$.

### Triglycerides

A **triglyceride** (triacylglycerol) is glycerol esterified with three fatty acids. Fats (mostly saturated, solid) and oils (mostly unsaturated, liquid) are both triglycerides. They store energy at about 9 kcal/g, more than twice the energy of carbohydrates.

Key reactions:

- **Saponification**: triglyceride + 3 NaOH (or KOH) gives glycerol + 3 sodium carboxylates (**soap**). The soap forms micelles with hydrophobic tails inward.
- **Hydrogenation**: H$_2$/Ni adds across C=C bonds, converting oils to solid fats (3 mol H$_2$ per mole of a triglyceride with three C=C bonds).
- **Hydrolysis** by lipases in the intestine gives free fatty acids and 2-monoacylglycerol.
- Unsaturated chains undergo **autoxidation** (rancidity) through radical chain reactions at allylic C-H bonds, which antioxidants such as vitamin E interrupt.

### Phospholipids and membranes

A **glycerophospholipid** has glycerol esterified at C1 and C2 with fatty acids and at C3 with phosphate, which is further esterified to a polar head group (choline in phosphatidylcholine, ethanolamine, serine). Sphingolipids use sphingosine instead of glycerol. These molecules are **amphiphilic**: a polar head and two nonpolar tails. In water they spontaneously form **bilayers**, the basic structure of cell membranes, with hydrophobic tails buried inside.

### Steroids

Steroids have a fused four-ring skeleton, three six-membered rings and one five-membered ring (17 carbons, the gonane nucleus). **Cholesterol** (C$_{27}$H$_{46}$O) has a 3-hydroxyl group, one C=C bond in ring B, and a branched eight-carbon side chain. It stiffens membranes and is the precursor of bile acids and steroid hormones (cortisol, estradiol, testosterone) and, after photochemical ring opening, vitamin D. Statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase, a key enzyme of cholesterol synthesis.

### Terpenes and waxes

**Terpenes** are built from five-carbon isoprene units joined head to tail: monoterpenes (10 carbons), sesquiterpenes (15), diterpenes (20; for example retinol, vitamin A), triterpenes (30; squalene, the precursor of cholesterol). **Waxes** are esters of a long-chain fatty acid with a long-chain alcohol.
@example
title: Saponification of a triglyceride
problem: Tristearin (glyceryl tristearate) is heated with aqueous NaOH. Give the products and the moles of NaOH needed per mole of triglyceride.
step: Tristearin contains three ester groups, each from stearic acid (C$_{17}$H$_{35}$CO$_2$-) and glycerol.
step: Each ester undergoes base-promoted hydrolysis, consuming one hydroxide. Three equivalents of NaOH are needed per mole.
step: The products are glycerol (propane-1,2,3-triol) and three equivalents of sodium stearate, C$_{17}$H$_{35}$CO$_2$Na.
step: Sodium stearate is soap: its ionic head dissolves in water and the long hydrocarbon tail dissolves in grease, allowing micelle formation.
@mistakes
- Treating all fats as the same; saturation determines melting point and health effects.
- Assuming natural double bonds are trans; most are cis.
- Writing that saponification needs only one equivalent of base per triglyceride.
- Forgetting that cholesterol is a steroid (four fused rings) and not a triglyceride.

answer: 32
check: 36 - 2 * 2
hint: Each C=C bond removes two hydrogens from the saturated formula.
hint: Two double bonds remove four hydrogens.
solution: $36 - 2\times2 = 32$, so linoleic acid is C$_{18}$H$_{32}$O$_2$.

@practice self
prompt: Explain why a phospholipid in water forms a bilayer, and describe in one or two sentences what part of the molecule faces each phase.
solution: A phospholipid is amphiphilic: its polar phosphate head group is hydrophilic and its two long fatty acid tails are hydrophobic. In water the tails avoid contact with water and cluster together, while the heads face the aqueous phases. Two sheets of molecules oriented tail to tail form a bilayer with a hydrophobic interior and hydrophilic surfaces, the structure of cell membranes. Two tails give a cylindrical shape that favors flat sheets rather than micelles.
rubric: Identifies a polar head and nonpolar tails (amphiphilic).
rubric: Says tails are buried away from water and heads face the water.
rubric: Describes the bilayer as the resulting organization.
hint: Decide which part of the molecule is attracted to water and which is not.
