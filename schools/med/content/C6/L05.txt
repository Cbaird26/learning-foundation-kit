@article
A **carboxylic acid** contains the carboxyl group, -CO$_2$H. Typical pK$_a$ values are about 4 to 5 (acetic acid 4.76, benzoic acid 4.20, formic acid 3.75), which is about eleven pK$_a$ units, or $10^{11}$ times, stronger than ethanol (about 16). This difference is why carboxylic acids are largely ionized at physiological pH while alcohols are not.

### Why are they acidic?

Deprotonation gives the **carboxylate** anion, in which the negative charge is delocalized equally over two oxygens (two equivalent resonance forms; both C-O bonds are the same length). An alkoxide has the charge localized on one oxygen. A more stable conjugate base means a stronger acid.

**Substituent effects.** Electron-withdrawing groups stabilize the carboxylate by induction and increase acidity, with the effect falling off with distance:

| Acid | pK$_a$ |
|---|---|
| CH$_3$CO$_2$H | 4.76 |
| ClCH$_2$CO$_2$H | 2.86 |
| Cl$_2$CHCO$_2$H | 1.29 |
| CF$_3$CO$_2$H | 0.5 |
| ClCH$_2$CH$_2$CH$_2$CO$_2$H | 4.52 |

Electron-donating alkyl groups slightly weaken acidity. Benzoic acids with electron-withdrawing para substituents (NO$_2$) are stronger acids than benzoic acid.

### Henderson-Hasselbalch

$$\text{pH} = \text{pK}_a + \log\frac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}.$$

When pH $=$ pK$_a$, the acid is half ionized. Each pH unit above pK$_a$ multiplies the ratio $[\text{A}^-]/[\text{HA}]$ by 10. This matters for medicine: aspirin (pK$_a \approx 3.5$) is mostly un-ionized in the stomach (pH about 1.5), where it crosses membranes easily, but mostly ionized in blood (pH 7.4). A carboxylic acid is extracted into aqueous NaHCO$_3$ as its sodium salt, leaving neutral organic compounds behind, and recovered by acidifying with HCl.

### Preparation

- **Oxidation** of a primary alcohol or aldehyde (CrO$_3$/H$_2$SO$_4$, KMnO$_4$) gives the acid. Alkylbenzenes with a benzylic hydrogen give benzoic acid with hot KMnO$_4$.
- **Grignard carboxylation**: R-MgX + CO$_2$, then H$_3$O$^+$, gives R-CO$_2$H with one more carbon than the halide.
- **Nitrile hydrolysis**: R-X + CN$^-$ (S$_N$2) gives R-CN, then H$_3$O$^+$ and heat gives R-CO$_2$H. This also adds one carbon, but works only with primary or methyl halides.

### Reactions

- **Fischer esterification**: RCO$_2$H + R'OH $\rightleftharpoons$ RCO$_2$R' + H$_2$O with acid catalyst (equilibrium).
- **Acid chloride formation**: SOCl$_2$ (or oxalyl chloride) converts RCO$_2$H to RCOCl, a reactive intermediate.
- **Reduction**: LiAlH$_4$ (then H$_3$O$^+$) gives a primary alcohol; NaBH$_4$ does not reduce carboxylic acids.
- **Deprotonation** with base gives carboxylate salts, which are water soluble (soaps are sodium salts of long-chain acids).

Spectroscopy: very broad O-H stretch from 2500 to 3300 cm$^{-1}$, C=O near 1710 cm$^{-1}$; the acidic proton at 10 to 12 ppm, and carbonyl carbon at 165 to 185 ppm.
@example
title: Separating a carboxylic acid from a neutral compound
problem: A mixture contains benzoic acid (pK$_a$ 4.20) and naphthalene in diethyl ether. Describe how to separate them using aqueous NaHCO$_3$.
step: Shake the ether solution with aqueous NaHCO$_3$ (the conjugate acid of bicarbonate, H$_2$CO$_3$, has pK$_a$ about 6.4). Bicarbonate is a strong enough base to deprotonate benzoic acid completely.
step: Sodium benzoate is an ionic salt and dissolves in the water layer, while naphthalene, which is neutral and nonpolar, stays in the ether layer.
step: Separate the layers. Evaporate the ether to recover naphthalene.
step: Acidify the aqueous layer with HCl to pH well below 4.2. Benzoic acid is re-protonated, becomes neutral, is poorly soluble in water, and precipitates; it can be filtered off.
@mistakes
- Thinking the acidic hydrogen is on carbon; the acidic proton is the O-H of the carboxyl group.
- Using NaBH$_4$ to reduce a carboxylic acid.
- Forgetting that nitrile and carboxylation routes add one carbon, and that cyanide S$_N$2 fails with tertiary halides.
- Inverting the Henderson-Hasselbalch ratio: it is $[\text{A}^-]/[\text{HA}]$, not $[\text{HA}]/[\text{A}^-]$.
@practice num
prompt: Aspirin has pK$_a \approx 3.5$. In stomach acid at pH 1.5, what is the ratio [HA]/[A$^-$] (neutral to ionized)?
answer: 100
check: 10 ** (3.5 - 1.5)
tol: 1%
hint: Rearrange Henderson-Hasselbalch: $\log([\text{HA}]/[\text{A}^-]) = \text{pK}_a - \text{pH}$.
hint: $\text{pK}_a - \text{pH} = 2.0$.
solution: $\log([\text{HA}]/[\text{A}^-]) = 3.5 - 1.5 = 2.0$, so the ratio is $10^2 = 100$.

@practice num
prompt: Acid X has pK$_a$ 3.0 and acid Y has pK$_a$ 5.0. How many times larger is the K$_a$ of X than that of Y?
answer: 100
check: 10 ** (5.0 - 3.0)
tol: 1%
hint: $K_a = 10^{-\text{pK}_a}$.
hint: Divide $10^{-3.0}$ by $10^{-5.0}$.
solution: $K_{a,X}/K_{a,Y} = 10^{-3.0}/10^{-5.0} = 10^{2} = 100$.

@practice num
prompt: Bromobenzene (0.050 mol) is converted to benzoic acid (molar mass 122.1 g/mol) by Grignard formation, CO$_2$ and acid workup, with an overall yield of 80%. What mass of benzoic acid is obtained?
answer: 4.88
check: 0.050 * 0.80 * 122.1
tol: 1%
unit: g
hint: Each mole of aryl bromide gives at most one mole of acid.
hint: Multiply moles by the yield, then by molar mass.
solution: $0.050 \times 0.80 = 0.040$ mol, and $0.040 \times 122.1 = 4.88$ g.

@practice mc
prompt: Which acid is the strongest?
choice: Acetic acid
choice: Chloroacetic acid
choice*: Trifluoroacetic acid
choice: Butanoic acid
hint: Rank by the electron-withdrawing ability of the substituent on the alpha carbon.
solution: Three fluorines withdraw electron density by induction, stabilizing the carboxylate most (pK$_a$ about 0.5).

@practice mc
prompt: Which sequence converts 1-bromobutane into pentanoic acid?
choice: Mg, ether; then H$_3$O$^+$
choice*: NaCN; then H$_3$O$^+$, heat
choice: NaOH; then H$_3$O$^+$
choice: KMnO$_4$, heat
hint: Pentanoic acid has one more carbon than 1-bromobutane.
solution: Cyanide displaces bromide (S$_N$2) to give pentanenitrile, whose acid hydrolysis gives pentanoic acid. Grignard formation followed by CO$_2$ would also work, but Mg then H$_3$O$^+$ alone gives butane.

@quiz num
prompt: Acetic acid has pK$_a$ 4.76. In a solution at pH 5.76, what fraction of the acid is present as acetate?
answer: 0.909
check: 1 / (1 + 10 ** (4.76 - 5.76))
tol: 0.005
hint: At pH 5.76 the ratio [A$^-$]/[HA] is $10^{1}$.
hint: Fraction ionized $= r/(1 + r)$ with $r = 10$.
solution: $r = 10^{5.76 - 4.76} = 10$, so fraction $= 10/11 = 0.909$.

@quiz mc
prompt: Which reagent reduces a carboxylic acid to a primary alcohol?
choice: NaBH$_4$ in methanol
choice*: LiAlH$_4$, then aqueous acid
choice: H$_2$ with Lindlar catalyst
choice: SOCl$_2$
hint: Carboxylates are poor electrophiles; a very strong hydride donor is needed.
solution: LiAlH$_4$ reduces the carboxylic acid to the primary alcohol. NaBH$_4$ is too weak, and SOCl$_2$ makes an acid chloride.
