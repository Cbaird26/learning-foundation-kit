@article
An **$\alpha$-amino acid** has an amino group and a carboxyl group on the same ($\alpha$) carbon, along with H and a side chain R: H$_2$N-CH(R)-CO$_2$H. All standard amino acids except glycine (R = H) have a stereocenter at the alpha carbon, and the ones found in proteins are the **L** enantiomers (S configuration for most, R for cysteine because of sulfur's CIP priority).

### Zwitterions and isoelectric point

An amino acid in water is a **zwitterion**: the carboxyl group (pK$_a$ about 2) is deprotonated to CO$_2^-$ and the amino group (pK$_a$ about 9 to 10) is protonated to NH$_3^+$. At low pH the molecule carries a net $+1$ charge; at high pH, $-1$. The **isoelectric point (pI)** is the pH at which the net charge is zero. For an amino acid without an ionizable side chain, it is the average of the two pK$_a$ values on either side of the neutral form:

$$\text{pI} = \frac{\text{pK}_{a1} + \text{pK}_{a2}}{2}.$$

For alanine (pK$_a$ 2.34 and 9.69), pI $= 6.02$. For acidic amino acids (aspartic, glutamic) average the two acidic pK$_a$ values; for basic ones (lysine, arginine, histidine), average the two highest. At a pH above the pI a molecule is net negative and moves toward the anode in electrophoresis; below the pI, toward the cathode.

### Classes of side chains

- **Nonpolar**: Gly, Ala, Val, Leu, Ile, Met, Pro, Phe, Trp.
- **Polar, uncharged**: Ser, Thr, Cys, Tyr, Asn, Gln.
- **Acidic**: Asp, Glu (side chain pK$_a$ about 4; negatively charged at pH 7.4).
- **Basic**: Lys (pK$_a$ about 10.5), Arg (about 12.5), His (about 6; the only one near physiological pH).

Two cysteine thiols oxidize to a **disulfide** (cystine), a covalent cross-link in extracellular proteins such as insulin and antibodies. Proline's cyclic side chain disrupts helices.

### Peptide bond

Amino acids link by **amide (peptide) bonds**, formed by condensation of the carboxyl group of one with the amino group of the next, releasing water. A chain of $n$ residues has $n - 1$ peptide bonds. Peptides are written from the **N-terminus** to the **C-terminus** (Gly-Ala is not the same as Ala-Gly). Resonance gives the peptide bond about 40% double-bond character, so the six atoms of the amide group are **planar** and usually **trans**; rotation occurs only about the N-C$_\alpha$ ($\phi$) and C$_\alpha$-C ($\psi$) bonds.

**Synthesis**: in the lab, the amine of one amino acid is protected (Boc or Fmoc) while the carboxyl is activated (with DCC or similar reagents), then the protecting group is removed; solid-phase synthesis automates this. **Sequencing**: Edman degradation (phenyl isothiocyanate) cleaves the N-terminal residue sequentially; today mass spectrometry is standard. Trypsin cleaves after Lys and Arg.

### Levels of structure

1. **Primary**: the amino acid sequence.
2. **Secondary**: local backbone patterns stabilized by N-H...O=C hydrogen bonds, the **$\alpha$-helix** (3.6 residues per turn, bonds between residue $i$ and $i+4$) and the **$\beta$-sheet** (hydrogen bonds between strands).
3. **Tertiary**: the overall 3D fold, stabilized by hydrophobic effect (nonpolar side chains buried), hydrogen bonds, salt bridges and disulfide bonds.
4. **Quaternary**: assembly of multiple chains (hemoglobin is a tetramer).

Denaturation by heat, extreme pH or urea disrupts the folded structure without breaking peptide bonds. A single substitution (sickle cell disease: Glu to Val at position 6 of the $\beta$-globin chain) can change the surface properties.
@example
title: Isoelectric point of glutamic acid
problem: Glutamic acid has pK$_a$ values 2.19 ($\alpha$-COOH), 4.25 (side-chain COOH) and 9.67 ($\alpha$-NH$_3^+$). Find its pI.
step: Write the ionization sequence: net charge $+1$ (all protonated) $\to$ 0 (after the $\alpha$-COOH loses a proton) $\to$ $-1$ (after the side chain loses one) $\to$ $-2$ (after the amine deprotonates).
step: The neutral (zwitterion) form exists between the first and second deprotonations, so the relevant pK$_a$ values are 2.19 and 4.25.
step: pI $= (2.19 + 4.25)/2 = 3.22$. Glutamic acid is net negative at physiological pH.
@mistakes
- Averaging the lowest and highest pK$_a$ values for every amino acid; average the two surrounding the neutral form.
- Writing the amino acid as uncharged H$_2$N-CHR-CO$_2$H in water; it exists as a zwitterion.
- Giving a peptide sequence from C-terminus to N-terminus.
- Counting $n$ peptide bonds in a chain of $n$ residues; there are $n-1$.

@practice self
prompt: Explain why the peptide bond is planar and why that restricts the conformations a polypeptide backbone can adopt.
solution: The nitrogen lone pair delocalizes into the carbonyl, giving the C-N bond partial double-bond character (about 40%). Partial double bonds do not rotate freely, so the carbonyl C, O, amide N, its H and the two flanking alpha carbons lie in one plane, usually trans. The only free rotations are about N-C$_\alpha$ ($\phi$) and C$_\alpha$-C ($\psi$), so the backbone folds in a limited set of allowed ($\phi$, $\psi$) combinations, which give $\alpha$-helices and $\beta$-sheets.

rubric: Says that only the $\phi$ and $\psi$ bonds rotate, limiting backbone conformations.
