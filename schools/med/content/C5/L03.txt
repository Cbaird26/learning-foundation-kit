@article
Almost every organic reaction begins or ends with a proton moving. Learning acidity quantitatively lets you predict which reactions go and which do not, and it is the basis of drug absorption: whether a drug is ionized decides whether it crosses a membrane.

### Brønsted-Lowry and Lewis definitions

A **Brønsted-Lowry acid** donates a proton and a **base** accepts one. Every acid has a **conjugate base** (the acid minus $\text{H}^+$). A **Lewis acid** accepts an electron pair and a **Lewis base** donates one; this broader definition covers $\text{BF}_3$ and $\text{AlCl}_3$ and, as you will see, nucleophiles and electrophiles.

### Ka and pKa

For $\text{HA} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{A}^- + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+$,

$$K_a = \frac{[\text{A}^-][\text{H}_3\text{O}^+]}{[\text{HA}]}, \qquad \text{p}K_a = -\log_{10} K_a.$$

**Lower pKa means stronger acid.** Each unit of pKa is a factor of ten in $K_a$. Approximate values worth memorizing:

| Acid | Approximate pKa |
|---|---|
| Hydronium | $-1.7$ |
| Carboxylic acid | 4-5 |
| Ammonium ($\text{R-NH}_3^+$) | 9-11 |
| Phenol | 10 |
| Water | 15.7 |
| Alcohol | 16-18 |
| Ketone $\alpha$-hydrogen | 19-20 |
| Terminal alkyne | 25 |
| Amine N-H | 35-40 |
| Alkane C-H | 50 |

### Direction of an acid-base reaction

An acid-base equilibrium favors the side with the **weaker acid** (higher pKa), that is, the more stable conjugate base. For $\text{HA} + \text{B}^- \rightleftharpoons \text{A}^- + \text{HB}$,

$$\log_{10} K_{eq} = \text{p}K_a(\text{HB}) - \text{p}K_a(\text{HA}).$$

A base removes a proton essentially completely when the conjugate acid of the base has a pKa at least about 2 to 3 units higher than the acid.

### What makes an acid strong: ARIO

Compare conjugate-base stability using four factors in this order:
- **A**tom: more electronegative atoms, and larger atoms down a group, stabilize negative charge ($\text{H-F}$ is weaker than $\text{H-I}$ because iodide is large, but across a row $\text{CH}_4 < \text{NH}_3 < \text{H}_2\text{O} < \text{HF}$).
- **R**esonance: delocalized charge is far more stable. Carboxylates share charge over two oxygens; that is why $\text{CH}_3\text{CO}_2\text{H}$ (pKa 4.76) is about $10^{11}$ times stronger than ethanol.
- **I**nduction: nearby electronegative atoms withdraw electron density through bonds; $\text{ClCH}_2\text{CO}_2\text{H}$ is stronger than acetic acid.
- **O**rbital (hybridization): more s character holds the pair closer to the nucleus, so acidity is $\equiv\text{C-H} > =\text{C-H} > -\text{C-H}$.

### Henderson-Hasselbalch and drugs

$$\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log_{10}\frac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}.$$

When pH equals pKa the acid is half ionized. For an acidic drug the ionized form dominates at pH above its pKa; for a basic drug (using the pKa of its conjugate acid) the protonated, charged form dominates at pH below the pKa. The neutral form crosses lipid membranes more readily.

@example
title: Ranking acidity
problem: Rank ethanol, acetic acid and phenol from strongest to weakest acid and justify.
step: Write the conjugate bases: ethoxide, acetate and phenoxide. All three carry the negative charge on oxygen, so atom identity does not separate them.
step: Acetate delocalizes the charge over two oxygens; phenoxide delocalizes it into the ring over several atoms but places charge on carbon in some forms; ethoxide is localized on one oxygen.
step: Acetate is best stabilized, then phenoxide, then ethoxide. The order of acidity is acetic acid (pKa 4.76) > phenol (pKa 10) > ethanol (pKa 16).

@example
title: Direction and size of an equilibrium
problem: Will sodium ethoxide deprotonate acetic acid, and by how much is the equilibrium favored? Use pKa 4.76 for acetic acid and pKa 16 for ethanol.
step: The reaction is $\text{CH}_3\text{CO}_2\text{H} + \text{EtO}^- \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{CO}_2^- + \text{EtOH}$. The acid on the left is acetic acid and the acid on the right is ethanol.
step: The equilibrium favors the weaker acid, ethanol (pKa 16), so the reaction goes forward.
step: $\log_{10} K_{eq} = 16 - 4.76 = 11.24$, so $K_{eq} \approx 10^{11}$.

@mistakes
- Reading "stronger acid" as "higher pKa"; it is the opposite.
- Comparing acidity of the acid hydrogens by the stability of the acid rather than the stability of the conjugate base.
- Using the pKa of the neutral base instead of the pKa of its conjugate acid when applying Henderson-Hasselbalch to amines.
- Ranking by induction when resonance or atom identity already decides the order.

prompt: Why is acetic acid a much stronger acid than ethanol?
choice: The O-H bond in ethanol is stronger than the O-H bond in acetic acid by a large amount, and nothing else matters.
choice*: Acetate is stabilized by resonance that delocalizes the negative charge over two oxygens, whereas ethoxide is localized.
choice: Acetic acid contains more hydrogens than ethanol.

hint: Compare the conjugate bases, not the acids.

@practice num
prompt: Aspirin has a pKa of about 3.5. In the stomach at pH 1.5, what is the ratio $[\text{A}^-]/[\text{HA}]$? Give a decimal.
answer: 0.01
check: 10 ** (1.5 - 3.5)
tol: 2%
hint: Use $\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log([\text{A}^-]/[\text{HA}])$.
hint: $\log$ of the ratio is $1.5 - 3.5 = -2.0$.

@practice self
prompt: Use the pKa values to rank acidity and explain. The following pKa values are supplied: acetic acid (O-H) 4.8, phenol (O-H) 10, ethanol (O-H) 16, acetone ($\alpha$ C-H) 19, ethyne (C-H) 25, ammonia (N-H) 38. (a) Rank the six from strongest to weakest acid. (b) Explain the gap between ethyne and ammonia using the hybridization and atom factors. (c) Is the equilibrium for ethanol plus sodium amide ($\text{NaNH}_2$) favorable? Estimate $K_{eq}$.
hint: Lower pKa means stronger acid.
hint: For (c), log Keq equals the pKa of the weaker acid minus the pKa of the stronger.
solution: (a) Acetic acid > phenol > ethanol > acetone > ethyne > ammonia (increasing pKa). (b) The conjugate base of ethyne is an sp carbanion (50% s character), whose lone pair is held close to the nucleus and is relatively stable, although carbon is less electronegative than nitrogen. The amide anion has a lone pair in an $sp^3$-like orbital on nitrogen, which has far less s character; the pKa 38 reflects a very basic, poorly stabilized anion. In the comparison of the acids, ethyne is much more acidic than ammonia by 13 pKa units. (c) Ethanol (pKa 16) plus $\text{NH}_2^-$ gives ethoxide and ammonia (pKa 38): $\log K_{eq} = 38 - 16 = 22$, so $K_{eq} \approx 10^{22}$, a completely favorable reaction.
rubric: The ranking runs from acetic acid through ammonia in order of increasing pKa.
rubric: The explanation mentions the stability of the conjugate base and the role of orbital s character.
rubric: The $K_{eq}$ estimate uses the pKa difference, 38 minus 16, giving about $10^{22}$.

answer: 5.7
check: 15.7 - 10.0
tol: 0.005
hint: The equilibrium favors the weaker acid.
