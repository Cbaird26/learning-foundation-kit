@article
A **functional group** is a specific arrangement of atoms that gives a molecule its characteristic reactivity. Once you can see functional groups, a huge drug molecule becomes a handful of familiar pieces.

### The groups you must know

| Group | Key feature | Suffix or prefix |
|---|---|---|
| Alkene | C=C | -ene |
| Alkyne | C$\equiv$C | -yne |
| Arene | benzene ring | -benzene |
| Haloalkane | C-X (F, Cl, Br, I) | fluoro-, chloro-, bromo-, iodo- |
| Alcohol | C-OH | -ol |
| Ether | C-O-C | alkoxy- |
| Amine | C-N (no C=O on N) | -amine |
| Aldehyde | RCHO | -al |
| Ketone | RC(=O)R | -one |
| Carboxylic acid | RCO$_2$H | -oic acid |
| Ester | RCO$_2$R' | -oate |
| Amide | RC(=O)N | -amide |
| Nitrile | C$\equiv$N | -nitrile |

### Classifying carbons, alcohols and amines

A carbon is **primary, secondary, tertiary or quaternary** when it is bonded to one, two, three or four other carbons. An alcohol is classified by the carbon bearing the OH: $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-OH}$ is a tertiary alcohol. An **amine** is classified by the number of carbons on nitrogen, not on the attached carbon: $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-NH}_2$ is a primary amine even though it has a tertiary carbon.

### IUPAC naming of alkanes

1. Find the **longest continuous carbon chain**; its length gives the parent: meth-, eth-, prop-, but-, pent-, hex-, hept-, oct-, non-, dec-.
2. Number from the end that gives the **lowest number at the first point of difference** among substituents.
3. Name each substituent (methyl, ethyl, propyl, chloro, and so on) with its locant; use di-, tri-, tetra- for repeats.
4. List substituents in **alphabetical order**, ignoring multiplying prefixes (di-, tri-), so *ethyl* comes before *dimethyl*.
5. Write as one word with hyphens between numbers and letters and commas between numbers.

For a compound with a principal functional group, that group's suffix is used and it gets the lowest possible number, ahead of substituents and even ahead of double bonds. In an alcohol, the chain must contain the carbon bearing OH: $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$ is propan-1-ol.

### Cycloalkanes

Add **cyclo-** to the parent: cyclohexane. With one substituent no number is needed (methylcyclohexane). With two or more, number the ring so substituents get the lowest locants, breaking ties alphabetically. If a ring has a principal group such as OH, that carbon is C1.

### Skeletal structures

In a line-angle drawing each vertex or end is a carbon, hydrogens on carbon are implied to complete four bonds, and heteroatoms with their hydrogens are drawn explicitly. Learn to count: a zigzag with five vertices is a five-carbon chain. Always return to the name by finding the longest chain first.

@example
title: Naming a branched alkane
problem: Name $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$.
step: The longest chain runs through all main-chain carbons and has seven carbons, so the parent is heptane. The two methyl groups are branches.
step: Number from the left: methyls on C3 and C4. Number from the right: methyls on C4 and C5. The first set is lower at the first point of difference.
step: Two identical substituents take the prefix di-: 3,4-dimethyl.
step: The name is **3,4-dimethylheptane**.

@example
title: Naming an alcohol
problem: Name $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$.
step: The longest chain containing the carbon bonded to OH has four carbons: butane, so the parent is butan-1-ol when numbered from the OH end.
step: The OH must get the lowest number, so C1 is the carbon bearing OH.
step: The methyl group is on C3 (counting C1 to C4 from the OH carbon).
step: The name is **3-methylbutan-1-ol**.

@mistakes
- Choosing the chain drawn horizontally rather than the longest chain; always count all possible paths.
- Alphabetizing by the prefix di- or tri- (it is ignored), or forgetting to alphabetize at all.
- Numbering an alcohol from the end nearest a substituent instead of giving the OH the lowest locant.
- Calling $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-NH}_2$ a tertiary amine; amines are classified by substituents on nitrogen.

@practice ms
prompt: Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) contains a benzene ring bearing a carboxyl group and an acetoxy group. Which functional groups are present in aspirin?
choice*: Carboxylic acid
choice*: Ester
choice: Amide
choice: Ketone
choice: Ether
hint: One substituent is $\text{CO}_2\text{H}$. The other is $\text{OC(=O)CH}_3$ attached to the ring through oxygen.
hint: A carbonyl bonded to an oxygen that is bonded to another carbon is an ester.
solution: The $\text{CO}_2\text{H}$ is a carboxylic acid and the $\text{OC(=O)CH}_3$ is an ester (the carbonyl is bonded to O, which is bonded to the ring). There is no amide, no ketone and no ether.

choice: 2-ethylbutane
choice: 2-methylpentane
choice*: 3-methylpentane
choice: 3-ethylbutane

@practice num
prompt: How many carbon atoms are in 3-ethyl-2,2-dimethylhexane?
answer: 10
check: 6 + 2 + 2
hint: Add the parent chain and every substituent.
solution: Hexane has 6 carbons, ethyl adds 2, and two methyl groups add 2: 10 carbons. The formula is $\text{C}_{10}\text{H}_{22}$.

@practice mc
prompt: A six-membered saturated ring bears an OH group on one carbon and a methyl group on the adjacent carbon. What is the name?
choice: 1-methylcyclohexan-2-ol
choice*: 2-methylcyclohexan-1-ol
choice: 6-methylcyclohexanol
choice: 2-hydroxy-1-methylcyclohexane
hint: The principal group (OH) defines C1 in a ring.
solution: The alcohol is the principal group, so the carbon bearing OH is C1 and the methyl is on the adjacent carbon, C2: 2-methylcyclohexan-1-ol (also written 2-methylcyclohexanol).

@practice self
prompt: Name and draw three molecules. (a) Draw the skeletal structure of 2,4-dimethylhexan-3-ol and classify the alcohol. (b) Name $\text{CH}_3\text{CH(Cl)CH}_2\text{CH(CH}_3)_2$. (c) Acetaminophen is a benzene ring with an OH group and an $\text{NHC(=O)CH}_3$ group on opposite (para) positions; list its functional groups.
hint: Find the longest chain first, then number from the end giving the OH the lowest locant.
hint: For (b), when locant sets tie, alphabetical order decides.
solution: (a) A six-carbon zigzag with methyl branches on C2 and C4 and an OH on C3. The carbon bearing OH is bonded to two other carbons, so this is a secondary alcohol. (b) The longest chain has five carbons. Locants are $\{2,4\}$ either way, so alphabetical order decides: chloro is cited first and gets the lower number, giving 2-chloro-4-methylpentane. (c) Phenol (aromatic OH), secondary amide (the N-H amide) and a benzene ring.
rubric: Skeletal drawing has six chain carbons, two methyls at C2 and C4, and OH at C3.
rubric: The alcohol in (a) is classified as secondary.
rubric: The name in (b) is 2-chloro-4-methylpentane, using alphabetical priority to break the tie.
rubric: Acetaminophen's groups include a phenol, an amide and an arene.

@quiz num
prompt: How many hydrogen atoms are in 4-ethyl-2,2-dimethylheptane, a saturated acyclic hydrocarbon?
answer: 24
check: 2 * (7 + 2 + 2) + 2
hint: Count carbons first: parent plus substituents.
hint: An acyclic alkane is $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$ with $n = 11$.
solution: There are $7 + 2 + 2 = 11$ carbons, so the formula is $\text{C}_{11}\text{H}_{24}$: 24 hydrogens.

@quiz mc
prompt: Which of the following is a tertiary amine?
choice*: $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N}$
choice: $\text{CH}_3\text{NHCH}_3$
choice: $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-NH}_2$
choice: $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$
hint: Count the carbon groups attached directly to nitrogen.
solution: A tertiary amine has three carbon groups on nitrogen. $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N}$ qualifies. $\text{CH}_3\text{NHCH}_3$ is secondary, and $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-NH}_2$ and $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$ are primary (one carbon on nitrogen).
