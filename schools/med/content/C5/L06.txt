@article
A **mechanism** is the step-by-step account of how bonds break and form. In this course nearly all reactions are **polar**: electron-rich sites attack electron-poor sites. Curved arrows are the shorthand that records electron movement, and they are the most important notation you will learn.

### Curved arrows

A curved arrow starts where a pair of electrons **is** (a lone pair or a bond) and ends where the pair **goes** (a new bond, a lone pair on an atom, or a new bond position). Rules:
1. Arrows show **electron** movement, never atom movement.
2. The tail begins at a lone pair or bond, never at a positive charge or an atom without electrons.
3. The head points to an atom or to the middle of a bond to be formed.
4. Check every atom after the arrow: octets, formal charges and total charge must balance.

Three elementary steps account for most reactions:
- **Proton transfer:** a lone pair on a base forms a bond to $\text{H}$ while the H-A bond electrons move onto A.
- **Nucleophilic attack:** a lone pair or $\pi$ bond forms a bond to an electrophilic atom.
- **Loss of a leaving group:** a bond to the leaving group breaks and both electrons leave with it (a carbocation or a new bond results).

### Nucleophiles, electrophiles and leaving groups

A **nucleophile** (Lewis base) donates an electron pair to an electrophilic atom; it may be anionic ($\text{HO}^-$, $\text{CN}^-$) or neutral with a lone pair ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{NH}_3$) or a $\pi$ bond. An **electrophile** (Lewis acid) accepts the pair: carbocations, carbonyl carbons and carbons bearing a good leaving group. **Good leaving groups are weak bases** (stable on their own): $\text{I}^-$, $\text{Br}^-$, $\text{Cl}^-$, tosylate and water (after protonation of OH); $\text{HO}^-$ and $\text{RO}^-$ are poor leaving groups.

### Thermodynamics and kinetics

The **Gibbs free energy** change controls the position of equilibrium:

$$\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ = -RT\ln K_{eq}.$$

At 298 K, a $\Delta G^\circ$ of $-5.7\ \text{kJ/mol}$ corresponds to $K_{eq} \approx 10$, and each additional $-5.7\ \text{kJ/mol}$ multiplies $K$ by another factor of ten. The **rate** depends on the activation energy $\Delta G^\ddagger$, the height of the barrier, not on $\Delta G^\circ$. A reaction can be strongly favorable but slow.

### Energy diagrams

On a diagram of energy versus reaction coordinate, a **transition state** is a maximum (not isolable) and an **intermediate** is a local minimum between two transition states. A multistep reaction has a **rate-determining step**: the step whose transition state is highest in energy on the diagram.

The **Hammond postulate**: the transition state of a step resembles whichever neighbor (reactant or product) is closer to it in energy. For an endergonic step the transition state resembles the product, so factors that stabilize the product (like a more stable carbocation) also lower the barrier.

### Rates and temperature

In general, raising the temperature increases the rate; as a rough rule, a $10^\circ\text{C}$ rise about doubles the rate of many reactions near room temperature. The Arrhenius equation $k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}$ gives this precisely.

@example
title: A proton transfer with curved arrows
problem: Draw the mechanism for hydroxide removing the acidic proton from acetic acid.
step: Identify the nucleophile/base: hydroxide has lone pairs on oxygen. The acidic hydrogen is on the O-H of acetic acid.
step: Arrow 1 starts at a lone pair on $\text{HO}^-$ and points to the acidic hydrogen, forming a new O-H bond (making water).
step: Arrow 2 starts at the O-H bond of acetic acid and points to the oxygen, giving it a third lone pair and a negative charge.
step: The product is acetate and water; check that the total charge is $-1$ on each side.

@example
title: From $\Delta G^\circ$ to $K_{eq}$
problem: A reaction has $\Delta G^\circ = -11.4\ \text{kJ/mol}$ at $298\ \text{K}$. Estimate $K_{eq}$ using $RT = 2.478\ \text{kJ/mol}$.
step: $K_{eq} = e^{-\Delta G^\circ/RT}$.
step: $-\Delta G^\circ/RT = 11.4/2.478 \approx 4.60$.
step: $K_{eq} = e^{4.60} \approx 100$. Each $5.7\ \text{kJ/mol}$ is a factor of ten, and $11.4$ is two such steps.

@mistakes
- Starting a curved arrow at an atom or a positive charge rather than at a pair of electrons.
- Using a double-headed arrow, a half-headed arrow or a straight arrow when a curved arrow is required.
- Confusing a transition state (a maximum, not isolable) with an intermediate (a minimum that can sometimes be isolated).
- Assuming a large negative $\Delta G^\circ$ guarantees a fast reaction; the rate depends on the barrier.

prompt: Show the mechanism for the reaction of hydroxide with methyl bromide, $\text{HO}^- + \text{CH}_3\text{Br} \to \text{CH}_3\text{OH} + \text{Br}^-$ (a single step). Draw the curved arrows, identify the nucleophile, electrophile and leaving group, and describe how the energy diagram would look. Then draw the mechanism of protonation of water by $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ to show you can distinguish proton transfer from substitution.
solution: Arrow 1: from a lone pair on the oxygen of $\text{HO}^-$ to the carbon of $\text{CH}_3\text{Br}$ (forming the O-C bond). Arrow 2: from the C-Br bond to bromine (bromide leaves with the pair). Nucleophile: hydroxide. Electrophile: the carbon of methyl bromide (polarized $\delta+$ by bromine). Leaving group: bromide. With a single concerted step, the energy diagram has one barrier and no intermediate; the products are lower in energy than the reactants, so $\Delta G^\circ < 0$. Proton transfer between $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ and water: one arrow from a lone pair on water oxygen to a hydrogen of hydronium and one arrow from that O-H bond to the oxygen of hydronium, giving a new $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ and water; no carbon is involved.
hint: Start every arrow at a lone pair or a bond.
hint: The first arrow starts on the nucleophile; the second starts at the bond to the leaving group and ends on the leaving group, which leaves with the pair.
rubric: Arrows begin at electron pairs and end at atoms or bonds, with charges balancing.
rubric: Hydroxide is identified as the nucleophile, carbon as the electrophile and bromide as the leaving group.
rubric: A one-barrier energy diagram with no intermediate is described.
