@article
Molecules with the same connectivity can still differ in three-dimensional arrangement. These are **stereoisomers**. Stereochemistry matters most in medicine because proteins, enzymes and receptors are themselves chiral, so they respond differently to different stereoisomers of a drug.

### Chirality and stereocenters

An object is **chiral** if it is not superimposable on its mirror image (your hands are the model). The usual source in organic molecules is a **stereocenter**: an $sp^3$ carbon bonded to four different groups. A molecule with one stereocenter is always chiral. Its mirror image is a different molecule called its **enantiomer**. Enantiomers have identical melting points, boiling points and NMR spectra, and rotate plane-polarized light by equal amounts in opposite directions.

### Assigning R and S

The **Cahn-Ingold-Prelog (CIP)** rules label each stereocenter:
1. Rank the four substituents by atomic number of the atom directly attached (highest number is priority 1).
2. If tied, move outward and compare the sets of atoms attached to each; compare at the first point of difference, set by set. Double bonds count as two single bonds to the same atom (duplicate atoms).
3. Orient the molecule with the **lowest-priority group pointing away** from you. Trace $1 \to 2 \to 3$. Clockwise is **R** (rectus); counterclockwise is **S** (sinister).
4. If the lowest priority points toward you, assign as above and then **reverse** the answer.

Example priorities: $-\text{OH} > -\text{NH}_2 > -\text{CO}_2\text{H} > -\text{CH}_3 > -\text{H}$ (a carbon with three oxygens outranks a carbon with three carbons or hydrogens). $\text{R}$ and $\text{S}$ describe configuration; they say nothing directly about the sign of rotation. A $(+)$ or $(-)$ label (dextrorotatory or levorotatory) must be measured.

### Counting stereoisomers

A molecule with $n$ stereocenters has at most $2^n$ stereoisomers. **Diastereomers** are stereoisomers that are not mirror images; they differ in physical properties. A **meso** compound has stereocenters but is achiral because of an internal mirror plane; (2R,3S)-2,3-dibromobutane is the standard example, so 2,3-dibromobutane has only three stereoisomers, not four.

### Optical activity

A chiral sample rotates plane-polarized light. The **specific rotation** is

$$[\alpha] = \frac{\alpha_{obs}}{l \cdot c},$$

with $l$ the path length in decimeters and $c$ the concentration in g/mL. A 50:50 mixture of enantiomers is a **racemic mixture**, optically inactive. **Enantiomeric excess** measures purity:

$$\text{ee} = \frac{|[R]-[S]|}{[R]+[S]} \times 100\% = \frac{\text{observed } [\alpha]}{[\alpha]_{pure}} \times 100\%.$$

### Why enantiomers act differently in the body

A receptor or enzyme active site is a chiral pocket. One enantiomer can fit the binding groups in the right places and the other cannot, just as a right hand does not fit a left-handed glove. Often one enantiomer is the active drug (the **eutomer**) and the other is less active, inactive or has different effects. Examples: (S)-ibuprofen is the active cyclooxygenase inhibitor, although the drug is sold as a racemate because the body converts much of the (R) form to the (S) form; (S)-naproxen is marketed as a single enantiomer; esomeprazole is the (S)-enantiomer of omeprazole. Enantiomers can also differ in metabolism and transport, and diastereomers of a drug are different compounds with different properties.

@example
title: Assigning R and S
problem: 2-Butanol, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}(\text{OH})\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$, is viewed with its hydrogen pointing away from you. Going from OH to ethyl to methyl, the path runs clockwise. Is it R or S?
step: Rank substituents: OH (oxygen) is first. Ethyl and methyl are both carbon; ethyl has attached (C,H,H) versus methyl (H,H,H), so ethyl is second and methyl third. Hydrogen is last.
step: The lowest priority (H) is pointing away, as required.
step: Trace OH $\to$ ethyl $\to$ methyl: clockwise, so the configuration is **R**.

@example
title: Counting stereoisomers
problem: How many stereoisomers does 2,3-dibromobutane have?
step: There are two stereocenters (C2 and C3), so the maximum is $2^2 = 4$.
step: The (2R,3R) and (2S,3S) forms are enantiomers of each other.
step: The (2R,3S) form has an internal mirror plane because both centers carry the same four groups; it is meso and identical to (2S,3R).
step: The total is **three** stereoisomers: one pair of enantiomers and one meso compound.

@mistakes
- Assuming R means dextrorotatory (+) or S levorotatory (-); the label and the sign are unrelated.
- Forgetting to reverse the answer when the lowest-priority group points toward you.
- Counting $2^n$ stereoisomers for a molecule with an internal plane of symmetry (meso compounds reduce the count).
- Ranking substituents by size or by the first atom's connectivity instead of atomic number at the first point of difference.
