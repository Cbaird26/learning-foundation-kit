@article
Spectroscopy lets you find out what a molecule is without breaking it up chemically. Infrared (IR) spectroscopy tells you which **functional groups** are present; mass spectrometry (MS) tells you the **molecular mass** and pieces of the structure. Together they are the first step in identifying an unknown, and IR is used in drug quality control.

### Infrared spectroscopy

Bonds vibrate (stretch and bend) at characteristic frequencies. A bond absorbs IR light when the vibration changes its dipole moment, so polar bonds give strong bands. The horizontal axis is **wavenumber** in $\text{cm}^{-1}$ (higher means higher energy); stronger bonds and lighter atoms vibrate at higher wavenumber. Read the spectrum from left to right and ask which bands are present, and just as importantly which are absent.

| Region ($\text{cm}^{-1}$) | Bond and appearance |
|---|---|
| 3200-3600 | O-H of alcohol: strong, **broad** |
| 2500-3300 | O-H of carboxylic acid: very broad, overlapping C-H |
| 3300-3500 | N-H: one band (secondary amine or amide) or two (primary); sharper than O-H |
| about 3300 | $\equiv$C-H of a terminal alkyne: sharp, strong |
| 3000-3100 | $sp^2$ C-H (alkene, arene) |
| 2850-3000 | $sp^3$ C-H |
| 2700-2850 | aldehyde C-H (a pair of weak bands) |
| 2100-2260 | C$\equiv$C (weak) and C$\equiv$N (medium, sharp) |
| 1735-1750 | C=O of ester |
| 1700-1725 | C=O of ketone, aldehyde or carboxylic acid |
| 1630-1690 | amide C=O; also C=C |
| 1450-1600 | aromatic ring C=C |
| 1050-1250 | C-O stretch of alcohols, ethers, esters |

Conjugation lowers the C=O frequency by about $30\ \text{cm}^{-1}$; ring strain raises it. The region below about $1500\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ is the **fingerprint region**, unique to each molecule.

### Mass spectrometry

In electron-impact MS the molecule loses an electron to form the **molecular ion** $\text{M}^{\bullet+}$, whose $m/z$ gives the molecular mass. The ion fragments and the spectrum plots relative abundance against $m/z$; the tallest peak is the **base peak**.

- **Nitrogen rule**: a molecule with an odd number of nitrogen atoms has an odd molecular mass; zero or two nitrogens give an even mass.
- **Isotope patterns**: chlorine gives M and M+2 in a ratio of about **3:1** ($^{35}\text{Cl}$ : $^{37}\text{Cl}$); bromine gives M and M+2 in about **1:1** ($^{79}\text{Br}$ : $^{81}\text{Br}$).
- **Common losses**: 15 (CH$_3$), 18 ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$, typical of alcohols), 28 (CO or $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$), 29 (C$_2$H$_5$ or CHO), 43 (C$_3$H$_7$ or CH$_3$CO), 45 ($\text{OC}_2\text{H}_5$ or $\text{CO}_2\text{H}$).
- **Alpha cleavage**: a bond next to a carbonyl, an alcohol carbon or an amine breaks to give a stabilized cation: methyl ketones give the acylium ion at $m/z$ 43, and primary alcohols give $\text{CH}_2=\text{OH}^+$ at $m/z$ 31.
- **McLafferty rearrangement**: a carbonyl compound with a $\gamma$-hydrogen transfers it through a six-membered transition state, losing an alkene and giving a fragment with an even $m/z$ (for example, $m/z$ 58 for 2-pentanone).

### A strategy for unknowns

1. Use MS to get $M$, check the nitrogen rule and halogen patterns, and calculate the degrees of unsaturation from a likely formula.
2. Use IR to find the functional groups (O-H, N-H, C=O, C$\equiv$N, C$\equiv$C).
3. Use fragment peaks to place groups: the base peak and the major losses.
4. Check that the structure explains **all** the data.

@example
title: Identifying a carbonyl compound from IR
problem: An IR spectrum shows a strong sharp band at $1715\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, $sp^3$ C-H bands just below $3000\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, and no absorption between $3200$ and $3600\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ or near $2700\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. What functional group is present?
step: The strong band near $1715\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ indicates a carbonyl.
step: No broad band at $3200$ to $3600$ or at $2500$ to $3300\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ rules out an alcohol or a carboxylic acid; no aldehyde C-H bands near $2700$ to $2850$ rules out an aldehyde.
step: $1715\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ fits a saturated ketone; an ester would absorb nearer $1740$.

@example
title: Reading a mass spectrum
problem: An unknown has a molecular ion at $m/z$ 86, a base peak at $m/z$ 43, and peaks at $m/z$ 71 and 58. IR shows a strong band at $1715\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. Propose a structure.
step: The molecular mass is 86 (even, so zero nitrogens). A ketone formula $\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}\text{O}$ has mass $5(12) + 10 + 16 = 86$ with one degree of unsaturation (the C=O).
step: The peak at 71 is $\text{M} - 15$ (loss of methyl), and the base peak at 43 is the acylium ion $\text{CH}_3\text{C}\equiv\text{O}^+$ from alpha cleavage.
step: The peak at 58 is a McLafferty product. The compound is **pentan-2-one**.

@mistakes
- Treating the strongest peak in an MS as the molecular ion; the base peak is the most abundant fragment, the molecular ion is the highest mass peak apart from isotope peaks.
- Forgetting that a broad O-H band at $3300\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ is not a carbonyl, and confusing the O-H of an acid with that of an alcohol.
- Reading the C-H region only: the key evidence is usually the presence or absence of O-H, N-H and C=O bands.
- Ignoring the nitrogen rule when checking a proposed formula.

choice: Alcohol
choice: Carboxylic acid
choice*: Ketone
choice: Primary amine
hint: Strong carbonyl band, and no O-H or N-H.
solution: The strong $1715\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ absorption indicates C=O, and without an O-H or N-H band the compound is a ketone (or aldehyde, but there are no aldehyde C-H bands). Carboxylic acids show a very broad band from $2500$ to $3300\ \text{cm}^{-1}$.

@practice num
prompt: A compound has a molecular ion at $m/z$ 114. A fragment is observed after loss of water ($18$) and then a methyl radical ($15$). What is the $m/z$ of this fragment?
answer: 81
check: 114 - 18 - 15
hint: Subtract each loss from the molecular mass.
solution: $114 - 18 - 15 = 81$.

@practice mc
prompt: The mass spectrum of an alkyl halide shows peaks at $m/z$ 122 and 124 of nearly equal height. What does this indicate?
choice: One chlorine atom
choice*: One bromine atom
choice: Two chlorine atoms
choice: One fluorine atom
hint: Compare isotope abundance ratios.
solution: A 1:1 doublet two mass units apart reflects $^{79}\text{Br}$ and $^{81}\text{Br}$. One chlorine would give a 3:1 pattern, and fluorine has a single stable isotope.

@practice num
prompt: Acetophenone has the molecular formula $\text{C}_8\text{H}_8\text{O}$. How many degrees of unsaturation does it have?
answer: 5
check: (2 * 8 + 2 - 8) / 2
hint: Ignore oxygen.
solution: $(18 - 8)/2 = 5$: a benzene ring (4) and a C=O (1).

MS has $M^{\bullet+}$ at $m/z$ 86, a base peak at 43 and peaks at 71 and 58. Unknown B: IR has a strong, broad band centered at $3350\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, $sp^3$ C-H near $2950\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, a strong band at $1050\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, and no C=O band. MS has a weak $M^{\bullet+}$ at $m/z$ 74, peaks at $m/z$ 56 and 31 (the base peak). For each, name the functional group, state the evidence, and propose a structure.

Unknown B: primary alcohol (broad O-H at $3350\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, C-O at $1050\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, no carbonyl). $M$ = 74 fits $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}\text{O}$ with no unsaturation. The peak at 56 is $\text{M} - 18$ (loss of water), and the base peak at 31 is $\text{CH}_2=\text{OH}^+$ from alpha cleavage, so the structure is butan-1-ol.

@quiz num
prompt: Bromoethane, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{Br}$, shows a pair of molecular ion peaks. Using $^{12}\text{C}$, $^{1}\text{H}$ and integer masses of 79 and 81 for bromine, what is the $m/z$ of the heavier molecular ion peak?
answer: 110
check: 2 * 12 + 5 * 1 + 81
hint: The heavier peak contains the heavier bromine isotope.
solution: $24 + 5 + 81 = 110$; the lighter peak is at 108 and the two have about equal height.

@quiz mc
prompt: An unknown shows a very broad IR absorption from $2500$ to $3300\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ and a strong band at $1710\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. Which functional group is present?
choice: Ketone
choice: Alcohol
choice*: Carboxylic acid
choice: Ester
hint: The very broad O-H plus the carbonyl point to one group.
solution: The very broad O-H band centered below $3000\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ combined with a C=O near $1710\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ is the signature of a carboxylic acid. A ketone has no O-H band, an alcohol has no C=O band, and an ester has no O-H band.
