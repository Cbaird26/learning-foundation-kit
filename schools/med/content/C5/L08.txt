@article
In an **elimination** (specifically $\beta$-elimination) a base removes a proton from the carbon next to the one bearing the leaving group, and a $\pi$ bond forms:

$$\text{H-C-C-LG} + \text{B}^- \to \text{C=C} + \text{BH} + \text{LG}^-.$$

Elimination competes with substitution, so predicting the outcome is the main skill of this week.

### The E2 reaction

**E2** is one concerted step with rate $= k[\text{R-LG}][\text{base}]$. The base removes the $\beta$-hydrogen while the C-H bond electrons form the $\pi$ bond and the leaving group departs.

- **Anti-periplanar geometry**: the $\beta$-H and the leaving group must lie in the same plane on opposite sides ($180^\circ$ dihedral). In cyclohexanes both must be **axial** (trans-diaxial), so a ring flip may be needed, and a substituent that cannot become axial affects which alkene forms.
- **Zaitsev's rule**: with a small base (ethoxide, hydroxide), the major product is the **more substituted, more stable alkene**. 2-Bromobutane with ethoxide gives mostly but-2-ene (trans > cis).
- **Hofmann product**: a bulky base (potassium tert-butoxide) removes the more accessible, less hindered hydrogen and favors the **less substituted alkene**.
- A tertiary or secondary halide with a strong base promotes E2. Primary halides eliminate with a strong, bulky base.

### The E1 reaction

**E1** shares its first step with SN1: slow ionization to a carbocation. A weak base (often the solvent) then removes a $\beta$-hydrogen. Rate $= k[\text{R-LG}]$. E1 and SN1 give mixtures; heat favors elimination because elimination increases entropy ($\Delta S > 0$ and a larger $-T\Delta S$ term). E1 follows Zaitsev's rule and can involve carbocation rearrangements. Acid-catalyzed dehydration of alcohols is an E1 reaction.

### Substitution or elimination?

Work through these questions in order:
1. **What is the substrate?** Methyl: SN2 only. Primary: SN2 (with a strong, bulky base, E2). Secondary: all four are possible. Tertiary: E2 with a strong base, or SN1/E1 with weak nucleophiles and solvolysis.
2. **How strong is the base?** A strong base (alkoxide, hydroxide, amide) with a secondary or tertiary halide gives E2. A good nucleophile that is a weak base ($\text{I}^-$, $\text{Br}^-$, $\text{CN}^-$, $\text{N}_3^-$, $\text{RS}^-$) gives SN2 on primary and secondary halides.
3. **Is the base bulky?** Bulky bases such as $t$-BuOK and DBU favor elimination.
4. **Temperature:** higher temperature favors elimination over substitution.

| Substrate | Strong, small base/Nu ($\text{HO}^-$, $\text{EtO}^-$) | Strong, bulky base | Good Nu, weak base ($\text{I}^-$, $\text{CN}^-$) | Weak Nu/base, protic solvent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1$^\circ$ | SN2 | E2 | SN2 | no reaction |
| 2$^\circ$ | E2 (and SN2) | E2 | SN2 | slow SN1/E1 |
| 3$^\circ$ | E2 | E2 | E2 or SN1 | SN1 and E1 |

@example
title: Zaitsev versus Hofmann
problem: Predict the major product when 2-bromo-2-methylbutane reacts with (a) sodium ethoxide and (b) potassium tert-butoxide.
step: The substrate is tertiary and the bases are strong, so both reactions are E2. The $\beta$-hydrogens are on the methyl groups (leading to 2-methylbut-1-ene) and on the $\text{CH}_2$ (leading to 2-methylbut-2-ene).
step: With ethoxide, Zaitsev's rule applies. The major product is the more substituted alkene, the trisubstituted 2-methylbut-2-ene.
step: With the bulky tert-butoxide, access to the $\text{CH}_2$ hydrogens is hindered, and the less substituted 2-methylbut-1-ene (Hofmann) becomes the major product.

@example
title: Elimination in a cyclohexane
problem: Why does menthyl chloride eliminate more slowly than neomenthyl chloride with ethoxide? (Menthyl chloride is the trans isomer in which the chlorine is equatorial in the preferred chair, with all substituents equatorial.)
step: E2 needs the leaving group axial and an anti-periplanar axial $\beta$-hydrogen.
step: In menthyl chloride, the chlorine is axial only in the ring-flipped chair, which has the other substituents also axial and is high in energy, so very little reactive conformer is present.
step: In neomenthyl chloride, the chlorine is already axial in the stable chair, with axial hydrogens available on both sides, so the reaction is fast.

@mistakes
- Predicting Zaitsev with a bulky base such as potassium tert-butoxide; the Hofmann product dominates.
- Ignoring the anti-periplanar requirement in rings, where the leaving group must be axial.
- Predicting substitution of a tertiary halide by a strong base like hydroxide; E2 wins.
- Forgetting that heat favors elimination and that E1 and SN1 compete.
