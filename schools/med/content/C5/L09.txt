@article
An **alkene** contains a C=C double bond: two $sp^2$ carbons joined by one $\sigma$ and one $\pi$ bond. The $\pi$ bond is the reactive part, a region of electron density that attacks electrophiles. Alkene chemistry is mostly **addition**: the $\pi$ bond breaks and two new $\sigma$ bonds form.

### Structure, naming and stability

Rotation about a C=C bond is blocked by the $\pi$ bond, so alkenes can be stereoisomers. Name the parent chain as the longest chain **containing** the double bond, number to give the double bond the lowest locant, and give $E$/$Z$: rank the two groups on each carbon by CIP priority; if the two higher-priority groups are on the same side, the alkene is **Z** (zusammen), if opposite, **E** (entgegen). For simple disubstituted alkenes $Z$ is cis and $E$ is trans.

Alkene stability increases with substitution (hyperconjugation): tetrasubstituted > trisubstituted > disubstituted > monosubstituted. Trans is more stable than cis.

### Electrophilic addition of HX

The $\pi$ bond attacks $\text{H}^+$, forming a carbocation; the halide then attacks the cation. **Markovnikov's rule**: the hydrogen adds to the carbon with more hydrogens, so the halogen ends on the more substituted carbon, because the more stable carbocation forms. The carbocation is planar, so a new stereocenter is formed as a racemic mixture, and the carbocation can **rearrange** by a hydride or methyl shift to a more stable cation before capture.

### Acid-catalyzed hydration

Dilute aqueous $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ adds water (Markovnikov), via the same cation. It is reversible, and it can rearrange.

### Halogenation

$\text{Br}_2$ or $\text{Cl}_2$ adds across the double bond through a cyclic **bromonium ion**; the halide opens it from the back, so the addition is **anti**. Cyclohexene gives trans-1,2-dibromocyclohexane (a racemic pair). In water the solvent opens the bromonium ion and a **halohydrin** (Br and OH, anti) forms, with OH on the more substituted carbon.

### Hydroboration-oxidation

$\text{BH}_3\cdot\text{THF}$ followed by $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ and $\text{NaOH}$ gives the **anti-Markovnikov** alcohol: the boron (and then OH) goes to the **less** substituted carbon, and the addition is **syn** (H and OH on the same face). There is no carbocation, so no rearrangements.

### Hydrogenation and epoxidation

$\text{H}_2$ with a metal catalyst ($\text{Pd/C}$, $\text{Pt}$, $\text{Ni}$) adds two hydrogens to one face (**syn**) and gives the alkane. A peroxyacid such as mCPBA converts an alkene to an **epoxide** in one syn step, retaining the alkene's geometry. **Ozonolysis** ($\text{O}_3$ then $\text{Me}_2\text{S}$) cleaves C=C into two carbonyl compounds (aldehydes or ketones).

### Quick reference

| Reagent | Product | Regiochemistry | Stereochemistry |
|---|---|---|---|
| HBr | alkyl bromide | Markovnikov | racemic |
| $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ | alcohol | Markovnikov | racemic |
| $\text{Br}_2$ | vicinal dibromide | none needed | anti |
| 1. $\text{BH}_3$; 2. $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, $\text{HO}^-$ | alcohol | anti-Markovnikov | syn |
| $\text{H}_2$, Pd/C | alkane | none | syn |
| mCPBA | epoxide | none | syn |

@example
title: Markovnikov addition with rearrangement
problem: Predict the major product of 3-methylbut-1-ene with HCl.
step: Protonation of the terminal carbon gives a secondary carbocation at C2.
step: A hydride shift from C3 to C2 gives a tertiary carbocation, which is more stable.
step: Chloride attacks the tertiary cation, giving 2-chloro-2-methylbutane as the major product (with the unrearranged 2-chloro-3-methylbutane as a minor product).

@example
title: Syn versus anti addition
problem: Give the product of 1-methylcyclohexene with $\text{BH}_3$ then $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2/\text{NaOH}$, including its stereochemistry.
step: Boron adds to the less substituted carbon (C2, the CH), and hydrogen adds to the more substituted carbon (C1, which bears the methyl).
step: Addition is syn, so H on C1 and B on C2 are on the same face.
step: Oxidation replaces B with OH with retention, so OH on C2 and H on C1 are cis. The methyl on C1 is therefore trans to the OH: **trans-2-methylcyclohexan-1-ol** (as a racemate).

@mistakes
- Applying Markovnikov's rule to hydroboration-oxidation, which is anti-Markovnikov.
- Forgetting that carbocation pathways (HX, acid hydration) can rearrange but hydroboration cannot.
- Calling bromine addition syn; the bromonium ion forces anti addition.
- Assigning E or Z by the position of the alkyl chains rather than by CIP priority.

answer: 1
check: (2 * 10 + 2 - 16) / 2 - 2
hint: Compute the degrees of unsaturation first.
hint: Each $\text{H}_2$ consumed removes one $\pi$ bond; remaining unsaturation is rings.
