@review
### Key ideas by week
1. **Bonding.** Formal charge is valence minus nonbonding minus bonds; hybridization follows electron domains ($sp^3$, $sp^2$, $sp$); a double bond is $\sigma + \pi$; DoU $= (2c + 2 + n - h - x)/2$.
2. **Nomenclature.** Longest chain, lowest locants, alphabetical order; the principal group takes the suffix and the lowest number; classify amines by substituents on nitrogen.
3. **Acids and bases.** Lower pKa means stronger acid; $\log K_{eq} = \text{p}K_a(\text{product acid}) - \text{p}K_a(\text{reactant acid})$; use ARIO for conjugate-base stability; Henderson-Hasselbalch for ionization.
4. **Conformations.** Anti beats gauche; chairs have axial and equatorial positions; large groups prefer equatorial; $K = e^{\Delta G/RT}$.
5. **Stereochemistry.** CIP priorities, R and S (reverse if the lowest priority faces you); $2^n$ stereoisomers unless meso; enantiomers differ only toward chiral partners such as receptors.
6. **Mechanisms.** Curved arrows start at electron pairs; good leaving groups are weak bases; the rate-determining step has the highest transition state.
7. **SN1 and SN2.** SN2: second order, inversion, methyl > 1$^\circ$, strong nucleophile, polar aprotic. SN1: first order, racemization, 3$^\circ$, polar protic.
8. **E1 and E2.** E2 needs anti-periplanar H and leaving group; Zaitsev with small bases, Hofmann with bulky ones; heat favors elimination.
9. **Alkenes.** Markovnikov addition and rearrangements; anti addition of $\text{Br}_2$; syn, anti-Markovnikov hydroboration-oxidation; syn hydrogenation and epoxidation.
10. **Alkynes.** Acetylide alkylation (primary halides only); Lindlar gives Z, Na/NH$_3$ gives E; $\text{Hg}^{2+}$ hydration gives ketones.
11. **Alcohols and ethers.** PCC versus chromic acid; $\text{SOCl}_2$ and $\text{PBr}_3$ invert; Williamson synthesis uses the less hindered halide; epoxides open at the less substituted carbon under base and the more substituted under acid.
12. **Spectra.** Broad O-H, C=O near 1715, $\equiv$C-H at 3300; M and M+2 patterns, nitrogen rule, alpha cleavage and McLafferty.

### Self-check
- Can you draw every mechanism above from memory with arrows, charges and stereochemistry?
- Can you predict SN1, SN2, E1 or E2 for a new substrate in under a minute and justify it?
- Can you plan a two-step synthesis and say which step sets the stereochemistry?

### Most common errors
- Arrows that start at atoms or charges, or that move atoms.
- Using the wrong direction for pKa (higher pKa is the weaker acid).
- Forgetting to reverse an R/S assignment when the lowest priority faces the viewer.
- Predicting SN2 at a tertiary center or SN1 at a primary one.
- Using Zaitsev's rule with a bulky base, or Markovnikov's rule for hydroboration.

answer: 2.69

tol: 1%
unit: g

hint: Multiply by the molar mass of the alkene, then by the fractional yield.

choice: (R), because SN2 retains configuration
choice*: (S), because SN2 inverts configuration and the CIP priority order is unchanged
choice: A racemic mixture, because the reaction is SN1
choice: (S), because the reaction goes by E2
hint: Identify the mechanism from the substrate, nucleophile and solvent.
hint: Compare the priorities before and after: Br or SH, then hexyl, methyl, hydrogen.
solution: A secondary halide with a strong nucleophile in a polar aprotic solvent reacts by SN2, with inversion. Bromine and sulfur both rank first, so the priorities are unchanged and inversion changes R to S.

@exam self
prompt: Starting from 1-methylcyclohexene, propose a two-step synthesis of 2-methylcyclohexanone. Draw the mechanism of the first step with curved arrows, state its regiochemistry and stereochemistry, and name the reagent for the second step. Then say how you would use IR to confirm that the product has formed and that the intermediate alcohol is gone.
solution: Step 1: hydroboration-oxidation ($\text{BH}_3\cdot\text{THF}$, then $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, $\text{NaOH}$) gives trans-2-methylcyclohexan-1-ol by syn, anti-Markovnikov addition: boron goes to the less substituted carbon and H adds to the more substituted carbon on the same face, with no carbocation. Step 2: oxidize the secondary alcohol with PCC (or chromic acid) to 2-methylcyclohexanone. In IR, the alcohol shows a broad O-H band near $3350\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ and the ketone a strong C=O band near $1715\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. Successful conversion shows the C=O band appearing and the O-H band disappearing.
hint: Which alkene hydration gives OH at the less substituted carbon?
hint: Oxidizing a secondary alcohol gives a ketone.
rubric: Hydroboration-oxidation is identified, with anti-Markovnikov regiochemistry and syn addition.
rubric: PCC or chromic acid is named for the oxidation.
rubric: IR evidence cites loss of broad O-H and appearance of C=O near 1715.
