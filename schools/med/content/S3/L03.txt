@article
The glomerulus filters about 180 L of plasma a day, and the tubule returns more than 99% of the water and sodium to the blood. Each segment has its own transporters, permeabilities and hormone responses, which is why diuretics, hormones and genetic diseases produce such predictable patterns.

### Proximal convoluted tubule

The proximal tubule reabsorbs about 65% of filtered sodium and water, almost all filtered glucose, amino acids and bicarbonate, and much of the phosphate. Fluid leaves **isosmotic** with plasma because water follows solute through aquaporin-1. Key features:

- **Sodium** enters through the Na$^+$/H$^+$ exchanger (NHE3) and cotransporters, driven by the basolateral Na$^+$/K$^+$-ATPase.
- **Glucose** enters via SGLT2 (early) and SGLT1 (late) and leaves by GLUT2. The transport maximum is about 375 mg/min; glucose first appears in urine at a plasma level of about 180 to 200 mg/dL because of nephron heterogeneity (splay).
- **Bicarbonate** is reabsorbed with the help of luminal and intracellular **carbonic anhydrase** (acetazolamide blocks it).
- **Phosphate** is reabsorbed by NaPi-IIa; PTH inhibits it.
- Organic acids and bases (PAH, many drugs, creatinine) are secreted here.

**Fanconi syndrome** is generalized proximal dysfunction: glycosuria with normal plasma glucose, aminoaciduria, phosphaturia and proximal (type 2) renal tubular acidosis. Causes include cystinosis, multiple myeloma light chains, tenofovir and heavy metals.

### Loop of Henle

The **descending limb** is permeable to water (aquaporin-1) but not solute, so fluid becomes concentrated. The **thick ascending limb** is impermeable to water and actively reabsorbs about 25% of filtered sodium through **NKCC2** (Na$^+$-K$^+$-2Cl$^-$), the target of loop diuretics. Potassium recycles back through ROMK, creating a lumen-positive potential that drives paracellular reabsorption of calcium and magnesium. This segment is the **diluting segment**: fluid leaving it is hypotonic. The **countercurrent multiplier** uses this single effect, plus urea recycling and the vasa recta (which act as a countercurrent exchanger), to create a medullary interstitial osmolality of up to about 1200 mOsm/kg.

### Distal convoluted tubule

The DCT reabsorbs sodium and chloride through the **Na$^+$-Cl$^-$ cotransporter (NCC)**, blocked by thiazides. It is also impermeable to water. Calcium reabsorption here is regulated by PTH through TRPV5; thiazides raise calcium reabsorption, and loop diuretics lower it.

### Collecting duct

**Principal cells** reabsorb sodium through the epithelial sodium channel (ENaC) and secrete potassium through ROMK; aldosterone increases both. ADH (acting at V2 receptors) inserts **aquaporin-2** channels and makes the duct water permeable, so water follows the medullary gradient and urine concentrates. ADH also increases urea reabsorption in the medullary collecting duct. **Alpha-intercalated cells** secrete H$^+$ (H$^+$-ATPase, H$^+$/K$^+$-ATPase) and generate new bicarbonate; beta-intercalated cells secrete bicarbonate.

### Fractional excretion of sodium

$$\text{FE}_{Na} = \frac{U_{Na} \times P_{Cr}}{P_{Na} \times U_{Cr}} \times 100.$$

Under 1% suggests avid sodium retention (prerenal states, early glomerulonephritis); over 2% suggests tubular injury (acute tubular necrosis), although diuretics distort it.

### Inherited tubular disorders

| Syndrome | Defect | Mimics | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bartter | NKCC2, ROMK or related loop channels | Loop diuretic | Hypokalemia, metabolic alkalosis, hypercalciuria |
| Gitelman | NCC | Thiazide | Hypokalemia, metabolic alkalosis, hypomagnesemia, hypocalciuria |
| Liddle | Gain of function of ENaC | Hyperaldosteronism with low aldosterone | Hypertension, hypokalemia, metabolic alkalosis, low renin; treat with amiloride |

@example
title: Fractional excretion of sodium in acute kidney injury
problem: A patient with oliguria after prolonged hypotension has urine sodium 60 mEq/L, plasma sodium 140 mEq/L, urine creatinine 40 mg/dL and plasma creatinine 3.0 mg/dL. Calculate FE$_{Na}$ and interpret.
step: Substitute into the formula: $\frac{60 \times 3.0}{140 \times 40} \times 100$.
step: The numerator is 180 and the denominator is 5600, so the fraction is 0.032, or 3.2%.
step: A value above 2% fits acute tubular necrosis: injured tubules cannot reabsorb sodium well, in contrast to the sodium-avid tubules of a prerenal state.

@mistakes
- Believing that the proximal tubule produces dilute urine: fluid leaving it is isosmotic, and dilution occurs in the thick ascending limb and distal tubule.
- Mixing Bartter and Gitelman: Bartter (loop) causes hypercalciuria; Gitelman (thiazide-like) causes hypocalciuria and hypomagnesemia.
- Forgetting that glucose excretion starts below the theoretical transport maximum: the renal threshold is around 180 to 200 mg/dL.
- Using FE$_{Na}$ during diuretic therapy without caution, because diuretics raise sodium excretion; fractional excretion of urea is more reliable then.

answer: 360
check: 120 * (300 / 100)
tol: 1%
unit: mg/min

hint: Filtered load equals GFR times plasma concentration.
solution: 300 mg/dL is 3 mg/mL, so filtered load is $120 \times 3 = 360$ mg/min.

@quiz num
prompt: A patient has a GFR of 100 mL/min and a plasma glucose of 500 mg/dL. Assume glucose reabsorption is at its transport maximum of 375 mg/min. How much glucose is excreted in the urine, in mg/min?
answer: 125
check: 100 * (500 / 100) - 375
tol: 1%
unit: mg/min
hint: Excretion equals filtered load minus reabsorption.
hint: Filtered load is GFR times plasma glucose in mg/mL.
solution: Filtered load is $100 \times 5 = 500$ mg/min; excretion is $500 - 375 = 125$ mg/min.
