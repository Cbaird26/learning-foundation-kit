@article
Glomerular diseases fall into two syndromes that overlap but have very different mechanisms. In **nephritic** disease, inflammation damages the capillary wall and red cells leak. In **nephrotic** disease, injury to the podocyte or barrier lets protein leak with little inflammation.

### The two syndromes

| Feature | Nephritic | Nephrotic |
|---|---|---|
| Hallmark | Hematuria with dysmorphic red cells and **red cell casts** | **Proteinuria over 3.5 g/day** |
| Protein loss | Subnephrotic (under 3.5 g/day) | Heavy; hypoalbuminemia (under 3 g/dL) |
| Other | Hypertension, edema, oliguria, azotemia | Edema, hyperlipidemia, lipiduria (oval fat bodies, Maltese crosses) |
| Complications | Rapid loss of GFR | Thrombosis (loss of antithrombin III; renal vein thrombosis), infection (loss of immunoglobulin) |
| Pathology | Proliferative, inflammatory | Podocyte injury or thickened barrier |

Spot urine protein to creatinine ratio (mg/mg) approximates grams of protein per day: a ratio above about 3.5 is nephrotic range.

### Nephritic diseases

- **Post-streptococcal GN**: children, 1 to 3 weeks after pharyngitis (or about 6 weeks after skin infection); cola-colored urine, low C3, elevated ASO. Biopsy: subepithelial **humps**, granular immunofluorescence. Supportive care.
- **IgA nephropathy (Berger)**: hematuria within 1 to 3 days of an upper respiratory infection; normal complement; mesangial IgA deposits. Henoch-Schönlein purpura is the systemic form.
- **Alport syndrome**: X-linked type IV collagen defect (COL4A5): hematuria, sensorineural hearing loss, eye abnormalities; basket-weave GBM.
- **Rapidly progressive GN** (crescents): **anti-GBM disease** (Goodpasture) has linear IgG deposits and pulmonary hemorrhage, treated with plasma exchange, steroids and cyclophosphamide; **ANCA vasculitis** (granulomatosis with polyangiitis, c-ANCA/PR3; microscopic polyangiitis, p-ANCA/MPO) is pauci-immune; lupus nephritis is immune-complex mediated.
- **Lupus nephritis**: class IV (diffuse proliferative) is the most common severe form; wire-loop lesions, "full house" immunofluorescence, low C3 and C4, anti-dsDNA.
- **Membranoproliferative GN**: tram-track GBM; associated with hepatitis C and cryoglobulinemia.

### Nephrotic diseases

- **Minimal change disease**: most common in children; normal light microscopy, **foot process effacement on electron microscopy**, selective albuminuria; follows infection or NSAIDs or Hodgkin lymphoma; excellent response to **corticosteroids**.
- **Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS)**: African ancestry (APOL1), HIV (collapsing), heroin, obesity; segmental scarring; poorer steroid response.
- **Membranous nephropathy**: common adult primary cause; anti-PLA2R antibodies; thickened GBM with "spike and dome" on silver stain and subepithelial deposits; secondary causes are hepatitis B, SLE, solid tumors, NSAIDs and penicillamine; high risk of renal vein thrombosis.
- **Diabetic nephropathy**: mesangial expansion, GBM thickening, **Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules**, hyaline arteriolosclerosis of both afferent and efferent arterioles; first sign is microalbuminuria; treat with ACE inhibitor or ARB and SGLT2 inhibitor.
- **Amyloidosis**: apple-green birefringence on Congo red stain.

### Treating the nephrotic state

Salt restriction and diuretics for edema, ACE inhibitor or ARB for proteinuria, statin for hyperlipidemia, and anticoagulation considered for severe hypoalbuminemia in membranous disease. Treat the primary or secondary cause (steroids for minimal change; immunosuppression for lupus and ANCA).

@example
title: Nephrotic range proteinuria from a spot sample
problem: A patient has edema. Spot urine protein is 210 mg/dL and urine creatinine is 50 mg/dL. Estimate daily protein excretion and classify.
step: The ratio is $210/50 = 4.2$ mg/mg.
step: This approximates 4.2 g/day, above 3.5 g/day: nephrotic range.
step: Confirm with serum albumin and a lipid panel; look for oval fat bodies in the urine.

@mistakes
- Calling any proteinuria "nephrotic": the syndrome needs heavy proteinuria with hypoalbuminemia and edema.
- Confusing PSGN (low C3, subepithelial humps) with IgA nephropathy (normal C3, mesangial IgA).
- Expecting light microscopy to show minimal change disease: the lesion is seen on electron microscopy.
- Missing a secondary cause of membranous nephropathy such as hepatitis B, SLE or cancer.

answer: 8.8
check: 7.0 + 0.8 * (4.0 - 1.8)
tol: 0.05
unit: mg/dL
hint: Albumin is 2.2 g/dL below 4.0.
solution: $7.0 + 0.8 \times 2.2 = 8.76 \approx 8.8$ mg/dL, within the lower normal range, so the low total calcium reflects hypoalbuminemia.
