@article
**Chronic kidney disease (CKD)** is abnormal kidney structure or function present for **at least 3 months**: either a **GFR below 60 mL/min/1.73 m$^2$** or markers of kidney damage (albuminuria, abnormal urine sediment, structural abnormality on imaging, histologic or electrolyte abnormality from tubular disease, or a kidney transplant). The leading causes are **diabetes mellitus** and **hypertension**, followed by glomerulonephritis, polycystic kidney disease, and obstructive and tubulointerstitial disease.

### Staging

Stage by both GFR category and albuminuria category, because both predict outcome.

| GFR category | eGFR (mL/min/1.73 m$^2$) | Albuminuria category | Albumin to creatinine ratio (mg/g) |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | 90 or more | A1 | under 30 |
| G2 | 60 to 89 | A2 | 30 to 300 |
| G3a | 45 to 59 | A3 | over 300 |
| G3b | 30 to 44 | | |
| G4 | 15 to 29 | | |
| G5 | under 15 | | |

Albuminuria is expressed as the urine albumin to creatinine ratio (ACR), conveniently in mg/g; with both concentrations in mg/dL, $\text{ACR} = (\text{albumin}/\text{creatinine}) \times 1000$.

### Mechanism of progression

When nephrons are lost, the survivors hyperfilter: afferent dilation and raised glomerular capillary pressure increase single-nephron GFR. This is protective at first, but the pressure and workload cause glomerulosclerosis and further nephron loss. Proteinuria itself is tubulotoxic. This is the logic behind ACE inhibitors and ARBs (efferent dilation lowers capillary pressure) and SGLT2 inhibitors (restore tubuloglomerular feedback and constrict the afferent arteriole).

### Complications

- **Anemia**: reduced erythropoietin from damaged peritubular interstitial cells, usually normocytic and normochromic. Check iron stores first; give iron, and an **erythropoiesis-stimulating agent** when hemoglobin is persistently under about 10 g/dL after iron repletion, avoiding targets above roughly 11.5 g/dL because of stroke and thrombosis risk.
- **Mineral and bone disorder (CKD-MBD)**: falling GFR causes **phosphate retention**, rising **FGF23**, reduced **calcitriol** (kidney 1-alpha-hydroxylase loss), low calcium and **secondary hyperparathyroidism**. Bone disease ranges from osteitis fibrosa cystica (high turnover) to adynamic bone; vascular calcification is a major cardiovascular risk. Manage with dietary phosphate restriction, binders with meals (sevelamer, calcium acetate), vitamin D analogs and cinacalcet when needed. Tertiary hyperparathyroidism has autonomous PTH and high calcium.
- **Metabolic acidosis**: reduced ammonium excretion; promotes muscle loss and bone disease. Treat with oral sodium bicarbonate when persistently low (thresholds differ between guidelines).
- **Hyperkalemia**: especially with ACE inhibitors, ARBs or mineralocorticoid antagonists; dietary limits, binders and loop diuretics.
- **Volume overload and hypertension**: sodium restriction and diuretics (loop diuretics at low GFR).
- **Uremia**: pericarditis, platelet dysfunction and bleeding, pruritus, neuropathy, nausea and encephalopathy.
- **Cardiovascular disease** is the leading cause of death; treat lipids (statins) and blood pressure.

### Slowing progression

- **Blood pressure control**: ACE inhibitor or ARB when albuminuria is present; targets differ between guidelines (KDIGO suggests a systolic target below 120 mmHg with standardized measurement; ACC/AHA below 130/80).
- **SGLT2 inhibitors** (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin) slow progression and reduce heart failure admissions in CKD, with or without diabetes, down to low eGFR.
- **Finerenone** (a nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid antagonist) in type 2 diabetes with albuminuric CKD.
- Glycemic and lipid control; smoking cessation; avoid NSAIDs, nephrotoxins and contrast where possible; adjust drug doses.
- A rise in creatinine of up to about 30% after starting an ACE inhibitor or ARB is acceptable if potassium is controlled and volume is adequate; larger rises prompt a search for renal artery stenosis or volume depletion.

### Renal replacement

Plan early for G4 and G5: patient education, vein preservation, vascular access, and referral for transplant (the preferred therapy). Dialysis is started for symptomatic uremia, refractory volume overload, hyperkalemia or acidosis, not simply by an eGFR number. Modalities: hemodialysis (arteriovenous fistula preferred) and peritoneal dialysis (risk of peritonitis); long-term hemodialysis can cause dialysis-related amyloidosis from beta-2 microglobulin.

@example
title: Staging a patient
problem: A 60-year-old man with type 2 diabetes has an eGFR of 38 mL/min/1.73 m$^2$ for 5 months and a urine albumin of 12 mg/dL with urine creatinine 20 mg/dL. Stage his CKD.
step: Duration is over 3 months, so this is chronic. The eGFR of 38 lies in 30 to 44, which is G3b.
step: ACR is $(12/20) \times 1000 = 600$ mg/g, above 300, so A3.
step: Stage G3b A3: high risk of progression; use an ACE inhibitor or ARB, an SGLT2 inhibitor and monitor for complications.

@mistakes
- Staging by GFR alone, ignoring albuminuria.
- Diagnosing CKD from a single low eGFR without the 3-month duration.
- Stopping an ACE inhibitor for a creatinine rise under 30%.
- Giving an ESA before correcting iron deficiency, or targeting a normal hemoglobin.
