### Week-by-week key ideas

1. **Anatomy and embryology**: RPF = RBF x (1 - Hct). The ureteric bud forms the collecting system; the metanephric mesenchyme forms the nephron. Potter sequence, horseshoe kidney, PKD1 and PKD2.
2. **GFR and clearance**: $C = UV/P$; inulin equals GFR; creatinine overestimates it; Cockcroft-Gault; FF = GFR/RPF; effects of afferent and efferent changes.
3. **Tubular transport**: proximal reabsorption of about 65%, the diluting thick ascending limb (NKCC2), NCC, ENaC and ADH; FENa; Bartter, Gitelman and Liddle.
4. **Sodium**: osmolality, urine osmolality and volume status for hyponatremia; SIADH; correction limits and osmotic demyelination; free water deficit.
5. **Potassium, calcium, phosphate**: hyperkalemia (calcium, shift, remove); corrected calcium; hypercalcemia and saline; magnesium and refractory electrolyte defects.
6. **Acid–base physiology**: Henderson-Hasselbalch, buffers, renal bicarbonate handling, anion gap, Winter's formula.
7. **Acid–base disorders**: causes of AG and non-AG acidosis, RTA types, osmolal gap, metabolic alkalosis by urine chloride, delta ratio.
8. **AKI**: KDIGO staging, prerenal versus ATN versus AIN versus postrenal, FEurea, dialysis indications.
9. **CKD**: G and A staging, anemia, mineral and bone disorder, slowing progression.
10. **Glomerular disease**: nephritic versus nephrotic; the major diseases and their biopsy findings.
11. **UTI and stones**: cystitis and pyelonephritis therapy, asymptomatic bacteriuria, stone types, obstructed infected system.
12. **Diuretics**: site, mechanism and electrolyte signature; free water clearance; SGLT2 inhibitors and RAAS blockade.

### Common errors

Forgetting to correct the anion gap for albumin; using Winter's formula outside metabolic acidosis; correcting sodium too fast; giving insulin before calcium in hyperkalemia with ECG changes; confusing PSGN with IgA nephropathy; and reporting the creatinine clearance without the 0.85 factor in women.

@exam num
prompt: A 70 kg man (total body water 0.6 of body weight) is admitted with a serum sodium of 168 mEq/L after several days without water. Calculate his free water deficit and then the rate, in mL/h, of dextrose 5% in water needed to replace the deficit evenly over 72 hours (ignore ongoing losses).
answer: 116.7
check: 0.6 * 70 * (168 / 140 - 1) * 1000 / 72
tol: 1%
unit: mL/h
hint: The deficit is TBW times the fractional excess of sodium over 140.
hint: Convert the deficit from liters to mL, then divide by 72 hours.
solution: TBW is 42 L. Deficit $= 42 \times (168/140 - 1) = 42 \times 0.2 = 8.4$ L $= 8400$ mL. Over 72 hours that is $8400/72 = 116.7$ mL/h. Lowering sodium from 168 to 140 over 72 hours is about 9 mEq/L per day, within the 10 to 12 mEq/L per day limit; replacing it in 48 hours would lower sodium about 14 mEq/L per day, which is too fast.

@exam num
prompt: A patient has sodium 141, chloride 99, bicarbonate 11 mEq/L and albumin 2.0 g/dL. Using an albumin-corrected anion gap (add 2.5 mEq/L per 1 g/dL below 4.0) and normal values of 12 for the gap and 24 for bicarbonate, calculate the delta ratio (to 2 decimal places).
answer: 1.85
check: ((141 - 99 - 11) + 2.5 * (4.0 - 2.0) - 12) / (24 - 11)
tol: 0.02
hint: Calculate the gap, then correct it for albumin.
hint: The delta ratio is the corrected gap minus 12, divided by 24 minus bicarbonate.
solution: Gap $= 141 - 110 = 31$; corrected gap $= 31 + 5 = 36$. Delta ratio $= (36 - 12)/(24 - 11) = 24/13 = 1.85$, a pure anion gap acidosis.

@exam self
prompt: A 62-year-old man with end-stage kidney disease and diabetes missed dialysis for a week. He has weakness, potassium 7.2 mEq/L, pH 7.19, bicarbonate 13 mEq/L, glucose 150 mg/dL, and an ECG with peaked T waves and a widened QRS. Write a stepwise management plan with the rationale for each step and the monitoring.
solution: Step 1, stabilize the myocardium with IV calcium gluconate (typical adult dose 1 g over several minutes, repeated if the ECG does not improve); it acts in minutes and does not lower potassium. Step 2, shift potassium into cells with regular insulin and dextrose (glucose monitoring, and a reduced insulin dose in kidney failure to limit hypoglycemia), nebulized albuterol, and sodium bicarbonate because he is acidemic. Step 3, remove potassium: urgent hemodialysis is definitive since he has no kidney function; binders can bridge. Monitor ECG continuously and recheck potassium and glucose within 1 to 2 hours, because the shift effect is temporary and rebound occurs. Stop potassium-raising drugs and arrange dialysis scheduling.
rubric: Gives the correct order: calcium, then shift, then removal.
rubric: Names insulin with dextrose, albuterol and bicarbonate as shift therapies with a reason for bicarbonate.
rubric: Chooses hemodialysis as definitive treatment.
rubric: Plans repeat potassium and glucose checks and continuous ECG monitoring.
hint: Which step protects the heart within minutes?
