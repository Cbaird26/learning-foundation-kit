@article
Diuretics work by blocking sodium transport at a defined nephron site, so their effects on sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium and acid–base are predictable from week 3 physiology. Learn them as a map: site, transporter, drug, then the electrolyte signature.

### Site of action and effects

| Class | Site and target | Examples | Electrolyte and other effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor | Proximal tubule, carbonic anhydrase | Acetazolamide | Bicarbonate loss: non-anion gap metabolic acidosis; hypokalemia |
| Loop | Thick ascending limb, NKCC2 | Furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide; ethacrynic acid | Hypokalemia, metabolic alkalosis, **hypocalcemia** (more urinary calcium), hypomagnesemia, ototoxicity, hyperuricemia |
| Thiazide | Distal convoluted tubule, NCC | Hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone, indapamide, metolazone | Hypokalemia, metabolic alkalosis, **hypercalcemia** (less urinary calcium), hyponatremia, hyperglycemia, hyperuricemia, hyperlipidemia |
| Aldosterone antagonist | Collecting duct, mineralocorticoid receptor | Spironolactone, eplerenone | Hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis; gynecomastia with spironolactone |
| ENaC blocker | Collecting duct, ENaC | Amiloride, triamterene | Hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis |
| Osmotic | Proximal tubule and descending limb | Mannitol | Water loss; raises plasma osmolality; contraindicated in heart failure and pulmonary edema |

**Loop diuretics** are the most potent (they block the transporter handling about 25% of filtered sodium) and still work at low GFR. They are used for edema (heart failure, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome), acute pulmonary edema, hypercalcemia (after volume repletion) and hyperkalemia. Furosemide and bumetanide are sulfonamide derivatives; ethacrynic acid is not. Common oral dose equivalence for study: furosemide 40 mg is about equal to bumetanide 1 mg and torsemide 20 mg; oral bioavailability is more predictable for bumetanide and torsemide than furosemide.

**Thiazides** treat hypertension, and are used for calcium stones (hypercalciuria), nephrogenic diabetes insipidus and osteoporosis. They lose efficacy at low GFR, except metolazone. Thiazides and loops together achieve **sequential nephron blockade**: blocking the distal tubule prevents the compensatory sodium retention downstream of a loop diuretic. This gives strong diuresis, with high risk of hypokalemia and hyponatremia, so monitor electrolytes closely.

**Aldosterone antagonists** reduce mortality in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, treat primary hyperaldosteronism, resistant hypertension and cirrhotic ascites. Check potassium and creatinine; eplerenone avoids spironolactone's antiandrogen effects. Amiloride also treats lithium-induced nephrogenic diabetes insipidus.

**Acetazolamide** is used for altitude sickness, glaucoma, idiopathic intracranial hypertension and metabolic alkalosis; side effects include paresthesias and, with sulfonamide allergy, cross-reaction.

### Other renal drug classes

- **ACE inhibitors and ARBs**: dilate the efferent arteriole, lowering intraglomerular pressure; reduce proteinuria and slow CKD; adverse effects include hyperkalemia, a creatinine rise, teratogenicity, and for ACE inhibitors cough and angioedema (bradykinin). They are contraindicated in pregnancy and with bilateral renal artery stenosis.
- **SGLT2 inhibitors**: block glucose reabsorption in the proximal tubule; they cause glucosuria, volume loss and benefit in heart failure and CKD. Adverse effects: genital mycotic infections, volume depletion, and euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis; avoid during acute illness with poor intake.
- **Vasopressin antagonists** (tolvaptan, conivaptan): free water excretion for SIADH and hypervolemic hyponatremia; overly rapid correction and liver injury are risks.
- **NSAIDs**: afferent constriction and AKI in at-risk patients, hyperkalemia and sodium retention.

### Clearance of water

Osmolar clearance is $C_{osm} = U_{osm} \times V / P_{osm}$ and free water clearance is

$$C_{H_2O} = V - C_{osm}.$$

Positive values mean dilute urine (water excreted without solute); negative values mean concentrated urine (water retained).

@example
title: Free water clearance
problem: A patient has urine flow 2 mL/min, urine osmolality 150 mOsm/kg and plasma osmolality 300 mOsm/kg. Calculate free water clearance.
step: $C_{osm} = 150 \times 2 / 300 = 1$ mL/min.
step: $C_{H_2O} = V - C_{osm} = 2 - 1 = 1$ mL/min.
step: A positive value means the kidney is excreting free water; urine is dilute relative to plasma.

@mistakes
- Thinking loop and thiazide diuretics have the same effect on calcium: loops increase urinary calcium, thiazides decrease it.
- Giving thiazide-type drugs for edema in low GFR and expecting an effect.
- Using potassium-sparing diuretics with ACE inhibitors, ARBs and kidney disease without monitoring potassium.
- Continuing an SGLT2 inhibitor during acute illness with vomiting and poor intake.

@practice self
prompt: A 70-year-old woman with heart failure has persistent edema despite furosemide 80 mg twice daily. Explain why adding a thiazide-type diuretic may help, and what you would monitor.
solution: Chronic loop diuretic use causes distal tubular hypertrophy and increases sodium reabsorption at the distal convoluted tubule, limiting the response. Adding a thiazide (for example metolazone) blocks NCC and produces sequential nephron blockade with a large synergistic diuresis. Monitor potassium, magnesium, sodium and creatinine closely (hypokalemia and hyponatremia are common), daily weights, urine output, blood pressure and signs of volume depletion.
rubric: Explains compensatory sodium reabsorption in the distal tubule.
rubric: Names sequential nephron blockade as the mechanism.
rubric: Lists monitoring of potassium, sodium, magnesium and creatinine.
hint: Think of what happens downstream of a blocked thick ascending limb.
