@article
Your kidneys receive about a fifth to a quarter of the cardiac output, roughly 1 to 1.2 L/min of blood, although they make up well under 1% of body weight. That flow is not for the kidney's own metabolism: it exists so that plasma can be filtered, about 180 L/day, and then edited by the tubule down to about 1.5 L of urine. Anatomy and embryology are the map for everything else in this course.

### Gross anatomy and blood supply

The kidneys lie in the retroperitoneum at roughly the T12 to L3 vertebral levels; the right kidney sits slightly lower because of the liver. Each has an outer **cortex** and an inner **medulla** arranged in **pyramids**. The tip of a pyramid, the **papilla**, drains into a **minor calyx**; minor calyces merge into **major calyces**, then the **renal pelvis**, then the **ureter**. The ureter has three narrow points where stones lodge: the ureteropelvic junction, the crossing of the iliac vessels at the pelvic brim, and the ureterovesical junction.

Blood follows a fixed path: renal artery, segmental, interlobar, arcuate, interlobular arteries, **afferent arteriole**, **glomerular capillaries**, **efferent arteriole**, then **peritubular capillaries** (cortical nephrons) or the **vasa recta** (juxtamedullary nephrons). The kidney is unusual in having two capillary beds in series. The right renal artery passes behind the inferior vena cava. The left renal vein is longer, passes in front of the aorta just below the superior mesenteric artery, and receives the left gonadal and left adrenal veins; the right gonadal vein drains directly into the vena cava.

Renal plasma flow follows from renal blood flow and hematocrit:

$$\text{RPF} = \text{RBF} \times (1 - \text{Hct}).$$

### The nephron

The nephron runs from the **renal corpuscle** (glomerulus plus Bowman capsule) through the **proximal convoluted tubule**, the **loop of Henle** (descending limb, thin ascending limb, thick ascending limb), the **distal convoluted tubule** and into the **collecting duct**. Juxtamedullary nephrons have long loops that create the medullary concentration gradient.

The **filtration barrier** has three layers: fenestrated **endothelium**, the **glomerular basement membrane (GBM)**, and **podocyte** foot processes joined by the **slit diaphragm** (nephrin, podocin). The GBM is made of type IV collagen, laminin and heparan sulfate. Size restricts large molecules, and the negative charge of heparan sulfate repels anionic proteins such as albumin. Loss of charge selectivity causes selective albuminuria (minimal change disease); structural damage to the barrier leaks larger proteins as well. **Mesangial cells** support the capillary tuft and clear debris.

The **juxtaglomerular apparatus** sits where the thick ascending limb returns to touch its own glomerulus. It has three parts: **juxtaglomerular cells** (modified smooth muscle of the afferent arteriole, which secrete renin), the **macula densa** (tubule cells that sense NaCl delivery through NKCC2) and extraglomerular mesangial cells.

### Embryology

Three kidney systems form in sequence: the pronephros (nonfunctional, regresses), the mesonephros (transient; its duct, the mesonephric or Wolffian duct, persists in the male genital tract) and the **metanephros**, the permanent kidney, functioning from about week 10. The metanephros has two sources:

| Source | Becomes |
|---|---|
| Ureteric bud (outgrowth of the mesonephric duct) | Collecting ducts, minor and major calyces, renal pelvis, ureter |
| Metanephric mesenchyme | Glomerulus, Bowman capsule, proximal tubule, loop of Henle, distal convoluted tubule |

The two must meet: the bud induces the mesenchyme to form nephrons, and the mesenchyme induces the bud to branch. The urinary bladder develops mostly from the urogenital sinus, with the trigone derived from the mesonephric ducts. The urachus is the remnant of the allantois and becomes the median umbilical ligament.

### Congenital anomalies

- **Bilateral renal agenesis** leads to oligohydramnios and **Potter sequence**: pulmonary hypoplasia (the cause of death), flattened facies and limb deformities. Fetal urine is the main source of amniotic fluid in the second half of pregnancy.
- **Horseshoe kidney**: the lower poles fuse and ascent is halted by the inferior mesenteric artery. It is associated with Turner syndrome and with obstruction, infection and stones.
- **Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD)**: PKD1 (chromosome 16, about 85%) or PKD2 (chromosome 4); adults with bilateral enlarging cystic kidneys, hypertension, flank pain, hematuria, hepatic cysts and intracranial berry aneurysms.
- **Autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease**: PKHD1 mutation; enlarged kidneys in neonates, Potter features and congenital hepatic fibrosis.
- **Multicystic dysplastic kidney**: a nonfunctioning kidney of cysts, usually unilateral, from failure of normal ureteric bud and mesenchyme interaction.

@example
title: Renal plasma flow from blood flow
problem: A healthy adult has a renal blood flow of 1000 mL/min and a hematocrit of 0.42. What is the renal plasma flow?
step: Plasma is the non-cellular fraction of blood, so the plasma fraction is $1 - \text{Hct} = 1 - 0.42 = 0.58$.
step: Multiply: $\text{RPF} = 1000 \times 0.58 = 580$ mL/min.
step: Sanity check: plasma flow must be smaller than blood flow, and 580 mL/min is in the usual range of about 600 mL/min.

@example
title: Linking a neonate to embryology
problem: A newborn has a flattened nose, low-set ears, bilateral clubfeet and severe respiratory distress. Prenatal ultrasound showed oligohydramnios. What is the underlying defect?
step: Oligohydramnios plus pulmonary hypoplasia, flat facies and limb deformities is Potter sequence. Fetal urine is the main source of amniotic fluid late in gestation, so absent urine output starves the amniotic space.
step: The common cause is bilateral renal agenesis, in which the ureteric bud fails to form or fails to reach the metanephric mesenchyme, so no nephrons are induced.
step: Lungs need amniotic fluid movement to grow; hypoplastic lungs are the cause of death.

@mistakes
- Saying the ureteric bud forms the whole nephron: it gives the collecting duct and the ureter, while the nephron from glomerulus to distal tubule comes from the metanephric mesenchyme.
- Subtracting the hematocrit instead of multiplying: RPF is RBF times $(1 - \text{Hct})$, not RBF minus Hct.
- Placing the macula densa in the proximal tubule; it is part of the distal end of the thick ascending limb.
