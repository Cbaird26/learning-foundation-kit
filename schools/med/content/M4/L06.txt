@article
**Lysosomes** are acidic organelles whose hydrolases degrade glycolipids, glycosaminoglycans (GAGs), glycoproteins and other macromolecules. A deficient hydrolase causes **progressive accumulation of its substrate** within lysosomes, enlarging cells and organs. Almost all are **autosomal recessive**; the key exceptions are **Fabry disease** and **Hunter syndrome**, which are **X-linked recessive**. Diagnosis is by enzyme assay (leukocytes, fibroblasts or dried blood spots) and gene testing.

### Sphingolipidoses and related disorders

| Disease | Enzyme | Stored substance | Key features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tay-Sachs | Hexosaminidase A | GM2 ganglioside | Neurodegeneration from about 6 months, **cherry-red macular spot**, **hyperacusis (startle)**, **no hepatosplenomegaly**, onion-skin lysosomes; common in Ashkenazi Jewish and certain French-Canadian populations |
| Niemann-Pick (A and B) | Sphingomyelinase | Sphingomyelin | **Hepatosplenomegaly**, foam cells, cherry-red spot (type A with neurodegeneration) |
| Gaucher | Glucocerebrosidase (beta-glucosidase) | Glucocerebroside | Hepatosplenomegaly, pancytopenia, **bone pain and avascular necrosis**, Erlenmeyer flask deformity of the femur, macrophages like **crumpled tissue paper**; no neurologic disease in type 1; enzyme replacement (imiglucerase) |
| Fabry | Alpha-galactosidase A | Ceramide trihexoside | **X-linked**; burning pain in hands and feet, **angiokeratomas**, hypohidrosis, corneal verticillata, kidney failure, cardiomyopathy, stroke |
| Krabbe | Galactocerebrosidase | Galactocerebroside (psychosine) | Irritability, spasticity, peripheral neuropathy, optic atrophy, **globoid cells** |
| Metachromatic leukodystrophy | Arylsulfatase A | Sulfatide | Demyelination: ataxia, dementia, peripheral neuropathy |
| Niemann-Pick C | NPC1 or NPC2 (cholesterol transport) | Cholesterol and glycolipids | Vertical gaze palsy, ataxia, hepatosplenomegaly |

### Mucopolysaccharidoses

**Hurler syndrome** (alpha-L-iduronidase) and **Hunter syndrome** (iduronate sulfatase) accumulate **heparan sulfate and dermatan sulfate**. Both cause coarse facies, hepatosplenomegaly, dysostosis multiplex, developmental delay and airway and cardiac valve disease. **Hurler** has **corneal clouding** and is autosomal recessive; **Hunter** (X-linked) has **no corneal clouding** and often aggressive behavior (mnemonic: "Hunters see clearly"). Enzyme replacement or hematopoietic stem cell transplantation may be used.

### Other lysosomal disorders

- **Pompe disease**: lysosomal acid alpha-glucosidase deficiency (see glycogen storage diseases).
- **I-cell disease (mucolipidosis II)**: deficiency of **N-acetylglucosaminyl-1-phosphotransferase** in the Golgi, so lysosomal enzymes are not tagged with **mannose-6-phosphate**. They are secreted instead of delivered, so plasma levels of lysosomal enzymes are high while lysosomes are empty and filled with inclusions. Features: coarse facies, clouded corneas, joint contractures, skeletal changes, severe developmental delay.

### Peroxisomal disorders

Peroxisomes carry out beta-oxidation of very-long-chain fatty acids (VLCFA), alpha-oxidation of branched-chain fatty acids (phytanic acid), and plasmalogen synthesis.

- **Zellweger syndrome (spectrum)**: autosomal recessive defects of peroxisome assembly (*PEX* genes). Neonate with severe hypotonia, seizures, high forehead, hepatomegaly with cholestasis, and often death in infancy; **VLCFA are elevated**.
- **X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy**: *ABCD1* defect (VLCFA transport into the peroxisome). Boys develop school or behavioral decline with progressive cerebral demyelination and **adrenal insufficiency**; **VLCFA elevated**. Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation can arrest early cerebral disease; adrenal insufficiency needs replacement.
- **Refsum disease**: phytanic acid alpha-hydroxylase (*PHYH*) deficiency; **phytanic acid** accumulates (found in dairy, ruminant fat and fish). Retinitis pigmentosa, peripheral neuropathy, cerebellar ataxia, ichthyosis, hearing loss. Treatment: dietary restriction of phytanic acid.

### Comparing the familial dyslipidemias

Review last week's patterns. Compare by **defect**, **particle that accumulates** and **clue**:

- Type I: LPL or apo C-II; chylomicrons; milky plasma, pancreatitis.
- Type IIa (FH): LDL receptor, apo B-100 or PCSK9; LDL; tendon xanthomas and early coronary disease.
- Type III: apo E2/E2; remnants (IDL and chylomicron remnants); palmar xanthomas.
- Type IV: overproduction of VLDL; VLDL; high triglycerides, alcohol and diabetes worsen it.
- Abetalipoproteinemia: MTTP; absent apo B-containing particles; acanthocytes and vitamin E deficiency.
- Tangier: ABCA1; absent HDL; orange tonsils.

@example
title: Tay-Sachs or Niemann-Pick?
problem: A 7-month-old boy has lost the ability to sit, startles to ordinary noise and has a cherry-red spot on funduscopy. Liver and spleen are not enlarged. Which enzyme is deficient and what is stored?
step: A cherry-red macular spot occurs because the fovea has few ganglion cells, so the surrounding retina is whitened by storage material while the fovea shows normal red color. It appears in both Tay-Sachs and Niemann-Pick.
step: Hepatosplenomegaly separates them: Niemann-Pick has it; Tay-Sachs does not. Hyperacusis and neurodegeneration also favor Tay-Sachs.
step: Tay-Sachs is due to hexosaminidase A deficiency, with GM2 ganglioside storage.

@example
title: Risk for the sons of a carrier
problem: A woman is a carrier of Fabry disease (X-linked recessive). What is the probability that a son is affected?
step: A carrier mother has one normal and one mutant X chromosome.
step: A son receives one of her X chromosomes at random, each with probability one-half, and his Y from his father.
step: If he gets the mutant X he is affected, so the probability is $1/2$. Daughters have a one-half chance of being carriers.

@mistakes
- Forgetting that Fabry and Hunter are X-linked; all other classic lysosomal diseases listed here are autosomal recessive.
- Calling any cherry-red spot "Tay-Sachs" without asking about hepatosplenomegaly.
- Mixing up Hurler (corneal clouding) with Hunter (clear corneas).
- Forgetting that in I-cell disease serum lysosomal enzymes are high, not low.

check: 2 * (1/10000)**0.5 * (1 - (1/10000)**0.5)

hint: The incidence equals $q^2$.

@practice self
prompt: Compare familial chylomicronemia (type I), familial hypercholesterolemia, dysbetalipoproteinemia (type III) and abetalipoproteinemia. For each, give the defect, the lipoprotein pattern, and one distinguishing clinical clue.
solution: Familial chylomicronemia: LPL or apo C-II deficiency; chylomicrons and triglycerides greatly raised (milky plasma); eruptive xanthomas and pancreatitis. Familial hypercholesterolemia: LDL receptor (or apo B-100 or PCSK9) defect; LDL raised with high cholesterol and normal triglycerides; Achilles tendon xanthomas and premature coronary disease. Dysbetalipoproteinemia: apo E2/E2 with impaired remnant clearance; remnants and IDL raised, so cholesterol and triglycerides both rise; palmar xanthomas. Abetalipoproteinemia: MTTP defect; absent chylomicrons, VLDL and LDL with very low cholesterol and triglyceride; acanthocytes, steatorrhea and vitamin E deficiency (ataxia, retinopathy).
