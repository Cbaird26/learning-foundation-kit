@article
Sugars other than glucose enter metabolism through dedicated pathways. A block in one of them makes the **sugar or its phosphate** accumulate, and the clinical picture usually appears when the sugar is first introduced into the diet.

### Galactose

Lactose is hydrolyzed by intestinal lactase to glucose and galactose. Galactose is phosphorylated by **galactokinase** to galactose-1-phosphate, which **galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase (GALT)** converts, with UDP-glucose, to glucose-1-phosphate and UDP-galactose.

- **Classic galactosemia (GALT deficiency)**: autosomal recessive. Within days of feeding breast milk or lactose formula an infant develops vomiting, jaundice, hepatomegaly, hypoglycemia, poor feeding, **cataracts** and a predisposition to ***Escherichia coli* sepsis**. Galactose-1-phosphate damages liver, kidney and brain; galactose is also reduced by **aldose reductase** to **galactitol**, which accumulates in the lens and draws in water, causing cataracts. Urine contains a **reducing substance** (positive with Clinitest) but is negative on a glucose-oxidase dipstick. Treatment is **lifelong removal of lactose and galactose**. Late sequelae (speech problems, primary ovarian insufficiency) can occur despite treatment.
- **Galactokinase deficiency**: galactose and galactitol accumulate but galactose-1-phosphate does not, so the main finding is **cataracts** without liver or kidney failure.

### Fructose

Fructose is phosphorylated by **fructokinase** to fructose-1-phosphate, which **aldolase B** splits into DHAP and glyceraldehyde.

- **Essential fructosuria**: fructokinase deficiency; benign, with fructose in urine.
- **Hereditary fructose intolerance (aldolase B deficiency)**: autosomal recessive. Symptoms start when fructose, sucrose or sorbitol is introduced (fruit, juice, weaning foods): vomiting, **hypoglycemia**, jaundice, hepatomegaly and liver failure, and a tubulopathy. Fructose-1-phosphate accumulates and **traps inorganic phosphate**, so ATP falls; it also inhibits glycogen phosphorylase and gluconeogenesis, producing hypoglycemia. Reducing substance is positive in urine. Treatment is strict avoidance of fructose, sucrose and sorbitol. Many patients refuse sweets, so dental caries are unusually rare.

### Lactose intolerance

Lactase on the brush border declines in many adults (**primary lactase non-persistence**) or is lost after gastroenteritis, celiac disease or surgery (secondary). Undigested lactose is osmotic and is fermented by colonic bacteria to gas and short-chain acids. Findings: bloating, flatulence and watery diarrhea after dairy; low stool pH; increased **breath hydrogen** after a lactose load; an **osmotic stool**. The **stool osmotic gap** is

$$290 - 2\,(\text{stool Na} + \text{stool K}),$$

with electrolytes in mEq/L. A gap above about 100 mOsm/kg suggests an osmotic diarrhea and a gap below 50 suggests secretory diarrhea; values from 50 to 100 are intermediate (some laboratories use 125 as the osmotic cutoff). Management: lactose avoidance or lactase supplements.

### Pentose phosphate pathway and G6PD deficiency

The pentose phosphate pathway's oxidative phase makes **NADPH** (and ribose-5-phosphate). In red cells, NADPH keeps glutathione reduced; reduced glutathione removes hydrogen peroxide via glutathione peroxidase. **G6PD deficiency** is **X-linked recessive** and the commonest enzymopathy. Oxidant stress (infection, **fava beans**, sulfonamides, primaquine, dapsone, nitrofurantoin, rasburicase) causes **hemolysis**: back pain, jaundice, dark urine; blood smear shows **Heinz bodies** (precipitated hemoglobin) and **bite cells**. It is prevalent in people of African, Mediterranean and Asian ancestry because it protects against malaria. Management is avoidance of triggers and supportive care.

### Explaining hypoglycemia in von Gierke disease

Blood glucose between meals comes from **glycogenolysis** (first about 12 to 24 hours) and **gluconeogenesis**. Both pathways converge on glucose-6-phosphate, and only **glucose-6-phosphatase** converts it to free glucose that can leave the hepatocyte. When it is deficient, neither pathway can supply blood glucose, so fasting hypoglycemia is rapid and severe, glucagon cannot reverse it, and glucose-6-phosphate accumulates, driving lactate, urate and lipid overproduction.

### Other disorders

**Pyruvate dehydrogenase deficiency** (X-linked, *PDHA1*) blocks pyruvate oxidation, causing lactic acidosis and neurologic disease. A **ketogenic diet** (rich in fat, with lysine and leucine as purely ketogenic amino acids) helps because it bypasses the block. **Thiamine** (a cofactor) may help some patients.

title: Why fruit juice made this infant sick
problem: A 6-month-old girl develops vomiting, lethargy and a glucose of 30 mg/dL within an hour of her first feed of apple puree. She has hepatomegaly and jaundice. Urine tests positive for reducing substances. What is the diagnosis, the toxic intermediate and the mechanism of hypoglycemia?
step: Symptoms begin with the introduction of fruit (fructose and sucrose), and the infant has hypoglycemia, jaundice and hepatomegaly: hereditary fructose intolerance (aldolase B deficiency).
step: Fructokinase works normally, so fructose-1-phosphate accumulates because aldolase B cannot cleave it.
step: Fructose-1-phosphate sequesters inorganic phosphate (lowering ATP) and inhibits glycogen phosphorylase and gluconeogenesis, so the liver cannot make or release glucose.
step: Treatment is lifelong avoidance of fructose, sucrose and sorbitol.

@example
title: Stool osmotic gap
problem: A patient with chronic diarrhea has stool sodium 40 mEq/L and stool potassium 30 mEq/L. Calculate the stool osmotic gap and say what it suggests.
step: Add the measured electrolytes: $40 + 30 = 70$ mEq/L.
step: Multiply by 2 to account for accompanying anions: $2 \times 70 = 140$ mOsm/kg.
step: Gap = $290 - 140 = 150$ mOsm/kg.
step: A gap well above 100 indicates an osmotic diarrhea, as seen with carbohydrate malabsorption or osmotic laxatives.

@mistakes
- Calling a positive Clinitest (reducing substance) "glucosuria"; galactose and fructose also reduce copper, so check a glucose-oxidase test.
- Forgetting *E. coli* sepsis as a life-threatening complication of untreated classic galactosemia.
- Confusing G6PD deficiency (X-linked, oxidant hemolysis, Heinz and bite cells) with hereditary spherocytosis (spherocytes, osmotic fragility).
- Treating hypoglycemia in von Gierke disease with fructose or galactose; both are converted to glucose-6-phosphate and cannot be released as glucose.

solution: Glucose in blood between meals comes from glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis, and both pathways end with glucose-6-phosphate.
