@article
Inborn errors of amino acid metabolism are mostly autosomal recessive enzyme deficiencies. Substrate upstream of the block accumulates (and is often toxic), the product downstream is deficient, and alternative pathways make abnormal metabolites that are excreted in urine and give diagnostic clues.

### Phenylalanine and tyrosine

Phenylalanine is converted to tyrosine by **phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH)**, which needs the cofactor **tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4)**. Tyrosine is then used for catecholamines, thyroid hormone and melanin, or degraded via homogentisate to fumarate and acetoacetate.

**Phenylketonuria (PKU)**: PAH deficiency. Phenylalanine accumulates and is shunted to phenylketones (phenylpyruvate, phenylacetate), which give urine a **musty or mousy odor**. Tyrosine becomes **conditionally essential**. Untreated, infants develop intellectual disability, seizures, microcephaly, eczema and **fair hair and skin** (tyrosine, hence melanin, is deficient and phenylalanine inhibits tyrosinase). Neurotoxicity is from high phenylalanine competing with other large neutral amino acids for transport into the brain.

**Newborn screening**: a dried blood spot collected at about 24 to 48 hours of age (after protein feeding has started) is analyzed by tandem mass spectrometry for elevated phenylalanine and a high phenylalanine-to-tyrosine ratio. Collection before 24 hours risks a false negative. Positive screens are confirmed with quantitative plasma amino acids and a BH4 workup.

Treatment: **lifelong phenylalanine-restricted diet** with phenylalanine-free medical formula, tyrosine supplementation as needed, and **avoidance of aspartame** (contains phenylalanine). **Sapropterin** (a BH4 analog) helps BH4-responsive patients; **pegvaliase** (phenylalanine ammonia lyase) is used in adults. **Maternal PKU**: uncontrolled maternal phenylalanine is teratogenic, causing microcephaly, intellectual disability and congenital heart disease in the fetus, so women need tight control before conception and throughout pregnancy.

**BH4 deficiency** (biopterin synthesis defects or dihydropteridine reductase deficiency) also raises phenylalanine but, because BH4 is also a cofactor of tyrosine and tryptophan hydroxylases, causes deficiency of dopamine and serotonin and neurologic deterioration despite a low-phenylalanine diet. Treatment adds BH4 and neurotransmitter precursors (levodopa-carbidopa, 5-hydroxytryptophan).

**Alkaptonuria**: homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase deficiency. Homogentisic acid accumulates; urine **darkens on standing**; pigment deposits in cartilage (**ochronosis**: blue-black ear cartilage, scleral pigmentation) and causes early degenerative arthritis of the spine and large joints. Nitisinone lowers homogentisate. **Oculocutaneous albinism** (tyrosinase deficiency in type 1): decreased melanin, with nystagmus, photophobia, reduced visual acuity and a high skin-cancer risk.

### Sulfur and branched-chain amino acids

**Homocystinuria** (most often **cystathionine beta-synthase deficiency**): methionine to homocysteine to cystathionine requires CBS and **pyridoxal phosphate**. Homocysteine and methionine accumulate and cysteine becomes essential. Features: marfanoid habitus, **downward (inferior) lens dislocation**, intellectual disability, **thromboembolism** (stroke, venous thrombosis), osteoporosis. In **Marfan syndrome** the lens dislocates **upward**, intelligence is normal, and there is aortic root dilation. Treatment: pyridoxine (about half respond), methionine restriction, cysteine and **betaine**, folate and B12. Remember that high homocysteine also occurs in B12 and folate deficiency.

**Maple syrup urine disease (MSUD)**: deficient **branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase** (needs thiamine). **Leucine, isoleucine and valine** and their ketoacids accumulate; **sweet maple-syrup odor** in urine and cerumen; poor feeding, lethargy, dystonia and encephalopathy in the first week. Treatment: restrict branched-chain amino acids; trial of thiamine.

### Urea cycle

The urea cycle detoxifies ammonia: **carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I (CPS-I)** and **ornithine transcarbamylase (OTC)** act in mitochondria; argininosuccinate synthetase, lyase and arginase act in cytosol. **N-acetylglutamate** activates CPS-I. Deficiency causes **hyperammonemia**: poor feeding, vomiting, lethargy, seizures, cerebral edema and respiratory alkalosis, especially after protein intake. **OTC deficiency is X-linked** and the most common; carbamoyl phosphate spills into pyrimidine synthesis, so **urine orotic acid is high** (it is low in CPS-I deficiency). BUN is low. Treatment: stop protein, give dextrose, intravenous nitrogen scavengers (sodium phenylacetate and sodium benzoate), arginine or citrulline, and dialysis for severe cases.

### Transport and other defects

- **Cystinuria**: defect of the renal and intestinal transporter for **c**ystine, **o**rnithine, **l**ysine and **a**rginine ("COLA") in the proximal tubule. Cystine is poorly soluble and forms **hexagonal stones**; urinary cyanide-nitroprusside test is positive. Treatment: high fluid intake, alkalinization of urine (potassium citrate), and tiopronin.
- **Hartnup disease**: defect in neutral amino acid transport (*SLC6A19*) causing pellagra-like findings from poor tryptophan absorption, with neutral aminoaciduria.
- **Tyrosinemia type I**: fumarylacetoacetate hydrolase deficiency, with liver failure, tubulopathy and elevated succinylacetone; treated with nitisinone and diet.

@example
title: Phenylketonuria and its newborn screen
problem: A newborn's screening blood spot was collected at 8 hours of age and showed a normal phenylalanine level. At 3 weeks, the infant is irritable with a musty odor and eczema. Explain the biochemistry and why the screen was normal.
step: Phenylalanine hydroxylase converts phenylalanine to tyrosine using BH4. If it is deficient, phenylalanine rises and spills into phenylketones that give the musty odor.
step: Tyrosine becomes essential, and low tyrosine and melanin cause fair skin; high phenylalanine impairs brain development.
step: The sample was drawn before significant protein feeding, so phenylalanine had not yet risen: an early sample can be falsely negative. Repeat the screen and quantify plasma phenylalanine and tyrosine.
step: Management is a phenylalanine-restricted diet with medical formula, plus tyrosine as required.

@example
title: Orotic acid in hyperammonemia
problem: A male infant becomes lethargic on day 3 with ammonia 600 micromol/L, low BUN and respiratory alkalosis. Urine orotic acid is high. Which enzyme is deficient and why is orotic acid raised?
step: Hyperammonemia with low BUN points to a proximal urea cycle block.
step: High orotic acid separates OTC deficiency from CPS-I deficiency, where carbamoyl phosphate is not made and orotic acid is low.
step: When OTC is blocked, mitochondrial carbamoyl phosphate exits to the cytosol and enters pyrimidine synthesis, producing orotic acid.
step: OTC deficiency is X-linked recessive, which fits a male infant.

@mistakes
- Collecting the newborn PKU screen before protein feeding is established (before 24 hours), giving a false negative.
- Mixing the direction of lens dislocation: homocystinuria is downward; Marfan is upward.
- Saying that pyridoxine treats all homocystinuria; only some patients respond.
- Forgetting that phenylalanine is in aspartame.
