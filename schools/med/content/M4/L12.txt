### Insulin signaling

Insulin binds the **insulin receptor**, a receptor **tyrosine kinase**. Autophosphorylation recruits **insulin receptor substrates (IRS)**, which activate **PI3K** and then **Akt**. Akt mediates metabolic effects: translocation of **GLUT4** to the membrane in **muscle and adipose tissue**, glycogen synthesis (inhibition of GSK3), protein synthesis (mTOR), and suppression of hepatic gluconeogenesis and of lipolysis. A parallel **MAPK** pathway mediates growth effects. GLUT2 (liver, pancreatic beta cells) and GLUT1 and GLUT3 (brain) are insulin-independent.

**Insulin resistance** is a reduced response to insulin. Drivers: **excess visceral fat** and ectopic lipid in liver and muscle (diacylglycerol activating protein kinase C and impairing IRS signaling), high free fatty acids, inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha, IL-6), lower **adiponectin** and mitochondrial dysfunction. Early on, beta cells compensate with hyperinsulinemia; **type 2 diabetes** appears when beta cells fail (with islet amyloid deposits of amylin). **Type 1 diabetes** is autoimmune destruction of beta cells.

### Diabetes diagnosis (American Diabetes Association)

Diabetes: fasting plasma glucose of 126 mg/dL or more, 2-hour glucose of 200 mg/dL or more in a 75-g oral glucose tolerance test, **hemoglobin A1c of 6.5 percent or more**, or a random glucose of 200 mg/dL or more with classic symptoms (confirmation is required in the absence of unequivocal hyperglycemia). Prediabetes: fasting glucose 100 to 125 mg/dL, 2-hour OGTT glucose 140 to 199 mg/dL, or A1c 5.7 to 6.4 percent. The A1c is converted to **estimated average glucose (eAG)** by

$$\text{eAG (mg/dL)} = 28.7 \times \text{A1c} - 46.7.$$

**HOMA-IR**, a simple index of insulin resistance:

$$\text{HOMA-IR} = \frac{\text{fasting insulin }(\mu\text{U/mL}) \times \text{fasting glucose (mg/dL)}}{405}.$$

### Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)

With absolute insulin deficiency (type 1) and high glucagon, adipose **hormone-sensitive lipase** is unrestrained, releasing fatty acids. The liver oxidizes them to acetyl-CoA faster than the TCA cycle can use it (oxaloacetate is diverted to gluconeogenesis), so acetyl-CoA is converted by mitochondrial **HMG-CoA synthase** to **ketone bodies**: acetoacetate, **beta-hydroxybutyrate** (the predominant ketone in DKA) and acetone (fruity breath). The acids cause an **elevated anion gap metabolic acidosis**:

$$\text{Anion gap} = \text{Na}^+ - \text{Cl}^- - \text{HCO}_3^-.$$

Osmotic diuresis from hyperglycemia causes dehydration; total-body potassium is depleted even when serum potassium is normal or high (acidosis and insulin deficiency shift K$^+$ out of cells). Nitroprusside tests detect acetoacetate but not beta-hydroxybutyrate, so they can underestimate ketosis. Treatment: IV fluids, insulin infusion once potassium is at least 3.3 mEq/L, potassium replacement, treating the precipitant. **Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state** has insulin deficiency not severe enough to cause ketosis.

### Obesity and appetite

- **Leptin** (adipocytes) signals energy sufficiency through the hypothalamus (arcuate nucleus), reducing appetite. Obesity is a leptin-resistant state; congenital leptin deficiency causes severe early obesity treatable with leptin.
- **Ghrelin** (stomach) stimulates hunger and is high before meals and in Prader-Willi syndrome.
- **GLP-1** and **GIP** (incretins) enhance glucose-dependent insulin secretion; GLP-1 also suppresses glucagon, slows gastric emptying and promotes satiety. **GLP-1 receptor agonists** (semaglutide, liraglutide) and the dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist **tirzepatide** treat type 2 diabetes and obesity.
- **PYY** and **CCK** are satiety signals. Mutations of the **MC4R** receptor are the commonest monogenic cause of obesity.

### Metabolic syndrome

Harmonized criteria: three or more of five: **waist circumference** at or above 102 cm (men) or 88 cm (women) in the U.S. (lower thresholds in some ethnic groups), **triglycerides** 150 mg/dL or more (or treatment), **HDL** below 40 mg/dL in men or below 50 in women (or treatment), **blood pressure** 130/85 mm Hg or more (or treatment), **fasting glucose** 100 mg/dL or more (or treatment). It predicts cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes and fatty liver disease.

### Diabetes drugs by mechanism

Metformin (reduces hepatic gluconeogenesis; contraindicated in severe renal impairment), sulfonylureas (close beta-cell K$_{ATP}$ channels; hypoglycemia), thiazolidinediones (PPAR-gamma; edema, heart failure), DPP-4 inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors (glucosuria; genital infections; euglycemic DKA; cardiorenal benefit), GLP-1 agonists and insulin.

### Kwashiorkor versus marasmus

Both are forms of protein-energy undernutrition at the opposite end of the nutritional spectrum from obesity. **Marasmus**: total energy deficit, severe wasting, no edema, alert child, adaptive low insulin and high cortisol. **Kwashiorkor**: edema, ascites, fatty liver, skin and hair changes, apathy, low albumin, often on a starchy diet low in protein. The edema is the key distinguishing sign.

@example
title: Does he meet criteria for metabolic syndrome?
problem: A man has waist circumference 108 cm, triglycerides 190 mg/dL, HDL 45 mg/dL, blood pressure 128/78 mm Hg (untreated) and fasting glucose 98 mg/dL. How many criteria does he meet?
step: Waist 108 cm is at or above 102 cm: criterion met.
step: Triglycerides 190 are at or above 150: criterion met.
step: HDL 45 is not below 40; blood pressure 128/78 is below 130/85; glucose 98 is below 100: not met.
step: He meets 2 of 5, so he does not have metabolic syndrome by these criteria, although he has risk factors worth addressing.

@example
title: Anion gap in DKA
problem: A patient with type 1 diabetes has Na 138, Cl 100 and HCO$_3$ 10 mEq/L. Calculate the anion gap and interpret it.
step: Apply the formula: $138 - 100 - 10 = 28$ mEq/L.
step: The normal gap is about 8 to 12 mEq/L, so this is an elevated anion gap metabolic acidosis.
step: The unmeasured anions are acetoacetate and beta-hydroxybutyrate.
step: Check ketones (beta-hydroxybutyrate preferred), glucose and potassium before starting insulin.

@mistakes
- Starting insulin before confirming serum potassium is at least 3.3 mEq/L in DKA.
- Relying on a urine nitroprusside test to exclude or follow ketosis, since it misses beta-hydroxybutyrate.
- Saying GLUT4 is the main transporter in liver or brain; those use GLUT2 and GLUT1 or GLUT3.
- Calling a patient with only two of the five criteria "metabolic syndrome".

prompt: Compare kwashiorkor and marasmus: give the nutritional deficit, the key physical findings, one laboratory finding, and the mechanism of the edema and fatty liver in kwashiorkor.
solution: Marasmus is a deficiency of total energy (calories and protein) leading to severe wasting of fat and muscle, a wizened face, no edema, an alert child, and a low weight-for-height. Kwashiorkor is a relatively greater protein deficiency, often with a starchy diet, that produces bilateral pitting edema and ascites, an enlarged fatty liver, skin desquamation, hair depigmentation and apathy; serum albumin is low. The edema reflects low albumin and reduced oncotic pressure plus capillary leak from infection and oxidative stress. The fatty liver reflects reduced synthesis of apolipoprotein B and lipoproteins, so triglyceride cannot be exported as VLDL. Both increase infection risk and require careful refeeding with attention to electrolytes and thiamine.
