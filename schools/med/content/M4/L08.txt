@article
**Nucleotides** carry energy and genetic information. **Purines** (adenine, guanine) are built on a ribose-5-phosphate scaffold; **pyrimidines** (cytosine, uracil, thymine) are built as a ring and then attached to ribose.

### Purine synthesis, salvage and degradation

- **De novo synthesis:** ribose-5-phosphate becomes **PRPP** (PRPP synthetase). **Glutamine-PRPP amidotransferase** is the committed, rate-limiting step; it is inhibited by AMP, GMP and IMP. The pathway forms **IMP**, then AMP and GMP.
- **Salvage:** **HGPRT** returns hypoxanthine to IMP and guanine to GMP using PRPP; APRT salvages adenine.
- **Degradation:** purines go to hypoxanthine, then **xanthine**, then **uric acid** via **xanthine oxidase**. Humans lack uricase, so urate is the end product (other mammals make the more soluble allantoin).

### Gout

**Hyperuricemia** (urate above about 6.8 mg/dL exceeds solubility) leads to deposition of **monosodium urate crystals** in joints, causing acute **gout**: sudden severe monoarthritis, classically the first metatarsophalangeal joint (podagra). Synovial fluid shows **needle-shaped crystals with negative birefringence** (yellow when parallel to the compensator axis). Pseudogout crystals (calcium pyrophosphate) are rhomboid and positively birefringent. About 90 percent of patients under-excrete urate; contributing factors include chronic kidney disease, thiazide and loop diuretics, low-dose aspirin, alcohol, lactic acidosis and obesity. Overproduction occurs with Lesch-Nyhan syndrome, PRPP synthetase superactivity, glycogen storage disease type I, and high cell turnover (tumor lysis).

- **Acute attack:** NSAIDs, colchicine or glucocorticoids.
- **Chronic urate lowering:** **allopurinol** or **febuxostat** (xanthine oxidase inhibitors); **probenecid** (uricosuric); **pegloticase** and **rasburicase** (recombinant uricase) for refractory gout and tumor lysis. Allopurinol is begun at a low dose; *HLA-B\*58:01* is associated with severe cutaneous reactions in some ancestries. Allopurinol **inhibits metabolism of azathioprine and 6-mercaptopurine**, risking marrow toxicity unless doses are reduced. Rasburicase can cause hemolysis in G6PD deficiency.

The **fractional excretion of uric acid** is

$$\text{FE}_{\text{UA}} = \frac{U_{\text{UA}} \times P_{\text{Cr}}}{P_{\text{UA}} \times U_{\text{Cr}}} \times 100.$$

### Lesch-Nyhan syndrome

**HGPRT deficiency**, **X-linked recessive**. With salvage blocked, PRPP accumulates (and is no longer consumed), driving de novo purine synthesis and uric acid overproduction: gout, urate stones, "orange sand" crystals in diapers. Neurologic features: **intellectual disability, dystonia, choreoathetosis and self-mutilating behavior** (lip and finger biting). A megaloblastic anemia can occur. Allopurinol treats the hyperuricemia but not the neurologic features.

### Immunodeficiency from purine pathway defects

- **ADA deficiency**: autosomal recessive cause of **severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID)**. Adenosine and deoxyadenosine accumulate, so dATP inhibits **ribonucleotide reductase** and prevents DNA synthesis in lymphocytes, which are particularly vulnerable. Infants have recurrent infections, failure to thrive and absent T and B lymphocytes. Treatment: enzyme replacement, hematopoietic stem cell transplant or gene therapy.
- **PNP deficiency**: T-cell immunodeficiency with accumulation of dGTP.

### Pyrimidine synthesis

Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase II (cytosolic, uses glutamine) begins the pathway, then aspartate transcarbamylase and dihydroorotase, **dihydroorotate dehydrogenase** (mitochondrial), then **UMP synthase** (orotate phosphoribosyltransferase and OMP decarboxylase) forms UMP. **Thymidylate synthase** converts dUMP to dTMP using N5,N10-methylene-tetrahydrofolate, regenerated by **dihydrofolate reductase**.

**Hereditary orotic aciduria**: UMP synthase deficiency. Orotic acid is excreted in urine; there is **megaloblastic anemia that does not respond to B12 or folate**, with growth failure and **no hyperammonemia**. Treatment: **uridine** supplementation, which bypasses the block and also inhibits the pathway by feedback.

### Drugs that target nucleotide enzymes

| Drug | Enzyme |
|---|---|
| Methotrexate, trimethoprim | Dihydrofolate reductase (bacterial for trimethoprim) |
| 5-Fluorouracil | Thymidylate synthase |
| Hydroxyurea | Ribonucleotide reductase |
| Mycophenolate | IMP dehydrogenase |
| Leflunomide | Dihydroorotate dehydrogenase |
| Allopurinol, febuxostat | Xanthine oxidase |

### Phenylketonuria revisited

A good test of whether you can explain a metabolic disease from enzyme to screen: PKU (phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency) causes high phenylalanine and low tyrosine; a dried blood spot taken at 24 to 48 hours after protein feeding is analyzed by tandem mass spectrometry; diet restriction started in the first weeks prevents intellectual disability.

@example
title: Why does Lesch-Nyhan syndrome cause hyperuricemia?
problem: A 14-month-old boy has dystonia, bites his lips, and has orange crystals in his diapers. Serum urate is elevated. Explain the hyperuricemia.
step: HGPRT salvages hypoxanthine and guanine to IMP and GMP using PRPP.
step: With HGPRT absent, PRPP is not consumed and rises, and purine bases are not recycled, so nothing inhibits the amidotransferase.
step: The result is increased de novo purine synthesis and breakdown to hypoxanthine, xanthine and uric acid.
step: Allopurinol blocks xanthine oxidase and lowers urate, but the neurologic features persist.

@example
title: Fractional excretion of uric acid
problem: A patient has urine uric acid 20 mg/dL, plasma creatinine 1.0 mg/dL, plasma uric acid 8.0 mg/dL and urine creatinine 100 mg/dL. Calculate the fractional excretion of uric acid.
step: Multiply urine uric acid by plasma creatinine: $20 \times 1.0 = 20$.
step: Multiply plasma uric acid by urine creatinine: $8.0 \times 100 = 800$.
step: Divide and convert to percent: $20/800 = 0.025$, so 2.5 percent.
step: A low value suggests under-excretion of urate, as in chronic kidney disease or diuretic use.

@mistakes
- Treating the neurologic features of Lesch-Nyhan syndrome with allopurinol; it lowers urate only.
- Giving B12 or folate for orotic aciduria; the anemia is due to pyrimidine deficiency and responds to uridine.
- Giving allopurinol with full-dose azathioprine without dose reduction.
- Confusing needle-shaped, negatively birefringent urate crystals with rhomboid, positively birefringent pyrophosphate crystals.

@practice self
prompt: A newborn's phenylketonuria (PKU) screen is positive. Explain to the parents, in plain language, what is different in how the baby's body handles protein, why the screen is done at 24 to 48 hours, and what treatment prevents harm.
solution: Protein contains an amino acid called phenylalanine. Normally an enzyme converts it to another amino acid, tyrosine. In PKU that enzyme is missing or weak, so phenylalanine builds up in the blood and, if untreated, damages the developing brain. The heel-prick screen is done at 24 to 48 hours because phenylalanine only rises after the baby has had enough protein feeding; testing too early can miss it. A positive screen is confirmed with a blood test, and treatment is a special low-phenylalanine diet with medical formula, started in the first weeks of life and continued for life, with tyrosine supplements and sometimes medication. With early treatment, children can have normal development.
