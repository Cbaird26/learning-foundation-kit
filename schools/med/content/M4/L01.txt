@article
A **vitamin** is an organic molecule the body needs in small amounts and cannot make in sufficient quantity. Most act as **coenzymes** or as hormone-like regulators, so a deficiency blocks one specific reaction and produces a predictable syndrome. Learn each vitamin as a pair: *what reaction it supports* and *what breaks when it is missing*.

### Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K)

These are absorbed with dietary fat in micelles, stored in liver and adipose tissue, and become deficient in **fat malabsorption** (cystic fibrosis, cholestasis, celiac disease, orlistat, abetalipoproteinemia).

- **Vitamin A (retinol).** Retinal is the chromophore of rhodopsin; retinoic acid regulates epithelial differentiation. Deficiency causes **night blindness**, xerophthalmia, Bitot spots, keratomalacia and susceptibility to infection (measles is more severe). Excess causes teratogenicity (isotretinoin), hepatotoxicity and raised intracranial pressure.
- **Vitamin D.** Skin or diet gives cholecalciferol (D3); the liver makes **25-hydroxyvitamin D** (the storage form that is measured) and the kidney 1-alpha-hydroxylase makes **1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D** (calcitriol). Deficiency causes **rickets** in children (bowed legs, rachitic rosary, widened growth plates) and **osteomalacia** in adults, with low or low-normal calcium, low phosphate and high PTH and alkaline phosphatase. Conversion: 1 mcg of vitamin D equals 40 IU.
- **Vitamin E (tocopherol).** A lipid-phase antioxidant protecting membranes. Deficiency causes hemolytic anemia, spinocerebellar ataxia, peripheral neuropathy and loss of vibration sense.
- **Vitamin K.** Cofactor for **gamma-carboxylation** of glutamate residues in factors II, VII, IX, X and proteins C and S. Deficiency prolongs the PT first (factor VII has the shortest half-life) and causes bleeding. Newborns are at risk (sterile gut, low stores, little in breast milk), hence the routine intramuscular dose at birth. Warfarin blocks vitamin K epoxide reductase.

### Water-soluble vitamins

- **B1, thiamine.** As thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP) it is a cofactor for pyruvate dehydrogenase, alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, branched-chain ketoacid dehydrogenase and transketolase. Deficiency: **dry beriberi** (neuropathy), **wet beriberi** (high-output heart failure) and **Wernicke encephalopathy** (confusion, ataxia, ophthalmoplegia) that can progress to Korsakoff syndrome. Risk: alcohol use disorder, malnutrition, refeeding. Give thiamine before or with glucose in an at-risk patient. Red-cell transketolase activity falls.
- **B2, riboflavin.** FAD and FMN. Cheilosis, angular stomatitis, glossitis, normocytic anemia.
- **B3, niacin.** NAD and NADP. **Pellagra**: diarrhea, dermatitis (photosensitive, Casal necklace), dementia, and death if untreated. Causes: corn-based diet (bound niacin, little tryptophan), Hartnup disease, carcinoid syndrome (tryptophan diverted to serotonin), isoniazid. Tryptophan supplies niacin: 60 mg tryptophan yields 1 mg niacin equivalent.
- **B5, pantothenic acid.** Part of coenzyme A and the acyl carrier protein. Deficiency is rare.
- **B6, pyridoxine.** Pyridoxal phosphate is the cofactor for transamination, decarboxylation (GABA, serotonin, dopamine), heme synthesis (ALA synthase) and cystathionine synthase. Deficiency: sideroblastic anemia, peripheral neuropathy, seizures, cheilosis. Isoniazid and cycloserine are common causes; give pyridoxine with isoniazid.
- **B7, biotin.** Carboxylase cofactor (pyruvate carboxylase, acetyl-CoA carboxylase, propionyl-CoA carboxylase). Deficiency: dermatitis, alopecia, enteritis; caused by large amounts of raw egg white (avidin).
- **B9, folate.** Tetrahydrofolate carries one-carbon units for purine and thymidylate synthesis. Deficiency: **megaloblastic anemia** and, in pregnancy, neural tube defects. Homocysteine is high; **methylmalonic acid (MMA) is normal**. Causes include alcohol, methotrexate, trimethoprim and phenytoin.
- **B12, cobalamin.** Cofactor for **methionine synthase** (homocysteine to methionine) and **methylmalonyl-CoA mutase** (methylmalonyl-CoA to succinyl-CoA). Deficiency: megaloblastic anemia plus **subacute combined degeneration** (loss of vibration and proprioception, spastic weakness). Both homocysteine and **MMA are high**. Causes: pernicious anemia (anti-intrinsic factor or anti-parietal cell antibodies), terminal ileal disease or resection, *Diphyllobothrium latum*, strict veganism, metformin and long-term proton pump inhibitors. Folate alone can correct the anemia but not the neurologic damage, so do not treat an unknown macrocytic anemia with folate alone.
- **C, ascorbic acid.** Cofactor for **prolyl and lysyl hydroxylases** in collagen synthesis, for dopamine beta-hydroxylase, and for iron reduction (aiding non-heme iron absorption). Deficiency: **scurvy**, with perifollicular hemorrhages, corkscrew hairs, bleeding and swollen gums, poor wound healing and subperiosteal bleeding.

### How to study

Group by organ: nerve (B1, B6, B12, E), blood (B6, B9, B12, E, K), skin and mucosa (A, B2, B3, B7, C), bone (C, D). Then confirm with a laboratory discriminator.

@example
title: A diet-based diagnosis
problem: A 45-year-old woman living on a diet of mostly corn tortillas and little else presents with several weeks of watery diarrhea, a red scaly rash on her neck and hands that worsened after sun exposure, and new forgetfulness. Which vitamin is deficient, and why does her diet matter?
step: The triad of diarrhea, photosensitive dermatitis and dementia is pellagra, the classic niacin (B3) deficiency syndrome.
step: Niacin is the precursor of NAD and NADP, which are needed by many dehydrogenases, so tissues with high turnover (gut, skin) and the brain are hit first.
step: In corn, niacin is chemically bound and poorly absorbed, and corn protein is low in tryptophan, which the body can convert to niacin (60 mg of tryptophan gives 1 mg of niacin equivalent). A corn-dominated diet therefore starves her of both sources.
step: Treatment is oral niacin or nicotinamide plus a better diet; nicotinamide is preferred when flushing from nicotinic acid is a concern.

@example
title: Converting a vitamin D dose
problem: A supplement contains 125 mcg of cholecalciferol per capsule. How many international units (IU) is that?
step: Use the conversion for vitamin D: 1 mcg equals 40 IU.
step: Multiply: $125 \times 40 = 5000$ IU.
step: Check against intake advice: typical adult recommended intakes are 600 to 800 IU per day, so this is a high-dose capsule intended for treating deficiency under supervision.

@mistakes
- Treating the macrocytic anemia of B12 deficiency with folate alone, which may improve the blood count while the neurologic damage progresses.
- Giving glucose before thiamine to a malnourished or alcohol-dependent patient, which can precipitate or worsen Wernicke encephalopathy.
- Confusing vitamin D forms: 25-hydroxyvitamin D is what you measure; 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D is the active hormone and may be normal or high in deficiency.
- Forgetting that fat-soluble vitamin deficiency often signals fat malabsorption, not just poor intake.
