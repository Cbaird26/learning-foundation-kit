@article
Lipids are transported in plasma inside **lipoproteins**: a core of triglyceride and cholesteryl ester surrounded by phospholipids, free cholesterol and **apolipoproteins** that direct each particle to enzymes and receptors.

### The lipoproteins

| Particle | Main apolipoproteins | Main cargo | Made in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chylomicron | B-48, C-II, E | dietary triglyceride | intestine |
| VLDL | B-100, C-II, E | hepatic triglyceride | liver |
| IDL | B-100, E | triglyceride and cholesterol | from VLDL |
| LDL | B-100 | cholesterol (cholesteryl ester) | from IDL |
| HDL | A-I, C-II, E | cholesterol (reverse transport) | liver, intestine |

**Apo B-48** is the intestinal form (apo B-100 truncated by RNA editing). **Apo C-II** activates **lipoprotein lipase (LPL)**. **Apo E** mediates hepatic uptake of remnants. **Apo A-I** activates **LCAT**.

### Transport pathways

- **Exogenous (dietary):** chylomicrons carry dietary triglyceride via lymph to blood; LPL on capillary endothelium hydrolyzes triglyceride, delivering fatty acids to muscle and adipose; the **chylomicron remnant** is taken up by the liver through apo E.
- **Endogenous:** the liver secretes VLDL; LPL converts it to IDL and then, with hepatic lipase, to LDL. LDL delivers cholesterol to cells by the **LDL receptor** (clathrin-coated pits) and to the liver. The receptor is recycled unless degraded by **PCSK9**.
- **Reverse cholesterol transport:** nascent HDL takes up free cholesterol from cells through **ABCA1**; **LCAT** esterifies it; **CETP** transfers cholesteryl ester from HDL to VLDL and LDL; the liver clears the remainder.

### The familial dyslipidemias

| Disorder (Fredrickson type) | Defect | Elevated | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Familial chylomicronemia (I) | LPL or apo C-II deficiency (or GPIHBP1, LMF1) | Chylomicrons; triglycerides often above 1000 mg/dL | Milky plasma, eruptive xanthomas, lipemia retinalis, hepatosplenomegaly, **pancreatitis**; no early atherosclerosis |
| Familial hypercholesterolemia (IIa) | LDL receptor (autosomal dominant), apo B-100 defect or PCSK9 gain of function | LDL | **Tendon (Achilles) xanthomas**, xanthelasma, corneal arcus, premature coronary disease; LDL often 190 mg/dL or more |
| Dysbetalipoproteinemia (III) | Apo E2/E2 (impaired remnant clearance) plus a second hit | Chylomicron remnants and IDL; both cholesterol and triglycerides | **Palmar xanthomas**, tuberoeruptive xanthomas, premature atherosclerosis |
| Familial hypertriglyceridemia (IV) | Increased hepatic VLDL production | VLDL | Pancreatitis at very high levels; worsened by alcohol, diabetes, obesity |
| Combined (IIb, V) | Various | LDL and VLDL (IIb); VLDL and chylomicrons (V) | Mixed |
| Abetalipoproteinemia | **MTTP** (microsomal triglyceride transfer protein); apo B cannot be lipidated | Absent chylomicrons, VLDL and LDL; very low cholesterol and triglyceride | Steatorrhea, **acanthocytes**, vitamin E deficiency (ataxia, retinitis pigmentosa), fat-soluble vitamin deficiency; enterocytes full of lipid |
| Tangier disease | ABCA1 | Very low HDL | Orange tonsils, neuropathy, hepatosplenomegaly |
| LCAT deficiency | LCAT | Low HDL, free cholesterol | Corneal opacities, anemia, proteinuria |

### Calculating LDL

Total cholesterol is the sum of HDL, LDL and VLDL cholesterol; VLDL cholesterol is estimated as triglyceride divided by 5 (mg/dL). The **Friedewald equation**:

$$\text{LDL} = \text{TC} - \text{HDL} - \frac{\text{TG}}{5}\quad(\text{mg/dL}).$$

It is invalid when triglycerides exceed 400 mg/dL, and is less accurate at very low LDL; direct measurement or newer equations (Martin-Hopkins, Sampson) are then used. **Non-HDL cholesterol** equals TC minus HDL.

### Treatment

- **Statins** inhibit HMG-CoA reductase and upregulate LDL receptors: first-line for LDL lowering and for heterozygous FH. The 2018 AHA/ACC guideline used percent LDL reduction and the pooled cohort equations; the 2026 ACC/AHA guideline instead uses PREVENT-ASCVD risk, LDL-C goals of below 100, 70 or 55 mg/dL, and measurement of lipoprotein(a) at least once in every adult.
- **Ezetimibe** blocks NPC1L1 absorption in the intestine; **bile acid sequestrants** lower LDL but can raise triglycerides.
- **PCSK9 inhibitors** (evolocumab, alirocumab) preserve LDL receptors; **bempedoic acid** inhibits ATP-citrate lyase.
- Homozygous FH may need **lomitapide** (inhibits MTP), **evinacumab** (ANGPTL3 antibody) or LDL apheresis.
- **Fibrates** (PPAR-alpha agonists) lower triglycerides; omega-3 fatty acids lower triglycerides; in familial chylomicronemia, dietary fat restriction is the mainstay because fibrates and statins work poorly.

@example
title: Calculating LDL cholesterol
problem: A fasting lipid panel shows total cholesterol 280 mg/dL, HDL 40 mg/dL and triglycerides 200 mg/dL. Calculate LDL and non-HDL cholesterol.
step: Check validity: triglycerides are below 400 mg/dL, so Friedewald applies.
step: Estimate VLDL cholesterol: $200 / 5 = 40$ mg/dL.
step: LDL $= 280 - 40 - 40 = 200$ mg/dL.
step: Non-HDL $= 280 - 40 = 240$ mg/dL. An LDL of 190 mg/dL or more should prompt evaluation for familial hypercholesterolemia and statin therapy.

@example
title: Recognizing a primary lipid disorder
problem: A 6-year-old boy has recurrent abdominal pain, small yellow papules on the buttocks, and plasma that separates into a creamy upper layer after standing overnight. Triglycerides are 3200 mg/dL and cholesterol 400 mg/dL. Which particle is elevated and what is the likely defect?
step: A creamy layer on top of cooled plasma means chylomicrons, which are the least dense lipoprotein.
step: Eruptive xanthomas and abdominal pain from pancreatitis match triglycerides above 1000 mg/dL.
step: Chylomicrons are cleared by LPL, which is activated by apo C-II, so the defect is in LPL or apo C-II (familial chylomicronemia, type I).
step: Management is a very-low-fat diet and treatment of pancreatitis episodes; statins and fibrates help little.

@mistakes
- Using the Friedewald equation when triglycerides exceed 400 mg/dL.
- Linking apo B-48 to the liver; B-48 is intestinal, B-100 is hepatic.
- Assuming familial hypercholesterolemia is recessive; heterozygous FH is autosomal dominant and common (about 1 in 250).
- Treating milky plasma as just an artifact; it indicates chylomicrons or very high VLDL and a risk of pancreatitis.
