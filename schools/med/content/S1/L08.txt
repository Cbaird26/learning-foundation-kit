@article
**Heart failure (HF)** is a clinical syndrome in which the heart cannot deliver enough blood for metabolic needs, or can do so only with elevated filling pressures. It is classified by left ventricular ejection fraction (EF): **HFrEF** (EF 40% or less), **HFmrEF** (41 to 49%) and **HFpEF** (50% or more).

### Mechanisms

Reduced output activates compensatory systems: the **sympathetic nervous system** and the **renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS)** raise heart rate, contractility, vasoconstriction and sodium and water retention, and ADH adds free water retention. These maintain pressure acutely but chronically increase preload, afterload and wall stress and drive **remodeling**. Pressure overload (hypertension, aortic stenosis) produces concentric hypertrophy and diastolic dysfunction (HFpEF); volume overload and myocyte loss (infarct, dilated cardiomyopathy) produce eccentric hypertrophy and a dilated, poorly contracting ventricle (HFrEF). Natriuretic peptides (ANP, BNP) are released by stretch and promote natriuresis and vasodilation, so BNP is a biomarker and neprilysin (which degrades them) is a drug target.

Common causes: ischemic heart disease, hypertension, valvular disease, cardiomyopathies, myocarditis, alcohol, tachyarrhythmias, and in HFpEF, hypertension, diabetes, obesity and amyloid. **Precipitants of decompensation:** medication or dietary nonadherence, arrhythmia (AF), ischemia, infection, NSAIDs, anemia and uncontrolled hypertension.

### Presentation and diagnosis

Left-sided: dyspnea, **orthopnea**, **paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea**, fatigue, **S3**, displaced apex and pulmonary crackles. Right-sided: **jugular venous distension**, hepatic congestion (with **hepatojugular reflux**), ascites and peripheral edema; the most common cause of right HF is left HF. Chest radiograph: cardiomegaly, cephalization, Kerley B lines, interstitial edema and pleural effusions. **BNP** above 100 pg/mL (NT-proBNP above 300 pg/mL, higher with age) supports the diagnosis but is raised by renal failure and falsely lowered by obesity; **echocardiography** gives EF, chamber size, valves and diastolic function. Staging: **ACC/AHA stages** A (at risk), B (structural disease, no symptoms), C (symptomatic) and D (advanced); **NYHA classes** I to IV describe symptoms with activity.

### Treatment of HFrEF

Four classes reduce mortality and hospitalization (**guideline-directed medical therapy, GDMT**), started early, often in parallel:

1. **ARNI (sacubitril/valsartan)**, or an ACE inhibitor or ARB if an ARNI is not possible. Never combine an ARNI with an ACE inhibitor (angioedema); allow 36 hours after the last ACE inhibitor dose.
2. **Evidence-based beta blocker** (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol). Start low once euvolemic and titrate; avoid starting in decompensation.
3. **Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist** (spironolactone, eplerenone): hyperkalemia (check potassium and creatinine); spironolactone causes gynecomastia.
4. **SGLT2 inhibitor** (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin): benefit regardless of diabetes; genital mycotic infections, volume depletion and euglycemic ketoacidosis risk.

Also: **loop diuretics** for congestion (relieve symptoms, no proven mortality benefit); **hydralazine plus isosorbide dinitrate** in patients who self-identify as Black with persistent NYHA III to IV symptoms, or for patients who cannot take ACE inhibitors, ARBs or ARNI; **ivabradine** (heart rate 70 or more in sinus rhythm despite a maximally tolerated beta blocker); digoxin for symptoms. An **ICD** is indicated for EF 35% or less despite at least 3 months of GDMT (and 40 days after MI); **cardiac resynchronization therapy** for EF 35% or less with LBBB and QRS 150 ms or more in sinus rhythm. Avoid NSAIDs, nondihydropyridine calcium channel blockers (in HFrEF), thiazolidinediones, and class I antiarrhythmics.

**HFpEF:** treat congestion with diuretics, control blood pressure and other comorbidities, and use an SGLT2 inhibitor; MRAs and ARNI may help in some patients (EF in the lower normal range).

**Acute decompensated HF:** classify by profile. "Warm and wet" (adequate perfusion, congested): IV loop diuretic (the initial IV dose at least equal to the total daily oral dose) and vasodilators if hypertensive. "Cold and wet": inotropes (dobutamine, milrinone) and possible mechanical support. Noninvasive ventilation for respiratory failure.

### Applying this to ACS

After a myocardial infarction with reduced EF, discharge on aspirin, a P2Y12 inhibitor (typically 12 months), high-intensity statin, beta blocker, ACE inhibitor or ARB (or ARNI), an MRA if EF 40% or less with heart failure or diabetes, cardiac rehabilitation, and smoking cessation.

title: Cardiac index from output and body size
problem: A patient has a cardiac output of 3.2 L/min and a body surface area of 2.0 m$^2$. Is the cardiac index normal?
step: $CI = CO/BSA = 3.2/2.0 = 1.6$ L/min/m$^2$.
step: Normal is about 2.5 to 4.0 L/min/m$^2$; below 2.2 suggests low-output state.
step: A cardiac index of 1.6 with elevated wedge pressure fits cardiogenic shock ("cold and wet").
step: Management includes inotropic support and treating the cause.

@example
title: Selecting therapy in new HFrEF
problem: A 62-year-old man has an EF of 30%, NYHA class II symptoms, BP 124/78 mm Hg, potassium 4.4 mEq/L and eGFR of 70. He is euvolemic after diuresis. What regimen do you start?
step: HFrEF: all four pillars are indicated.
step: Start an ARNI (or ACE inhibitor if unavailable), a low-dose beta blocker, an MRA (potassium and renal function permit), and an SGLT2 inhibitor.
step: Add a loop diuretic only as needed for congestion.
step: Recheck potassium and creatinine within 1 to 2 weeks, titrate every few weeks, and reassess EF after 3 months for an ICD decision.

@mistakes
- Starting a beta blocker in acutely decompensated, congested patients, or stopping it for mild congestion.
- Using an ACE inhibitor and sacubitril/valsartan together.
- Assuming loop diuretics improve survival; they relieve symptoms.
- Using an elevated BNP alone as proof of HF without considering renal failure and age.
