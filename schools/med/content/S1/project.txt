title: Cardiology casebook
overview: You will write a casebook of five supplied cases. Each has an ECG description and hemodynamic or imaging data. For each one you will interpret the data, name the diagnosis, explain the mechanism and give first-line treatment, in the style of a short teaching note. The cases are invented and use only the data written below.
goal: Interpret rate, rhythm, axis and intervals from written ECG descriptions and link them to a mechanism.
goal: Calculate and use hemodynamic values (stroke volume, ejection fraction, cardiac output, MAP, SVR, shunt ratio, valve gradient and area).
goal: Justify first-line treatment and name the key adverse effects and contraindications of each recommended drug.
step: Read the five cases below. For each, copy the data into a table before analyzing it.
Case 1 (68-year-old man, palpitations and mild dyspnea). Hypertension for 10 years. BP 124/78 mm Hg. ECG: no P waves, irregularly irregular rhythm, 13 QRS complexes in a 6-second strip, QRS 90 ms, normal axis, QT 300 ms at the average rate.
Case 2 (58-year-old man, 40 minutes of crushing chest pain). ECG: sinus bradycardia 48 per minute, 3 mm ST elevation in leads II, III and aVF with ST depression in I and aVL, R-R interval about 31 small boxes. BP 88/54 mm Hg, jugular veins distended, lungs clear. Catheter: right atrial pressure 14 mm Hg, pulmonary capillary wedge pressure 8 mm Hg, cardiac output 3.0 L/min, heart rate 48.
Case 3 (45-year-old woman, 3 months of dyspnea and orthopnea). ECG: sinus rhythm 92 per minute, PR 180 ms, QRS 160 ms with a broad notched R wave in V6 and a deep S wave in V1, QT 400 ms. Echo: end-diastolic volume 190 mL, end-systolic volume 149 mL. BP 104/70 mm Hg, central venous pressure 12 mm Hg. Cardiac output by thermodilution 3.8 L/min, body surface area 1.9 m2.
Case 4 (6-year-old girl, found to have a murmur). ECG: sinus rhythm 90 per minute, rSR' pattern in V1 with QRS 100 ms, axis +110 degrees. Exam: soft systolic ejection murmur at the left upper sternal border with a wide fixed split S2. Catheter saturations: systemic arterial 97%, mixed venous 65%, pulmonary vein 99%, pulmonary artery 83%.
Case 5 (74-year-old woman, fainted while climbing stairs). BP 128/80 mm Hg. ECG: sinus rhythm 70 per minute, PR 180 ms, QRS 100 ms, axis -10 degrees, tall R waves in V5 and V6 with ST depression and T inversion. Echo: peak aortic velocity 4.4 m/s, LVOT diameter 2.0 cm, LVOT velocity-time integral 21 cm, aortic valve velocity-time integral 80 cm.
step: ECG pass: for each case, state rate (show the calculation), rhythm, axis, PR, QRS and QTc (Bazett, $QT/\sqrt{RR}$; it overcorrects at fast rates and is unreliable in atrial fibrillation, so say so where that applies), and abnormalities such as blocks, ST changes or hypertrophy.
step: Hemodynamic pass: calculate stroke volume, EF, cardiac output or cardiac index, MAP, SVR (use $80 \times (MAP - CVP)/CO$), shunt ratio $Q_p/Q_s$, valve gradient ($4v^2$) and valve area by continuity, wherever the data allow.
step: Diagnosis: name the diagnosis for each case and explain the mechanism in two or three sentences, including how the numbers support it.
step: Management: state first-line treatment, the key drugs with mechanism and one adverse effect each, what to avoid, and one follow-up step. Where guidelines differ among organizations, say so in a clause.
step: Compare: write a half-page comparing cases 2 and 3 (which has preload-dependent physiology, and which drug classes are helpful or harmful in each).
step: Draw a left ventricular pressure-volume loop for case 3 and a second loop showing the change expected after effective treatment; explain preload and afterload in your own words.
deliverable: A written casebook of five entries, each with a data table, interpretation, diagnosis, mechanism and first-line treatment.
deliverable: Two pressure-volume loop drawings with captions explaining preload, afterload and contractility.
deliverable: A one-page summary comparing the five diagnoses with a list of the single most important distinguishing finding for each.
rubric: Calculations are correct, show their formulas and carry units (rate, QTc, cardiac index, shunt ratio, gradient and valve area).
rubric: Each diagnosis is supported by specific data from the case, not by pattern naming alone.
rubric: First-line treatments are guideline-concordant, with at least one mechanism and one adverse effect or contraindication for each drug.
rubric: Cases 2 and 3 are correctly contrasted (RV infarct avoids nitrates and diuretics and needs volume; HFrEF needs RAAS inhibitor/ARNI, beta blocker, MRA and SGLT2 inhibitor).
rubric: Pressure-volume loops are correctly labeled with the four phases and valve events.
safety: This is a study exercise with invented cases. Doses and plans are for learning and must be checked against current references and supervised practice before any clinical use; it is not medical advice.

@review
### Week by week

- **Week 1, anatomy and embryology.** The heart tube loops rightward and divides; neural crest builds the aorticopulmonary septum; the RCA supplies the inferior wall and nodes, the LAD the anterior wall and septum, the LCx the lateral wall. Fetal shunts become ligaments.
- **Week 2, cycle and hemodynamics.** Phases and sounds; $SV = EDV - ESV$, $EF = SV/EDV$, $CO = SV \times HR$, $MAP - CVP = CO \times SVR$; Fick; pressure-volume loop with preload (EDV), afterload (ESV, taller loop) and contractility (steeper end-systolic line).
- **Week 3, electrophysiology and ECG.** Action potential phases and drug targets; rate, rhythm, axis and intervals; Bazett QTc; hyperkalemia (peaked T, wide QRS) versus hypokalemia (U waves).
- **Week 4, arrhythmias.** Block types; AF management and CHA2DS2-VASc; AVNRT and adenosine; WPW and AV nodal blockers; VT versus VF; torsades and magnesium; Vaughan Williams classes.
- **Week 5, hypertension.** Stages, secondary causes (aldosteronism, renal artery stenosis, pheochromocytoma), first-line classes, and emergencies (25% MAP reduction).
- **Week 6, atherosclerosis and lipids.** Plaque stages, Friedewald, statins and add-ons, NNT, and matching an antihypertensive to the patient.
- **Week 7, ischemic heart disease.** Angina, NSTEMI and STEMI, biomarkers, histology timeline, complications, and reperfusion timing and contraindications.
- **Week 8, heart failure.** HFrEF four pillars, ARNI and ACE inhibitor safety, decompensation profiles, HFpEF, and the ACS discharge bundle.
- **Week 9, valvular disease.** Murmur timing, shape and radiation; maneuvers; $4v^2$, continuity and pressure half-time.
- **Week 10, myocardium, pericardium, endocarditis.** DCM, HCM, restrictive and constrictive physiology, pericarditis, tamponade, Duke criteria and prophylaxis.
- **Week 11, congenital heart disease.** Shunts, cyanosis, Eisenmenger, ductal-dependent lesions, $Q_p/Q_s$.
- **Week 12, vascular disease and pharmacology.** Aneurysm, dissection, PAD, vasculitis, ABI, drug classes and infusion calculations.

### Common errors

1. Mixing up preload and afterload, or treating Wood units and dyn$\cdot$s/cm$^5$ as interchangeable.
2. Using nitrates or diuretics in right ventricular infarction, or AV nodal blockers in pre-excited AF.
3. Starting beta blockers in decompensated heart failure, or combining an ACE inhibitor with an ARNI.
4. Using a rate formula incorrectly (300 over large boxes or 1500 over small boxes) and forgetting to correct the QT.
5. Waiting for a biopsy or troponin before treating giant cell arteritis or STEMI.
6. Giving a vasodilator before a beta blocker in aortic dissection.

@exam self
prompt: A 32-year-old woman has palpitations, dizziness and confusion. Her ECG shows a regular narrow-complex tachycardia at 190 per minute with no visible P waves. Her BP is 80/48 mm Hg. Interpret the rhythm, state whether she is stable, name the immediate treatment, and say what you would do if the same rhythm occurred in a stable patient and vagal maneuvers failed.
hint: Decide stability first: hypotension and altered mentation matter.
hint: Compare the unstable and stable pathways separately.
solution: Regular narrow-complex tachycardia at 190 without visible P waves suggests AVNRT (or another supraventricular tachycardia, such as atrial flutter with 2:1 block). She is unstable (hypotension, confusion), so she needs immediate synchronized cardioversion, not drugs first, with sedation if time allows. If she were stable, try vagal maneuvers (modified Valsalva); if they fail, give adenosine as a rapid IV push followed by a flush (typical adult doses of 6 mg then 12 mg, for study and to be checked against current references before any clinical use), and if that fails use a beta blocker or diltiazem or verapamil (avoiding them in reduced ejection fraction), with later ablation for recurrent AVNRT.
rubric: Names a supraventricular tachycardia (AVNRT) and notes that flutter with 2:1 block is a differential.
rubric: Declares her unstable and chooses synchronized cardioversion.
rubric: Gives the stable pathway: vagal maneuvers, then adenosine, then AV nodal blocking drugs.
rubric: Mentions ablation for recurrent AVNRT.
