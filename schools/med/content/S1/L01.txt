@article
The heart is a pump built from a tube. If you know how the tube folds, divides and is plumbed, most congenital heart disease becomes predictable, and if you know where the coronary arteries run, you can predict which wall of the ventricle dies when one is blocked.

### From tube to four chambers

The heart forms from **splanchnic (lateral plate) mesoderm** in the cardiogenic area. Paired endocardial tubes fuse into a single **primitive heart tube**, which begins beating around week 4. Its segments, from inflow to outflow, are the **sinus venosus, primitive atrium, primitive ventricle, bulbus cordis and truncus arteriosus**. The tube then **loops rightward** (d-looping), which places the future ventricles side by side and the atria behind them. Failure of normal rightward looping gives dextrocardia.

Adult derivatives to know:

| Embryonic segment | Adult structure |
|---|---|
| Sinus venosus, right horn | Smooth part of right atrium (sinus venarum), SVC and IVC entry |
| Sinus venosus, left horn | Coronary sinus and oblique vein of left atrium |
| Primitive atrium | Trabeculated parts of both atria (pectinate muscles, auricles) |
| Primitive pulmonary vein (outgrowth) | Smooth part of left atrium |
| Primitive ventricle | Trabeculated parts of the ventricles, including most of the left ventricle |
| Bulbus cordis | Smooth outflow portions of both ventricles (conus arteriosus on the right, aortic vestibule on the left) |
| Truncus arteriosus | Ascending aorta and pulmonary trunk |

### Septation

**Atria.** The septum primum grows down toward the endocardial cushions, leaving the ostium primum, which closes; a new hole, the ostium secundum, appears in the septum primum by apoptosis. The thicker septum secundum then grows beside it and leaves an opening, the **foramen ovale**, so blood can pass right to left while the lungs are collapsed.

**Ventricles.** The muscular septum grows up from the floor; the **membranous septum** forms from the endocardial cushions and the aorticopulmonary septum. A failure of the membranous part to close gives the most common congenital heart defect, the ventricular septal defect (VSD).

**Outflow tract.** **Neural crest cells** migrate into the truncus and bulbus and form the **aorticopulmonary septum**, which spirals so that the pulmonary trunk connects to the right ventricle and the aorta to the left. Failure of spiraling gives transposition of the great arteries; failure of septation gives persistent truncus arteriosus (often with 22q11.2 deletion). **Endocardial cushions** also form the AV valves (mitral, tricuspid) and the atrial and ventricular septa near them; semilunar valves develop from outflow tract tissue.

### Fetal circulation and the first breaths

Oxygenated blood leaves the placenta in the **umbilical vein** (about 80% saturated), bypasses most of the liver through the **ductus venosus** into the IVC, and streams across the **foramen ovale** to the left atrium, so the brain and coronaries receive the best blood. Deoxygenated blood returning in the SVC goes to the right ventricle, then the pulmonary trunk; because fetal pulmonary vascular resistance is high, most of it crosses the **ductus arteriosus** into the descending aorta. Two **umbilical arteries** return blood to the placenta.

At birth, lung expansion drops pulmonary vascular resistance and raises left atrial pressure, which presses the septum primum against the septum secundum (functional closure of the foramen ovale). Rising oxygen and falling prostaglandin E2 constrict the ductus. Remnants: foramen ovale becomes the **fossa ovalis**; ductus arteriosus becomes the **ligamentum arteriosum**; ductus venosus the **ligamentum venosum**; umbilical vein the **ligamentum teres hepatis**; umbilical arteries the **medial umbilical ligaments**. A persistent ductus is held open by prostaglandin E2 and closed pharmacologically with a nonselective cyclooxygenase inhibitor such as indomethacin or ibuprofen (typical for premature infants).

### Adult anatomy that matters clinically

The heart lies in the middle mediastinum within the pericardium. The right ventricle forms most of the anterior surface, the left ventricle the apex and left border, and the left atrium most of the posterior surface (which is why an enlarged left atrium can compress the esophagus and the left recurrent laryngeal nerve).

**Coronary arteries** arise from the aortic sinuses and fill mainly in diastole. The **left main** divides into the **left anterior descending (LAD)**, which supplies the anterior left ventricle and anterior two thirds of the septum, and the **left circumflex (LCx)**, which supplies the lateral wall. The **right coronary artery (RCA)** supplies the right ventricle, the SA node in about 60% of people and the AV node in about 80 to 90%. The **posterior descending artery** comes from the RCA in about 85% of people (**right dominance**) and supplies the inferior wall and posterior septum. The **coronary sinus** drains most venous blood to the right atrium.

@example
title: Following fetal blood and predicting a defect
problem: A drop of blood leaves the placenta in a fetus. Trace it to the brain, and then say what happens to a drop returning from the head in the SVC.
step: Placenta to umbilical vein: this is the most oxygenated blood in the fetus.
step: The ductus venosus carries it past the liver into the IVC, where it mixes with a little deoxygenated lower-body blood and enters the right atrium.
step: The IVC stream is directed across the foramen ovale into the left atrium, then left ventricle, ascending aorta, and the head and coronary arteries.
step: A drop from the SVC enters the right atrium, goes to the right ventricle and pulmonary trunk, and, because pulmonary resistance is high, crosses the ductus arteriosus into the descending aorta, distal to the arch vessels.
step: Prediction: a fetus whose foramen ovale and ductus both persist after birth keeps a right-to-left or left-to-right route open, which is why neonatal pulmonary resistance and pressures decide the direction of any shunt.

@example
title: From ECG leads to a culprit artery
problem: A patient has ST-segment elevation in leads II, III and aVF with a slow pulse. Which artery is the likely culprit and why does bradycardia fit?
step: Leads II, III and aVF look at the inferior wall.
step: The inferior wall is supplied by the posterior descending artery, which arises from the RCA in right-dominant hearts (about 85%).
step: The RCA also supplies the AV node (and the SA node in most people), so ischemia there can cause sinus bradycardia or AV block.
step: Likely culprit: the RCA (a left circumflex origin of the posterior descending artery in left dominance is the less common alternative).

@mistakes
- Saying the bulbus cordis forms the whole right ventricle; the primitive ventricle forms most of the trabeculated myocardium and the bulbus forms smooth outflow portions.
- Mixing up the remnants: the ductus arteriosus becomes the ligamentum arteriosum, whereas the foramen ovale becomes the fossa ovalis and the umbilical vein becomes the ligamentum teres hepatis.
- Assuming the LAD supplies the whole septum; it supplies the anterior two thirds, and the posterior third comes from the posterior descending artery.

@practice self
prompt: Explain (a) why the foramen ovale closes functionally at birth, (b) what a probe-patent foramen ovale is, and (c) why it can cause a stroke in a patient with deep vein thrombosis.
hint: Compare right and left atrial pressures before and after the first breaths.
solution: (a) At birth the lungs expand and pulmonary vascular resistance falls, so pulmonary blood flow and left atrial pressure rise; right atrial pressure falls as placental flow stops. The higher left atrial pressure pushes the septum primum against the septum secundum and closes the flap. (b) In a probe-patent foramen ovale the flap never fuses anatomically, so a probe can pass through, though no shunt occurs at rest. (c) When right atrial pressure transiently exceeds left (cough, Valsalva, pulmonary hypertension), a venous clot from a deep vein thrombosis can cross to the left atrium and embolize to the brain: a paradoxical embolism.
rubric: States that falling pulmonary resistance raises left atrial pressure above right atrial pressure at birth.
rubric: Describes probe patency as incomplete fusion of the septum primum and secundum with a potential right-to-left route.
rubric: Explains paradoxical embolism: venous thrombus, transient right-to-left shunt, systemic arterial embolus.
