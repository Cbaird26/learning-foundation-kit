title: Self-assessment cycle: two timed blocks, an error log and a four-week plan
overview: You will complete two supplied mixed blocks of 10 original Step 1 style questions under time, score them with the supplied key, and categorize every error by system and by cause. You will then convert the results into a targeted four-week study plan that you can follow and re-test.
goal: Practice the test, log, diagnose, repair, retest cycle on realistic mixed questions.
goal: Separate knowledge gaps from application, misread and timing errors, because each needs a different remedy.
goal: Produce a realistic four-week plan whose time is allocated by error counts, with a built-in re-test.
step: Set up your error log as a table with these columns: item number, system, discipline, your answer, correct answer, cause (knowledge gap, application error, misread, timing), and a one-sentence rule. Allow 90 seconds per item, so each 10-item block is 15 minutes. Use no notes.
step: Take Block 1 in the materials below, timed at 15 minutes, and write your answer letter for each item. Mark any item you guessed. Do not look at the answer key until you finish.
step: Score Block 1 with the answer key in the materials. For every wrong or guessed item, fill in a row of your error log, choosing a cause honestly. Write the one-sentence rule you would put on a flash card.
step: Wait at least one day, then take Block 2 in the materials, timed at 15 minutes, and score it with the answer key.
step: Add the errors from Block 2 to the same log. Then tally the whole log: errors by system, errors by discipline and errors by cause. Compute accuracy for each block and the share of errors in each category (errors in the category divided by total errors). With only 20 items the numbers are small, so treat them as signals rather than proof; add errors from any further practice blocks you have.
step: Build the four-week plan. Week 1: repair the system with the most errors (flash cards for knowledge gaps, short vignette sets for application errors). Week 2: the second and third priorities plus a pacing drill if timing errors appear. Week 3: mixed timed blocks that include all repaired areas. Week 4: a full mixed block, error-log update and a decision about what to repair next. For each week give hours per day, methods matched to causes, and a measurable target such as accuracy on a timed set.

## Week-by-week review

- **Week 1 (blueprint and planning):** Step 1 is pass/fail, up to 280 items in seven 60-minute blocks, about 90 seconds an item; confirm current rules in the official content outline. Plan from a diagnostic block and an error log.
- **Week 2 (biochemistry and genetics):** Enzyme, substrate, product, symptom. $2pq$ for carriers; X-linked male incidence is $q$. Imprinting, repeats, mitochondrial inheritance.
- **Week 3 (immunology and microbiology):** Infection pattern points to the defect. ANC includes bands. Know hypersensitivity types I to IV and catalase, coagulase and hemolysis tests.
- **Week 4 (pathology and pharmacology):** Necrosis patterns. $V_d$, clearance, half-life, loading and maintenance doses. CYP inducers and inhibitors. Competitive antagonists shift the curve right.
- **Week 5 (cardiovascular and respiratory):** MAP, EF, A-a gradient, oxygen content. Infarct timeline and murmur maneuvers.
- **Week 6 (renal and gastrointestinal):** Anion gap, Winter formula, $FE_{Na}$. Nephrotic versus nephritic biopsies. Crohn versus ulcerative colitis.
- **Week 7 (endocrine and reproductive):** Corrected calcium and sodium, effective osmolality. CAH enzymes, Graves, Turner, preeclampsia.
- **Week 8 (hematology and oncology):** MCV, Mentzer index, corrected reticulocytes, B12 versus folate, PT and PTT, translocations, chemotherapy toxicities.
- **Week 9 (neurology and psychiatry):** Stroke localization, CPP, GCS, neuromuscular disorders, lithium, neuroleptic malignant versus serotonin syndrome.
- **Week 10 (musculoskeletal and skin):** Nerve injuries, T-score, crystal arthropathies, autoantibodies, blistering diseases, Parkland formula.
- **Week 11 (behavioral science, ethics, biostatistics):** 2 by 2 tables, NNT, confidence intervals, bias, capacity and consent, confidentiality.
- **Week 12 (mixed blocks):** Last-line-first routine, mixed practice, error-log repair.

## Most common errors

- Answering a diagnosis when the question asks for the next step.
- Skipping the compensation check in acid-base problems.
- Forgetting bioavailability in oral dosing, or using clearance for a loading dose.
- Treating a nonsignificant result as proof of no effect.
- Studying favorite topics instead of the highest-error system.

choice: Caspofungin; hemolytic anemia
choice: Terbinafine; ototoxicity
choice: Voriconazole alone; pulmonary fibrosis
hint: The organism is Cryptococcus neoformans, which causes meningitis in advanced HIV.
hint: Induction therapy uses a polyene plus a pyrimidine analog, followed by an azole later.
solution: Cryptococcal meningitis in advanced HIV is treated with amphotericin B plus flucytosine for induction, then fluconazole. answer: 9
check: 34 - (1.5 * 10 + 8 + 2)
hint: Compute the expected $P_aCO_2$ first, then add 2 to get the upper limit.
hint: The measured value minus that upper limit is your answer.
solution: Expected $P_aCO_2 = 1.5 \times 10 + 8 = 23$, with an upper limit of 25. The measured 34 exceeds it by 9 mm Hg, which suggests an additional respiratory acidosis (inadequate compensation). The anion gap is $138 - (98 + 10) = 30$, a high-gap acidosis.

prompt: An autosomal recessive disease has an incidence of 1 in 10,000. A carrier test has a sensitivity of 99 percent and a specificity of 99 percent. Assuming Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium and testing a person from the general population, what is the positive predictive value of the test for being a carrier? Give a decimal rounded to three places.
answer: 0.667
check: (0.99 * (2 * 0.01 * 0.99)) / (0.99 * (2 * 0.01 * 0.99) + 0.01 * (1 - 2 * 0.01 * 0.99))
tol: 0.005
hint: First find the carrier frequency, which is the prevalence for this test.
hint: Then use true positives over true positives plus false positives, as for any 2 by 2 table.
solution: $q = 0.01$, $p = 0.99$, so the carrier frequency is $2pq = 0.0198$. In 100,000 people there are 1980 carriers and 98,020 non-carriers. True positives are $0.99 \times 1980 = 1960$; false positives are $0.01 \times 98{,}020 = 980$. $PPV = 1960/(1960 + 980) = 0.667$. Even an excellent test has a modest PPV when the condition is uncommon.
