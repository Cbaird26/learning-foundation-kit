@article
Hematology rewards a systematic approach: look at the **size** of the red cell, the **marrow response**, and the **destruction or loss**, then match each pattern to its mechanism.

### Anemia by MCV

$MCV = \dfrac{\text{Hct}(\%)}{\text{RBC count}(10^6/\mu L)} \times 10$ in femtoliters; normal is about 80 to 100.

| MCV | Cause | Key tests |
|---|---|---|
| Microcytic | Iron deficiency | Low ferritin, low iron, high TIBC |
| Microcytic | Thalassemia | Normal or high iron and ferritin, target cells, high RBC count |
| Microcytic | Anemia of chronic disease | Low iron, low TIBC, high ferritin (hepcidin) |
| Macrocytic | B12 or folate deficiency | Hypersegmented neutrophils, macro-ovalocytes |
| Normocytic | Hemolysis, marrow failure, kidney disease, acute blood loss | Reticulocyte count, bilirubin, LDH, haptoglobin |

The **Mentzer index** $= MCV/RBC$ count: a value below 13 favors thalassemia (many small cells), above 13 favors iron deficiency. The **corrected reticulocyte count** $=$ reticulocyte percent $\times$ Hct$/45$; a value above 2 percent suggests a good marrow response (hemolysis or bleeding), below that suggests production failure.

**Vitamin B12** deficiency (pernicious anemia from anti-intrinsic-factor and anti-parietal-cell antibodies, ileal disease, strict vegan diet) raises **both methylmalonic acid and homocysteine** and causes subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord. **Folate** deficiency raises homocysteine but not methylmalonic acid, and has no neurologic findings.

### Hemolytic anemias

- **G6PD deficiency** (X-linked): oxidative stress (infection, fava beans, dapsone, primaquine, nitrofurantoin, sulfonamides) causes hemolysis with Heinz bodies and bite cells. The enzyme is the first of the pentose phosphate pathway and supplies the NADPH needed to regenerate glutathione.
- **Hereditary spherocytosis**: ankyrin or spectrin defect, spherocytes with increased MCHC and a positive osmotic fragility or EMA-binding test; splenectomy helps.
- **Sickle cell disease**: glutamate to valine at position 6 of beta globin; hydroxyurea raises fetal hemoglobin.
- **Autoimmune hemolytic anemia**: positive direct Coombs test.

### Bleeding and clotting

PT (extrinsic and common pathways, factor VII) is prolonged by warfarin and monitored as the INR. PTT (intrinsic pathway) is prolonged by heparin and by factors VIII, IX, XI or XII deficiency. **Hemophilia A** (factor VIII, X-linked) prolongs PTT with normal PT, platelet count and bleeding time. **Von Willebrand disease**, the most common inherited bleeding disorder, prolongs the bleeding time and may prolong PTT. **TTP** (ADAMTS13 deficiency) and **HUS** (Shiga toxin from *E. coli* O157:H7) cause microangiopathic hemolysis with thrombocytopenia. **Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia** is caused by antibodies to platelet factor 4 and heparin; stop heparin and start a non-heparin anticoagulant such as argatroban.

### Oncology

Cancer arises from activation of oncogenes and loss of tumor suppressors such as *TP53* and *RB1*.

| Translocation | Disease | Product |
|---|---|---|
| t(9;22) | CML | BCR-ABL (imatinib) |
| t(8;14) | Burkitt lymphoma | MYC with immunoglobulin heavy chain |
| t(14;18) | Follicular lymphoma | BCL2 overexpression |
| t(11;14) | Mantle cell lymphoma | Cyclin D1 |
| t(15;17) | Acute promyelocytic leukemia | PML-RARA (ATRA treatment, DIC risk) |

Tumor markers aid follow-up, not screening: AFP (hepatocellular carcinoma, yolk sac tumor), CEA (colorectal), CA-125 (ovarian), calcitonin (medullary thyroid), beta-hCG (choriocarcinoma, germ cell tumors). Notable toxicities: doxorubicin (cardiomyopathy), bleomycin (pulmonary fibrosis), cisplatin (nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity), vincristine (peripheral neuropathy), cyclophosphamide (hemorrhagic cystitis; mesna protects) and methotrexate (inhibits dihydrofolate reductase; leucovorin rescues).

@example
title: Mentzer index
problem: A patient has MCV 62 fL and an RBC count of 5.6 million per microliter. Calculate the Mentzer index and interpret it.
step: Mentzer index $= 62/5.6 = 11.07$.
step: A value below 13 points to thalassemia trait rather than iron deficiency. A high RBC count with a low MCV fits.
step: Confirm with iron studies (normal) and hemoglobin electrophoresis as indicated.

@example
title: Corrected reticulocyte count
problem: A patient has a hematocrit of 30 percent and a reticulocyte count of 6 percent. What is the corrected reticulocyte count, and what does it suggest?
step: Corrected count $= 6 \times 30/45$.
step: $= 6 \times 0.667 = 4.0$ percent.
step: A value above 2 percent means the marrow is responding, so look for hemolysis or blood loss rather than production failure.

@mistakes
- Using the Mentzer index with the wrong cutoff direction.
- Treating a raised homocysteine as proof of B12 deficiency; methylmalonic acid separates B12 from folate.
- Starting folate alone in B12 deficiency, which can improve anemia while the neurologic damage progresses.
- Giving heparin again to a patient with HIT.

choice: Elevated methylmalonic acid and normal homocysteine
choice: Low homocysteine and low methylmalonic acid

hint: B12 acts on two reactions; folate acts on one.
