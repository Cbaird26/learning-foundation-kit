@article
Endocrine questions follow one logic: a gland, its stimulating hormone and its target. A defect can sit at the hypothalamus, pituitary, gland or target, and the **direction of the hormones** tells you where. Primary hypothyroidism has high TSH and low free T4; secondary (pituitary) hypothyroidism has low TSH and low free T4.

### Diabetes and its emergencies

Type 1 diabetes is autoimmune destruction of beta cells; type 2 is insulin resistance with relative deficiency. **Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)** is insulin deficiency with ketone production: high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis, glucose of 200 mg/dL or more or known diabetes (2024 consensus; classically above 250), Kussmaul respirations, abdominal pain and volume depletion. Total body potassium is depleted even when the serum level is normal or high, because acidosis and insulin lack shift potassium out of cells. Treatment is IV fluids, then insulin, with potassium replaced (the 2024 hyperglycemic-crisis consensus delays insulin until potassium is at least 3.5 mEq/L; older protocols used 3.3). **Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state** has very high glucose, high effective osmolality and little ketosis.

Useful formulas:

- Corrected sodium $= Na + 1.6 \times \dfrac{\text{glucose} - 100}{100}$ (some sources use 2.4 per 100 mg/dL; conventions differ).
- Effective serum osmolality $= 2 \times Na + \dfrac{\text{glucose}}{18}$ (BUN is omitted because urea crosses membranes freely).
- Corrected calcium $= Ca + 0.8 \times (4.0 - \text{albumin})$, with calcium and albumin in mg/dL and g/dL.

### Thyroid

**Graves disease**: IgG antibodies stimulate the TSH receptor. Features are weight loss, heat intolerance, diffuse goiter, exophthalmos and pretibial myxedema; TSH is low and free T4 is high, with diffusely increased radioiodine uptake. **Hashimoto thyroiditis** involves anti-thyroid peroxidase and antithyroglobulin antibodies, lymphocytic infiltrate with germinal centers and Hurthle cells; hypothyroidism follows. **Subacute (de Quervain) thyroiditis** is painful and follows a viral illness, with low radioiodine uptake. Thyroid storm is treated with a beta blocker, a thionamide, iodine after the thionamide and a glucocorticoid. For Graves disease in the first trimester, propylthiouracil is usually chosen because methimazole is associated with birth defects; many clinicians then switch to methimazole because of propylthiouracil hepatotoxicity, and guidelines differ on details.

### Adrenal

**Cushing syndrome** is cortisol excess (most commonly iatrogenic). In congenital adrenal hyperplasia, a block in cortisol synthesis raises ACTH and drives the pathway toward androgens:

| Enzyme deficiency | Cortisol | Mineralocorticoids | Key findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| 21-hydroxylase | Low | Low | Salt wasting, hypotension, hyperkalemia, virilization; high 17-hydroxyprogesterone |
| 11-beta-hydroxylase | Low | High (11-deoxycorticosterone) | Hypertension, hypokalemia, virilization |
| 17-alpha-hydroxylase | Low | High | Hypertension, hypokalemia, low sex hormones |

**Pheochromocytoma** causes paroxysmal headache, sweating and hypertension; give an alpha blocker (phenoxybenzamine) before a beta blocker. It is part of MEN 2A and 2B (RET), with medullary thyroid cancer; MEN 1 affects the pituitary, parathyroids and pancreas.

### Reproductive

**Turner syndrome** (45,X): short stature, webbed neck, streak ovaries with primary amenorrhea and high FSH, bicuspid aortic valve and coarctation. **Klinefelter syndrome** (47,XXY): tall stature, small firm testes, gynecomastia, low testosterone and high FSH and LH. **Polycystic ovary syndrome** combines oligo-ovulation, hyperandrogenism and polycystic ovaries on ultrasound (two of three). Early in pregnancy, hCG rises first from the syncytiotrophoblast. **Preeclampsia** is new hypertension (140/90 or higher) after 20 weeks with proteinuria or end-organ dysfunction; magnesium sulfate prevents seizures. **Ectopic pregnancy** presents with amenorrhea, pain and a positive hCG without an intrauterine pregnancy; pelvic inflammatory disease is a major risk factor.

@example
title: Correcting calcium for albumin
problem: A patient with cirrhosis has total calcium 7.6 mg/dL and albumin 2.0 g/dL. What is the corrected calcium?
step: Albumin is 2.0 g/dL below the reference value of 4.0, a deficit of 2.0.
step: Corrected $Ca = 7.6 + 0.8 \times 2.0 = 7.6 + 1.6$.
step: Corrected calcium is 9.2 mg/dL, which is normal. The low total value reflects low protein binding, not true hypocalcemia.

@example
title: Sodium and osmolality in hyperglycemia
problem: A patient with DKA has Na 128 mEq/L and glucose 600 mg/dL. Find the corrected sodium using 1.6 mEq/L per 100 mg/dL above 100.
step: Glucose is $600 - 100 = 500$ mg/dL above 100, which is 5 units of 100.
step: Correction $= 1.6 \times 5 = 8$ mEq/L.
step: Corrected $Na = 128 + 8 = 136$ mEq/L, so the low measured sodium is dilutional from glucose-driven water shift.

@mistakes
- Treating a low total calcium as hypocalcemia without correcting for albumin or checking ionized calcium.
- Giving a beta blocker before an alpha blocker in pheochromocytoma.
- Saying 21-hydroxylase deficiency causes hypertension; that is 11-beta- or 17-alpha-hydroxylase deficiency.
- Starting insulin in DKA without checking potassium.

answer: 8.4

tol: 0.05

solution: Albumin deficit is 1.5, so $7.2 + 0.8 \times 1.5 = 8.4$ mg/dL.

@practice num
prompt: A patient has Na 130 mEq/L and glucose 500 mg/dL. Using 1.6 mEq/L for each 100 mg/dL of glucose above 100, calculate the corrected sodium.
answer: 136.4
check: 130 + 1.6 * (500 - 100) / 100
tol: 0.1
hint: Find how many hundreds of mg/dL the glucose is above 100.
solution: Four units of 100 give $1.6 \times 4 = 6.4$, so corrected sodium is $130 + 6.4 = 136.4$ mEq/L.
unit: mEq/L

@practice num
prompt: A patient has Na 135 mEq/L and glucose 540 mg/dL. Calculate the effective serum osmolality in mOsm/kg using $2 \times Na + \text{glucose}/18$.
answer: 300
check: 2 * 135 + 540 / 18
tol: 0.5
hint: Double the sodium and add glucose divided by 18.
solution: $2 \times 135 = 270$ and $540/18 = 30$, so 300 mOsm/kg.
unit: mOsm/kg

prompt: A newborn with ambiguous genitalia and a 46,XX karyotype becomes hypotensive and vomits in the second week. Serum sodium is low, potassium is high, and 17-hydroxyprogesterone is markedly elevated. Which enzyme is most likely deficient?
choice*: 21-hydroxylase
choice: 11-beta-hydroxylase
choice: 17-alpha-hydroxylase
choice: 5-alpha-reductase
choice: Aromatase
hint: Salt wasting with virilization narrows the choices.
solution: Deficient 21-hydroxylase blocks both cortisol and aldosterone production, causing salt wasting, high 17-hydroxyprogesterone and androgen excess. 11-beta- and 17-alpha-hydroxylase deficiencies cause hypertension.
