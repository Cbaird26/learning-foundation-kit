@article
Musculoskeletal and skin questions are pattern-recognition questions, but each pattern has a mechanism. A nerve injured at a known bony landmark yields a predictable deficit. A crystal with a known shape and optical property identifies an arthritis. An autoantibody against a known adhesion protein places a blister in a specific layer of skin.

### Peripheral nerve injuries

| Nerve | Typical injury | Deficit |
|---|---|---|
| Axillary | Surgical neck humerus fracture, anterior shoulder dislocation | Deltoid weakness, loss of sensation over lateral shoulder |
| Radial | Midshaft humerus fracture | Wrist drop, loss of sensation on dorsal hand |
| Median | Supracondylar humerus fracture, carpal tunnel | Weak thenar muscles, loss of sensation over the lateral palm and first three digits |
| Ulnar | Medial epicondyle fracture, hook of hamate fracture | Claw hand, loss of sensation over the medial hand |
| Common fibular | Fibular neck fracture | Foot drop with high-stepping gait |
| Long thoracic | Axillary node dissection | Winged scapula |

### Bone and joint

**Osteoporosis** is low bone mass with normal mineralization. The **T-score** compares a patient's bone mineral density with the young adult mean:

$$T = \frac{\text{BMD}_{\text{patient}} - \text{BMD}_{\text{young adult mean}}}{SD_{\text{young adult}}}.$$

A T-score of $-2.5$ or lower defines osteoporosis; between $-1.0$ and $-2.5$ defines osteopenia. **Paget disease** has high alkaline phosphatase with normal calcium and phosphate, bone pain and increasing hat size. **Osteogenesis imperfecta** is a type I collagen defect with blue sclerae. **Achondroplasia** is an FGFR3 gain-of-function mutation. **Osteosarcoma** arises at the metaphysis and shows Codman triangle; **Ewing sarcoma** has t(11;22) and onion-skin periosteal reaction.

**Arthritis**:

- **Osteoarthritis**: wear and tear, asymmetric, worse at day's end; osteophytes, Heberden nodes (DIP) and Bouchard nodes (PIP).
- **Rheumatoid arthritis**: symmetric, morning stiffness above 1 hour, MCP and PIP involvement sparing the DIP, pannus; anti-CCP is most specific, rheumatoid factor is common; methotrexate is a first-line disease-modifying drug.
- **Gout**: urate crystals are needle-shaped with strong negative birefringence (yellow when parallel to the polarizer axis). Allopurinol and febuxostat inhibit xanthine oxidase; probenecid is uricosuric; colchicine, NSAIDs or glucocorticoids treat acute attacks. Allopurinol raises azathioprine and 6-mercaptopurine levels.
- **Pseudogout**: calcium pyrophosphate dihydrate crystals are rhomboid and weakly positively birefringent, typically in the knee.
- **Septic arthritis**: hot swollen joint with fever, usually *Staphylococcus aureus* (and *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* in sexually active young adults); synovial white cell count is often above 50,000 per microliter.

### Autoimmune connective tissue disease

**SLE**: ANA is sensitive; anti-dsDNA and anti-Smith are specific; low complement and immune complex nephritis. Drug-induced lupus (hydralazine, procainamide, isoniazid) has anti-histone antibodies. **Systemic sclerosis**: diffuse disease has anti-Scl-70 (topoisomerase I), limited disease (CREST) has anticentromere. **Sjogren syndrome**: anti-Ro and anti-La. **Polymyositis and dermatomyositis**: proximal weakness, anti-Jo-1, heliotrope rash and Gottron papules in dermatomyositis. **Giant cell arteritis**: older adult with new headache, jaw claudication and high ESR; start glucocorticoids immediately to prevent blindness, then confirm with temporal artery biopsy.

### Skin

**Basal cell carcinoma**: pearly papule with telangiectasia, palisading nuclei, rarely metastasizes. **Squamous cell carcinoma**: keratin pearls, arises from actinic keratosis. **Melanoma**: asymmetry, border irregularity, color variation, diameter above 6 mm and evolution; Breslow depth is the main prognostic factor, and BRAF V600E mutations are targeted by BRAF inhibitors.

| Disease | Target | Level of split | Clinical |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pemphigus vulgaris | Desmoglein 3 (and 1) | Intraepidermal | Flaccid bullae, mucosal erosions, Nikolsky positive |
| Bullous pemphigoid | BP180 and BP230 (hemidesmosomes) | Subepidermal | Tense bullae, older adults, Nikolsky negative |

**Psoriasis** has silvery plaques with Auspitz sign. **Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis** follow drugs such as allopurinol, carbamazepine and sulfonamides.

### Burns

The rule of nines estimates burned surface area in adults (head 9, each arm 9, anterior trunk 18, posterior trunk 18, each leg 18, genitals 1). The **Parkland formula** estimates crystalloid for the first 24 hours: $4 \text{ mL} \times \text{weight (kg)} \times \%\text{TBSA}$, with half given in the first 8 hours from the time of injury. Resuscitation is titrated to urine output, and protocols vary (current burn-center guidance often starts lower, near 2 mL per kg per percent). Treat the Parkland formula as a study calculation, to be checked against current references before any clinical use.

@example
title: Interpreting a T-score
problem: A 66-year-old woman has a femoral neck bone mineral density of 0.76 g/cm2. The young adult mean is 1.00 g/cm2 and the standard deviation is 0.12 g/cm2. Calculate the T-score and classify it.
step: $T = (0.76 - 1.00)/0.12$.
step: $T = -0.24/0.12 = -2.0$.
step: A T-score between $-1.0$ and $-2.5$ is osteopenia. A fragility fracture or the FRAX risk estimate could still justify treatment.

@example
title: Parkland formula
problem: A 70 kg adult has a 30 percent total body surface area burn. How much fluid does the Parkland formula (a study calculation) give for the first 24 hours and for the first 8 hours?
step: Total $= 4 \times 70 \times 30 = 8400$ mL in 24 hours.
step: Half goes in the first 8 hours: $8400/2 = 4200$ mL.
step: The rest, 4200 mL, is given over the following 16 hours. Rates are adjusted to urine output.

@mistakes
- Treating all bullae as pemphigus; the level of the split and the autoantibody target differ.
- Reversing gout (negative birefringence, needles) and pseudogout (positive birefringence, rhomboids).
- Giving the Parkland volume over 24 hours evenly instead of half in the first 8 hours.
- Waiting for biopsy before starting steroids in suspected giant cell arteritis.

prompt: A 70-year-old woman has a spine bone mineral density of 0.64 g/cm2. The young adult mean is 0.94 g/cm2 and the standard deviation is 0.12 g/cm2. Calculate her T-score.
answer: -2.5
check: (0.64 - 0.94) / 0.12
tol: 0.05
hint: Subtract the young adult mean from the patient value and divide by the standard deviation.
solution: $(0.64 - 0.94)/0.12 = -2.5$, which meets the definition of osteoporosis.

check: 4 * 80 * 25
