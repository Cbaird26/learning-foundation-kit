@article
Immunology and microbiology questions on Step 1 rarely ask you to memorize a list. They describe a pattern of infection and ask which defense is broken, or describe an organism and ask for its virulence factor, test result or treatment. Work backward from the infection pattern to the defect.

### The infection pattern points to the defect

| Pattern | Think |
|---|---|
| Recurrent encapsulated bacteria (Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae) after 6 months of age | B-cell or antibody defect |
| Viral, fungal and opportunistic infections, failure to thrive | T-cell or combined defect |
| Catalase-positive organisms, abscesses | Phagocyte defect |
| Recurrent Neisseria infections | Terminal complement (C5-C9) |

Specific diseases:

- **X-linked (Bruton) agammaglobulinemia**: mutation in **BTK**, a tyrosine kinase needed for B-cell maturation. Boys become ill after maternal IgG wanes (around 6 months). Mature B cells (CD19-positive) and all immunoglobulin classes are very low.
- **Hyper-IgM syndrome**: defective **CD40 ligand** on T cells, so class switching fails. IgM is high; IgG, IgA and IgE are low.
- **DiGeorge (22q11 deletion)**: failure of the third and fourth pharyngeal pouches, with absent thymus (low T cells), hypocalcemia from hypoparathyroidism and cardiac outflow defects.
- **Severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID)**: absent T cells and impaired B cells; adenosine deaminase deficiency is one cause.
- **Chronic granulomatous disease (CGD)**: defective **NADPH oxidase**, so phagocytes cannot produce the respiratory burst. Patients get recurrent infections with catalase-positive organisms (Staphylococcus aureus, Serratia, Burkholderia cepacia, Nocardia, Aspergillus) because those organisms destroy their own hydrogen peroxide. Diagnose with the dihydrorhodamine flow-cytometry test (or the older nitroblue tetrazolium test).
- **Leukocyte adhesion deficiency type 1**: defective CD18 integrin; delayed separation of the umbilical cord, marked neutrophilia, and infections without pus.
- **Terminal complement deficiency (C5-C9)**: recurrent Neisseria infections. **C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency** causes hereditary angioedema, with low C4.

### Hypersensitivity

| Type | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| I | IgE on mast cells | Anaphylaxis, atopic disease |
| II | Antibody to cell or tissue antigen | Autoimmune hemolytic anemia, Goodpasture, Graves, myasthenia gravis |
| III | Immune complex deposition | Serum sickness, SLE, post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis |
| IV | Sensitized T cells | Tuberculin skin test, contact dermatitis, transplant rejection |

Rejection: **hyperacute** (minutes, preformed antibody), **acute** (weeks, T cells), **chronic** (months to years, vascular fibrosis).

### Bacteria by test

Gram-positive cocci split on **catalase**: staphylococci are catalase-positive, streptococci catalase-negative. **S. aureus** is coagulase-positive; **S. epidermidis** forms biofilms on prosthetic material; **S. saprophyticus** causes urinary tract infection in sexually active young women. Streptococci are classified by hemolysis. **Group A (S. pyogenes)** is beta-hemolytic and bacitracin-sensitive, causing pharyngitis, skin infection, rheumatic fever and post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis. **Group B** causes neonatal sepsis. **S. pneumoniae** is alpha-hemolytic, optochin-sensitive and lancet-shaped. Neisseria are Gram-negative diplococci.

### Antibiotic mechanisms in brief

Beta-lactams block transpeptidase cross-linking of peptidoglycan. Vancomycin binds D-Ala-D-Ala. Aminoglycosides bind the 30S subunit and need oxygen to enter cells, so they do not work against anaerobes. Macrolides bind 50S. Fluoroquinolones inhibit DNA gyrase (topoisomerase II) and topoisomerase IV.

### Two quick calculations

**Absolute neutrophil count (ANC)** equals total white count times the fraction of segmented neutrophils plus bands. Neutropenia is commonly graded as mild (1000 to 1500 per microliter), moderate (500 to 1000) and severe (below 500), which carries a high risk of infection.

**Bacterial growth** with a fixed doubling time $t_d$: $N = N_0 \times 2^{t/t_d}$.

@example
title: Absolute neutrophil count
problem: A patient receiving chemotherapy has a white count of 3000 per microliter with 40 percent segmented neutrophils and 5 percent bands. Calculate the ANC and grade the neutropenia.
step: Neutrophils plus bands are $40\% + 5\% = 45\%$ of the white count.
step: ANC $= 3000 \times 0.45 = 1350$ per microliter.
step: This lies between 1000 and 1500, so it is mild neutropenia.

@example
title: Bacterial doubling
problem: A culture starts with 2000 bacteria that double every 20 minutes. How many are present after 3 hours?
step: Three hours is 180 minutes, so there are $180/20 = 9$ doublings.
step: $N = 2000 \times 2^9 = 2000 \times 512$.
step: $N = 1{,}024{,}000$ bacteria.

@mistakes
- Forgetting to include bands in the ANC.
- Saying "no immunoglobulin deficiency" for hyper-IgM; IgG, IgA and IgE are low while IgM is high.
- Treating a Gram-positive coccus as an S. aureus without catalase and coagulase evidence.
- Assuming an aminoglycoside will treat an anaerobic abscess.
