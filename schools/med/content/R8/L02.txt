@article
Biochemistry and genetics are tested as **mechanism to patient**: a missing enzyme explains a pattern of lab abnormalities, and a pattern of inheritance explains who in a family is affected. Learn each disease as one chain: enzyme, substrate that accumulates, product that is missing, clinical result.

### Energy metabolism and the fasting state

**Phosphofructokinase-1** is the rate-limiting enzyme of glycolysis. It is stimulated by AMP and fructose-2,6-bisphosphate and inhibited by ATP and citrate. In fasting, the liver makes glucose by gluconeogenesis using four enzymes that bypass irreversible glycolytic steps: **pyruvate carboxylase** (needs biotin, activated by acetyl-CoA), PEP carboxykinase, fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase and **glucose-6-phosphatase**. Glucose-6-phosphatase is also the last step of glycogen breakdown, so its absence blocks both pathways.

### Glycogen storage diseases

- **von Gierke (type I), glucose-6-phosphatase deficiency**: severe fasting hypoglycemia, hepatomegaly, lactic acidosis, hyperuricemia and hyperlipidemia. Glucagon does not raise blood glucose.
- **Pompe (type II), lysosomal acid alpha-glucosidase deficiency**: cardiomegaly, hypotonia and early death in infants.
- **Cori (type III), debranching enzyme deficiency**: hypoglycemia and hepatomegaly, but lactate and uric acid are typically normal.
- **McArdle (type V), muscle glycogen phosphorylase deficiency**: painful cramps and myoglobinuria with exercise, and no rise in lactate during an ischemic forearm exercise test.

### Amino acids and the urea cycle

**Ornithine transcarbamylase (OTC) deficiency** is X-linked recessive and is the most common urea cycle disorder. Ammonia accumulates (vomiting, lethargy, cerebral edema after protein feeds), BUN is low, and carbamoyl phosphate spills into pyrimidine synthesis, producing **orotic aciduria**. In **phenylketonuria**, phenylalanine hydroxylase (or its cofactor tetrahydrobiopterin) is deficient; phenylalanine accumulates, **tyrosine becomes essential**, and untreated children have intellectual disability, hypopigmentation and a musty odor. Treatment is a low-phenylalanine diet.

### Lysosomal storage diseases

| Gaucher | Glucocerebrosidase | Hepatosplenomegaly, bone pain, crumpled-tissue macrophages |
| Fabry (X-linked) | Alpha-galactosidase A | Neuropathic pain, angiokeratomas, renal failure |
| Hurler | Alpha-L-iduronidase | Coarse facies, corneal clouding |

### Hardy-Weinberg and inheritance

For a locus with two alleles, $p + q = 1$ and genotype frequencies are $p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$, assuming random mating, no selection, migration or mutation, and a large population. For an autosomal recessive disease, **incidence $= q^2$**, so $q = \sqrt{\text{incidence}}$ and the **carrier frequency is $2pq$**. For an X-linked recessive disease, affected male frequency is $q$ and affected female frequency is $q^2$.

Other patterns to know:

- **Mitochondrial inheritance** is maternal; all children of an affected mother are at risk, and **heteroplasmy** explains variable severity.
- **Imprinting**: **Prader-Willi** results from loss of the paternal 15q11-q13 contribution (paternal deletion or maternal uniparental disomy): hypotonia, hyperphagia, obesity and hypogonadism. **Angelman** results from loss of the maternal contribution (UBE3A): happy demeanor, seizures and ataxia.
- **Trinucleotide repeats**: Huntington (CAG), fragile X (CGG, FMR1), myotonic dystrophy (CTG) and Friedreich ataxia (GAA). Repeats can expand across generations (anticipation).

### Vitamin links

Thiamine is the cofactor for pyruvate dehydrogenase, alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase and transketolase. In alcohol use disorder, thiamine deficiency causes Wernicke encephalopathy (confusion, ophthalmoplegia, ataxia).

@example
title: Carrier frequency from disease incidence
problem: An autosomal recessive disorder affects 1 in 3600 newborns. Assuming Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, what fraction of the population are carriers? Round to three decimals.
step: Incidence is $q^2 = 1/3600$, so $q = 1/60$.
step: Then $p = 1 - q = 59/60$.
step: Carrier frequency is $2pq = 2 \times (59/60) \times (1/60) = 118/3600 \approx 0.0328$.
step: Rounded, about 0.033, or about 1 in 30. The approximation $2q$ gives $1/30$, which is nearly the same because $p$ is close to 1.

@example
title: Recognizing a fasting-hypoglycemia pattern
problem: A 2-year-old has a large liver, fasting hypoglycemia that does not respond to glucagon, lactic acidosis and high uric acid. Name the enzyme and explain the lab findings.
step: Hepatomegaly with fasting hypoglycemia points to a hepatic glycogen or gluconeogenesis defect. Normal glucose cannot be released from glycogen or made from lactate.
step: Lactic acidosis and hyperuricemia fit von Gierke disease: lactate and glucose-6-phosphate accumulate, and excess glucose-6-phosphate shunts into pathways that make urate and lipids.
step: The enzyme is glucose-6-phosphatase. Glucagon cannot help because it works upstream of the missing step.

@mistakes
- Using $q$ instead of $2pq$ when asked for carriers, or using $q^2$ as the carrier frequency.
- Mixing up Tay-Sachs (no organomegaly) and Niemann-Pick (hepatosplenomegaly) because both show a cherry-red macula.
- Forgetting that in X-linked recessive disease male incidence is $q$, not $q^2$.
- Confusing Prader-Willi (paternal copy lost) and Angelman (maternal copy lost).

@practice num
prompt: An autosomal recessive disease has an incidence of 1 in 10,000. Assuming Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, what is the carrier frequency $2pq$? Give a decimal rounded to four places.
answer: 0.0198
check: 2 * math.sqrt(1/10000) * (1 - math.sqrt(1/10000))
tol: 0.0005
hint: Incidence equals $q^2$.
hint: Find $q$, then $p = 1 - q$.
solution: $q = 0.01$, $p = 0.99$, so $2pq = 2(0.99)(0.01) = 0.0198$.

@practice num
prompt: An X-linked recessive disease affects 1 in 5000 males. Assuming Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, what is the frequency of affected females? Give the answer as a decimal in scientific notation.
answer: 4e-8
check: (1/5000)**2
tol: 1%
hint: For X-linked recessive disease, the male incidence equals the allele frequency $q$.
hint: Affected females are homozygous, so use $q^2$.
solution: $q = 1/5000 = 0.0002$, and $q^2 = 4 \times 10^{-8}$.

@quiz num
prompt: An autosomal recessive disorder has an incidence of 1 in 6400. Assuming Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, what is the carrier frequency? Give a decimal rounded to four places.
answer: 0.0247
check: 2 * (1/80) * (79/80)
tol: 0.0005
hint: Take the square root of the incidence to get $q$.
hint: $q = 1/80$, so $p = 79/80$.
solution: $2pq = 2 \times (79/80)(1/80) = 158/6400 \approx 0.0247$.

@quiz mc
