@article
Letters have names, and they also have **sounds**. The sound is what a letter says inside a word. Knowing sounds lets you read.

### Note for the grown-up

Say each sound short and clean. Do not add "uh" at the end if you can help it: say /b/ with a quick puff of the lips, not "buh". Some sounds can be stretched.

- /m/ hum with closed lips: mmm, like m in mom.
- /s/ hiss like a snake: sss, like s in sun.
- /f/ top teeth on bottom lip: fff, like f in fish.
- /t/ a quick tap of the tongue, like t in top.
- c says /k/ as in cat. g says /g/ as in goat.

### The five vowels (short sounds)

- a as in apple
- e as in egg
- i as in itch
- o as in octopus
- u as in umbrella

### First sounds

The **first sound** is the sound you hear at the start of a word. In "dog" the first sound is /d/. The letter that makes that sound is d.

### Try it with your grown-up

Look around the room. Pick three things and say the first sound of each. For now, stay with single letters: "table, /t/", "lamp, /l/", "bed, /b/".

@example
title: Find the first sound
problem: What is the first sound in "sun", and which letter makes it?
step: Say the word slowly: sss-un.
step: The first sound you hear is /s/, the hissing snake sound.
step: The letter that says /s/ is s. So the first letter of "sun" is s.

@mistakes
- Saying "buh" for b. Try a quick /b/ with no extra "uh".
- Reading c as "see" inside a word. In "cat" it says /k/.
- Using the letter name instead of the sound. The letter m is named "em", but it says /m/.
