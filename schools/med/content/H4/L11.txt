@article
If a car travels $150$ km in $2$ hours, its average speed is $75$ km/h, even though the speedometer wandered up and down. An **average rate of change** compresses a whole interval into one number.

### Definition

For a function $f$ on the interval $[a, b]$,

$$\text{average rate of change} = \frac{\Delta f}{\Delta x} = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}.$$

Subtract in the same order on top and bottom. The units are (output units) per (input unit): meters per second, dollars per year, liters per minute.

### Geometric meaning: the secant line

The number above is the slope of the **secant line** through $(a, f(a))$ and $(b, f(b))$. A positive value means $f$ ends higher than it started; a negative value means it ends lower. A value of $0$ does **not** mean $f$ is constant: for $f(x) = x^2$ on $[-2, 2]$ we get $\frac{4 - 4}{4} = 0$, although $f$ dips to $0$ in the middle.

### How the families differ

- **Linear** $f(x) = mx + b$: the average rate is $m$ on every interval.
- **Quadratic** $f(x) = x^2$: on $[x, x+h]$ the average rate is $2x + h$, so it depends on where the interval sits.
- **Exponential** $f(x) = ab^x$: on $[x, x+1]$ the average rate is $ab^x(b - 1)$, which is proportional to the function itself. Growth gets faster as the output gets larger.

### The difference quotient

Using the interval $[x, x+h]$ gives the **difference quotient**

$$\frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}, \qquad h \neq 0.$$

The workflow is always the same: expand $f(x+h)$, subtract $f(x)$ so that the terms without $h$ cancel, then divide by $h$. Four standard results:

| $f(x)$ | $\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$ |
|---|---|
| $x^2$ | $2x + h$ |
| $x^3$ | $3x^2 + 3xh + h^2$ |
| $\dfrac1x$ | $-\dfrac{1}{x(x+h)}$ (combine the fractions first) |
| $\sqrt{x}$ | $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x+h} + \sqrt{x}}$ (multiply top and bottom by the conjugate) |

### Looking ahead

Shrink $h$ and the secant slope depends only on a tiny interval around $x$. If those slopes settle toward a number, that number is the **instantaneous rate of change** at $x$. Next week turns this idea into the limit.

@example
title: Average velocity on two intervals
problem: A cart's position is $s(t) = 5t^2 + 2t$ meters at time $t$ seconds. Find its average velocity on $[1, 3]$ and on $[1, 1.1]$.
step: Evaluate the positions: $s(1) = 5 + 2 = 7$ m and $s(3) = 45 + 6 = 51$ m.
step: Average velocity on $[1, 3]$: $\dfrac{51 - 7}{3 - 1} = \dfrac{44}{2} = 22$ m/s. The unit is meters per second, a change in position per change in time.
step: Now $s(1.1) = 5(1.21) + 2.2 = 8.25$ m, so on $[1, 1.1]$ the average velocity is $\dfrac{8.25 - 7}{0.1} = 12.5$ m/s.
step: The two answers differ because the cart speeds up. The shorter interval starting at $t = 1$ describes the motion near $t = 1$ more closely.

@example
title: Simplify a difference quotient
problem: Simplify $\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$ for $f(x) = x^2 - 3x$, then evaluate it at $x = 2$ for $h = 1,\ 0.1,\ 0.01$.
step: Expand: $f(x+h) = (x+h)^2 - 3(x+h) = x^2 + 2xh + h^2 - 3x - 3h$.
step: Subtract $f(x) = x^2 - 3x$: the difference is $2xh + h^2 - 3h$.
step: Divide by $h$ (allowed because $h \neq 0$): $\dfrac{2xh + h^2 - 3h}{h} = 2x + h - 3$.
step: At $x = 2$ this is $1 + h$, giving the secant slopes $2,\ 1.1,\ 1.01$ for $h = 1,\ 0.1,\ 0.01$.
step: The slopes are settling toward $1$ as $h$ shrinks, which previews the instantaneous rate at $x = 2$.

@mistakes
- Subtracting in different orders, as in $\dfrac{f(b) - f(a)}{a - b}$, which flips the sign.
- Confusing the average rate of change with the average of the function values, $\frac{f(a) + f(b)}{2}$.
- Expanding $(x + h)^2$ as $x^2 + h^2$. The middle term $2xh$ is the one that survives.
- Reporting a rate without units, or substituting $h = 0$ before simplifying and getting $\frac{0}{0}$.

@practice num
prompt: Find the average rate of change of $f(x) = x^3 - 2x$ on the interval $[1, 3]$.
answer: 11
check: (lambda f: (f(3) - f(1))/(3 - 1))(lambda x: x**3 - 2*x)
hint: Compute $f(1)$ and $f(3)$ separately.
hint: $f(1) = -1$ and $f(3) = 21$. Divide the change in $f$ by the change in $x$.
solution: $f(3) = 27 - 6 = 21$ and $f(1) = 1 - 2 = -1$. The average rate is $\dfrac{21 - (-1)}{3 - 1} = \dfrac{22}{2} = 11$.

@practice num
prompt: A cup of tea (simulated data) is at $90$ degrees C at $0$ min, $62$ degrees C at $10$ min and $46$ degrees C at $20$ min. Find the average rate of change of its temperature from $t = 0$ to $t = 20$ minutes.
answer: -2.2
check: (46 - 90)/(20 - 0)
unit: deg C/min
hint: Use only the endpoint values of the interval.
hint: Temperature fell by $44$ degrees over $20$ minutes. Keep the sign.
solution: $\dfrac{46 - 90}{20 - 0} = -2.2$ degrees C per minute. The negative sign says the tea cools. The middle reading is not needed, though it shows the cooling slows down: $-2.8$ on $[0, 10]$ and $-1.6$ on $[10, 20]$.

@practice mc
prompt: The average rate of change of $f(x) = x^2$ on $[-2, 2]$ is $0$. What does this tell you?
choice*: The secant line through the endpoints is horizontal, but $f$ is not constant on the interval.
choice: $f$ is constant on $[-2, 2]$.
choice: The graph of $f$ has slope $0$ at every point of $[-2, 2]$.
choice: $f(-2) \neq f(2)$.
hint: Compute $f(-2)$ and $f(2)$ and look at the numerator of the rate.
hint: An average over an interval can hide ups and downs in the middle.
solution: $f(-2) = f(2) = 4$, so the change is $0$ and the secant is horizontal. But $f(0) = 0$, so $f$ goes down and then back up. An average rate of $0$ says nothing about what happens in between.

@practice expr
prompt: Simplify the difference quotient $\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$ for $f(x) = 3x^2 - x$.
vars: x, h
lead: $\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} =$
answer: 6x + 3h - 1
check: lambda x, h: ((3*(x + h)**2 - (x + h)) - (3*x**2 - x))/h
hint: Expand $3(x+h)^2$ completely before subtracting $f(x)$.
hint: After subtracting, every term has a factor $h$. Divide it out.
solution: $f(x+h) - f(x) = 3(x^2 + 2xh + h^2) - x - h - 3x^2 + x = 6xh + 3h^2 - h$. Divide by $h$: $6x + 3h - 1$.

@practice expr
prompt: Simplify the difference quotient $\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$ for $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ into a single fraction.
vars: x, h
lead: $\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} =$
answer: -1/(x(x + h))
check: lambda x, h: (1/(x + h) - 1/x)/h
hint: Combine $\frac{1}{x+h} - \frac{1}{x}$ over the common denominator $x(x+h)$.
hint: The numerator becomes $x - (x + h) = -h$. Then divide by $h$.
solution: $\dfrac{1}{x+h} - \dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{x - (x+h)}{x(x+h)} = \dfrac{-h}{x(x+h)}$. Dividing by $h$ gives $-\dfrac{1}{x(x+h)}$.

@quiz num
prompt: A population is modeled by $P(t) = 500\cdot 1.2^t$ people, with $t$ in years. Find the average rate of change from $t = 2$ to $t = 5$, in people per year, rounded to one decimal place.
answer: 174.7
check: (lambda P: (P(5) - P(2))/3)(lambda t: 500 * 1.2**t)
tol: 0.05
unit: people/year
hint: Compute $P(2)$ and $P(5)$ first.
hint: $P(2) = 720$ and $P(5) \approx 1244.16$. Divide the change by $3$ years.
solution: $P(5) = 500(2.48832) = 1244.16$ and $P(2) = 500(1.44) = 720$. The rate is $\dfrac{1244.16 - 720}{3} = \dfrac{524.16}{3} \approx 174.7$ people per year.

@quiz expr
prompt: Simplify $\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$ for $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ so that $h$ no longer appears in the numerator or as a common factor to cancel. (Hint: multiply by the conjugate.)
vars: x, h
lead: $\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} =$
answer: 1/(sqrt(x + h) + sqrt(x))
check: lambda x, h: (math.sqrt(x + h) - math.sqrt(x))/h
hint: Multiply the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{x+h} + \sqrt{x}$.
hint: The numerator becomes a difference of squares: $(x + h) - x = h$.
solution: $\dfrac{\sqrt{x+h} - \sqrt{x}}{h}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{x+h} + \sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{x+h} + \sqrt{x}} = \dfrac{(x+h) - x}{h\left(\sqrt{x+h} + \sqrt{x}\right)} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x+h} + \sqrt{x}}$.
