@article
A single number that tells where a data set is centered is a **measure of center**. A number that tells how far the data are spread is a **measure of spread**. Together they summarize a data set.

### Mean

The **mean** is the "fair share" value: add all values and divide by how many there are. For $4, 8, 6, 10, 12$ the mean is $\frac{4 + 8 + 6 + 10 + 12}{5} = \frac{40}{5} = 8$.

### Median

The **median** is the middle value when the data are in order. With an even number of values, take the mean of the two middle ones. For $1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11$ the median is $\frac{5 + 7}{2} = 6$.

### Outliers

An **outlier** is a value far from the rest. In $3, 4, 4, 5, 40$ the mean is $11.2$ but the median is $4$. The mean is pulled toward the outlier, while the median is **resistant**. For skewed data or data with outliers, the median is usually the better center.

### Range and quartiles

The **range** is the maximum minus the minimum. The **quartiles** cut the ordered data in four parts: the **median** is $Q_2$, $Q_1$ is the median of the lower half and $Q_3$ is the median of the upper half. (If the number of values is odd, leave the median out of both halves.) The **interquartile range** is

$$IQR = Q_3 - Q_1,$$

the spread of the middle half of the data. The IQR is hardly affected by outliers.

### Box plots

A **box plot** uses the **five-number summary**: minimum, $Q_1$, median, $Q_3$, maximum. Draw a box from $Q_1$ to $Q_3$, a line inside at the median, and whiskers out to the minimum and maximum. A long whisker or a skewed box shows an uneven spread.

### Describing a data set

Say where the center is, how wide the spread is (range or IQR), and note the shape and any outliers, always with units: "The typical commute is about $7$ minutes, and the middle half of commutes lie within $3$ minutes of each other."

@example
title: Five-number summary
problem: Find the five-number summary and the IQR of $1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 11$.
step: The data are already ordered and there are $7$ values. The median is the fourth value, $6$.
step: Leave out the median. The lower half is $1, 3, 4$, so $Q_1 = 3$. The upper half is $7, 9, 11$, so $Q_3 = 9$.
step: The minimum is $1$ and the maximum is $11$. The five-number summary is $1, 3, 6, 9, 11$.
step: $IQR = 9 - 3 = 6$.

@mistakes
- Finding the median without putting the data in order first.
- Forgetting to average the two middle values when the count is even.
- Using the mean to describe a typical value when a large outlier is present.

answer: 8
check: (12 + 14) / 2 - (4 + 6) / 2
hint: Split the ordered data into a lower half and an upper half.
hint: $Q_1$ is the median of $2, 4, 6, 8$ and $Q_3$ is the median of $10, 12, 14, 16$.
solution: $Q_1 = \frac{4 + 6}{2} = 5$ and $Q_3 = \frac{12 + 14}{2} = 13$, so $IQR = 13 - 5 = 8$.

@quiz num
prompt: The mean of four test scores is $82$. Three of the scores are $78$, $85$ and $90$. What is the fourth score?
answer: 75
check: 4 * 82 - (78 + 85 + 90)
hint: The mean times the number of scores gives the total.
hint: The total is $4 \times 82$. Subtract the three known scores.
solution: The total is $4 \times 82 = 328$. The known scores sum to $253$, so the fourth score is $328 - 253 = 75$.

@quiz mc
prompt: The data $2, 3, 3, 4, 38$ contain an outlier. Which value best describes a typical data value?
choice*: The median, $3$
choice: The mean, $10$
choice: The range, $36$
choice: The maximum, $38$
hint: Which measure is not pulled by one extreme value?
hint: Compare the median and the mean.
solution: The outlier $38$ pulls the mean up to $10$, which is larger than four of the five values. The median $3$ is resistant, so it better describes a typical value.
