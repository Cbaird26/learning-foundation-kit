@article
An object is **in motion** if its position changes compared to a reference point. We describe motion with three ideas: speed, velocity and acceleration.

### Speed

**Average speed** is the total distance traveled divided by the total time:

$$v = \frac{d}{t}$$

where $d$ is distance in meters (m), $t$ is time in seconds (s), and speed is in meters per second (m/s). By rearranging, $d = vt$ and $t = d/v$. A toy car that travels $2.0\ \mathrm{m}$ in $4.0\ \mathrm{s}$ has an average speed of $0.5\ \mathrm{m/s}$.

### Velocity

**Velocity** is speed together with a direction, such as $5\ \mathrm{m/s}$ north. Speed is a quantity with only a size; velocity also has a direction. Two cars that move at $20\ \mathrm{m/s}$ in opposite directions have the same speed but different velocities. If you run once around a circular track and end where you started, your average speed is not zero, but your average velocity is zero because your overall change in position is zero.

### Acceleration

**Acceleration** is how quickly velocity changes. If speed increases, decreases, or direction changes, the object is accelerating:

$$a = \frac{v_f - v_i}{t}$$

where $v_i$ is the starting velocity and $v_f$ is the final velocity. Acceleration is in meters per second per second, written $\mathrm{m/s^2}$. A car that speeds up from $0$ to $10\ \mathrm{m/s}$ in $5\ \mathrm{s}$ has $a = (10 - 0)/5 = 2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. A negative acceleration means slowing down (when the motion is in the positive direction).

### Timing a moving toy

To measure the speed of a toy car or a rolling ball:

1. Mark a start line and a finish line with tape and measure the distance with a ruler or tape measure.
2. Release the toy the same way each time and time it from the start line to the finish line with a phone stopwatch.
3. Repeat at least three times and take the **average** time. One timing can be off because of your reaction time.
4. Compute $v = d/t$ using the average time.

Changing only one factor at a time (such as the ramp height) keeps the test fair.

@example
title: Average speed of a toy car
problem: A toy car rolls $18\ \mathrm{m}$ across a gym floor in $6.0\ \mathrm{s}$. What is its average speed? How long would the car take to roll $30\ \mathrm{m}$ at that speed?
step: Use $v = d/t$: $v = 18 \div 6.0 = 3.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$.
step: To find time, rearrange to $t = d/v$.
step: $t = 30 \div 3.0 = 10\ \mathrm{s}$.

@mistakes
- Mixing units, such as using centimeters for distance and seconds for time and reporting m/s.
- Using the speed formula when the object does not move at a steady rate and forgetting that the result is an average.
- Saying an object that moves at a constant speed around a curve has constant velocity. Its direction changes, so its velocity changes.
- Timing only once and trusting a single result.
