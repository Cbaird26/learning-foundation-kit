### Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP)
**Pneumonia** is infection of the lung parenchyma. Typical presentation: fever, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, dyspnea, crackles, egophony and dullness over the consolidated lobe; a chest radiograph shows a new infiltrate (needed for diagnosis). Oxygen saturation, respiratory rate and mental status guide severity. The most common cause is **Streptococcus pneumoniae** (lobar consolidation, rusty sputum, gram-positive lancet-shaped diplococci). Other causes by setting:
- **Atypical** (Mycoplasma pneumoniae in young adults with a walking pneumonia, cold agglutinins and bullous myringitis; Chlamydophila pneumoniae; Legionella pneumophila: contaminated water or air conditioning, **hyponatremia, diarrhea, confusion, elevated liver enzymes**, diagnosed with the **urinary antigen**; no Gram stain organisms and poor growth on routine media, requiring buffered charcoal yeast extract agar). Atypical organisms either lack a cell wall (Mycoplasma) or live intracellularly (Legionella, Chlamydophila), so beta-lactams do not treat them; macrolides, doxycycline and respiratory fluoroquinolones do.
- **Haemophilus influenzae** and **Moraxella catarrhalis**: COPD patients.
- **Staphylococcus aureus**: after influenza; cavitation, pneumatoceles.
- **Klebsiella pneumoniae**: alcohol use disorder, aspiration; thick "currant jelly" sputum, upper-lobe cavitation.
- **Anaerobes** (aspiration): dependent segments (right lower lobe, posterior upper lobes), abscess.
- **Pseudomonas aeruginosa**: structural lung disease (bronchiectasis, cystic fibrosis), recent hospitalization.

**Severity: CURB-65.** One point each for **C**onfusion, **U**rea above 19 mg/dL (BUN), **R**espiratory rate of 30 or more, **B**lood pressure (systolic below 90 or diastolic 60 or below), and age **65** or older. Scores 0 to 1 favor outpatient care, 2 suggest hospital admission, and 3 or more indicate severe pneumonia (consider ICU). The Pneumonia Severity Index is a longer alternative. The IDSA/ATS 2019 guideline also defines severe CAP by major criteria (septic shock needing pressors; respiratory failure needing mechanical ventilation) or at least 3 minor criteria.

**Empiric therapy (IDSA/ATS 2019, for adults).**
| Setting | Regimen |
|---|---|
| Outpatient, healthy, no comorbidity | Amoxicillin (high dose), or doxycycline, or a macrolide only where pneumococcal macrolide resistance is below 25% |
| Outpatient with comorbidities (COPD, diabetes, heart, liver or renal disease, alcohol use disorder, malignancy, asplenia) | Amoxicillin-clavulanate or a cephalosporin **plus** a macrolide or doxycycline; or respiratory fluoroquinolone monotherapy (levofloxacin, moxifloxacin) |
| Inpatient, nonsevere | Beta-lactam (ampicillin-sulbactam, ceftriaxone, ceftaroline) **plus** a macrolide, or respiratory fluoroquinolone alone |
| Inpatient, severe | Beta-lactam plus macrolide, or beta-lactam plus fluoroquinolone |

Add MRSA or antipseudomonal coverage **only** with prior isolation of the organism or recent hospitalization with IV antibiotics and locally validated risk factors. Treat for a minimum of 5 days, and until clinically stable (afebrile 48 to 72 hours with improving vital signs). Prevent with pneumococcal and influenza vaccination and smoking cessation. Complications include parapneumonic effusion and empyema (covered in a later lesson), lung abscess and ARDS.

### Tuberculosis
*Mycobacterium tuberculosis* is an acid-fast bacillus transmitted by airborne droplet nuclei. In **primary TB**, bacilli reach the lower or mid lung, are engulfed by macrophages and form the **Ghon focus**; with hilar node involvement this is the **Ghon complex** (calcified: Ranke complex). Cell-mediated immunity (Th1: IFN-gamma activating macrophages, TNF-alpha) walls the organisms off in **caseating granulomas** with Langhans giant cells; most people develop **latent TB**, asymptomatic and noninfectious. Risks for reactivation include HIV, TNF-alpha inhibitors, steroids, diabetes and malnutrition. **Secondary (reactivation) TB** favors the **upper lobes and apices** (high oxygen tension), with cavitation, hemoptysis, night sweats, weight loss and chronic cough. Immunosuppressed patients may have **miliary** disease (millet-seed nodules), or extrapulmonary disease (Pott disease of the spine, lymphadenitis (scrofula), meningitis, renal, adrenal).

**Diagnosis.** Latent infection: **interferon-gamma release assay** (IGRA) or the **tuberculin skin test** (positive induration: 5 mm or more in HIV, recent contacts, immunosuppression or fibrotic changes; 10 mm or more for those with other risks such as health workers, immigrants from high-prevalence countries and the homeless; 15 mm or more in low-risk persons). IGRA is not affected by BCG vaccination. Active disease: three sputum samples for **acid-fast smear and culture**, plus a **nucleic acid amplification test** (for example Xpert MTB/RIF) which also detects rifampin resistance. Culture remains the gold standard.

**Treatment of active drug-susceptible TB:** 2 months of **rifampin, isoniazid, pyrazinamide and ethambutol (RIPE)**, then 4 months of isoniazid plus rifampin. **Adverse effects:**
- **Rifampin:** orange-red body fluids, CYP450 induction (drug interactions), hepatitis.
- **Isoniazid:** hepatitis and **peripheral neuropathy** (prevented with **pyridoxine, vitamin B6**), lupus-like syndrome; slow acetylators are at higher risk.
- **Pyrazinamide:** hyperuricemia and gout, hepatitis.
- **Ethambutol:** optic neuritis (red-green color blindness and reduced acuity).

Latent TB is treated to prevent reactivation: rifampin for 4 months, isoniazid plus rifapentine weekly for 3 months, or isoniazid for 6 to 9 months. Airborne isolation is required for suspected active pulmonary TB. Report cases to public health and treat with directly observed therapy where possible.

@example
title: Scoring CURB-65
problem: A 72-year-old woman is brought in with fever, new confusion, a respiratory rate of 32/min, blood pressure 85/50 mmHg and a BUN of 28 mg/dL. Calculate the CURB-65 score and decide on the setting.
step: Confusion: yes (1). Urea above 19 mg/dL: 28, yes (1). Respiratory rate of 30 or more: 32, yes (1).
step: Blood pressure (systolic below 90 or diastolic 60 or below): yes (1). Age 65 or more: 72, yes (1).
step: The total is 5, indicating severe pneumonia: admit, usually to an ICU, with a beta-lactam plus macrolide (or fluoroquinolone).

@example
title: Empiric therapy for a young outpatient
problem: A healthy 26-year-old non-smoker has 4 days of fever and productive cough. Vital signs are normal and SpO$_2$ is 97 percent. A chest radiograph shows a right lower lobe infiltrate. Choose therapy.
step: He has no comorbidities and no risk factors for resistant organisms, and a CURB-65 of 0, so outpatient treatment is appropriate.
step: IDSA/ATS recommends high-dose amoxicillin, or doxycycline, as monotherapy (a macrolide only if local pneumococcal resistance is under 25 percent).
step: Treat for at least 5 days and until clinically stable, with return precautions.

@mistakes
- Using azithromycin or amoxicillin alone for a patient with significant comorbidities.
- Adding vancomycin and an antipseudomonal drug routinely, without risk factors.
- Forgetting pyridoxine when giving isoniazid to patients at risk of neuropathy.
- Treating a positive IGRA or tuberculin test as active disease; check symptoms and a radiograph first.
