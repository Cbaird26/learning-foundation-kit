### Week by week
- **Week 1, anatomy and mechanics:** conducting versus respiratory zones, dead space about 150 mL, blood-gas barrier, FRC as the balance of lung and chest-wall recoil, compliance $= \Delta V/\Delta P$, Laplace and surfactant, resistance $\propto 1/r^4$.
- **Week 2, V/Q and gas exchange:** $\dot V_A = RR(V_T - V_D)$, $P_AO_2 = F_iO_2(P_{atm} - 47) - PaCO_2/0.8$, A-a gradient, five mechanisms of hypoxemia, shunt does not respond to oxygen.
- **Week 3, PFTs:** FEV1/FVC below 0.70 means obstruction; restriction needs a low TLC; DLCO separates emphysema, intrinsic restriction and extrinsic restriction.
- **Week 4, transport:** $CaO_2 = 1.34 \times Hb \times SaO_2 + 0.003 \times PaO_2$, delivery, curve shifts, carbon monoxide, methemoglobin, Henderson-Hasselbalch and compensation rules.
- **Week 5, asthma:** type 2 inflammation, reversibility, exacerbation care, ICS-formoterol based stepwise therapy, biologics.
- **Week 6, COPD:** emphysema versus bronchitis, alpha-1 antitrypsin, GOLD ABE groups, oxygen targets, NIV, long-term oxygen.
- **Week 7, pneumonia and TB:** CURB-65, empiric therapy by setting, Legionella, RIPE, toxicities.
- **Week 8, ILD:** UIP pattern and antifibrotics, sarcoidosis, pneumoconioses, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, organizing pneumonia.
- **Week 9, PE and PH:** Wells, PERC, age-adjusted D-dimer, CTPA versus V/Q, anticoagulation, thrombolysis, WHO groups.
- **Week 10, lung cancer:** screening criteria, histology and syndromes, targeted therapy.
- **Week 11, pleura:** Light's criteria, empyema drainage, pneumothorax and tension pneumothorax.
- **Week 12, failure and ventilation:** type 1 versus type 2, ARDS, 6 mL/kg PBW, peak versus plateau pressure, respiratory drugs.

### Common errors
- Forgetting water vapor pressure when computing alveolar PO2.
- Diagnosing restriction without TLC, or obstruction without a post-bronchodilator ratio.
- Trusting pulse oximetry in carbon monoxide or methemoglobinemia.
- Giving a LABA or ICS alone in the wrong disease, and steroids in IPF.
- Using PERC when the pretest probability is not low.
- Waiting for imaging in a tension pneumothorax.

prompt: A 70-year-old man with a COPD exacerbation breathes room air at sea level (barometric pressure 760 mmHg, water vapor pressure 47 mmHg, R = 0.8). His arterial blood gas shows PaO$_2$ 48 mmHg and PaCO$_2$ 70 mmHg. What is his A-a gradient in mmHg, rounded to one decimal place? (The expected gradient for age is about age/4 + 4.)
answer: 14.2
check: 0.21 * (760 - 47) - 70 / 0.8 - 48
hint: Calculate the alveolar PO$_2$ first: $0.21 \times 713 - PaCO_2/0.8$.
hint: $0.21 \times 713 = 149.7$ and $70/0.8 = 87.5$.
solution: $P_AO_2 = 149.7 - 87.5 = 62.2$ mmHg, so A-a $= 62.2 - 48 = 14.2$ mmHg. Expected for age 70 is $70/4 + 4 = 21.5$, so the gradient is not elevated: the hypoxemia is explained by hypoventilation (hypercapnia), and he needs ventilatory support such as NIV, with oxygen titrated to an SpO$_2$ of 88 to 92 percent.
unit: mmHg
tol: 1

@exam self
prompt: A 52-year-old woman with metastatic breast cancer (on chemotherapy) presents with 2 days of pleuritic chest pain, dyspnea and a heart rate of 112/min. She has no leg swelling, no hemoptysis and no recent surgery or immobilization. Blood pressure is 124/78 mmHg and SpO$_2$ is 92 percent on room air. Her clinician thinks pulmonary embolism is the most likely diagnosis. Calculate her Wells score, state the pretest probability category, name the diagnostic test of choice (and an alternative if she has a contrast allergy), describe the expected blood gas findings, and outline initial treatment.
hint: Add points for malignancy, heart rate above 100 and PE as the most likely diagnosis, then use the cutoff of 4.
solution: Wells score: active malignancy (1), heart rate above 100 (1.5), PE most likely diagnosis (3), so 5.5, which is above 4 (PE likely; moderate probability in the three-tier system). A D-dimer is not useful because malignancy raises it and probability is not low, so proceed directly to CT pulmonary angiography; with a contrast allergy or severe renal impairment use a V/Q scan. Expected ABG: hypoxemia with a widened A-a gradient from V/Q mismatch, and respiratory alkalosis from hyperventilation (low PaCO$_2$). She is hemodynamically stable with no RV strain data, so start therapeutic anticoagulation (low-molecular-weight heparin is preferred in cancer-associated VTE, with DOACs such as apixaban as alternatives), for at least 3 months and generally as long as the cancer is active. If she became hypotensive, systemic thrombolysis would be considered. Check troponin, BNP and RV size to risk-stratify.

rubric: Blood gas findings include hypoxemia with a widened A-a gradient and respiratory alkalosis.
