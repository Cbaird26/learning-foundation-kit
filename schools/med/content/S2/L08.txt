@article
**Interstitial lung diseases (ILDs)** are a heterogeneous group of disorders that inflame or scar the alveolar walls and surrounding interstitium. They share a clinical picture: progressive exertional dyspnea, nonproductive cough, fine **"Velcro" crackles**, sometimes clubbing, a **restrictive pattern** (reduced FVC and TLC, normal or high FEV1/FVC), **reduced DLCO**, and exertional hypoxemia. High-resolution CT (HRCT) is the key test: reticulation, traction bronchiectasis, honeycombing, ground glass or nodules, and their distribution.

### Classification
- **Known cause:** occupational and environmental (pneumoconioses), drugs and radiation, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, connective tissue disease (rheumatoid arthritis, systemic sclerosis, myositis).
- **Granulomatous:** sarcoidosis.
- **Idiopathic interstitial pneumonias:** idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), nonspecific interstitial pneumonia, cryptogenic organizing pneumonia, desquamative interstitial pneumonia and respiratory bronchiolitis-ILD (smokers), acute interstitial pneumonia.

### Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis
Older adults (usually above 60, men, smokers) with progressive dyspnea over months to years. HRCT shows a **usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) pattern**: **subpleural, basal-predominant** reticulation with **honeycombing** and traction bronchiectasis, without features pointing to another cause. When the HRCT pattern is typical in the right context, no biopsy is needed; histology shows patchy fibrosis of varying age with fibroblastic foci. Repetitive alveolar epithelial injury (with telomere shortening, MUC5B variants, gastroesophageal reflux and smoking as contributors) causes aberrant repair. Prognosis is poor (median survival around 3 to 5 years from diagnosis). **Antifibrotics, nintedanib** (tyrosine kinase inhibitor) **and pirfenidone**, slow the decline in FVC but do not reverse fibrosis. **Corticosteroids and immunosuppression are harmful in IPF.** Other care: oxygen, rehabilitation, vaccination, treating reflux and comorbidities, and **lung transplantation** for suitable patients.

### Sarcoidosis
A multisystem granulomatous disease of unknown cause, more common in young adults and African American women. Findings: **noncaseating granulomas** (with Schaumann and asteroid bodies); **bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy** on chest film, with or without parenchymal disease; elevated serum ACE; **hypercalcemia** (macrophages express 1-alpha-hydroxylase and make excess 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D); increased CD4/CD8 ratio in lavage fluid. Extrapulmonary: **erythema nodosum**, uveitis, lupus pernio, arthritis, cardiac conduction disease and arrhythmia, hepatic and neurological involvement (facial nerve palsy). **Löfgren syndrome** (erythema nodosum, bilateral hilar adenopathy, migratory polyarthralgia) has an excellent prognosis. Diagnose with the compatible picture plus biopsy showing noncaseating granulomas after excluding TB, fungi and berylliosis. Many patients need no treatment; use **oral corticosteroids** for symptomatic or progressive pulmonary disease, and for cardiac, neurological, ocular or hypercalcemic disease, with steroid-sparing agents (methotrexate) for chronic disease.

### Pneumoconioses
| Disease | Exposure | Imaging | Associations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Silicosis | Sandblasting, mining, quarrying | Upper-lobe nodules; "eggshell" calcification of hilar nodes | Increased risk of TB and lung cancer |
| Coal workers' pneumoconiosis | Coal mining | Upper-lobe nodules | **Caplan syndrome** (with rheumatoid arthritis); anthracosis |
| Asbestosis | Shipbuilding, insulation, construction | **Lower-lobe** fibrosis; calcified **pleural plaques** | Lung cancer (more common than mesothelioma), mesothelioma; ferruginous bodies |
| Berylliosis | Aerospace, electronics, nuclear | Nodules, hilar adenopathy | Noncaseating granulomas resembling sarcoidosis; beryllium lymphocyte proliferation test |

### Hypersensitivity pneumonitis
Immune (type III and IV) reaction to inhaled organic antigens: moldy hay (farmer's lung, thermophilic actinomycetes), bird proteins (bird fancier's lung), humidifiers and hot tubs. Acute form: fever, cough and dyspnea hours after exposure; chronic form: insidious fibrosis. HRCT: ground-glass, poorly defined centrilobular nodules and air trapping. Treat by **antigen avoidance**, with corticosteroids for severe disease.

### Drug and radiation injury, and organizing pneumonia
Drugs include **bleomycin** (oxygen toxicity worsens the injury), **amiodarone**, **methotrexate**, **nitrofurantoin** (chronic use) and busulfan. **Cryptogenic organizing pneumonia** produces fever, cough and patchy, peripheral or migratory consolidations that mimic CAP and do not respond to antibiotics; biopsy shows granulation tissue plugs in alveoli (**Masson bodies**) and it responds to corticosteroids. Always reconsider the diagnosis of "pneumonia" that has not improved after appropriate empiric antibiotics.

@example
title: Recognizing IPF
problem: A 68-year-old former smoker has had 2 years of progressive exertional dyspnea and a dry cough. He has clubbing and bibasilar fine crackles. FVC is 58 percent of predicted, FEV1/FVC is 0.86 and DLCO is 38 percent. HRCT shows basal, subpleural reticulation with honeycombing and no ground-glass nodules. What is the diagnosis and the best disease-modifying treatment?
step: The restrictive pattern with low DLCO, crackles and clubbing indicates intrinsic fibrotic ILD.
step: Basal subpleural honeycombing is the UIP pattern; in an older smoker without exposures or systemic disease this is **IPF**, and biopsy is not required.
step: Treat with an antifibrotic (nintedanib or pirfenidone), which slows FVC decline. Do not give corticosteroids, and evaluate for oxygen needs, rehabilitation and transplant referral.

@example
title: Löfgren syndrome
problem: A 27-year-old African American woman has tender red shin nodules, ankle pain and low-grade fever. A chest radiograph shows bilateral hilar enlargement and the lungs are clear. What is the best management?
step: Erythema nodosum, hilar adenopathy and arthralgia define Löfgren syndrome, an acute presentation of sarcoidosis.
step: The prognosis is excellent and most cases resolve spontaneously within months.
step: Manage symptoms with NSAIDs and observe; corticosteroids are reserved for severe or progressive disease. Exclude TB and check calcium.

@mistakes
- Starting prednisone for IPF; it does not help and it harms.
- Treating a pneumonia that fails antibiotics with a broader antibiotic instead of reconsidering the diagnosis (for example, organizing pneumonia or malignancy).
- Assuming a low DLCO and restrictive pattern come from the chest wall; DLCO is normal in extrinsic restriction.
- Calling asbestos exposure a mesothelioma risk but forgetting that lung cancer is more common.
