@article
**COPD** is a common, preventable and treatable disease characterized by persistent respiratory symptoms and airflow limitation that is not fully reversible, caused by airway and alveolar abnormalities after significant exposure to noxious particles, most often **cigarette smoke**. Other causes include biomass fuel smoke, occupational dusts and **alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency**. Pack-years equals packs per day times years smoked.

### Pathology
Two overlapping processes:
- **Chronic bronchitis** (a clinical definition): productive cough on most days for at least 3 months in each of 2 consecutive years. Mucus gland hypertrophy and goblet-cell hyperplasia; the **Reid index** (gland layer thickness to wall thickness) exceeds 50 percent. Small-airway inflammation and fibrosis drive obstruction.
- **Emphysema** (a pathologic definition): permanent enlargement of airspaces distal to the terminal bronchiole with destruction of walls. Smoking-related damage is **centriacinar** (upper lobes); **alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency** (PiZZ genotype) causes **panacinar** emphysema in the **lower lobes** at a young age, and also liver disease: the misfolded Z protein accumulates in hepatocytes as PAS-positive, diastase-resistant globules, causing neonatal cholestasis and cirrhosis. The mechanism is unopposed neutrophil elastase from smoke-recruited neutrophils and macrophages destroying elastin. Suspect it with emphysema before age 45 to 50, in non-smokers, with basilar predominance or with unexplained liver disease; treat with smoking avoidance and IV augmentation therapy in selected patients.

### Presentation and findings
Chronic cough, sputum, exertional dyspnea and exacerbations. Examination: prolonged expiration, wheeze, hyperresonance, distant heart sounds, pursed-lip breathing, accessory muscle use, barrel chest. Radiograph: hyperinflation with flattened diaphragms, increased retrosternal air space and vascular pruning. Spirometry confirms post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC below 0.70 (GOLD). TLC and RV are increased; **DLCO is reduced in emphysema** but may be normal in chronic bronchitis. GOLD grades severity by FEV1 (grade 1 at 80 percent or above, 2 at 50 to 79, 3 at 30 to 49, 4 below 30).

### Stable-disease management
The GOLD 2023-2025 approach groups patients by symptoms (mMRC dyspnea scale or CAT score) and exacerbation history in the past year:
- **Group A** (mMRC 0 to 1, CAT below 10, fewer than 2 moderate exacerbations and none leading to hospitalization): one bronchodilator.
- **Group B** (mMRC 2 or more, CAT 10 or more, same exacerbation limits): **LABA plus LAMA**.
- **Group E** (2 or more moderate exacerbations, or 1 or more leading to hospitalization): **LABA plus LAMA**; consider **adding ICS (triple therapy)** if blood eosinophils are 300 per microliter or more (or 100 or more with persistent exacerbations on LABA+LAMA).

ICS is not used as monotherapy in COPD, and LAMA/LABA combinations are preferred over LABA/ICS. Add-ons for persistent exacerbations include roflumilast (PDE4 inhibitor, for chronic bronchitis with FEV1 below 50 percent), and azithromycin in former smokers. All patients need **smoking cessation** (counseling plus pharmacotherapy), vaccination (influenza, pneumococcal, RSV when eligible, COVID-19, Tdap) and **pulmonary rehabilitation**. Interventions that improve survival: **smoking cessation, long-term oxygen therapy** (PaO$_2$ 55 mmHg or less or SaO$_2$ 88 percent or less at rest; or PaO$_2$ 56 to 59 with cor pulmonale, edema or hematocrit above 55 percent; reassessed in the stable state, 30 to 90 days after an exacerbation) for at least 15 hours a day, and for selected patients **lung volume reduction surgery** (upper-lobe-predominant emphysema with low exercise capacity) and long-term home noninvasive ventilation for persistent hypercapnia.

### Exacerbation
Increased dyspnea, sputum volume and sputum purulence, often from viruses or bacteria (Haemophilus influenzae, Streptococcus pneumoniae, Moraxella catarrhalis). Management: short-acting bronchodilators, **systemic corticosteroids** (prednisone 40 mg daily for 5 days, typical adult dose for study), **antibiotics** when sputum purulence is increased with either more dyspnea or more sputum volume, or when ventilated, and controlled oxygen with a **target SpO$_2$ of 88 to 92 percent**, since excess oxygen worsens hypercapnia through V/Q changes and the Haldane effect. **Noninvasive ventilation (BiPAP)** reduces intubation and mortality in acute hypercapnic respiratory acidosis (pH of 7.35 or less with raised PaCO$_2$); intubate if it fails or there is shock, arrest or inability to protect the airway. Chronic CO$_2$ retainers have a compensatory metabolic alkalosis (raised bicarbonate), reducing the pH change.

@example
title: Choosing initial COPD therapy
problem: A 66-year-old former smoker has FEV1 55 percent of predicted after bronchodilator, FEV1/FVC 0.52, mMRC dyspnea grade 3, a CAT score of 18 and no exacerbations in the last year. Choose initial therapy.
step: Spirometry confirms COPD (ratio below 0.70) with GOLD grade 2 severity (55 percent).
step: Exacerbation history is 0 (fewer than 2 moderate and none hospitalized), so he is not in group E.
step: Symptoms are high (mMRC 2 or more, CAT 10 or more), so he is in **group B**: start LABA plus LAMA.
step: Add smoking cessation support, vaccines and pulmonary rehabilitation.

@example
title: Does this patient qualify for home oxygen?
problem: A stable patient with COPD, 6 weeks after discharge, has resting PaO$_2$ 53 mmHg on room air and no cor pulmonale. Should long-term oxygen be prescribed?
step: Criteria: PaO$_2$ of 55 mmHg or less (or SaO$_2$ of 88 percent or less) at rest, measured in the stable state.
step: Here PaO$_2$ is 53 mmHg, which meets the threshold, and the measurement is more than 30 days after the exacerbation.
step: Yes: prescribe oxygen for at least 15 hours daily. It improves survival.

@mistakes
- Prescribing ICS alone, or starting with ICS before bronchodilators, in COPD.
- Giving high-flow oxygen to a CO$_2$ retainer; aim for SpO$_2$ 88 to 92 percent.
- Diagnosing COPD from symptoms alone without post-bronchodilator spirometry.
- Missing alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency in a young non-smoker with basilar emphysema.
