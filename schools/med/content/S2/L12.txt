@article
### Types of respiratory failure
- **Type 1 (hypoxemic):** PaO$_2$ below 60 mmHg on room air with normal or low PaCO$_2$. Mechanisms: V/Q mismatch and shunt. Causes: pneumonia, pulmonary edema, ARDS, PE, interstitial disease.
- **Type 2 (hypercapnic, ventilatory):** PaCO$_2$ above 50 mmHg with acidosis (pH below 7.35 if acute). Mechanism: inadequate alveolar ventilation, from increased load (COPD, asthma, obesity), reduced drive (opioids, sedatives, brainstem lesions) or weakness (Guillain-Barre syndrome, myasthenia gravis, ALS, spinal injury). Hypoxemia with a normal A-a gradient accompanies it.

Presentation: dyspnea, tachypnea, accessory-muscle use, paradoxical abdominal motion, confusion, cyanosis; hypercapnia causes headache, flapping tremor, somnolence and **CO$_2$ narcosis**. Treat the cause, give oxygen, and escalate: high-flow nasal oxygen, noninvasive ventilation, then intubation.

### ARDS
Acute diffuse inflammatory lung injury (pneumonia, sepsis, aspiration, pancreatitis, trauma, transfusion) damages the alveolar-capillary barrier, causing protein-rich edema, hyaline membranes, loss of surfactant and a low-compliance lung with shunt. **Berlin definition:** onset within 1 week of a known insult; bilateral opacities on imaging not fully explained by effusions, collapse or nodules; respiratory failure not fully explained by cardiac failure or fluid overload (pulmonary capillary wedge pressure is not required); and a **PaO$_2$/FiO$_2$ (P/F) ratio of 300 or below on PEEP or CPAP of at least 5 cmH$_2$O**: mild 200 to 300, moderate 100 to 200, severe 100 or below. Management:
- **Lung-protective ventilation:** tidal volume of **6 mL/kg predicted body weight** (range 4 to 8), **plateau pressure of 30 cmH$_2$O or less**, adequate PEEP, with permissive hypercapnia accepted.
- **Predicted body weight (PBW):** men $50 + 0.91\,(\text{height in cm} - 152.4)$ kg; women $45.5 + 0.91\,(\text{height in cm} - 152.4)$ kg. Use height, not actual weight.
- **Prone positioning** for at least 12 to 16 hours daily in moderate to severe ARDS (P/F below 150) lowers mortality; neuromuscular blockade for severe disease with ventilator dyssynchrony (routine early use did not improve survival in the ROSE trial); conservative fluid strategy after resuscitation; **ECMO** at specialized centers. Corticosteroids may be considered; inhaled nitric oxide improves oxygenation but not mortality.

### Ventilator basics
Settings: mode (assist-control volume or pressure; pressure support for spontaneous breathing; SIMV is less common), tidal volume, rate, FiO$_2$, PEEP, inspiratory flow. **Peak pressure** reflects airway resistance plus compliance; the **plateau pressure** (end-inspiratory pause) reflects compliance alone.
- **High peak, normal plateau (large gap):** resistance problem, such as bronchospasm, mucus plug, kinked or biting tube.
- **High peak and high plateau (small gap):** compliance problem: pneumothorax, mainstem intubation, pulmonary edema, ARDS, abdominal distention.
- Sudden hypoxemia and hypotension: use DOPE (displacement, obstruction, pneumothorax, equipment failure).
- **Auto-PEEP** (air trapping) occurs with obstruction and high rates; lower the respiratory rate and lengthen expiratory time, treat bronchospasm.

Complications: barotrauma and volutrauma, ventilator-associated pneumonia, oxygen toxicity (limit FiO$_2$), delirium. Daily sedation interruption and **spontaneous breathing trials** support weaning. The **rapid shallow breathing index** is $RR/V_T$ (with $V_T$ in liters); a value below 105 predicts successful extubation. **Noninvasive ventilation** (BiPAP) is indicated for COPD exacerbation with acute respiratory acidosis and cardiogenic pulmonary edema; avoid it with altered mental status, vomiting, inability to protect the airway or hemodynamic instability.

### Respiratory drugs
| Class (examples) | Mechanism | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| SABA (albuterol) | beta-2 agonist | Tremor, tachycardia, hypokalemia |
| LABA (salmeterol, formoterol) | beta-2 agonist | Never alone in asthma; formoterol acts rapidly |
| SAMA/LAMA (ipratropium, tiotropium) | Muscarinic antagonist | Dry mouth, urinary retention, glaucoma caution |
| Inhaled corticosteroids (budesonide, fluticasone) | Reduce inflammation | Oral candidiasis, dysphonia; rinse mouth |
| Montelukast; zafirlukast | CysLT1 receptor antagonist | Neuropsychiatric boxed warning (montelukast) |
| Zileuton | 5-lipoxygenase inhibitor | Hepatotoxicity |
| Theophylline | Phosphodiesterase inhibition, adenosine antagonism | Narrow therapeutic window; nausea, tachyarrhythmia, seizures; CYP1A2 interactions (ciprofloxacin, smoking changes clearance) |
| Roflumilast | PDE-4 inhibitor | COPD with chronic bronchitis; weight loss, diarrhea |
| Omalizumab, mepolizumab, dupilumab | Anti-IgE, anti-IL-5, anti-IL-4R alpha | Severe asthma |
| Cromolyn | Mast cell stabilizer | Prophylaxis only |
| Guaifenesin; N-acetylcysteine | Mucolytic or expectorant | Limited evidence |
Also: **alveolar ventilation** and PaCO$_2$ are inversely related, so halving alveolar ventilation doubles PaCO$_2$; oxygen can suppress hypercapnic drive only partially, and the main cause of CO$_2$ rise in COPD with oxygen is worsening V/Q and the Haldane effect.

@example
title: Lung-protective tidal volume
problem: A 175 cm man with ARDS is intubated. What tidal volume corresponds to 6 mL/kg predicted body weight?
step: Predicted body weight for men: $50 + 0.91(175 - 152.4) = 50 + 0.91(22.6)$.
step: $0.91 \times 22.6 = 20.57$, so PBW $= 70.6$ kg.
step: Tidal volume $= 6 \times 70.6 \approx 424$ mL. Keep the plateau pressure at 30 cmH$_2$O or below, and adjust PEEP and rate.

@example
title: Grading ARDS by P/F ratio
problem: A patient with bilateral infiltrates after aspiration is ventilated with PEEP 10 cmH$_2$O and FiO$_2$ 0.6. Arterial PaO$_2$ is 72 mmHg. Classify the severity.
step: P/F ratio $= 72/0.6 = 120$.
step: PEEP is 5 cmH$_2$O or more, so the Berlin criteria apply.
step: A P/F of 100 to 200 is **moderate ARDS**. Prone positioning is indicated for P/F below 150.

@mistakes
- Using actual body weight rather than height-based predicted body weight to set tidal volume.
- Calling a high peak pressure with a normal plateau "stiff lungs"; it indicates airway resistance.
- Using oxygen alone for hypercapnic failure; ventilation is the problem.
- Ordering a cardiac wedge pressure to diagnose ARDS; the Berlin definition does not require it.
