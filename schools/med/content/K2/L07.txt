@article
**Division** means splitting a number into equal parts. There are two ways to think about it.

### Sharing equally

You have 15 grapes and 3 plates. Put the same number of grapes on each plate. Deal them out one at a time: one on each plate, again and again. At the end each plate has 5 grapes.

We write $15 \div 3 = 5$. The sign $\div$ is read "divided by." The answer is called the **quotient**.

### Making equal groups

You have 20 pencils and put 4 pencils in each box. How many boxes do you need? Keep making groups of 4 until none are left: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20. That is 5 groups. So $20 \div 4 = 5$.

### Division and multiplication

Division undoes multiplication. They form a **fact family**:

- $3 \times 5 = 15$
- $5 \times 3 = 15$
- $15 \div 3 = 5$
- $15 \div 5 = 3$

To solve $15 \div 3$, ask: "3 times what makes 15?" You know that $3 \times 5 = 15$, so the answer is 5.

### Equal means equal

Sometimes things cannot be shared equally with none left. 10 apples cannot be shared among 4 children so that each gets the same whole number of apples. 2 apples are left over. In this lesson we use numbers that share equally.

### Check your answer

Multiply the answer by the number you divided by. If $15 \div 3 = 5$, check that $5 \times 3 = 15$.

@example
title: Sharing equally
problem: 15 grapes are shared equally on 3 plates. How many grapes are on each plate?
step: Deal the grapes out one at a time to the 3 plates until none are left.
step: Each plate gets 5. We write $15 \div 3 = 5$.
step: Check: $5 \times 3 = 15$.

@example
title: Making equal groups
problem: 20 pencils are put in boxes of 4. How many boxes are used?
step: Count by 4s until you reach 20: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20.
step: You counted 5 numbers, so there are 5 boxes. We write $20 \div 4 = 5$.

@mistakes
- Thinking that $15 \div 3$ is the same as $3 \div 15$. The order matters.
- Making groups that are not equal.
- Forgetting that division undoes multiplication, so a multiplication fact can help.

@practice num

check: 12 / 3
hint: Deal the cookies out one at a time to 3 friends.
hint: Think: 3 times what makes 12?
solution: $3 \times 4 = 12$, so $12 \div 3 = 4$. Each friend gets 4 cookies.
