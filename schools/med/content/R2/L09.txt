### Breast mass and breast cancer

**Screening** guidelines differ: the USPSTF recommends mammography every 2 years from 40 to 74; the American Cancer Society offers annual screening beginning at 45 (with the option from 40) and moves to every other year at 55. High-risk women (for example, BRCA carriers) add MRI.

**A palpable mass** is evaluated with imaging and, if suspicious, tissue. In women **under 30 years**, start with **ultrasound** (dense breasts, low cancer incidence); in women **30 and older**, obtain **diagnostic mammography plus ultrasound**. A solid suspicious mass needs a **core needle biopsy**, which gives histology and receptors; fine needle aspiration is used for cysts. **BI-RADS** categories: 0 incomplete, 1 negative, 2 benign, 3 probably benign (under 2 percent risk; short-interval follow-up), 4 suspicious (biopsy), 5 highly suggestive of malignancy (95 percent or more; biopsy), 6 known biopsy-proven cancer.

Benign causes: **fibroadenoma** (young women, mobile, rubbery), **phyllodes tumor** (rapidly growing, leaf-like), fibrocystic change, cysts and intraductal papilloma (bloody nipple discharge).

**Cancer types.** **DCIS** is noninvasive. **Invasive ductal carcinoma** is the most common. **Invasive lobular carcinoma** is often bilateral and multifocal, with loss of E-cadherin. **Inflammatory breast cancer** presents with erythema and peau d'orange rather than a mass. **Paget disease** is an eczematous nipple lesion with underlying carcinoma. Test every invasive cancer for **ER, PR and HER2**.

**Treatment.** Early-stage cancer can be treated with **breast-conserving surgery plus radiation** or **mastectomy**, with equivalent survival. Contraindications to breast conservation include multicentric disease, prior chest-wall radiation, persistently positive margins and early pregnancy. **Sentinel lymph node biopsy** stages the axilla and avoids full dissection when negative. Adjuvant therapy: **endocrine therapy** for hormone receptor-positive disease (tamoxifen, which carries risks of endometrial cancer and thromboembolism, or aromatase inhibitors in postmenopausal women, which cause bone loss), **trastuzumab** for HER2-positive disease, and chemotherapy by stage and subtype.

### Thyroid nodules

Most nodules are benign. **Step one is TSH.** If TSH is low, the nodule may be hyperfunctioning; a radioiodine scan will show it as "hot," and these are rarely malignant. If TSH is normal or high, obtain a **thyroid ultrasound** and perform **fine needle aspiration (FNA)** for nodules with suspicious features or above a size threshold. FNA is reported by **Bethesda categories**: I nondiagnostic, II benign, III atypia of undetermined significance, IV follicular neoplasm, V suspicious for malignancy, VI malignant.

**Thyroid cancers:**

- **Papillary** (most common): childhood radiation exposure, BRAF or RET/PTC changes, "Orphan Annie eye" nuclei, psammoma bodies, spread through lymphatics to cervical nodes; excellent prognosis.
- **Follicular:** spreads by blood to lung and bone. FNA cannot distinguish adenoma from carcinoma because the diagnosis requires **capsular or vascular invasion**, so a follicular neoplasm is diagnosed by lobectomy.
- **Medullary:** arises from parafollicular C cells, secretes **calcitonin**, has amyloid in the stroma, and is associated with **RET mutations and MEN 2**. Before surgery, **exclude pheochromocytoma**.
- **Anaplastic:** elderly, rapidly enlarging mass, dismal prognosis.

Treatment of differentiated cancer is lobectomy or total thyroidectomy, with radioactive iodine for selected higher-risk patients and levothyroxine for TSH suppression or replacement. Complications of thyroidectomy: **hypocalcemia** from parathyroid injury or removal (perioral numbness, Chvostek and Trousseau signs), **recurrent laryngeal nerve injury** (hoarseness; bilateral injury causes stridor), and neck hematoma, which can compromise the airway and requires immediate evacuation.

### Principles of surgical oncology

Obtain a **tissue diagnosis before definitive resection** (core or incisional biopsy placed so the tract can be excised). The goal is an **R0 resection**, with microscopically negative margins. Stage by TNM. Sentinel node biopsy stages the regional nodes for breast cancer and melanoma. **Colon cancer:** stage III (node-positive) disease receives adjuvant chemotherapy, and CEA is used for surveillance. **Melanoma:** wide excision margins depend on **Breslow depth**: 1 cm for 1 mm or less, 1 to 2 cm for 1 to 2 mm, and 2 cm for over 2 mm. Plan treatment with a multidisciplinary team.

@example
title: A breast mass in a young woman
problem: A 24-year-old woman finds a 2-cm mobile, rubbery, nontender breast mass. There is no family history. What is the initial evaluation?
step: Under age 30, the first imaging test is ultrasound. Mammography is less sensitive in dense breasts and adds radiation.
step: Ultrasound shows a well-circumscribed, oval, solid mass parallel to the skin, which suggests fibroadenoma (BI-RADS 3 or 4 depending on features).
step: Obtain a core needle biopsy if it is suspicious, growing or the patient prefers a definitive diagnosis.
step: A biopsy-proven fibroadenoma can be observed; excision is for growth, symptoms or leaf-like phyllodes features.

@mistakes
- Obtaining mammography first in a woman under 30 with a palpable mass.
- Ordering a radioiodine scan before checking the TSH in a thyroid nodule.
- Assuming FNA can distinguish follicular adenoma from follicular carcinoma.
- Operating on a medullary thyroid carcinoma without checking for pheochromocytoma.
