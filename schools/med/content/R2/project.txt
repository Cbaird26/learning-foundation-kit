### Week by week

**Week 1, preoperative assessment.** ASA class describes comorbidity; the RCRI counts six factors (high-risk surgery, ischemic heart disease, heart failure, cerebrovascular disease, insulin-treated diabetes, creatinine above 2.0). Four METs is the functional threshold. Continue beta-blockers, statins and aspirin (especially with stents); hold SGLT2 inhibitors about 3 days and warfarin about 5 days. Fast 2, 6 and 8 hours for clear liquids, light meal and fatty meal.

**Week 2, fluids and nutrition.** Maintenance by 4-2-1; saline causes hyperchloremic acidosis; match GI losses; free water deficit is TBW times (Na/140 minus 1); enteral over parenteral; watch for refeeding hypophosphatemia.

**Week 3, acute abdomen.** Visceral then somatic pain; peritoneal signs mean surgery; location differential; pain out of proportion means mesenteric ischemia; always check hCG.

**Week 4, appendicitis and obstruction.** Alvarado score; ultrasound then MRI in pregnancy; adhesions cause most SBO; strangulation signs mandate surgery; sigmoid volvulus is decompressed endoscopically then resected.

**Week 5, hepatobiliary and pancreas.** Cholecystitis: ultrasound, early cholecystectomy. Choledocholithiasis: ASGE risk groups lead to ERCP or MRCP. Cholangitis needs drainage. Pancreatitis: two of three criteria, fluids, early feeding, no prophylactic antibiotics, same-admission cholecystectomy.

**Week 6, hernias.** Indirect is lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels; femoral is the strangulation risk; watchful waiting is reasonable in minimally symptomatic men.

**Week 7, trauma.** Catastrophic hemorrhage then ABCDE; tension pneumothorax is clinical; shock classes; blood products; TXA within 3 hours; GCS.

**Week 8, burns and wound healing.** Rule of nines, Parkland (4 mL, half in the first 8 hours), inhalation injury and carbon monoxide, phases of healing.

**Week 9, breast, thyroid, oncology.** Ultrasound under 30; BI-RADS; TSH first for a nodule; medullary needs pheochromocytoma screening; melanoma margins by Breslow depth.

**Week 10, vascular.** The six Ps, heparin first, reperfusion injury; ABI; AAA at 5.5 cm; type A surgery, type B medicine with beta blocker first.

**Week 11, postoperative complications.** Five Ws; open the infected wound; Wells; dehiscence; FENa.

**Week 12, anesthesia.** Malignant hyperthermia and dantrolene 2.5 mg/kg; succinylcholine hazards; local anesthetic maximum doses; ERAS.

### Common errors

Obtaining imaging before resuscitating an unstable patient; using CA 19-9 for screening; confusing direct and indirect hernias; counting first-degree burns in TBSA; trusting pulse oximetry in carbon monoxide poisoning; stopping aspirin early after a stent; treating cholangitis without drainage.

Using the Parkland formula (4 mL/kg per percent TBSA over 24 hours, half in the first 8 hours from injury), what is the initial hourly rate for the first 8 hours?
answer: 675
check: 4 * 75 * (18 + 18) / 2 / 8
unit: mL/h
hint: Add the two regions to get total body surface area burned.
hint: Compute the 24-hour volume, then take half and divide by 8 hours.
solution: TBSA is 36 percent. Total is $4 \times 75 \times 36 = 10800$ mL; half is 5400 mL over 8 hours, which is 675 mL/h. Titrate to urine output of about 0.5 mL/kg/h.

@exam self
prompt: A 55-year-old man with a history of regular ibuprofen use has 3 hours of sudden severe epigastric pain that is now diffuse. Temperature 38.1 C, heart rate 116/min, blood pressure 104/60 mmHg, respiratory rate 24/min. His abdomen is rigid with rebound. Upright chest radiograph shows free air under the diaphragm. He weighs 80 kg, takes metoprolol for hypertension and has no other history. Write his surgical case plan: differential, first resuscitation steps, workup, indication and type of surgery, and postoperative orders including the complications to watch for by postoperative day.
hint: Start with resuscitation and source control; imaging should not delay an operation for generalized peritonitis.
hint: Organize the postoperative orders by system, then list complications by postoperative day.
solution: Differential: perforated peptic ulcer (most likely), perforated diverticulum or other viscus, acute pancreatitis (less likely with a rigid abdomen and free air), mesenteric ischemia. He meets SIRS (fever, tachycardia, tachypnea). Resuscitate with IV access, lactated Ringer's, analgesia, antiemetic, NPO, broad-spectrum antibiotics (covering gram-negatives and anaerobes), type and screen, and monitor urine output at 0.5 mL/kg/h (40 mL/h). Workup: CBC, BMP, lactate, lipase, ECG and troponin, and CT if stable and the diagnosis is uncertain; do not delay the operating room. Indication: generalized peritonitis with free perforation requires emergency laparoscopic or open repair (omental patch) with irrigation; continue his beta-blocker. Postoperative orders: NPO with nasogastric decompression until ileus resolves, maintenance fluids at 4-2-1 (120 mL/h for 80 kg: 40 + 20 + 60, adjusted for losses), IV proton pump inhibitor, test and treat for Helicobacter pylori, avoid NSAIDs, multimodal analgesia, VTE prophylaxis, incentive spirometry, antibiotics for a short course. Watch for atelectasis and aspiration on days 1 to 2, urinary infection on days 3 to 5, and wound infection, abscess, leak from the repair, and DVT or PE on days 5 to 7 and later.
rubric: Identifies perforated peptic ulcer as the leading diagnosis and states the indication for emergency repair.
rubric: Gives resuscitation steps in a sensible order including antibiotics and a urine output target.
rubric: Orders proportionate workup without delaying operation and addresses NSAIDs and H. pylori.
rubric: Lists postoperative orders and complications by postoperative day.
