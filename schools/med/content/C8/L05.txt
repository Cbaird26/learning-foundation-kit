@article
Constants used here: elementary charge $e = 1.602\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}$, proton mass $m_p = 1.673\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}$, electron mass $m_e = 9.11\times10^{-31}\ \mathrm{kg}$, permeability of free space $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{T\,m/A}$. The magnetic field $B$ is measured in teslas ($1\ \mathrm{T} = 1\ \mathrm{N/(A\,m)}$). For scale, Earth's field is about $5\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{T}$ and a clinical MRI magnet is $1.5\ \mathrm{T}$ or $3\ \mathrm{T}$.

### Magnetic force on a moving charge

Magnetic fields exert a force only on **moving** charges. For a charge $q$ moving with velocity $\vec v$ in a field $\vec B$,

$$\vec F = q\,\vec v\times\vec B, \qquad F = |q|\,v\,B\sin\theta,$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between $\vec v$ and $\vec B$. **Right-hand rule (positive charge):** point the fingers along $\vec v$, curl them toward $\vec B$, and the thumb points along $\vec F$. For a negative charge the force is reversed. The force is zero if the velocity is parallel or antiparallel to $\vec B$ and is largest when they are perpendicular.

Because the force is always perpendicular to $\vec v$, it does **no work**: it changes the direction of the velocity but not the speed or the kinetic energy.

### Circular motion

If a charge enters a uniform field perpendicular to $\vec B$, the magnetic force supplies the centripetal force: $qvB = mv^2/r$. Therefore

$$r = \frac{mv}{|q|B}, \qquad f = \frac{|q|B}{2\pi m}.$$

The frequency $f$ (the **cyclotron frequency**) does not depend on the speed. Faster particles move on larger circles but take the same time per revolution. This principle underlies mass spectrometers, which separate ions by mass, and cyclotrons, which produce radioisotopes for nuclear medicine.

### Force on a current

A wire of length $L$ carrying current $I$ in a field $B$ experiences $\vec F = I\vec L\times\vec B$, with magnitude $F = ILB\sin\theta$, where $\vec L$ points along the conventional current. The same right-hand rule applies, with the fingers along the current direction.

### Fields made by currents

Moving charges make magnetic fields. A long straight wire carrying current $I$ produces a field of magnitude

$$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$$

at distance $r$. The field lines are circles around the wire; curl the fingers of your right hand and the thumb points along the current. At the center of a circular loop of radius $R$ with $N$ turns, $B = N\mu_0 I/(2R)$. A **solenoid** (a long coil) produces a nearly uniform interior field $B = \mu_0 n I$, where $n$ is the number of turns per meter. Superconducting solenoids are how MRI scanners create their strong, uniform main field.

### Magnetic materials

Ferromagnetic materials such as iron are strongly magnetized by an external field and are attracted to magnets. This is a safety issue around MRI machines: iron or steel objects can become dangerous projectiles. Most body tissue is only very weakly magnetic.

@example
title: An electron in a magnetic field
problem: An electron moves at $3.0\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{m/s}$ perpendicular to a uniform field of $1.00\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{T}$. Find the radius of its circular path. Use $m_e = 9.11\times10^{-31}\ \mathrm{kg}$ and $e = 1.602\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}$.
step: The magnetic force provides the centripetal force: $evB = m_ev^2/r$, so $r = m_ev/(eB)$.
step: Substitute: $r = \dfrac{(9.11\times10^{-31})(3.0\times10^{7})}{(1.602\times10^{-19})(1.00\times10^{-2})}$.
step: Numerator $= 2.733\times10^{-23}$, denominator $= 1.602\times10^{-21}$, so $r \approx 1.7\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{m} = 1.7\ \mathrm{cm}$.

@mistakes
- Using the right-hand rule for a negative charge without reversing the direction of the force.
- Thinking that a magnetic field can speed a charge up; it only changes its direction.
- Using $\sin\theta$ with the wrong angle: $\theta$ is the angle between $\vec v$ (or the current) and $\vec B$, not between the velocity and the force.
- Forgetting that a charge moving parallel to the field feels no magnetic force.

@practice num
prompt: An electron ($e = 1.602\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}$) moves at $2.0\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{m/s}$ perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field of $0.50\ \mathrm{T}$. Find the magnitude of the magnetic force on it, in newtons.
answer: 1.602e-13
tol: 1%
unit: N
check: 1.602e-19 * 2.0e6 * 0.50
hint: For $\theta = 90^\circ$, $F = |q|vB$.
solution: $F = (1.602\times10^{-19})(2.0\times10^{6})(0.50) = 1.6\times10^{-13}\ \mathrm{N}$.

@practice num
prompt: A proton ($m_p = 1.673\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}$, $e = 1.602\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}$) moves at $1.0\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{m/s}$ perpendicular to a uniform field of $0.20\ \mathrm{T}$. Find the radius of its circular path, in meters.
answer: 0.05222
tol: 1%
unit: m
check: 1.673e-27 * 1.0e6 / (1.602e-19 * 0.20)
hint: Set the magnetic force equal to the centripetal force.
hint: $r = mv/(|q|B)$.
solution: $r = (1.673\times10^{-27})(1.0\times10^{6})/[(1.602\times10^{-19})(0.20)] \approx 0.052\ \mathrm{m}$.

@practice num
prompt: A straight wire of length $0.20\ \mathrm{m}$ carries a current of $5.0\ \mathrm{A}$ in a uniform field of $0.40\ \mathrm{T}$. The wire makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the field. Find the magnitude of the force on the wire, in newtons.
answer: 0.2
tol: 1%
unit: N
check: 5.0 * 0.20 * 0.40 * math.sin(math.radians(30))
hint: Use $F = ILB\sin\theta$.
solution: $F = (5.0)(0.20)(0.40)\sin30^\circ = 0.40 \times 0.5 = 0.20\ \mathrm{N}$.

@practice mc
prompt: A proton moves through a region containing only a uniform magnetic field. Which statement is correct?
choice: The magnetic force increases the proton's speed.
choice*: The magnetic force changes the direction of the proton's velocity but does no work on it.
choice: The magnetic force is largest when the velocity is parallel to the field.
choice: The magnetic force acts only if the proton is at rest.
hint: Think about the angle between the force and the displacement.
solution: Since $\vec F\perp\vec v$, the force does no work; kinetic energy and speed stay constant while the direction changes. The force is zero for velocity parallel to $\vec B$, and only moving charges feel it.

@practice num
prompt: Use $\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{T\,m/A}$. Find the magnetic field magnitude, in teslas, at a distance of $5.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ from a long straight wire carrying $10\ \mathrm{A}$.
answer: 4e-5
tol: 1%
unit: T
check: 4 * math.pi * 1e-7 * 10 / (2 * math.pi * 0.050)
hint: Use $B = \mu_0I/(2\pi r)$ and convert $r$ to meters.
solution: $B = (4\pi\times10^{-7})(10)/(2\pi \times 0.050) = 4.0\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{T}$, comparable to Earth's field.

@quiz num
prompt: Use $e = 1.602\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}$ and $m_p = 1.673\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}$. Find the cyclotron frequency of a proton in a uniform magnetic field of $0.80\ \mathrm{T}$, in megahertz.
answer: 12.19
tol: 1%
unit: MHz
check: 1.602e-19 * 0.80 / (2 * math.pi * 1.673e-27) / 1e6
hint: Use $f = |q|B/(2\pi m)$.
hint: Convert hertz to megahertz by dividing by $10^6$.
solution: $f = (1.602\times10^{-19})(0.80)/(2\pi \times 1.673\times10^{-27}) = 1.219\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{Hz} \approx 12.2\ \mathrm{MHz}$.

@quiz mc
