@article
Constants used here: wavelengths are measured in vacuum or air unless stated; refractive indices: air $1.000$, water $1.33$. The condition $\sin\theta\approx\theta$ is used for small angles. Lens sign convention: as in the previous lessons, with diverging lenses having negative power.

### Interference

Light is a wave, so two waves that overlap add. Where crests meet crests (path difference a whole number of wavelengths) there is **constructive interference**, a bright region; where crests meet troughs (path difference a half-integer number of wavelengths) there is **destructive interference**.

In **Young's double-slit experiment**, two narrow slits a distance $d$ apart illuminate a screen a distance $L$ away. Bright fringes occur at angles satisfying

$$d\sin\theta = m\lambda, \qquad m = 0, \pm1, \pm2,\dots$$

For small angles the fringe spacing on the screen is $\Delta y = \lambda L/d$. Smaller slit separation gives wider fringes; longer wavelengths give wider fringes.

### Diffraction

Waves spread when they pass through an opening comparable in size to the wavelength. For a single slit of width $a$, the **dark** fringes satisfy $a\sin\theta = m\lambda$ with $m = \pm1, \pm2,\dots$ and the central bright maximum is broad. For a circular opening of diameter $D$, the first dark ring appears at

$$\theta = 1.22\,\frac{\lambda}{D}.$$

Two point sources can be just resolved when their angular separation equals this value (**Rayleigh criterion**). This sets a fundamental limit on the sharpness of images from the eye, from telescopes, and from microscopes, whose resolution can be no better than about half a wavelength of light.

### Thin films

Light reflected from the top and bottom of a thin film interferes. A reflection from a boundary with a higher-index medium flips the phase by $180^\circ$; one from a lower-index medium does not. For a soap film ($n = 1.33$) in air, the top reflection flips and the bottom does not, so reflected light is constructive when $2nt = (m + \tfrac12)\lambda$, with $m = 0,1,2,\dots$, and the thinnest bright film has $t = \lambda/(4n)$. Anti-reflection coatings on lenses use the same principle with the opposite outcome (destructive reflection).

### Polarization

Light polarized along one direction passing through a polarizing filter (analyzer) at angle $\theta$ to that direction has intensity given by **Malus's law**:

$$I = I_0\cos^2\theta.$$

Unpolarized light passing through an ideal polarizer loses half its intensity. Polarizing filters reduce glare, and some biological tissues rotate the plane of polarization (optical activity), which is used in measuring sugar concentrations.

### Back to correcting the eye

Wave optics reminds us that sharpness is limited by diffraction through the pupil, but most blur in everyday eyes comes from refractive error, which geometric optics can fix. For a myopic eye with far point $d_{far}$, the corrective lens power is $P = -1/d_{far}$ (negative diopters).

@example
title: Fringe spacing in a double-slit experiment
problem: Light of wavelength $500\ \mathrm{nm}$ passes through two slits $0.10\ \mathrm{mm}$ apart onto a screen $2.0\ \mathrm{m}$ away. Find the spacing of the bright fringes on the screen.
step: Use the small-angle result $\Delta y = \lambda L/d$.
step: Convert: $\lambda = 500\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{m}$, $d = 1.0\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m}$.
step: $\Delta y = (500\times10^{-9})(2.0)/(1.0\times10^{-4}) = 1.0\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{m} = 10\ \mathrm{mm}$.

@mistakes
- Mixing nanometers, millimeters and meters in $\Delta y = \lambda L/d$.
- Using the single-slit dark-fringe formula for bright fringes (or the reverse).
- Forgetting the phase flip on reflection from a higher-index medium in thin-film problems.
- Using the full angle between polarizer axes incorrectly in Malus's law; $\theta$ is the angle between the light's polarization and the analyzer axis, and the result involves $\cos^2\theta$.

@practice num
prompt: Light of wavelength $600\ \mathrm{nm}$ passes through two slits separated by $0.20\ \mathrm{mm}$, and the screen is $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ away. Find the spacing between adjacent bright fringes, in millimeters.
answer: 4.5
tol: 1%
unit: mm
check: 600e-9 * 1.5 / 0.20e-3 * 1000
hint: Use $\Delta y = \lambda L/d$ with all lengths in meters.
solution: $\Delta y = (600\times10^{-9})(1.5)/(2.0\times10^{-4}) = 4.5\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m} = 4.5\ \mathrm{mm}$.

@practice num
prompt: Light of wavelength $500\ \mathrm{nm}$ passes through a single slit of width $1.0\ \mu\mathrm{m}$. Find the angle of the first dark fringe, in degrees, from $a\sin\theta = \lambda$.

@practice num
prompt: Polarized light of intensity $80\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$ passes through an analyzer whose axis makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the light's polarization direction. Find the transmitted intensity in $\mathrm{W/m^2}$.
answer: 20
tol: 1%
unit: W/m²
check: 80 * math.cos(math.radians(60))**2
hint: Use Malus's law, $I = I_0\cos^2\theta$.
solution: $I = 80\cos^2 60^\circ = 80 \times 0.25 = 20\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$.

@practice num
prompt: A myopic patient has a far point of $40\ \mathrm{cm}$. Find the power of the corrective lens, in diopters, using $P = -1/d_{far}$ (ignore the vertex distance).
answer: -2.5
tol: 1%
unit: D
check: -1 / 0.40
hint: The lens must form a virtual image of a distant object at the far point, $d_i = -0.40\ \mathrm{m}$.
solution: With $d_o\to\infty$, $P = 1/d_i = 1/(-0.40) = -2.5\ \mathrm{D}$, a diverging lens.

@quiz num
prompt: A soap film ($n = 1.33$) in air is illuminated with light of vacuum wavelength $550\ \mathrm{nm}$. Find the smallest nonzero film thickness that gives constructive interference in reflected light, using $2nt = (m + \tfrac12)\lambda$ with $m = 0$, in nanometers.
answer: 103.4
tol: 1%
unit: nm
check: 550 / (4 * 1.33)
hint: With $m=0$, $2nt = \lambda/2$.
solution: $t = \lambda/(4n) = 550/(4\times1.33) = 103\ \mathrm{nm}$.
