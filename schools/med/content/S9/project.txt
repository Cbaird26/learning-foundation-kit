@review
### Week by week

- **Week 1, anatomy and supply:** cortex crosses, cerebellum does not. ACA leg, MCA face and arm and language, PCA vision, PICA lateral medulla. MAP $= (\text{SBP} + 2\,\text{DBP})/3$.
- **Week 2, physiology:** Nernst equation, hyperkalemia depolarizes, myelin speeds conduction, benzodiazepines open GABA-A more often.
- **Week 3, stroke:** last known well, glucose, non-contrast CT; lysis within 4.5 hours with BP below 185/110; thrombectomy up to 24 hours when selected; reverse anticoagulants in hemorrhage.
- **Week 4, seizures:** benzodiazepine first for status epilepticus, then second-line drug; ethosuximide for absence; avoid sodium-channel drugs in myoclonic epilepsy.
- **Week 5, headache:** SNOOP red flags; steroids first in giant cell arteritis; oxygen for cluster.
- **Week 6, head injury:** GCS, epidural versus subdural, CPP $= \text{MAP} - \text{ICP}$.
- **Week 7, movement:** Parkinson, Huntington, Wilson, NMS versus serotonin syndrome.
- **Week 8, weakness:** upper versus lower motor neuron signs; MS, GBS, myasthenia, Lambert-Eaton, ALS.
- **Week 9, infection and tumors:** CSF patterns; do not delay antibiotics; tumor by age and site.
- **Week 10, dementia and delirium:** acute fluctuating inattention versus insidious decline; sensitivity, specificity, PPV.
- **Week 11, eye:** field defects by site, angle-closure, artery occlusion, lens power.
- **Week 12, ear:** Rinne and Weber, peripheral versus central vertigo, decibels.

### Self-check

- Can you localize any deficit to side, level and artery?
- Can you state the time windows and contraindications for lysis?
- Can you interpret CSF and give empiric meningitis therapy?
- Can you work the calculations: MAP, CPP, Nernst, weight-based doses, sensitivity?

### Common errors

- Giving anything antithrombotic before a head CT.
- Delaying antibiotics for imaging.
- Forgetting that Weber lateralizes to the better ear in sensorineural loss.
- Mixing upper and lower motor neuron signs.

@exam num
prompt: An 80-kg patient with ischemic stroke is eligible for alteplase at 0.9 mg/kg (maximum 90 mg), with 10% as a bolus and the remainder as a 60-minute infusion. How many mg are infused over the 60 minutes?
answer: 64.8
check: 0.9 * 80 * 0.9
unit: mg
tol: 0.1
hint: Find the total dose, then remove the 10% bolus.
solution: Total $= 0.9 \times 80 = 72$ mg; the bolus is 7.2 mg, so the infusion is $72 - 7.2 = 64.8$ mg.

@exam num
prompt: A patient with severe head injury has a blood pressure of 105/60 mmHg and an ICP of 30 mmHg. By how many mmHg must the MAP rise to reach a cerebral perfusion pressure of 60 mmHg?
answer: 15
check: 60 - ((105 + 2*60) / 3 - 30)
unit: mmHg
tol: 0.5
hint: Calculate the current MAP, then the current CPP.
solution: MAP $= (105 + 120)/3 = 75$ mmHg; CPP $= 45$ mmHg; the MAP must rise by $60 - 45 = 15$ mmHg.

prompt: A 63-year-old man has 3 hours of left face and arm weakness and left neglect. CT is normal. He is on apixaban, last dose 6 hours ago. CTA shows a right M1 occlusion. Which action is most appropriate?
choice*: Proceed to mechanical thrombectomy
choice: Give intravenous alteplase immediately
choice: Give aspirin 325 mg and observe
choice: Start heparin infusion
choice: Obtain a lumbar puncture
hint: A recent direct oral anticoagulant is a contraindication to lysis.
solution: A large-vessel occlusion within the thrombectomy window warrants thrombectomy; recent apixaban use precludes thrombolysis.
