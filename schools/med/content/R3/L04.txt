@article
Nutrition is the largest modifiable determinant of growth in the first 2 years. The guiding principles are simple: milk first, then complementary foods; supplement what milk lacks; avoid foods that are unsafe; and watch growth.

### Milk feeding

**Exclusive breastfeeding for about 6 months**, then continued breastfeeding with solids, is the recommendation of the AAP and WHO. Breast milk supplies about 20 kcal/oz (0.67 kcal/mL). Standard infant formula is also about 20 kcal/oz. Typical needs are about **100–120 kcal/kg/day** in the first months, which is roughly 150–180 mL/kg/day of 20 kcal/oz milk, and the infant regulates intake by hunger and satiety cues. Expect 8–12 feeds a day in the first weeks, 6–8 wet diapers daily after the first week, and weight regain by 10–14 days.

Breastfeeding is contraindicated in infants with **galactosemia**. Guidance for maternal HIV, medications and some infections is changing or case specific and should be checked in current references.

### Supplements

- **Vitamin D, 400 IU (10 mcg) per day**, for breastfed and partially breastfed infants starting in the first days of life, and for formula-fed infants taking less than about 1 L of formula daily. Deficiency causes **rickets**: widened wrists, rachitic rosary, bowed legs, craniotabes.
- **Iron, about 1 mg/kg/day of elemental iron** from 4 months for exclusively or mostly breastfed infants until iron-rich complementary foods are eaten. Preterm infants need iron earlier (typically 2 mg/kg/day from 1 month). These are typical study doses and should be checked against current references.
- **Fluoride:** varnish from the first tooth, and fluoridated water after 6 months as appropriate.

### Complementary foods

Introduce solids at about **6 months** when the infant sits with support, has good head control, has lost the tongue-thrust reflex, and shows interest in food. Offer iron-rich foods (meat, iron-fortified cereal, legumes) and a variety of textures. **Introduce potentially allergenic foods early:** the LEAP trial showed that early peanut introduction (around 4–6 months) lowers peanut allergy in high-risk infants, and delaying does not prevent allergy. Infants with severe eczema or egg allergy should be evaluated before peanut introduction. Avoid:

- **Honey** before 12 months (infant botulism).
- **Cow's milk as a main drink** before 12 months (iron deficiency from occult gastrointestinal blood loss and low iron content).
- **Choking hazards:** whole nuts, whole grapes, hot dogs in rounds, popcorn, hard raw vegetables.
- **Juice** under 12 months; limited juice (about 4 oz/day) at 1–3 years.

### Toddlers

From 12 to 24 months offer whole milk (about 16–24 oz/day); at 2 years, reduced-fat milk. Excess milk (over about 24 oz/day) displaces iron-rich foods and causes **iron deficiency anemia** (microcytic, low ferritin). Screen hemoglobin at about 12 months. Toddlers are often picky; offer a variety without pressure.

### Malnutrition and obesity

**Marasmus** is total energy deficiency (wasting, no edema); **kwashiorkor** is protein-deficient diet with edema, hepatomegaly and skin changes. Obesity in children is a BMI at or above the 95th percentile; counsel the family on diet, activity, screen time and sleep, screen for comorbidities (hypertension, dyslipidemia, prediabetes, fatty liver, sleep apnea), and refer for intensive health behavior treatment.

### Calculations

For a formula volume, calculate the calories needed and divide by the formula's caloric density:
$$\text{volume (mL/day)} = \frac{\text{weight (kg)} \times \text{kcal/kg/day}}{0.67 \text{ kcal/mL}}.$$

@example
title: Formula volume for a young infant
problem: An infant weighs 4.4 kg and needs about 110 kcal/kg/day. Standard formula is 20 kcal/oz (0.67 kcal/mL). Estimate the daily volume.
step: Calories needed: $4.4 \times 110 = 484$ kcal/day.
step: Volume: $484 / 0.67 \approx 722$ mL/day.
step: Convert to practical terms: 722 mL is about 24 oz/day, or roughly 3 oz every 3 hours. Feeds are guided by hunger cues, and the estimate is a check on adequacy, not a prescription.

@example
title: Iron supplementation
problem: An exclusively breastfed 5.0 kg infant is 4 months old. Using a typical study dose of 1 mg/kg/day of elemental iron, how much elemental iron is given daily?
step: Multiply weight by dose: $5.0 \times 1 = 5.0$ mg/day of elemental iron.
step: Check the product's elemental iron concentration on the label before converting to a volume, since drops differ.
step: Continue until the infant eats iron-rich solids regularly.

@mistakes
- Starting cow's milk as the main drink before 12 months of age.
- Forgetting vitamin D for exclusively breastfed infants.
- Adding honey to feedings or foods before 12 months.
- Delaying peanut introduction in infants with no allergy risk, which does not prevent allergy.
