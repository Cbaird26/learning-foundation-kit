@review
### Week by week

1. **Newborn care.** Apgar (0–10; 1 and 5 min); vitamin K, erythromycin, hepatitis B vaccine; screening (blood spot, hearing, pulse oximetry for critical heart disease); weight loss up to 7–10% and regain by 10–14 days; jaundice in the first 24 hours is pathologic; persistent or conjugated jaundice needs evaluation; GBS prophylaxis.
2. **Growth and development.** Weight doubles by 4–5 months and triples by 1 year; length +50% by 1 year; head circumference about 35 cm to 47 cm; milestones at 2, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18 months and 2, 3, 4, 5 years; screening at 9, 18, 30 months and autism screening at 18 and 24 months; loss of skills is a red flag; primitive reflexes fade by 4–6 months.
3. **Well-child care and immunizations.** Visit schedule; schedule patterns for HepB, rotavirus, DTaP, Hib, PCV, IPV, influenza, MMR, varicella, HepA, Tdap, HPV, MenACWY; true contraindications are anaphylaxis and severe immunocompromise for live vaccines; mild illness and prematurity are not.
4. **Nutrition.** Breast milk for 6 months; vitamin D 400 IU; iron 1 mg/kg/day in breastfed infants from 4 months; solids at about 6 months; early allergen introduction; no honey or cow's milk as a main drink before 12 months; iron deficiency from excess milk.
5. **Fever and infections.** Febrile infants by age (AAP 2021, well-appearing 8–60 days: 8–21 days need blood, urine and CSF testing, admission and parenteral antibiotics; 22–28 and 29–60 days are risk-stratified with urinalysis, blood culture and inflammatory markers; 7 days or younger and ill-appearing infants are outside the guideline and get a full sepsis evaluation); otitis media, streptococcal pharyngitis, UTI, meningitis, Kawasaki disease; typical antipyretic doses.
6. **Respiratory illness.** Distress signs; croup (steeple sign, dexamethasone), bronchiolitis (supportive), epiglottitis, asthma, pneumonia, foreign body, pertussis.
7. **GI illness and fluids.** Dehydration percent, ORS first line, 20 mL/kg isotonic bolus, 4-2-1 maintenance, deficit = percent × kg × 10, slow correction of hypernatremia, intussusception, volvulus, pyloric stenosis.
8. **Congenital and genetic conditions.** Trisomies, Turner, Klinefelter, 22q11.2, Williams, imprinting, CF, PKU, congenital heart disease, Hardy–Weinberg and recurrence risks.
9. **Emergencies.** Assessment triangle, compensated shock, age-specific hypotension, epinephrine doses, defibrillation, febrile and prolonged seizures, button battery.
10. **Adolescent medicine.** Confidentiality, HEEADSSS, puberty, amenorrhea, contraception, eating disorders, SCFE, pneumothorax.
11. **Child abuse.** Mismatch between history and injury, reasonable suspicion, mandated reporting, objective documentation.
12. **Dosing.** mg/kg per day vs per dose, mL conversion, maximum caps, neonatal pharmacology, safe prescribing.

### Common errors

Treating early jaundice as physiologic, deferring vaccines for mild illness, waiting for hypotension to treat pediatric shock, mixing mg/kg/day with mg/kg/dose, forgetting maximum dose caps, and documenting opinions instead of observations.

solution: Total deficit $= 10\% \times 10 \text{ kg} \times 10 = 1000$ mL. Bolus $= 200$ mL, so remaining deficit $= 800$ mL. Maintenance for 10 kg $= 100 \times 10 = 1000$ mL/day. Total $= 800 + 1000 = 1800$ mL over 24 hours (about 75 mL/h).

hint: A well appearance does not exclude invasive bacterial infection in the first month.
solution: Well-appearing febrile infants aged 8 to 21 days need a full evaluation (blood, urine and CSF testing with inflammatory markers), hospitalization and empiric parenteral antibiotics such as ampicillin plus gentamicin or cefotaxime, with acyclovir if herpes is suspected. @exam self
prompt: Plan an 18-month well-child visit for a healthy girl who weighs 11 kg and is following the 50th percentile. She walks without holding on, says about five words, scribbles, and points to show things. She has received all vaccines through the 15-month visit except DTaP dose 4 and the second hepatitis A dose. Her parents report that she drinks about 16 oz of whole milk daily and will not sleep through the night. It is October. List the elements of the visit.
hint: Cover growth, development and autism screening, vaccines due, nutrition, and anticipatory guidance.
solution: Growth: plot weight, length, head circumference and weight-for-length on WHO charts; examine. Development: milestones are on track (walking, words, pointing, scribbling); perform a standardized developmental screen (for example ASQ) and the M-CHAT-R/F autism screen at this age. Vaccines: DTaP dose 4 (due at 15–18 months), hepatitis A dose 2 (at least 6 months after dose 1), and an annual influenza vaccine in the fall; check the current schedule for COVID-19; a mild illness is not a reason to defer. Nutrition: milk volume of 16 oz is appropriate (below 24 oz); offer iron-rich foods; fluoride varnish and dental referral; limit juice. Anticipatory guidance: toddler behavior and tantrums, consistent bedtime routine and sleep, toilet-training readiness, screen-time limits, car seat rear-facing as long as the seat allows, water, poison and firearm safety. Follow-up: next visit at 24 months with autism screening and lead and hemoglobin results reviewed as needed.

rubric: Lists DTaP dose 4, hepatitis A dose 2 and influenza vaccine, without deferring for mild illness.
