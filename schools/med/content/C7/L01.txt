@article
**Kinematics** describes how things move without asking why. Every later topic in this course, from forces on a limb to blood flowing in a vessel, builds on three quantities.

**Displacement** $\Delta x$ is the change in position, a vector with a sign (direction). It is not the same as distance travelled. **Velocity** is the rate of change of displacement, $v = \Delta x/\Delta t$, in m/s. **Acceleration** is the rate of change of velocity, $a = \Delta v/\Delta t$, in m/s$^2$. Choose a positive direction and keep it for the whole problem; a velocity or acceleration in the opposite direction is negative.

### Reading graphs

On a position-time graph the slope is the velocity. On a velocity-time graph the slope is the acceleration and the **area** under the curve is the displacement. A horizontal line on a velocity-time graph means zero acceleration.

### Constant acceleration

When $a$ is constant, four equations connect the five variables $x - x_0$, $v_0$, $v$, $a$ and $t$:

$$v = v_0 + at, \qquad \Delta x = v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2, \qquad v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\,\Delta x, \qquad \Delta x = \tfrac{1}{2}(v_0 + v)t.$$

Strategy: list what is given, name what is asked, then choose the equation that contains those variables and not the one you neither know nor want. These equations are valid only while $a$ is constant.

### Free fall

Near Earth's surface, ignoring air resistance, every object accelerates downward at the same rate $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$. Mass does not matter. If you choose up as positive, then $a = -g$. At the top of a thrown object's path the velocity is momentarily zero but the acceleration is still $-g$. An object thrown up returns to its starting height with the same speed it left with (in the opposite direction), and the trip up takes as long as the trip down.

### Projectiles

A **projectile** moves under gravity alone. The key idea is that the horizontal and vertical motions are independent:

- Horizontal: $a_x = 0$, so $v_x$ is constant and $x = v_x t$.
- Vertical: $a_y = -g$, so the free-fall equations apply.

Time is the only link between the two. For an object launched horizontally from height $h$, the fall time is $t = \sqrt{2h/g}$, and the range is $v_x t$. This is how you would analyse, for example, the path of a jumper leaving a ledge or a thrown medical-training dummy.

Throughout this course use $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$.

@example
title: Braking and free fall
problem: A car moving at $20\ \text{m/s}$ brakes with constant acceleration $-4.0\ \text{m/s}^2$. How far does it travel before stopping? (Use $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ where needed.)
step: Given: $v_0 = 20\ \text{m/s}$, $v = 0$, $a = -4.0\ \text{m/s}^2$. Unknown: $\Delta x$. Time is neither given nor wanted.
step: Choose $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\Delta x$, which has no $t$.
step: Solve: $\Delta x = \dfrac{v^2 - v_0^2}{2a} = \dfrac{0 - 400}{2(-4.0)} = 50\ \text{m}$.
step: Check the sign: a positive displacement with deceleration is reasonable, and the stopping time $t = 20/4.0 = 5.0$ s gives $\tfrac{1}{2}(20)(5.0) = 50$ m again.

@example
title: A horizontal launch
problem: A ball rolls off a table $0.80\ \text{m}$ high at $3.0\ \text{m/s}$. How far from the table edge does it land? Use $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$.
step: Vertical motion: $v_{0y} = 0$, so $h = \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2$ and $t = \sqrt{2h/g} = \sqrt{2(0.80)/9.8} = 0.404\ \text{s}$.
step: Horizontal motion has no acceleration: $x = v_x t = (3.0)(0.404) = 1.21\ \text{m}$.
step: The fall time does not depend on the horizontal speed, so a faster roll lands farther but at the same moment.

@mistakes
- Using the constant-acceleration equations when acceleration changes, or mixing signs by calling up positive for $v$ but $g$ positive too.
- Saying acceleration is zero at the top of a toss; velocity is zero there, but $a = -g$.
- Confusing distance with displacement, or reading the slope of a velocity-time graph as velocity instead of acceleration.
- Giving the projectile a horizontal acceleration or using total speed in the vertical equation.

@practice num
prompt: A cyclist starts at $3.0\ \text{m/s}$ and accelerates uniformly at $2.0\ \text{m/s}^2$ for $5.0\ \text{s}$. What is the final speed in m/s?
answer: 13
check: 3.0 + 2.0*5.0
unit: m/s
hint: Which equation links $v$, $v_0$, $a$ and $t$?
hint: $v = v_0 + at$.
solution: $v = 3.0 + (2.0)(5.0) = 13\ \text{m/s}$.

@practice num
prompt: A sled starts from rest and accelerates at $1.5\ \text{m/s}^2$ for $4.0\ \text{s}$. How far does it travel, in metres?
answer: 12
check: 0.5*1.5*4.0**2
unit: m
hint: The initial velocity is zero, so one term of $\Delta x = v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2$ vanishes.
solution: $\Delta x = \tfrac{1}{2}(1.5)(4.0)^2 = 12\ \text{m}$.

@practice num
prompt: A ball is dropped from rest from a height of $20\ \text{m}$. Ignoring air resistance and using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, how long does it take to reach the ground? Give seconds to two decimal places.
answer: 2.02
check: (2*20/9.8)**0.5
tol: 0.01
unit: s
hint: Initial velocity is zero. Which equation contains $\Delta x$, $a$ and $t$ only?
hint: $20 = \tfrac{1}{2}(9.8)t^2$.
solution: $t = \sqrt{2(20)/9.8} = 2.02\ \text{s}$.

@practice num
prompt: A stone is thrown horizontally at $6.0\ \text{m/s}$ from a bridge $1.5\ \text{m}$ above the water. Using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, how far horizontally from the throw point does it land, in metres?
answer: 3.32
check: 6.0*(2*1.5/9.8)**0.5
tol: 0.02
unit: m
hint: Find the fall time from the vertical motion alone.
hint: $t = \sqrt{2h/g}$, then multiply by the constant horizontal speed.
solution: $t = \sqrt{2(1.5)/9.8} = 0.553\ \text{s}$, so $x = (6.0)(0.553) = 3.32\ \text{m}$.

@quiz num
prompt: A ball is thrown straight up at $14.7\ \text{m/s}$. Ignoring air resistance and using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, what maximum height above the release point does it reach, in metres?
answer: 11.0
check: 14.7**2/(2*9.8)
tol: 0.05
unit: m
hint: At the top, $v = 0$. Which equation does not need the time?
hint: $0 = v_0^2 - 2g\,\Delta x$.
solution: $\Delta x = v_0^2/(2g) = (14.7)^2/(2 \times 9.8) = 11.0\ \text{m}$.

@quiz num
prompt: A person jumps straight up, leaving the floor at $3.0\ \text{m/s}$. Using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ and ignoring air resistance, how long is the person in the air before returning to floor level, in seconds?
answer: 0.612
check: 2*3.0/9.8
tol: 0.005
unit: s
hint: The time up equals the time down.
hint: Time to the top is $v_0/g$.
solution: Up takes $3.0/9.8 = 0.306$ s, so the round trip is $0.612$ s.
