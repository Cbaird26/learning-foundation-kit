@article
The skeleton is a system of levers. Muscles pull on bones attached close to a joint, and the joint acts as the pivot (fulcrum). Applying the equilibrium conditions from the previous week, $\sum F = 0$ and $\sum\tau = 0$, gives the forces inside the body. The results are surprising: muscle forces are often several times the weight being held.

### Classes of levers

- **First class:** the fulcrum is between the effort and the load. Example: the head balanced on the atlanto-occipital joint, with neck extensors providing the effort.
- **Second class:** the load is between the fulcrum and the effort. Example: standing on tiptoe, with the ball of the foot as the fulcrum, body weight as the load, and the calf muscle pulling at the heel.
- **Third class:** the effort is between the fulcrum and the load. Example: the forearm flexed by the biceps. This arrangement favours speed and range of motion rather than force.

**Mechanical advantage** is $MA = F_{\text{load}}/F_{\text{effort}} = r_{\text{effort}}/r_{\text{load}}$ (lever arms from the pivot). In a third-class lever $MA < 1$: the muscle must exert more force than the load.

### The forearm held horizontal

Consider a forearm horizontal, with the elbow as the pivot. The biceps attaches about $0.04$ m from the elbow and pulls straight up. Weight in the hand acts about $0.35$ m from the elbow, and the weight of the forearm acts at its centre of mass, roughly $0.15$ m away. For equilibrium, torques about the elbow sum to zero:

$$F_B\,(0.04) = m_{\text{load}}\,g\,(0.35) + m_{\text{arm}}\,g\,(0.15).$$

Because the biceps lever arm is much smaller than the load's, $F_B$ is roughly ten times the load weight. Then $\sum F_y = 0$ gives the **joint reaction force** $J$ (from the humerus on the forearm):

$$F_B = m_{\text{load}}g + m_{\text{arm}}g + J.$$

If the biceps pulls at an angle $\theta$ to the forearm, only $F_B\sin\theta$ contributes to the torque. All problems use $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$.

### The lower back

In bending forward and lifting, the spine is a lever with its pivot at the lumbar vertebrae. The erector spinae muscles act only a few centimetres behind the pivot, while the trunk's weight and the load act many centimetres in front of it. The muscle force is therefore very large, and the compressive force on the lumbar discs is larger still. This is why lifting with a straight back and the load close to the body, which shortens the load's lever arm, reduces the risk of back injury.

### Clinical connections

A joint replacement or tendon transfer changes the lever arm, and therefore the muscle force required. Weakness of a muscle is partly compensated by moving the load closer to the joint (a patient holding a heavy object near the body rather than at arm's length). Estimates from these models treat muscle forces as acting along straight lines and ignore co-contraction, so they are approximate.

@example
title: The biceps force to hold a weight
problem: A person holds a $5.0\ \text{kg}$ mass in the hand, forearm horizontal. The hand is $0.35\ \text{m}$ from the elbow. The forearm has mass $1.5\ \text{kg}$ with its centre of mass $0.15\ \text{m}$ from the elbow. The biceps pulls vertically at $0.04\ \text{m}$ from the elbow. Find the biceps force and the elbow joint force. Use $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$.
step: Torques about the elbow (the joint force then has zero lever arm): $F_B(0.04) = (5.0)(9.8)(0.35) + (1.5)(9.8)(0.15)$.
step: Compute the right side: $17.15 + 2.205 = 19.355\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}$.
step: $F_B = 19.355/0.04 = 484\ \text{N}$, about ten times the $49$ N weight of the object.
step: Vertical balance on the forearm, with $J$ downward: $F_B = 49 + 14.7 + J$, so $J = 484 - 63.7 = 420\ \text{N}$, directed downward on the forearm (the humerus pushes the forearm down).

@mistakes
- Using the weight of the load as the muscle force and forgetting the lever arm ratio.
- Omitting the weight of the forearm itself, or placing it at the hand.
- Taking torques about the biceps attachment but still including the joint force with a lever arm of zero.
- Calling the biceps lever a first-class or second-class lever; with the effort between the elbow and the hand it is third class.

answer: 0.114
check: 0.04/0.35
tol: 0.002
hint: Mechanical advantage is the ratio of lever arms: effort arm over load arm.
solution: $MA = 0.04/0.35 = 0.114$. The muscle force is about nine times the load.

@practice num
prompt: A person holds a $3.0\ \text{kg}$ mass $0.30\ \text{m}$ from the elbow with the forearm horizontal. The biceps pulls vertically $0.04\ \text{m}$ from the elbow. Ignoring the weight of the forearm and using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, find the biceps force in newtons.
answer: 220.5
check: 3.0*9.8*0.30/0.04
tol: 0.5
unit: N
hint: Take torques about the elbow.
solution: $F_B(0.04) = (3.0)(9.8)(0.30) = 8.82$, so $F_B = 220.5\ \text{N}$.

@practice num
prompt: A person holds a $4.0\ \text{kg}$ mass in the hand, $0.35\ \text{m}$ from the elbow, with the forearm horizontal. The forearm has mass $1.2\ \text{kg}$ and its centre of mass is $0.15\ \text{m}$ from the elbow. The biceps pulls vertically at $0.04\ \text{m}$ from the elbow. Using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, what force must the biceps exert, in newtons?
answer: 387
check: (4.0*9.8*0.35 + 1.2*9.8*0.15)/0.04
tol: 1
unit: N
hint: Include two torques about the elbow: the held mass and the forearm.
solution: $F_B(0.04) = 13.72 + 1.764 = 15.484$, so $F_B = 387\ \text{N}$.

@practice num
prompt: A person bends forward and lifts a $20\ \text{kg}$ box held $0.40\ \text{m}$ horizontally from the lumbar pivot. The trunk, head and arms have mass $35\ \text{kg}$ with their centre of mass $0.20\ \text{m}$ horizontally from the pivot. The back muscles act perpendicular to the lever, $0.05\ \text{m}$ behind the pivot. Treating all forces as vertical and using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, find the muscle force in newtons.
answer: 2940
check: (20*9.8*0.40 + 35*9.8*0.20)/0.05
tol: 5
unit: N
hint: Sum the two weight torques about the pivot and divide by the muscle's lever arm.
solution: $F(0.05) = 78.4 + 68.6 = 147$, so $F = 2940\ \text{N}$, more than $4$ times the total weight of $539$ N.
