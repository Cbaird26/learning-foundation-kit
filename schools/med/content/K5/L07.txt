@article
Nonfiction books tell true facts. They have special parts, called **text features**, that help you find facts fast. You do not have to read every word to find an answer.

### Common text features

- **Title:** the name of the book or article. It tells the topic.
- **Heading:** a small title above a part of the text. It tells what that part is about.
- **Table of contents:** a list at the start of a book showing where each part begins.
- **Bold words:** words in dark print. They are important, and often explained in the glossary.
- **Caption:** a sentence under a photo that tells what the photo shows.
- **Label:** a word with a line pointing to a part of a picture or diagram.
- **Glossary:** a short list of important words and their meanings, in ABC order, near the end.
- **Index:** a list at the back of topics in ABC order with page numbers.

### Using them

Say you want to know what frogs eat. Look at the headings. A heading called "Food" is a good place to start. Want to know what a bird's wing looks like? Look for a labeled picture.

A caption can tell you more than the photo shows. A glossary can help you learn a hard word without a dictionary.

### Tip

Before you read, look at the title, headings and pictures. This is a "text feature walk". It helps your brain get ready, and you will understand more when you read the words.

problem: You want to find out what the word "habitat" means in a book about turtles. Which feature should you use?
step: Habitat is a hard word that may be in bold print.
step: A glossary lists hard words and tells what they mean.
step: Look in the glossary, in ABC order under h, to find habitat.

@mistakes
- Thinking a caption is part of the story. A caption tells about the picture.
- Skipping headings. They are a map to the facts.
- Mixing up the glossary (word meanings) with the index (page numbers).
