@article
An **opinion** tells what you think or feel. A **fact** can be proven true. "Dogs have four legs" is a fact. "Dogs are the best pets" is an opinion, because other people may think cats are better.

### Parts of an opinion piece

- **Opinion sentence:** say what you think. "I think our school should have a garden."
- **Reasons:** tell why you think so. Give at least two. "A garden would teach us how plants grow. Also, we could eat the vegetables."
- **Closing sentence:** tell the reader again what you think. "That is why our school needs a garden."

### Useful words

Use **because**, **also**, **for example** and **another reason**. These words connect your opinion to your reasons: "I like soccer because it keeps me moving."

### Good reasons

A good reason helps the reader agree. "It is fun" is okay, but "It is fun because we all play together" is better. Add a real example: "Last week, we planted a bean seed and it sprouted."

### Check your work

Does your first sentence say what you think? Does each reason explain why? Does the ending match your opinion? Read it to your grown-up and see whether they agree or at least understand.

You can use opinions in letters, book reviews and posters. Every time you say what you think and why, you are writing an opinion.

@example
title: Fact or opinion?
problem: Is "Apples are the tastiest fruit" a fact or an opinion?
step: Ask: can this be proven true? People taste things differently.
step: Since not everyone agrees, it cannot be proven.
step: It is an opinion.

@mistakes
- Giving an opinion with no reasons.
- Using a fact as an opinion, like "The sky is blue".
- Giving reasons that do not match the opinion.

answer: 2
check: len([1, 3])
hint: A fact can be proven true. An opinion is what a person thinks.
hint: Ask about each sentence: could someone disagree?
solution: Sentences 1 and 3 can be checked and proven, so they are facts. Sentences 2 and 4 are what people think, so they are opinions. That makes 2 facts.
