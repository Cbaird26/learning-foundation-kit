@article
Every clinical problem in this course starts with a question of structure: where did this tissue come from, what supplies it, and where does it drain? Build the map once and the diseases follow from it.

### The indifferent stage

Through about week 6 of development, embryos of both sexes carry the same primordia: bipotential gonads (from the genital ridge, near the mesonephros), two paired duct systems, and an undifferentiated external genitalia (genital tubercle, urethral folds, labioscrotal swellings). The **mesonephric (Wolffian) ducts** and **paramesonephric (Müllerian) ducts** lie side by side. Which one survives depends on hormones, the subject of the next lesson.

| Embryonic structure | Female derivative | Male derivative |
|---|---|---|
| Paramesonephric duct | Fallopian tubes, uterus, cervix, upper one third of the vagina | Appendix testis (vestige) |
| Mesonephric duct | Gartner duct (vestige) | Epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, ejaculatory duct |
| Urogenital sinus | Lower two thirds of the vagina, urethra, Bartholin and Skene glands | Prostate, bulbourethral glands, prostatic urethra |
| Genital tubercle | Clitoris | Glans penis |
| Urethral folds | Labia minora | Ventral shaft of penis (spongy urethra) |
| Labioscrotal swellings | Labia majora | Scrotum |
| Gubernaculum | Round ligament of the uterus and ovarian ligament | Gubernaculum testis |

The upper vagina is Müllerian, the lower vagina is sinus-derived; the junction explains why vaginal septa and agenesis occur.

### Müllerian anomalies

The two paramesonephric ducts fuse in the midline, then the septum between them resorbs. Failure at each step gives a recognizable anomaly: **uterus didelphys** (complete non-fusion, two cervices), **bicornuate uterus** (partial fusion failure, heart-shaped fundus with a cleft), **septate uterus** (fusion normal, septum not resorbed; the most common anomaly and the one most associated with pregnancy loss; treated by hysteroscopic septum resection). The external fundal contour is normal in a septate uterus and clefted in a bicornuate one, which is how MRI separates them. Renal anomalies accompany Müllerian anomalies because the ducts develop beside the urinary tract, so image the kidneys.

**Müllerian agenesis (Mayer–Rokitansky–Küster–Hauser syndrome)** gives a 46,XX girl with normal ovaries, normal breast and pubic hair development, a blind vaginal pouch and no uterus; she presents with primary amenorrhea.

### Descent of the testis

The gubernaculum shortens and pulls the testis through the inguinal canal into the scrotum during the second half of gestation. The processus vaginalis follows; if it stays open, it produces an **indirect inguinal hernia** or a **communicating hydrocele**. A testis that has not descended (**cryptorchidism**) raises the risk of infertility and **germ cell tumor**, and orchiopexy is typically done in infancy (around 6 to 18 months). Orchiopexy does not fully erase the tumor risk and the contralateral testis is also at somewhat greater risk.

**Hypospadias** (urethral meatus on the ventral side; failed fusion of the urethral folds) is far more common than **epispadias** (dorsal meatus; associated with bladder exstrophy). Do not circumcise a child with hypospadias, because the foreskin may be needed for repair.

### Surgical and clinical anatomy

- **Gonadal vessels.** The ovarian and testicular arteries come directly from the aorta below the renal arteries. The right gonadal vein drains to the inferior vena cava; the **left gonadal vein drains to the left renal vein**, at a right angle, which is why varicocele is predominantly left sided.
- **Lymph.** Testis and ovary drain to **para-aortic** nodes (explaining retroperitoneal spread of testicular tumors). The scrotum, vulva and lower vagina drain to superficial inguinal nodes; the uterine fundus drains partly along the round ligament to inguinal nodes.
- **Ligaments.** The **suspensory (infundibulopelvic) ligament** carries the ovarian vessels. The **cardinal (transverse cervical) ligament** carries the uterine vessels at the cervix base. The **round ligament** runs through the inguinal canal to the labium majus.
- **Ureter.** The uterine artery crosses **over the ureter** ("water under the bridge") about 2 cm lateral to the cervix. Ureteral injury is the classic complication of hysterectomy when the uterine artery is clamped.
- **Nerves.** The pudendal nerve (S2 to S4) supplies the perineum and is blocked near the ischial spine for delivery. Parasympathetic S2 to S4 (pelvic splanchnic) drives erection; sympathetic (hypogastric) drives emission.

@example
title: Deriving the findings of Müllerian agenesis
problem: A 16-year-old girl has never menstruated. She has Tanner stage 4 breasts and normal pubic hair. Examination shows a short, blind-ending vaginal pouch. Pelvic ultrasound shows no uterus and normal ovaries; karyotype is 46,XX. Explain the findings.
step: Normal breast and pubic hair development means ovaries are making estrogen and adrenal androgens normally. The gonads are not the problem.
step: The ovaries form from the genital ridge independently of the ducts, so they are normal. The uterus, cervix and upper vagina come from the paramesonephric ducts.
step: Failure of the paramesonephric ducts to develop leaves no uterus and no upper vagina. The lower vagina, formed from the urogenital sinus, makes only a short blind pouch.
step: Diagnosis: Müllerian agenesis. Next step is renal ultrasound, because renal agenesis and skeletal anomalies are associated.

@example
title: Why the ureter is at risk
problem: During an abdominal hysterectomy for fibroids, the surgeon clamps the uterine artery lateral to the cervix. Which structure lies immediately beneath it, and why?
step: The uterine artery arises from the internal iliac artery and runs medially in the cardinal ligament toward the cervix.
step: The ureter descends in the pelvis and passes under the uterine artery about 2 cm lateral to the cervix on its way to the bladder.
step: A clamp or suture placed blindly on the artery may therefore catch the ureter. The surgeon identifies the ureter first.
step: Postoperative flank pain, fever or leaking fluid from the vagina suggest ureteral injury or ureterovaginal fistula.

@mistakes
- Saying the whole vagina is Müllerian. Only the upper third is; the lower two thirds comes from the urogenital sinus.
- Forgetting that the left testicular vein enters the left renal vein, not the vena cava, which explains left-sided varicocele.
- Confusing the ovarian ligament and the suspensory ligament: the suspensory (infundibulopelvic) ligament carries the ovarian vessels.
- Assuming the prostate is Wolffian. It arises from the urogenital sinus, while the seminal vesicles are Wolffian.
