@article
### The axis

Pulsatile **GnRH** from the hypothalamus stimulates the anterior pituitary to release **LH** and **FSH**. Think of the testis as two cooperating compartments:

| Cell | Stimulated by | Product | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leydig (interstitial) | LH | Testosterone | Spermatogenesis (locally), libido, secondary sex characteristics, negative feedback |
| Sertoli (in tubules) | FSH and testosterone | Inhibin B, androgen-binding protein, AMH (in fetus) | Nourish germ cells, form blood-testis barrier, inhibit FSH |

Testosterone exerts negative feedback on GnRH and LH; **inhibin B** from Sertoli cells inhibits FSH. High intratesticular testosterone (many times the serum level) is needed for sperm production. This explains an important clinical fact: a man taking **exogenous testosterone or anabolic steroids** suppresses LH, so intratesticular testosterone falls, spermatogenesis falls, and the testes shrink. Gonadotropins, not testosterone, are used to restore fertility.

### Spermatogenesis

Spermatogonia (diploid) divide mitotically; one daughter becomes a **primary spermatocyte** that undergoes meiosis I to give two **secondary spermatocytes**, and meiosis II gives four haploid **spermatids**. Spermiogenesis shapes spermatids into sperm (acrosome, flagellum, condensed nucleus). The process takes about 70 to 75 days in the tubules, plus roughly 2 weeks of epididymal transit, so a change in the testis shows up in the semen about 3 months later. The tight junctions between Sertoli cells form the **blood-testis barrier**, protecting haploid cells from immune attack.

### Erection, emission, ejaculation

- **Erection**: parasympathetic (S2 to S4, pelvic splanchnic nerves) releases nitric oxide, raising cGMP and relaxing the corpus cavernosum smooth muscle.
- **Emission**: sympathetic (hypogastric nerves, T10 to L2) moves sperm and fluid into the posterior urethra.
- **Ejaculation**: somatic (pudendal nerve, S2 to S4) contracts the bulbospongiosus and ischiocavernosus muscles; the bladder neck closes.

"Parasympathetic points, sympathetic shoots." Spinal cord injury above the sacral center may leave reflex erections but disturbs ejaculation.

### Semen analysis

Standard lower reference limits (WHO, 2021, fifth percentile) are roughly: volume 1.4 mL, concentration 16 million/mL, **total sperm number 39 million**, total motility 42%, progressive motility 30%, and normal forms about 4% by strict morphology (the fifth edition used slightly different values). Total sperm number is volume multiplied by concentration. Two abnormal samples several weeks apart are required before labeling a man infertile.

### Scrotal disorders

- **Testicular torsion**: peak in adolescence; sudden severe pain, nausea and vomiting, a high-riding testis with horizontal lie ("bell-clapper deformity"), **absent cremasteric reflex**, no pain relief on elevation. It is a surgical emergency, with best salvage within about 6 hours. If suspicion is high, go directly to surgery; Doppler ultrasound (absent or reduced flow) is for equivocal cases and must not delay care. Surgery includes bilateral orchiopexy.
- **Epididymitis**: gradual pain, fever, dysuria; cremasteric reflex preserved; pain may improve on elevation (Prehn sign). Men under 35 are usually infected with *Chlamydia trachomatis* or *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*; older men and those with anal intercourse or obstruction have enteric organisms such as *E. coli*.
- **Varicocele**: dilated pampiniform plexus, most often left, "bag of worms"; a common correctable cause of male infertility.
- **Hydrocele**: painless, transilluminates. **Spermatocele** is a cyst of the epididymis.

### Testicular tumors

Most are **germ cell tumors**, presenting as a painless, firm testicular mass in a young man. The most common is **seminoma** (uniform cells with clear cytoplasm, lymphocytic infiltrate, radiosensitive, normal AFP; placental alkaline phosphatase and LDH are often raised and hCG may be mildly raised). Nonseminomatous types: **yolk sac** (AFP, Schiller-Duval bodies), **choriocarcinoma** (high hCG, hematogenous spread), embryonal carcinoma and teratoma. Evaluate with scrotal ultrasound and tumor markers (AFP, hCG, LDH); treat with **radical inguinal orchiectomy**, not scrotal biopsy, which could spread tumor to different lymphatics. Testicular lymph goes to para-aortic nodes.

### Prostate

**BPH** arises in the periurethral transition zone: weak stream, hesitancy, nocturia, retention. Alpha-1 blockers (tamsulosin) relax smooth muscle quickly; 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (finasteride) shrink the gland over months. **Prostate adenocarcinoma** arises in the peripheral zone, so is palpable on digital exam and rarely causes early obstruction; it spreads to bone as osteoblastic metastases. PSA is organ- and not cancer-specific (raised in BPH, prostatitis). Grading uses the Gleason (Grade Group) system; screening is a shared decision for ages 55 to 69.

title: Interpreting a semen analysis
problem: A 31-year-old man's semen sample has a volume of 2.5 mL and a concentration of 12 million sperm/mL. Total motility is 55%. What is the total sperm number, and how does it compare with the lower reference limit?
step: Total sperm number equals volume times concentration: $2.5 \times 12 = 30$ million.
step: The lower reference limit for total sperm number is about 39 million.
step: Thirty million is below the limit (oligospermia), while motility of 55% is above the lower limit of 42%.
step: Repeat the analysis after several weeks (two abnormal samples are needed), and evaluate for varicocele, medication or testosterone use, and endocrine causes.

@example
title: The acute scrotum
problem: A 15-year-old boy has had sudden severe left scrotal pain for 3 hours with vomiting. The left testis is high-riding with a horizontal lie, and the left cremasteric reflex is absent. What is the next step?
step: Sudden onset in an adolescent with a high-riding horizontal testis and an absent cremasteric reflex is torsion until proven otherwise.
step: Testis viability falls sharply after about 6 hours, so time matters more than confirmation.
step: Proceed to emergent surgical exploration with detorsion and bilateral orchiopexy; do not delay for imaging or give antibiotics for epididymitis first.
step: If the exam were equivocal and the history subacute, color Doppler ultrasound could be used first.

@mistakes
- Treating a suspected torsion with antibiotics and a follow-up visit, or waiting for imaging when the picture is classic.
- Biopsying a testicular mass through the scrotum. Use radical inguinal orchiectomy.
- Giving exogenous testosterone to a man who wants fertility; it suppresses LH and sperm production.
- Confusing the zones: BPH is transition zone, cancer is peripheral zone.
