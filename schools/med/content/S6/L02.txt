@article
**Genetic sex** is set at fertilization, **gonadal sex** by the gonad that forms, and **phenotypic sex** by the hormones that gonad makes. The clinical rule: the default pathway is female; male development has to be actively driven.

### The male pathway

The **SRY** gene on the short arm of the Y chromosome (the sex-determining region) triggers **SOX9**, which drives bipotential gonad cells to become **Sertoli cells**. Sertoli cells make **anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH, also called Müllerian-inhibiting substance)**, which causes ipsilateral regression of the paramesonephric ducts. Leydig cells (stimulated first by placental hCG, later by fetal LH) make **testosterone**, which acts locally on the Wolffian duct (via the androgen receptor) to form the epididymis, vas deferens and seminal vesicles. In the skin and urogenital sinus, testosterone is converted by **5-alpha-reductase** to **dihydrotestosterone (DHT)**, a more potent androgen that masculinizes the external genitalia (penis, scrotum) and the prostate.

In summary:

| Hormone | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| AMH | Sertoli cells | Regresses Müllerian structures |
| Testosterone | Leydig cells | Wolffian duct derivatives |
| DHT | Peripheral conversion by 5-alpha-reductase | External genitalia, prostate |

### The female pathway

Without SRY, the gonad becomes an ovary. Without AMH, the Müllerian ducts persist; without testosterone, the Wolffian ducts regress; without DHT, the external genitalia are female. The ovary is not needed for this: a streak gonad or no gonad still gives a female phenotype.

### Disorders of sex development (DSD)

**46,XY DSD (undervirilization)**

- **Complete androgen insensitivity syndrome (CAIS)**: X-linked recessive androgen receptor defect. The testes make AMH (no uterus or upper vagina) and normal or high testosterone, but the receptor is nonfunctional so there is no Wolffian development and the external genitalia are female. Presentation: a phenotypic girl with primary amenorrhea, normal breasts (peripheral aromatization of testosterone to estradiol), **scant pubic and axillary hair**, a blind vaginal pouch and testes (often inguinal or intra-abdominal). Labs: elevated testosterone and elevated LH (no negative feedback). Management: gonadectomy after puberty because of tumor risk, estrogen replacement, counseling. Half of the 46,XY children of a carrier mother are affected.
- **5-alpha-reductase deficiency (type 2)**: testosterone normal, DHT low. Wolffian structures develop, but external genitalia are ambiguous or female at birth. At puberty testosterone rises and virilization occurs (deepening voice, muscle, phallic growth). **Elevated testosterone:DHT ratio.** Sperm production is possible.
- **Gonadal dysgenesis (Swyer syndrome)**: 46,XY with a defective SRY or SOX9 pathway; streak gonads, female phenotype *with* a uterus (no AMH), no puberty, high FSH and LH.
- **17-alpha-hydroxylase deficiency**: decreased sex steroids and cortisol, increased mineralocorticoid (hypertension, hypokalemia); 46,XY patients have female external genitalia; 46,XX patients have no puberty.

**46,XX DSD (overvirilization)**

**Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH)** is the leading cause of ambiguous genitalia in 46,XX newborns. **21-hydroxylase deficiency** (about 90 to 95% of CAH) blocks cortisol and aldosterone synthesis. ACTH rises, driving adrenal androgens up, and the 17-hydroxyprogesterone precursor accumulates. The newborn girl has virilized genitalia but normal internal female organs. Salt-wasting forms present at 1 to 2 weeks with vomiting, hypotension, **hyponatremia and hyperkalemia**, high renin. Diagnose with elevated **17-hydroxyprogesterone**. Treat with glucocorticoid and, when salt-wasting, mineralocorticoid replacement. **11-beta-hydroxylase deficiency** produces virilization plus **hypertension** (excess 11-deoxycorticosterone) and low renin.

**Sex chromosome disorders**

- **Turner syndrome (45,X)**: streak ovaries (from accelerated oocyte loss), short stature (SHOX), webbed neck, shield chest, widely spaced nipples, lymphedema, **bicuspid aortic valve and coarctation**, horseshoe kidney, primary amenorrhea, **high FSH and LH**. No Barr body.
- **Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY)**: tall stature, small firm testes, gynecomastia, infertility (azoospermia), low testosterone, high FSH and LH, low inhibin B; one Barr body. Increased risk of breast cancer and venous thromboembolism.
- **Kallmann syndrome**: failure of GnRH neuron migration with anosmia and low LH, FSH and testosterone.

A useful tool: number of **Barr bodies** (inactive X) equals the number of X chromosomes minus 1.

@example
title: A phenotypic girl with a blind vagina and no uterus
problem: A 16-year-old has primary amenorrhea, Tanner 4 breasts, sparse axillary and pubic hair, and a blind vaginal pouch. No uterus is seen on ultrasound. Karyotype is 46,XY; testosterone is in the adult male range and LH is elevated. Explain each finding.
step: Karyotype 46,XY with testes: Sertoli cells make AMH, so Müllerian structures regress (no uterus, no fallopian tubes, no upper vagina).
step: The androgen receptor is nonfunctional, so testosterone and DHT have no effect: no Wolffian derivatives and external genitalia are female. Lack of androgen action also explains the sparse sexual hair.
step: With no androgen feedback on the pituitary, LH rises and testosterone is high. Some testosterone is aromatized to estradiol, which causes breast development.
step: Diagnosis: complete androgen insensitivity. Management includes gonadectomy after puberty because of germ cell tumor risk, followed by estrogen replacement.

@example
title: Salt-wasting 21-hydroxylase deficiency in a newborn
problem: A 10-day-old with ambiguous genitalia has vomiting and poor feeding. Sodium is 126 mEq/L, potassium 6.4 mEq/L, glucose is low. Ultrasound shows a uterus and ovaries. What is the likely enzyme defect, and what hormone confirms the diagnosis?
step: Ovaries and uterus are present, so the karyotype is 46,XX; the genitalia were virilized in utero by excess androgen.
step: The enzyme defect must block cortisol and aldosterone synthesis while sending precursors to the androgen pathway: 21-hydroxylase.
step: Lack of aldosterone causes sodium loss and potassium retention; lack of cortisol contributes to hypoglycemia and shock.
step: Confirm by a markedly elevated 17-hydroxyprogesterone; treat with hydrocortisone, fludrocortisone and saline.

@mistakes
- Thinking the ovary is required for female development. The default pathway proceeds without ovaries.
- Mixing up testosterone and DHT: testosterone drives the Wolffian ducts; DHT drives external genitalia and prostate.
- Expecting a uterus in complete androgen insensitivity. Testes make AMH, so there is no uterus.
- Calling 11-beta-hydroxylase deficiency salt-wasting. It causes hypertension; classic 21-hydroxylase deficiency is the salt-wasting form.

@practice self
prompt: Explain the hormonal control of sexual differentiation in a 46,XY embryo. Include the cell types, hormones, and target tissues, and explain what would change if each hormone or receptor were absent.
hint: Go cell by cell: Sertoli cell product, Leydig cell product, then the peripheral conversion step.
solution: SRY (Y short arm) activates SOX9, so the gonad develops as a testis with Sertoli and Leydig cells. Sertoli cells secrete AMH, which regresses the ipsilateral Müllerian duct; without AMH a uterus and tubes persist (as in a persistent Müllerian duct syndrome). Leydig cells secrete testosterone (driven by hCG and then LH), which stimulates the Wolffian duct to form epididymis, vas deferens and seminal vesicles; without testosterone or its receptor these fail and the genitalia are female (androgen insensitivity). In the urogenital sinus and genital skin, 5-alpha-reductase converts testosterone to DHT, which masculinizes the penis, scrotum and prostate; without it the external genitalia are ambiguous at birth. Without SRY, or with defective SOX9, the default female pathway occurs.
