### Bleeding in the first trimester

**Ectopic pregnancy** (implantation outside the uterus, usually the ampulla of the tube) presents with amenorrhea, pelvic pain and vaginal bleeding; risks include prior ectopic, pelvic inflammatory disease, tubal surgery, and an intrauterine device in place if pregnancy occurs. A positive hCG with **no intrauterine pregnancy on transvaginal ultrasound once hCG is above the discriminatory zone** (about 1500 to 3500 mIU/mL) is ectopic until proven otherwise. A **ruptured ectopic** with hemoperitoneum and shock needs surgery. Stable patients with a small unruptured ectopic, hCG below about 5000 mIU/mL, no fetal cardiac activity and normal liver and renal function may receive **methotrexate** (a folate antagonist; avoid folic acid supplements, NSAIDs and prolonged sun exposure during treatment), with hCG follow-up.

**Spontaneous abortion** (loss before 20 weeks) is mostly caused by chromosomal abnormalities. Types: **threatened** (bleeding, closed cervix, viable fetus), **inevitable** (open cervix), **incomplete** (some tissue passed), **complete** (all passed), **missed** (fetal demise, closed cervix) and **septic**. Management is expectant, medical (mifepristone plus misoprostol, or misoprostol alone) or surgical (suction curettage). Give Rh immune globulin to Rh-negative women. **Recurrent loss** (two or more) prompts evaluation for antiphospholipid syndrome (treated with low-dose aspirin and heparin), parental karyotype, uterine anomalies (septum) and thyroid and glucose disorders. **Cervical insufficiency** is painless second-trimester dilation; treated with cerclage.

**Gestational trophoblastic disease.** A **complete mole** (46,XX, all paternal chromosomes from an empty egg fertilized by one sperm that duplicates) has no fetal tissue; it presents with bleeding, a uterus larger than dates, **very high hCG**, hyperemesis, early-onset preeclampsia, hyperthyroidism and theca lutein cysts; ultrasound shows a "snowstorm" pattern; histology shows diffuse swelling of villi. A **partial mole** (69,XXY triploid; two sperm and one egg) has fetal parts and lower hCG. Treatment is suction curettage with serial hCG until undetectable; **choriocarcinoma** (hematogenous spread to lung, with very high hCG) is a feared sequel, responding to methotrexate or dactinomycin.

### Bleeding in the third trimester

| | Placenta previa | Placental abruption |
|---|---|---|
| Pain | Painless | Painful |
| Blood | Bright red | Dark, may be concealed |
| Uterus | Soft, relaxed | Firm, tender, contractions |
| Risk | Prior cesarean, multiparity, multiple gestation | Hypertension, cocaine, smoking, trauma |
| Complication | Hemorrhage, accreta | DIC, fetal distress or death, hemorrhage |

**Never do a digital cervical examination before an ultrasound** has excluded previa. Previa persisting near term is delivered by cesarean. **Placenta accreta spectrum** (accreta, increta, percreta) occurs when decidua is deficient, classically over a uterine scar with previa; planned cesarean hysterectomy is the usual treatment. **Vasa previa** (fetal vessels over the cervix) causes painless bleeding with rupture of membranes and rapid fetal exsanguination.

### Hypertensive disorders

- **Chronic hypertension**: before 20 weeks. **Gestational hypertension**: new BP of at least 140/90 after 20 weeks without proteinuria or severe features.
- **Preeclampsia**: new hypertension after 20 weeks (BP at least 140/90 on two occasions 4 hours apart) with **proteinuria** (at least 300 mg per 24 hours, protein-to-creatinine ratio at least 0.3, or dipstick at least 2+) **or**, without proteinuria, any severe feature. Pathophysiology: abnormal placental implantation (inadequate spiral artery remodeling), placental ischemia, anti-angiogenic factors (sFlt-1) and endothelial dysfunction.
- **Severe features**: BP at least **160/110**; platelets below **100,000/microliter**; liver transaminases at least twice normal or severe right upper quadrant or epigastric pain; creatinine above **1.1 mg/dL** or doubled; **pulmonary edema**; new-onset **headache** unresponsive to medication; **visual disturbances**. (Massive proteinuria alone is no longer a severe feature.)
- **Eclampsia**: seizure in a woman with preeclampsia. **HELLP syndrome**: Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets.
- **Management**: delivery is the cure. Deliver at 37 weeks for gestational hypertension or preeclampsia without severe features, and at 34 0/7 weeks or later for preeclampsia with severe features once the mother is stabilized (earlier if maternal or fetal status is unstable; stable patients before 34 weeks may be managed expectantly in a center with maternal-fetal medicine support). **Magnesium sulfate** prevents and treats seizures (typical regimen for study: 4 to 6 g IV load, then 1 to 2 g/h); toxicity shows first as **loss of deep tendon reflexes**, then respiratory depression, then cardiac arrest; antidote **calcium gluconate**. Treat severe-range BP with IV labetalol, IV hydralazine or oral nifedipine. Avoid ACE inhibitors and ARBs. **Low-dose aspirin (81 mg) starting between 12 and 28 weeks (ideally before 16)** reduces risk in high-risk women (previous preeclampsia, chronic hypertension, diabetes, renal disease, multiple gestation, autoimmune disease).

### Diabetes in pregnancy

**Gestational diabetes** is managed with diet and exercise, then insulin (or metformin in some settings). Fetal hyperinsulinism causes **macrosomia**, **shoulder dystocia**, neonatal hypoglycemia, polycythemia and hyperbilirubinemia. **Pregestational diabetes** adds congenital anomalies (neural tube defects, cardiac defects, caudal regression syndrome): aim for good control before conception.

### Preterm labor

Labor before 37 weeks. Assess with cervical length and fetal fibronectin. Give **antenatal corticosteroids (betamethasone)** for fetal lung maturity (24 to 34 weeks, and sometimes late preterm), **magnesium sulfate** for fetal neuroprotection below 32 weeks, and group B streptococcus prophylaxis; short-term **tocolysis** (nifedipine, or indomethacin before 32 weeks) can gain 48 hours for steroids. **Preterm premature rupture of membranes** gets latency antibiotics. Vaginal progesterone helps in short cervix.

@example
title: Preeclampsia with severe features
problem: A 31-year-old woman at 35 weeks has a headache and blurry vision. BP is 168/112 mmHg on two readings 15 minutes apart. Urine protein-to-creatinine ratio is 0.8; platelets are 82,000/microliter; creatinine 1.0 mg/dL. Diagnose and manage.
step: New hypertension after 20 weeks with proteinuria (ratio 0.8, above 0.3) is preeclampsia.
step: Severe features: BP at least 160/110, platelets below 100,000 and new headache with visual symptoms. The creatinine is not elevated.
step: Start magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis and IV labetalol or hydralazine (or oral nifedipine) for sustained severe-range BP within 30 to 60 minutes.
step: Delivery after maternal stabilization is indicated at 34 weeks or beyond. Monitor reflexes, urine output and respiratory rate on magnesium.

@example
title: A stable ectopic pregnancy
problem: A 25-year-old woman at 6 weeks of amenorrhea has mild left lower quadrant pain and spotting. She is stable. hCG is 2300 mIU/mL; transvaginal ultrasound shows an empty uterus and a 2.2-cm left adnexal mass with no cardiac activity and no free fluid. Hemoglobin, creatinine and liver tests are normal. What is the management?
step: hCG above the discriminatory zone with an empty uterus and an adnexal mass is ectopic pregnancy.
step: She is stable with unruptured ectopic, low hCG, no cardiac activity and normal organ function: she qualifies for methotrexate.
step: Give methotrexate (single-dose IM regimen), check hCG on days 4 and 7, expect a fall of at least 15% between them, and follow hCG weekly to zero.
step: If unstable, peritoneal signs, rupture or failure of medical therapy occurs, perform laparoscopic salpingectomy or salpingostomy. Give Rh immune globulin if she is Rh-negative.

@mistakes
- Performing a digital cervical exam in a patient with painless third-trimester bleeding before ultrasound.
- Treating "proteinuria" as required for preeclampsia with severe features; thrombocytopenia, renal or liver abnormality, pulmonary edema or neurologic symptoms are enough.
- Missing magnesium toxicity: check deep tendon reflexes, respiratory rate and urine output, and give calcium gluconate.
- Giving ACE inhibitors for hypertension in pregnancy.
