@article
### Estrogens and progestins

**Estrogens** (estradiol, conjugated estrogens, ethinyl estradiol) are used for contraception, menopausal vasomotor symptoms and genitourinary atrophy, hypogonadism and Turner syndrome. Adverse effects: venous thromboembolism, stroke, breast tenderness, nausea, hypertension, gallstones; **unopposed estrogen increases endometrial cancer risk, so women with a uterus take a progestin**. Estrogen-progestin therapy slightly increases breast cancer and cardiovascular risk and is used at the lowest effective dose for the shortest time for symptoms; it is not for prevention of chronic disease.

**Progestins** (medroxyprogesterone, norethindrone, levonorgestrel, etonogestrel, drospirenone) protect the endometrium, are contraceptives, and treat abnormal bleeding and endometriosis. **DMPA** lowers bone density. **Drospirenone** has antimineralocorticoid action (monitor potassium).

### Receptor modulators and enzyme inhibitors

| Drug | Mechanism | Uses | Key adverse effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clomiphene | SERM; blocks hypothalamic estrogen receptors, raising GnRH, FSH and LH | Ovulation induction | Ovarian cysts, hot flashes, visual symptoms, multiple pregnancy |
| Letrozole, anastrozole | Aromatase inhibitors | Breast cancer in postmenopausal women; ovulation induction (letrozole) | Bone loss, arthralgia |
| Tamoxifen | Estrogen antagonist in breast, agonist in uterus and bone | ER-positive breast cancer | Endometrial cancer, thromboembolism, hot flashes |
| Raloxifene | Antagonist in breast and uterus, agonist in bone | Osteoporosis, breast cancer risk reduction | Thromboembolism, hot flashes; no endometrial stimulation |
| Mifepristone | Progesterone receptor antagonist | Medical abortion (with misoprostol) | Bleeding, incomplete abortion |
| Ulipristal | Selective progesterone receptor modulator | Emergency contraception | Headache |
| Finasteride, dutasteride | 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors | BPH, male pattern hair loss | Sexual dysfunction; teratogenic to male fetus |
| Flutamide, bicalutamide | Androgen receptor antagonists | Prostate cancer | Flutamide: hepatotoxicity |
| Spironolactone | Androgen receptor antagonist and aldosterone antagonist | Hirsutism, PCOS | Hyperkalemia, menstrual irregularity, gynecomastia |

### GnRH and gonadotropins

**Leuprolide** is a GnRH agonist. **Pulsatile** administration stimulates LH and FSH; **continuous** administration desensitizes the pituitary and suppresses gonadal steroids. It is used for prostate cancer (an initial testosterone flare can be prevented by starting an antiandrogen first), endometriosis, fibroids, central precocious puberty and in IVF protocols. **GnRH antagonists** (cetrorelix, ganirelix, degarelix, elagolix) suppress immediately without flare. **Menotropins**, follitropins and **hCG** (which mimics LH and triggers ovulation) are used for ovulation induction and IVF.

### Androgens

Testosterone treats hypogonadism; it suppresses LH and spermatogenesis (testicular atrophy) and can cause erythrocytosis, acne, worsened sleep apnea and, with anabolic steroids, hepatic toxicity.

### Erectile dysfunction and prostate drugs

**PDE5 inhibitors** (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil) inhibit breakdown of cGMP, enhancing nitric oxide-mediated vasodilation in the corpus cavernosum. They cause flushing, headache, dyspepsia, visual disturbance (sildenafil, PDE6 cross-reaction) and priapism. They are **contraindicated with nitrates** (and caution with alpha blockers) because of severe hypotension. Sildenafil acts for about 4 hours; tadalafil has a half-life of about 17.5 hours. **Tamsulosin** (alpha-1A selective antagonist) relieves BPH symptoms, causing orthostatic hypotension, retrograde ejaculation and intraoperative floppy iris syndrome.

### Drugs in obstetrics

- **Oxytocin** (induction, augmentation, postpartum hemorrhage; adverse effects: tachysystole, water intoxication from antidiuretic effect, hypotension if given as a bolus).
- **Dinoprostone** (PGE2) and **misoprostol** (PGE1) for cervical ripening; **carboprost** (PGF2-alpha) and **methylergonovine** for atony (contraindicated in asthma and hypertension, respectively).
- **Tocolytics**: nifedipine; indomethacin (before 32 weeks, up to 48 hours; premature closure of the ductus arteriosus and oligohydramnios); terbutaline (maternal tachycardia, hyperglycemia, hypokalemia; not for prolonged use). Magnesium sulfate is given for neuroprotection and seizure prophylaxis.
- **Antenatal betamethasone** matures fetal lungs.

### Pregnancy: unsafe and preferred drugs

| Drug | Fetal effect |
|---|---|
| ACE inhibitors, ARBs | Renal dysgenesis, oligohydramnios, pulmonary hypoplasia |
| Warfarin | Nasal hypoplasia, stippled epiphyses; use heparin |
| Isotretinoin | Craniofacial, cardiac and CNS defects |
| Valproate | Neural tube defects |
| Carbamazepine, phenytoin | Neural tube defects; fetal hydantoin syndrome (phenytoin) |
| Lithium | Ebstein anomaly |
| Tetracyclines | Tooth discoloration, bone growth inhibition |
| Aminoglycosides | Ototoxicity |
| Methotrexate, thalidomide, misoprostol | Malformations, pregnancy loss; thalidomide causes limb defects |
| Diethylstilbestrol | Clear cell vaginal adenocarcinoma |
| Alcohol | Fetal alcohol syndrome |
| Methimazole | Aplasia cutis, choanal atresia; use propylthiouracil in the first trimester |

Preferred in pregnancy: hypertension (labetalol, nifedipine, methyldopa, hydralazine), anticoagulation (low-molecular-weight heparin), diabetes (insulin), hypothyroidism (levothyroxine, with dose increase), nausea (doxylamine with pyridoxine). Folic acid reduces neural tube defects.

@example
title: An oxytocin infusion rate
problem: Oxytocin 30 units is mixed in 500 mL. What pump rate (mL/h) delivers 6 milliunits per minute? (Study calculation; follow institutional protocols.)
step: Convert units to milliunits: $30\ \text{units} = 30{,}000$ mU in 500 mL, so the concentration is $30{,}000/500 = 60$ mU/mL.
step: Convert the dose to per hour: $6$ mU/min $\times 60$ min/h $= 360$ mU/h.
step: Divide the hourly dose by the concentration: $360/60 = 6$ mL/h.
step: Rates are titrated slowly, with monitoring of contraction frequency and fetal heart rate for tachysystole.

@example
title: Treating hyperthyroidism in early pregnancy
problem: A woman at 7 weeks of gestation has Graves disease controlled with methimazole. How should treatment be managed?
step: Methimazole in the first trimester is associated with aplasia cutis, choanal atresia and other defects during organogenesis.
step: Propylthiouracil is preferred in the first trimester (risk of hepatotoxicity is why it is not used longer).
step: Switch to methimazole at the start of the second trimester in many protocols, keeping free T4 at the upper-normal range with the lowest dose.
step: Check TSH-receptor antibodies late in pregnancy because they cross the placenta and can cause fetal and neonatal thyrotoxicosis.

@mistakes
- Prescribing a PDE5 inhibitor to a patient taking nitrates.
- Giving estrogen alone to a woman with an intact uterus.
- Starting a GnRH agonist for prostate cancer without considering the initial flare.
- Forgetting that indomethacin can close the fetal ductus arteriosus, so use is limited to before 32 weeks and 48 hours.

choice: Reflex tachycardia leading to hyperkalemia

hint: Both drugs increase cGMP signaling in vascular smooth muscle.
solution: Nitrates release nitric oxide to increase cGMP, and PDE5 inhibitors prevent cGMP breakdown. The combination causes severe hypotension, so it is contraindicated.

@practice self
prompt: A 49-year-old woman has a 2-cm palpable breast mass. Outline the workup, and then, if biopsy shows ER-positive, HER2-negative invasive ductal carcinoma, describe endocrine therapy choices for premenopausal versus postmenopausal women and the main adverse effects.
hint: Use triple assessment for the workup, then match tamoxifen and aromatase inhibitors to menopausal status.
solution: Workup: history and clinical breast and nodal examination; diagnostic mammography with ultrasound; image-guided core needle biopsy; confirm concordance; pathology for histology, grade, ER, PR and HER2. Endocrine therapy: tamoxifen is used in premenopausal women (and may be used postmenopausally); aromatase inhibitors (anastrozole, letrozole, exemestane) are used in postmenopausal women, and in premenopausal women only with ovarian suppression. Tamoxifen adverse effects: endometrial cancer, venous thromboembolism, hot flashes. Aromatase inhibitors: bone loss, arthralgia, vaginal dryness; check bone density.
rubric: Gives the triple assessment workup including core needle biopsy and receptor testing.
rubric: Matches tamoxifen to premenopausal and aromatase inhibitors to postmenopausal women.
rubric: States the key adverse effects of each class.

@quiz mc
prompt: A woman at 28 weeks of gestation with preterm contractions is given a tocolytic. Two days later fetal ultrasound shows oligohydramnios and constriction of the ductus arteriosus. Which drug was most likely used?
choice*: Indomethacin
choice: Nifedipine
choice: Magnesium sulfate
choice: Betamethasone
choice: Penicillin G
hint: Which tocolytic inhibits prostaglandin synthesis?
solution: Indomethacin inhibits prostaglandins, which keep the ductus arteriosus open and support fetal urine output. Therefore it causes premature ductus closure and oligohydramnios, limiting its use to under 32 weeks and 48 hours.

@quiz mc
prompt: A 27-year-old woman with polycystic ovary syndrome is anovulatory and wants to conceive. Which mechanism describes clomiphene's effect on ovulation?
choice*: Blocks estrogen receptors in the hypothalamus, increasing GnRH, FSH and LH secretion

solution: Clomiphene antagonizes hypothalamic estrogen receptors; the brain perceives low estrogen and increases GnRH and gonadotropins, stimulating follicular growth. Letrozole is now commonly first-line in PCOS.
