@article
### Normal labor

Labor depends on the "three Ps": **power** (uterine contractions and maternal effort), **passenger** (fetal size, presentation, position) and **passage** (maternal pelvis and soft tissues). Oxytocin and prostaglandins increase contractions; cervical ripening involves collagen remodeling.

- **First stage**: onset of regular contractions to full dilation (10 cm). The **latent phase** has slow change; the **active phase** begins at about 6 cm, with faster dilation.
- **Second stage**: full dilation to delivery. Prolonged if over about 3 hours in a nulliparous woman without an epidural (4 hours with an epidural), or 2 hours (3 hours) in a multiparous woman.
- **Third stage**: delivery of the baby to delivery of the placenta (usually within 30 minutes). Signs of separation: gush of blood, lengthening cord, a rising uterine fundus.

**Cardinal movements** of vertex delivery: engagement, descent, flexion, internal rotation, extension, restitution (external rotation) and expulsion. The **Bishop score** (dilation, effacement, station, consistency, position of cervix) of 8 or more predicts successful induction.

### Fetal heart rate monitoring

A normal baseline is 110 to 160 beats per minute with moderate variability (6 to 25 bpm) and accelerations. Decelerations are defined by timing relative to contractions:

| Pattern | Cause | Shape | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early | Head compression | Gradual, mirrors contraction | None (benign) |
| Variable | Umbilical cord compression | Abrupt drop, variable timing | Change maternal position; amnioinfusion; deliver if persistent |
| Late | Uteroplacental insufficiency | Gradual, nadir after contraction peak | Lateral position, IV fluids, oxygen if hypoxemic, stop oxytocin, treat hypotension; deliver if persistent |

**Category I** tracings are normal, **category II** are indeterminate, and **category III** (absent variability with recurrent late or variable decelerations or bradycardia, or a sinusoidal pattern) predicts fetal acidemia and requires urgent resuscitation and delivery. **Tachysystole** (more than 5 contractions in 10 minutes averaged over 30 minutes), often from oxytocin or misoprostol, can cause late decelerations.

### Labor emergencies

**Shoulder dystocia** (anterior shoulder trapped behind the pubic symphysis) is more likely with diabetes, macrosomia and prior dystocia; the head retracts against the perineum ("turtle sign"). Call for help; **McRoberts maneuver** (hyperflex the maternal thighs) and **suprapubic pressure**, then deliver the posterior arm or rotational maneuvers. Complications: brachial plexus injury (Erb palsy, C5 to C6), clavicular fracture, hypoxic injury. Other emergencies: cord prolapse (elevate the presenting part, cesarean), uterine rupture (prior cesarean scar; sudden pain, fetal bradycardia, loss of station) and amniotic fluid embolism.

### Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH)

PPH is cumulative blood loss of 1000 mL or more, or loss with signs of hypovolemia, within 24 hours of birth. Remember the **four Ts**: **Tone** (uterine atony, about 70%), **Trauma** (lacerations, hematoma, inversion, rupture), **Tissue** (retained placenta or fragments, accreta) and **Thrombin** (coagulopathy). Treat with bimanual uterine massage, oxytocin, then second-line uterotonics: **methylergonovine** (contraindicated in hypertension and preeclampsia), **carboprost** (contraindicated in asthma) and **misoprostol**. **Tranexamic acid** within 3 hours of birth reduces death from bleeding. Add fluids and blood, a balloon tamponade, uterine artery embolization, and surgery (compression sutures, hysterectomy) as needed. Shock index (heart rate divided by systolic pressure) above about 0.9 to 1 signals significant hemorrhage.

### The puerperium

Lochia (rubra, then serosa, then alba) lasts weeks. **Postpartum endometritis** (fever, uterine tenderness, foul lochia, most often after cesarean) is treated with **clindamycin plus gentamicin**. **Mastitis** is treated with continued breastfeeding or pumping and an antistaphylococcal antibiotic. **Postpartum blues** peak around day 3 to 5 and resolve by 2 weeks; **postpartum depression** persists and is screened with the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale; **postpartum psychosis** (hallucinations, delusions, onset within days to weeks) is an emergency. **Sheehan syndrome** (pituitary infarction after hemorrhage) presents with failure to lactate and amenorrhea. Lactation: **prolactin** drives milk production, **oxytocin** drives let-down. **Postpartum preeclampsia** can appear up to 6 weeks after delivery with headache, visual symptoms, severe-range BP, or seizure; treat like antepartum disease with magnesium sulfate and antihypertensives. The **Apgar score** (0 to 2 each for appearance, pulse, grimace, activity, respiration) is recorded at 1 and 5 minutes.

@example
title: Atony after delivery
problem: Ten minutes after delivery of a 4.2-kg infant to a woman with a long oxytocin-augmented labor, there is brisk vaginal bleeding of about 1500 mL. The uterus is boggy and above the umbilicus. The placenta is intact. The woman is not hypertensive and has no asthma. What is the management?
step: The cause is tone (atony): macrosomia and prolonged oxytocin exposure are classic risks. Lacerations and retained tissue are checked next.
step: Begin bimanual uterine massage and give IV oxytocin; start fluids and prepare blood products.
step: If bleeding continues, add methylergonovine IM or carboprost IM (she has no contraindication to either) or misoprostol, and give tranexamic acid.
step: If medications fail, use an intrauterine balloon, then surgery. Check coagulation studies to exclude thrombin problems.

@example
title: Reading a monitor strip
problem: In the second stage, the fetal heart rate drops abruptly to 70 beats per minute at the start of each contraction and recovers rapidly. Variability is moderate. What is the cause and the first response?
step: Abrupt drops with variable timing relative to contractions are variable decelerations from cord compression.
step: Moderate variability reassures that the fetus is not acidotic yet.
step: Change maternal position; if oligohydramnios, amnioinfusion; check for cord prolapse.
step: If the tracing worsens (loss of variability, bradycardia), prepare for expedited delivery.

@mistakes
- Using methylergonovine in a hypertensive patient, or carboprost in an asthmatic patient.
- Calling every deceleration "fetal distress": variable decelerations with moderate variability are common.
- Pulling on the fetal head during shoulder dystocia rather than maneuvers.
- Overlooking preeclampsia after delivery. Severe headache and BP at least 160/110 in the first weeks postpartum need evaluation.
