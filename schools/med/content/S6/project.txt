overview: You will write a counseling brief for a clinic handout that compares contraceptive methods by mechanism, effectiveness and contraindications, applies the comparison to three supplied patients, and outlines routine prenatal screening by trimester. The brief must be accurate, written in plain language for patients and in precise language for clinicians.
goal: Compare at least six contraceptive methods by mechanism, typical-use effectiveness and contraindications.
goal: Apply US medical eligibility reasoning to choose methods for specific patients.
goal: Outline routine prenatal screening and counseling by trimester, with the reason for each test.
step: Read the three patient cases below. Case A: a 29-year-old with migraine with aura who wants a highly effective method with no daily task. Case B: a 38-year-old who smokes 20 cigarettes per day, has no other illness, and wants reversible contraception. Case C: a 22-year-old with PCOS, regular use of her phone alarm, and a wish to regulate periods and reduce acne, with no cardiovascular risks.
step: Build a table of at least six methods (combined pill, progestin-only implant, levonorgestrel IUD, copper IUD, DMPA injection, male condom, and optionally vasectomy or tubal occlusion) with columns for mechanism, typical-use first-year failure rate, one key adverse effect, and contraindications or cautions.
step: For each case, choose a first-choice method and a second-choice method, naming the contraindicated methods and explaining why using the eligibility reasoning from week 5.
step: Write a one-page patient-friendly script for Case A that explains how the chosen method works, what bleeding changes to expect, when to return, and what it does not protect against.

step: Add a short section on preeclampsia: the blood pressure and laboratory criteria, severe features, and why low-dose aspirin is recommended for high-risk women (start between 12 and 28 weeks, ideally before 16 weeks).
step: Review your brief against the rubric, check each rate and criterion against a reference such as StatPearls, and note where guidelines differ among organizations.

@review
### Week-by-week key ideas

- **Week 1, anatomy and development.** Müllerian (paramesonephric) ducts form tubes, uterus, cervix and upper vagina; Wolffian (mesonephric) ducts form epididymis, vas deferens and seminal vesicles; the urogenital sinus makes the prostate and lower vagina. The left gonadal vein enters the left renal vein; the uterine artery crosses over the ureter.
- **Week 2, sexual differentiation.** SRY leads to Sertoli cells and AMH (regresses Müllerian ducts); testosterone drives Wolffian ducts; DHT drives external genitalia. Know complete androgen insensitivity, 5-alpha-reductase deficiency, 21-hydroxylase deficiency, Turner and Klinefelter syndromes.
- **Week 3, male physiology.** LH to Leydig cells (testosterone); FSH to Sertoli cells (inhibin B). Torsion is a surgical emergency; seminoma versus yolk sac markers; BPH versus prostate cancer zones.
- **Week 4, menstrual cycle.** Two-cell model; estradiol positive feedback causes the LH surge; luteal phase is a constant 14 days; PCOS by Rotterdam criteria; the first test in amenorrhea is hCG.
- **Week 5, fertility and contraception.** Infertility is 12 months (6 months at 35 or older); typical-use failure rates; estrogen contraindications; emergency contraception options.
- **Week 6, pregnancy physiology.** hCG, hPL, progesterone; creatinine falls and PaCO2 falls; prenatal screening schedule; Rh immune globulin at 28 weeks.
- **Week 7, complications.** Ectopic pregnancy, moles, previa versus abruption, preeclampsia and severe features, magnesium sulfate toxicity.
- **Week 8, labor and postpartum.** Cardinal movements, fetal heart rate decelerations, shoulder dystocia, four Ts of hemorrhage, postpartum preeclampsia.
- **Week 9, infections.** Vaginitis by pH and microscopy; chlamydia, gonorrhea, PID regimens; syphilis stages and benzathine penicillin; HPV vaccine.
- **Week 10, gynecologic neoplasia.** Cervical screening and colposcopy; postmenopausal bleeding needs sampling; fibroid versus adenomyosis; ovarian tumor markers.
- **Week 11, breast disease.** Fibroadenoma, papilloma, DCIS, invasive ductal and lobular carcinoma, receptor status, workup of a mass with triple assessment and core biopsy.
- **Week 12, pharmacology.** SERMs, aromatase inhibitors, GnRH analogs, PDE5 inhibitors and nitrates, tocolytics, uterotonics, teratogens.

### Common errors

- Treating suspected torsion with antibiotics and delay.
- Giving estrogen-containing contraception when it is contraindicated.
- Calling a normal nonpregnant creatinine or PaCO2 normal in pregnancy.
- Doing a digital cervical examination before an ultrasound in third-trimester bleeding.
- Using methylergonovine in hypertension or carboprost in asthma.
- Overlooking postmenopausal bleeding as a sign of endometrial cancer.

@exam num
prompt: A first-trimester combined screening test for trisomy 21 has a detection rate (sensitivity) of 85% and a false-positive rate of 5%. In a population in which the prevalence of trisomy 21 is 0.2% (1 in 500), what is the positive predictive value of a positive screen, as a decimal rounded to three decimals?
answer: 0.033
check: (0.85 * 0.002) / (0.85 * 0.002 + 0.05 * (1 - 0.002))
tol: 0.001
hint: Imagine 100,000 pregnancies: count the affected ones, then the true positives and false positives.
hint: Affected = 200; true positives = 170; unaffected = 99,800, false positives = 4,990.
solution: Among 100,000 pregnancies, 200 are affected and 170 are detected. Of 99,800 unaffected, 5% (4,990) screen positive. PPV $= 170/(170 + 4990) \approx 0.033$. A positive screen therefore needs diagnostic testing (CVS or amniocentesis) before any decision.

prompt: A 27-year-old woman at 7 weeks of amenorrhea has mild right lower quadrant pain and spotting. She is stable. Serum hCG is 2900 mIU/mL, transvaginal ultrasound shows an empty uterus with a 2.5-cm right adnexal mass, no cardiac activity and no free pelvic fluid. Hemoglobin, creatinine and liver tests are normal, and she is reliable for follow-up. Which is the best management?
choice*: Intramuscular methotrexate with serial hCG monitoring
choice: Dilation and curettage
choice: Expectant management with a repeat ultrasound in 6 weeks
choice: Misoprostol and mifepristone
choice: Immediate laparotomy
hint: She is stable, with unruptured ectopic pregnancy and a low hCG.
solution: An empty uterus with an adnexal mass above the discriminatory hCG level is ectopic pregnancy. A stable patient with an unruptured mass, hCG below about 5000 mIU/mL, no cardiac activity and normal renal and liver function is a candidate for methotrexate, with hCG follow-up to zero. Surgery is for instability, rupture or failure of medical therapy.

@exam self
prompt: A 30-year-old woman presents at 12 weeks of gestation for her first prenatal visit. She is Rh-negative, has chronic hypertension treated with lisinopril, and had preeclampsia in a previous pregnancy. Write a prenatal plan that addresses: the medication problem, preeclampsia prevention, screening tests with their timing, Rh management, and the warning symptoms she should report.
hint: Go through the drug list first, then risk-based prevention, then the schedule of tests by gestational age.
solution: Stop lisinopril (ACE inhibitors cause fetal renal dysgenesis, oligohydramnios and pulmonary hypoplasia in later pregnancy) and switch to labetalol or nifedipine, with BP goals per current guidance. Start low-dose aspirin 81 mg daily (study dose; verify before clinical use), beginning at 12 weeks and ideally before 16 weeks, because of prior preeclampsia and chronic hypertension. Baseline labs: CBC, creatinine, liver tests and urine protein-to-creatinine ratio, in addition to routine first-visit tests (blood type and antibody screen, infections, urine culture). Offer aneuploidy screening (cell-free DNA or first-trimester screen with quad screen later), anatomy ultrasound at 18 to 22 weeks, and glucose screening at 24 to 28 weeks (earlier if risk factors). Rh immune globulin at 28 weeks and within 72 hours after delivery of an Rh-positive infant, and after any bleeding or invasive procedure. GBS culture at 36 0/7 to 37 6/7 weeks. Vaccines: influenza, Tdap at 27 to 36 weeks, and others as indicated. Warning symptoms: severe headache, visual changes, right upper quadrant pain, shortness of breath, sudden swelling, bleeding, decreased fetal movement, leaking fluid or regular contractions before 37 weeks.
rubric: Replaces the ACE inhibitor with a safe agent and gives the reason.
rubric: Starts aspirin 81 mg (study dose) at 12 weeks or later, ideally before 16 weeks, for preeclampsia prevention.
rubric: Gives correct timing for Rh immune globulin (28 weeks and within 72 hours of delivery).
rubric: Schedules aneuploidy screening, anatomy ultrasound, glucose screening and GBS culture correctly.
rubric: Lists key warning symptoms such as headache, visual change and right upper quadrant pain.
