@article
Transfusion is a transplant of living tissue, and most of its hazards come from immunology and volume. Know the blood groups, the products and the reactions.

### ABO and Rh

People make naturally occurring IgM antibodies against the A and B antigens they lack. Group **O** has anti-A and anti-B; **A** has anti-B; **B** has anti-A; **AB** has neither.

- **Red cells:** O is the universal donor (no A or B antigen); AB is the universal recipient.
- **Plasma** is the mirror image: AB plasma is the universal donor (no antibodies), and group O recipients can receive any ABO plasma.

The **Rh (D) antigen** is the major other determinant; Rh-negative people exposed to D-positive cells can form anti-D IgG. Rh-negative girls and women of childbearing potential should receive Rh-negative red cells. **Anti-D immune globulin (RhIG)** is given to Rh-negative mothers at about 28 weeks and within 72 hours of delivering an Rh-positive infant (and after bleeding events) to prevent sensitization. Hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn from anti-D (IgG crosses the placenta) can cause hydrops; ABO incompatibility (O mother, A or B baby) is usually milder.

Pre-transfusion testing: **type and screen** (ABO/Rh plus antibody screen, an indirect Coombs); **crossmatch** (donor red cells mixed with recipient serum). In an emergency, uncrossmatched O red cells (O-negative for females of childbearing age) are given.

### Blood products

| Product | Contains | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Packed red cells | Red cells | Symptomatic anemia, hemorrhage |
| Platelets | Platelets | Thrombocytopenia with bleeding or prophylaxis |
| Fresh frozen plasma (FFP) | All coagulation factors | Multiple factor deficiency with bleeding, TTP (plasma exchange) |
| Cryoprecipitate | Fibrinogen, factor VIII, vWF, XIII | Hypofibrinogenemia, DIC with bleeding |
| Prothrombin complex concentrate | Factors II, VII, IX, X | Warfarin reversal with serious bleeding |

Common thresholds (restrictive strategy): transfuse red cells for **hemoglobin below 7 g/dL** in stable hospitalized adults, below 7.5 g/dL for cardiac surgery, and below 8 g/dL for orthopedic surgery or preexisting cardiovascular disease (AABB 2023; evidence in acute myocardial infarction is evolving). Each unit raises hemoglobin by about 1 g/dL (hematocrit about 3 points) in a 70 kg adult without ongoing loss. Prophylactic platelets for count below 10,000/µL; higher thresholds (50,000/µL) for active bleeding or major procedures. In massive hemorrhage give red cells, plasma and platelets in balanced ratios (about 1:1:1), consider tranexamic acid early in trauma, and watch for **hypocalcemia** (citrate), hyperkalemia and hypothermia.

Special modifications: **leukoreduction** (reduces febrile reactions, CMV and HLA alloimmunization), **irradiation** (prevents graft-versus-host disease in immunocompromised and related-donor recipients), **washing** (for severe allergic reactions or IgA deficiency).

### Transfusion reactions

| Reaction | Timing | Mechanism | Features | Management / prevention |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acute hemolytic | Minutes to hours | ABO-incompatible red cells (clerical error) with complement-mediated lysis | Fever, chills, flank or infusion-site pain, hypotension, hemoglobinuria, DIC; DAT positive | Stop transfusion, keep IV line open with saline, verify identity, send blood and urine for hemolysis testing and DAT, support BP and renal output |
| Febrile non-hemolytic | During or within hours | Recipient antibodies and donor-cell cytokines | Fever, chills, no hemolysis | Stop, exclude hemolysis and sepsis, give acetaminophen; leukoreduce |
| Allergic (urticarial) | During | IgE to donor plasma proteins | Hives, itching | Pause, antihistamine, restart if resolved |
| Anaphylactic | Seconds to minutes | Anti-IgA in IgA-deficient recipient | Hypotension, bronchospasm, angioedema | Epinephrine; use IgA-deficient or washed products |
| TRALI | Within 6 hours | Donor anti-HLA or anti-neutrophil antibodies injure lung capillaries | Noncardiogenic pulmonary edema, hypoxia, fever, normal or low BNP | Supportive ventilation; no diuretics |
| TACO | During or within 6 hours | Volume overload | Dyspnea, hypertension, JVD, high BNP | Stop, diuretic, slower transfusion |
| Delayed hemolytic | 3 to 14 days | Anamnestic response to minor antigens (Kidd, Rh) | Falling hemoglobin, jaundice, positive DAT | Monitor; transfuse antigen-negative units |
| Transfusion-associated GVHD | 1 to 6 weeks | Donor lymphocytes attack host | Fever, rash, diarrhea, pancytopenia | Prevent by irradiation |
| Bacterial contamination | Rapid | Contaminated product (platelets most often) | Fever, rigors, shock | Stop, culture product and patient, broad antibiotics |

TRALI and TACO both cause pulmonary edema; distinguish them by cardiac features: TACO has high BNP, hypertension and response to diuresis; TRALI does not.

### Quantifying platelet response

The **corrected count increment (CCI)** compares response with dose and body size:

$$\text{CCI} = \frac{(\text{post-count} - \text{pre-count})(/\mu\text{L}) \times \text{BSA}(\text{m}^2)}{\text{platelets transfused}(\times 10^{11})}.$$

A CCI above about 7,500 at 1 hour suggests an adequate response; poor responses suggest alloimmunization, fever, sepsis, splenomegaly or DIC.

@example
title: Platelet response
problem: A patient with BSA 1.8 m$^2$ has a pre-transfusion platelet count of 8,000/µL. One hour after receiving $3 \times 10^{11}$ platelets, the count is 38,000/µL. Calculate the CCI and interpret it.
step: Increment $= 38000 - 8000 = 30000$/µL.
step: $\text{CCI} = 30000 \times 1.8 / 3 = 18000$.
step: A CCI far above 7,500 shows a good response, so alloimmunization or consumption is not suggested.

@mistakes
- Believing group AB is the universal donor for plasma and red cells alike; AB is the universal donor only for plasma (red cells: O).
- Giving diuretics for TRALI or failing to stop the transfusion in a suspected hemolytic reaction.
- Forgetting irradiation for immunocompromised recipients, or leukoreduction to prevent febrile reactions.
- Treating urticaria as anaphylaxis, or the reverse: hives alone allow pause, antihistamine and restart; hypotension and wheeze require epinephrine.
