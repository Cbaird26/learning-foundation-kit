@article
Lymphomas are clonal malignancies of lymphocytes, usually presenting as painless lymphadenopathy, sometimes with **B symptoms**: fever above 38 C, drenching night sweats and weight loss of more than 10 percent over 6 months. Diagnosis needs an **excisional lymph node biopsy** (fine-needle aspiration is inadequate for architecture), then staging with PET-CT.

### Hodgkin lymphoma

Hodgkin lymphoma (HL) arises from germinal-center B cells. The diagnostic cell is the **Reed-Sternberg cell** (large, binucleate, "owl-eye" nucleoli), CD15 positive and CD30 positive, surrounded by a mixed reactive infiltrate. Bimodal age peaks (young adults and older adults). Spread is **contiguous** from node group to node group, usually above the diaphragm, often with a mediastinal mass. Features: pruritus, alcohol-induced node pain, Pel-Ebstein fever (rare).

Subtypes of classical HL: **nodular sclerosis** (most common; young women; lacunar cells; mediastinum), **mixed cellularity** (EBV-associated, older adults and HIV), lymphocyte-rich and lymphocyte-depleted. **Nodular lymphocyte-predominant HL** has CD20-positive popcorn cells and is CD15/CD30 negative.

**Ann Arbor stage:** I (one node region), II (two or more regions on the same side of the diaphragm), III (both sides of the diaphragm), IV (diffuse extranodal involvement, such as marrow or liver). Suffix **A** (no B symptoms) or **B**.

Treatment: **ABVD** (doxorubicin, bleomycin, vinblastine, dacarbazine) with or without radiation; brentuximab vedotin (anti-CD30) and PD-1 inhibitors for advanced or relapsed disease. Most patients are cured, so long-term toxicities matter: doxorubicin cardiomyopathy, bleomycin pulmonary fibrosis, secondary malignancies, hypothyroidism after neck radiation.

### Non-Hodgkin lymphomas (NHL)

NHL is heterogeneous (most are B-cell). Typical features: **noncontiguous** spread, frequent extranodal disease, no Reed-Sternberg cells. Associations: immunosuppression and HIV, EBV, H. pylori, hepatitis C, HTLV-1.

| Lymphoma | Features | Genetics | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diffuse large B-cell (DLBCL) | Most common NHL in adults; aggressive, rapidly growing mass | BCL2, BCL6, MYC variations | R-CHOP (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, prednisone) |
| Follicular | Indolent, painless waxing and waning nodes; can transform to DLBCL | **t(14;18) BCL2** | Observe if asymptomatic; rituximab-based therapy |
| Mantle cell | Older men, CD5 positive, GI involvement | **t(11;14) cyclin D1**; CD5+ | Chemoimmunotherapy, BTK inhibitors |
| Burkitt | Fastest-growing, "starry sky" histology; jaw (endemic, EBV) or abdominal (sporadic) mass; Ki-67 near 100 percent; high risk of tumor lysis | **t(8;14) c-MYC** | Intensive short-course chemotherapy with TLS prophylaxis |
| Marginal zone (MALT) | Gastric, associated with H. pylori | t(11;18) | Treat H. pylori; rituximab if needed |
| Mycosis fungoides / Sezary | Cutaneous T-cell; Pautrier microabscesses | CD4 T cells | Skin-directed therapy |

CNS lymphoma in AIDS (EBV-driven) appears as a ring-enhancing lesion; consider it alongside toxoplasmosis.

### Hodgkin versus non-Hodgkin: the deliverable comparison

| | Hodgkin | Non-Hodgkin |
|---|---|---|
| Hallmark cell | Reed-Sternberg (CD15+, CD30+) | None; clonal B, T or NK cells |
| Spread | Contiguous | Noncontiguous |
| Extranodal disease | Uncommon | Common |
| Age | Bimodal | Increases with age (except Burkitt and ALCL) |
| B symptoms | Common | Less common (more with aggressive types) |
| Typical treatment | ABVD plus or minus radiation | R-CHOP and others |
| Cure rate | High | Varies by subtype |

### Plasma-cell disorders

**Multiple myeloma** is clonal plasma-cell proliferation producing monoclonal protein (M protein: IgG most often, then IgA) or light chains. Pathology explains the features, summarized by **CRAB**: **C**alcium elevation (bone resorption), **R**enal failure (light-chain cast nephropathy), **A**nemia (marrow replacement), **B**one lesions (osteolytic "punched-out" lesions via RANKL activation and osteoblast inhibition, pathologic fractures, back pain, cord compression). Other features: recurrent bacterial infections (hypogammaglobulinemia of normal antibodies), rouleaux on smear, high total protein with a **globulin gap** (total protein minus albumin above 4 g/dL), and a **low anion gap** (cationic IgG). Diagnosis: SPEP and immunofixation (serum and urine), serum free light chains, at least 10 percent clonal plasma cells on marrow biopsy (or a plasmacytoma) together with myeloma-defining events (CRAB or specific biomarkers such as marrow plasma cells 60 percent or more or an involved-to-uninvolved free light chain ratio of 100 or more, or more than one focal MRI lesion); use whole-body low-dose CT, PET-CT or MRI because bone scans miss lytic lesions. Treatment combines a proteasome inhibitor (bortezomib), an immunomodulatory drug (lenalidomide) and an anti-CD38 antibody (daratumumab) with dexamethasone, with autologous stem cell transplant for eligible patients and bone-modifying agents (zoledronic acid or denosumab).

- **MGUS:** M protein below 3 g/dL, less than 10 percent marrow plasma cells, no end-organ damage; about 1 percent per year progress. Observe.
- **Smoldering myeloma:** M protein 3 g/dL or more or 10 to 60 percent plasma cells, no myeloma-defining events.
- **Waldenstrom macroglobulinemia:** lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma secreting **IgM** (MYD88 L265P) causing hyperviscosity (visual change, headache, bleeding) and no lytic bone lesions; treat symptomatic hyperviscosity with plasmapheresis.
- **AL amyloidosis:** light chains deposit as amyloid (Congo red apple-green birefringence) causing nephrotic syndrome, restrictive cardiomyopathy, macroglossia and neuropathy.

@example
title: Does this patient have myeloma?
problem: A 66-year-old man has back pain, Hb 9.4 g/dL, creatinine 2.4 mg/dL, total calcium 11.9 mg/dL with albumin 3.0 g/dL and total protein 9.8 g/dL. Smear shows rouleaux. Outline the diagnosis.
step: Compute the globulin gap: $9.8 - 3.0 = 6.8$ g/dL, far above 4: suspect a paraprotein.
step: He has hypercalcemia, renal injury, anemia and bone pain: all four CRAB features.
step: Send SPEP with immunofixation, serum free light chains, and a marrow biopsy; image the skeleton with whole-body low-dose CT or PET-CT.
step: If at least 10 percent clonal plasma cells and CRAB features are present, multiple myeloma is diagnosed; begin a proteasome inhibitor-based triplet regimen with daratumumab and bone-protective therapy, with hydration and treatment for hypercalcemia.

@mistakes
- Using FNA or a core with inadequate architecture to diagnose lymphoma instead of excisional biopsy.
- Using a bone scan to evaluate myeloma bone disease (lytic lesions are not seen).
- Ordering only SPEP and missing light-chain-only myeloma (use free light chains or urine).
- Treating every patient with a monoclonal protein as myeloma; MGUS needs observation.
