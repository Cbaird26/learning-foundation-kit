@article
Every blood cell descends from a **hematopoietic stem cell (HSC)** in the bone marrow. HSCs self-renew and also give rise to progenitors that commit step by step to a lineage. This lesson builds the map you will use for the rest of the course: where cells come from, what regulates them, and how the complete blood count (CBC) and the smear let you read the marrow from a tube of blood.

### The hierarchy

The HSC gives rise to a **common myeloid progenitor** and a **common lymphoid progenitor**. The myeloid branch splits into a **megakaryocyte-erythroid progenitor** (red cells and platelets) and a **granulocyte-monocyte progenitor** (neutrophils, monocytes, and through other branches eosinophils and basophils). The lymphoid branch yields B cells, T cells and NK cells. Erythroid maturation runs proerythroblast, basophilic, polychromatic and orthochromatic normoblasts, then the **reticulocyte**, which leaves the marrow, spends about one day maturing in blood, and loses its remaining RNA to become a mature red cell with a lifespan of about 120 days.

### Growth factors

- **Erythropoietin (EPO)** is made by peritubular interstitial cells of the kidney. Hypoxia stabilizes HIF, which raises EPO transcription. Chronic kidney disease therefore causes a normocytic anemia with inappropriately low EPO.
- **Thrombopoietin (TPO)** is made mainly by the liver and drives megakaryocytes; it is cleared by binding to platelets.
- **G-CSF** and **GM-CSF** drive neutrophil and monocyte production; **IL-5** drives eosinophils; **IL-3** and **stem cell factor (SCF)** act on early progenitors.

Adults make blood in the axial skeleton (vertebrae, pelvis, sternum, ribs, skull) and proximal femurs. When marrow is replaced by fibrosis or must expand (myelofibrosis, severe thalassemia), the liver and spleen resume blood production, called **extramedullary hematopoiesis**.

### The CBC and red-cell indices

Anemia is a low hemoglobin: below 13 g/dL in men and below 12 g/dL in non-pregnant women (WHO thresholds). The indices describe the cells:

$$\text{MCV} = \frac{\text{Hct}(\%) \times 10}{\text{RBC}(10^6/\mu\text{L})}\ \text{fL},\qquad \text{MCH} = \frac{\text{Hb}(\text{g/dL}) \times 10}{\text{RBC}}\ \text{pg},\qquad \text{MCHC} = \frac{\text{Hb}}{\text{Hct}} \times 100\ \text{g/dL}.$$

Normal MCV is about 80 to 100 fL. The **RDW** measures variation in cell size (anisocytosis); it is high when two populations coexist, as in early iron deficiency.

### Reticulocytes: is the marrow responding?

The reticulocyte percentage is misleading when the hematocrit is low, so correct it:

$$\text{Corrected reticulocyte count} = \text{retic}(\%) \times \frac{\text{patient Hct}}{\text{normal Hct (about 45)}}.$$

The **reticulocyte production index (RPI)** further divides by a maturation factor (about 1.5 to 2.5, larger with lower Hct, because immature reticulocytes are released early). An RPI above 2 indicates an appropriate marrow response (hemolysis, acute blood loss, treated deficiency). An RPI below 2 means a **hypoproliferative** anemia (iron, B12 or folate deficiency, marrow failure, kidney disease, inflammation).

First-pass framework: classify by **MCV** (micro-, normo-, macrocytic), then by **reticulocyte response**, then confirm with smear and targeted tests.

### Morphology you must recognize

| Finding | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hypochromic microcytes, pencil cells | Iron deficiency |
| Target cells (codocytes) | Thalassemia, HbC, liver disease, asplenia |
| Spherocytes (no central pallor) | Hereditary spherocytosis, warm autoimmune hemolysis |
| Schistocytes (fragments, helmet cells) | Microangiopathic or mechanical hemolysis (TTP, DIC, mechanical valve) |
| Teardrop cells (dacrocytes) | Myelofibrosis, marrow infiltration |
| Bite cells, Heinz bodies | G6PD deficiency (oxidant injury) |
| Howell-Jolly bodies | Absent or hypofunctioning spleen |
| Basophilic stippling | Lead poisoning, thalassemia |
| Rouleaux | Increased immunoglobulins, as in myeloma or inflammation |
| Hypersegmented neutrophils (5 or more lobes) | Megaloblastic anemia (B12 or folate deficiency) |
| Auer rods | Myeloblasts of acute myeloid leukemia |
| Smudge cells | Chronic lymphocytic leukemia |
| Polychromasia | Reticulocytes: increased red-cell production |

### Putting it together

A smear is worth a thousand indices. For example, a normal MCV with a high RDW can hide coexisting microcytic and macrocytic populations, and an automated count can falsely report thrombocytopenia from platelet clumping, which the smear reveals.

@example
title: Indices and marrow response from a CBC
problem: A patient has Hb 9.0 g/dL, Hct 28%, RBC $3.5 \times 10^6/\mu$L and a reticulocyte count of 4%. Calculate MCV, MCH, MCHC and the corrected reticulocyte count, and classify the anemia.
step: MCV $= 28 \times 10 / 3.5 = 80$ fL, at the lower limit of normal, so the anemia is borderline normocytic.
step: MCH $= 9.0 \times 10 / 3.5 \approx 25.7$ pg, which is slightly low, and MCHC $= 9.0/28 \times 100 \approx 32.1$ g/dL, at the lower limit of normal.
step: Corrected reticulocyte count $= 4 \times 28/45 \approx 2.5$ percent.
step: At a hematocrit near 28 the maturation factor is about 1.5 to 2, so the RPI is about $2.5/2 \approx 1.3$ to $2.5/1.5 \approx 1.7$, below 2. The marrow response is inadequate for the degree of anemia.
step: Conclusion: a borderline normocytic, hypoproliferative anemia directs you toward chronic disease, kidney disease or early nutritional deficiency rather than hemolysis or blood loss.

@mistakes
- Using the raw reticulocyte percentage without correcting for the low hematocrit, which overestimates marrow response.
- Reading the MCV alone: mixed deficiencies can produce a normal MCV with a high RDW.
- Treating schistocytes as artifact; they signal a mechanical or microangiopathic process that needs urgent evaluation.
- Forgetting that the spleen removes inclusions, so Howell-Jolly bodies point to asplenia, not to a marrow disorder.

@quiz mc
prompt: A smear from a 35-year-old woman shows numerous helmet cells and triangular fragments, with few platelets. These findings indicate which process?
