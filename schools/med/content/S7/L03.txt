@article
Macrocytosis is an MCV above 100 fL. The first split is whether the macrocytes are **megaloblastic** (impaired DNA synthesis, with asynchronous nuclear and cytoplasmic maturation) or **non-megaloblastic**. The second skill in this lesson is the normocytic anemias, where the reticulocyte response is the key decision point.

### Megaloblastic anemia: the biochemistry

DNA synthesis needs thymidylate (dTMP), made from dUMP by thymidylate synthase using methylene-tetrahydrofolate. Two vitamins feed this pathway:

- **Folate** is the one-carbon donor. It is found in leafy greens, absorbed in the proximal small intestine, and body stores last only about 3 to 4 months.
- **Vitamin B12 (cobalamin)** is a cofactor for methionine synthase, which converts homocysteine to methionine while regenerating tetrahydrofolate from methyl-folate (otherwise folate is "trapped"). B12 is also a cofactor for **methylmalonyl-CoA mutase**, which converts methylmalonyl-CoA to succinyl-CoA. B12 stores last years.

Rapidly dividing cells fail first: marrow cells make large, poorly divided precursors, many die in the marrow (ineffective erythropoiesis, causing mild hemolysis features such as high LDH and indirect bilirubin), and neutrophils become **hypersegmented** (5 or more lobes). Pancytopenia can result.

### B12 deficiency

Absorption chain: acid and pepsin free B12 from food, it binds haptocorrin, pancreatic proteases release it, it binds **intrinsic factor** (from gastric parietal cells), and the complex is absorbed in the **terminal ileum**. Causes:

- **Pernicious anemia**: autoimmune atrophic gastritis with antibodies to parietal cells and intrinsic factor (anti-intrinsic factor antibody is more specific). It is associated with other autoimmune disease and with increased gastric cancer risk.
- Gastrectomy, terminal ileal disease or resection (Crohn disease), fish tapeworm (Diphyllobothrium latum), strict vegan diet, long-term metformin.

Neurologic features occur with B12 but not folate deficiency: paresthesias, loss of vibration and proprioception, ataxia, and **subacute combined degeneration** (dorsal columns and lateral corticospinal tracts), plus cognitive changes and optic neuropathy. These can occur with a normal hemoglobin.

### Folate deficiency

Causes: poor intake (alcohol use disorder, malnutrition), increased demand (pregnancy, hemolysis), malabsorption (celiac disease), and drugs that block folate metabolism (methotrexate, trimethoprim, phenytoin). Folate also prevents neural tube defects, so supplementation is recommended before and during early pregnancy.

### Laboratory localization

| Test | B12 deficiency | Folate deficiency |
|---|---|---|
| Methylmalonic acid (MMA) | High | Normal |
| Homocysteine | High | High |
| Serum B12 | Low (borderline values need MMA) | Normal |
| Serum or red-cell folate | Normal or high | Low |

Because MMA rises only when B12 is lacking, **normal MMA with high homocysteine points to folate deficiency**. Treat B12 deficiency with cyanocobalamin (intramuscular, or high-dose oral for adherence in non-severe malabsorption); a reticulocyte surge occurs in about 5 to 8 days, and early potassium may fall as new cells form. **Never treat an unrecognized B12 deficiency with folic acid alone**: it improves the anemia but allows the neurologic damage to progress.

### Non-megaloblastic macrocytosis

Round macrocytes with normal neutrophil segmentation suggest: **alcohol use**, **liver disease** (target cells), **hypothyroidism**, **reticulocytosis** (young cells are larger), myelodysplastic syndromes, and drugs interfering with DNA synthesis (hydroxyurea, zidovudine, azathioprine, methotrexate). Check TSH, liver tests, reticulocytes and smear before assuming a vitamin deficiency.

### Normocytic anemias: use the reticulocyte index

If the RPI is above 2, think hemolysis or recent blood loss (next lesson for hemolysis). If it is below 2, the anemia is hypoproliferative:

- **Anemia of chronic disease** and **chronic kidney disease** (low EPO).
- **Early iron deficiency** or mixed deficiency.
- **Marrow failure or infiltration:** aplastic anemia, pure red cell aplasia, myelophthisis (teardrop cells and nucleated red cells from marrow replaced by fibrosis, tumor or granulomas).

### Aplastic anemia

Aplastic anemia is **pancytopenia with a hypocellular, fat-replaced marrow** without abnormal infiltrate or fibrosis. Most cases are idiopathic, driven by T-cell-mediated destruction of stem cells; others follow drugs (chloramphenicol, carbamazepine), radiation, viral hepatitis, or are inherited (Fanconi anemia, a DNA crosslink repair defect with short stature, radial-ray anomalies, café-au-lait spots and a positive chromosome breakage test; telomere diseases). Severe disease has a neutrophil count below 500/µL with additional criteria. Patients have no splenomegaly. Management is supportive (irradiated, leukoreduced products, infection treatment) plus definitive therapy: **allogeneic stem cell transplantation** for younger patients with a matched donor, otherwise **immunosuppression** (antithymocyte globulin plus cyclosporine, with eltrombopag). **Parvovirus B19** can cause transient red-cell aplasia (aplastic crisis) in patients with chronic hemolysis.

@example
title: Localizing a megaloblastic anemia
problem: A 68-year-old woman has fatigue, numbness of both feet, Hb 8.0 g/dL, MCV 114 fL and hypersegmented neutrophils. Serum B12 is borderline low; MMA is elevated. What is the diagnosis, what confirms its cause, and what is the treatment?
step: Macrocytosis with hypersegmented neutrophils is megaloblastic. Neurologic symptoms plus elevated MMA localize to B12 deficiency (folate deficiency spares MMA and the nervous system).
step: The commonest cause in an older woman is pernicious anemia. Confirm with anti-intrinsic factor antibodies (and gastric biopsy or gastrin if needed); look for other autoimmune disease.
step: Treat with cyanocobalamin replacement, intramuscular initially given the neurologic involvement, and continue it for life if the cause is not reversible.
step: Do not use folic acid alone: the anemia would improve but the neuropathy could worsen.

@mistakes
- Giving folate to a patient with unrecognized B12 deficiency, which masks the anemia while neurologic damage progresses.
- Relying on a borderline-normal serum B12 without checking MMA when suspicion is high.
- Missing alcohol, hypothyroidism or liver disease in a macrocytic patient with a normal smear and normal B12.
- Calling aplastic anemia in a pancytopenic patient without a marrow biopsy to exclude leukemia, myelodysplasia and infiltration.
