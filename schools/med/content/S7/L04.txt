@article
**Hemolysis** is premature destruction of red cells (lifespan under 120 days). The marrow can raise output about sevenfold, so anemia appears only when destruction outpaces production. This lesson gives you a systematic workup, then the diseases.

### Confirming hemolysis

| Marker | Finding in hemolysis |
|---|---|
| Reticulocyte count / RPI | High (RPI above 2) with polychromasia |
| LDH | High (released from red cells) |
| Haptoglobin | Low (binds free hemoglobin and is cleared) |
| Indirect (unconjugated) bilirubin | High; urine bilirubin absent |
| Smear | Spherocytes, schistocytes, bite cells, sickle cells, etc. |

Haptoglobin is most sensitive in **intravascular** hemolysis, where free hemoglobin also appears in plasma and urine (hemoglobinuria) and hemosiderinuria follows. **Extravascular** hemolysis occurs in splenic and hepatic macrophages: splenomegaly, jaundice and pigment gallstones. Next, the **direct antiglobulin test (DAT, direct Coombs)** detects antibody or complement on red cells: positive means immune hemolysis; negative points to membrane, enzyme, hemoglobin or mechanical causes. Bilirubin is not the only clue: chronic hemolysis also depletes folate, so give folic acid.

### Intrinsic (inherited) causes

**Hereditary spherocytosis (HS):** mostly autosomal dominant defects in ankyrin, spectrin, band 3 or protein 4.2 (vertical membrane-skeleton linkage). Cells lose membrane, become spherocytes and are culled by the spleen. Findings: **high MCHC**, spherocytes without central pallor, splenomegaly, gallstones, family history, negative DAT, eosin-5-maleimide (EMA) binding test reduced (osmotic fragility is the older test). Treat with folate; splenectomy for severe disease (give pneumococcal, meningococcal and Haemophilus vaccines first).

**G6PD deficiency:** X-linked recessive, most common red-cell enzyme defect, protective against malaria. G6PD supplies NADPH to regenerate reduced glutathione, which detoxifies peroxides. Oxidant stress (infection most commonly, fava beans, and drugs such as primaquine, dapsone, nitrofurantoin, rasburicase and methylene blue) denatures hemoglobin into **Heinz bodies**; splenic macrophages remove the inclusions, leaving **bite cells**. Episodic hemolysis begins 1 to 3 days after exposure with dark urine; the enzyme assay may be falsely normal during the acute episode because older, deficient cells are gone and reticulocytes have high G6PD levels, so test after recovery.

**Pyruvate kinase deficiency:** autosomal recessive; low ATP from glycolysis makes rigid cells.

### Acquired immune and complement causes

- **Warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA):** IgG on red cells, extravascular (spleen), spherocytes, DAT positive for IgG. Associated with SLE, CLL and lymphoma, and drugs (methyldopa). Treat with glucocorticoids, then rituximab or splenectomy for relapse.
- **Cold agglutinin disease:** IgM binds at low temperature and fixes complement (DAT positive for C3), after Mycoplasma pneumoniae or EBV infection or with lymphoproliferative disease; acrocyanosis in cold. Keep warm; rituximab; steroids are usually not effective.
- **Paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH):** acquired PIGA mutation in a stem cell removes GPI anchors, so cells lack **CD55 and CD59** and are lysed by complement. Hemoglobinuria, thrombosis (hepatic veins) and cytopenias. Diagnose by flow cytometry for CD55/CD59 (or FLAER). Treat with C5 inhibitors (eculizumab, ravulizumab) after meningococcal vaccination.
- **Microangiopathic hemolytic anemia:** schistocytes with a negative DAT in TTP, HUS, DIC and mechanical valves (see the coagulation lessons).

### Hemoglobinopathies and sickle cell disease

A glutamate-to-valine substitution at position 6 of the beta chain (**HbS**) causes deoxygenated hemoglobin to polymerize, sickling the cell. Triggers are hypoxia, acidosis, dehydration and infection; **HbF** protects. Sickled cells are rigid and adhere to endothelium, causing vaso-occlusion and hemolysis.

Complications by organ:
- Pain crisis, dactylitis in infants, avascular necrosis of the femoral head.
- **Acute chest syndrome:** chest pain, fever, hypoxia and a new infiltrate; the leading cause of death. Treat with oxygen, analgesia, antibiotics, incentive spirometry and transfusion when severe.
- Autosplenectomy in adults (**Howell-Jolly bodies**) and risk of *Streptococcus pneumoniae* sepsis; children get penicillin prophylaxis and vaccines. Acute splenic sequestration occurs in young children.
- *Salmonella* osteomyelitis; aplastic crisis from parvovirus B19; stroke (screen children with transcranial Doppler); priapism; renal papillary necrosis; pigment gallstones; pulmonary hypertension.

Treatment: hydration, analgesia, hydroxyurea (raises HbF and reduces crises), L-glutamine, chronic transfusion for stroke prevention, and curative stem cell transplantation or approved gene therapies in selected patients. Diagnose with hemoglobin electrophoresis (HbS above 80 percent in HbSS; no HbA). **Sickle trait** (HbAS) is usually benign but can cause hematuria and impaired urine concentration. **HbC** (glutamate to lysine) gives target cells; HbSC disease is milder than HbSS.

@example
title: Working up an unexplained anemia as hemolysis
problem: A 28-year-old woman has fatigue and jaundice. Hb 7.2 g/dL, reticulocytes 9 percent, LDH high, haptoglobin undetectable, total bilirubin 3.6 mg/dL with direct 0.5. Smear shows spherocytes. What is the next test and what does each result imply?
step: High reticulocytes, high LDH, low haptoglobin and high indirect bilirubin ($3.6 - 0.5 = 3.1$ mg/dL) confirm hemolysis.
step: Spherocytes occur in hereditary spherocytosis and in warm AIHA. The next step is the DAT.
step: A DAT positive for IgG indicates warm AIHA; a negative DAT with family history, high MCHC and abnormal EMA binding would indicate hereditary spherocytosis.
step: If warm AIHA is confirmed, look for secondary causes (SLE, CLL, lymphoma, drugs), start glucocorticoids and folate, and check for transfusion compatibility problems.

@mistakes
- Stopping at the diagnosis of hemolysis without sending a DAT, which divides immune from non-immune causes.
- Testing G6PD activity during the acute hemolytic episode and calling a normal result conclusive.
- Giving fava beans or oxidant drugs to G6PD-deficient patients, or giving rasburicase without G6PD screening.
- Forgetting vaccines and penicillin prophylaxis in sickle cell disease and before splenectomy.
