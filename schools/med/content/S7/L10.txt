@article
Cancer is a genetic disease of somatic cells, arising from a clone that accumulates mutations in genes controlling growth, survival, DNA repair and invasion. Its hallmarks include sustained proliferative signaling, evasion of growth suppressors and cell death, unlimited replication (telomerase), angiogenesis, invasion and metastasis, and immune evasion.

### Oncogenes and tumor suppressors

**Proto-oncogenes** promote growth; a **gain-of-function** mutation in a single allele (dominant at the cellular level) creates an **oncogene**. Examples: RAS (small GTPase), BRAF V600E, MYC (t(8;14) in Burkitt lymphoma), BCR-ABL (CML), HER2/ERBB2 amplification (breast cancer), N-MYC amplification (neuroblastoma), RET (MEN2).

**Tumor suppressor genes** restrain growth; cancer requires **loss of both alleles** (recessive at the cellular level, Knudson **two-hit hypothesis**). In hereditary syndromes the first hit is germline (so every cell needs only one further hit, explaining early onset and bilateral or multifocal tumors); in sporadic cancer both hits are somatic.

| Gene | Function | Syndrome or cancer |
|---|---|---|
| RB1 | Holds E2F, blocks G1 to S | Retinoblastoma, osteosarcoma |
| TP53 | Guardian of the genome: arrest, repair, apoptosis | Li-Fraumeni syndrome (sarcomas, breast, brain, adrenal, leukemia) |
| APC | Degrades beta-catenin (Wnt) | Familial adenomatous polyposis |
| BRCA1/BRCA2 | Homologous recombination repair | Breast, ovarian, prostate, pancreatic cancer |
| NF1, VHL, PTEN | Various | Neurofibromatosis, von Hippel-Lindau, Cowden |

**DNA mismatch repair** genes (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2) are lost in **Lynch syndrome**, with microsatellite instability and early colorectal and endometrial cancers. BRCA-deficient cells depend on PARP for repair, so PARP inhibitors are synthetically lethal in them.

### Carcinogens

Tobacco (lung, head and neck, bladder, pancreas), UV light (skin cancers), asbestos (mesothelioma, lung), aflatoxin (hepatocellular carcinoma), alcohol, ionizing radiation (leukemia, thyroid), benzene (AML), aromatic amines (bladder), vinyl chloride (angiosarcoma). **Oncogenic infections:** HPV (E6 inactivates p53, E7 inactivates Rb; cervical and oropharyngeal cancer), HBV and HCV (hepatocellular carcinoma), EBV (Burkitt lymphoma, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, Hodgkin lymphoma), H. pylori (gastric adenocarcinoma, MALT lymphoma), HHV-8 (Kaposi sarcoma), HTLV-1 (adult T-cell leukemia).

### Grade, stage and markers

**Grade** describes histologic differentiation (how abnormal the cells look). **Stage** describes anatomic extent, usually **TNM** (tumor size, nodes, metastasis), and is the strongest prognostic factor. **Tumor markers** (PSA in prostate cancer, AFP in hepatocellular carcinoma and yolk sac tumors, beta-hCG in choriocarcinoma and germ cell tumors, CEA in colorectal cancer, CA-125 in ovarian cancer, CA 19-9 in pancreatic cancer, calcitonin in medullary thyroid cancer) are used to **monitor treatment and recurrence**, not to diagnose; tissue biopsy establishes diagnosis. Performance status (ECOG scale 0 to 5, where 0 is fully active and 5 is death) guides treatment choice.

### Metastasis

Carcinomas usually spread first through lymphatics (sentinel node); sarcomas spread hematogenously. Common sites: liver, lung, bone, brain. Common primaries: brain metastases from lung, breast, melanoma, kidney and colon; osteoblastic bone metastases from prostate cancer; lytic bone lesions from kidney, thyroid, lung and myeloma. Renal cell carcinoma can invade the renal vein.

### Paraneoplastic syndromes

Small cell lung cancer causes SIADH (hyponatremia with inappropriately concentrated urine), ectopic ACTH (Cushing syndrome) and Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome. Squamous cell lung cancer causes hypercalcemia via PTHrP. Renal cell carcinoma can cause erythrocytosis (EPO). Thymoma is linked to myasthenia gravis and pure red cell aplasia. Carcinoma can cause migratory thrombophlebitis (Trousseau syndrome).

### Screening principles

Screening needs a disease that is common, has a detectable presymptomatic phase, and has treatment that improves outcomes. Approximate U.S. Preventive Services Task Force positions (check current versions; other organizations differ): **colorectal** cancer screening 45 to 75; **breast** mammography biennially 40 to 74; **cervical** cytology and HPV testing 21 to 65; **lung** low-dose CT yearly for ages 50 to 80 with at least 20 pack-years who currently smoke or quit within 15 years. Biases: **lead-time bias** (earlier diagnosis lengthens apparent survival without prolonging life), **length-time bias** (slow tumors are overrepresented in screen-detected cases) and **overdiagnosis**. The positive predictive value depends on prevalence:

$$\text{PPV} = \frac{\text{sens} \times \text{prev}}{\text{sens} \times \text{prev} + (1 - \text{spec})(1 - \text{prev})}.$$

### Lymphoma staging revisited

Hodgkin lymphoma is staged by Ann Arbor (contiguous spread, A or B symptoms, usually curable), while most non-Hodgkin lymphomas spread noncontiguously, can involve extranodal sites at diagnosis, and are classified by subtype (indolent or aggressive) more than by stage.

@example
title: Predictive value of a screening test
problem: A screening test for a cancer has sensitivity 80 percent and specificity 90 percent. The disease prevalence in the screened population is 2 percent. What is the PPV?
step: Express values as proportions: sens 0.80, spec 0.90, prevalence 0.02.
step: True positives per person screened: $0.80 \times 0.02 = 0.016$.
step: False positives: $(1 - 0.90)(1 - 0.02) = 0.10 \times 0.98 = 0.098$.
step: $\text{PPV} = 0.016/(0.016 + 0.098) = 0.140$, about 14 percent: most positive screens are false, which is why confirmatory testing is required.

@mistakes
- Using a tumor marker to screen the general population or to establish a diagnosis.
- Forgetting that a tumor suppressor requires loss of both alleles, whereas an oncogene needs only one activated allele.
- Interpreting longer survival after screening detection as proof of benefit, ignoring lead-time bias.
- Confusing grade (differentiation) with stage (extent of spread).

prompt: A screening test has sensitivity 90 percent and specificity 95 percent. The prevalence of disease in the screened population is 1 percent. Calculate the positive predictive value as a decimal, rounded to two decimal places.
answer: 0.15
check: (0.90 * 0.01) / (0.90 * 0.01 + (1 - 0.95) * (1 - 0.01))
tol: 0.005
hint: Compute true positives and false positives per person screened.
hint: True positives $0.90 \times 0.01 = 0.009$; false positives $0.05 \times 0.99 = 0.0495$.
solution: $\text{PPV} = 0.009/(0.009 + 0.0495) \approx 0.15$.
