@article
Cancer drugs have narrow therapeutic windows because they injure dividing normal cells: marrow, gut mucosa, hair follicles and gonads. Your exam goal is to link **drug, mechanism and toxicity**. Doses below are typical adult values for study; verify against current references before any clinical use.

### Principles

Chemotherapy kills a constant fraction of cells with each cycle (log-kill hypothesis), so regimens combine drugs with different mechanisms and toxicities, given in cycles to allow marrow recovery. Resistance arises from efflux pumps (P-glycoprotein), altered targets and DNA repair. Drug doses are often scaled to **body surface area (BSA)**; the Mosteller formula is
$$\text{BSA} = \sqrt{\frac{\text{height(cm)} \times \text{weight(kg)}}{3600}}\ \text{m}^2.$$

### Cytotoxic drugs

| Class and drugs | Mechanism | Signature toxicity |
|---|---|---|
| Alkylators: cyclophosphamide, ifosfamide | Cross-link DNA | **Hemorrhagic cystitis** (acrolein); prevent with hydration and **mesna**; myelosuppression |
| Platinum: cisplatin, carboplatin | DNA cross-links | Cisplatin: **nephrotoxicity**, **ototoxicity**, neuropathy, severe emesis; carboplatin: myelosuppression |
| Busulfan; nitrosoureas | Alkylation | Pulmonary fibrosis (busulfan); crosses blood-brain barrier (nitrosoureas) |
| Antimetabolite: **methotrexate** | Inhibits dihydrofolate reductase (S phase) | Myelosuppression, mucositis, hepatotoxicity, pulmonary toxicity, renal tubular injury; rescue with **leucovorin** (folinic acid) |
| **5-fluorouracil**, capecitabine | Inhibit thymidylate synthase (leucovorin enhances binding) | Myelosuppression, mucositis, diarrhea, hand-foot syndrome; severe toxicity in DPD deficiency |
| Cytarabine | DNA polymerase inhibition | Myelosuppression; cerebellar toxicity at high dose |
| 6-mercaptopurine, azathioprine | Purine analogs | Myelosuppression; **allopurinol** blocks xanthine oxidase, so reduce the dose; TPMT deficiency raises toxicity |
| Hydroxyurea | Ribonucleotide reductase inhibitor | Myelosuppression, megaloblastic changes |
| **Doxorubicin**, daunorubicin | Intercalate DNA, topoisomerase II inhibition, free radicals | **Cardiomyopathy** (cumulative dose-dependent; **dexrazoxane** protects), extravasation injury |
| **Bleomycin** | DNA strand breaks | **Pulmonary fibrosis**, skin changes; little myelosuppression |
| **Vincristine** | Blocks microtubule assembly (M phase) | **Peripheral neuropathy**, constipation or ileus; little myelosuppression |
| Vinblastine | Same | Myelosuppression |
| **Paclitaxel** | Stabilizes microtubules | Neuropathy, hypersensitivity, myelosuppression |
| Etoposide; **irinotecan** | Topoisomerase II; topoisomerase I | Myelosuppression; irinotecan: diarrhea |

Myelosuppression is generally the dose-limiting toxicity; the neutrophil nadir comes about 7 to 14 days after dosing. **G-CSF** (filgrastim, pegfilgrastim) reduces febrile neutropenia. **Rasburicase** and allopurinol prevent urate nephropathy. **Ondansetron** (5-HT3 antagonist) plus aprepitant (NK1 antagonist) and dexamethasone prevent chemotherapy-induced nausea.

### Targeted therapies

- **Imatinib** (BCR-ABL, c-KIT in GIST); dasatinib, nilotinib.
- **Trastuzumab** (HER2 antibody): **cardiomyopathy**; monitor ejection fraction.
- **Rituximab** (anti-CD20): infusion reactions, hepatitis B reactivation, progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy.
- **Bevacizumab** (anti-VEGF): hypertension, proteinuria, bleeding, GI perforation, poor wound healing.
- **Cetuximab** (anti-EGFR): acneiform rash; no benefit with RAS-mutated colorectal cancer. Erlotinib and osimertinib inhibit EGFR in lung cancer with EGFR mutations.
- **Vemurafenib, dabrafenib** (BRAF V600E melanoma); **olaparib** (PARP inhibitor in BRCA-mutated cancers); **palbociclib** (CDK4/6, with hormone therapy in ER-positive breast cancer); **venetoclax** (BCL2; tumor lysis); **bortezomib** (proteasome; neuropathy); **ibrutinib** (BTK; atrial fibrillation and bleeding).

### Hormonal therapy

**Tamoxifen** is a selective estrogen receptor modulator: antagonist in breast, partial agonist in endometrium and bone, so it raises the risk of **endometrial cancer** and **venous thromboembolism**. **Aromatase inhibitors** (anastrozole, letrozole) are used in postmenopausal women and cause bone loss. **GnRH agonists** (leuprolide) suppress gonadal hormone after an initial flare (an antiandrogen is given first in prostate cancer); androgen-receptor antagonists include bicalutamide and enzalutamide; abiraterone blocks androgen synthesis.

### Immunotherapy

Checkpoint inhibitors block **PD-1** (pembrolizumab, nivolumab), **PD-L1** (atezolizumab) or **CTLA-4** (ipilimumab), releasing T-cell brakes. Toxicity is autoimmune: **immune-related adverse events** including colitis, pneumonitis, hepatitis, dermatitis and endocrinopathies (thyroiditis, hypophysitis, adrenal insufficiency, type 1 diabetes). Treat with holding the drug and glucocorticoids after excluding infection. **CAR T-cell therapy** causes cytokine release syndrome (tocilizumab) and neurotoxicity.

### Calvert formula

For carboplatin, dose is based on renal function and target area under the curve (AUC):

$$\text{Dose (mg)} = \text{AUC} \times (\text{GFR} + 25).$$

@example
title: Dosing by BSA
problem: A patient is 170 cm tall and weighs 70 kg. A regimen calls for 75 mg/m$^2$ of a drug. Calculate the BSA and the dose.
step: $\text{BSA} = \sqrt{170 \times 70 / 3600} = \sqrt{11900/3600} = \sqrt{3.306} \approx 1.82$ m$^2$.
step: $\text{Dose} = 75 \times 1.82 \approx 136$ mg.
step: Then check the dose against institutional limits and renal and hepatic function, and reduce it if the marrow has not recovered.

@mistakes
- Forgetting mesna with ifosfamide or high-dose cyclophosphamide, or leucovorin rescue after high-dose methotrexate.
- Giving full-dose 6-mercaptopurine with allopurinol, which blocks its metabolism.
- Confusing bleomycin (pulmonary fibrosis, little marrow toxicity) with vincristine (neuropathy, little marrow toxicity).
- Dismissing diarrhea on a checkpoint inhibitor as infectious or dietary without considering immune colitis.
