@article
Weeks 3 to 8 are the **embryonic period**, when each germ layer is shaped into the primordia of every organ system. Two large movements dominate: the ectoderm rolls up into the neural tube, and the flat trilaminar disc folds into a cylindrical body.

### Neurulation

The notochord induces the overlying ectoderm to thicken into the **neural plate**. The edges rise as **neural folds**, the center sinks as a **neural groove**, and the folds meet and fuse to form the **neural tube**. Closure begins near the future cervical region and proceeds both cranially and caudally, leaving two openings. The **anterior (cranial) neuropore** closes at about day 25 and the **posterior (caudal) neuropore** at about day 27 to 28 after fertilization. The neural tube gives the central nervous system (brain, spinal cord, retina, posterior pituitary), while its lumen becomes the ventricles and central canal.

At the crest of each fold lie the **neural crest cells**. They detach, migrate, and form melanocytes, dorsal root ganglia and other peripheral sensory and autonomic neurons, Schwann cells, adrenal medulla chromaffin cells, enteric ganglia, craniofacial cartilage and bone, and parts of the heart's outflow septum.

**Neural tube defects (NTDs)** result from failed closure. Failure of the anterior neuropore gives **anencephaly** (the forebrain fails to form, which is incompatible with life). Failure of the posterior neuropore gives **spina bifida**: *occulta* (a vertebral arch defect, often with only a tuft of hair or dimple), *meningocele* (meninges herniate), and *myelomeningocele* (meninges and spinal cord herniate, with paralysis and neurogenic bowel and bladder, often with **Chiari II malformation** and hydrocephalus). Fetal NTDs leak alpha-fetoprotein into amniotic fluid, so **maternal serum AFP** is elevated, and amniotic fluid acetylcholinesterase is elevated in open defects. Ultrasound shows a "lemon" skull and "banana" cerebellum. **Folate** before and during early pregnancy lowers risk, and all people who could become pregnant are advised to take a folic acid supplement; higher-risk patients, such as those with a previous affected child, are given higher doses.

### Mesoderm divides into three zones

- **Paraxial mesoderm** segments into **somites** (paired blocks beside the neural tube). Each somite forms a **sclerotome** (vertebrae and ribs), **myotome** (skeletal muscle) and **dermatome** (dermis of the back).
- **Intermediate mesoderm** gives the urogenital system: kidneys and gonads.
- **Lateral plate mesoderm** splits into a **somatic (parietal)** layer, which forms the body wall, and a **splanchnic (visceral)** layer, which forms the gut wall and heart. The space between them is the **intraembryonic coelom**, which becomes the pericardial, pleural and peritoneal cavities.

### Folding

Rapid growth of the neural tube and somites makes the disc fold. **Cephalocaudal folding** carries the heart tube and septum transversum from the head end to a ventral position in front of the foregut. **Lateral folding** brings the two sides together at the midline, closing the ventral body wall and pinching off the yolk sac into the **gut tube** (foregut, midgut, hindgut), leaving only the narrow **vitelline duct** connecting the midgut and yolk sac at the umbilicus. Persistence of the vitelline duct is a **Meckel diverticulum**.

Failure of these folds produces ventral wall defects:

- **Omphalocele**: abdominal contents herniate through the umbilical ring at the midline, covered by a membrane (amnion and peritoneum). The gut fails to return from physiologic herniation (weeks 6 to 10). It is often associated with other anomalies and aneuploidy (trisomy 13 and 18) or Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome.
- **Gastroschisis**: a full-thickness paraumbilical defect, usually to the right of a normally inserted cord, with loops of bowel exposed to amniotic fluid and **no covering sac**. Other anomalies are uncommon, and it is associated with young maternal age.

### The first organs

The **heart** is the first functioning organ: the cardiogenic mesoderm makes a heart tube that begins to beat in the fourth week, loops to the right, and is septated during weeks 4 to 8 (ventricular septal defect is the most common congenital cardiac defect). The **pharyngeal arches** and pouches form in weeks 4 to 5. The third pouch gives the thymus and inferior parathyroids, and the fourth gives the superior parathyroids; failed development of pouches 3 and 4 causes **DiGeorge syndrome** (22q11.2 deletion) with thymic aplasia, hypocalcemia and conotruncal heart defects. The permanent **kidney** forms from the metanephros, from the ureteric bud and metanephric mesenchyme. Limb buds appear in week 4.

@example
title: Locating the failed neuropore
problem: Prenatal ultrasound shows a fetus with an open lumbosacral defect containing spinal cord. Which neuropore failed, by about what embryonic day should it have closed, and what is the likely screening finding?
step: The defect is at the caudal end of the spinal cord, so the failure involves the posterior (caudal) neuropore.
step: The posterior neuropore closes at about day 27 to 28 after fertilization; this is about 4 weeks after fertilization, and about 6 weeks of gestational age.
step: Neural tissue exposed to amniotic fluid leaks alpha-fetoprotein, so maternal serum AFP is elevated, and amniotic fluid acetylcholinesterase is positive. Ultrasound may show lemon and banana signs.

@mistakes
- Mixing up the paraxial (somites) and intermediate (kidney, gonads) mesoderm.
- Saying omphalocele has no sac and gastroschisis has a sac; it is the reverse.
- Forgetting that neural crest, not neural tube, forms melanocytes and dorsal root ganglia.
