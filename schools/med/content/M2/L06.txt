@article
Connective tissue binds, supports and protects other tissues. Unlike epithelium, it consists of **few cells scattered in abundant extracellular matrix (ECM)**. The matrix has **fibers** and **ground substance**, and nearly all connective tissue derives from embryonic **mesoderm** (mesenchyme).

### Cells

- **Fibroblasts** make collagen, elastin and ground substance. They are spindle-shaped, with pale oval nuclei in the matrix. Quiescent ones are fibrocytes. In healing, fibroblasts differentiate into myofibroblasts.
- **Macrophages** derive from blood monocytes; they phagocytose debris and present antigen. In tissues they have specific names (Kupffer cells in the liver, microglia in the brain, alveolar macrophages).
- **Mast cells** have cytoplasm filled with granules that stain **metachromatically** (purple with toluidine blue) and contain **histamine** and heparin. IgE bound to their surface triggers degranulation, which is the basis of allergic reactions and anaphylaxis.
- **Plasma cells** arise from B cells and secrete antibodies. They have an eccentric nucleus with clumped chromatin (the "clock-face" or "cartwheel" pattern), basophilic cytoplasm from abundant rough ER, and a pale perinuclear Golgi zone (hof).
- **Adipocytes** store triglyceride. In **white fat** one large lipid droplet pushes the nucleus to the edge, giving a "signet ring" appearance. **Brown fat** has multiple small droplets and many mitochondria and generates heat through uncoupling protein.

### Fibers

**Collagen** is the most abundant protein in the body. Mnemonic by type:

| Type | Where | Disease link |
|---|---|---|
| I | Bone, skin, tendon, dentin, fascia; most common | Osteogenesis imperfecta |
| II | Cartilage, vitreous humor, nucleus pulposus | Some chondrodysplasias |
| III | Reticular fibers: liver, spleen, lymph nodes, vessel walls, granulation tissue | Vascular Ehlers-Danlos syndrome |
| IV | Basement membrane | Alport syndrome, Goodpasture disease |

**Elastic fibers** consist of an **elastin** core surrounded by **fibrillin-1** microfibrils; they give recoil in the skin, lung and large arteries. Defective fibrillin-1 causes **Marfan syndrome** (tall stature, arachnodactyly, ectopia lentis, aortic root dilation).

### Collagen synthesis

1. Pre-pro-alpha chains are made on the **rough ER**.
2. **Hydroxylation** of proline and lysine residues requires **vitamin C**; deficiency causes **scurvy** (perifollicular hemorrhage, bleeding gums, poor healing).
3. **Glycosylation** and assembly of a **triple helix**; the chains have a repeating Gly-X-Y sequence, so every third residue is **glycine**.
4. Secretion of **procollagen**, where peptidases cleave the terminal propeptides.
5. Extracellular **cross-linking** by **lysyl oxidase** (a copper-requiring enzyme; deficient in Menkes disease).

Defects: **osteogenesis imperfecta** (type I collagen; brittle bones, blue sclerae, hearing loss, dental defects); **Ehlers-Danlos syndromes** (hypermobile joints, stretchy skin; the vascular type involves type III collagen and arterial or bowel rupture).

### Ground substance

Ground substance is a hydrated gel of **glycosaminoglycans (GAGs)**, proteoglycans and adhesive glycoproteins (such as fibronectin). It resists compression and allows diffusion.

### Types of connective tissue proper

- **Loose (areolar)**: sparse fibers, many cells; fills spaces under epithelia and around vessels.
- **Dense irregular**: thick collagen bundles in random directions; dermis, organ capsules.
- **Dense regular**: parallel bundles of collagen with rows of flattened fibroblast nuclei between; **tendons and ligaments**.
- **Reticular**: a network of fine type III fibers; the framework of lymphoid organs, liver and bone marrow.
- **Adipose** tissue, described above.

Special connective tissues are cartilage, bone and blood, covered in coming weeks.

@example
title: Identifying a tissue from its description
problem: A slide shows thick, pink, wavy bundles of collagen running in parallel. Between the bundles lie rows of flattened, dark nuclei. There are very few blood vessels. Name the tissue, its likely location and its main collagen.
step: Parallel orientation of fibers distinguishes dense regular from dense irregular connective tissue (random orientation).
step: Rows of flattened nuclei squeezed between bundles are fibroblasts (tenocytes in tendon).
step: The tissue is therefore tendon (or ligament), and its collagen is mainly type I. It is poorly vascularized, which is why tendon injuries heal slowly.

@mistakes
- Confusing collagen type II (cartilage) with type III (reticular fibers) or type IV (basement membrane).
- Thinking vitamin C is required for collagen secretion; it is needed for hydroxylation of proline and lysine in the ER.
- Assigning Marfan syndrome to collagen; it is a fibrillin-1 (elastic fiber) defect.
