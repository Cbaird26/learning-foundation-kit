@article
During the first three weeks the embryo converts from a ball of cells into a layered disc that will give rise to every organ. Embryologic age is counted from fertilization, whereas obstetric gestational age is counted from the last menstrual period, about two weeks earlier. Keep that offset in mind when reading vignettes.

### Implantation (week 1 to 2)

The blastocyst reaches the uterine cavity about day 4 to 5 and begins to implant around **day 6 to 7**, when the endometrium is in the progesterone-driven secretory phase. The trophoblast differentiates into two layers:

- The **cytotrophoblast**: mitotically active, mononuclear cells with distinct borders (the inner layer).
- The **syncytiotrophoblast**: a multinucleated mass with no cell borders, which invades the endometrium, erodes maternal capillaries and forms fluid-filled lacunae. It secretes **human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG)**.

hCG, structurally related to LH, keeps the **corpus luteum** secreting progesterone until the placenta takes over, at about 8 to 10 weeks. It is detectable in maternal serum about 8 to 11 days after fertilization, which is why it is the basis of pregnancy tests.

### The bilaminar disc (week 2)

The inner cell mass splits into two sheets. The **epiblast** is a layer of tall columnar cells that faces the new **amniotic cavity**. The **hypoblast** is a layer of small cuboidal cells that faces the original blastocyst cavity, now becoming the **primary yolk sac**. Extraembryonic mesoderm fills the space between, and later forms the **chorionic cavity**; the embryo remains attached to the chorion by the **connecting stalk**.

### Gastrulation (week 3)

**Gastrulation** converts the two-layer disc into three layers. A groove, the **primitive streak**, appears on the surface of the epiblast at the caudal midline, with a swollen cranial end, the **primitive node**. Epiblast cells move toward the streak, dive through it (invaginate), and spread beneath.

- Cells that displace the hypoblast become **definitive endoderm**.
- Cells that settle between the layers become **intraembryonic mesoderm**.
- Cells that remain on the surface are **ectoderm**.

Cells passing through the node form the **notochord**, a rod of mesoderm along the midline that induces the overlying ectoderm to thicken as the **neural plate**. Remnants of the notochord become the nucleus pulposus of the intervertebral discs. The epiblast therefore gives rise to all three germ layers, and gastrulation is the moment the body axes become fixed. Errors can produce **caudal dysgenesis** (sirenomelia) and **sacrococcygeal teratoma**, a tumor of leftover primitive streak cells and the most common neonatal tumor.

### Derivatives

| Layer | Examples |
|---|---|
| Ectoderm (surface and neuroectoderm) | Epidermis, hair, nails, nervous system, retina, lens, inner ear, anterior pituitary, neural crest (melanocytes, peripheral neurons, adrenal medulla, craniofacial cartilage) |
| Mesoderm | Muscle, bone, cartilage, connective tissue, blood and vessels, heart, kidneys, gonads, spleen, adrenal cortex, serous membranes |
| Endoderm | Epithelial lining of the gut, liver and pancreas epithelium, lung epithelium, thyroid, parathyroids, thymus epithelium, bladder lining |

### Ectopic pregnancy

An **ectopic pregnancy** is implantation outside the uterine cavity. About 95 percent are in the uterine tube, most often the **ampulla**, followed by the isthmus. Others occur in the ovary, abdomen or cervix. The common mechanism is delayed or blocked transport of the blastocyst: scarring or damaged cilia from **pelvic inflammatory disease** (often chlamydia), previous ectopic pregnancy, tubal surgery or ligation, an intrauterine device that fails (pregnancy that occurs is relatively more likely to be ectopic), smoking, and assisted reproduction.

A typical patient has **amenorrhea, lower abdominal pain and vaginal bleeding** at about 6 to 8 weeks of gestation. The tube has no decidua-supported wall, so the growing trophoblast eventually ruptures it, causing hemoperitoneum, shoulder-tip pain and shock. Ectopic rupture remains a leading cause of first-trimester maternal death.

Evaluate any reproductive-age patient with pain and bleeding by checking a pregnancy test. **Quantitative serum hCG** and **transvaginal ultrasound** together are key. Above the "discriminatory zone" (a serum hCG of roughly 1,500 to 3,500 mIU/mL, depending on the institution; ACOG advises caution with any single cutoff, and a conservative value near 3,500 mIU/mL is favored before acting on an empty uterus in a stable patient), an intrauterine gestational sac should usually be visible; its absence suggests ectopic pregnancy (or failed pregnancy). In an early ectopic pregnancy the hCG rises more slowly than expected.

Management depends on stability:
- **Hemodynamically unstable or ruptured**: emergency surgery (salpingectomy or salpingostomy).
- **Stable, unruptured, with low hCG (commonly below about 5,000 mIU/mL), a small mass (commonly under about 3.5 to 4 cm), no fetal cardiac activity and reliable follow-up**: intramuscular **methotrexate**, a folate antagonist that stops trophoblast division. Check renal and hepatic function first, and follow hCG until it is undetectable.
- Give **Rh immune globulin** if the patient is Rh-negative.

@example
title: Dating the day of implantation
problem: A patient has a regular 28-day cycle with ovulation and fertilization on cycle day 14. Implantation begins on day 6 after fertilization. Which cycle day is this, and what is the gestational age (from the last menstrual period) when the next period is first missed?
step: Fertilization occurs on cycle day 14, and implantation begins 6 days later, so cycle day $14 + 6 = 20$.
step: The next period is expected on cycle day 28, which is the end of gestational week 4, so the first missed period corresponds to a gestational age of about 4 weeks.
step: The embryo is then only about 2 weeks past fertilization, still in the bilaminar to early trilaminar stage. This is why hCG can detect a pregnancy before the embryo has any recognizable organs.

@mistakes
- Treating the hypoblast as the source of all three germ layers; the epiblast is the source, and the hypoblast is displaced by definitive endoderm.
- Confusing cytotrophoblast with syncytiotrophoblast: the multinucleated invasive layer that makes hCG is the syncytiotrophoblast.
- Giving methotrexate to an unstable patient with suspected rupture; instability calls for surgery.

solution: During week 3, gastrulation begins when a primitive streak forms on the epiblast. Epiblast cells migrate through the streak. solution: An ectopic pregnancy is implantation of the blastocyst outside the uterine cavity, most often in the ampulla of the uterine tube.
