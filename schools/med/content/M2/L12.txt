### The respiratory tract

The conducting airways are lined by **pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium with goblet cells**, which gradually thins toward the alveoli.

- **Trachea**: C-shaped rings of hyaline cartilage, with the open part closed by smooth **trachealis muscle** posteriorly; mucous and serous glands in the submucosa.
- **Bronchi**: irregular plates of cartilage and a continuous layer of smooth muscle between the epithelium and the cartilage.
- **Bronchioles** (diameter under about 1 mm): **no cartilage and no glands**; the epithelium becomes simple ciliated columnar then cuboidal, with **club cells** (secretory, detoxifying, and a progenitor population). They have a prominent smooth muscle layer that is the target of bronchodilators in asthma. **Terminal bronchioles** end the conducting zone.
- **Respiratory bronchioles** have scattered alveoli in their walls; they lead to **alveolar ducts**, **alveolar sacs** and **alveoli**, where gas exchange takes place.

The alveolar wall has two key cells:

- **Type I pneumocytes**: very thin, flat squamous cells that cover about 95 percent of the alveolar surface and make up the gas exchange surface.
- **Type II pneumocytes**: cuboidal cells that secrete **surfactant**, a phospholipid-rich mixture (mainly dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine) stored in **lamellar bodies**, which lowers surface tension and prevents collapse. They also regenerate type I cells after injury.

**Alveolar macrophages** ("dust cells") patrol the airspaces. The **blood-air barrier** is only about 0.5 µm thick: a type I cell, the fused basal laminae of the epithelium and capillary, and the capillary endothelium. Surfactant production begins at about 24 to 28 weeks and becomes adequate only late in gestation (about 34 to 36 weeks), and the **lecithin to sphingomyelin ratio** in amniotic fluid of 2 or more indicates fetal lung maturity. A preterm infant with insufficient surfactant has **neonatal respiratory distress syndrome**, and antenatal corticosteroids given to mothers at risk of preterm delivery speed maturation.

### The kidney and nephron

The cortex contains the **renal corpuscles** and convoluted tubules; the medulla contains loops of Henle and collecting ducts. In embryology, the **metanephric mesenchyme** forms the nephron (glomerulus through distal tubule) and the **ureteric bud** forms the collecting system.

Segments in order:

1. **Renal corpuscle**: a tuft of **fenestrated glomerular capillaries** inside **Bowman capsule**, whose parietal layer is simple squamous. The filtration barrier has three parts: fenestrated endothelium, the **glomerular basement membrane** (type IV collagen with negatively charged heparan sulfate), and **podocyte** foot processes bridged by **slit diaphragms** (nephrin). **Mesangial cells** support the capillaries.
2. **Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)**: simple cuboidal to low columnar cells with a prominent **brush border** that narrows and blurs the lumen, abundant mitochondria giving **deeply eosinophilic cytoplasm** and basal striations. It reabsorbs about two-thirds of filtered sodium and water, nearly all glucose and amino acids.
3. **Loop of Henle**: the **thin limbs** are lined with simple squamous cells, looking like capillaries but containing no red cells. The **thick ascending limb** has cuboidal cells without a brush border, pale cytoplasm and active NaCl transport (loop diuretics act here).
4. **Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)**: simple cuboidal cells with **no brush border**, a clear open lumen, and more nuclei per profile than the PCT. It reabsorbs sodium and calcium (thiazides act here). The **macula densa** is a patch of crowded tall nuclei in the distal tubule where it touches the afferent arteriole; it senses NaCl. The adjacent **juxtaglomerular cells** (modified smooth muscle of the afferent arteriole) secrete **renin**.
5. **Collecting duct**: simple cuboidal to columnar cells with pale cytoplasm and sharply defined borders; **principal cells** (ENaC and aquaporin-2; respond to aldosterone and ADH) and **intercalated cells** (acid-base balance).

In **minimal change disease** (nephrotic syndrome in children), light microscopy is normal, but electron microscopy shows effacement of podocyte foot processes. Renal agenesis leads to Potter sequence.

### Glands

**Exocrine glands** secrete through ducts. By mechanism: **merocrine** (exocytosis; most), **apocrine** (part of the apical cytoplasm is pinched off; apocrine sweat glands, lactating breast lipid) and **holocrine** (whole cell disintegrates; sebaceous glands). **Serous** acini have round basal nuclei, basophilic basal cytoplasm and apical zymogen granules (parotid, pancreas); **mucous** acini have pale foamy cytoplasm with flattened basal nuclei (sublingual).

**Endocrine glands** release hormones into the blood and have rich capillary networks. The **thyroid** consists of round **follicles** filled with pink **colloid** (thyroglobulin), lined by simple cuboidal follicular cells (taller when active); **parafollicular C cells** secrete calcitonin. The **adrenal cortex** has three zones, from the outside in: **glomerulosa** (aldosterone), **fasciculata** (cortisol) and **reticularis** (androgens), while the **medulla** contains neural crest-derived **chromaffin cells** that secrete catecholamines. The **pancreas** has exocrine serous acini and pale-staining **islets of Langerhans** (beta cells make insulin, alpha cells glucagon, delta cells somatostatin).

@example
title: Telling the PCT from the DCT on a slide
problem: In a section of renal cortex, one tubular profile has a narrow, ragged lumen filled with a fuzzy border and deeply pink cells. Another has a wide, clean lumen and paler cells with more nuclei visible. Name them and a drug class that acts on the second.
step: A brush border blurs the lumen, and many mitochondria make the cytoplasm intensely eosinophilic: this is the proximal convoluted tubule.
step: The second profile lacks a brush border, has a clear open lumen and paler cytoplasm: the distal convoluted tubule.
step: Thiazide diuretics block the NaCl cotransporter in the distal convoluted tubule.

@mistakes
- Putting cartilage and glands in bronchioles; both are absent there.
- Saying type I pneumocytes secrete surfactant; type II cells do.
- Describing the DCT with a brush border; only the PCT has one.
