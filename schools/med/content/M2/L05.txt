@article
Histology is the study of tissues under the microscope, and almost every slide you will see in medical school is stained with **hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)**. Hematoxylin acts as a basic dye: it binds acidic, negatively charged molecules, so DNA, RNA and ribosomes appear **blue to purple (basophilic)**. Eosin is an acidic dye that binds basic proteins and appears **pink (eosinophilic or acidophilic)**. Therefore a nucleus is purple, and cytoplasm full of mitochondria or protein is pink. A cell that makes a lot of protein for export, such as a plasma cell or pancreatic acinar cell, has abundant **rough endoplasmic reticulum** (ribosomes), and its basal cytoplasm looks blue. A cell that makes steroids has abundant **smooth ER** and appears pale and foamy. Total magnification is the eyepiece power multiplied by the objective power, for example $10 \times 40 = 400\times$.

### Epithelium: sheets that cover and line

**Epithelium** is a sheet of tightly joined cells with very little extracellular matrix, which covers body surfaces, lines cavities and forms glands. It is **avascular** (nutrients diffuse from the connective tissue below), **highly innervated**, and regenerates quickly. Epithelial cells are **polarized**:

- The **apical** surface faces the lumen or exterior and may bear **microvilli** (brush border; absorption, with core actin filaments), **cilia** (9+2 microtubules; moving mucus or fluid) or **stereocilia** (long, immotile microvilli of the epididymis and inner ear).
- The **lateral** surfaces carry cell junctions.
- The **basal** surface rests on the **basement membrane**: the basal lamina (type IV collagen, laminin) made by the epithelium, plus a reticular lamina (type III collagen) from connective tissue.

### Cell junctions

From apical to basal along the lateral surface:

| Junction | Key proteins | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Tight junction** (zonula occludens) | Claudins, occludin | Seals the space between cells; forms the barrier and separates apical from basolateral membrane |
| **Adherens junction** (zonula adherens) | E-cadherin, actin | Belt-like adhesion linked to the actin cytoskeleton |
| **Desmosome** (macula adherens) | Desmoglein, desmocollin, keratin filaments | Spot welds that resist shear |
| **Gap junction** | Connexons (connexins) | Channels that pass ions and small molecules; electrical and metabolic coupling |
| **Hemidesmosome** | Integrins, keratin | Anchors the basal cell surface to the basement membrane |

Clinical links: autoantibodies to desmoglein cause **pemphigus vulgaris**, with loss of keratinocyte adhesion and blistering. Mutations in connexin 26 cause one common form of congenital deafness.

### Classification

Classify by **number of layers**, then **shape of the surface cells**.

- **Simple** (one layer) or **stratified** (two or more layers). **Pseudostratified**: a single layer, but cells of different heights make the nuclei look like several rows; all cells touch the basement membrane.
- **Squamous** (flat, with a bulging nucleus in a thin cell), **cuboidal** (about as tall as wide, round central nucleus), **columnar** (taller than wide, oval nuclei at the base).
- Stratified epithelia are named for the **top** layer.

| Type | Typical location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Simple squamous | Alveoli, endothelium, mesothelium, Bowman capsule | Diffusion, filtration |
| Simple cuboidal | Kidney tubules, thyroid follicles, small ducts | Secretion and absorption |
| Simple columnar (brush border, goblet cells) | Small intestine, colon; ciliated in uterine tube | Absorption, secretion |
| Pseudostratified ciliated columnar with goblet cells | Trachea, bronchi | Mucociliary clearance |
| Stratified squamous, keratinized | Epidermis | Barrier against abrasion and water loss |
| Stratified squamous, non-keratinized | Oral cavity, esophagus, vagina, anal canal | Protection of moist surfaces |
| Transitional (urothelium) | Renal calyces, ureter, bladder | Stretch; barrier to urine |

### Metaplasia

**Metaplasia** is the reversible replacement of one mature cell type by another, usually in response to chronic irritation. In **Barrett esophagus**, the stratified squamous lining of the lower esophagus is replaced by intestinal-type columnar epithelium with goblet cells after chronic acid reflux, which raises the risk of adenocarcinoma. In smokers, the ciliated columnar bronchial lining changes to stratified squamous epithelium, which tolerates smoke better but loses mucociliary clearance. Metaplasia is not itself cancer, but persistent injury may cause dysplasia and neoplasia.

@example
title: Naming an epithelium from a slide
problem: A section of a duct shows a single layer of cells. Each cell is about as tall as it is wide, with a round nucleus in the center, a pink cytoplasm, and a fuzzy brush border on the luminal side. Name the epithelium and give a likely location.
step: Count the layers: one, so the epithelium is simple.
step: Shape: the cells are as tall as wide, with round central nuclei, which indicates cuboidal.
step: A brush border of microvilli means an absorptive surface with abundant mitochondria (pink cytoplasm).
step: Combine: simple cuboidal epithelium with a brush border, characteristic of the proximal convoluted tubule of the kidney.

@mistakes
- Naming a stratified epithelium for its basal cells instead of its surface cells.
- Calling pseudostratified epithelium "stratified"; every cell touches the basement membrane.
- Mixing up desmosomes (cell to cell) with hemidesmosomes (cell to basement membrane).
- Treating metaplasia as malignant; it is a reversible adaptation.

solution: Polarity means that an epithelial cell has different structure and function at its apical, lateral and basal surfaces. The apical surface faces the lumen and may carry microvilli or cilia.
