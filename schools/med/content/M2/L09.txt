@article
Nervous tissue has two cell classes: **neurons**, which receive and transmit signals, and **glia**, which support them. Neurons and most glia arise from neuroectoderm of the neural tube; **microglia** come from mesoderm-derived progenitors (yolk sac), and peripheral glia such as Schwann cells come from neural crest.

### The neuron

A typical **multipolar neuron** has:

- A **soma** (cell body) with a large, pale, round nucleus and a prominent nucleolus. In H&E or with a Nissl stain, the cytoplasm contains blue clumps called **Nissl bodies**, which are rough ER and free ribosomes: sites of protein synthesis. The abundance of Nissl substance reflects metabolic activity.
- Several **dendrites** that receive input; the cell surface area is multiplied by **dendritic spines**.
- A single **axon**, leaving the soma at the **axon hillock**, which lacks Nissl bodies. The **initial segment** next to it is the site where action potentials are generated. The axon ends in **terminals** that release neurotransmitter. Axonal transport uses microtubules: **kinesin** carries cargo anterograde (away from the soma) and **dynein** carries cargo retrograde (toward the soma), which is the route by which rabies virus and tetanus toxin ascend.

By shape, neurons are **multipolar** (most; motor neurons), **bipolar** (retina, olfactory epithelium, inner ear), and **pseudounipolar** (a single process that splits into a peripheral and a central branch; dorsal root ganglion sensory neurons). Mature neurons do not divide, so injured CNS neurons are poorly replaced.

### Glial cells of the CNS

- **Astrocytes** are star-shaped. They contain **GFAP** (glial fibrillary acidic protein) filaments, and their end-feet surround capillaries, contributing to the **blood-brain barrier**. They buffer extracellular potassium, take up glutamate, supply metabolic support and form glial scars after injury.
- **Oligodendrocytes** form **myelin** in the CNS. One oligodendrocyte can myelinate segments (internodes) on **many axons**, often dozens.
- **Microglia** are the resident phagocytes of the CNS, small cells with dark, elongated nuclei.
- **Ependymal cells** are ciliated cuboidal cells lining the ventricles and central canal; specialized ependymal cells of the choroid plexus produce **cerebrospinal fluid**.

### Peripheral nervous system

**Schwann cells** myelinate PNS axons, but each Schwann cell wraps **one internode of one axon**. Unmyelinated axons are still enveloped in Schwann cell cytoplasm, several axons per cell. **Satellite cells** surround neuronal cell bodies in ganglia. Because Schwann cells form a basal lamina tube, peripheral axons can regenerate, at about 1 mm per day, after injury. In **Wallerian degeneration** the axon distal to the cut breaks down, and the Schwann cells and macrophages clear debris and guide regrowth; the cell body shows **chromatolysis** (swelling with dispersal of Nissl bodies).

### Myelin and conduction

Myelin is a lipid-rich, multilayered sheath that insulates the axon. Gaps between internodes are the **nodes of Ranvier**, where voltage-gated sodium channels cluster. The action potential jumps from node to node (**saltatory conduction**), which raises velocity and saves energy. Large myelinated fibers conduct at about 50 to 120 m/s, while unmyelinated C fibers conduct at about 1 m/s.

**Multiple sclerosis (MS)** is an immune-mediated loss of **CNS myelin** (oligodendrocytes), with plaques in white matter, relapsing neurologic episodes such as optic neuritis, internuclear ophthalmoplegia and sensory loss, and oligoclonal bands in cerebrospinal fluid. **Guillain-Barre syndrome** is an acute immune attack on **PNS myelin** (Schwann cell-derived), often after an infection such as *Campylobacter jejuni*, causing ascending symmetric weakness with areflexia, and cerebrospinal fluid with high protein and few cells (albuminocytologic dissociation).

### Layers of a peripheral nerve

- **Endoneurium**: delicate connective tissue around each axon with its Schwann cell.
- **Perineurium**: layers of flattened cells joined by tight junctions that surround each **fascicle**; it forms the **blood-nerve barrier**.
- **Epineurium**: dense irregular connective tissue around the whole nerve.

@example
title: Estimating conduction time
problem: A sensory axon carries an impulse 1.5 m from the foot to the spinal cord at 50 m/s. What is the conduction time in milliseconds?
step: Time equals distance divided by velocity: $t = 1.5\ \text{m} / 50\ \text{m/s}$.
step: This gives $0.03$ s.
step: Convert to milliseconds by multiplying by 1000: 30 ms. A demyelinated axon with a lower velocity would take much longer, which is the basis of slowed nerve conduction studies.

@mistakes
- Saying Schwann cells myelinate many axons; one Schwann cell makes one internode on one axon. Oligodendrocytes make many.
- Assigning microglia to neuroectoderm; they derive from mesodermal (yolk sac) precursors.
- Confusing MS (CNS) with Guillain-Barre syndrome (PNS).
