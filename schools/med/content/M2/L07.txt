@article
Cartilage and bone are specialized connective tissues in which a firm matrix surrounds sparse cells. Cartilage is resilient and avascular; bone is rigid, mineralized and richly vascular.

### Cartilage

**Chondroblasts** secrete matrix and become **chondrocytes** sitting in small spaces called **lacunae**. The matrix contains collagen, **aggrecan** (a proteoglycan with chondroitin sulfate), hyaluronan and water. Cartilage has no blood vessels, so nutrients diffuse through the matrix, and healing is slow. Most cartilage is surrounded by a **perichondrium** of dense connective tissue that supplies cells and nutrients. Cartilage grows by **appositional growth** (new cells added at the surface) and **interstitial growth** (chondrocytes divide within the matrix).

- **Hyaline cartilage** is the most common. The matrix looks glassy and blue-pink with fine **type II collagen** fibrils that are invisible with H&E. Sites: articular surfaces (these have no perichondrium), costal cartilage, trachea and bronchi, nose, the growth plate, and the embryonic skeleton.
- **Elastic cartilage** has type II collagen plus a dense network of **elastic fibers** (stained dark with orcein). Sites: external ear, epiglottis, auditory tube. It has a perichondrium.
- **Fibrocartilage** has coarse **type I collagen** bundles with rows of chondrocytes between them and **no perichondrium**. Sites: the annulus fibrosus of the intervertebral disc, the pubic symphysis, menisci and the temporomandibular disc. It resists tension and compression.

### Bone: matrix and cells

Bone matrix has an organic part, **osteoid** (mostly type I collagen), and an inorganic part, **hydroxyapatite** crystals of calcium and phosphate. The organic part gives tensile strength, and the mineral gives compressive strength.

- **Osteoprogenitor cells** lie in the periosteum and endosteum.
- **Osteoblasts** are cuboidal cells lining bone surfaces that secrete osteoid and promote mineralization; they express alkaline phosphatase and make osteocalcin.
- **Osteocytes** are mature, entrapped osteoblasts in lacunae. Their long processes in **canaliculi** connect them to each other by gap junctions; they sense mechanical load.
- **Osteoclasts** are large, multinucleated cells derived from **monocyte-macrophage lineage** cells. They sit in resorption pits (Howship lacunae) and have a **ruffled border** through which they secrete hydrogen ions and cathepsin K to dissolve mineral and matrix. Osteoblasts and stromal cells express **RANKL**, which binds RANK on osteoclast precursors and drives their differentiation. **Osteoprotegerin** is a decoy receptor for RANKL. Denosumab blocks RANKL, and bisphosphonates inhibit osteoclast function.

### Bone architecture

**Compact (cortical) bone** is made of **osteons** (Haversian systems): concentric lamellae of bone around a central **Haversian canal** containing vessels and nerves. **Volkmann canals** run perpendicular and connect osteons. **Spongy (trabecular) bone** consists of branching trabeculae filled with marrow. **Woven bone** has disorganized collagen and appears early in development, in fracture callus, and in Paget disease; **lamellar bone** is mature, with organized layers.

### Ossification

**Intramembranous ossification** forms bone directly within mesenchyme, with no cartilage model; it forms the flat bones of the skull, mandible and clavicle. **Endochondral ossification** replaces a hyaline cartilage model and forms the long bones, vertebrae and pelvis. In a long bone, a primary center appears in the diaphysis during the embryonic and early fetal period, and secondary centers appear later in the epiphyses. Growth in length continues at the **epiphyseal growth plate** (physis), which has five zones from the epiphysis to the diaphysis:

1. **Reserve** (resting) zone: scattered small chondrocytes.
2. **Proliferative** zone: flat chondrocytes stacked in columns that divide.
3. **Hypertrophic** zone: swollen chondrocytes; the weakest zone.
4. **Calcification** zone: matrix calcifies, and chondrocytes die.
5. **Ossification** zone: osteoblasts deposit bone on the cartilage scaffold and vessels invade.

### Clinical correlations

- **Achondroplasia**: an activating mutation of **FGFR3** that inhibits chondrocyte proliferation. Endochondral growth fails, giving short limbs with a large head; intramembranous bone is normal.
- **Osteoporosis**: decreased bone mass with normal mineralization. A DXA **T-score** at or below -2.5 defines osteoporosis. The T-score is (patient BMD minus young-adult mean BMD) divided by the young-adult standard deviation.
- **Rickets and osteomalacia**: defective mineralization of osteoid, from vitamin D deficiency; the growth plate widens in children.
- **Osteopetrosis**: osteoclast failure, giving dense but brittle bones, marrow crowding with anemia, and cranial nerve compression.
- **Slipped capital femoral epiphysis** in adolescents occurs through the hypertrophic zone.

@example
title: Reading a cross-section of compact bone
problem: A ground bone section shows rings of lamellae around a central channel, with small dark spaces between the rings joined by hair-like lines. Identify each structure and what lies in it.
step: The ring-and-channel unit is an osteon. The central channel is the Haversian canal, carrying a capillary and a nerve.
step: The small dark spaces between lamellae are lacunae housing osteocytes.
step: The thin lines linking lacunae are canaliculi, which carry the osteocytes' processes and let nutrients and signals pass, since the matrix itself is solid.
step: A channel crossing the lamellae at right angles would be a Volkmann canal that links neighboring osteons and the periosteum.

@example
title: Ordering the growth plate
problem: In a child with a growth plate injury, the stacked, dividing flat cells are damaged. Which zone is this, and what are the zones on either side?
step: Stacked flat cells in columns that divide are the proliferative zone, which drives lengthening.
step: Toward the epiphysis lies the reserve zone, and toward the diaphysis lies the hypertrophic zone.
step: Damage to proliferating chondrocytes may shorten the bone, since lengthening depends on their number.

@mistakes
- Saying cartilage is well vascularized; it is avascular and relies on diffusion.
- Assuming osteoclasts derive from osteoblasts; they come from monocyte-macrophage precursors.
- Confusing osteoporosis (less bone, normal mineral) with osteomalacia (normal amount of osteoid, poor mineralization).
