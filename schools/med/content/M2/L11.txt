@article
These three organs let you practice a general method: identify the lining epithelium, then the supporting layers, then the specialized cells that explain the organ's job.

### Skin

The skin has three layers: the **epidermis** (keratinized stratified squamous epithelium, from ectoderm), the **dermis** (connective tissue, from mesoderm) and the **hypodermis** (subcutaneous fat).

The epidermis has five layers in thick skin (palms and soles), from deep to superficial:

1. **Stratum basale**: a single layer of cuboidal stem cells attached to the basement membrane by hemidesmosomes, with **melanocytes** (neural crest-derived, producing melanin) and **Merkel cells** (touch receptors).
2. **Stratum spinosum**: polygonal keratinocytes joined by **desmosomes**, which look like "spines" in a shrunken section; **Langerhans cells** (dendritic, antigen-presenting) are here.
3. **Stratum granulosum**: flattened cells with dark **keratohyalin granules**.
4. **Stratum lucidum**: a pale, clear layer only in thick skin.
5. **Stratum corneum**: dead, flattened, anucleate keratin-filled cells.

The dermis has a thin **papillary layer** of loose connective tissue with capillary loops and sensory corpuscles, and a deeper **reticular layer** of dense irregular connective tissue. Sebaceous glands (**holocrine** secretion), eccrine sweat glands and hair follicles lie in the dermis. In **bullous pemphigoid**, antibodies to hemidesmosome proteins cause a subepidermal split with tense blisters, whereas in **pemphigus vulgaris** antibodies to desmosomes cause intraepidermal flaccid blisters.

### The digestive tube: four layers

From lumen outward: **mucosa** (epithelium, lamina propria, thin smooth muscle layer called muscularis mucosae), **submucosa** (dense connective tissue, vessels, and the **Meissner submucosal plexus**), **muscularis externa** (inner circular and outer longitudinal smooth muscle, with the **Auerbach myenteric plexus** between them) and **serosa** (or adventitia where the organ is retroperitoneal). The two enteric plexuses derive from **neural crest**; in **Hirschsprung disease**, crest cells fail to migrate fully to the distal colon, which then lacks ganglion cells, stays contracted, and the bowel proximal to it dilates (megacolon).

Regional features:

- **Esophagus**: non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium; mucous glands in the submucosa; muscle is skeletal in the upper part, mixed in the middle and smooth in the lower part.
- **Stomach**: simple columnar mucous epithelium with deep pits and **gastric glands**. **Parietal cells** (round, eosinophilic) secrete HCl and intrinsic factor. **Chief cells** (basophilic from rough ER) secrete pepsinogen. **Mucous neck cells** secrete mucus. G cells (gastrin) lie in the antrum.
- **Small intestine**: simple columnar epithelium with a brush border, **goblet cells**, finger-like **villi** with a lymphatic **lacteal** and **crypts of Lieberkuhn**; **Paneth cells** at the crypt base (eosinophilic granules containing lysozyme and defensins). The duodenum has **Brunner glands** in the submucosa (bicarbonate-rich mucus); the ileum has **Peyer patches** (lymphoid aggregates). Surface area is amplified by plicae circulares (about 3-fold), villi (about 10-fold) and microvilli (about 20-fold). In **celiac disease**, villi are blunted (atrophy), crypts are lengthened and the epithelium contains many intraepithelial lymphocytes.
- **Colon**: no villi; straight crypts packed with **goblet cells**; absorbs water.

### Liver

The liver is organized into classic hexagonal **lobules**, each with a **central vein** in the middle and **portal triads** at the corners. A **portal triad** contains a branch of the **hepatic artery**, a branch of the **portal vein**, and a **bile duct** (cuboidal epithelium). Blood runs from the periphery to the center through **sinusoids** lined by discontinuous, fenestrated endothelium and containing **Kupffer cells** (macrophages). Between the endothelium and the plates of **hepatocytes** is the **space of Disse**, where plasma contacts hepatocytes; **stellate (Ito) cells** here store vitamin A and, when activated by chronic injury, lay down collagen to cause **fibrosis and cirrhosis**. Bile is made by hepatocytes and flows in the opposite direction, into **bile canaliculi**, then to bile ducts.

Hepatocytes differ by zone: zone 1 (periportal) receives the most oxygen; zone 3 (around the central vein) receives the least and has the most cytochrome P450 activity, so it is most vulnerable to **ischemia** and **acetaminophen toxicity**.

@example
title: Locating a split in the epidermis
problem: A skin biopsy shows a blister with the roof made of the full epidermis and the floor made of dermis, with a clean split at the dermal-epidermal junction. Which structure is the likely target, and what is the disease?
step: A split below the basal layer means that the basal keratinocytes remain attached to the roof, so the failure is in anchorage to the basement membrane.
step: Basal cells attach to the basement membrane by hemidesmosomes, so the target is a hemidesmosome component such as BP180.
step: This is bullous pemphigoid (subepidermal, tense blisters), in contrast with pemphigus vulgaris, where the target is desmoglein and the split lies within the epidermis.

@mistakes
- Placing Meissner plexus in the muscularis externa; it is in the submucosa, whereas Auerbach plexus lies between the muscle layers.
- Reversing the direction of flow: blood flows from the portal triad to the central vein, and bile flows toward the portal triad.
- Confusing parietal cells (acid and intrinsic factor) with chief cells (pepsinogen).
