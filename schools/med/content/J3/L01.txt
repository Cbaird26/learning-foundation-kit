@article
An **exponent** tells you how many times to use a number as a factor. In $2^5$, the **base** is $2$ and the **exponent** is $5$, so $2^5 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 32$. Exponents are a short way to write repeated multiplication, and they let us talk about numbers as huge as the distance to a star or as tiny as the width of a cell.

### The laws of exponents

Count the factors and the rules appear on their own. For $2^3 \cdot 2^2$ you have three twos and then two more twos, which is five twos in all.

- **Product rule:** $a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$.
- **Quotient rule:** $\dfrac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$ for $a \neq 0$.
- **Power rule:** $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$.
- **Zero exponent:** $a^0 = 1$ for $a \neq 0$.
- **Negative exponent:** $a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}$.

The zero and negative rules come from the quotient rule. Since $\frac{3^2}{3^2} = 1$, we need $3^{2-2} = 3^0 = 1$. Since $\frac{3^2}{3^5} = \frac{1}{3^3}$, we need $3^{-3} = \frac{1}{27}$. A negative exponent does **not** make the number negative; it flips the base to the bottom of a fraction.

### Scientific notation

A number is in **scientific notation** when it is written as
$$a \times 10^n, \qquad 1 \le a < 10, \quad n \text{ an integer}.$$
For a large number the exponent $n$ is positive: $93{,}000{,}000 = 9.3 \times 10^7$. For a small number it is negative: $0.00042 = 4.2 \times 10^{-4}$. To find $n$, count how many places the decimal point moves.

### Computing with scientific notation

Multiply the front numbers, add the exponents of ten. Divide the front numbers, subtract the exponents. If the front number leaves the range $1 \le a < 10$, adjust it and change the power of ten to match:
$$(5 \times 10^3)(4 \times 10^2) = 20 \times 10^5 = 2 \times 10^6.$$

Sizes in nature span many powers of ten. A typical human cell is about $10^{-5}$ m across, while the Sun is about $1.4 \times 10^9$ m across. Scientific notation makes comparing such sizes a matter of subtracting exponents.

@example
title: Multiplying in scientific notation
problem: Compute $(2.4 \times 10^5)(3 \times 10^{-2})$ and write the answer in scientific notation.
step: Multiply the front numbers: $2.4 \cdot 3 = 7.2$.
step: Add the exponents of ten: $5 + (-2) = 3$, so the powers combine to $10^3$.
step: Put the pieces together: $7.2 \times 10^3$. The front number is between $1$ and $10$, so we are done.
step: Check by ordinary arithmetic: $240{,}000 \times 0.03 = 7200 = 7.2 \times 10^3$.

@mistakes
- Multiplying the exponents in the product rule: $2^3 \cdot 2^2$ is $2^5$, not $2^6$.
- Reading $a^{-n}$ as a negative number; $2^{-3} = \frac{1}{8}$, which is positive.
- Leaving an answer like $20 \times 10^5$ without adjusting the front number into $1 \le a < 10$.
- Adding the front numbers when multiplying two numbers in scientific notation.

check: 5**7 / 5**4

hint: $\frac{5^7}{5^4} = 5^{7-4}$.
solution: $\frac{5^7}{5^4} = 5^3 = 125$.

@practice num
prompt: Evaluate $(2^3)^2 \cdot 2^{-4}$.
answer: 4
check: (2**3)**2 * 2**-4

hint: You get $2^6 \cdot 2^{-4}$. Add the exponents.
solution: $(2^3)^2 = 2^6$, and $2^6 \cdot 2^{-4} = 2^2 = 4$.

@quiz num
prompt: Compute $\dfrac{8 \times 10^6}{2 \times 10^2}$ and enter the result as a number in the form $a\text{e}n$.
answer: 4e4
check: 8e6 / 2e2
hint: Divide the front numbers, then subtract the exponents of ten.
hint: $8 \div 2 = 4$ and $6 - 2 = 4$.
solution: $\frac{8}{2} \times 10^{6-2} = 4 \times 10^4$.

@quiz num
prompt: Evaluate $\dfrac{7^{-2}}{7^{-4}}$.
answer: 49
check: 7**-2 / 7**-4
hint: Use the quotient rule, being careful with the signs of the exponents.
hint: $-2 - (-4) = 2$.
solution: $7^{-2 - (-4)} = 7^2 = 49$.
