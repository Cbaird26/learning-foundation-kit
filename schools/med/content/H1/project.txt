@project
title: One linear model and one exponential model
overview: You will invent two small data sets, one that changes by a steady amount and one that changes by a steady percentage, and build a linear model for the first and an exponential model for the second using the algebra from this course. You will test each model against its data, answer a question with the inverse operation (an equation for the linear model, a logarithm for the exponential model), and explain in plain words what each model says and where it stops making sense.
goal: Build a linear model $y = mx + b$ from data and interpret its slope and intercept with units.
goal: Build an exponential model $y = a\,b^x$ from data, interpret the growth factor as a percentage rate, and use a logarithm to find a time.
goal: Test both models against data, state their domain and range, and explain their limits.
step: Invent two data sets of 5 or 6 points each, using friendly whole numbers. Set L should change by about the same amount at each step (for example the fare for a taxi ride by distance, or litres in a filling tank by minute). Set E should change by about the same percentage at each step (for example followers each month, or a balance earning yearly interest). Write what $x$ and $y$ mean and their units.
step: Plot both sets by hand or in a spreadsheet. Write a sentence saying why you expect L to be linear and E to be exponential.
step: Linear model: choose two points from set L, compute the slope with its units, find the intercept, and write $y = mx + b$. Explain the slope and the intercept in words.
step: Test the linear model. Compute its prediction at every other point of L, list the errors (data minus prediction), and comment on the quality of the fit.
step: Exponential model: take the starting value $a$ from set E, compute the ratio of each value to the one before, and use a typical ratio as the growth factor $b$. Write $y = a\,b^x$, convert $b$ to a percentage rate, and test the model on every point of E.
step: Show that a line describes E badly: draw the line through the first and last points of E and compare its errors with the errors of the exponential model.
step: Inverse questions. For the linear model, choose a target value of $y$ and solve the equation for $x$. For the exponential model, use logarithms to find when $y$ reaches double its starting value. Check each answer by substitution.
step: State the domain and range of each model in context, give one reason each model would fail if extended far enough, and write the one-page explanation. Practise explaining one result aloud without notes.

deliverable: The worked algebra for both models (slope, intercept, growth factor, equation solving), with every solution checked by substitution.
deliverable: A one-page plain-language explanation covering what each model means, its domain and range, the inverse questions you answered, and the limits of each model.

@review
This review gathers the key results of the course. Work through it with pencil and paper, and before you read each line, try to produce it from memory on a fresh example.

### Week by week

- **Week 1, signed numbers.** Same signs multiply to a positive, different signs to a negative. Subtracting is adding the opposite. Estimate first, then compute.
- **Week 2, fractions and ratios.** Add and subtract with a common denominator, multiply across, and divide by multiplying by the reciprocal. A proportion sets two ratios equal.
- **Week 3, linear equations.** Do the same to both sides, undo operations in reverse order, multiply **every** term by the LCD to clear fractions, and check by substitution.
- **Week 4, inequalities.** Solve like an equation, but reverse the symbol when multiplying or dividing by a negative number. Write answers in interval notation. $|x - a| < r$ means "within $r$ of $a$".
- **Week 5, graphs and slope.** $m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$ and $y = mx + b$. Parallel lines have equal slopes; perpendicular slopes multiply to $-1$.
- **Week 6, systems.** Substitution and elimination give exact answers; a graph shows the crossing point. There are one, none or infinitely many solutions. Check in both equations.
- **Week 7, exponents and radicals.** $a^m a^n = a^{m+n}$, $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$, $a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}$, $a^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{a}$. Simplify radicals by pulling out perfect squares.
- **Week 8, polynomial operations.** Combine like terms, distribute, and use $(a \pm b)^2$ and $(a + b)(a - b)$. Test a value to catch errors.
- **Week 9, factoring and quadratics.** Take out the GCF first, then factor trinomials and special forms. Solve by factoring, completing the square, or the quadratic formula. The discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$ counts the real solutions.
- **Week 10, rational expressions.** Find excluded values from the original denominator, cancel factors (not terms), use common denominators, and reject extraneous solutions.
- **Week 11, functions and inverses.** Function notation, domain and range, composition from the inside out, and inverses by swapping and solving. Domain and range swap.
- **Week 12, exponentials and logarithms.** $f(x) = a\,b^x$ with growth factor $1 + r$. $\log_b y = x \iff b^x = y$. Solve $b^t = c$ with $t = \dfrac{\ln c}{\ln b}$.

### Self-check

You are ready for the final if you can do these without notes:

- Solve a linear equation with fractions on both sides and check the answer.
- Solve a linear inequality, say why the symbol flips, and write the answer as an interval.
- Find the equation of a line from two points, and solve a system by two methods.
- Simplify an expression with negative and fractional exponents.
- Expand $(2x - 3)^2$ and factor $6x^2 - x - 2$.
- State the excluded values of a rational expression and reject an extraneous solution.
- Find the inverse of a simple function and state its domain.
- Solve a growth problem for the time, using a logarithm.

### The most common errors

1. Losing a sign when a minus sign sits in front of a bracket.
2. Multiplying only some terms when clearing fractions.
3. Forgetting to reverse an inequality after dividing by a negative number.
4. Writing $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2$ or $\sqrt{a + b} = \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$.
5. Cancelling terms instead of factors.
6. Skipping the check, so extraneous solutions and arithmetic slips survive.
7. Using the rate $r$ instead of the factor $1 + r$, or taking a logarithm before isolating the exponential.

@exam num
prompt: A line passes through the points $(1, 3)$ and $(5, 11)$. A second line has the equation $x + y = 10$. At what $x$-coordinate do the two lines cross?
answer: 3
check: (lambda m, b: lin([[-m, 1], [1, 1]], [b, 10])[0])(*lin([[1, 1], [5, 1]], [3, 11]))
hint: First find the equation of the first line: the slope, then the intercept.
hint: The first line turns out to be $y = 2x + 1$. Substitute this $y$ into $x + y = 10$.
solution: The slope is $\frac{11 - 3}{5 - 1} = 2$, and $3 = 2(1) + b$ gives $b = 1$, so the line is $y = 2x + 1$. Substituting into $x + y = 10$ gives $x + 2x + 1 = 10$, so $3x = 9$ and $x = 3$ (with $y = 7$). Check: $2(3) + 1 = 7$ and $3 + 7 = 10$, and the point $(5, 11)$ satisfies $2(5) + 1 = 11$.

@exam num
prompt: Solve $\dfrac{x}{x - 3} + \dfrac{2}{x + 1} = \dfrac{12}{(x - 3)(x + 1)}$. Exactly one value of $x$ is a valid solution. Enter it.
answer: -6
check: root(lambda x: x/(x - 3) + 2/(x + 1) - 12/((x - 3)*(x + 1)), -50, -2)
hint: State the excluded values first, then multiply every term by the common denominator $(x - 3)(x + 1)$.
hint: You should reach $x(x + 1) + 2(x - 3) = 12$. Collect everything on one side, factor the quadratic, and then reject any solution that is excluded.
solution: The excluded values are $x \neq 3, -1$. Multiplying every term by $(x - 3)(x + 1)$ gives $x(x + 1) + 2(x - 3) = 12$, so $x^2 + 3x - 18 = 0$, which factors as $(x + 6)(x - 3) = 0$. So $x = -6$ or $x = 3$, and $x = 3$ is excluded (extraneous). Check $x = -6$: $\frac{-6}{-9} + \frac{2}{-5} = \frac{2}{3} - \frac{2}{5} = \frac{4}{15}$, and $\frac{12}{(-9)(-5)} = \frac{12}{45} = \frac{4}{15}$.

@exam self
prompt: A culture starts with 300 cells and doubles every 4 hours, so the number of cells after $t$ hours is $N(t) = 300 \cdot 2^{t/4}$. (a) State the domain and range of $N$ in context. (b) Use a logarithm to find the inverse function, which gives the time $t$ at which the culture has $n$ cells, and state its domain. (c) Use it to find when the culture reaches 4800 cells, and verify your answer by substitution. (d) Explain in a sentence or two what the inverse function tells you that $N$ does not.
hint: To invert, set $n = 300 \cdot 2^{t/4}$ and isolate the exponential before taking a logarithm.
hint: Divide by $300$ to get $2^{t/4} = \frac{n}{300}$, then take the logarithm base $2$ of both sides.
solution: (a) In context the domain is $t \ge 0$ and the range is $N \ge 300$. (b) From $n = 300 \cdot 2^{t/4}$ we get $\frac{n}{300} = 2^{t/4}$, so $\frac{t}{4} = \log_2\frac{n}{300}$ and $t = 4\log_2\frac{n}{300}$. Its domain in context is $n \ge 300$, the range of $N$. (c) For $n = 4800$: $\frac{4800}{300} = 16$, $\log_2 16 = 4$, so $t = 16$ hours. Check: $N(16) = 300 \cdot 2^{4} = 4800$. (d) $N$ answers "how many cells after this much time?", while the inverse answers "how long until there are this many cells?". They undo each other, and the logarithm is what brings the unknown time down from the exponent.
rubric: The domain $t \ge 0$ and range $N \ge 300$ are stated in context.
rubric: The inverse $t = 4\log_2\frac{n}{300}$ is found by isolating the exponential first, with a domain consistent with the range of $N$.
rubric: The time $16$ hours is found and checked by substituting into $N(t)$.
rubric: The explanation contrasts the two questions and says that the functions undo each other.
