@review
### Week-by-week review

1. **Preventive care and screening.** Know USPSTF grades. Breast: biennial mammography 40 to 74. Cervical: cytology every 3 years at 21 to 29; at 30 to 65, cytology every 3 years, hrHPV every 5 years or cotesting every 5 years. Colorectal: 45 to 75. Lung: 50 to 80, 20 pack-years, current smoker or quit within 15 years. AAA: men 65 to 75 who ever smoked. Hepatitis C once at 18 to 79; HIV at 15 to 65; DXA for women 65 and older.
2. **Immunizations.** Live vaccines (MMR, varicella) are contraindicated in pregnancy and severe immunocompromise. Tdap each pregnancy at 27 to 36 weeks. Recombinant zoster at 50 and older. Pneumococcal conjugate at 50 and older. Hepatitis B for 19 to 59. Mild illness is not a contraindication.
3. **Hypertension.** Goal below 130/80; confirm out of office; two drugs when more than 20/10 above goal; no ACE inhibitor plus ARB; avoid ACE inhibitors and ARBs in pregnancy; evaluate resistant hypertension for aldosteronism and sleep apnea.
4. **Diabetes.** A1c 6.5% or more, fasting glucose 126 or more; target below 7% for most; choose SGLT2 inhibitor or GLP-1 receptor agonist for cardiovascular disease, heart failure or CKD; stop metformin at eGFR below 30; titrate basal insulin to fasting 80 to 130 mg/dL.
5. **Lipids.** LDL by Friedewald; statin groups (ASCVD, LDL 190 or more, diabetes 40 to 75, risk of 7.5% or more); intensity; risk enhancers and CAC; add ezetimibe then PCSK9 inhibitor (the 2026 ACC/AHA guideline instead uses PREVENT-ASCVD risk, LDL-C goals of below 100, 70 or 55 mg/dL and a one-time lipoprotein(a)).
6. **Obesity.** BMI, 5% to 10% weight loss, intensive behavioral therapy, GLP-1-based drugs (MTC/MEN2 contraindication), surgery at BMI 35 or more.
7. **Acute complaints.** McIsaac score, CURB-65, antibiotic stewardship, red flags for headache.
8. **Musculoskeletal.** Red flags, no imaging for 6 weeks without them, NSAIDs and activity; cauda equina is an emergency; gout, carpal tunnel, polymyalgia rheumatica.
9. **Women's and men's health.** Estrogen contraindications; emergency contraception; hormone therapy with progestogen if uterus; T-score of -2.5 or lower; PDE5 inhibitors and nitrates; PSA shared decision at 55 to 69.
10. **Older adults.** Beers Criteria, prescribing cascades, Cockcroft-Gault, tapering and stopping one drug at a time.
11. **Mental health.** PHQ-9 bands (0-4, 5-9, 10-14, 15-19, 20-27), item 9, SSRI risks, GAD-7, AUDIT-C.
12. **Population health.** PPV depends on prevalence; lead-time bias; ARR, RRR and NNT; social determinants; teach-back and interpreters.

### Common errors

- Annual mammography or Pap tests where the USPSTF recommends intervals of 2 or 3 years or longer.
- Forgetting that lung screening needs both 20 pack-years and quitting within 15 years.
- Giving live vaccines in pregnancy, or combining an ACE inhibitor with an ARB.
- Treating borderline lab values as a diagnosis without confirmation.
- Stopping long-term benzodiazepines abruptly and changing several drugs at once.
- Ignoring item 9 on the PHQ-9.

@exam num
prompt: A preventive medication lowers the 10-year risk of a cardiovascular event by 25% (relative risk reduction) in patients whose baseline 10-year risk is 15%. How many patients with this baseline risk must be treated for 10 years to prevent one event? Round to one decimal place.
answer: 26.7
check: 1 / (0.15 * 0.25)
tol: 0.1
hint: The absolute risk reduction is the baseline risk times the relative risk reduction.
hint: Take the reciprocal of the absolute risk reduction.
solution: $ARR = 0.15 \times 0.25 = 0.0375$, and $NNT = 1/0.0375 = 26.7$. At a baseline risk of 7.5% the NNT would double, which is why treatment thresholds depend on baseline risk.

prompt: A 67-year-old man with type 2 diabetes has an A1c of 8.2%, an eGFR of 38 mL/min per 1.73 m², a urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio of 220 mg/g and blood pressure of 148/90 mm Hg on amlodipine. He takes metformin 1,000 mg twice daily and has no history of heart failure. Which is the best management?
choice: Stop metformin, start glyburide, and increase amlodipine
choice: Add both lisinopril and losartan, and continue metformin at the current dose
choice*: Start an ACE inhibitor or ARB, add an SGLT2 inhibitor, and reduce the metformin to a maximum of about 1,000 mg daily
choice: Add a beta blocker and a sulfonylurea
hint: Think about albuminuric CKD, the eGFR, and which drug classes protect the kidney.
solution: He needs an ACE inhibitor or ARB (albuminuria, hypertension, diabetes) and an SGLT2 inhibitor (CKD progression, effective at eGFR 38), with metformin reduced because eGFR is between 30 and 45. Glyburide is a poor choice, combining an ACE inhibitor with an ARB adds harm, and beta blockers and sulfonylureas lack kidney benefit.
