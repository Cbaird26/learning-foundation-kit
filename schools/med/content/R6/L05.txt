@article
Atherosclerosis begins when LDL particles enter the arterial intima, are oxidized and are taken up by macrophages to form foam cells. Cumulative exposure to LDL, blood pressure, glucose and smoking sets lifetime risk, so primary care lowers risk by treating several factors together.

### The lipid panel

$$\text{LDL-C} = \text{TC} - \text{HDL-C} - \frac{\text{TG}}{5} \quad (\text{mg/dL, Friedewald}).$$

The Friedewald estimate is unreliable when triglycerides are 400 mg/dL or higher (and less reliable with low LDL); use a direct LDL measurement or the **non-HDL cholesterol**, $\text{TC} - \text{HDL-C}$, which does not require fasting and captures all atherogenic particles. Total cholesterol 200 mg/dL is considered desirable; LDL 190 mg/dL or higher suggests familial hypercholesterolemia, especially with tendon xanthomas or premature disease. Before labeling hyperlipidemia as primary, exclude secondary causes: hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome, cholestasis, poorly controlled diabetes, and drugs (thiazides, glucocorticoids, protease inhibitors).

### Statin intensity

Statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase, lowering hepatic cholesterol and upregulating LDL receptors.
- **High intensity** (LDL reduction 50% or more): atorvastatin 40 to 80 mg, rosuvastatin 20 to 40 mg.
- **Moderate intensity** (30% to 49%): atorvastatin 10 to 20 mg, rosuvastatin 5 to 10 mg, simvastatin 20 to 40 mg, pravastatin 40 to 80 mg, pitavastatin 1 to 4 mg.

Adverse effects: myalgias (often nocebo; check creatine kinase and thyroid function with significant symptoms), uncommon myopathy or rhabdomyolysis, mild transaminase elevation, a small increase in new diabetes (benefit still outweighs it). Statins are generally stopped in pregnancy (the FDA removed its blanket contraindication in 2021, but most patients still discontinue them). Simvastatin has interactions with amlodipine (limit dose), clarithromycin and gemfibrozil.

### Who benefits (2018 AHA/ACC)

1. **Clinical ASCVD** (prior MI, stroke, symptomatic peripheral artery disease): high-intensity statin, or moderate if age over 75 or intolerance.
2. **LDL 190 mg/dL or higher:** high-intensity statin without risk calculation.
3. **Diabetes, age 40 to 75:** at least moderate intensity; high intensity with multiple risk factors.
4. **Primary prevention, age 40 to 75, LDL 70 to 189 mg/dL:** estimate 10-year ASCVD risk with the **pooled cohort equations** (age, sex, race, total cholesterol, HDL, systolic BP, treated BP, diabetes, smoking). Categories: low below 5%; borderline 5% to under 7.5%; intermediate 7.5% to under 20%; high 20% or more. Intermediate risk or higher favors a moderate-intensity statin after a clinician-patient risk discussion (high intensity at 20% or more). The AHA PREVENT equations (introduced in 2023; the 2025 AHA/ACC blood pressure guideline uses them) estimate total cardiovascular risk and give lower values than the pooled cohort equations, so their thresholds differ; check which tool your guideline uses. The numbers in this lesson are pooled-cohort-equation values. (The 2026 ACC/AHA guideline instead uses PREVENT-ASCVD, with lower primary-prevention categories of about 3% to under 5% borderline, 5% to under 10% intermediate and 10% or more high; LDL-C of 190 mg/dL or more still warrants a high-intensity statin.)

The USPSTF uses a similar threshold: statin for ages 40 to 75 with at least one risk factor (dyslipidemia, diabetes, hypertension, smoking) and 10-year risk of 10% or more (B), 7.5% to under 10% (C).

### Risk enhancers and calcium scoring

When risk is borderline or intermediate and the decision is uncertain, consider **risk enhancers**: family history of premature ASCVD, persistently elevated LDL (160 or higher), metabolic syndrome, CKD, chronic inflammatory conditions (rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, HIV), preeclampsia or premature menopause, South Asian ancestry, high-sensitivity CRP of 2 mg/L or more, lipoprotein(a) of 50 mg/dL or more, ankle-brachial index below 0.9. (The 2026 guideline advises measuring lipoprotein(a) at least once in every adult.)

If still uncertain, the **coronary artery calcium (CAC) score** helps: 0 favors withholding a statin (except in smokers, diabetes, strong family history), 1 to 99 favors a statin especially at 55 and older, and 100 or more (or above the 75th percentile) favors a statin.

### Add-on therapy

Treatment goals in the 2018 guideline are percent reductions (30% to 49% or 50% or more) and thresholds (the 2026 ACC/AHA guideline instead sets LDL-C goals of below 100 mg/dL for borderline or intermediate primary-prevention risk, below 70 for high-risk primary prevention and clinical ASCVD, and below 55 for very high-risk ASCVD, and names ezetimibe, bempedoic acid or a PCSK9 monoclonal antibody as add-ons). In very high-risk ASCVD (multiple major events or one major event plus high-risk conditions), add **ezetimibe** (blocks NPC1L1 in the intestine) if LDL stays at 70 mg/dL or higher on maximal statin, then a **PCSK9 inhibitor** (evolocumab, alirocumab; monoclonal antibodies that increase LDL receptor recycling) if still above threshold. **Bempedoic acid** is an oral alternative for statin intolerance. For triglycerides of 500 mg/dL or higher, treat to prevent pancreatitis (diet, alcohol, glucose control, fibrate or fish oil); **icosapent ethyl** reduces events in selected patients with ASCVD or diabetes on a statin with triglycerides of 150 to 499 mg/dL.

@example
title: Statin decision with a 10-year risk estimate
problem: Using the 2018 AHA/ACC categories, a 58-year-old man with no diabetes or ASCVD has an LDL of 132 mg/dL, a 10-year ASCVD risk of 14% and a brother who had an MI at 48. What do you recommend?
step: Classify: he is in the primary-prevention group aged 40 to 75, with LDL 70 to 189 mg/dL; 14% is intermediate risk (7.5% to under 20%).
step: Note the risk enhancer: a first-degree male relative with premature ASCVD (before 55) increases risk further.
step: Recommend a moderate-intensity statin, such as atorvastatin 10 to 20 mg, after discussing benefit and adverse effects, with lifestyle therapy.
step: Aim for at least a 30% LDL reduction and recheck lipids in 4 to 12 weeks (the 2026 guideline instead sets explicit LDL-C goals).

@example
title: Estimating LDL and escalating after a myocardial infarction
problem: A 66-year-old woman had an acute coronary syndrome 8 months ago and takes atorvastatin 80 mg. Her lipid panel: total cholesterol 160 mg/dL, HDL 45 mg/dL, triglycerides 150 mg/dL. Is further therapy needed?
step: Calculate LDL: $160 - 45 - 150/5 = 85$ mg/dL.
step: She has clinical ASCVD and is on maximal statin therapy; LDL is at or above 70 mg/dL.
step: Add ezetimibe 10 mg, which lowers LDL by about 15% to 25% further.
step: If LDL remains at 70 mg/dL or higher and she is very high risk, consider a PCSK9 inhibitor (the 2026 guideline instead sets LDL-C goals of below 70 mg/dL for clinical ASCVD and below 55 for very high risk).

@mistakes
- Using the Friedewald formula when triglycerides are 400 mg/dL or higher.
- Calculating 10-year risk for patients who already have ASCVD or LDL of 190 mg/dL or higher, who qualify for statins directly.
- Stopping statins for mild myalgias without checking CK or trying a different statin or dose.
- Starting aspirin for primary prevention in an older adult instead of treating lipids and blood pressure.

@practice self

solution: Risk is intermediate (7.5% to under 20% in 2018; the 2026 guideline also calls 8.5% intermediate, at about 5% to under 10%) but without enhancers the decision is uncertain, so CAC adds information. In all cases continue lifestyle therapy.

prompt: A fasting lipid panel shows total cholesterol 260 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 55 mg/dL and triglycerides 200 mg/dL. Using the Friedewald equation, what is the LDL cholesterol in mg/dL?
answer: 165
check: 260 - 55 - 200 / 5

solution: VLDL is $200/5 = 40$, so LDL is $260 - 55 - 40 = 165$ mg/dL.
