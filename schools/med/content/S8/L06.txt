@article
Connective tissue diseases are autoimmune diseases in which autoantibodies and immune complexes damage many organs. The pattern of organs plus a small set of antibodies usually names the disease.

### Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)

SLE predominantly affects women of reproductive age, and is more severe in Black, Hispanic and Asian patients. Type III hypersensitivity (immune complexes) and type II (cell-directed antibodies) cause damage, with impaired clearance of apoptotic debris, and early complement deficiency (C1q, C2, C4) predisposes.

**Features:** malar rash sparing the nasolabial folds, discoid rash (scarring), photosensitivity, oral ulcers, **non-erosive arthritis** (Jaccoud arthropathy is deforming but reducible), **serositis** (pleuritis, pericarditis), **cytopenias** (hemolytic anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia), **kidney disease** (lupus nephritis), seizures and psychosis, fever and fatigue.

**Antibodies.** **ANA** is the sensitive screening test (nearly all patients are positive; a negative test makes SLE unlikely) but is not specific. **Anti-double-stranded DNA** and **anti-Smith** are specific. Anti-dsDNA titers rise and **C3 and C4 fall** with active disease, especially nephritis. **Antihistone** antibodies suggest drug-induced lupus (hydralazine, procainamide, isoniazid, others), which spares the kidney and CNS and resolves after stopping the drug. **Anti-Ro (SSA) and anti-La (SSB)** in a pregnant patient raise the risk of neonatal lupus and **congenital heart block**.

**Lupus nephritis.** Persistent proteinuria (about 0.5 g/day or more) or an active urine sediment in a patient with SLE warrants kidney biopsy. **Class IV (diffuse proliferative)** is the most common and most severe, treated with glucocorticoids plus mycophenolate or cyclophosphamide.

**Treatment.** Every patient benefits from **hydroxychloroquine** (reduces flares and thrombosis) with retinal screening (a baseline eye examination, then yearly after 5 years of use, sooner with risk factors such as kidney disease); keep the dose to a maximum of about 5 mg/kg/day of actual body weight to limit retinal toxicity (confirm with current references). Add glucocorticoids for flares and immunosuppressants for organ-threatening disease; sun protection is essential.

### Antiphospholipid syndrome

Arterial or venous thrombosis, or pregnancy losses, with persistent **lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin or anti-beta-2-glycoprotein I** antibodies. Paradoxically the aPTT is prolonged and does not correct with mixing studies, and syphilis serology (VDRL, RPR) can be falsely positive. Treat thrombosis with long-term anticoagulation (warfarin for most; direct oral anticoagulants are not preferred in high-risk patients, particularly triple-positive or arterial disease).

### Other connective tissue diseases

- **Systemic sclerosis:** skin thickening, Raynaud phenomenon, esophageal dysmotility. **Limited** (CREST: calcinosis, Raynaud, esophageal dysmotility, sclerodactyly, telangiectasia) has **anticentromere** antibody and risk of pulmonary arterial hypertension. **Diffuse** has **anti-Scl-70 (topoisomerase I)** and interstitial lung disease; **anti-RNA polymerase III** is associated with **scleroderma renal crisis**, treated with an ACE inhibitor.
- **Sjogren syndrome:** dry eyes and mouth, parotid enlargement, anti-Ro and anti-La, risk of B-cell lymphoma, Schirmer test.
- **Dermatomyositis and polymyositis:** proximal muscle weakness, high CK, anti-Jo-1 (also interstitial lung disease). Dermatomyositis adds **heliotrope rash and Gottron papules** and is associated with malignancy.
- **Mixed connective tissue disease:** anti-U1 RNP.

### Vasculitis by vessel size

- **Large vessel:** **giant cell arteritis (GCA)**: age over 50, new headache, scalp tenderness, **jaw claudication**, visual loss, very high ESR, polymyalgia rheumatica. **Start high-dose glucocorticoids immediately** without waiting for **temporal artery biopsy**, because vision loss can be permanent. **Takayasu arteritis**: young women with arm pulse asymmetry.
- **Medium vessel:** **polyarteritis nodosa** (associated with hepatitis B; spares the lungs; renal and mesenteric aneurysms; no ANCA); **Kawasaki disease** (child under 5, fever 5 days or more, conjunctivitis, rash, cervical lymphadenopathy, red cracked lips, extremity changes; coronary aneurysms; treat with **IVIG and aspirin**).
- **Small vessel, ANCA-associated:** **granulomatosis with polyangiitis** (c-ANCA/PR3; upper airway, lung, kidney, with granulomas), **microscopic polyangiitis** (p-ANCA/MPO; no granulomas), **eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis** (asthma, eosinophilia). Treat with glucocorticoids plus rituximab or cyclophosphamide.
- **Small vessel, immune-complex:** IgA vasculitis (palpable purpura, abdominal pain, arthritis, hematuria), cryoglobulinemic vasculitis (hepatitis C).

### Synovial fluid in connective tissue disease

Lupus effusions are typically **noninflammatory** (WBC under 2,000/microL) despite the arthralgia; counts above 2,000 suggest RA or crystals, and counts above 50,000 demand a search for infection.

@example
title: Suspected giant cell arteritis
problem: A 74-year-old woman has 3 weeks of temporal headache, jaw pain when chewing and one episode of transient vision loss in the right eye. ESR is 96 mm/h. What do you do first?
step: The age over 50, headache, jaw claudication, amaurosis fugax and high ESR make giant cell arteritis very likely.
step: Start high-dose glucocorticoids immediately (for example prednisone 40-60 mg daily, or IV methylprednisolone if vision is threatened; typical adult study values, to be verified against current references before any clinical use) to protect vision.
step: Arrange temporal artery biopsy within days; steroid treatment does not make the biopsy useless for the first couple of weeks. Check CRP and consider ophthalmology evaluation.

@mistakes
- Using ANA to confirm SLE; ANA is sensitive but not specific, and anti-dsDNA and anti-Smith confirm.
- Waiting for biopsy results before starting steroids in suspected giant cell arteritis.
- Mixing up limited and diffuse systemic sclerosis antibodies: anticentromere is limited, Scl-70 is diffuse.
- Treating scleroderma renal crisis with an ARB or anything but an ACE inhibitor, or avoiding the ACE inhibitor because of the creatinine.

choice*: Falling C3 and C4 with rising anti-dsDNA suggests active nephritis.

solution: Active nephritis lowers complement and raises anti-dsDNA. Hydroxychloroquine reduces flares. Antiphospholipid antibodies cause a prolonged aPTT in vitro and thrombosis in vivo. Antihistone antibodies suggest drug-induced lupus, which seldom involves the kidney.
