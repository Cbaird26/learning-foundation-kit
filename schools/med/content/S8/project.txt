@review
**Week 1.** Bone is type I collagen plus hydroxyapatite. Osteoblasts build, osteoclasts resorb through RANKL; OPG blocks RANKL; sclerostin inhibits Wnt. Growth plate zones, FGFR3 in achondroplasia. Corrected calcium = measured + 0.8(4 - albumin).

**Week 2.** Describe a fracture by bone, site, pattern, displacement, open or closed and neurovascular status. Salter-Harris SALTR. Compartment syndrome: pain out of proportion; delta pressure under 30 mmHg; fasciotomy. Open fractures need early antibiotics. Scaphoid: snuffbox tenderness.

**Week 3.** OA: DIP and PIP, no erosions, stiffness under 30 minutes. Back pain: red flags (cancer, infection, fracture, neurologic deficit, cauda equina). L5 foot drop; S1 Achilles; L4 knee jerk.

**Week 4.** RA: MCP, PIP, wrist, anti-CCP, methotrexate, screen for TB before TNF inhibitors. Spondyloarthropathies: HLA-B27, sacroiliitis, uveitis, psoriatic and reactive arthritis.

**Week 5.** Gout: needle, negative, yellow; CPPD: rhomboid, positive, blue. Synovial WBC cutoffs 2,000 and 50,000. Treat flare with NSAID, colchicine or steroid; allopurinol to urate under 6.

**Week 6.** SLE: ANA, anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm, complement, hydroxychloroquine. Giant cell arteritis: steroids first. Kawasaki: IVIG and aspirin. ANCA vasculitis. Scleroderma renal crisis: ACE inhibitor.

**Week 7.** Osteoporosis: T-score -2.5 or fragility fracture; normal labs. Osteomalacia: low phosphate, high PTH and ALP. Paget disease: high ALP, normal calcium.

**Week 8.** Osteosarcoma (metaphysis, sunburst), Ewing (diaphysis, t(11;22)), metastases outnumber primary tumors. Osteomyelitis: MRI. Septic arthritis: aspirate, antibiotics, drainage.

**Week 9.** Epidermal layers, lesion terms (macule, patch, papule, plaque, nodule), rule of nines, Parkland formula.

**Week 10.** Psoriasis, atopic dermatitis, pemphigus versus pemphigoid, SJS/TEN with BSA cutoffs, isotretinoin precautions.

**Week 11.** Impetigo, cellulitis, abscess drainage, necrotizing fasciitis (surgery), tinea (KOH), zoster, scabies, RMSF (doxycycline).

**Week 12.** BCC, SCC, melanoma (ABCDE, Breslow), topical steroid potency and fingertip units.

**Self-check.** Can you describe a lesion in standard terms? Read synovial fluid? Name red flags for back pain? Apply ABCDE?

**Common errors.** Reversing birefringence; trusting normal pulses in compartment syndrome; shave biopsy of suspected melanoma; steroid injection into possibly infected joints; missing TB screening before biologics.

hint: Choose a drug safe in ulcer disease and kidney impairment.
solution: Crystals indicate gout. NSAIDs are unsafe with CKD and ulcer, and full-dose colchicine accumulates. Glucocorticoids are suitable. Culture is still sent; plan urate-lowering therapy after the flare with prophylaxis.

answer: 900

tol: 1
unit: mL/h
hint: Add the regional percentages for the TBSA.
hint: Total volume is 4 mL times kg times %TBSA; half goes in the first 8 hours.
solution: TBSA is $9 + 18 + 18 = 45\%$. Total is $4 \times 80 \times 45 = 14400$ mL; half is $7200$ mL over 8 hours, or $900$ mL/h.

@exam self
prompt: A 45-year-old man who injects drugs has 3 weeks of progressive low back pain, fever of 38.7 C and tenderness over L3. ESR is 95 mm/h. He now has new weakness of the left leg. List the red flags, give your leading diagnosis, tests and first management steps, and the likely organisms.
solution: Red flags: fever, IV drug use, progressive neurologic deficit, night or constant pain, high ESR. Leading diagnosis: vertebral osteomyelitis with epidural abscess. Tests: urgent MRI of the whole spine with contrast, blood cultures, CBC, ESR and CRP. Management: neurosurgical consultation for decompression, bone or abscess culture, and IV antibiotics covering S. aureus including MRSA (for example vancomycin) after cultures where stable, for about 6 weeks. Organisms: S. aureus most common, also Pseudomonas in injection drug use.
