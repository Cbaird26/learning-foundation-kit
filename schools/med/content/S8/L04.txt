@article
Inflammatory arthritis differs from OA in mechanism, pattern and consequence: the synovium is the target, stiffness lasts hours, and early treatment prevents irreversible damage.

### Rheumatoid arthritis (RA)

RA is a systemic autoimmune disease with symmetric small-joint **synovitis**. Susceptibility involves **HLA-DR4** (shared epitope) and smoking, which promotes citrullination of proteins; the immune system then makes **anti-CCP** antibodies and **rheumatoid factor** (an IgM against the Fc portion of IgG). Activated T cells, macrophages and cytokines (**TNF-alpha, IL-6, IL-1**) turn the synovium into **pannus**, which erodes cartilage and bone.

**Presentation.** Women more often than men, 30s to 60s, with swollen, tender **MCP, PIP, wrist and MTP** joints, symmetric, with **morning stiffness longer than 1 hour**, fatigue and low-grade fever. **The DIP joints are spared.** Deformities in late disease are **ulnar deviation, swan-neck** and **boutonniere**. Extra-articular disease includes subcutaneous **rheumatoid nodules**, interstitial lung disease and pleural effusion, pericarditis, scleritis, anemia of chronic disease, vasculitis, **Felty syndrome** (RA, splenomegaly, neutropenia), and **Caplan syndrome** (RA with pneumoconiosis nodules). Cervical spine disease risks **atlantoaxial subluxation**; take care with airway manipulation and obtain cervical imaging before intubation or surgery in long-standing RA.

**Diagnosis.** Elevated ESR and CRP; **anti-CCP is the most specific test** and rheumatoid factor is less specific (it is also positive in hepatitis C, endocarditis and aging). Radiographs show periarticular osteopenia, symmetric joint space loss and **marginal erosions**. Synovial fluid is inflammatory (2,000-50,000 WBC/microL).

**Treatment.** Start a **disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD)** early, ideally within weeks to months. **Methotrexate** is the anchor drug: it is a folate antagonist given **weekly**, with folic acid supplementation; monitor liver tests and blood counts; it is teratogenic and contraindicated in pregnancy. Alternatives and add-ons: hydroxychloroquine, sulfasalazine, leflunomide, and then biologics (**TNF inhibitors** such as adalimumab, etanercept and infliximab; abatacept; tocilizumab; rituximab) or JAK inhibitors. **Before a TNF inhibitor, screen for latent tuberculosis and hepatitis B.** NSAIDs and short-term glucocorticoids provide symptomatic relief and bridge therapy.

### The seronegative spondyloarthropathies

These share axial inflammation, asymmetric oligoarthritis, **enthesitis** (inflammation where tendon meets bone), dactylitis, uveitis and **HLA-B27**, with **negative rheumatoid factor**.

- **Ankylosing spondylitis (AS).** A young man (onset before 40) with insidious low back and buttock pain, morning stiffness, **improvement with exercise but not rest**, and night pain, from **sacroiliitis** (earliest radiographic sign) that progresses to **syndesmophytes** and a fused "bamboo spine" with reduced chest expansion. Extra-spinal features: **anterior uveitis**, aortic regurgitation, apical pulmonary fibrosis. Treat with NSAIDs and exercise; then TNF or IL-17 inhibitors. Methotrexate does not help axial disease.
- **Psoriatic arthritis.** Psoriasis with **DIP involvement, dactylitis ("sausage digit"), nail pitting** and onycholysis; radiographs show **pencil-in-cup** deformity.
- **Reactive arthritis.** Follows GI (Salmonella, Shigella, Yersinia, Campylobacter) or genitourinary (Chlamydia) infection by 1-4 weeks: asymmetric lower-limb oligoarthritis, **urethritis and conjunctivitis** ("can't see, can't pee, can't climb a tree"). Treat with NSAIDs; antibiotics for active chlamydial infection.
- **Enteropathic arthritis** accompanies inflammatory bowel disease.

### Inflammatory versus mechanical back pain

| Feature | Inflammatory | Mechanical |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Insidious, often under 40 | Often acute, after strain |
| Morning stiffness | Over 30-60 minutes | Brief |
| Exercise | Improves | Worsens |
| Rest | Worsens, night pain | Improves |

### Back pain red flags revisited

When you evaluate any back pain, ask for: cancer history or weight loss, fever or immunosuppression or IV drug use, major trauma or osteoporosis, progressive neurologic deficit, saddle anesthesia or retention or incontinence, age under 20 or over 50 with new pain, and unrelenting night pain. Inflammatory back pain is not a red flag for emergency but needs rheumatology evaluation.

@example
title: Choosing therapy for new rheumatoid arthritis
problem: A 44-year-old woman has 3 months of symmetric MCP, PIP and wrist swelling, 90 minutes of morning stiffness, high-titer anti-CCP and marginal erosions on radiographs. Her QuantiFERON test is positive and she has no symptoms of active TB. What do you do?
step: The picture is established RA with poor prognostic markers (erosions, high-titer anti-CCP), so start a DMARD promptly: methotrexate once weekly with folic acid.
step: Check baseline liver tests, creatinine, complete blood count, and hepatitis B and C serologies; counsel on contraception because methotrexate is teratogenic.
step: A positive TB test means latent TB. Rule out active TB (chest radiograph, symptoms) and treat latent TB before any TNF inhibitor is added if methotrexate fails.

@mistakes
- Calling DIP involvement RA; DIP-predominant arthritis suggests OA or psoriatic arthritis.
- Relying on rheumatoid factor alone; anti-CCP is more specific, and RF is positive in many other conditions.
- Starting a TNF inhibitor without screening for latent TB and hepatitis B.
- Expecting HLA-B27 to be diagnostic; it is present in about 6-8% of the general white population, and the diagnosis is clinical and radiographic.
