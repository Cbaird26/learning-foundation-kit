@article
T cells cannot see free antigen. Their receptors recognize **short peptides displayed in the groove of a major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecule**, which in humans is called the **human leukocyte antigen (HLA)** system. This arrangement is why T cells are "MHC-restricted" and why MHC genes matter in transplantation and autoimmunity.

### The HLA genes

The HLA complex lies on **chromosome 6p**. It is the most polymorphic region of the human genome, which means the population as a whole can present a huge range of peptides.

- **Class I:** HLA-A, HLA-B, HLA-C.
- **Class II:** HLA-DP, HLA-DQ, HLA-DR.
- **Class III** genes (not true MHC molecules) encode complement components C2, C4 and factor B, and TNF-alpha.

HLA genes are **codominantly expressed**: each person expresses the alleles from both chromosomes. The set of HLA alleles on one chromosome is a **haplotype**, inherited as a block (crossover is rare). Each child receives one haplotype from each parent. For two parents with four different haplotypes, any two siblings have a 25% chance of being HLA-identical, a 50% chance of sharing exactly one haplotype, and a 25% chance of sharing none. A parent and child are always at least **haploidentical**.

### Structure and distribution

| Feature | Class I | Class II |
|---|---|---|
| Chains | one heavy chain (alpha) plus beta-2 microglobulin | alpha and beta chains |
| Expressed on | all nucleated cells and platelets (not mature red cells) | antigen-presenting cells: dendritic cells, macrophages, B cells, some activated endothelium |
| Peptide length | about 8 to 10 amino acids, closed groove | about 13 to 25 amino acids, open groove |
| Peptide source | endogenous (cytosolic) proteins | exogenous (internalized) proteins |
| T-cell coreceptor | **CD8** binds the alpha-3 domain | **CD4** binds the beta-2 domain |
| Effector | cytotoxic T cell | helper T cell |

A useful rule is **"CD8 x MHC I = 8"** and **"CD4 x MHC II = 8"** (that is, $8 \times 1$ and $4 \times 2$).

### The class I pathway (endogenous antigens)

1. Cytosolic proteins, including viral and mutated proteins, are tagged and degraded by the **proteasome** (with interferon-gamma, the immunoproteasome) into peptides.
2. The **TAP** transporter (transporter associated with antigen processing) moves peptides into the endoplasmic reticulum.
3. In the ER, the peptide-loading complex (tapasin, calreticulin, ERp57) helps load peptide onto newly made class I heavy chain plus beta-2 microglobulin.
4. The stable complex travels through the Golgi to the surface and is recognized by CD8+ T cells.

Many viruses (herpesviruses, cytomegalovirus, adenovirus) block TAP or remove class I from the surface, which is why NK cells monitor for "missing self." **TAP deficiency** (bare lymphocyte syndrome type I) causes low class I expression, few CD8+ T cells and recurrent sinopulmonary infections.

### The class II pathway (exogenous antigens)

1. Class II alpha and beta chains are assembled in the ER with the **invariant chain (Ii)**, which blocks the groove so no endogenous peptide binds.
2. The complex travels in a vesicle toward the endocytic pathway.
3. A professional APC engulfs extracellular antigen into an endosome, which acidifies and fuses with the vesicle. Proteases degrade the antigen and the invariant chain, leaving the **CLIP** fragment in the groove.
4. **HLA-DM** swaps CLIP for antigenic peptide; the complex goes to the surface and is recognized by CD4+ T cells.

**Bare lymphocyte syndrome type II** results from mutation of the class II transactivator (**CIITA**) or related regulators, so class II is not expressed; patients have few CD4+ T cells and severe combined-type infections.

**Cross-presentation** is a special ability of some dendritic cells to load exogenous (phagocytosed) antigen on class I, activating CD8+ T cells against tumors and viruses that do not infect the dendritic cell itself.

### HLA associations to know

- **HLA-B27:** ankylosing spondylitis, reactive arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, enteropathic arthritis.
- **HLA-DR4:** rheumatoid arthritis (also DR1).
- **HLA-DR3 and DR4:** type 1 diabetes; **DR3** also with Graves disease.
- **HLA-DQ2 and DQ8:** celiac disease.
- **HLA-DR2 (DRB1*15):** multiple sclerosis; Goodpasture disease.
- **HLA-B51:** Behcet disease.
- **HLA-B*57:01:** hypersensitivity to abacavir; test before prescribing. HLA-B*58:01 is linked to allopurinol hypersensitivity.

These associations show increased risk; they do not cause disease alone.

@example
title: Probability of an HLA-identical sibling
problem: Mother has HLA haplotypes $a/b$ and father has $c/d$ (all four different). Her first child has $a/c$. What is the probability that a second child is HLA-identical to the first?
step: The second child must receive haplotype $a$ from the mother, out of her two haplotypes: probability $1/2$.
step: The second child must receive haplotype $c$ from the father: probability $1/2$.
step: The two events are independent, so the probability is $1/2 \times 1/2 = 1/4$.

@example
title: Tracing a viral protein into the class I pathway
problem: A newly synthesized influenza protein in the cytosol of an infected epithelial cell is recognized by CD8+ T cells. List the steps from protein to T-cell recognition.
step: The protein is cleaved by the proteasome into peptides of about 8 to 10 amino acids.
step: TAP transports peptides into the endoplasmic reticulum, where the peptide-loading complex places them in the groove of class I heavy chain with beta-2 microglobulin.
step: The complex goes through the Golgi to the cell surface. CD8 binds the alpha-3 domain of class I, and the T-cell receptor binds peptide plus MHC, allowing the CD8+ T cell to kill the infected cell.

@mistakes
- Saying that class I is on antigen-presenting cells only; class I is on every nucleated cell, and class II is the restricted one.
- Linking CD4 to MHC I or CD8 to MHC II. Use the rule of eight.
- Placing peptide loading for class II in the ER; it occurs in a late endosome after the invariant chain is degraded to CLIP.
- Assuming siblings have a 50% chance of being HLA-identical; it is 25%.

@quiz self
prompt: Explain why a virus-infected hepatocyte can be recognized by CD8+ T cells although hepatocytes are not professional antigen-presenting cells, and why dendritic cells can also activate CD8+ T cells against a virus that does not infect them.
hint: Follow the peptide from its source to the groove for each pathway.
solution: Hepatocytes, like all nucleated cells, express MHC class I. Viral proteins made in the cytosol are degraded by the proteasome, transported into the ER by TAP and loaded onto class I, so CD8+ T cells recognize the infected cell directly. Dendritic cells phagocytose antigen from dying infected cells and can route it to the class I pathway (cross-presentation), together with co-stimulatory molecules, so naive CD8+ T cells can be primed without the dendritic cell being infected.
