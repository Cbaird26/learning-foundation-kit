### Checkpoint inhibitors

Activated T cells carry inhibitory receptors that restrain the response (lesson 4). **CTLA-4** acts early, in lymph nodes, by outcompeting CD28 for B7. **PD-1** acts later, in tissues, when engaged by **PD-L1** that tumor cells and other cells display. Tumors exploit this brake. **Checkpoint inhibitors** are antibodies that block it and restore T-cell activity against tumor:

- anti-CTLA-4: **ipilimumab**
- anti-PD-1: **pembrolizumab, nivolumab, cemiplimab**
- anti-PD-L1: **atezolizumab, durvalumab, avelumab**

They are used in melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer, renal cell carcinoma, many other solid tumors and Hodgkin lymphoma. Response is more likely with high PD-L1 expression, microsatellite instability (mismatch-repair deficiency) or high tumor mutational burden, which create neoantigens.

Releasing the brakes on T cells causes **immune-related adverse events (irAEs)** that can affect any organ: colitis, pneumonitis, hepatitis, dermatitis, thyroiditis, hypophysitis, adrenal insufficiency, type 1 diabetes and myocarditis. Anti-CTLA-4 therapy causes more colitis and hypophysitis than anti-PD-1 therapy, and combinations cause more irAEs. Management is to exclude infection, hold the drug and give glucocorticoids, with infliximab or vedolizumab for refractory colitis. Endocrine toxicities are managed by hormone replacement.

### CAR T cells and bispecific antibodies

**Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cells** are a patient's T cells collected, engineered with a receptor gene (an antibody single-chain variable fragment fused to CD3-zeta and costimulatory signaling domains, such as CD28 or 4-1BB), expanded and reinfused. Because recognition is antibody-based, it does not need MHC. CD19-directed CAR T cells treat refractory B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia and B-cell lymphomas. Key toxicities are **cytokine release syndrome** (fever, hypotension; treated with tocilizumab), **immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity (ICANS)** and **B-cell aplasia** with hypogammaglobulinemia, managed with immunoglobulin replacement. **Blinatumomab** is a bispecific CD19 by CD3 T-cell engager.

### Monoclonal antibodies

Nomenclature offers clues: the stem **-mab** marks an antibody; **-ximab** is chimeric, **-zumab** humanized and **-umab** fully human; **-cept** marks a receptor fusion protein (etanercept, abatacept). Examples: **rituximab** (CD20: lymphoma, rheumatoid arthritis, ANCA vasculitis), **trastuzumab** (HER2), **bevacizumab** (VEGF), **cetuximab** (EGFR), **omalizumab** (IgE), **eculizumab** (C5), **natalizumab** (alpha-4 integrin; progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy risk from JC virus), **denosumab** (RANKL), and the anti-cytokine antibodies of lesson 6. Cytokines used as drugs include interferon-gamma (chronic granulomatous disease), interferon-beta (multiple sclerosis) and interleukin-2 (aldesleukin; melanoma, renal cell carcinoma). Intravesical BCG stimulates local immunity for bladder cancer.

### Immune-based tests

- **ELISA** (enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay): an enzyme-linked antibody gives a color change in proportion to antigen or antibody; sandwich forms detect antigen, indirect forms detect patient antibody.
- **Flow cytometry:** cells labeled with fluorescent antibodies are counted one by one; used for CD4 counts, leukemia and lymphoma immunophenotyping (CD19, CD20, CD3), and PNH (CD55, CD59).
- **Immunofluorescence:** ANA patterns on cells; direct immunofluorescence of skin or kidney biopsies shows immune deposits (for example linear IgG in Goodpasture disease).
- **Agglutination and Coombs tests** for red-cell antibodies; **serum protein electrophoresis with immunofixation** for monoclonal proteins.
- **Interferon-gamma release assays** and tuberculin skin tests for latent TB.
- **Titer:** the reciprocal of the highest dilution of a two-fold serial dilution that still gives a positive result.

**HIV testing** uses a fourth-generation combined antigen (p24) and antibody immunoassay; a reactive result is followed by an HIV-1/HIV-2 antibody differentiation immunoassay, and by an HIV-1 RNA test if the results are discordant or negative but acute infection is suspected.

### Applying test characteristics

$$\text{PPV} = \frac{\text{sens} \times \text{prev}}{\text{sens} \times \text{prev} + (1 - \text{spec})(1 - \text{prev})}.$$
Even an excellent test has a low positive predictive value when the condition is rare, which is why screening results are confirmed.

@example
title: Predictive value of a screening immunoassay
problem: A screening immunoassay has sensitivity 99% and specificity 95%. The prevalence of the condition is 1%. What is the PPV?
step: In 10,000 people, 100 have the condition and 9,900 do not.
step: True positives: $0.99 \times 100 = 99$. False positives: $0.05 \times 9900 = 495$.
step: PPV $= 99/(99+495) = 99/594 \approx 0.167$. Most positives are false, so confirmatory testing is needed.

@example
title: Pattern of immune-related toxicity
problem: A man with metastatic melanoma on ipilimumab and nivolumab has 6 watery stools daily 8 weeks into therapy. What is the likely process and next steps?
step: Diarrhea weeks into combined checkpoint blockade suggests immune-related colitis.
step: Exclude infection (stool studies, *C. difficile*), then hold therapy.
step: Give glucocorticoids, adding infliximab or vedolizumab if no improvement.

@mistakes
- Believing that a positive screening test in a low-prevalence population means disease is likely.
- Treating immune-related adverse events as infections and giving antibiotics alone, or continuing the checkpoint drug.
- Confusing CTLA-4 (priming, lymph nodes) with PD-1 (effector phase, tissues) when describing mechanism.
- Thinking CAR T cells require MHC presentation; they recognize surface antigen directly.

@quiz num
prompt: A screening immunoassay has sensitivity 90% and specificity 98%. The prevalence of the condition in the tested population is 2%. What is the positive predictive value, as a decimal rounded to two places?
answer: 0.48
check: (0.90 * 0.02) / (0.90 * 0.02 + (1 - 0.98) * (1 - 0.02))
tol: 0.01
hint: Imagine 10,000 people and count true and false positives.
hint: There are 200 with the condition and 9,800 without.
solution: True positives are $0.90 \times 200 = 180$; false positives are $0.02 \times 9800 = 196$. PPV $= 180/376 \approx 0.48$.
