@article
**Cytokines** are small secreted proteins that act on nearby cells (paracrine), on the cell that made them (autocrine) or occasionally systemically (endocrine). Many signal through receptors coupled to **JAK-STAT** pathways. Cytokines of the innate response are made mainly by macrophages and dendritic cells; adaptive cytokines come mostly from T cells.

### Key cytokines

| Cytokine | Main source | Main actions |
|---|---|---|
| IL-1 | macrophages | fever, acute-phase response, endothelial activation |
| IL-2 | T cells | T-cell growth and survival; essential for Tregs |
| IL-4 | Th2, mast cells | Th2 differentiation, IgE and IgG4 switching |
| IL-5 | Th2 | eosinophil growth and activation; IgA switching |
| IL-6 | macrophages, T cells | acute-phase proteins, fever, plasma-cell survival |
| IL-8 (CXCL8) | macrophages, endothelium | neutrophil chemotaxis |
| IL-10 | Treg, macrophages | inhibits macrophages and dendritic cells and Th1 |
| IL-12 | dendritic cells, macrophages | Th1 differentiation; NK-cell activation |
| IL-17 | Th17 | neutrophil recruitment, mucosal defense |
| IFN-gamma | Th1, NK cells | activates macrophages, raises MHC II expression |
| IFN-alpha and IFN-beta | most cells, plasmacytoid DCs | antiviral state |
| TNF-alpha | macrophages | endothelial activation, fever, granuloma formation; septic shock and cachexia |
| TGF-beta | Treg, many cells | anti-inflammatory, IgA switching, fibrosis |

### Therapeutic targeting

- **Anti-TNF** agents (infliximab, adalimumab, golimumab, certolizumab, and the soluble receptor etanercept) treat rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, psoriasis and ankylosing spondylitis. TNF-alpha is required to maintain granulomas, so blockade can **reactivate latent tuberculosis**; patients are screened with an interferon-gamma release assay or tuberculin test, and for hepatitis B, before treatment.
- **Tocilizumab** blocks the IL-6 receptor (used in rheumatoid arthritis, giant cell arteritis and cytokine release syndrome).
- **Anakinra** is an IL-1 receptor antagonist; **mepolizumab** blocks IL-5 (eosinophilic asthma); **dupilumab** blocks the IL-4 receptor alpha (atopic dermatitis, asthma); **ustekinumab** (IL-12 and IL-23) and **secukinumab** (IL-17) are used in psoriasis.
- **Tofacitinib** and other **JAK inhibitors** block signaling of many cytokines.
- **Cyclosporine and tacrolimus** inhibit calcineurin, preventing NFAT activation and IL-2 transcription.

**Cytokine release syndrome (CRS)** is a massive release of IL-6, IFN-gamma and others after CAR T-cell or bispecific antibody therapy, with fever, hypotension and hypoxia; grade-appropriate treatment includes tocilizumab.

### Tolerance and regulation

The immune system must not attack self. **Central tolerance** removes strongly self-reactive T cells (thymic negative selection, with AIRE) and B cells (bone marrow deletion, receptor editing). **Peripheral tolerance** handles those that escape:

- **Anergy**: antigen without costimulation.
- **Regulatory T cells** (CD4+ CD25+ **FOXP3**+) suppress by IL-10, TGF-beta, consumption of IL-2 and CTLA-4. **IPEX** (immune dysregulation, polyendocrinopathy, enteropathy, X-linked) arises from FOXP3 mutation: neonatal diabetes, severe enteropathy and eczema in boys.
- **Activation-induced cell death**: Fas on the activated cell binds Fas ligand and triggers apoptosis. **Autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome (ALPS)** is due to FAS mutation, with lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly and autoimmune cytopenias.
- **Immune-privileged sites** (eye, testis, brain) limit immune access.

### Matching antibody classes to functions

| Need | Class |
|---|---|
| Early response, strong complement activation, naive B-cell receptor (with IgD) | IgM |
| Opsonization, neutralization, placental transfer, long half-life | IgG |
| Mucosal protection and breast milk | IgA |
| Helminths and mast-cell degranulation | IgE |

@example
title: Why was tuberculosis reactivated?
problem: A 55-year-old man treated with infliximab for rheumatoid arthritis develops weight loss, night sweats and an upper-lobe cavitary lesion. What mechanism explains the infection, and what should have been done before treatment?
step: *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* is contained in granulomas, made of macrophages activated by IFN-gamma from Th1 cells, with TNF-alpha holding the structure together.
step: Infliximab neutralizes TNF-alpha, so granulomas break down and latent organisms reactivate.
step: Before starting an anti-TNF drug, screen for latent TB with an interferon-gamma release assay or tuberculin skin test and treat latent infection if present.

@example
title: Regulatory T-cell numbers
problem: A patient has a CD4+ count of $600/\mu\text{L}$, of which 6% are FOXP3+ regulatory T cells. How many regulatory T cells per $\mu$L?
step: Multiply the CD4+ count by the Treg fraction.
step: $600 \times 0.06 = 36$.
step: There are $36$ regulatory T cells per $\mu\text{L}$.

@mistakes
- Assuming IL-12 drives Th2 or IL-4 drives Th1; reverse is the rule (IL-12 gives Th1, IL-4 gives Th2).
- Saying that FOXP3 is a surface marker of cytotoxic T cells; it is the transcription factor of regulatory T cells.
- Overlooking latent tuberculosis screening before anti-TNF therapy.
- Thinking IL-2 is only a stimulator of effectors; it is also required for regulatory T-cell survival.

@quiz self
prompt: Describe four mechanisms that keep self-reactive lymphocytes from causing disease, indicating whether each is central or peripheral tolerance.
hint: Consider where self-reactive T cells are removed and what happens to the ones that escape.
solution: (1) Thymic negative selection, with AIRE-driven expression of tissue-specific antigens, deletes strongly self-reactive T cells: central. (2) Bone marrow receptor editing and deletion of self-reactive immature B cells: central. (3) Anergy of T cells that meet antigen without costimulation: peripheral. (4) Regulatory T cells (FOXP3+) suppress through IL-10, TGF-beta and CTLA-4: peripheral. Fas-Fas ligand activation-induced cell death is another peripheral mechanism.
