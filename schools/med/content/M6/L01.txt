@article
The immune system has two arms. **Innate immunity** is present from birth, acts within minutes to hours, and uses a fixed set of receptors that recognize broad classes of microbes. **Adaptive immunity** (lessons 3 to 5) takes days to develop, is specific for individual antigens, and leaves memory. The innate response is not only a stopgap: it decides whether and how the adaptive response will start.

### Barriers come first

Skin (keratinized epithelium, dry, acidic, with commensal flora), mucus and cilia in the airway, gastric acid, lysozyme in tears and saliva, defensins and other antimicrobial peptides, and competition from normal flora all keep most microbes from ever reaching tissue. A break in a barrier (a burn, a catheter, loss of ciliary clearance in cystic fibrosis) is a classic setup for infection.

### Recognizing microbes: PAMPs and PRRs

Innate cells carry **pattern-recognition receptors (PRRs)** that bind **pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs)**: conserved molecules that microbes make and host cells do not. PRRs also sense host danger signals released by damaged cells.

| Receptor | Location | Ligand |
|---|---|---|
| TLR4 (with MD-2, CD14) | cell surface | lipopolysaccharide (LPS) of Gram-negative bacteria |
| TLR2 (with TLR1 or TLR6) | cell surface | lipoproteins, peptidoglycan, lipoteichoic acid |
| TLR5 | cell surface | flagellin |
| TLR3 | endosome | double-stranded RNA |
| TLR7 and TLR8 | endosome | single-stranded RNA |
| TLR9 | endosome | unmethylated CpG DNA |
| NOD-like receptors (NOD1, NOD2) | cytosol | peptidoglycan fragments |
| RIG-I-like receptors | cytosol | viral RNA |

Toll-like receptor (TLR) signaling through MyD88 activates the transcription factor **NF-kB**, which turns on genes for the cytokines **TNF-alpha, IL-1 and IL-6** and for co-stimulatory molecules. Sensing of viral nucleic acids instead drives **type I interferons (IFN-alpha and IFN-beta)**, which put neighboring cells into an antiviral state. Some NOD-like receptors assemble **inflammasomes**, which activate caspase-1 to process pro-IL-1-beta into active IL-1-beta. Overwhelming TLR4 activation by LPS in bacteremia produces the TNF- and IL-1-driven vasodilation and capillary leak of septic shock.

### The innate cells

- **Neutrophils** are the most numerous circulating leukocytes and the first to arrive. They phagocytose and kill bacteria and fungi, and they die in the process, forming pus.
- **Macrophages** (derived from circulating monocytes or resident in tissues, for example Kupffer cells and alveolar macrophages) phagocytose, clear debris, and secrete cytokines.
- **Dendritic cells** sample tissue, then migrate to lymph nodes to present antigen to T cells: the bridge to adaptive immunity.
- **NK cells** are lymphocytes of the innate system. They kill cells that lack MHC class I ("missing self"), as virally infected and some tumor cells do, using perforin and granzymes. They also perform antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) through CD16 (Fc receptor for IgG). They secrete IFN-gamma.
- **Innate lymphoid cells**, mast cells, eosinophils and basophils round out the system; the last three are central to allergy and parasite defense.

**Opsonins** (IgG, C3b, mannose-binding lectin) coat microbes and enhance phagocytosis; the process is called opsonization.

### The respiratory burst

After engulfment, a phagosome fuses with lysosomes. Killing is oxygen-independent (lysozyme, defensins, acid, proteases) and oxygen-dependent. In the **respiratory burst**, **NADPH oxidase** converts O2 to superoxide ($\mathrm{O_2^-}$); superoxide dismutase makes hydrogen peroxide; and **myeloperoxidase (MPO)** combines H2O2 and chloride to form hypochlorous acid (bleach), the most potent microbicide.

**Chronic granulomatous disease (CGD)** is a defect of NADPH oxidase (most often X-linked CYBB, the gp91phox subunit; others are autosomal recessive). Patients get recurrent infections and granulomas from **catalase-positive organisms** (*Staphylococcus aureus*, *Serratia*, *Burkholderia cepacia*, *Nocardia*, *Aspergillus*). Catalase-negative organisms such as streptococci make their own H2O2, which MPO can use, so they are handled normally. The **dihydrorhodamine (DHR) flow cytometry** test is now preferred over the older nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT) test: neutrophils fail to oxidize the dye. Prophylaxis is with trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, an azole antifungal and interferon-gamma.

### Counting neutrophils

The **absolute neutrophil count (ANC)** is the total white count multiplied by the fraction that are segmented neutrophils plus bands:

$$\text{ANC} = \text{WBC} \times \frac{\%\text{ segs} + \%\text{ bands}}{100}.$$

An ANC below $1500/\mu\text{L}$ is neutropenia, and below $500/\mu\text{L}$ is severe, with a high risk of serious bacterial and fungal infection.

@example
title: Is this patient neutropenic?
problem: A patient receiving chemotherapy has a white blood cell count of $4{,}000/\mu\text{L}$ with 30% segmented neutrophils, 5% bands, 55% lymphocytes and 10% monocytes. Calculate the ANC and classify it.
step: Add the neutrophil forms: segmented neutrophils plus bands is $30\% + 5\% = 35\%$. Lymphocytes and monocytes are not counted.
step: Multiply by the total count: $4000 \times 0.35 = 1400/\mu\text{L}$.
step: Compare with the cutoffs: $1400 < 1500$ so this is neutropenia, but it is above $500$, so it is not severe.

@example
title: Why does this child get these infections?
problem: A 4-year-old boy has had a liver abscess due to *Staphylococcus aureus* and now has a pneumonia from *Aspergillus*. Streptococcal infections have been uncommon. A dihydrorhodamine test shows no increase in fluorescence after neutrophil stimulation. Name the defect and the mechanism.
step: The dye is oxidized by reactive oxygen species made by NADPH oxidase, so a failure to fluoresce means the oxidase is not working: chronic granulomatous disease.
step: Without superoxide there is no hydrogen peroxide for myeloperoxidase to convert to hypochlorous acid, so killing of intracellular organisms fails.
step: The pattern of catalase-positive organisms fits, because catalase-positive microbes destroy their own H2O2 and give the host's defective phagocyte nothing to use. Sparing of streptococci (catalase-negative) completes the picture.

@mistakes
- Using the total WBC or only segmented neutrophils for the ANC; bands are included, lymphocytes and monocytes are not.
- Saying that CGD is a problem of phagocytosis or chemotaxis: the cell engulfs normally but cannot make reactive oxygen species.
- Confusing TLR4 (LPS, Gram-negative) with TLR5 (flagellin) or TLR9 (unmethylated CpG DNA), or forgetting that nucleic-acid TLRs sit in endosomes.
- Describing NK cells as needing prior sensitization or antigen specificity; they respond to missing self and stress ligands.
