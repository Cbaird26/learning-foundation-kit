### Antibody structure

An **immunoglobulin** consists of two identical **heavy chains** and two identical **light chains** (kappa or lambda) joined by disulfide bonds. Each chain has a variable (V) and a constant (C) region. The two **Fab** arms (antigen-binding fragments) each contain one variable region from a heavy and a light chain. The three **hypervariable loops (complementarity-determining regions, CDRs)** form the antigen-binding site. The **Fc** region (the heavy-chain constant domains) determines the **isotype** and binds Fc receptors and complement C1q. The unique antigen-binding sequence of an antibody is its **idiotype**.

### Generating diversity

During B-cell development in bone marrow, the heavy chain gene segments **V, D and J** and then light-chain **V and J** segments are joined by **V(D)J recombination**, catalyzed by **RAG1 and RAG2**. Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase (TdT) adds random nucleotides at junctions (junctional diversity). Each B cell expresses one heavy and one light-chain allele (**allelic exclusion**), so each B cell has one specificity.

The developmental sequence is pro-B cell (heavy-chain rearrangement, requires Bruton tyrosine kinase signaling from the pre-B receptor), pre-B cell (mu heavy chain with surrogate light chain), immature B cell (surface IgM), then mature naive B cell (IgM and IgD) that leaves for secondary lymphoid tissue. Self-reactive immature cells are deleted, edited or made anergic. **X-linked agammaglobulinemia (Bruton)** is a BTK mutation that arrests development at the pre-B stage: boys have very few B cells, absent lymph node germinal centers and tonsils, low immunoglobulins of all classes, and recurrent sinopulmonary infections with encapsulated bacteria after maternal IgG fades at about 6 months.

### Activation

When a B cell binds antigen through its surface immunoglobulin, it internalizes and presents peptide on MHC class II to a helper T cell.

- **T-dependent activation** (protein antigens): the follicular helper T cell (Tfh) delivers **CD40 ligand** to **CD40** on the B cell and secretes cytokines. Activated B cells enter a **germinal center** where two events occur, both mediated by the enzyme **activation-induced cytidine deaminase (AID)**: **somatic hypermutation** of V genes, followed by selection of the highest-affinity clones (**affinity maturation**), and **class switching**, in which the heavy-chain constant region changes (the variable region and therefore specificity stay the same). Cytokines determine the isotype: **IL-4 gives IgE (and IgG4), IFN-gamma gives IgG, and TGF-beta (with IL-5) gives IgA.** Output cells are **plasma cells** (antibody factories) and **memory B cells**.
- **T-independent activation** (polysaccharide capsules, repetitive antigens): cross-linking of many receptors gives mostly IgM with little affinity maturation or memory, and responses are poor in children under 2 years. **Conjugate vaccines** link polysaccharide to a protein carrier so that T-cell help is recruited.

**Hyper-IgM syndrome** (most often X-linked CD40 ligand deficiency) has elevated or normal IgM with low IgG, IgA and IgE and recurrent sinopulmonary infections and opportunistic infection such as *Pneumocystis*, because class switching fails.

### The classes of antibody

| Class | Structure | Key properties |
|---|---|---|
| IgM | pentamer in serum (J chain) | first produced in a primary response; best activator of classical complement; does not cross the placenta; surface receptor of naive B cells as a monomer |
| IgG | monomer, four subclasses | most abundant in serum; crosses the placenta via the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn); opsonin, neutralizing, activates complement, mediates ADCC; main secondary response; half-life about 21 days |
| IgA | dimer in secretions (J chain plus secretory component) | mucosal immunity, breast milk, tears, saliva; neutralizes toxins and blocks attachment; does not activate complement well |
| IgE | monomer | binds FcepsilonRI on mast cells and basophils; helminths and allergy; lowest serum concentration |
| IgD | monomer | surface receptor on mature naive B cells with IgM |

Antibody functions are **neutralization** (blocking toxins and viral attachment), **opsonization** (coating for phagocytes), **complement activation** and **ADCC** by NK cells.

### Maternal antibody and the infant

IgG crosses the placenta mainly in the third trimester, giving the newborn passive immunity. Maternal IgG declines with a half-life of about 21 days, producing a physiologic trough in infant IgG at 3 to 6 months before the infant's own production rises. In the first days of life, **IgM is the isotype that the fetus itself makes**, so elevated IgM in cord blood suggests intrauterine infection.

$$N(t) = N_0 \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{t / t_{1/2}}.$$

@example
title: How much maternal IgG remains?
problem: A newborn has maternal IgG of $1000\ \text{mg/dL}$ at birth. If the half-life of IgG is 21 days, how much maternal IgG remains at 84 days?
step: Compute the number of half-lives: $84 / 21 = 4$.
step: Halve the amount four times: $1000 \times (1/2)^4 = 1000/16$.
step: The result is $62.5\ \text{mg/dL}$. This falls below what the infant needs, unless the infant is making its own IgG.

@example
title: Interpreting a class-switching defect
problem: A 3-year-old boy with repeated sinopulmonary infections and *Pneumocystis* pneumonia has IgM 400 mg/dL (high), IgG, IgA and IgE all very low, and normal B-cell numbers. Explain the defect.
step: High IgM with low other classes means B cells make IgM but cannot switch classes.
step: Switching needs CD40 on the B cell to engage CD40 ligand on the helper T cell.
step: X-linked CD40 ligand deficiency (hyper-IgM syndrome) is the most likely cause. CD40L is also needed for macrophage activation by T cells, explaining the *Pneumocystis* infection.

@mistakes
- Saying class switching changes antigen specificity; it changes only the constant region.
- Confusing somatic hypermutation (V region, affinity) with V(D)J recombination (diversity before antigen).
- Assuming IgM crosses the placenta; IgG does, via FcRn.
- Forgetting that T-independent polysaccharide responses give mainly IgM and poor memory in infants.
