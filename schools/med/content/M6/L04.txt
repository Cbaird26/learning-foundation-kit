### Development in the thymus

T-cell precursors come from bone marrow and mature in the **thymus**. In the cortex, double-negative cells (CD4- CD8-) rearrange the T-cell receptor (TCR) beta chain using the recombinases **RAG1 and RAG2**, then the alpha chain, becoming double-positive (CD4+ CD8+) cells. Two selection steps follow:

- **Positive selection (cortex).** Cortical epithelial cells present self peptide on self MHC. Thymocytes whose TCR binds self MHC weakly receive a survival signal; those that cannot bind die by neglect. Recognition of MHC class II makes a CD4+ cell; recognition of class I makes a CD8+ cell. This produces **MHC restriction**.
- **Negative selection (medulla).** Thymocytes that bind self peptide-MHC strongly undergo apoptosis. The transcription factor **AIRE** drives medullary epithelial cells to express tissue-specific proteins (such as insulin) so that tissue-reactive cells can be deleted. Some strongly self-reactive CD4+ cells instead become **regulatory T cells (Tregs)**.

Clinical correlations: **DiGeorge syndrome** (22q11.2 deletion) causes thymic hypoplasia with low T cells, hypocalcemia from hypoparathyroidism and conotruncal heart defects. **AIRE mutations** cause autoimmune polyendocrine syndrome type 1 (APECED): chronic mucocutaneous candidiasis, hypoparathyroidism and adrenal insufficiency. **FOXP3 mutation** causes IPEX (lesson 6).

### Activation: three signals

Naive T cells recirculate through lymph nodes, where dendritic cells present antigen.

1. **Signal 1:** TCR (with CD3) binds peptide-MHC, with CD4 or CD8 as coreceptor. Signaling proceeds through Lck and ZAP-70.
2. **Signal 2 (costimulation):** **CD28** on the T cell binds **B7 (CD80/CD86)** on the APC.
3. **Signal 3:** cytokines (for example IL-12 or IL-4) direct differentiation. IL-2 drives proliferation.

Signal 1 without signal 2 produces **anergy**: the cell becomes unresponsive, which is one mechanism of peripheral tolerance. **CTLA-4** is a CD28 homolog on activated T cells that binds B7 with higher affinity and turns the response off; **PD-1** binding PD-L1 also inhibits T cells. These two checkpoint receptors are targets of immunotherapy (lesson 12). CD40 on the APC and **CD40 ligand** on the T cell supply additional activating signals, and are vital for B-cell help.

### Effector T-cell subsets

| Subset | Driving cytokines and factor | Effector cytokines | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Th1 | IL-12, IFN-gamma; T-bet | IFN-gamma, TNF | activates macrophages; intracellular microbes (mycobacteria); granulomas |
| Th2 | IL-4; GATA3 | IL-4, IL-5, IL-13 | IgE class switching, eosinophils, helminths; allergy |
| Th17 | TGF-beta, IL-6, IL-23; RORgammat | IL-17, IL-22 | neutrophil recruitment; extracellular bacteria and fungi at mucosa |
| Tfh | IL-6, IL-21; BCL6 | IL-21 | helps B cells in germinal centers |
| Treg | TGF-beta, IL-2; FOXP3 | IL-10, TGF-beta | suppresses immune responses |

Patients with defects in IL-12 or its receptor or the IFN-gamma receptor, or with STAT1 loss-of-function variants, show a Th1 axis failure with disseminated infection by weakly virulent mycobacteria and *Salmonella*.

**CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs)** kill target cells displaying peptide-class I, through **perforin** (makes pores) and **granzymes** (induce apoptosis through caspases), and through Fas ligand binding Fas. After the response, most effector cells die, leaving **memory T cells**.

### Superantigens

Staphylococcal toxic shock syndrome toxin-1 and streptococcal pyrogenic exotoxins are **superantigens**. They cross-link the MHC class II molecule (outside the peptide groove) to the V-beta region of the TCR, activating up to 20% or more of T cells irrespective of antigen specificity. The result is a massive release of IL-2, IFN-gamma and TNF-alpha: fever, hypotension, a diffuse erythematous rash and multiorgan failure.

### Comparing the MHC pathways

| | Class I | Class II |
|---|---|---|
| Antigen source | cytosolic (endogenous), viral and tumor | endocytosed (exogenous), bacterial and toxins |
| Processing | proteasome | endosomal proteases |
| Transport and loading | TAP, ER, tapasin | invariant chain, CLIP, HLA-DM in endosome |
| Cells | all nucleated cells | professional APCs |
| T cell | CD8+ cytotoxic | CD4+ helper |

@example
title: Following a thymocyte
problem: A double-positive thymocyte has a TCR that cannot bind any self MHC molecule. Another binds self MHC plus a self peptide with very high affinity. What happens to each?
step: The first cell receives no survival signal in the cortex: it dies by neglect, failing positive selection.
step: The second cell passes positive selection but is deleted by apoptosis in the medulla on strong recognition of self antigen, which is negative selection. Some such CD4+ cells instead become FOXP3+ regulatory T cells.
step: Both outcomes protect the host: cells unable to see self MHC would be useless, and cells strongly reactive to self would cause autoimmunity.

@example
title: Signal 1 without signal 2
problem: A naive T cell encounters its cognate peptide on a nonprofessional tissue cell that expresses MHC but lacks B7. What is the outcome and what does it prevent?
step: Signal 1 is delivered (TCR binding peptide-MHC) but there is no CD28-B7 costimulation (signal 2).
step: The T cell becomes anergic rather than activated.
step: This peripheral tolerance prevents a response to self antigens in tissues that are not inflamed and so do not display B7.

@mistakes
- Reversing the locations: positive selection is cortical, negative selection is medullary.
- Calling CD28 the inhibitory checkpoint; CD28 activates, CTLA-4 inhibits, and both bind B7.
- Claiming CD8+ cells recognize MHC class II or that CD4+ cells kill infected cells directly.
- Saying that superantigens are processed and presented in the groove; they bind outside it.

@quiz self
prompt: Compare the MHC class I and class II antigen-processing pathways, stating for each the source of antigen, the site of peptide loading, the cells expressing the molecule and the responding T cell. Then name one disease in which each pathway is defective.
hint: Organize your answer by source, processing, loading site, cells and T cell.
solution: Class I: antigen is endogenous (cytosolic, such as viral protein). It is degraded by the proteasome, peptides are carried by TAP into the ER and loaded with the help of tapasin; all nucleated cells express class I; CD8+ cytotoxic T cells recognize it. The invariant chain blocks the groove until it is cleaved to CLIP in an endosome, where HLA-DM exchanges CLIP for peptide; professional antigen-presenting cells express class II; CD4+ helper T cells recognize it. CIITA mutation (bare lymphocyte syndrome type II) is a defect.
