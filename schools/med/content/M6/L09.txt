@article
Suspect immunodeficiency when infections are **severe, persistent, unusual, recurrent or in unusual sites**. The pattern of infection points to the defective arm of immunity.

| Defect | Typical infections |
|---|---|
| Antibody (B cell) | encapsulated bacteria (*Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*) causing sinopulmonary infections, otitis; *Giardia*; enteroviruses in agammaglobulinemia; symptoms start after maternal IgG wanes (about 6 months) |
| T cell | viruses (CMV, EBV, herpes viruses), fungi (*Candida*, *Pneumocystis jirovecii*), protozoa, mycobacteria; earlier onset and more severe |
| Phagocyte | catalase-positive bacteria (*Staphylococcus aureus*, *Serratia*, *Burkholderia*, *Nocardia*) and molds (*Aspergillus*); skin, lung, lymph node and liver abscesses |
| Complement | terminal (C5-C9): *Neisseria*; C3: severe pyogenic infections; early classical: immune-complex disease |
| Asplenia | encapsulated organisms with overwhelming sepsis (*S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*, *N. meningitidis*) |

### B-cell and antibody disorders

- **X-linked (Bruton) agammaglobulinemia:** BTK mutation; absent B cells, all immunoglobulins low, small or absent tonsils and nodes (lesson 5).
- **Common variable immunodeficiency (CVID):** low IgG and IgA (often IgM) with poor vaccine responses; usual presentation in the second or third decade; recurrent sinopulmonary infections, autoimmunity, granulomatous disease and increased lymphoma risk.
- **Selective IgA deficiency:** the most common primary immunodeficiency. Often asymptomatic; recurrent sinopulmonary and gastrointestinal infections (*Giardia*), atopy, celiac disease, and **anaphylaxis to blood products containing IgA**.
- **Hyper-IgM syndrome:** CD40L (or CD40) defect; high IgM, low IgG, IgA and IgE; *Pneumocystis* infection.

### T-cell and combined disorders

- **DiGeorge syndrome (22q11.2 deletion):** hypocalcemia, cardiac defects, thymic hypoplasia, with variable T-cell deficiency.
- **Severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID):** defective T cells and (directly or indirectly) B cells. Causes include **common gamma-chain (IL2RG, X-linked, the most common form)**, **adenosine deaminase (ADA) deficiency** (autosomal recessive, accumulation of toxic metabolites in lymphocytes), RAG1 or RAG2 mutations (no V(D)J recombination) and JAK3. Infants present before 6 months with failure to thrive, chronic diarrhea, persistent thrush, and *Pneumocystis* pneumonia, with **very low lymphocyte counts** and absent thymic shadow on chest film. Newborn screening measures **T-cell receptor excision circles (TRECs)**. Treatment is hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (gene therapy for some forms). **Live vaccines (including rotavirus, MMR, varicella) are contraindicated**, blood products must be irradiated and CMV-safe, and *Pneumocystis* prophylaxis is given.
- **Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome:** X-linked (*WAS*); the triad is **thrombocytopenia with small platelets, eczema, and recurrent infections**; low IgM, high IgA and IgE; risk of autoimmunity and lymphoma.
- **Ataxia-telangiectasia:** *ATM* DNA-repair defect; cerebellar ataxia, telangiectasias, IgA deficiency, sinopulmonary infections, malignancy, elevated alpha-fetoprotein.
- **Hyper-IgE syndrome (Job):** *STAT3* mutation, impaired Th17 differentiation; coarse facies, "cold" staphylococcal abscesses, retained primary teeth, fractures, eczema, very high IgE, eosinophilia.
- **Chediak-Higashi syndrome:** *LYST* mutation; giant lysosomal granules, partial albinism, neuropathy and pyogenic infections.
- **Chronic granulomatous disease and leukocyte adhesion deficiency type 1** are phagocyte disorders (lessons 1 and 2).

### Acquired immunodeficiency

HIV destroys CD4+ T cells. Opportunistic infection risk rises as CD4+ counts fall: *Pneumocystis* prophylaxis below about 200/$\mu$L, *Toxoplasma* and cryptococcal disease at lower counts, and CMV retinitis and disseminated *Mycobacterium avium* complex below about 50 to 100/$\mu$L. Other causes include malignancy, malnutrition, asplenia, diabetes, chronic kidney disease and immunosuppressive drugs.

### Evaluation and management

Start with a CBC and differential (lymphocyte count), quantitative IgG, IgA and IgM, and **vaccine antibody titers**; then lymphocyte subsets by flow cytometry, a CH50 for complement, a dihydrorhodamine test for neutrophil oxidative burst and genetic testing. Management includes prophylactic antibiotics, prompt treatment of infections and, for antibody deficiencies, **immunoglobulin replacement** (a typical study dose is about 400 to 600 mg/kg of IVIG every 3 to 4 weeks, adjusted to trough IgG and clinical response).

@example
title: Immunoglobulin replacement dose
problem: A 25 kg child with X-linked agammaglobulinemia is prescribed IVIG at 400 mg/kg every 4 weeks. What is the dose in grams? (Study example only.)
step: Multiply weight by dose: $25\ \text{kg} \times 400\ \text{mg/kg} = 10{,}000\ \text{mg}$.
step: Convert to grams: $10{,}000\ \text{mg} = 10\ \text{g}$.
step: The child needs this every 4 weeks, with IgG trough levels used to adjust the dose.

@example
title: A 4-month-old with thrush and pneumonia
problem: A 4-month-old girl has persistent oral thrush, chronic diarrhea, poor weight gain and *Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia. Her total lymphocyte count is 600/$\mu$L and the thymic shadow is absent. What is the diagnosis and key management points?
step: Early-onset opportunistic infection with fungi, chronic diarrhea and a very low lymphocyte count indicate a profound T-cell defect: SCID.
step: Confirm with lymphocyte subsets and genetic testing; newborn TREC screening would be low.
step: Management includes protective isolation, *Pneumocystis* prophylaxis and treatment, irradiated blood products, no live vaccines and early stem cell transplantation.

@mistakes
- Giving live vaccines (rotavirus, MMR, varicella) to an infant with suspected SCID.
- Expecting antibody deficiencies to present in the first weeks of life; protection from maternal IgG delays symptoms to about 6 months.
- Assuming CGD patients have recurrent streptococcal infections; they have catalase-positive organisms.
- Forgetting that selective IgA deficiency is the most common primary immunodeficiency and is often asymptomatic.
