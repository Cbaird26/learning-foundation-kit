step: Read the case. Maya, a 34-year-old woman who is 8 weeks pregnant, is of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry. Her mother (alive, 61) had breast cancer at 48 and has never been tested. Her older sister died of ovarian cancer at 44. A maternal aunt (mother's sister) had breast cancer at 52. Her maternal grandmother died of an unknown cancer in her 60s. Maya's father (65) is healthy with no cancers in his family. Maya has a healthy brother (36) and no personal history of cancer. Her mammogram last year was normal.
step: Draw the pedigree with standard symbols (squares, circles, filled symbols for cancer with the age of diagnosis, a diagonal line for deceased, an arrow for Maya as consultand) and state which pattern of inheritance the family suggests and why (autosomal dominant, transmission through the maternal side, male relatives also at risk).

Explain why the best first test is a targeted or multigene panel in an affected relative (here, the mother, because the sister is deceased), why Ashkenazi ancestry matters (founder variants in BRCA1 and BRCA2), and why testing Maya first could give an uninformative negative.

Maya's targeted test for the same variant is positive, and her brother's targeted test is negative. State what each result means, including the risk for Maya's child (50% chance of inheriting the variant), the risk for the brother's children, and what a true negative means.

## Week-by-week key ideas

- **Week 1, patterns of inheritance:** autosomal dominant (50% risk, vertical transmission, male-to-male), autosomal recessive (25% for carrier parents, 2/3 for an unaffected sibling), X-linked recessive (no male-to-male transmission), X-linked dominant; penetrance versus expressivity; pleiotropy; heterogeneity; de novo variants and germline mosaicism.
- **Week 2, pedigrees and risk:** multiply along paths, add across paths; Bayesian table (prior, conditional, joint, posterior); unaffected children and age lower a carrier risk; coefficient of relationship and inbreeding coefficient (first cousins: 1/8 and 1/16).
- **Week 3, chromosomes:** nondisjunction and maternal age, trisomies 21, 18 and 13, Turner and Klinefelter, Robertsonian translocation (45 chromosomes, 10–15% risk if the mother carries a 14;21), 22q11.2 and 7q11.23 deletions.
- **Week 4, repeat disorders:** HTT CAG (40 or more, paternal expansion), FMR1 CGG (premutation 55–200, full mutation over 200, maternal expansion, methylation silencing), DMPK CTG, FXN GAA (recessive); anticipation.
- **Week 5, imprinting and mitochondria:** Prader–Willi (paternal loss), Angelman (maternal loss, UBE3A), methylation analysis first; maternal inheritance, heteroplasmy, threshold effect.
- **Week 6, populations:** $p+q=1$, $p^2+2pq+q^2=1$, carrier $\approx 2q$, X-linked male incidence $q$; residual risk after a negative screen; founder effect, heterozygote advantage.
- **Week 7, testing:** karyotype, FISH, microarray, panels, exome, genome; five-tier variant classification; sensitivity, specificity and PPV with prevalence.
- **Week 8, screening:** nuchal translucency, quad screen patterns, elevated AFP, cell-free DNA as a screen, CVS and amniocentesis, newborn screening.
- **Week 9, cancer:** two-hit hypothesis, BRCA1/2 and PARP inhibitors, Lynch tumor testing, FAP, Li–Fraumeni.
- **Week 10, pharmacogenomics:** prodrug versus active drug, CYP2C19 with clopidogrel, CYP2D6 with codeine, HLA-B*57:01, TPMT, DPYD.
- **Week 11, syndromes:** Marfan, NF1 criteria, tuberous sclerosis, Williams, Noonan, achondroplasia, cystic fibrosis.
- **Week 12, counseling and ethics:** nondirective counseling, absolute risk, SPIKES, testing minors, GINA limits.

## Common errors

- Using 1/2 rather than 2/3 for the unaffected sibling of a person with a recessive disease.
- Forgetting the conditional step in Bayesian risk.
- Reversing the parent of origin (paternal expansion in HD, maternal in fragile X, loss of the paternal copy in Prader–Willi).
- Quoting a rounded carrier frequency as the disease incidence.
- Ignoring prevalence when asked for predictive values.

@exam self
prompt: A 28-year-old man has a father with Huntington disease (diagnosed at 46; CAG repeat length 44 on testing). The man has no symptoms and requests predictive testing. He asks how the repeat length might have changed from his grandfather (the father's father, who had onset at 62 with a repeat length of 38). Write a counseling plan that (a) explains anticipation using this family, (b) states his own risk before testing, (c) describes the steps and safeguards of predictive testing, and (d) names the implications of a positive and a negative result, including insurance.
solution: (a) The CAG repeat in HTT is unstable and tends to expand when passed on by a father: from 38 in the grandfather (reduced-penetrance range, onset at 62) to 44 in the father (full-penetrance range, onset at 46), which explains the earlier onset; his own repeat, if he inherited the allele, could be larger still, so onset could be earlier than his father's, but the exact age cannot be predicted from repeat length alone. (b) His prior risk is 50%, since his father is an affected heterozygote and each child inherits the expanded allele with probability one half. (c) Predictive testing follows a protocol: pretest counseling about the meaning, limits and psychological risks; a support person; neurological and psychological assessment; informed consent with the right to withdraw or not to know; disclosure in person with follow-up; and no testing of minors. He should consider insurance before testing: GINA protects health insurance and employment (employers with 15 or more employees) but not life, disability or long-term care insurance. (d) A positive result (40 or more repeats) means he will develop Huntington disease with certainty at some age, there is no cure, his children each have a 50% risk, and reproductive options (prenatal diagnosis, preimplantation testing) and planning can be discussed. A negative result (no expanded allele) returns his risk to the population level and his children are not at increased risk, but it can still bring guilt or distress, so follow-up is offered.

rubric: Describes protocol safeguards (counseling, support person, consent, follow-up) and no testing of minors.
rubric: Discusses implications of positive and negative results, reproductive options and the limits of GINA.
