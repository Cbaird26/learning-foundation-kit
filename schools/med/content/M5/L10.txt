@article
**Pharmacogenomics** studies how germline variation affects drug response. Effects come from three kinds of genes: **pharmacokinetic** (metabolism and transport, which change drug levels), **pharmacodynamic** (the drug target), and **immune** genes (the HLA alleles that predispose to hypersensitivity).

### Metabolizer phenotypes

Most variants in drug-metabolizing enzymes either lose function or, less often, increase it. The resulting phenotype has five usual labels: **poor** (no activity), **intermediate**, **normal**, **rapid** and **ultrarapid** metabolizer. For *CYP2D6*, each allele is assigned an **activity value** (for example, fully functional alleles such as *1 and *2 score 1; the reduced-function allele *10 scores 0.25 and *41 scores 0.5; non-functional alleles such as *4 and *5 (gene deletion) score 0), and the genotype's **activity score** is the sum for the two alleles. Copy-number duplications of a functional allele add its value again. In the current consensus, an activity score of 0 is a poor metabolizer, above 0 and below 1.25 intermediate, 1.25–2.25 normal, and above 2.25 ultrarapid. Phenotype frequencies follow Hardy–Weinberg: if the frequency of non-functional alleles is $q$, then poor metabolizers are $q^2$ and intermediate metabolizers (one non-functional allele) are $2pq$.

### What the effect on the drug depends on

- A **prodrug** needs enzymatic activation, so a poor metabolizer gets **less** active drug (clopidogrel, codeine, tamoxifen), and an ultrarapid metabolizer more.
- An **active drug** cleared by the enzyme accumulates in a poor metabolizer (toxicity) and is cleared faster in an ultrarapid one (treatment failure).

### CYP2C19 and clopidogrel

Clopidogrel is a prodrug whose active thiol metabolite irreversibly blocks the platelet P2Y12 receptor. Activation requires **CYP2C19**. The loss-of-function alleles ***2** and ***3** (allele frequency of *2 is about 15% in people of European ancestry and up to 30% or more in East Asian populations) reduce active metabolite formation, so **intermediate and poor metabolizers have higher platelet reactivity and more major cardiovascular events and stent thrombosis** after percutaneous coronary intervention. The label has a boxed warning, and guidelines suggest using **prasugrel or ticagrelor** instead for patients with acute coronary syndrome or stenting who are CYP2C19 poor or intermediate metabolizers, if there is no contraindication (history of stroke or transient ischemic attack for prasugrel, high bleeding risk). The *17 allele increases function (rapid metabolizer). CYP2C19 also affects some proton pump inhibitors, voriconazole and citalopram.

### CYP2D6

CYP2D6 converts **codeine** (and tramadol) to the active opioid **morphine**. Poor metabolizers get little analgesia; **ultrarapid metabolizers** can have morphine toxicity, including life-threatening respiratory depression in children after tonsillectomy and in infants of breastfeeding mothers. Codeine is therefore contraindicated in children under 12 years, and it should be avoided in ultrarapid metabolizers and used with caution for others. CYP2D6 also activates tamoxifen to endoxifen (the effect of genotype on outcomes is debated; strong CYP2D6 inhibitors such as paroxetine and fluoxetine should be avoided with tamoxifen).

Genotyping can be **preemptive** (a panel result stored in the record for later) or **reactive**. Guidelines from the Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium give gene–drug recommendations; clinical use requires checking the current ones.

@example
title: Phenotype frequencies from allele frequency
problem: In a population the frequency of *CYP2C19* loss-of-function alleles (*2 and *3 combined) is $0.3$. What fraction are poor metabolizers and what fraction are intermediate metabolizers (one loss-of-function allele and one normal allele)?
step: Let $q=0.3$ be the loss-of-function frequency and $p=0.7$ the normal-function frequency.
step: Poor metabolizers carry two loss-of-function alleles: $q^2=0.09$, or 9%.
step: Intermediate metabolizers carry one: $2pq=2\times0.7\times0.3=0.42$, or 42%.
step: Altogether about half of this population has reduced activation of clopidogrel.

@example
title: A poor metabolizer after coronary stenting
problem: A 61-year-old man receives a drug-eluting stent for an acute myocardial infarction. Genotyping shows CYP2C19 *2/*2. What is the recommended antiplatelet choice?
step: *2/*2 is a poor metabolizer of CYP2C19, so clopidogrel, a prodrug, will not be adequately activated.
step: Platelet inhibition is reduced and the risk of stent thrombosis and recurrent events rises.
step: Choose an alternative P2Y12 inhibitor (prasugrel or ticagrelor) with aspirin, unless contraindicated by prior intracranial hemorrhage, stroke or high bleeding risk.
step: Prasugrel is contraindicated after a stroke or transient ischemic attack, so check his history first.

@mistakes
- Assuming that every poor metabolizer is at risk of toxicity; for a prodrug the poor metabolizer fails treatment.
- Applying the same rule to codeine in poor and ultrarapid metabolizers (opposite problems).
- Forgetting that HLA screening is done before starting abacavir.
- Treating pharmacogenomic results as substitutes for clinical monitoring (INR, response, bleeding).

@practice self
prompt: Explain, in a short note for a colleague, (a) how BRCA1/2 testing is used and what a positive result changes, and (b) how CYP2C19 genotype changes antiplatelet therapy after coronary stenting. Include the mechanism in each case.
solution: (a) BRCA1 and BRCA2 are tumor suppressor genes needed for homologous recombination repair. Testing is offered to people with suggestive personal or family history (early or multiple breast cancers, ovarian cancer, male breast cancer, founder ancestry); ideally an affected relative is tested first and relatives are then offered targeted testing. A pathogenic variant raises lifetime risk of breast cancer (about 55–72% for BRCA1 and 45–69% for BRCA2) and ovarian cancer (about 39–44% for BRCA1, 11–17% for BRCA2) and changes management: enhanced surveillance with MRI, risk-reducing mastectomy and salpingo-oophorectomy options (timed after childbearing), PARP inhibitor treatment for BRCA-associated cancers, and cascade testing of relatives, who each have a 50% chance of carrying it. (b) Clopidogrel is a prodrug activated by CYP2C19. People with loss-of-function alleles (*2 or *3) make less active metabolite, so platelets are inhibited less and stent thrombosis and cardiovascular events rise. For poor and intermediate metabolizers with acute coronary syndrome or stenting, guidelines suggest prasugrel or ticagrelor (not dependent on CYP2C19) when there is no contraindication.
