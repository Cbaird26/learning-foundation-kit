@article
Most ATP in aerobic cells is made by **oxidative phosphorylation** at the inner mitochondrial membrane. NADH and FADH$_2$ from glycolysis, the citric acid cycle and fatty acid oxidation donate electrons to a chain of carriers that ends at oxygen. The energy released pumps protons out of the matrix; the return of protons through **ATP synthase** makes ATP. This is the **chemiosmotic** mechanism: the **proton-motive force** (a pH gradient plus a membrane potential) couples oxidation to phosphorylation.

### The electron transport chain

| Complex | Electron donor | Protons pumped (per 2 e$^-$) |
|---|---|---|
| **I** (NADH dehydrogenase) | NADH, passes e$^-$ to coenzyme Q (ubiquinone) | 4 |
| **II** (succinate dehydrogenase) | FADH$_2$ from succinate, passes e$^-$ to CoQ | 0 |
| **III** (cytochrome $bc_1$) | CoQH$_2$, passes e$^-$ to cytochrome c | 4 |
| **IV** (cytochrome c oxidase) | cytochrome c, passes e$^-$ to O$_2$ forming H$_2$O | 2 |

Electrons from NADH enter at complex I and pass through I, III and IV: about 10 protons pumped. Electrons from FADH$_2$ enter at complex II and skip complex I: about 6 protons. Oxygen is the final electron acceptor; four electrons reduce one O$_2$ to two H$_2$O.

### ATP synthase and yield

ATP synthase (complex V) has an F$_0$ portion that conducts protons and an F$_1$ portion that makes ATP. Roughly 4 protons flow per ATP made (including the proton cost of moving phosphate and ATP/ADP across the membrane). Therefore:

$$\text{NADH} \approx \frac{10}{4} = 2.5\ \text{ATP}, \qquad \text{FADH}_2 \approx \frac{6}{4} = 1.5\ \text{ATP}.$$

Older textbooks use 3 and 2; current texts and exam questions most often use 2.5 and 1.5, so check which convention a question states.

**Shuttles.** The inner membrane is impermeable to NADH. The **malate-aspartate shuttle** (heart, liver) carries cytosolic NADH electrons into the matrix as NADH (2.5 ATP each). The **glycerol 3-phosphate shuttle** (skeletal muscle, brain) delivers them as FADH$_2$ (1.5 ATP each). Complete oxidation of one glucose therefore yields about 30 to 32 ATP (using 2.5 ATP per NADH and 1.5 per FADH$_2$, with 1 ATP per GTP), not the 36 to 38 of the older 3 and 2 convention.

### Inhibitors and uncouplers

- **Complex I inhibitors:** rotenone, amobarbital.
- **Complex III inhibitor:** antimycin A.
- **Complex IV inhibitors:** cyanide, azide, carbon monoxide. Electron flow stops, and cells shift to glycolysis with lactic acidosis. Hydroxocobalamin or nitrites plus thiosulfate are antidotes for cyanide.
- **ATP synthase inhibitor:** oligomycin. Protons cannot return, the gradient builds, and electron transport slows. **Respiratory control** means that the rate of electron transport depends on ADP availability.
- **Uncouplers:** 2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP), FCCP and high-dose aspirin carry protons across the membrane. The gradient collapses, so oxygen consumption **increases**, ATP synthesis **falls**, and the energy is released as **heat**. Patients develop fever and sweating.
- **Thermogenin (UCP1)** in brown fat is a physiologic uncoupler used in newborn heat production.

The key contrast: **inhibitors** of electron transport stop oxygen consumption; **uncouplers** accelerate it while ATP falls.

@example
title: ATP yield from glucose with the malate-aspartate shuttle
problem: Using 2.5 ATP/NADH, 1.5 ATP/FADH$_2$ and counting cytosolic NADH via the malate-aspartate shuttle, how many ATP does one glucose yield?
step: Glycolysis: 2 ATP and 2 NADH. The NADH, through malate-aspartate, give $2 \times 2.5 = 5$.
step: PDH: 2 NADH, so $2 \times 2.5 = 5$.
step: Citric acid cycle (two turns): 6 NADH $= 15$, 2 FADH$_2$ $= 3$, and 2 GTP $= 2$.
step: Total $= 2 + 5 + 5 + 15 + 3 + 2 = 32$ ATP. With the glycerol 3-phosphate shuttle the glycolytic NADH gives $2 \times 1.5 = 3$, total $30$.

@mistakes
- Saying uncouplers inhibit the electron transport chain; they accelerate it while preventing ATP synthesis.
- Forgetting that electrons from FADH$_2$ bypass complex I, so they yield fewer protons pumped.
- Believing oligomycin acts on the electron carriers; it blocks the F$_0$ proton channel of ATP synthase.
- Using 36 to 38 ATP per glucose without noting the convention; check whether the question uses 2.5 and 1.5 ATP per carrier.

@practice self
prompt: Isolated mitochondria with ADP, phosphate and succinate are consuming oxygen. Predict what happens to oxygen consumption and ATP synthesis when you add (a) oligomycin, then (b) FCCP, and in a separate tube, (c) cyanide. Explain each in terms of the proton gradient.
solution: (a) Oligomycin blocks the F$_0$ channel of ATP synthase. Protons can no longer return, the gradient rises, and respiration slows (respiratory control); ATP synthesis stops. (b) FCCP is an uncoupler: it carries protons across the membrane, so the gradient collapses, respiration speeds up even with oligomycin present, and ATP synthesis remains absent; energy is released as heat. (c) Cyanide inhibits cytochrome c oxidase, so electron flow and oxygen consumption stop; the gradient dissipates and ATP synthesis ceases.
rubric: States that oligomycin stops ATP synthesis and slows oxygen consumption because the proton gradient builds up.
rubric: States that FCCP increases oxygen consumption while preventing ATP synthesis and generates heat.
rubric: States that cyanide stops oxygen consumption by blocking complex IV.
rubric: Explains each effect through the proton gradient or electron flow.
hint: Decide first whether each agent blocks electron flow or only the return of protons.
hint: Remember that an uncoupler works even when ATP synthase is blocked.

@quiz num
prompt: A mitochondrial preparation oxidizes 4 mol NADH (entering at complex I) and 6 mol FADH$_2$. Using 2.5 ATP per NADH and 1.5 per FADH$_2$, how many mol ATP are made by oxidative phosphorylation?
answer: 19
check: 4 * 2.5 + 6 * 1.5
hint: Multiply each carrier by its yield.
solution: $4(2.5) + 6(1.5) = 10 + 9 = 19$ mol ATP.
