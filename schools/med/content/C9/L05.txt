@article
Glucose is the central fuel of human metabolism. The brain normally depends on it, and red blood cells can use nothing else. **Glycolysis** converts one 6-carbon glucose into two 3-carbon pyruvate molecules in the cytosol, capturing a little energy as ATP and NADH.

### Carbohydrates in the diet

Dietary starch and glycogen are chains of glucose; sucrose is glucose plus fructose; lactose is glucose plus galactose. Pancreatic amylase and brush-border disaccharidases (sucrase, lactase and others) yield monosaccharides, which enter enterocytes through SGLT1 (glucose and galactose, sodium-coupled) and GLUT5 (fructose). Glucose enters cells by facilitated diffusion through **GLUT** transporters: GLUT4 in muscle and fat is insulin-dependent; GLUT2 in liver and pancreatic beta cells has a high Km and tracks blood glucose; GLUT1 serves red cells and the blood-brain barrier.

### Two phases of glycolysis

**Investment phase.** Glucose is phosphorylated to glucose 6-phosphate (G6P) by **hexokinase** (or **glucokinase** in liver and beta cells), isomerized to fructose 6-phosphate, and phosphorylated again by **phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1)** to fructose 1,6-bisphosphate. Two ATP are spent. Aldolase splits the six-carbon sugar into two triose phosphates, which interconvert (triose phosphate isomerase), so everything below runs twice per glucose.

**Payoff phase.** **Glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase** oxidizes G3P and adds phosphate to form 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate, producing NADH. Phosphoglycerate kinase transfers a phosphate to ADP (the first **substrate-level phosphorylation**). After rearrangement to phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP), **pyruvate kinase** produces pyruvate and a second ATP.

$$\text{Glucose} + 2\,\mathrm{NAD^+} + 2\,\mathrm{ADP} + 2\,\mathrm{P_i} \to 2\,\text{pyruvate} + 2\,\mathrm{NADH} + 2\,\mathrm{ATP} + 2\,\mathrm{H_2O} + 2\,\mathrm{H^+}$$

Four ATP are made and two are spent, so the **net yield is 2 ATP and 2 NADH per glucose**.

### Irreversible steps and regulation

Three reactions have large negative $\Delta G$ and are the control points:

| Enzyme | Activators | Inhibitors |
|---|---|---|
| Hexokinase | none notable | Glucose 6-phosphate (glucokinase is not inhibited by G6P; it is regulated by a regulatory protein and by induction by insulin) |
| **PFK-1** (rate-limiting) | AMP, fructose 2,6-bisphosphate | ATP, citrate |
| Pyruvate kinase | Fructose 1,6-bisphosphate (feed-forward); insulin (dephosphorylation) | ATP, alanine; glucagon (phosphorylation, in liver) |

**Fructose 2,6-bisphosphate** is the most potent activator of PFK-1 and is made by PFK-2, which is turned on by insulin. High ATP signals that energy is plentiful, so PFK-1 slows.

### Fates of pyruvate

With oxygen and mitochondria, pyruvate enters the mitochondrion for pyruvate dehydrogenase and the citric acid cycle. Without them (red blood cells, or exercising muscle that outruns oxygen delivery) pyruvate is reduced to **lactate** by lactate dehydrogenase. This reaction regenerates NAD$^+$, which GAPDH requires, so glycolysis can continue. In yeast, pyruvate is converted to ethanol.

### Clinical connections

- **Pyruvate kinase deficiency** is an autosomal recessive cause of hemolytic anemia: red cells lack mitochondria, so they rely on glycolytic ATP to maintain membrane pumps, and ATP-depleted cells are destroyed in the spleen.
- **Galactosemia** (classic form: galactose 1-phosphate uridyltransferase deficiency) presents in neonates after milk feeding with jaundice, hepatomegaly, vomiting and cataracts; urine tests positive for reducing substances but negative for glucose oxidase. Treatment is to remove galactose and lactose from the diet.
- **Fructose intolerance** (aldolase B deficiency) causes hypoglycemia and liver injury after fructose or sucrose is introduced.
- **Diabetic cataracts** result from aldose reductase converting excess glucose to sorbitol in the lens.

@example
title: Accounting for ATP and NADH in glycolysis
problem: Find the net ATP and NADH produced when one glucose is converted to two pyruvate.
step: Investment: hexokinase uses 1 ATP and PFK-1 uses 1 ATP, so $-2$ ATP.
step: Each glucose gives 2 G3P. Each G3P gives 1 NADH (GAPDH): $2 \times 1 = 2$ NADH.
step: Each G3P gives 1 ATP at phosphoglycerate kinase and 1 ATP at pyruvate kinase: $2 \times 2 = +4$ ATP.
step: Net ATP $= 4 - 2 = 2$; NADH $= 2$. Under anaerobic conditions the 2 NADH are reoxidized as 2 pyruvate become 2 lactate.

@mistakes
- Forgetting that everything after aldolase happens twice per glucose.
- Calling hexokinase or pyruvate kinase the rate-limiting step; it is PFK-1.
- Saying lactate formation produces extra ATP; its purpose is to regenerate NAD$^+$.
- Treating glucokinase and hexokinase as identical: glucokinase has a much higher (approximate half-saturation) Km and is not inhibited by G6P.
