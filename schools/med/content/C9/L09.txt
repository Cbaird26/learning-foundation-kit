@article
Triglycerides in adipose tissue store far more energy than glycogen does: fat yields about 9 kcal/g, versus about 4 kcal/g for carbohydrate or protein, and it is stored without water. Most tissues oxidize fatty acids; the brain does not do so directly, and red cells cannot.

### Mobilization and activation

In fasting, glucagon and epinephrine activate **hormone-sensitive lipase** in adipocytes through cAMP and PKA; insulin inhibits it. Triglycerides are hydrolyzed to glycerol (which goes to the liver for gluconeogenesis) and free fatty acids bound to albumin. In the cytosol, **acyl-CoA synthetase** activates a fatty acid to fatty acyl-CoA, using ATP and producing AMP + PP$_i$: this costs **2 high-energy phosphate bonds**.

### The carnitine shuttle

Long-chain acyl-CoA cannot cross the inner mitochondrial membrane. **CPT-I** (on the outer membrane) swaps CoA for carnitine; a translocase carries acylcarnitine in; **CPT-II** restores acyl-CoA in the matrix. **CPT-I is the rate-limiting, regulated step**, inhibited by **malonyl-CoA**, which is made by acetyl-CoA carboxylase during fatty acid synthesis. Thus in the fed state, synthesis switches off oxidation. Carnitine deficiency and CPT-II deficiency cause muscle weakness and cramps with fasting or exercise.

### Beta-oxidation

Each cycle in the matrix shortens the chain by two carbons, in four steps:

1. **Acyl-CoA dehydrogenase** forms a trans double bond, producing **FADH$_2$**.
2. **Enoyl-CoA hydratase** adds water.
3. **3-Hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase** oxidizes the alcohol, producing **NADH**.
4. **Thiolase** cleaves off **acetyl-CoA** using a new CoA.

For a saturated fatty acid with $n$ carbons ($n$ even): number of cycles $= n/2 - 1$; acetyl-CoA $= n/2$; FADH$_2$ and NADH $= n/2 - 1$ each. Each acetyl-CoA yields 10 ATP through the citric acid cycle (using 2.5 per NADH, 1.5 per FADH$_2$), and each cycle yields $1.5 + 2.5 = 4$ ATP. For palmitate (C16):

$$\text{ATP} = 8(10) + 7(1.5 + 2.5) - 2 = 80 + 28 - 2 = 106.$$

This net 106 ATP uses 2.5 per NADH and 1.5 per FADH$_2$ (the older 3 and 2 convention gives 129).

**Odd-chain fatty acids** end with a three-carbon **propionyl-CoA**: propionyl-CoA carboxylase (biotin) gives methylmalonyl-CoA, an epimerase and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase (**vitamin B$_{12}$**) give succinyl-CoA, which enters the cycle. Vitamin B$_{12}$ deficiency raises methylmalonic acid. Unsaturated fatty acids need additional isomerase and reductase steps. Very-long-chain fatty acids start in peroxisomes (defective in Zellweger syndrome), and branched phytanic acid undergoes alpha-oxidation (defective in Refsum disease).

**MCAD deficiency** is the most common fatty acid oxidation disorder: fasting in infancy causes vomiting, lethargy and **hypoketotic hypoglycemia** with dicarboxylic aciduria, because the liver cannot oxidize medium-chain fatty acids or make ketones, and glucose use rises.

### Ketone bodies

In prolonged fasting, hepatic acetyl-CoA outstrips the cycle (oxaloacetate is diverted to gluconeogenesis) and is converted to **ketone bodies**: acetoacetate, 3-hydroxybutyrate and acetone. The key enzyme is mitochondrial **HMG-CoA synthase**. Heart, muscle and, in prolonged fasting, brain use ketones; the liver cannot, because it lacks **succinyl-CoA:acetoacetate CoA transferase (thiophorase)**. Red cells cannot, as they lack mitochondria. In uncontrolled type 1 diabetes, low insulin raises lipolysis, and diabetic ketoacidosis results.

### Fatty acid and cholesterol synthesis

In the fed state, mitochondrial acetyl-CoA leaves as **citrate**, which cytosolic ATP-citrate lyase splits back into acetyl-CoA. **Acetyl-CoA carboxylase** (biotin; rate-limiting) forms malonyl-CoA; it is activated by citrate and insulin and inhibited by palmitoyl-CoA and AMP-activated protein kinase. **Fatty acid synthase** builds palmitate using NADPH, from the pentose phosphate pathway and malic enzyme. Cholesterol synthesis is controlled by **HMG-CoA reductase**, inhibited by statins and by cholesterol, and induced by insulin.

@example
title: Beta-oxidation of decanoic acid (C10)
problem: Count the cycles, acetyl-CoA, FADH$_2$, NADH and net ATP from one decanoic acid.
step: Cycles $= 10/2 - 1 = 4$. Acetyl-CoA $= 10/2 = 5$. FADH$_2 = 4$ and NADH $= 4$.
step: Acetyl-CoA through the citric acid cycle: $5 \times 10 = 50$ ATP.
step: FADH$_2$ and NADH: $4 \times 1.5 + 4 \times 2.5 = 16$ ATP.
step: Subtract 2 for activation: $50 + 16 - 2 = 64$ net ATP.

@mistakes
- Using $n/2$ cycles; the last cycle yields two acetyl-CoA, so the cycles number $n/2 - 1$.
- Forgetting the 2 ATP-equivalents for activation (ATP to AMP).
- Saying malonyl-CoA inhibits CPT-II or acetyl-CoA carboxylase; it inhibits CPT-I.
- Claiming the liver burns ketone bodies; it makes them but lacks thiophorase.
