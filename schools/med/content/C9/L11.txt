@article
Nucleotides are building blocks of DNA and RNA and also of ATP, GTP, NAD$^+$, FAD and coenzyme A. Cells can make them **de novo** or recover bases by **salvage**. Rapidly dividing cells (marrow, gut, cancers, lymphocytes) depend on these pathways, which is why so many chemotherapy and immunosuppressive drugs target them.

### Purine synthesis

Purines are built on a ribose scaffold. **PRPP** (5-phosphoribosyl-1-pyrophosphate) is made from ribose 5-phosphate by PRPP synthetase. The committed step is **glutamine-PRPP amidotransferase**, which forms phosphoribosylamine; it is inhibited by AMP, GMP and IMP (feedback). Glycine, glutamine, aspartate, CO$_2$ and **N$^{10}$-formyl tetrahydrofolate** supply atoms for the first purine product, **inosine monophosphate (IMP)**, which is then converted to **AMP** (GTP-dependent) or **GMP** (ATP-dependent), a reciprocal arrangement that balances the two pools.

**Salvage.** **HGPRT** (hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase) returns hypoxanthine and guanine to IMP and GMP using PRPP; APRT does the same for adenine. **Lesch-Nyhan syndrome** (X-linked recessive HGPRT deficiency) causes overproduction of purines and uric acid: gout, dystonia, intellectual disability and self-mutilating behavior in a boy.

### Purine degradation and gout

AMP and GMP are degraded to hypoxanthine and guanine, then xanthine, and finally **uric acid** through **xanthine oxidase**. Adenosine deaminase (ADA) converts adenosine to inosine. **ADA deficiency** leads to dATP accumulation, which inhibits ribonucleotide reductase and kills lymphocytes: a cause of severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID).

Uric acid is a weak acid with a pKa of about 5.8, so it is less soluble when the pH is low. **Gout** is inflammation caused by monosodium urate crystals in joints (needle-shaped, negatively birefringent under polarized light), typically the first metatarsophalangeal joint. Causes include underexcretion (most common; diuretics, renal disease) and overproduction (Lesch-Nyhan, tumor lysis, high cell turnover). Management: acute attacks use NSAIDs, colchicine or glucocorticoids; long-term prevention uses **allopurinol** or febuxostat (xanthine oxidase inhibitors) or probenecid (uricosuric); **rasburicase** (urate oxidase) is used in tumor lysis syndrome. Allopurinol lowers urate while raising hypoxanthine and xanthine, which are excreted instead.

### Pyrimidine synthesis

Pyrimidines are built first and then attached to ribose. **Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase II (CPS II)**, in the cytosol, uses **glutamine** (not free ammonia) and is the regulated step: activated by PRPP and inhibited by UTP. Carbamoyl phosphate and aspartate produce **orotic acid**. **UMP synthase** attaches PRPP and decarboxylates it to UMP. **Hereditary orotic aciduria** (UMP synthase deficiency) causes megaloblastic anemia that does not respond to B$_{12}$ or folate, with orotic acid in the urine and failure to thrive; unlike OTC deficiency there is **no hyperammonemia**. Treatment is oral uridine.

### Deoxynucleotides and thymine

**Ribonucleotide reductase** converts ribonucleoside diphosphates to deoxyribonucleoside diphosphates; hydroxyurea inhibits it, and dATP provides feedback inhibition. **Thymidylate synthase** methylates dUMP to dTMP using **N$^{5}$,N$^{10}$-methylene tetrahydrofolate**, which is oxidized to dihydrofolate; **dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR)** regenerates tetrahydrofolate. **5-Fluorouracil** (via FdUMP) inhibits thymidylate synthase, and **methotrexate** (human), **trimethoprim** (bacterial) and pyrimethamine (protozoal) inhibit DHFR. These drugs cause "thymineless death" in rapidly dividing cells and an anemia resembling folate deficiency.

@example
title: Fraction of uric acid in the poorly soluble form
problem: Urine pH is $5.0$ and uric acid has a pKa of $5.8$. What fraction of urate is in the undissociated uric acid form?
step: Use $\mathrm{pH} = \mathrm{p}K_a + \log([A^-]/[HA])$. Then $\log([A^-]/[HA]) = 5.0 - 5.8 = -0.8$.
step: The ratio is $10^{-0.8} = 0.158$.
step: The fraction in the acid (HA) form is $1/(1 + 0.158) = 0.863$.
step: About 86% is the poorly soluble undissociated form, which is why urinary alkalinization (pH above about 6.5) helps prevent uric acid stones.

@mistakes
- Mixing up CPS I and CPS II: CPS I uses ammonia in the urea cycle; CPS II uses glutamine in pyrimidine synthesis.
- Saying allopurinol is uricosuric; it blocks production of uric acid by inhibiting xanthine oxidase.
- Expecting hyperammonemia in orotic aciduria; ammonia is normal.
- Forgetting that purines are built on PRPP, whereas pyrimidines get ribose after the ring is formed.
