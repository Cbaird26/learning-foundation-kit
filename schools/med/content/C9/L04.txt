@article
Cells must turn enzymes up and down in seconds. The tools are inhibitors, allosteric effectors, covalent modification and proteolytic activation. Drug action is largely enzyme inhibition, so these ideas are heavily tested in medicine.

### The Lineweaver-Burk plot

Taking the reciprocal of the Michaelis-Menten equation gives a straight line:

$$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{K_m}{V_{max}}\cdot\frac{1}{[S]} + \frac{1}{V_{max}}.$$

Plot $1/v$ (y-axis) against $1/[S]$ (x-axis):

- **y-intercept** $= 1/V_{max}$
- **x-intercept** $= -1/K_m$
- **slope** $= K_m/V_{max}$

So $V_{max} = 1/(\text{y-intercept})$, $K_m = -1/(\text{x-intercept})$, and also $K_m = \text{slope}\times V_{max}$. The plot is good for diagnosing inhibitor type; it is poor for accurate fitting because small velocities at low $[S]$ are magnified, so modern analysis uses nonlinear regression.

### Reversible inhibition

| Type | Binds | $K_m$ (apparent) | $V_{max}$ (apparent) | Plot lines | Overcome by more $[S]$? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Competitive | Free enzyme, active site | Increases | Unchanged | Cross on the y-axis | Yes |
| Noncompetitive | E and ES equally, a separate site | Unchanged | Decreases | Cross on the x-axis | No |
| Uncompetitive | ES only | Decreases | Decreases | Parallel lines | No |

With a competitive inhibitor of concentration $[I]$ and inhibition constant $K_i$, the apparent Michaelis constant is

$$K_m^{app} = K_m\left(1 + \frac{[I]}{K_i}\right).$$

Competitive inhibition looks like "needing more substrate to reach the same velocity." Clinical examples: fomepizole competes with ethylene glycol and methanol at alcohol dehydrogenase; statins compete with HMG-CoA at HMG-CoA reductase; methotrexate competes with dihydrofolate at DHFR. A noncompetitive (or mixed) inhibitor effectively removes active enzyme, reducing Vmax like lowering $[E]_T$. **Irreversible inhibitors** form covalent bonds, such as aspirin on cyclooxygenase and fluorouracil's metabolite on thymidylate synthase.

### Allosteric regulation

**Allosteric** enzymes have regulatory sites separate from the active site and usually several subunits. Effectors stabilize either the low-affinity T form or the high-affinity R form. A positive effector shifts the sigmoidal velocity curve to the left (lower $K_{0.5}$), and a negative effector shifts it to the right. Because the curve is sigmoidal, a small change in $[S]$ or effector gives a large change in velocity. Allosteric enzymes occupy control points in pathways, and **feedback inhibition** by end products is common.

### Covalent modification and zymogens

Phosphorylation by kinases and removal of phosphate by phosphatases switches enzymes on or off, as with glycogen phosphorylase. **Zymogens** are inactive precursors activated by cleavage: trypsinogen to trypsin (by enteropeptidase), and the clotting cascade. Unwanted activation of trypsinogen inside the pancreas contributes to pancreatitis. Regulation can also be slow: **induction** or repression of enzyme synthesis changes $[E]_T$ over hours.

@example
title: Km and Vmax from Lineweaver-Burk data
problem: A Lineweaver-Burk plot of an enzyme is a straight line through $(1/[S], 1/v) = (0.10\ \mathrm{mM^{-1}},\ 0.030)$ and $(0.30\ \mathrm{mM^{-1}},\ 0.050)$, with $1/v$ in $\min/\mu\mathrm{mol}$. Find Vmax and Km.
step: Find the slope: $(0.050 - 0.030)/(0.30 - 0.10) = 0.020/0.20 = 0.10$.
step: Find the y-intercept using the first point: $b = 0.030 - 0.10(0.10) = 0.020$.
step: $V_{max} = 1/b = 1/0.020 = 50\ \mu\mathrm{mol/min}$.
step: $K_m = \text{slope} \times V_{max} = 0.10 \times 50 = 5$ mM. Check with the x-intercept: $-b/\text{slope} = -0.20$, and $-1/(-0.20) = 5$.

@mistakes
- Reading the x-intercept as $1/K_m$; it is $-1/K_m$ (negative).
- Reporting the y-intercept as Vmax; it is $1/V_{max}$.
- Saying a competitive inhibitor lowers Vmax; Vmax is unchanged, and only the apparent Km rises.
- Assuming that more substrate overcomes noncompetitive inhibition; it does not, because the inhibitor removes active enzyme.

@practice num
prompt: A Lineweaver-Burk plot crosses the y-axis at $0.020\ \min/\mu\mathrm{mol}$. What is $V_{max}$ in $\mu\mathrm{mol/min}$?
answer: 50
check: 1 / 0.020
tol: 1%
unit: µmol/min
hint: The y-intercept is the reciprocal of one parameter.
solution: y-intercept $= 1/V_{max}$, so $V_{max} = 1/0.020 = 50\ \mu\mathrm{mol/min}$.

@practice num
prompt: A Lineweaver-Burk line crosses the x-axis at $-0.50\ \mathrm{mM^{-1}}$. What is $K_m$ in mM?
answer: 2
check: -1 / -0.50
tol: 1%
unit: mM
hint: The x-intercept equals $-1/K_m$.
solution: $-1/K_m = -0.50$, so $K_m = 2$ mM.

@practice num
prompt: A Lineweaver-Burk line passes through $(1/[S], 1/v) = (0.20, 0.030)$ and $(0.60, 0.050)$, with $[S]$ in mM and $v$ in $\mu\mathrm{mol/min}$. Find $K_m$ in mM.
answer: 2.5
check: ((0.050 - 0.030) / (0.60 - 0.20)) / (0.030 - ((0.050 - 0.030) / (0.60 - 0.20)) * 0.20)
tol: 1%
unit: mM
hint: Slope $= K_m/V_{max}$ and the y-intercept $= 1/V_{max}$, so $K_m = \text{slope}/\text{intercept}$.
hint: Slope is $0.020/0.40 = 0.050$; intercept is $0.030 - 0.050(0.20) = 0.020$.
solution: Slope $= 0.050$, intercept $= 0.020$, so $V_{max} = 50$ and $K_m = 0.050 \times 50 = 2.5$ mM.

@practice mc
prompt: A drug is added to an enzyme assay. The Lineweaver-Burk line has the same y-intercept as the control but a smaller (less negative) x-intercept magnitude and a steeper slope. What type of inhibitor is it?
choice*: Competitive: Vmax is unchanged and the apparent Km is increased
choice: Noncompetitive: Km is unchanged and Vmax is decreased
choice: Uncompetitive: both Km and Vmax are decreased
choice: An allosteric activator that lowers Km
hint: The same y-intercept means the same Vmax.
solution: Same y-intercept means unchanged Vmax; a steeper slope with an intercept nearer zero on the x-axis means $-1/K_m^{app}$ has smaller magnitude, so Km has increased. This is competitive inhibition, and it can be overcome by raising substrate.

@practice mc
prompt: An enzyme's plot of velocity against substrate is sigmoidal, and the curve shifts to the right when a pathway end product accumulates. Which description fits?
choice: A competitive inhibitor binding the active site, analyzed with Michaelis-Menten kinetics
choice*: Allosteric feedback inhibition by an end product stabilizing the low-affinity T state of a multi-subunit enzyme
choice: Irreversible covalent inhibition
choice: Zymogen activation by proteolysis
hint: Sigmoidal curves signal cooperativity.
solution: A sigmoidal curve indicates a multi-subunit allosteric enzyme; the rightward shift (higher $K_{0.5}$) with an end product is negative allosteric (feedback) regulation.

@quiz num
prompt: An enzyme has $K_m = 2$ mM. A competitive inhibitor is present at $4\ \mu\mathrm{M}$ with $K_i = 2\ \mu\mathrm{M}$. What is the apparent Km in mM?
answer: 6
check: 2 * (1 + 4 / 2)
tol: 1%
unit: mM
hint: Use $K_m^{app} = K_m(1 + [I]/K_i)$.
hint: $[I]/K_i = 2$.
solution: $K_m^{app} = 2(1 + 4/2) = 6$ mM; Vmax is unchanged.

@quiz mc
prompt: A new drug reduces the Vmax of an enzyme without changing its Km. Adding more substrate does not restore the velocity. Which best describes the drug?
choice: A competitive inhibitor
choice*: A noncompetitive inhibitor that binds a site other than the active site on E and ES
choice: A substrate analogue binding only the active site
choice: An uncompetitive inhibitor, because Km is unchanged
hint: Which type leaves Km alone and lowers Vmax?
solution: Noncompetitive inhibitors lower Vmax with no change in Km and are not overcome by substrate. Uncompetitive inhibitors lower both Km and Vmax.
