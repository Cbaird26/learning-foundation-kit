@article
Glycolysis harvests only a small fraction of glucose's energy. The rest is extracted in mitochondria. The **citric acid cycle** (also called the TCA or Krebs cycle) oxidizes the two-carbon acetyl group of acetyl-CoA to two CO$_2$ and transfers its electrons to NAD$^+$ and FAD. These carriers, NADH and FADH$_2$, deliver electrons to the electron transport chain (next lesson). The cycle takes place in the mitochondrial matrix, and it needs oxygen only indirectly, because the carriers must be reoxidized.

### Pyruvate dehydrogenase: the link reaction

Pyruvate enters the matrix and is converted to acetyl-CoA by the **pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDH)**:

$$\text{pyruvate} + \mathrm{NAD^+} + \mathrm{CoA} \to \text{acetyl-CoA} + \mathrm{CO_2} + \mathrm{NADH}.$$

The complex has three enzymes (E1, E2, E3) and five cofactors: **thiamine pyrophosphate** (B$_1$), **lipoic acid**, **coenzyme A** (pantothenate, B$_5$), **FAD** (riboflavin, B$_2$) and **NAD$^+$** (niacin, B$_3$). The reaction is irreversible, so humans cannot convert acetyl-CoA back to pyruvate (and therefore to glucose). Regulation: PDH kinase phosphorylates and inactivates the complex when ATP, acetyl-CoA and NADH are high; pyruvate, ADP and Ca$^{2+}$ promote activity, and PDH phosphatase (stimulated by insulin and Ca$^{2+}$) reactivates it.

### The eight reactions

1. **Citrate synthase:** acetyl-CoA (2C) + oxaloacetate (4C) $\to$ citrate (6C).
2. **Aconitase:** citrate $\to$ isocitrate.
3. **Isocitrate dehydrogenase:** isocitrate $\to$ $\alpha$-ketoglutarate + CO$_2$ + **NADH**.
4. **$\alpha$-Ketoglutarate dehydrogenase:** $\alpha$-ketoglutarate $\to$ succinyl-CoA + CO$_2$ + **NADH**. This complex resembles PDH and uses the same five cofactors.
5. **Succinyl-CoA synthetase:** succinyl-CoA $\to$ succinate + **GTP** (substrate-level phosphorylation).
6. **Succinate dehydrogenase** (also complex II of the electron transport chain): succinate $\to$ fumarate + **FADH$_2$**.
7. **Fumarase:** fumarate $\to$ malate.
8. **Malate dehydrogenase:** malate $\to$ oxaloacetate + **NADH**.

Per acetyl-CoA: **3 NADH, 1 FADH$_2$, 1 GTP, 2 CO$_2$**. Per glucose, two pyruvate become two acetyl-CoA, so the cycle turns twice.

### Regulation

The cycle is controlled by energy state. The key irreversible, regulated enzymes are **citrate synthase** (inhibited by ATP, citrate, succinyl-CoA), **isocitrate dehydrogenase** (activated by ADP and Ca$^{2+}$; inhibited by ATP and NADH) and **$\alpha$-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase** (inhibited by succinyl-CoA, NADH; activated by Ca$^{2+}$). The main idea is that high NADH/NAD$^+$ and ATP/ADP ratios slow the cycle. Muscle contraction raises Ca$^{2+}$, which speeds the cycle at the same time as ATP is used.

### Anaplerosis and biosynthesis

Intermediates leave for other uses (citrate to fatty acid synthesis, $\alpha$-ketoglutarate to glutamate, succinyl-CoA to heme, oxaloacetate to glucose and aspartate). They must be replaced by **anaplerotic** reactions, chiefly **pyruvate carboxylase** (pyruvate + CO$_2$ + ATP $\to$ oxaloacetate; activated by acetyl-CoA).

### Clinical connections

- **Thiamine deficiency** (beriberi; Wernicke encephalopathy in alcohol use disorder) impairs PDH and $\alpha$-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, so brain and heart, with high energy demands, fail, and lactate rises. Glucose given before thiamine can worsen the presentation.
- **Arsenic** inhibits lipoic acid, blocking PDH and $\alpha$-KG dehydrogenase.
- **PDH deficiency** (X-linked) causes lactic acidosis and neurologic injury; a ketogenic diet, which bypasses PDH, is used.
- **Fluoroacetate** is converted to fluorocitrate, which inhibits aconitase.

@example
title: Reduced carriers from one glucose after glycolysis
problem: How many NADH and FADH$_2$ are produced when the two pyruvate molecules from one glucose are fully oxidized through PDH and the citric acid cycle?
step: PDH makes 1 NADH per pyruvate: $2 \times 1 = 2$ NADH.
step: One turn of the cycle makes 3 NADH and 1 FADH$_2$; two turns (one per acetyl-CoA) make $6$ NADH and $2$ FADH$_2$.
step: Totals: $2 + 6 = 8$ NADH and $2$ FADH$_2$ (plus 2 GTP from the cycle).
step: Carbon check: 2 pyruvate (6 C) leave as 6 CO$_2$ (2 from PDH and 4 from the cycle).

@mistakes
- Saying the cycle makes most ATP directly; only 1 GTP per turn is made by substrate-level phosphorylation, and the rest comes through the electron transport chain.
- Forgetting that PDH is an irreversible reaction, so fatty acids cannot give net glucose.
- Counting the CO$_2$ released at PDH as coming from the cycle.
- Assuming the two CO$_2$ released in a turn are the carbons of the acetyl-CoA that just entered; the entering carbons are incorporated into oxaloacetate and are released only in later turns.

@practice mc
prompt: Which reaction of the citric acid cycle produces FADH$_2$?
choice: Isocitrate dehydrogenase
choice: Malate dehydrogenase
choice*: Succinate dehydrogenase
choice: Fumarase
hint: This enzyme is also part of the electron transport chain.
solution: Succinate dehydrogenase (complex II) converts succinate to fumarate and reduces FAD to FADH$_2$. Isocitrate and malate dehydrogenases reduce NAD$^+$, and fumarase makes no reduced carrier.
