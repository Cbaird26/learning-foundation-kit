@article
Between meals, the brain and red cells still need glucose. The liver (and, to a lesser extent, the kidney cortex) supplies it first by breaking down glycogen and then by building new glucose from non-carbohydrate precursors: lactate, glycerol and glucogenic amino acids (especially alanine). Fatty acids cannot be net converted to glucose in humans, because acetyl-CoA carbons are lost as CO$_2$ in the citric acid cycle.

### Gluconeogenesis

Gluconeogenesis runs most of glycolysis backward. Seven glycolytic reactions are freely reversible and are shared. The three irreversible glycolytic steps are bypassed by four enzymes:

| Glycolysis (irreversible) | Gluconeogenic bypass | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Pyruvate kinase | **Pyruvate carboxylase** (biotin, ATP; mitochondria) then **PEPCK** (GTP; cytosol or mitochondria) | Liver, kidney |
| PFK-1 | **Fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase** | Cytosol |
| Hexokinase/glucokinase | **Glucose 6-phosphatase** | Endoplasmic reticulum, liver and kidney |

Muscle lacks glucose 6-phosphatase, so muscle glycogen cannot raise blood glucose; it feeds only the muscle's own glycolysis.

**Regulation reciprocal to glycolysis:**

- Pyruvate carboxylase is activated by **acetyl-CoA** (abundant when fatty acids are oxidized).
- Fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase is inhibited by AMP and by **fructose 2,6-bisphosphate**.
- Glucagon, through cAMP and protein kinase A, phosphorylates the bifunctional PFK-2/FBPase-2, so FBPase-2 becomes active and fructose 2,6-bisphosphate falls. That slows glycolysis (less PFK-1 activation) and favors gluconeogenesis. Insulin does the opposite.
- Glucagon also induces gene expression of PEPCK and glucose 6-phosphatase; cortisol does the same.

**Energy cost.** Making one glucose from two pyruvate costs 2 ATP (pyruvate carboxylase), 2 GTP (PEPCK) and 2 ATP (phosphoglycerate kinase, running backward): **6 high-energy phosphate bonds**, plus 2 NADH. The **Cori cycle** returns lactate from muscle and red cells to the liver: glycolysis makes 2 ATP, gluconeogenesis spends 6, so the body pays 4 ATP-equivalents per cycle, with the liver's fatty acid oxidation providing the energy. Ethanol metabolism raises NADH/NAD$^+$, trapping pyruvate as lactate and malate, which is one reason alcohol causes fasting hypoglycemia.

### Glycogen

**Glycogen** is a branched glucose polymer ($\alpha$-1,4 chains with $\alpha$-1,6 branches). Liver glycogen (about 100 g) maintains blood glucose; muscle glycogen (a larger total) fuels muscle.

- **Synthesis:** glucose 6-phosphate to glucose 1-phosphate to UDP-glucose; **glycogen synthase** extends chains and the branching enzyme makes branches. Glycogenin primes new chains.
- **Breakdown:** **glycogen phosphorylase** (cofactor pyridoxal phosphate) removes glucose 1-phosphate; the debranching enzyme handles branch points. In liver, G6P is dephosphorylated and released as free glucose.

**Control.** Glucagon (liver) and epinephrine (liver and muscle) raise cAMP and activate PKA, which phosphorylates phosphorylase kinase, making phosphorylase **a** (active), and phosphorylates glycogen synthase (inactive). Insulin activates protein phosphatase 1, which does the reverse, so synthase is active and phosphorylase inactive. In muscle, Ca$^{2+}$ and AMP also activate breakdown during contraction.

### Glycogen storage diseases

| Disease | Enzyme | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| Von Gierke (I) | Glucose 6-phosphatase | Fasting hypoglycemia, hepatomegaly, lactic acidosis, hyperuricemia, hyperlipidemia |
| Pompe (II) | Lysosomal acid alpha-glucosidase | Cardiomegaly, hypotonia, early death in infantile form |
| Cori (III) | Debranching enzyme | Milder hypoglycemia, hepatomegaly, abnormal glycogen with short branches |
| McArdle (V) | Muscle phosphorylase | Exercise cramps, myoglobinuria, no rise in lactate with ischemic exercise |

@example
title: Energy cost of gluconeogenesis
problem: How many high-energy phosphate bonds are needed to make one glucose from two pyruvate?
step: Pyruvate carboxylase uses 1 ATP per pyruvate, so 2 ATP for two pyruvate.
step: PEPCK uses 1 GTP per oxaloacetate, so 2 GTP for two molecules.
step: Phosphoglycerate kinase runs in reverse, using 1 ATP per 3-phosphoglycerate, so 2 ATP.
step: Total $= 2 + 2 + 2 = 6$ high-energy phosphates (4 ATP and 2 GTP). The Cori cycle nets $-4$ because the muscle gains 2 ATP.

@mistakes
- Thinking gluconeogenesis is glycolysis in reverse using all the same enzymes; three steps need different enzymes.
- Believing muscle glycogen maintains blood glucose; muscle has no glucose 6-phosphatase.
- Claiming fatty acids make a net amount of glucose in humans.
- Forgetting that glucagon has opposite effects on synthase (inactivates) and phosphorylase (activates) through phosphorylation.

@quiz num
prompt: Muscle glycogen supplies a glucose unit that is converted to 2 lactate by glycolysis, with a net gain of 3 ATP (no hexokinase step). The liver converts the 2 lactate back to glucose at a cost of 6 high-energy phosphate bonds. What is the net energy cost to the body for this cycle, in ATP equivalents?
answer: 3
check: 6 - (4 - 1)
hint: Compare what the muscle gains with what the liver spends.
solution: The liver spends 6 and the muscle gains 3, so the net cost is $6 - 3 = 3$ ATP equivalents.
