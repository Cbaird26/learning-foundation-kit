@article
Myoglobin and hemoglobin both use a **heme** group (iron in the ferrous, $\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}$, state) to carry oxygen, yet they behave very differently, and that difference is the logic of oxygen delivery.

### Myoglobin: storage

**Myoglobin** is a single-chain protein in muscle. It binds one $\mathrm{O_2}$ and shows a **hyperbolic** binding curve:

$$Y = \frac{P_{O_2}}{P_{O_2} + P_{50}},$$

where $Y$ is fractional saturation and $P_{50}$ is the partial pressure giving 50% saturation. Myoglobin's $P_{50}$ is about $2.8$ mmHg, so it grabs oxygen even at the low pressures found in working muscle and releases it only when muscle is nearly anoxic.

### Hemoglobin: transport

**Hemoglobin** (adult HbA) is a tetramer, $\alpha_2\beta_2$, with four hemes. Its curve is **sigmoidal** and its $P_{50}$ is about $26$ mmHg. The shape arises from **cooperativity**: binding of one oxygen increases the affinity of the remaining sites. The two conformations are the low-affinity **T (tense)** state and the high-affinity **R (relaxed)** state. Oxygen binding triggers a shift from T to R, and loss of oxygen favors T. The steep middle of the curve lets hemoglobin load nearly fully in the lungs (PO2 about 100 mmHg) and unload a large fraction in tissues (PO2 about 40 mmHg).

### Shifts of the curve

A **right shift** (higher $P_{50}$, lower affinity, easier unloading) stabilizes the T state. Remember the mnemonic "CADET, face right": **C**O$_2$, **A**cid (low pH), **D**PG (2,3-bisphosphoglycerate), **E**xercise, **T**emperature (all increased).

- **Bohr effect:** protons and CO2 bind hemoglobin and promote release of O2 in metabolically active tissues. In the lungs, the reverse (the Haldane effect) helps release CO2.
- **2,3-BPG** is made in red cells from the glycolytic intermediate 1,3-BPG. It binds in the central cavity of deoxyhemoglobin and lowers affinity. Levels rise at altitude and in chronic hypoxia.

A **left shift** (lower $P_{50}$, higher affinity, poorer unloading) occurs with low CO2, high pH, low temperature and low 2,3-BPG.

- **Fetal hemoglobin** (HbF, $\alpha_2\gamma_2$) binds 2,3-BPG poorly, so it has higher affinity than HbA and extracts oxygen across the placenta.
- **Carbon monoxide** binds heme about 200 times more tightly than O2, blocks sites and shifts the curve left, so the remaining hemoglobin holds oxygen more tightly. Standard pulse oximetry misreads carboxyhemoglobin as oxyhemoglobin, so SpO2 can look normal in poisoning.
- **Methemoglobin** has iron in the $\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}$ state and cannot bind O2; it also shifts the curve left. Treat with methylene blue.

### Oxygen content

Most oxygen travels bound to hemoglobin; a small amount dissolves. In mL O2 per dL blood:

$$C_{aO_2} = 1.34 \times [\mathrm{Hb}] \times S_{aO_2} + 0.003 \times P_{aO_2},$$

with $[\mathrm{Hb}]$ in g/dL and $S_{aO_2}$ as a fraction. Anemia lowers content with a normal PaO2 and normal saturation, which is why a patient can be hypoxic in tissues but have a normal pulse oximeter reading.

### Disease: sickle cell

Sickle cell disease results from Glu6Val in the beta chain. Valine's hydrophobic side chain creates a sticky patch on deoxyhemoglobin S that polymerizes, distorting red cells. Anything that increases deoxygenation (hypoxia, acidosis, dehydration) promotes sickling.

@example
title: Oxygen content of arterial blood
problem: A patient has Hb $12$ g/dL, $S_{aO_2} = 0.96$ and $P_{aO_2} = 85$ mmHg. Find the arterial oxygen content in mL O2/dL.
step: Compute the bound oxygen: $1.34 \times 12 \times 0.96$.
step: $1.34 \times 12 = 16.08$, and $16.08 \times 0.96 = 15.44$ mL/dL.
step: Compute the dissolved oxygen: $0.003 \times 85 = 0.255$ mL/dL.
step: Add them: $C_{aO_2} = 15.44 + 0.26 \approx 15.7$ mL/dL. Notice that dissolved oxygen contributes under 2% of the total.

@mistakes
- Treating a right shift as "higher affinity"; a right shift means lower affinity and more unloading to tissues.
- Believing a normal pulse oximeter excludes carbon monoxide poisoning or methemoglobinemia.
- Forgetting that the dissolved term uses PO2 in mmHg, while the bound term uses saturation as a fraction.
- Thinking anemia lowers PaO2 or SaO2; it lowers content (the amount of hemoglobin) instead.
