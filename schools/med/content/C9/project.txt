@review
## Week-by-week review

1. **Amino acids and protein structure.** Zwitterions, side-chain classes, pI as the average of the two pKa values around the neutral form, four levels of structure, collagen and vitamin C.
2. **Hemoglobin and myoglobin.** Hyperbolic versus sigmoidal curves, T and R states, Bohr effect and 2,3-BPG (right shift), CO and HbF (left shift), $C_{aO_2} = 1.34\,\mathrm{Hb}\,S_{aO_2} + 0.003\,P_{aO_2}$.
3. **Enzyme kinetics.** $v = V_{max}[S]/(K_m + [S])$; Km at half Vmax, Vmax $= k_{cat}[E]_T$, zero-order at high substrate.
4. **Regulation and inhibition.** Lineweaver-Burk: y-intercept $1/V_{max}$, x-intercept $-1/K_m$; competitive (Km up), noncompetitive (Vmax down), uncompetitive (both down); allosteric, covalent and zymogen control.
5. **Glycolysis.** Net 2 ATP and 2 NADH; irreversible steps and PFK-1 as rate-limiting; lactate regenerates NAD$^+$; pyruvate kinase deficiency, galactosemia.
6. **Gluconeogenesis and glycogen.** Four bypass enzymes, 6 high-energy phosphates per glucose, glucagon versus insulin on synthase and phosphorylase, storage diseases.
7. **Citric acid cycle.** PDH cofactors; per acetyl-CoA 3 NADH, 1 FADH$_2$, 1 GTP, 2 CO$_2$; regulated enzymes; anaplerosis.
8. **Oxidative phosphorylation.** Complexes I to IV, proton gradient, 2.5 and 1.5 ATP per carrier; inhibitors versus uncouplers.
9. **Lipids.** Carnitine shuttle and malonyl-CoA, cycles $= n/2 - 1$, ketones, fatty acid synthesis.
10. **Amino acids.** Transamination, urea cycle (4 high-energy phosphates), OTC deficiency, PKU, MSUD.
11. **Nucleotides.** PRPP, HGPRT, xanthine oxidase and gout, orotic aciduria, DHFR and thymidylate synthase drugs.
12. **Integration.** Insulin and glucagon, fed to prolonged fast, ketone adaptation, exercise, diabetic ketoacidosis.

### Self-check

- Can you list the three irreversible glycolytic steps and the matching gluconeogenic enzymes?
- Can you say what an uncoupler does to oxygen use, ATP and heat?
- Can you count the ATP from palmitate and state your conventions?
- Can you predict hormone and fuel levels after 3 days of fasting?

### Common errors

- Reporting the intercepts of a Lineweaver-Burk plot as Vmax and Km instead of their reciprocals.
- Calling a right shift "increased affinity".
- Forgetting that muscle lacks glucose 6-phosphatase and the liver lacks thiophorase.
- Using $n/2$ rather than $n/2 - 1$ for beta-oxidation cycles, or forgetting the activation cost.
- Confusing CPS I with CPS II.

@exam num
prompt: A Lineweaver-Burk plot of an enzyme without inhibitor has a y-intercept of $0.040$ and a slope of $0.20$ (units: $1/v$ in $\min/\mu\mathrm{mol}$, $1/[S]$ in $\mathrm{mM^{-1}}$). In the presence of a competitive inhibitor at $[I] = 6\ \mu\mathrm{M}$ with $K_i = 2\ \mu\mathrm{M}$, what is the velocity (in $\mu\mathrm{mol/min}$) at $[S] = 10$ mM? Round to one decimal.
answer: 8.3
check: (1 / 0.040) * 10 / (0.20 * (1 / 0.040) * (1 + 6 / 2) + 10)
tol: 1%
unit: µmol/min
hint: Get Vmax from the y-intercept and Km from slope times Vmax.
hint: Vmax $= 25$ and Km $= 5$ mM. A competitive inhibitor multiplies Km by $1 + [I]/K_i$.
solution: $V_{max} = 1/0.040 = 25$; $K_m = 0.20 \times 25 = 5$ mM. $K_m^{app} = 5(1 + 6/2) = 20$ mM. $v = 25 \times 10/(20 + 10) = 8.3\ \mu\mathrm{mol/min}$.

@exam mc
prompt: A 28-year-old woman takes 2,4-dinitrophenol to lose weight and develops fever, sweating and tachypnea. Which combination best explains her presentation and what happens to her mitochondria?
choice: Complex IV is inhibited, so oxygen consumption falls and lactate rises
choice: ATP synthase is blocked, so the proton gradient rises and respiration slows
choice*: The proton gradient is dissipated, so electron transport and oxygen use rise while ATP synthesis falls and energy is released as heat
choice: Hemoglobin shifts left, so tissues receive less oxygen and temperature falls
choice: Increased malonyl-CoA blocks fatty acid entry into mitochondria
hint: Decide whether DNP blocks electron flow or only the return of protons.
solution: DNP is an uncoupler. Protons cross the inner membrane without passing through ATP synthase, so respiratory control is lost, oxygen consumption rises, the cell oxidizes more fuel, ATP synthesis falls, and the energy appears as heat.

@exam self
prompt: A 45-year-old man has been fasting for 2 days. Trace what the liver does with (a) glycogen, (b) lactate and alanine, (c) fatty acids from adipose tissue and (d) amino nitrogen. For each, name the key enzymes or control points and the hormone driving them, and state how muscle and brain fuel use differ from the fed state.
solution: (a) Glucagon raises cAMP and PKA activates phosphorylase kinase and glycogen phosphorylase and inactivates glycogen synthase; liver glycogen is largely depleted by 24 hours, and glucose 6-phosphatase releases free glucose. (b) Lactate (Cori cycle) and alanine (glucose-alanine cycle after transamination, with the alanine nitrogen becoming urea) are converted by gluconeogenesis: pyruvate carboxylase (activated by acetyl-CoA), PEPCK, fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase (less inhibited because fructose 2,6-bisphosphate falls after PKA phosphorylates PFK-2/FBPase-2) and glucose 6-phosphatase. (c) Low insulin and high glucagon activate hormone-sensitive lipase; fatty acids enter mitochondria through CPT-I (active because acetyl-CoA carboxylase is inhibited and malonyl-CoA is low) and undergo beta-oxidation, providing ATP and NADH for gluconeogenesis and acetyl-CoA for ketogenesis (HMG-CoA synthase). (d) Amino nitrogen is removed by transamination and glutamate dehydrogenase and enters the urea cycle (CPS I, activated by N-acetylglutamate). Muscle shifts from glucose to fatty acids and ketones; the brain still uses glucose but begins to use ketones, and red cells continue to use glucose.
