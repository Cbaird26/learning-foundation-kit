@article
**Protozoa** are single-celled eukaryotes; **helminths** are multicellular worms (nematodes, cestodes, trematodes). Diagnosis relies on microscopy of stool or blood, antigen or PCR tests, and often on **exposure history**: water, food, animals, insects, soil, travel. Eosinophilia suggests a helminth with tissue migration.

### Intestinal and urogenital protozoa

- ***Entamoeba histolytica***: fecal-oral cysts. Colonic **flask-shaped ulcers** and bloody diarrhea (dysentery); trophozoites with ingested red cells; liver abscess ("anchovy paste" pus). Treat with **metronidazole** followed by a luminal agent (paromomycin).
- ***Giardia lamblia***: hikers drinking stream water, day care. Foul-smelling fatty diarrhea, bloating and malabsorption; pear-shaped binucleate trophozoites or cysts in stool (antigen test is sensitive). Treat with metronidazole, tinidazole or nitazoxanide.
- ***Cryptosporidium***: swimming pools (chlorine resistant), farms; watery diarrhea, severe and prolonged in AIDS; **acid-fast oocysts**; nitazoxanide and immune restoration.
- ***Trichomonas vaginalis***: sexually transmitted; frothy malodorous discharge, strawberry cervix; motile flagellated trophozoites; treat with metronidazole, and treat partners.

### Blood and tissue protozoa

***Plasmodium*** (malaria) is transmitted by the **female *Anopheles*** mosquito. Sporozoites infect hepatocytes; **vivax and ovale form dormant hypnozoites**. Merozoites invade red cells, cycling as rings, trophozoites and schizonts, and rupture cells to cause fever, anemia and splenomegaly. Diagnose with **thick smear** (sensitive for finding parasites) and **thin smear** (species and **percent parasitemia**), repeated every 12 to 24 hours for three sets, with rapid antigen tests.

| Species | Features |
|---|---|
| *P. falciparum* | Irregular fever, **invades red cells of all ages**, multiple rings per cell, banana-shaped gametocytes; severe malaria (cerebral malaria, kidney failure, hypoglycemia, ARDS) |
| *P. vivax*, *P. ovale* | Tertian fever (48 hours); infect reticulocytes; **relapses from hypnozoites** |
| *P. malariae* | Quartan fever (72 hours); band forms; nephrotic syndrome |

Treatment depends on species and resistance patterns: **artemisinin-based combination therapy** for uncomplicated falciparum malaria and **IV artesunate** for severe disease. Vivax and ovale need a drug that clears hypnozoites (**primaquine or tafenoquine**), which causes hemolysis in **G6PD deficiency**, so test G6PD first. Sickle cell trait protects against falciparum; **Duffy-negative** red cells resist vivax.

Others:

- ***Toxoplasma gondii***: oocysts in cat feces, tissue cysts in undercooked meat. Congenital infection (chorioretinitis, intracranial calcifications, hydrocephalus); in AIDS, **ring-enhancing brain lesions**. Treat with pyrimethamine plus sulfadiazine plus leucovorin.
- ***Babesia microti***: *Ixodes* tick; hemolytic anemia with intraerythrocytic **"Maltese cross"** tetrads; severe in asplenia; atovaquone plus azithromycin.
- ***Trypanosoma cruzi*** (Chagas disease): reduviid ("kissing") bug, Latin America; chronic dilated cardiomyopathy, megaesophagus and megacolon; benznidazole. ***T. brucei***: tsetse fly, African sleeping sickness.
- ***Leishmania***: sandflies; visceral disease (kala-azar) with fever, splenomegaly and pancytopenia; amastigotes in macrophages; liposomal amphotericin B.
- ***Naegleria fowleri***: warm fresh water entering through the nose; rapidly fatal meningoencephalitis.

### Nematodes (roundworms)

| Worm | Clue | Drug |
|---|---|---|
| *Enterobius* (pinworm) | Perianal itching; cellophane tape test | Albendazole or pyrantel |
| *Ascaris* | Large worm; lung migration with eosinophilia; intestinal obstruction | Albendazole |
| Hookworm (*Necator*, *Ancylostoma*) | Larvae penetrate skin of bare feet; **iron deficiency anemia** | Albendazole |
| *Strongyloides* | Skin penetration; **autoinfection**; **hyperinfection with corticosteroids** | **Ivermectin** |
| *Trichinella* | Undercooked pork or wild game; myalgia, periorbital edema, eosinophilia | Albendazole |
| *Toxocara* | Dog or cat roundworm eggs; visceral larva migrans | Albendazole |
| *Wuchereria* | Mosquito; lymphedema (elephantiasis) | Diethylcarbamazine, albendazole |
| *Onchocerca* | Blackfly; river blindness | Ivermectin |

### Cestodes (tapeworms) and trematodes (flukes)

- ***Taenia saginata*** (beef): intestinal tapeworm only. ***T. solium*** (pork): eating larval cysts in undercooked pork causes an intestinal tapeworm, but **ingesting eggs** (from a human carrier by the fecal-oral route) causes **cysticercosis**, including **neurocysticercosis** (seizures; cystic lesions with a scolex).
- ***Diphyllobothrium latum***: raw freshwater fish; **vitamin B12 deficiency** with megaloblastic anemia.
- ***Echinococcus granulosus***: dogs and sheep; liver **hydatid cyst**; spillage during aspiration can cause anaphylaxis, so treat with albendazole plus surgery or a percutaneous protocol.
- ***Schistosoma***: cercariae penetrate skin during freshwater exposure where snails live (Africa, Asia, South America). *S. mansoni* and *S. japonicum* cause hepatosplenic disease and portal hypertension; ***S. haematobium*** causes hematuria and **squamous cell carcinoma of the bladder**. **Praziquantel** treats schistosomiasis and most flukes and intestinal tapeworm infections. *Clonorchis* (raw fish, Asia) predisposes to cholangiocarcinoma.

@example
title: Quantifying parasitemia
problem: On a thin blood smear, 2,500 red cells are counted and 100 contain *Plasmodium* rings. What is the percent parasitemia, and why does it matter?
step: Parasitemia = infected red cells / total red cells counted $\times 100 = 100/2500 \times 100 = 4\%$.
step: Higher parasitemia in falciparum malaria predicts severity; a value above about 5% (in non-immune patients) is one marker of severe malaria and suggests IV artesunate.
step: Repeat smears track the response to treatment; parasitemia should fall quickly.

@example
title: From exposure to organism
problem: A 52-year-old man from Puerto Rico who has been on prednisone for 3 months develops abdominal pain, diarrhea, pneumonia and gram-negative bacteremia. Stool shows larvae. Name the parasite and the drug.
step: Larvae in stool in a person from an endemic area suggest *Strongyloides stercoralis*.
step: Corticosteroids suppress the immune response that restrains autoinfection, producing hyperinfection with larval migration that carries gut bacteria into the blood.
step: Treat with ivermectin, and screen for *Strongyloides* before long-term steroids in people from endemic regions.

@mistakes
- Giving primaquine without checking G6PD.
- Treating neurocysticercosis as a pork tapeworm infection; it follows ingestion of eggs, not pork cysts.
- Aspirating a suspected hydatid cyst without antiparasitic cover; spillage may cause anaphylaxis.
- Forgetting *Strongyloides* before starting corticosteroids in a person from an endemic area.

answer: 4.5
check: 90 / 2000 * 100
hint: Divide parasitized cells by the cells counted.
solution: $90/2000 = 0.045$, which is 4.5%.

@quiz num
prompt: On a thick blood smear, 120 malaria parasites are counted against 200 white blood cells. The patient's white count is 8,000 per microliter. What is the parasite density in parasites per microliter?
answer: 4800
check: 120 / 200 * 8000
hint: Parasites per white cell multiplied by white cells per microliter gives parasites per microliter.
solution: $120/200 = 0.6$ parasites per white cell; $0.6 \times 8000 = 4800$ per microliter.
unit: parasites/microliter
tol: 1%
