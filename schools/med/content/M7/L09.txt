@article
**RNA viruses** replicate in the cytoplasm (influenza is the nuclear exception) using an **RNA-dependent RNA polymerase**, which lacks proofreading, so mutation rates are high.

- **Positive-sense ssRNA** genomes act as mRNA and are infectious on their own (picornaviruses, flaviviruses, togaviruses, coronaviruses, caliciviruses).
- **Negative-sense ssRNA** genomes must be transcribed first, so the virion carries its own polymerase (orthomyxoviruses, paramyxoviruses, rhabdoviruses, bunyaviruses, filoviruses, arenaviruses).
- **Segmented** genomes can **reassort**: orthomyxoviruses (influenza), reoviruses (rotavirus), bunyaviruses (hantavirus) and arenaviruses.

### Influenza

Orthomyxovirus with eight RNA segments. **Hemagglutinin (HA)** binds sialic acid on respiratory cells; **neuraminidase (NA)** cleaves it to release progeny virions. **Antigenic drift** (point mutations in HA and NA) causes seasonal epidemics and the need for a new vaccine yearly; **antigenic shift** (reassortment, for example of human and avian or swine strains) creates a novel subtype to which people lack immunity and can cause a pandemic. Presentation: abrupt fever, myalgia, cough. Complications include pneumonia, often secondary bacterial (*S. aureus*, *S. pneumoniae*). Treat as early as possible (ideally within 48 hours) with **oseltamivir**, a neuraminidase inhibitor; baloxavir inhibits the viral cap-dependent endonuclease.

### Paramyxoviruses (enveloped, negative-sense)

- **Measles (rubeola)**: three Cs (cough, coryza, conjunctivitis), **Koplik spots** (white spots on buccal mucosa), then a maculopapular rash that starts on the face and spreads downward. Complications: pneumonia, encephalitis, and years later **subacute sclerosing panencephalitis (SSPE)**. Vitamin A is given to children. It is highly contagious (airborne), with an R0 of about 12 to 18.
- **Mumps**: parotitis, orchitis, aseptic meningitis, pancreatitis.
- **RSV**: bronchiolitis in infants (wheeze, tachypnea); the monoclonal antibodies palivizumab and nirsevimab protect high-risk infants, and vaccines exist for older adults and for pregnancy.
- **Parainfluenza**: **croup** (barking cough, inspiratory stridor, subglottic narrowing, "steeple sign" on neck radiograph).

### Togavirus: rubella

Mild rash and posterior auricular lymphadenopathy, but **congenital rubella** (first trimester) causes **cataracts, patent ductus arteriosus and sensorineural deafness**. MMR is a live vaccine, contraindicated in pregnancy and severe immunodeficiency.

### Picornaviruses (naked, positive-sense)

- **Poliovirus**: replicates in the gut, rarely invades anterior horn motor neurons, causing asymmetric flaccid paralysis. The U.S. uses inactivated (injected) vaccine; the live oral vaccine can revert in rare cases.
- **Coxsackie A**: hand-foot-and-mouth disease, herpangina. **Coxsackie B**: myocarditis, pericarditis, pleurodynia. **Echovirus**: aseptic meningitis. **Rhinovirus**: common cold (acid labile, so does not survive the stomach). **Hepatitis A virus** is covered next week.

### Coronaviruses

Enveloped positive-sense viruses; SARS-CoV-2 spike binds the **ACE2** receptor. Presentation ranges from mild upper-respiratory illness to pneumonia and ARDS; prevention by vaccination, and antivirals such as nirmatrelvir-ritonavir and remdesivir for selected patients.

### Gastroenteritis viruses

**Rotavirus** (reovirus; double-stranded segmented RNA) causes watery diarrhea in infants and young children; oral live vaccine is given in infancy (rare intussusception risk). **Norovirus** (calicivirus) causes explosive vomiting outbreaks on cruise ships and in schools; alcohol hand rubs are less effective, so wash with soap and water.

### Rabies (rhabdovirus)

Bullet-shaped, negative-sense. Entering through a bite from a bat or other animal, the virus replicates locally, then travels **retrograde along peripheral nerves** to the CNS. Encephalitis follows, with agitation, hydrophobia and aerophobia; **Negri bodies** (eosinophilic cytoplasmic inclusions) are seen in neurons. Once symptomatic it is almost always fatal, so management is **post-exposure prophylaxis**: thorough wound washing, **rabies immune globulin** infiltrated around the wound, and a **rabies vaccine series**.

### Arboviruses and hemorrhagic fevers

**Flaviviruses** (positive-sense): **dengue** (*Aedes* mosquito; "breakbone" fever, thrombocytopenia; severe dengue follows a second infection with a different serotype through antibody-dependent enhancement), **yellow fever** (jaundice, hemorrhage; live vaccine), **Zika** (congenital microcephaly), **West Nile virus** (*Culex*; birds; encephalitis, flaccid paralysis). **Hantavirus** (bunyavirus; rodent excreta; pulmonary syndrome). **Ebola** (filovirus; hemorrhagic fever; vaccine and monoclonal antibody treatments exist).

### Herd immunity and vaccine effectiveness

The basic reproduction number $R_0$ is the mean number of secondary cases from one case in a fully susceptible population. The **herd-immunity threshold** is
$$1 - \frac{1}{R_0}.$$
Vaccine effectiveness is $\dfrac{\text{attack rate unvaccinated} - \text{attack rate vaccinated}}{\text{attack rate unvaccinated}}$.

@example
title: Herd-immunity threshold
problem: A disease has $R_0 = 5$. What fraction of the population must be immune to stop sustained transmission, assuming random mixing?
step: Use the threshold $1 - 1/R_0$.
step: $1 - 1/5 = 1 - 0.20 = 0.80$.
step: At least 80% must be immune. Because vaccines are not perfect, vaccine coverage must be higher than 80%.

@example
title: Drift or shift?
problem: A novel influenza A virus with an HA never before seen in humans emerges and spreads worldwide. Which process explains it, and why can the same process not explain seasonal changes?
step: A new HA subtype points to reassortment of segments between human and animal strains: antigenic shift.
step: Seasonal changes arise from point mutations in the HA gene: antigenic drift, which alters existing HA enough to partially evade immunity.
step: Shift needs a segmented genome and co-infection; drift needs only the polymerase's errors, so influenza A both drifts and shifts.

@mistakes
- Treating antigenic drift and shift as the same event; shift is reassortment and drives pandemics.
- Giving a live vaccine such as MMR to a pregnant or severely immunocompromised patient.
- Forgetting that rabies post-exposure prophylaxis includes both immune globulin and vaccine, plus wound washing.
- Using alcohol hand rubs alone for norovirus.
