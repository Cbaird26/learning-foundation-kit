@article
Microbiology reports are a chain of evidence: a **specimen**, a **direct stain**, a **culture** with identification, and a **susceptibility test**. This lesson teaches you to read that chain, which is the main deliverable of weeks 1 and 2.

### Getting a good specimen

Collect before antibiotics when possible, from the right site, with minimal contamination. Blood cultures are drawn as two or more sets from separate sites (aerobic and anaerobic bottles). Growth in all sets of a typical pathogen such as *Staphylococcus aureus*, *E. coli* or *Streptococcus pneumoniae* is almost always real; a coagulase-negative staphylococcus in one of four bottles is usually a skin contaminant. Sputum is judged by quality: many neutrophils and few squamous epithelial cells mean a lower-airway sample.

### The Gram stain

1. **Crystal violet** stains all cells purple.
2. **Gram's iodine** forms a crystal violet-iodine complex that is trapped in thick peptidoglycan.
3. **Alcohol or acetone** decolorizes: gram-negative cells lose the complex (thin peptidoglycan, outer membrane dissolved); gram-positive cells keep it.
4. **Safranin** counterstains the decolorized cells pink-red.

Over-decolorizing makes gram-positives look pink; under-decolorizing leaves gram-negatives purple. Typical reads: gram-positive cocci in clusters suggest *Staphylococcus*; in chains or pairs, *Streptococcus* or *Enterococcus*; lancet-shaped diplococci, *S. pneumoniae*; gram-negative diplococci, *Neisseria*; gram-negative coccobacilli, *Haemophilus*. A Gram stain gives an answer in minutes, but sensitivity varies by site and organism.

Organisms that stain poorly or not at all: **mycobacteria** (acid-fast, because of mycolic acids; Ziehl-Neelsen or Kinyoun stain), *Mycoplasma* (no cell wall), obligate intracellular bacteria (*Chlamydia*, *Rickettsia*), spirochetes (too thin: darkfield microscopy or silver stain), and *Legionella* (stains faintly; use silver stain or culture on buffered charcoal yeast extract agar). Fungal and parasite stains: KOH and calcofluor white (fungi), India ink (*Cryptococcus* capsule), Giemsa (blood parasites, *Chlamydia*, *Borrelia*).

### Culture media

| Medium | Purpose | Example finding |
|---|---|---|
| Blood agar | Enriched; shows hemolysis | Beta (clear), alpha (green), gamma (none) |
| Chocolate agar | Heated blood releases X (hemin) and V (NAD) factors | *Haemophilus*, *Neisseria* |
| Thayer-Martin (VPN) | Selective for *Neisseria* (vancomycin, polymyxin/colistin, nystatin) | *N. gonorrhoeae* |
| MacConkey | Selective for gram-negative rods (bile salts, crystal violet); differential for lactose | Lactose fermenters pink (*E. coli*, *Klebsiella*); non-fermenters colorless (*Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Pseudomonas*) |
| Eosin methylene blue | Same idea | *E. coli* green metallic sheen |
| Charcoal yeast extract | Iron and cysteine | *Legionella* |
| Regan-Lowe | Charcoal with antibiotics | *Bordetella pertussis* |
| Löwenstein-Jensen | Egg-based | *M. tuberculosis* |
| TCBS | Selective for *Vibrio* | *V. cholerae* yellow |

### Quick biochemical tests

- **Catalase** (bubbles with hydrogen peroxide): positive in *Staphylococcus*; negative in *Streptococcus* and *Enterococcus*.
- **Coagulase**: positive in *S. aureus* only among the common staphylococci.
- **Oxidase**: positive in *Neisseria*, *Pseudomonas*, *Vibrio*, *Campylobacter*; negative in the Enterobacterales.
- **Urease**: positive in *Proteus*, *Klebsiella*, *H. pylori*, *Ureaplasma*, *Cryptococcus*.
- **Lactose fermentation** and **H2S production** separate the enteric rods.

Modern labs also use **MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry** (identifies a colony in minutes by its protein spectrum), **nucleic acid amplification tests** (PCR) and multiplex panels, and **antigen tests** (rapid group A strep, urinary *Legionella* and pneumococcal antigens).

### Quantitative cultures

Urine is plated with a calibrated loop (commonly 0.001 mL or 0.01 mL). Colony-forming units per mL = colonies counted divided by the volume plated. Classically, $\ge 10^5$ CFU/mL of a single uropathogen indicates urinary infection in a clean-catch specimen, although lower counts matter in symptomatic patients and in catheter samples.

### Susceptibility testing

The **minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC)** is the lowest antibiotic concentration that prevents visible growth. It is interpreted against standardized **breakpoints**: **S** (susceptible), **I** (susceptible, increased exposure/intermediate) or **R** (resistant). A lower MIC does not mean one drug is better than another; each drug has its own breakpoint. **Kirby-Bauer** disk diffusion measures a zone of inhibition on Mueller-Hinton agar. **Bactericidal** drugs kill; **bacteriostatic** drugs inhibit growth. The minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC) is the lowest concentration killing at least 99.9% of the inoculum.

@example
title: Reading a short culture report
problem: A 29-year-old woman has dysuria. A clean-catch urine is plated with a 0.001 mL loop and shows 85 pink colonies on MacConkey agar. Gram stain of a colony: gram-negative rods. Oxidase negative, indole positive. Estimate the colony count per mL and name the likely organism.
step: CFU/mL = colonies / volume plated = $85 / 0.001 = 85{,}000 = 8.5 \times 10^4$ CFU/mL.
step: Pink colonies on MacConkey mean lactose fermentation; with gram-negative rods, oxidase negative (excludes *Pseudomonas*) and indole positive, the organism is *E. coli*.
step: The count is below $10^5$, but in a symptomatic woman a count of $10^2$ to $10^3$ CFU/mL or more of a typical uropathogen is treated as significant, so this result supports urinary tract infection.

@example
title: Interpreting an MIC
problem: An isolate of *E. coli* has an MIC of 4 mg/L to ciprofloxacin (breakpoint for susceptible: $\le 0.25$ mg/L; resistant: $\ge 1$ mg/L) and 8 mg/L to ceftriaxone (susceptible $\le 1$; resistant $\ge 4$). Is the isolate susceptible to either drug?
step: Compare each MIC with its own breakpoints, never with each other.
step: Ciprofloxacin MIC 4 exceeds the resistant breakpoint of 1, so it is resistant.
step: Ceftriaxone MIC 8 also exceeds 4, so it is resistant. Despite the different numbers, the isolate is resistant to both.

@mistakes
- Comparing MICs between drugs; a smaller number is not a better drug unless it is the same drug and the same breakpoint.
- Reading a gram-negative rod that looks purple after poor decolorization as gram-positive; check the control and the morphology.
- Treating one bottle with coagulase-negative staphylococci as bacteremia without considering contamination.
- Forgetting that *M. tuberculosis*, *Legionella*, *Chlamydia* and spirochetes need special stains or media.
