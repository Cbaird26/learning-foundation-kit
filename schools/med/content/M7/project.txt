### Week-by-week key ideas

**Week 1 (structure, growth, genetics).** Gram-positive: thick peptidoglycan, teichoic acids. Gram-negative: outer membrane with LPS (lipid A is endotoxin). Doubling: $N = N_0 2^{n}$. Gene transfer: transformation, conjugation, transduction, lysogenic conversion. Resistance: *mecA* (PBP2a), *vanA* (D-Ala-D-lactate), beta-lactamases.

**Week 2 (laboratory).** Gram stain order: crystal violet, iodine, alcohol, safranin. Special stains for mycobacteria, spirochetes, *Legionella*. Media: chocolate, Thayer-Martin, MacConkey. Tests: catalase, coagulase, oxidase, urease. CFU/mL = colonies divided by volume plated. MIC is compared with each drug's own breakpoint.

**Week 3 and 4 (gram-positive).** Catalase separates staphylococci from streptococci; coagulase identifies *S. aureus*. Group A: bacitracin sensitive, PYR positive; group B: CAMP positive; pneumococcus: optochin sensitive. Superantigens (TSST-1); rheumatic fever versus post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis. Anthrax toxin, diphtheria toxin (EF-2), *Listeria* (ampicillin), *Nocardia* versus *Actinomyces*.

**Week 5 and 6 (gram-negative and others).** Lactose fermenters versus non-fermenters; *Shigella* versus *Salmonella*; EHEC and no antibiotics; cholera toxin (Gs) and pertussis toxin (Gi). Clostridial toxins, syphilis serology (RPR titers, treponemal tests), Lyme, rickettsiae (doxycycline), chlamydia, mycoplasma.

**Week 7 (mycobacteria).** Granuloma, IL-12 and interferon-gamma, TNF-alpha. TST cutoffs (5, 10, 15 mm), IGRA. RIPE for 2 months then rifampin and isoniazid for 4 months; know each drug's toxicity.

**Week 8 (DNA viruses).** Herpesvirus latency sites: HSV-1 trigeminal, HSV-2 sacral, VZV dorsal root ganglia, EBV B cells. Acyclovir needs viral thymidine kinase. HPV 16 and 18 (E6, E7).

**Week 9 and 10 (RNA viruses, HIV, hepatitis).** Drift versus shift, herd-immunity threshold $1 - 1/R_0$. HIV: gp120, CCR5, reverse transcriptase, integrase, CD4 thresholds for opportunistic infections. HBV serology patterns.

**Week 11 and 12 (fungi, parasites).** Septate acute-angle versus nonseptate right-angle hyphae; endemic mycoses by geography; amphotericin, azoles, echinocandins; A-a gradient. Malaria (smear, G6PD before primaquine), giardia, toxoplasma, helminth drugs.

### Self-check

- I can order the Gram stain steps and name an organism that does not stain well.
- I can compute CFU/mL, doubling time, attack rate, PPV and an A-a gradient.
- I can interpret a hepatitis B panel and a TST result.
- I can give the precaution category (contact, droplet, airborne) for common organisms.

### Common errors

- Comparing MICs across drugs.
- Giving antibiotics for EHEC, or ceftriaxone for *Pseudomonas*.
- Reading anti-HBs alone, or forgetting the window period.
- Using alcohol rub alone against *C. difficile* or norovirus.
- Confusing septate and nonseptate molds, and missing the need for debridement in mucormycosis.

@exam mc
prompt: A 35-year-old man who injects drugs has fever and a new tricuspid regurgitation murmur. Blood cultures grow gram-positive cocci in clusters that are catalase positive and coagulase positive, resistant to nafcillin. Which statement correctly states the best empiric antibiotic and the molecular basis of the resistance?
choice*: Vancomycin (or daptomycin); resistance is due to *mecA* encoding PBP2a, a penicillin-binding protein with low beta-lactam affinity
choice: Nafcillin; resistance is due to a plasmid beta-lactamase
choice: Vancomycin; resistance is due to replacement of D-Ala-D-Ala with D-Ala-D-lactate
choice: Ceftriaxone; resistance is due to loss of the outer membrane porin
hint: Cluster-forming, catalase-positive, coagulase-positive cocci are *S. aureus*.
solution: This is MRSA endocarditis. Nafcillin resistance arises from PBP2a (encoded by *mecA*), so a non-beta-lactam such as vancomycin or daptomycin is used. D-Ala-D-lactate causes vancomycin resistance in enterococci, and *S. aureus* has no outer membrane.

prompt: A 24-year-old unvaccinated traveler arrives at the emergency department with fever, cough, conjunctivitis, white buccal mucosal spots and a maculopapular rash that started on the face. Name the diagnosis, the isolation precautions and room type, and the actions needed for exposed non-immune contacts.
hint: Think of the airborne diseases that need negative-pressure rooms.
hint: Consider both the room and the people who were exposed.
solution: Measles (rubeola), recognized by cough, coryza, conjunctivitis, Koplik spots and a cephalocaudal rash. Place the patient in airborne precautions in a negative-pressure airborne infection isolation room (with the patient masked if moved); staff wear a fitted N95 respirator, preferably staff with documented immunity; leave the room closed and keep it empty for the recommended period after the patient leaves. Notify infection prevention and public health immediately. Identify exposed contacts and check immunity; give MMR vaccine within 72 hours of exposure to eligible non-immune contacts, or immune globulin within 6 days for those who cannot be vaccinated (infants, pregnant people, immunocompromised). Confirm with measles RT-PCR and serology; provide supportive care and vitamin A.
