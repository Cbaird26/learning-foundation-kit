@article
Gram-negative bacteria have an outer membrane with LPS, so they release endotoxin and are intrinsically less permeable to many drugs. Organize them by shape, then by a few tests.

### Gram-negative cocci: *Neisseria*

Both species are oxidase-positive diplococci that grow on Thayer-Martin or chocolate agar and have IgA protease and antigenic variation of pili.

- ***N. meningitidis***: encapsulated, **maltose fermenter** (and glucose). Spread by respiratory droplets, causing meningitis and meningococcemia in adolescents, college students and military recruits. Petechial or purpuric rash, shock, adrenal hemorrhage (**Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome**). Patients with **terminal complement (C5 to C9) deficiency** have recurrent infections. Treat with ceftriaxone; close contacts get prophylaxis with rifampin, ciprofloxacin or ceftriaxone; vaccines cover serogroups A, C, W, Y and B.
- ***N. gonorrhoeae***: no capsule, **glucose only** fermenter. Urethritis or cervicitis, pelvic inflammatory disease, disseminated infection (migratory arthritis, tenosynovitis, pustular skin lesions), and neonatal conjunctivitis. Gram stain shows intracellular gram-negative diplococci in neutrophils. Current U.S. treatment is a single dose of ceftriaxone, with testing for *Chlamydia* (treated with doxycycline if present).

### *Haemophilus influenzae*

A small gram-negative coccobacillus requiring **factor X (hemin) and factor V (NAD)** on chocolate agar; it shows **satellite growth** around *S. aureus* on blood agar. Encapsulated type b (polysaccharide PRP) causes meningitis, epiglottitis and septic arthritis, now rare thanks to the Hib conjugate vaccine. Nontypeable strains cause otitis media, sinusitis and COPD exacerbations. Treatment of serious disease is ceftriaxone.

### Enterobacterales (enteric rods)

Lactose fermenters on MacConkey (pink): *E. coli*, *Klebsiella*, *Enterobacter*. Non-fermenters (colorless): *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Proteus*, *Yersinia*. All are oxidase negative.

- ***E. coli***: most common cause of urinary tract infection (P fimbriae drive pyelonephritis), neonatal meningitis (K1 capsule) and gram-negative sepsis. Diarrheal pathotypes: **ETEC** (traveler's diarrhea; heat-labile toxin raises cAMP like cholera toxin, heat-stable toxin raises cGMP), **EPEC** (infants), **EIEC** (invasive, dysentery), and **EHEC O157:H7** (undercooked beef; **Shiga toxin**; does not ferment sorbitol on sorbitol-MacConkey agar). In EHEC infection, antibiotics and antimotility agents raise the risk of **hemolytic uremic syndrome** (microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia and acute kidney injury), so treatment is supportive.
- ***Klebsiella pneumoniae***: mucoid capsule; pneumonia in people with alcohol use disorder or diabetes (thick "currant jelly" sputum), urinary infection and liver abscess. Carbapenem-resistant strains are an infection-control priority.
- ***Proteus mirabilis***: swarming, **strongly urease positive**; alkaline urine, **struvite (staghorn) stones**.
- ***Salmonella***: motile, **H2S positive**. Nontyphoidal strains (poultry, eggs, reptiles) cause gastroenteritis and, in sickle cell disease, osteomyelitis. ***S.* Typhi** causes **typhoid (enteric) fever**: Vi capsule, stepwise fever, relative bradycardia, rose spots, constipation then diarrhea, intestinal perforation and a gallbladder carrier state. Treat with ceftriaxone or azithromycin (fluoroquinolone resistance is common).
- ***Shigella***: nonmotile, **H2S negative**, infectious dose as low as 10 to 100 organisms; **Shiga toxin** inactivates the 60S ribosomal subunit (N-glycosidase on 28S rRNA); bloody mucoid diarrhea and tenesmus.
- ***Yersinia enterocolitica***: pork and unpasteurized milk, grows in the cold, causes terminal ileitis mimicking appendicitis.

### Nonfermenters and curved rods

- ***Pseudomonas aeruginosa***: **oxidase positive**, lactose negative, blue-green pigment (pyocyanin), grape-like odor. Causes burn and wound infection, ventilator-associated pneumonia, chronic lung infection in cystic fibrosis, hot-tub folliculitis, malignant otitis externa in diabetes, and ecthyma gangrenosum in neutropenia. Use an antipseudomonal beta-lactam (piperacillin-tazobactam, cefepime, ceftazidime, or a carbapenem) with susceptibility testing; ceftriaxone is not reliable.
- ***Vibrio cholerae***: comma-shaped, oxidase positive, grows on TCBS (yellow). Cholera toxin **ADP-ribosylates Gs, keeping adenylate cyclase on**, raising cAMP, so chloride and water secretion floods the lumen: painless **rice-water diarrhea**. Treatment is rapid fluid replacement (oral rehydration works because sodium-glucose cotransport is intact). *V. vulnificus* causes cellulitis and sepsis after raw oysters, especially in liver disease.
- ***Campylobacter jejuni***: curved, oxidase positive, grows at 42 degrees C; poultry; bloody diarrhea; a leading preceding infection for **Guillain-Barre syndrome** and reactive arthritis.
- ***Helicobacter pylori***: **urease** makes ammonia to neutralize acid. Causes chronic gastritis, duodenal and gastric ulcers, gastric adenocarcinoma and MALT lymphoma. Diagnose by stool antigen or urea breath test (stop PPIs first) or biopsy; treat with a PPI plus antibiotics (bismuth quadruple therapy is a common regimen).

### Fastidious respiratory organisms

- ***Legionella pneumophila***: grows on buffered charcoal yeast extract with iron and cysteine; waterborne (cooling towers, hot-water systems), pneumonia with hyponatremia, diarrhea and confusion. Diagnose by **urinary antigen** (detects serogroup 1); treat with a respiratory fluoroquinolone or azithromycin.
- ***Bordetella pertussis***: Regan-Lowe agar. Pertussis toxin ADP-ribosylates Gi, raising cAMP and causing lymphocytosis. Catarrhal, paroxysmal ("whooping") and convalescent stages; macrolide treatment shortens infectivity if early.

Other zoonotic gram-negatives: *Pasteurella multocida* (cat or dog bite; amoxicillin-clavulanate), *Brucella* (unpasteurized dairy; undulant fever), *Francisella tularensis* (rabbits, ticks) and *Yersinia pestis* (rodents, fleas).

@example
title: Working through an enteric isolate
problem: A stool culture grows colorless colonies on MacConkey agar. The organism is oxidase negative, nonmotile and produces no H2S. A 4-year-old has bloody diarrhea with tenesmus after a daycare outbreak. Which genus and toxin?
step: Colorless on MacConkey means non-lactose fermenter; oxidase negative places it among the Enterobacterales.
step: Non-lactose fermenter, nonmotile and H2S negative separates *Shigella* from *Salmonella* (which is motile and H2S positive).
step: *Shigella* makes Shiga toxin, which removes an adenine from 28S rRNA and halts protein synthesis in enterocytes.

@example
title: Why cholera diarrhea responds to oral rehydration
problem: Explain how cholera toxin causes secretory diarrhea and why a sugar-and-salt solution can still replace fluid.
step: The toxin's A subunit ADP-ribosylates the Gs protein, so adenylate cyclase stays active and cAMP rises in enterocytes.
step: Elevated cAMP opens CFTR so chloride, then sodium and water, move into the lumen; the epithelium itself is intact.
step: The sodium-glucose cotransporter does not use cAMP, so glucose-sodium solutions are still absorbed and pull water along.

@mistakes
- Calling every gram-negative rod a lactose fermenter; *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Proteus* and *Pseudomonas* are not.
- Giving antibiotics for suspected EHEC (Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli*), which raises the risk of hemolytic uremic syndrome.
- Using ceftriaxone for *Pseudomonas*; an antipseudomonal beta-lactam is needed.
- Confusing the G-protein targets: cholera toxin acts on Gs, pertussis toxin on Gi (both raise cAMP).

prompt: A 56-year-old man has epigastric pain relieved by meals and a duodenal ulcer on endoscopy. Which bacterial property is most important for the organism's survival in the acidic gastric lumen?
