@article
Viruses are obligate intracellular parasites with a nucleic acid genome inside a protein **capsid**, with or without a lipid **envelope**. Enveloped viruses are fragile (killed by drying, soap and alcohol) and usually spread by close contact or droplets; naked viruses (adenovirus, parvovirus, polyomavirus, picornaviruses) survive on surfaces and persist. Most DNA viruses replicate in the nucleus using host or viral DNA polymerase; **poxviruses** replicate in the cytoplasm. Exceptions to the "double-stranded" rule: **parvovirus B19** is single-stranded and **hepadnavirus (hepatitis B)** is partly double-stranded and uses reverse transcription.

### Herpesviruses and latency

Herpesviruses are enveloped, linear double-stranded DNA viruses that establish **lifelong latency**: after primary infection the viral genome persists in a non-replicating form (an episome in the nucleus) in a specific host cell, with minimal viral gene expression, which avoids immune detection. Reactivation, triggered by stress, immunosuppression, fever or UV light, produces recurrent disease. That is why immunocompromised patients develop zoster and CMV disease.

| Virus | Latency site | Key diseases |
|---|---|---|
| HSV-1 | Trigeminal ganglion | Orolabial herpes, keratitis, **temporal lobe encephalitis** |
| HSV-2 | Sacral ganglia | Genital herpes; neonatal herpes |
| VZV (HHV-3) | Dorsal root or cranial nerve ganglia | Varicella (chickenpox); zoster (shingles) |
| EBV (HHV-4) | B lymphocytes | Infectious mononucleosis; Burkitt lymphoma; nasopharyngeal carcinoma; Hodgkin lymphoma; post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder |
| CMV (HHV-5) | Monocytes, lymphocytes | Congenital infection; mononucleosis-like illness; retinitis, colitis in AIDS; transplant disease |
| HHV-6 and -7 | T cells | Roseola |
| HHV-8 | B cells | Kaposi sarcoma |

**HSV**: vesicles on an erythematous base; a **Tzanck smear** shows multinucleated giant cells (PCR is now preferred). HSV encephalitis causes fever, confusion, seizures and temporal lobe abnormalities with red cells in CSF; give IV acyclovir at once. Neonatal infection (skin-eye-mouth, CNS, disseminated) occurs at birth to women with a primary or shedding genital infection; cesarean delivery is recommended with active genital lesions.

**VZV**: varicella causes crops of vesicles in different stages; zoster is a unilateral dermatomal painful vesicular rash; complications include postherpetic neuralgia. The recombinant zoster vaccine (non-live) is recommended for older adults and some immunocompromised adults; a live attenuated varicella vaccine is part of childhood immunization. Varicella and disseminated zoster need airborne and contact precautions.

**EBV**: infects B cells through CD21. Mononucleosis presents with fever, pharyngitis, posterior cervical adenopathy and splenomegaly (avoid contact sports). The blood shows **atypical lymphocytes**, which are reactive CD8 T cells. Diagnosis: heterophile antibody (**Monospot**) or EBV-specific serology. Ampicillin or amoxicillin can produce a rash.

**CMV**: the most common congenital infection (hearing loss, microcephaly, periventricular calcifications, chorioretinitis); in transplant and AIDS patients causes retinitis, esophagitis and colitis. Tissue shows **"owl's-eye" intranuclear inclusions**. The mononucleosis it causes is **heterophile negative**. Treat with ganciclovir (or valganciclovir); foscarnet for resistance.

**Antiviral mechanism**: **Acyclovir** is a guanosine analog that must be phosphorylated first by **viral thymidine kinase** (HSV, VZV), so it is activated only in infected cells; the triphosphate then terminates viral DNA synthesis by inhibiting the viral DNA polymerase. Resistance is usually from absent thymidine kinase; **foscarnet** inhibits the polymerase directly and needs no activation. **Ganciclovir** for CMV is activated by the viral UL97 kinase.

### Other DNA viruses

- **Adenovirus** (naked, dsDNA): pharyngoconjunctivitis, epidemic keratoconjunctivitis, acute respiratory disease in military recruits, hemorrhagic cystitis, gastroenteritis in children.
- **HPV** (naked, circular dsDNA): types 1 to 4 skin warts; **6 and 11** anogenital warts (condylomata acuminata); **16 and 18** high-risk types causing cervical, anal and oropharyngeal cancers. **E6** inactivates p53 and **E7** inactivates Rb. Screening is by Pap cytology and HPV testing; the nine-valent vaccine prevents infection.
- **Parvovirus B19** (naked, ssDNA): infects erythroid precursors; erythema infectiosum (fifth disease, "slapped cheek"), transient aplastic crisis in hemolytic anemias such as sickle cell disease, hydrops fetalis and arthropathy in adults.
- **Polyomaviruses** (naked, circular dsDNA): **JC virus** causes progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy in AIDS and with natalizumab; **BK virus** causes hemorrhagic cystitis and nephropathy in kidney transplant recipients.
- **Poxviruses** (enveloped, large, cytoplasmic replication): smallpox (eradicated); **molluscum contagiosum** (umbilicated papules).

@example
title: Why acyclovir spares uninfected cells
problem: Explain why acyclovir is selective for HSV-infected cells, and predict what resistance and what alternative apply.
step: Acyclovir is a prodrug. The first phosphorylation requires thymidine kinase encoded by the virus; host kinases then add the second and third phosphates.
step: The triphosphate is incorporated into viral DNA by the viral polymerase and terminates the chain. Uninfected cells never make the monophosphate in meaningful amounts.
step: Resistance usually arises from loss of viral thymidine kinase. Foscarnet binds the polymerase directly, so it does not need activation.

@example
title: Where does a virus hide?
problem: A 68-year-old man has a painful vesicular rash across the right T6 dermatome. Name the virus, where it was latent, and what the Tzanck smear would show.
step: Unilateral dermatomal vesicular rash identifies reactivated varicella-zoster virus (shingles).
step: VZV remains latent in dorsal root (sensory) ganglia after chickenpox; reactivation travels along the sensory nerve to the skin of that dermatome.
step: A Tzanck smear would show multinucleated giant cells, identical to HSV, so it cannot separate them; PCR can.

@mistakes
- Saying herpes latency means the virus is "dead" or "cleared"; the genome persists and can reactivate.
- Attributing both HSV-1 and HSV-2 latency to the same ganglion; HSV-1 is trigeminal, HSV-2 sacral.
- Expecting a positive Monospot in CMV mononucleosis; it is heterophile negative.
- Forgetting that acyclovir needs viral thymidine kinase to be activated; CMV (which lacks a thymidine kinase) is treated with ganciclovir, and thymidine kinase-deficient HSV needs foscarnet.
