@article
Fungi are **eukaryotes** with cell walls of **chitin and beta-glucan** and membranes containing **ergosterol** (human cells use cholesterol). Those differences are the antifungal targets. Clinically, three forms exist: **yeasts** (single cells that bud), **molds** (filamentous hyphae) and **dimorphic fungi**, which are molds at room temperature and yeasts at body temperature ("cold = mold, heat = yeast"). Fungal infections cluster in people with neutropenia, T-cell defects, diabetes, burns, or indwelling catheters, and with broad-spectrum antibiotics or steroids. Wet mounts with KOH dissolve host cells and show fungi; calcofluor white fluoresces them; silver stains and India ink help for specific organisms.

### Superficial and cutaneous

**Dermatophytes** (*Trichophyton*, *Microsporum*, *Epidermophyton*) infect keratin: tinea capitis, corporis (ringworm), cruris, pedis and unguium. KOH shows septate hyphae; treat with topical azoles or terbinafine (oral for scalp and nails). ***Malassezia furfur*** causes tinea versicolor (hypopigmented or hyperpigmented macules, "spaghetti and meatballs" on KOH); treat with selenium sulfide or topical azoles.

### Opportunistic and invasive fungi

- ***Candida albicans***: yeast with **pseudohyphae and germ tubes**. Thrush (scrapable white plaques; HIV, inhaled steroids, antibiotics), esophagitis (AIDS-defining when CD4 low), vulvovaginitis, and **candidemia** (central lines, parenteral nutrition, abdominal surgery). Candidemia is treated with an **echinocandin** and removal of the catheter; fluconazole suits mucosal disease.
- ***Cryptococcus neoformans***: encapsulated yeast with narrow-based budding; **India ink** shows a halo and mucicarmine stains the capsule; urease positive. Inhaled from soil contaminated by **pigeon droppings**. Causes meningitis in AIDS (CD4 below 100) with high opening pressure; **cryptococcal antigen** in CSF or serum is highly sensitive. Induction therapy is **amphotericin B with flucytosine**, then fluconazole.
- ***Aspergillus fumigatus***: **septate hyphae with acute-angle (about 45 degree) branching**. Three patterns: **aspergilloma** (fungus ball in a preexisting cavity: hemoptysis), **allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis** (asthma or cystic fibrosis; eosinophilia, high IgE, central bronchiectasis) and **invasive aspergillosis** (neutropenia or transplant; nodules with a halo sign; serum galactomannan). Invasive disease: voriconazole. *A. flavus* makes aflatoxin, a risk factor for hepatocellular carcinoma.
- **Mucorales** (*Rhizopus*, *Mucor*): **broad, ribbon-like, nonseptate hyphae branching at right angles**. Diabetic ketoacidosis, neutropenia and iron overload are risk factors; **rhinocerebral mucormycosis** causes facial pain, black necrotic turbinate or palate, and orbital extension. Treat with **urgent surgical debridement** and **liposomal amphotericin B**; voriconazole is inactive.
- ***Pneumocystis jirovecii***: an atypical fungus seen with **silver stain** (cysts) in lung tissue or bronchoalveolar lavage. Causes subacute dry cough and dyspnea in HIV (CD4 below 200) or immunosuppressed patients: bilateral diffuse ground-glass infiltrates, hypoxemia, high LDH and high serum beta-D-glucan. Treat for 21 days with **trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole**, adding **corticosteroids if PaO2 is below 70 mmHg or the A-a gradient is 35 mmHg or higher**.

### Endemic (dimorphic) mycoses

| Fungus | Region and exposure | Tissue form | Clues |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Histoplasma capsulatum* | Ohio and Mississippi River valleys; bat and bird droppings, caves | Small yeast inside macrophages | Pulmonary, or disseminated with hepatosplenomegaly and pancytopenia; urine antigen |
| *Blastomyces dermatitidis* | Great Lakes, Ohio and Mississippi valleys; moist soil, decaying wood | **Broad-based budding** yeast | Pneumonia, verrucous skin lesions, bone |
| *Coccidioides* | Arid southwestern U.S. (Arizona, San Joaquin Valley of California); dust | **Spherules with endospores** | Valley fever; erythema nodosum; meningitis when disseminated |
| *Paracoccidioides* | Latin America | Multiple budding yeast ("ship's wheel") | Oral ulcers, lung disease |
| *Sporothrix schenckii* | Rose thorns, sphagnum moss | Cigar-shaped yeast | Ascending lymphocutaneous nodules |

Treatment is usually itraconazole for mild disease and amphotericin B for severe disease; coccidioidal meningitis uses fluconazole.

### Antifungal drugs

| Drug | Mechanism | Key adverse effects |
|---|---|---|
| Amphotericin B | Binds ergosterol and forms membrane pores | Infusion reactions, nephrotoxicity, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia (less with liposomal form) |
| Azoles (fluconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole, posaconazole, isavuconazole) | Inhibit fungal lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase (CYP450), blocking ergosterol synthesis | Hepatotoxicity; inhibit human CYP450 so many interactions; QT prolongation |
| Echinocandins (caspofungin, micafungin, anidulafungin) | Inhibit beta-1,3-glucan synthase in the cell wall | Well tolerated; infusion reactions |
| Terbinafine | Inhibits squalene epoxidase | Hepatotoxicity |
| Flucytosine | Converted to 5-fluorouracil, inhibiting DNA and RNA synthesis | Bone marrow suppression |
| Griseofulvin | Disrupts microtubules | Interactions, teratogenic |

### The A-a gradient

Pneumocystis pneumonia impairs gas exchange, widening the alveolar-arterial gradient:
$$P_AO_2 = F_iO_2 (P_{atm} - P_{H_2O}) - \frac{PaCO_2}{R}, \qquad \text{A-a} = P_AO_2 - PaO_2,$$
with $P_{atm} = 760$ mmHg, $P_{H_2O} = 47$ mmHg and $R = 0.8$.

@example
title: An A-a gradient on room air
problem: A man with AIDS has dyspnea. On room air, ABG shows $PaO_2 = 58$ mmHg and $PaCO_2 = 32$ mmHg. Calculate the A-a gradient and decide whether adjunctive steroids are indicated if *Pneumocystis* pneumonia is confirmed.
step: $P_AO_2 = 0.21 \times (760 - 47) - 32/0.8 = 0.21 \times 713 - 40 = 149.7 - 40 = 109.7$ mmHg.
step: A-a $= 109.7 - 58 = 51.7$ mmHg.
step: PaO2 is below 70 mmHg and the gradient exceeds 35 mmHg, so adjunctive corticosteroids are indicated alongside trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole.

@example
title: Mold or yeast?
problem: A biopsy of nasal tissue from a 46-year-old man in diabetic ketoacidosis shows broad ribbon-like hyphae with right-angle branching and few septa. Name the group and key management.
step: Broad, ribbon-like, nonseptate hyphae with right-angle branches are Mucorales (for example *Rhizopus*).
step: Diabetic ketoacidosis impairs neutrophil function and raises free iron, favoring these molds.
step: Management is urgent surgical debridement, liposomal amphotericin B and correction of ketoacidosis. Voriconazole and fluconazole do not cover Mucorales.

@mistakes
- Treating suspected mucormycosis with voriconazole alone; Mucorales are intrinsically resistant, so debride and give amphotericin B.
- Confusing hyphae: *Aspergillus* is septate with acute-angle branches; Mucorales are nonseptate with right-angle branches.
- Using fluconazole for invasive aspergillosis; it has no reliable mold activity.
- Forgetting steroids in moderate-to-severe *Pneumocystis* pneumonia (PaO2 below 70 mmHg or A-a gradient 35 mmHg or more).
