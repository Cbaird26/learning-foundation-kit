@article
MCAT physics is algebra-based and heavily contextual: blood in vessels, ultrasound, lenses in the eye, radioactive tracers. Most problems reduce to a handful of relationships. Use $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ unless told otherwise and keep SI units throughout.

### Mechanics

For constant acceleration, $v = v_0 + at$, $\Delta x = v_0 t + \tfrac12 a t^2$ and $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\Delta x$. Newton's second law is $\sum F = ma$; on a frictionless incline of angle $\theta$ the acceleration along the plane is $g\sin\theta$. Energy: $KE = \tfrac12 mv^2$, gravitational $PE = mgh$, and work $W = Fd\cos\theta$; mechanical energy is conserved when only conservative forces act. A projectile has constant horizontal velocity and vertical acceleration $g$ downward.

### Fluids

Pressure at depth $h$ is $P = P_0 + \rho g h$. The buoyant force equals the weight of fluid displaced, $F_B = \rho_{fluid} V_{displaced}\, g$. For steady flow of an incompressible fluid, the **continuity equation** is $A_1v_1 = A_2v_2$, and **Bernoulli's equation** says $P + \tfrac12\rho v^2 + \rho g h$ is constant along a streamline. Where a vessel narrows, speed increases and pressure falls. Poiseuille's law shows that flow resistance varies as $1/r^4$, so halving a radius multiplies resistance by 16.

### Electricity and circuits

Coulomb's law gives $F = kq_1q_2/r^2$. Ohm's law is $V = IR$. Resistors in series add: $R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2$. In parallel, $1/R_{eq} = 1/R_1 + 1/R_2$ and the voltage across each branch is the same. Power is $P = IV = I^2R = V^2/R$. A capacitor stores $Q = CV$ and energy $\tfrac12 CV^2$.

### Waves, sound and light

$v = f\lambda$. Sound intensity level in decibels changes by 10 dB for each factor of 10 in intensity. The **Doppler effect**: the observed frequency rises when source and observer approach each other and falls when they separate. For a thin lens or a spherical mirror,

$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i}.$$

A positive $d_i$ means a real image (on the opposite side of a lens from the object). A converging lens has $f > 0$. Magnification is $m = -d_i/d_o$, and a negative $m$ means the image is inverted. Snell's law is $n_1\sin\theta_1 = n_2\sin\theta_2$.

### Atomic and nuclear

Photon energy is $E = hf$. Radioactive decay is exponential, and after $n$ half-lives the remaining fraction is $(1/2)^n$. Alpha decay lowers the mass number by 4 and the atomic number by 2; beta-minus decay raises the atomic number by 1.

### Strategy

Write the relationship, plug in numbers with units, and check the order of magnitude. When two answer choices differ by a factor of 10 or by a sign, estimate before calculating.

@example
title: Image formed by a converging lens
problem: A converging lens has focal length $f = 10$ cm. An object is placed 30 cm from the lens. Where is the image, and is it real or virtual?
step: Use $1/f = 1/d_o + 1/d_i$, so $1/d_i = 1/10 - 1/30$.
step: $1/d_i = 3/30 - 1/30 = 2/30 = 1/15$, so $d_i = 15$ cm.
step: $d_i > 0$, so the image is real, on the far side of the lens. Magnification $m = -15/30 = -0.5$: inverted and half the size.

@example
title: Parallel resistors
problem: A 12 V source is connected across a 6 ohm resistor and a 12 ohm resistor wired in parallel. What is the total current from the source?
step: $1/R_{eq} = 1/6 + 1/12 = 2/12 + 1/12 = 3/12 = 1/4$, so $R_{eq} = 4$ ohm.
step: $I = V/R_{eq} = 12/4 = 3$ A.
step: Check by branch: $12/6 = 2$ A and $12/12 = 1$ A, which sum to 3 A.

@mistakes
- Adding parallel resistances like series ones; parallel branches have a smaller equivalent resistance than any single branch.
- Forgetting to convert cm or mL to SI units in $\rho g V$ or $PV$ problems.
- Treating buoyant force as the weight of the object instead of the weight of the displaced fluid.
- Using the wrong sign convention in the lens equation.

@practice num
prompt: An object is dropped from rest from a height of 20 m. Ignoring air resistance and using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, what is its speed just before impact? Give the answer to three significant figures.
answer: 19.8
check: math.sqrt(2 * 9.8 * 20)
tol: 1%
unit: m/s
hint: Either use energy conservation or $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\Delta x$.
hint: $v^2 = 2gh = 2(9.8)(20)$.
solution: $v = \sqrt{2(9.8)(20)} = \sqrt{392} = 19.8$ m/s.

@practice num
prompt: A radioactive tracer has a half-life of 6.0 hours. A dose contains 80 mg of the isotope. How many mg remain after 24 hours?
answer: 5
check: 80 * 0.5 ** (24 / 6.0)
tol: 1%
unit: mg
hint: Find how many half-lives fit in 24 hours.
hint: Four half-lives pass; each one halves the amount.
solution: $24/6.0 = 4$ half-lives, so $80 \times (1/2)^4 = 80/16 = 5$ mg.

@practice num
prompt: A solid object of volume $2.0\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3$ is completely submerged in water of density $1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$. Using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, what is the buoyant force in newtons?
answer: 19.6
check: 1000 * 2.0e-3 * 9.8
tol: 1%
unit: N
hint: The buoyant force depends on the displaced fluid and not on the object's mass.
hint: Use $F_B = \rho V g$ with $V$ as the submerged volume.
solution: $F_B = (1000)(2.0\times10^{-3})(9.8) = 19.6$ N.

@practice self
prompt: A 2.0 kg block slides down a frictionless incline that makes a 30 degree angle with the horizontal. Derive the acceleration of the block along the incline, state its numerical value using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ and explain why the mass does not matter.
hint: Draw the block and resolve gravity into components parallel and perpendicular to the plane.
hint: The normal force cancels the perpendicular component.
solution: The component of weight along the incline is $mg\sin\theta$ and the perpendicular component $mg\cos\theta$ is balanced by the normal force. Newton's second law along the plane gives $ma = mg\sin\theta$, so $a = g\sin\theta = 9.8 \times 0.5 = 4.9\ \text{m/s}^2$ down the incline. Mass cancels because the force along the plane is proportional to mass, as is the inertia.
rubric: Resolves weight into components parallel and perpendicular to the incline.
rubric: Obtains $a = g\sin\theta = 4.9\ \text{m/s}^2$.
rubric: Explains that mass cancels from both sides of Newton's second law.

@quiz num
prompt: A hair dryer heating element has a resistance of 24 ohms and is connected to a 120 V supply. What power does the element dissipate, in watts?
answer: 600
check: 120 ** 2 / 24
tol: 1%
unit: W
hint: Use the power relationship that involves voltage and resistance.
hint: $P = V^2/R$.
solution: $P = (120)^2/24 = 14400/24 = 600$ W.

@quiz mc
prompt: An ambulance siren emits a constant frequency while the ambulance approaches a stationary listener and then passes. How does the frequency the listener hears change?
choice: It stays the same, because the source frequency does not change.
choice: It is lower while the ambulance approaches and higher after it passes.
choice*: It is higher while the ambulance approaches and lower after it passes.
choice: It is higher both before and after the ambulance passes, because the speed is large.
hint: Think of the wavefronts being compressed or stretched in the direction of motion.
solution: An approaching source compresses wavefronts, shortening the wavelength and raising the observed frequency; after passing, the source recedes and the wavelength increases, lowering the frequency. The emitted frequency is unchanged, but the observed one is not.
