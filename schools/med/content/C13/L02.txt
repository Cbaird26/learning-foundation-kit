The Biological and Biochemical section (and parts of the Psychological and Social section) reward a small set of **reusable models**. You will rarely be asked for an isolated fact; you will be given an experiment and asked what a model predicts.

### Cells and the central dogma

DNA is replicated semiconservatively (S phase), transcribed to mRNA, and translated by ribosomes. Eukaryotic mRNA is capped, spliced and polyadenylated in the nucleus. Membrane proteins and secreted proteins are made on the rough endoplasmic reticulum, modified in the Golgi apparatus, and sorted by vesicles. Mitochondria perform oxidative phosphorylation; lysosomes digest. The **cell cycle** runs G1, S, G2, M, with checkpoints regulated by cyclins and cyclin-dependent kinases; p53 halts the cycle after DNA damage. If a passage describes a drug that blocks spindle formation, expect arrest in metaphase.

### Genetics

For Mendelian crosses, multiply independent probabilities: a cross of two $AaBb$ parents gives $aabb$ with probability $\frac14 \cdot \frac14 = \frac1{16}$. For X-linked recessive traits, affected males are far more common than affected females because males have one X. Meiosis I separates homologs and meiosis II separates sister chromatids; **nondisjunction** in either produces aneuploid gametes.

**Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium** describes allele frequencies that stay constant when there is random mating, no selection, no migration, no mutation and a large population:

$$p + q = 1, \qquad p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1.$$

Here $q^2$ is the frequency of the homozygous recessive genotype and $2pq$ the frequency of carriers. Start from whatever is given: usually $q^2$, from which $q = \sqrt{q^2}$.

### Organ systems

- **Cardiovascular**: cardiac output $= \text{heart rate} \times \text{stroke volume}$. Flow depends on the pressure gradient and on resistance, and resistance rises steeply as vessel radius falls.
- **Renal**: the nephron filters at the glomerulus, reabsorbs most solutes in the proximal tubule, builds a gradient in the loop of Henle, and fine-tunes at the distal tubule and collecting duct. Antidiuretic hormone (ADH) inserts aquaporins in the collecting duct and concentrates urine; aldosterone increases sodium reabsorption and potassium secretion.
- **Endocrine**: negative feedback dominates. High thyroid hormone suppresses TSH; low cortisol raises ACTH. A passage that reports a low hormone with a high trophic hormone points to a failure of the target gland.
- **Respiratory and digestive**: gas exchange follows partial-pressure gradients; digestion divides labor among stomach (pepsin, acid), pancreas (bicarbonate and enzymes), liver (bile) and small intestine (absorption).

@example
title: Carrier frequency from disease incidence
problem: A recessive condition affects 1 in 10,000 people in a population in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium. What fraction of the population are carriers (heterozygotes)? Give a decimal.
step: The affected frequency is $q^2 = 1/10{,}000 = 0.0001$.
step: Take the square root: $q = 0.01$, so $p = 1 - q = 0.99$.
step: Carriers have frequency $2pq = 2(0.99)(0.01) = 0.0198$.
step: About 2 in 100 people are carriers, roughly 200 times the frequency of affected people.

@example
title: Cardiac output
problem: A patient has a heart rate of 80 beats/min and a stroke volume of 65 mL. What is the cardiac output in L/min?
step: Use $CO = HR \times SV$, so $80 \times 65 = 5200$ mL/min.
step: Convert to liters: $5200 \text{ mL/min} = 5.2$ L/min.

@mistakes
- Using $q^2$ as if it were $q$ when computing carrier frequency; take the square root first.
- Forgetting that Hardy-Weinberg applies to alleles, so the carrier frequency is $2pq$ and not $q$.
- Confusing the roles of ADH (water permeability of the collecting duct) and aldosterone (sodium and potassium handling).
- Assuming that every high hormone level means an overactive gland; check whether the trophic hormone is high or low.

answer: 1/16

prompt: A recessive trait affects 1 in 2,500 people in a population assumed to be in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium. What is the carrier frequency, to four decimal places?
answer: 0.0392
check: 2 * math.sqrt(1/2500) * (1 - math.sqrt(1/2500))
tol: 0.0003
hint: The affected frequency is $q^2$; find $q$.
hint: $q = 0.02$, so $p = 0.98$; carriers are $2pq$.
solution: $q^2 = 0.0004$, so $q = 0.02$ and $p = 0.98$. Carriers $= 2pq = 2(0.98)(0.02) = 0.0392$.

@practice mc
prompt: A patient has water intake restriction and a very concentrated urine. In which structure does ADH act to produce this urine?
choice: Glomerulus, where it increases the filtration fraction of plasma proteins.
choice*: Collecting duct, where it inserts water channels (aquaporins) so water is reabsorbed.
choice: Proximal tubule, where it secretes sodium into the lumen.
choice: Ascending limb of the loop of Henle, where it makes the wall permeable to water.
hint: ADH changes water permeability, not solute transport, in the last segment of the nephron.
solution: ADH acts on principal cells of the collecting duct, adding aquaporin channels that let water follow the medullary osmotic gradient back into the blood. The ascending limb is impermeable to water, and ADH does not secrete sodium or change protein filtration.

@quiz num
prompt: In a population in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, the frequency of a dominant allele $A$ is $p = 0.7$. What fraction of the population is heterozygous ($Aa$)?
answer: 0.42
check: 2 * 0.7 * (1 - 0.7)
tol: 0.005
hint: First find the frequency $q$ of the other allele.
hint: Heterozygotes occur at $2pq$.
solution: $q = 1 - 0.7 = 0.3$, so $2pq = 2(0.7)(0.3) = 0.42$.

@quiz mc
prompt: A researcher treats cultured cells with a drug and sees that most cells accumulate with duplicated DNA content but do not divide, and chromosomes remain condensed without aligning. Which drug action best explains this observation?
choice: Inhibition of DNA polymerase, blocking replication in S phase.
choice*: Disruption of microtubule polymerization, preventing a functional spindle and arresting cells in mitosis.
choice: Inhibition of RNA polymerase II, blocking mRNA synthesis in G1.
choice: Activation of p53 target genes that arrest cells in G1 before DNA replication.
hint: Duplicated DNA means S phase finished.
hint: Chromosomes are condensed but unaligned; which structure moves them?
solution: Cells that have duplicated their DNA but have condensed, unaligned chromosomes are stuck in mitosis because the spindle cannot form, as with a drug that disrupts microtubules. Blocking DNA polymerase or G1 arrest would prevent duplication, so DNA content would stay at the G1 level.
