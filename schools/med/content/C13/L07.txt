### Learning

In **classical conditioning** a neutral stimulus paired with an unconditioned stimulus (US) becomes a conditioned stimulus (CS) that triggers a conditioned response (CR) resembling the unconditioned response (UR). Extinction occurs when the CS is repeatedly presented without the US. In **operant conditioning** consequences shape voluntary behavior: positive reinforcement adds something desirable, negative reinforcement removes something aversive (both increase behavior), and punishment decreases behavior. A **variable-ratio** schedule produces high, steady response rates that resist extinction.

### Memory, cognition and development

Encoding, storage and retrieval are the three stages of memory. Working memory holds roughly 7 plus or minus 2 chunks for seconds; long-term memory is divided into explicit (episodic and semantic, hippocampus-dependent) and implicit (procedural, priming; basal ganglia and cerebellum). Damage to both hippocampi causes anterograde amnesia: new explicit memories fail to form. The serial position effect favors the first (primacy) and last (recency) items of a list, and elaborative rehearsal encodes better than rote repetition. Piaget's stages of cognitive development are sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete operational and formal operational; Kohlberg's moral reasoning is preconventional, conventional and postconventional.

### Sensation

**Weber's law** says the just-noticeable difference is a constant fraction of the baseline stimulus: $\Delta I / I = k$. Signal detection theory separates sensitivity from response bias. Rods support dim, peripheral vision; cones support color and acuity.

### Social psychology

- **Attribution**: the fundamental attribution error is overestimating dispositional causes of other people's behavior; the self-serving bias credits ourselves for success and blames situations for failure.
- **Cognitive dissonance**: people reduce the discomfort of conflicting attitudes and behavior by changing one of them.
- **Conformity, obedience and the bystander effect**: responsibility diffuses in groups.
- **Stereotype, prejudice, discrimination**: cognition, attitude and behavior, respectively.

### Sociology

Three classic perspectives frame the same facts differently. **Functionalism** asks how institutions contribute to social stability. **Conflict theory** emphasizes competition and unequal power. **Symbolic interactionism** studies the meanings people build in everyday interaction. Social stratification refers to rankings by wealth, power and prestige; social capital is the benefit that comes from social networks. Durkheim's anomie describes normlessness in rapidly changing societies. Health disparities are linked to socioeconomic status, race, gender and neighborhood through access to care, stress exposure and discrimination.

### Biological links

Neurotransmitters are favorite connectors: dopamine (reward, movement), serotonin (mood, sleep), GABA (inhibition), glutamate (excitation), acetylcholine (memory, muscle). The amygdala processes fear, the hippocampus forms explicit memories, and the prefrontal cortex supports planning and impulse control.

@example
title: Identifying the conditioned stimulus
problem: A child receives painful injections at a pediatric clinic and cries. After several visits, the child begins to cry as soon as she sees the clinic's blue door. Identify the US, UR, CS and CR.
step: The injection causes pain, and crying in response to it is automatic, so the injection is the US and the crying during the injection is the UR.
step: The blue door was originally neutral. After being paired with injections it triggers crying, so the blue door is the CS.
step: Crying at the sight of the door alone is the CR. If she visits many times without injections, the response should weaken through extinction.

@example
title: Weber's law
problem: People can just detect a difference between a 500 g weight and a 510 g weight. Assuming Weber's law, what fraction is the Weber constant?
step: The difference is $\Delta I = 10$ g at baseline $I = 500$ g.
step: $k = \Delta I / I = 10/500 = 0.02$, or 2%. The just-noticeable difference for a 1000 g baseline would be 20 g.

@mistakes
- Confusing negative reinforcement with punishment; negative reinforcement increases behavior by removing something unpleasant.
- Calling the response to the neutral-then-conditioned stimulus the "UR"; once learned it is the CR.
- Using sociological perspectives interchangeably: look for stability (functionalism), power and inequality (conflict) or meaning in interaction (symbolic interactionism).
- Treating the fundamental attribution error as something we do to our own behavior; it concerns judging others.

@quiz ms
prompt: Which statements about memory are correct? Select all that apply.
choice*: Bilateral damage to the hippocampus produces anterograde amnesia for new explicit memories while procedural learning can remain intact.
choice*: The serial position effect predicts better recall for the first and last items on a list.
