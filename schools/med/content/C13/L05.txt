@article
MCAT organic chemistry is less about memorizing named reactions than about **recognizing a few mechanisms** and reading spectra. Passages usually give you a reaction scheme with unfamiliar molecules, and the question asks which general rule applies.

### Structure and stereochemistry

The **degrees of unsaturation** (rings plus pi bonds) of a compound C$_c$H$_h$N$_n$O$_o$ are

$$\text{DoU} = \frac{2c + 2 + n - h}{2}.$$

Oxygen is ignored and halogens count as hydrogens. A DoU of 4 or more suggests a benzene ring. A stereocenter has four different groups. A molecule with $n$ stereocenters has at most $2^n$ stereoisomers; **enantiomers** are non-superimposable mirror images, **diastereomers** are stereoisomers that are not mirror images, and a **meso** compound has stereocenters but an internal mirror plane, so it is achiral and the count falls below $2^n$. Assign R or S by ranking the groups by atomic number (Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priorities) and looking with the lowest priority group pointing away.

### Substitution and elimination

| Pathway | Favored by | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| SN2 | Methyl or primary substrate, strong nucleophile, polar aprotic solvent | One step, backside attack, inversion, rate = k[RX][Nu] |
| SN1 | Tertiary substrate, weak nucleophile, polar protic solvent | Carbocation intermediate, racemization, rate = k[RX] |
| E2 | Strong base, heat, anti-periplanar H and leaving group | One step, Zaitsev product unless the base is bulky |
| E1 | Tertiary substrate, weak base, heat | Carbocation, competes with SN1 |

### Carbonyl chemistry

The carbonyl carbon is electrophilic. Aldehydes and ketones undergo **nucleophilic addition**; carboxylic acid derivatives undergo **nucleophilic acyl substitution** through a tetrahedral intermediate. Reactivity falls in the order acyl chloride > anhydride > ester ~ carboxylic acid > amide. NaBH$_4$ reduces aldehydes and ketones; LiAlH$_4$ also reduces esters, acids and amides. Enolates, formed at the alpha carbon, drive aldol and Claisen reactions.

### Spectroscopy

- **IR**: broad O-H near 3300 cm$^{-1}$ (carboxylic acids broader, 2500 to 3300); sharp C=O near 1715 cm$^{-1}$; C$\equiv$N near 2250 cm$^{-1}$.
- **$^1$H NMR**: the number of signals shows the number of distinct hydrogen environments; the integration shows their ratio; a signal split by $n$ equivalent neighbors appears as $n+1$ peaks. Aldehyde H is near 9 to 10 ppm, carboxylic acid H near 10 to 12 ppm, aromatic H near 7 ppm.
- **Mass spectrometry** gives the molecular ion peak, and the molecular formula gives the DoU.

### Separations

An acidic compound is extracted from an organic layer into water by converting it to its **conjugate base** with aqueous base; acidify the water layer to recover the neutral compound. A base is extracted with aqueous acid. Choose a base that is strong enough: bicarbonate (conjugate acid pKa about 6.4) deprotonates a carboxylic acid (pKa about 4 to 5) but not a phenol (pKa about 10).

@example
title: Degrees of unsaturation
problem: A compound has the formula C$_8$H$_{10}$. How many degrees of unsaturation does it have, and what does that suggest?
step: Use $\text{DoU} = (2c + 2 + n - h)/2$ with $c = 8$, $n = 0$, $h = 10$.
step: $\text{DoU} = (16 + 2 - 10)/2 = 4$.
step: Four degrees is exactly a benzene ring (one ring plus three pi bonds), so C$_8$H$_{10}$ could be a dimethylbenzene or ethylbenzene.

@example
title: Predicting an NMR pattern
problem: Predict the $^1$H NMR pattern of ethyl acetate, CH$_3$C(=O)OCH$_2$CH$_3$.
step: Count the environments: the acetyl CH$_3$, the OCH$_2$ and the ethyl CH$_3$ give three signals in a 3:2:3 ratio.
step: The acetyl CH$_3$ has no neighboring hydrogens, so it is a singlet near 2.0 ppm.
step: The OCH$_2$ has 3 neighbors, so it is a quartet near 4.1 ppm; the ethyl CH$_3$ has 2 neighbors, so it is a triplet near 1.2 ppm.

@mistakes
- Counting oxygen atoms in the degrees-of-unsaturation formula; oxygen does not change the count.
- Assuming SN2 can occur at a tertiary carbon; steric hindrance blocks the backside attack.
- Assigning a splitting pattern from the neighbors on the same carbon rather than the adjacent carbons.
- Using too weak a base to extract an acid, or forgetting to re-acidify the aqueous layer to recover it.

prompt: Calculate the degrees of unsaturation of C$_9$H$_{10}$O$_2$.
answer: 5
check: (2*9 + 2 - 10) / 2
hint: Ignore the oxygen atoms.
hint: Use $(2c + 2 - h)/2$.
solution: $(18 + 2 - 10)/2 = 5$, consistent with, for example, a benzene ring plus one C=O.

@practice num
prompt: A molecule has three stereocenters and no internal plane of symmetry. What is the maximum number of stereoisomers?
answer: 8
check: 2 ** 3
hint: Each stereocenter can have two configurations.
hint: Use $2^n$.
solution: $2^3 = 8$ stereoisomers, forming 4 pairs of enantiomers.

@practice mc
prompt: A primary alkyl bromide is treated with sodium iodide in acetone, and the product is formed with inversion of configuration at the carbon bearing the leaving group in a single step. Which mechanism is operating?
choice: SN1, through a carbocation intermediate.
choice*: SN2, with backside attack by iodide.
choice: E1, through loss of a proton from a carbocation.
choice: E2, with an anti-periplanar proton removed.
hint: Which conditions give inversion in one step?
solution: A primary substrate, good nucleophile, polar aprotic solvent and inversion of configuration are the hallmarks of SN2. SN1 and E1 need a stable carbocation, and E2 would give an alkene and not a substitution product.

@practice ms
prompt: A student records an IR spectrum of an unknown with a very broad absorption from 2500 to 3300 cm$^{-1}$ and a strong sharp peak at 1710 cm$^{-1}$. Which additional observations are consistent with a carboxylic acid? Select all that apply.
choice*: A $^1$H NMR signal near 11 ppm that disappears after shaking with D$_2$O.
choice*: Soluble in aqueous sodium bicarbonate with release of gas.
choice: A $^1$H NMR signal near 9.7 ppm that is split into a triplet by an adjacent CH$_2$.
choice: A sharp absorption near 2250 cm$^{-1}$.
hint: Think of the carboxylic acid O-H proton and what a weak base does to it.
solution: Carboxylic acid protons appear near 10 to 12 ppm and exchange with D$_2$O, and bicarbonate deprotonates acids, releasing CO$_2$. A triplet near 9.7 ppm is an aldehyde proton, and 2250 cm$^{-1}$ would be a nitrile.

@practice self
prompt: A mixture contains benzoic acid and naphthalene dissolved in diethyl ether. Describe a procedure using only aqueous sodium bicarbonate and aqueous hydrochloric acid to separate and recover both solids. Explain the chemistry of each step.
hint: One of the two compounds can be made ionic by a weak base.
hint: The ionic form goes into the water layer; the neutral compound stays in the ether.
hint: How do you convert the benzoate back into the neutral acid so it precipitates?
solution: Shake the ether solution with aqueous sodium bicarbonate. Benzoic acid (pKa about 4.2) is deprotonated to sodium benzoate, which is ionic and moves into the aqueous layer, while neutral naphthalene stays in the ether. Separate the layers. Evaporate the ether to recover naphthalene. Acidify the aqueous layer with hydrochloric acid until it is strongly acidic; benzoate is protonated to neutral benzoic acid, which is poorly soluble in water and precipitates and can be filtered off.
rubric: Uses bicarbonate to convert benzoic acid to its water-soluble conjugate base.
rubric: Recovers naphthalene from the ether layer.
rubric: Re-acidifies the aqueous layer to precipitate benzoic acid and explains why.

@quiz mc
prompt: In the $^1$H NMR spectrum of 2-bromopropane, (CH$_3$)$_2$CHBr, which description of the signals is correct?
choice: A single signal, because all hydrogens are on carbons attached to the same bromine.
choice: A triplet and a doublet in a 1:6 ratio.
choice*: A doublet integrating for 6H and a septet integrating for 1H.
choice: A quartet for 6H and a triplet for 1H.
hint: Count the neighboring hydrogens for each type of hydrogen.
solution: The two methyl groups are equivalent: their 6 H have one neighbor (the CH) and give a doublet. The CH has 6 neighbors and gives a septet ($n+1 = 7$).
