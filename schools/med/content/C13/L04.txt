General chemistry is tested in the Chemical and Physical section and, increasingly, through biological contexts: buffers in blood, osmotic pressure across membranes, redox in the electron transport chain. You may not have a calculator on the exam, so practice **estimation**: round to one or two significant figures and keep track of powers of ten.

### Gases and solutions

The ideal gas law is $PV = nRT$ with $R = 0.0821\ \text{L atm mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$ when pressure is in atm, volume in L and temperature in kelvin ($T_K = T_C + 273$). Dalton's law says total pressure is the sum of partial pressures, $P_i = X_i P_{total}$. Real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature.

Colligative properties depend on particle concentration. Osmotic pressure is $\Pi = iMRT$, where $i$ is the van't Hoff factor (about 2 for NaCl). Water moves from low to high solute concentration across a semipermeable membrane.

### Acids, bases and buffers

For a weak acid HA, $K_a = [\text{H}^+][\text{A}^-]/[\text{HA}]$. If the acid is weakly dissociated, $[\text{H}^+] \approx \sqrt{K_a C}$. A **buffer** contains a weak acid and its conjugate base and resists pH change; its pH is given by

$$\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log\frac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}.$$

A buffer is most effective within about one pH unit of its $\text{p}K_a$. At the half-equivalence point of a weak acid titrated with a strong base, $[\text{A}^-] = [\text{HA}]$, so $\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a$.

### Equilibrium and thermodynamics

**Le Chatelier's principle**: a system at equilibrium responds to a disturbance by shifting to counteract it. Adding reactant shifts right; raising temperature shifts an endothermic reaction right and an exothermic reaction left, and the equilibrium constant changes only with temperature. Spontaneity at constant $T$ and $P$ follows

$$\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S.$$

If $\Delta H < 0$ and $\Delta S > 0$ the reaction is spontaneous at all temperatures; if $\Delta H > 0$ and $\Delta S < 0$ it is never spontaneous. The other two combinations switch sign at $T = \Delta H/\Delta S$.

### Electrochemistry

In a galvanic cell oxidation occurs at the anode and reduction at the cathode, and electrons flow from anode to cathode through the wire. The standard cell potential and free energy are related by

$$E^\circ_{cell} = E^\circ_{cathode} - E^\circ_{anode}, \qquad \Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ_{cell},$$

where $F = 96{,}485$ C/mol of electrons. A positive $E^\circ_{cell}$ means a spontaneous reaction.

### Periodic trends

Atomic radius decreases across a period and increases down a group; first ionization energy and electronegativity do the opposite.

@example
title: Buffer pH
problem: An acetate buffer contains 0.20 M acetic acid ($\text{p}K_a = 4.76$) and 0.10 M acetate. What is its pH?
step: Use $\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log([\text{A}^-]/[\text{HA}])$.
step: The ratio is $0.10/0.20 = 0.50$, and $\log 0.50 = -0.30$.
step: $\text{pH} = 4.76 - 0.30 = 4.46$. The pH is below the $\text{p}K_a$ because the acid form predominates.

@example
title: Weak acid pH
problem: What is the pH of a 0.050 M solution of a weak acid with $K_a = 2.0\times10^{-5}$?
step: The acid is weak and concentrated enough that $x \ll 0.050$, so $x^2 = K_a C$.
step: $x^2 = (2.0\times10^{-5})(0.050) = 1.0\times10^{-6}$, so $[\text{H}^+] = 1.0\times10^{-3}$ M.
step: $\text{pH} = -\log(1.0\times10^{-3}) = 3.00$. Check: $x$ is 2% of 0.050, so the approximation holds.

@mistakes
- Using Celsius temperature in $PV = nRT$ instead of kelvin.
- Forgetting the van't Hoff factor $i$ for ionic solutes in $\Pi = iMRT$.
- Reversing the cell potential formula; subtract the anode (oxidation) potential from the cathode (reduction) potential and never multiply the potential by the number of electrons.
- Assuming that a catalyst or a pressure change alters the equilibrium constant; only temperature does.

@practice self
prompt: A passage gives a reaction with $\Delta H = -92$ kJ/mol and $\Delta S = -199$ J/(mol K). Explain whether the reaction is spontaneous at low and at high temperature, and find the temperature at which $\Delta G = 0$.
hint: Write $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$ and check the signs of each term.
hint: Convert $\Delta H$ to joules (or $\Delta S$ to kJ) before using the crossover formula.
solution: Both $\Delta H$ and $\Delta S$ are negative. At low temperature the favorable $\Delta H$ term dominates, so $\Delta G < 0$ and the reaction is spontaneous. At high temperature the unfavorable $-T\Delta S$ term (positive) dominates, so the reaction is not spontaneous. The crossover is $T = \Delta H/\Delta S = (-92{,}000)/(-199) \approx 462$ K.
rubric: Correctly says spontaneous at low T and nonspontaneous at high T.
rubric: Attributes the change to the sign and size of the $-T\Delta S$ term.
rubric: Computes the crossover near 462 K with consistent units.

@quiz mc
prompt: A 0.10 M solution of a weak monoprotic acid is titrated with 0.10 M NaOH. At the volume of base equal to half of that needed to reach the equivalence point, the measured pH is 4.20. What is the $\text{p}K_a$ of the acid?
choice: 2.10, because the pH is halved at that point.
choice*: 4.20, because $[\text{A}^-] = [\text{HA}]$ at half-equivalence.
choice: 7.00, because the titration is complete.
choice: 9.80, because $\text{p}K_a + \text{pOH} = 14$.
hint: What is the ratio of base to acid when exactly half the acid has been neutralized?
solution: At half-equivalence, half the HA has been converted to A$^-$, so the ratio is 1 and $\log 1 = 0$. Then $\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a = 4.20$.
