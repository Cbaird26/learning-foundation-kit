## Week-by-week key ideas

- **Week 1, structure and planning.** Four sections scored 118 to 132 each (total 472 to 528); 230 questions in 375 minutes. Plan backward from the test date using a diagnostic, with hours weighted to weak areas and a mix of content review, recall and timed practice.
- **Week 2, biology.** Central dogma and cell cycle; Hardy-Weinberg ($p + q = 1$, carriers $2pq$); cardiac output $= HR \times SV$; nephron, ADH, aldosterone and feedback loops.
- **Week 3, biochemistry.** pI from pKa values; Michaelis-Menten and inhibitor patterns; $\Delta G = \Delta G^{\circ\prime} + RT\ln Q$; regulation of glycolysis and gluconeogenesis.
- **Week 4, general chemistry.** $PV = nRT$; buffers and Henderson-Hasselbalch; Le Chatelier; $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$; $E^\circ_{cell} = E^\circ_{cathode} - E^\circ_{anode}$; $\Pi = iMRT$.
- **Week 5, organic chemistry.** Degrees of unsaturation, $2^n$ stereoisomers, SN1/SN2/E1/E2, carbonyl reactivity, IR and NMR, acid-base extraction.
- **Week 6, physics.** Kinematics, energy, fluids (continuity, Bernoulli, buoyancy), circuits, lenses, Doppler and half-life.
- **Week 7, psychology and sociology.** Conditioning, memory, development, attribution, dissonance, and the three sociological perspectives.
- **Week 8, research design and statistics.** Designs, confounding, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, 95% intervals, p-values and $r^2$.
- **Week 9, CARS.** Passage maps, question families, scope and extremeness.
- **Week 10, data and figures.** Five-step routine, percent change, fold change, interpolation, Lineweaver-Burk slope and intercept, error bars.
- **Week 11, mixed passages.** Name the model for each stem, supply constants carefully, mark and move.
- **Week 12, test-day strategy.** Pacing checkpoints, answer every question, error log and repair plan.

## Most common errors

1. Using the wrong baseline or the wrong denominator (percent change, sensitivity).
2. Using Celsius where kelvin is needed, or forgetting $i$ in colligative properties.
3. Choosing extreme answers in CARS and importing outside knowledge.
4. Interpreting p-values as the probability that the null hypothesis is true.
5. Studying only favorite topics and not timing practice.

prompt: A phosphate buffer has total phosphate concentration 0.10 M, made of H$_2$PO$_4^-$ and HPO$_4^{2-}$, and $\text{p}K_a = 7.2$ for that pair. The buffer is adjusted to pH 7.0. What is the concentration of HPO$_4^{2-}$ in mol/L, to three significant figures? Use the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation.
answer: 0.0387
check: 0.10 * 10 ** (7.0 - 7.2) / (1 + 10 ** (7.0 - 7.2))
tol: 1%
unit: M
hint: Find the ratio $[\text{HPO}_4^{2-}]/[\text{H}_2\text{PO}_4^-]$ from $\text{pH} - \text{p}K_a$.
hint: The ratio is $10^{-0.2}$, about 0.63.
hint: If the ratio of base to acid is $r$, the base is the fraction $r/(1 + r)$ of the total.
solution: $r = 10^{7.0 - 7.2} = 10^{-0.2} = 0.631$. The base fraction is $0.631/1.631 = 0.387$, so $[\text{HPO}_4^{2-}] = 0.10 \times 0.387 = 0.0387$ M.

prompt: A screening test for a condition has sensitivity 90% and specificity 80%. It is used in a population in which the prevalence of the condition is 10%. What is the positive predictive value? Give an exact fraction.
answer: 1/3
check: (0.10 * 0.90) / (0.10 * 0.90 + 0.90 * (1 - 0.80))
hint: Imagine 1000 people and count true positives and false positives.
hint: There are 100 with the condition and 900 without it.
hint: True positives are 90% of 100; false positives are 20% of 900.
solution: Among 1000 people, true positives $= 0.90 \times 100 = 90$ and false positives $= 0.20 \times 900 = 180$. $\text{PPV} = 90/(90 + 180) = 1/3$. Most positive results are false at this low prevalence.
