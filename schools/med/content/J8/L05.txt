@article
### Writing about beliefs neutrally

Historians describe what religions teach and how they spread without saying which is true. A good habit is to attribute beliefs: write "Muslims believe that..." or "According to Buddhist teaching...", not a flat statement that treats a belief as proven fact or as false.

### Buddhism

Buddhism began in northern India around the 400s BCE (dates are debated), among followers of a teacher called the Buddha, "the awakened one." Buddhists teach the **Four Noble Truths**: life involves suffering; suffering has causes, especially craving; suffering can end; and the **Eightfold Path** of right understanding, conduct and mental discipline leads to its end. The goal is **nirvana**, freedom from the cycle of rebirth. The Maurya emperor Ashoka (about 268 to 232 BCE) supported Buddhism, and monks, merchants and travelers carried it along trade routes to Sri Lanka, Central Asia, China, Southeast Asia, Korea and Japan.

### Christianity

Christianity began in the 1st century CE in the Roman province of Judea, among followers of Jesus of Nazareth. Christians believe that Jesus is the Son of God, that he died and rose from the dead, and that salvation is offered through him. Their sacred text is the Bible. Early Christians spread their faith along Roman roads and sea routes; at first they were sometimes persecuted. The **Edict of Milan** (313 CE) gave Christians legal toleration, and in 380 CE the empire made Christianity its official religion.

### Islam

Islam began in Arabia in the early 600s CE. Muslims believe that God (Allah) revealed the **Quran** to Muhammad, whom they regard as the final prophet. Muslims follow the **Five Pillars**: the declaration of faith (*shahada*), prayer five times a day (*salat*), giving to the poor (*zakat*), fasting in the month of Ramadan (*sawm*), and pilgrimage to Mecca (*hajj*). Islam is divided into two main branches, **Sunni** and **Shia**. The first Muslim community moved from Mecca to Medina in 622 CE (the Hijra), the starting point of the Islamic calendar. Within a century, Muslim-ruled states stretched from Spain to Central Asia. Historians explain the spread through conquest, trade, and gradual conversion over centuries, and under Muslim rule many Christians, Jews and others continued to live as minorities.

### Other major traditions and empires

Judaism, whose sacred texts include the Torah, teaches belief in one God and is among the oldest of the major religions. Hinduism, which developed in South Asia, includes many traditions and sacred texts such as the Vedas. Empires helped spread ideas: the Persian, Maurya, Han, Roman and later Islamic caliphates built roads, ports and cities that moved goods and beliefs.

@example
title: Rewriting a sentence neutrally
problem: Rewrite "Jesus really rose from the dead, so Christianity is the true religion" so it is neutral and descriptive.
step: Spot the problem: the sentence states a religious belief as proven fact and makes a claim about which religion is "true."
step: Attribute the belief to the believers: "Christians believe that Jesus rose from the dead."
step: Keep the focus on history: add what can be described, such as "This belief is central to Christian teaching and worship."
step: Final: "Christians believe that Jesus rose from the dead, and this belief is central to Christian teaching and worship."

@mistakes
- Writing beliefs as flat facts ("Allah gave the Quran...") instead of attributing them ("Muslims believe...").
- Treating a religion as one single group when it has major branches, such as Sunni and Shia.
- Saying a religion spread only by war; trade, travel, teaching and long-term conversion mattered too.
- Judging one religion's beliefs as better or worse than another's.
