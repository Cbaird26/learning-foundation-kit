objective: Explain that slavery was the central cause of the Civil War and how it ended.

@article
After 1783, the United States grew. In **1803**, it bought a huge area of land called the **Louisiana Purchase** from France. Many Native nations already lived on that land. Over the next years, settlers moved west. Many Native nations were forced to leave their homes. In 1830, a law called the Indian Removal Act led to the forced removal of the Cherokee and other Southeastern nations from their homelands. The Cherokee were forced to leave in 1838 and 1839, and thousands died on the way. This is often called the **Trail of Tears**. These nations still exist today.

### Immigrants

**Immigrants** are people who move to a new country. Many came from Europe and Asia, and they worked on farms, in factories and on railroads.

### The Civil War

In the 1800s, many people in the North worked to end slavery, and enslaved people resisted and sought freedom in many ways. Many Southern leaders wanted to keep slavery. When some Southern states left the country, the **Civil War** began in **1861**. It ended in **1865**. On January 1, 1863, the **Emancipation Proclamation** declared people enslaved in areas fighting against the Union to be free. It did not cover the border states that stayed in the Union. The **13th Amendment**, approved in December 1865, ended slavery across the United States.

### Civil rights

**Civil rights** are the rights all people should have, such as the right to vote and to be treated fairly. After the war, many Black Americans still faced unfair laws. In the 1950s and 1960s, many people marched, spoke up and worked peacefully to change them. The **Civil Rights Act of 1964** made unfair treatment because of race illegal in public places and jobs. The **Voting Rights Act of 1965** protected the right to vote. The work for fairness continues today.

@mistakes
- Saying the Civil War was only about land or only about states' choices. Slavery was the central cause.
- Thinking civil rights were won by one law or one day. Many people worked for many years.
- Leaving out Native nations when talking about the country growing.
