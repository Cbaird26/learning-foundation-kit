@article
The **action potential** is a rapid, stereotyped, regenerative change in membrane potential that carries information along axons and triggers contraction in muscle. It depends on **voltage-gated Na$^+$ channels** and **voltage-gated K$^+$ channels**, which open and close in a time-dependent way.

### Phases of the neuronal action potential

1. **Resting** ($\approx -70$ mV): most Na$^+$ channels are closed but available; K$^+$ leak dominates.
2. **Depolarization to threshold** ($\approx -55$ mV): synaptic or stimulus current opens enough Na$^+$ channels that inward current exceeds outward K$^+$ current.
3. **Upstroke:** positive feedback. Depolarization opens more Na$^+$ channels (activation gate), Na$^+$ rushes in, and $V_m$ moves toward $E_{Na}$ ($+66$ mV), peaking at about $+30$ to $+40$ mV.
4. **Repolarization:** the Na$^+$ channel **inactivation gate** closes (milliseconds after opening) and delayed-rectifier K$^+$ channels open, so K$^+$ efflux returns $V_m$ toward $E_K$.
5. **Afterhyperpolarization:** K$^+$ channels close slowly, so $V_m$ transiently approaches $E_K$ ($-90$ mV) before returning to rest.

The net Na$^+$ and K$^+$ moved in a single spike is minute compared with the stored gradients; the pump restores them over time.

### Threshold, all-or-none and refractoriness

Below threshold, the response is graded and decays; at or above threshold, the spike has the same amplitude regardless of stimulus strength (**all-or-none**). Stimulus intensity is encoded by firing **frequency** and the number of fibers recruited.

- **Absolute refractory period:** while Na$^+$ channels are open or inactivated, no stimulus can trigger another spike. It sets the **maximum firing frequency** ($f_{max} \approx 1/\text{ARP}$) and makes conduction one-directional.
- **Relative refractory period:** some Na$^+$ channels have recovered and K$^+$ conductance is still high, so a stronger-than-normal stimulus is required.

### Propagation

Local currents from an active region depolarize the adjacent membrane to threshold. Velocity increases with **axon diameter** (lower internal resistance, larger length constant $\lambda = \sqrt{r_m/r_i}$) and with **myelination**. In myelinated axons, Na$^+$ channels cluster at the **nodes of Ranvier**, and the impulse jumps from node to node (**saltatory conduction**), which is faster and uses less energy.

| Fiber | Myelin | Speed | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| A$\alpha$ | heavy | 70-120 m/s | proprioception, alpha motor neurons |
| A$\beta$ | heavy | 30-70 m/s | touch, pressure |
| A$\delta$ | thin | 5-30 m/s | fast pain, cold |
| B | thin | 3-15 m/s | preganglionic autonomic |
| C | none | 0.5-2 m/s | slow pain, postganglionic autonomic |

**Conduction velocity** between two stimulation sites is $v = \Delta x/\Delta t$, the basis of nerve conduction studies.

### Local anesthetics

Lidocaine and related drugs are weak bases that cross the membrane in the **uncharged** form, then bind in the **charged** form to the inner pore of the voltage-gated Na$^+$ channel, preventing the upstroke. Block is **use-dependent**: channels that open and inactivate often are bound more readily. The fraction uncharged at a given pH follows Henderson-Hasselbalch:

$$f_{\text{uncharged}} = \frac{1}{1 + 10^{\,pK_a - pH}}.$$

Lidocaine has a pKa near 7.9, so only about a quarter is uncharged at pH 7.4, and even less in acidic infected tissue, which is why block there is less effective. Small-diameter fibers (myelinated B and A$\delta$, and unmyelinated C) are blocked first; clinically the order is usually sympathetic tone, then temperature and pain, then touch, then motor function and proprioception.

### Demyelination

In multiple sclerosis or Guillain-Barre syndrome, loss of myelin increases capacitance and current leak, slowing conduction or blocking it. Symptoms can worsen with heat (Uhthoff phenomenon), because higher temperature further shortens channel open times. Tetrodotoxin blocks voltage-gated Na$^+$ channels from the outside; tetraethylammonium blocks K$^+$ channels.

@example
title: Nerve conduction velocity
problem: In a nerve conduction study, the median nerve is stimulated at the wrist and at the elbow, $240$ mm apart. The recorded latencies are $3.0$ ms (wrist) and $7.8$ ms (elbow). Calculate the conduction velocity of the motor fibers between the sites.
step: Subtract the latencies to isolate the travel time between sites (this removes the distal junction and muscle delay): $7.8 - 3.0 = 4.8$ ms.
step: Convert units: $240$ mm $= 0.240$ m and $4.8$ ms $= 0.0048$ s.
step: $v = 0.240/0.0048 = 50$ m/s, normal for a large myelinated motor fiber.
step: A velocity near 20 m/s would suggest demyelination.

@example
title: Fraction of lidocaine in the uncharged form
problem: Lidocaine has $pK_a = 7.9$. Calculate the uncharged fraction at pH $7.4$.
step: $pK_a - pH = 0.5$, so $10^{0.5} = 3.16$.
step: $f = 1/(1 + 3.16) = 0.24$.
step: Only about 24% can cross the membrane at the physiologic pH, and the fraction falls in acid tissue.

@mistakes
- Saying the all-or-none principle means every action potential is identical at every site: it applies to a given axon, and amplitude and speed vary with fiber type and conditions.
- Attributing repolarization to the Na$^+$ pump; repolarization is due to Na$^+$ channel inactivation and K$^+$ efflux.
- Forgetting that local anesthetics act mainly in the charged form on the inside of the channel, so they must first cross the membrane in the uncharged form.
- Using the full latency instead of the latency difference between two sites in a conduction velocity calculation.

@quiz mc
prompt: A 28-year-old woman with multiple sclerosis notices blurred vision in her right eye after a hot shower. Which mechanism best explains the temporary worsening?
choice: Heat increases the number of functioning Na$^+$ channels at the nodes.
choice*: In demyelinated axons with marginal safety factor, higher temperature shortens Na$^+$ channel open times so conduction fails.
choice: Heat accelerates the synthesis of myelin.
choice: Heat directly destroys axons in the optic nerve.
hint: Conduction in damaged axons is barely above threshold.
solution: Demyelination lowers the safety factor for conduction. Warming speeds channel kinetics and shortens the depolarizing current, so impulses fail in marginal fibers. The effect is reversible when the temperature falls (Uhthoff phenomenon).
