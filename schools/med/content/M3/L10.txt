@article
A **hormone** is a chemical messenger released into blood by an endocrine cell and acting on distant target cells that express its receptor. Specificity comes from the receptor, not the hormone. **Paracrine** signals act on neighbors; **autocrine** signals act on the secreting cell.

### Chemical classes

| Class | Examples | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Peptide and protein | insulin, glucagon, ACTH, GH, PTH, ADH, oxytocin | stored in vesicles, water-soluble, short half-life, membrane receptors |
| Amine, catecholamines | epinephrine, NE, dopamine | water-soluble, membrane receptors |
| Steroid | cortisol, aldosterone, sex steroids, vitamin D | lipophilic, made on demand from cholesterol, carried bound to proteins, intracellular receptors |
| Thyroid hormone | T$_3$, T$_4$ | amino-acid derived but lipophilic: nuclear receptors, long half-life |

### Receptor signaling

- **Gs** $\to$ adenylyl cyclase $\to$ cAMP $\to$ PKA: ACTH, TSH, FSH, LH, glucagon, PTH, calcitonin, ADH at V$_2$, CRH, GHRH, $\beta_1$, $\beta_2$.
- **Gq** $\to$ phospholipase C $\to$ IP$_3$ (Ca$^{2+}\uparrow$) and DAG (PKC): GnRH, TRH, oxytocin, ADH at V$_1$, angiotensin II, $\alpha_1$, M$_3$.
- **Gi** $\to$ cAMP$\downarrow$: somatostatin, $\alpha_2$, M$_2$, D$_2$.
- **Receptor tyrosine kinase:** insulin and IGF-1 (insulin receptor: tyrosine kinase, PI3K-Akt and MAP kinase pathways).
- **Cytokine-type receptors with JAK-STAT:** growth hormone, prolactin, erythropoietin, many cytokines.
- **Guanylyl cyclase:** ANP and BNP (membrane receptor); nitric oxide (soluble enzyme).
- **Nuclear receptors:** cortisol, aldosterone, sex steroids, thyroid hormone, vitamin D, retinoids. They change gene transcription, so the response takes hours.

### Control of secretion

Most axes run by **negative feedback**: hypothalamic releasing hormone $\to$ anterior pituitary tropic hormone $\to$ target gland hormone, which inhibits both higher levels (long-loop feedback). Reading the pattern is a key diagnostic skill: in primary hypothyroidism TSH is high and free T$_4$ low; in central (pituitary) hypothyroidism TSH is low or inappropriately normal with low free T$_4$.

### Bound versus free hormone

Steroid and thyroid hormones circulate largely bound to carriers (for example thyroxine-binding globulin, cortisol-binding globulin). Only the **free** fraction is active and regulated by feedback. Estrogen (pregnancy, oral contraceptives) raises binding globulin, increasing **total** T$_4$ with normal **free** T$_4$ and normal TSH.

### Receptor regulation

Prolonged exposure to a ligand causes **desensitization** and **downregulation** (receptor phosphorylation, internalization), while deficiency can cause **upregulation**. Pulsatile GnRH stimulates gonadotropins but continuous GnRH (or agonists such as leuprolide) suppresses them.

### Quantitative ideas

**Receptor occupancy** for a simple binding model: $\text{fraction bound} = \dfrac{[H]}{[H] + K_d}$. At $[H] = K_d$ half the receptors are occupied.

**Metabolic clearance rate (MCR)** is the volume of plasma cleared of hormone per unit time. At steady state,

$$\text{production (or infusion) rate} = \text{MCR} \times \text{plasma concentration}.$$

**Half-life** $t_{1/2}$ is the time for concentration to halve, so after $n$ half-lives, $C = C_0/2^n$.

@example
title: Clearance from a steady infusion
problem: A hormone is infused at $12\ \mu\text{g/h}$ and reaches a steady plasma concentration of $0.6\ \mu\text{g/L}$. Calculate its metabolic clearance rate.
step: At steady state, infusion rate equals clearance times concentration.
step: $\text{MCR} = 12/0.6 = 20$ L/h.
step: In other words, $20$ L of plasma are cleared of the hormone each hour, which is far more than total plasma volume, so clearance involves rapid, repeated passage through the liver and kidneys.

@mistakes
- Assuming all hormones act on membrane receptors: steroids and thyroid hormone use nuclear receptors and act slowly.
- Interpreting total T$_4$ without considering binding proteins; free T$_4$ is the regulated variable.
- Forgetting that continuous GnRH stimulation suppresses the gonadotropins while pulsatile GnRH stimulates them.
