## Week-by-week key ideas

1. **Homeostasis:** sensor, integrator, effector; negative feedback opposes change; gain $=$ correction/remaining error; positive feedback amplifies until a terminating event; fever raises the set point.
2. **Fluid compartments:** TBW 60% of body weight (men); ICF 2/3 of TBW; ECF 1/3, of which plasma is 1/4; indicator dilution $V=\text{amount}/\text{concentration}$; osmolarity $\approx 2\text{Na}+\text{glucose}/18+\text{BUN}/2.8$. Isotonic saline expands the ECF only; D5W is free water and distributes across TBW.
3. **Transport:** Fick's law; facilitated diffusion saturates; $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$-ATPase (3 out, 2 in); secondary active transport; $\pi = iCRT$; tonicity depends on effective osmoles.
4. **Resting potential:** Nernst $E = (61.5/z)\log([X]_o/[X]_i)$; Goldman weights ions by permeability; hyperkalemia depolarizes, then inactivates Na$^+$ channels.
5. **Action potential:** Na$^+$ activation, then inactivation and K$^+$ efflux; refractory periods; saltatory conduction; local anesthetics block Na$^+$ channels, with uncharged fraction $1/(1+10^{pK_a-pH})$.
6. **Synapse and NMJ:** Ca$^{2+}$, SNAREs, ACh, nicotinic receptor; myasthenia (postsynaptic, decrement) versus Lambert-Eaton (presynaptic, increment); botulism, organophosphates, blockers.
7. **Skeletal muscle:** sarcomere, cross-bridge cycle, DHPR-RyR1, troponin C, SERCA, length-tension, motor units, malignant hyperthermia.
8. **Smooth and cardiac muscle:** calmodulin-MLCK versus troponin C; CICR; long cardiac plateau; ejection fraction, cardiac output, Laplace.
9. **ANS:** two-neuron chain; receptors and G proteins; baroreflex; $R\propto 1/r^4$.
10. **Hormones:** membrane versus nuclear receptors; Gs, Gq, tyrosine kinase, JAK-STAT; occupancy, clearance, half-life; free versus bound hormone.
11. **Thermoregulation:** fever versus hyperthermia; RQ; $4.8$ kcal/L O$_2$; $\Delta T = Q/(0.83m)$.
12. **Exercise:** Fick $\dot V_{O_2}=\text{CO}\times(a\text{-}v)$; stroke volume, metabolite vasodilation; ventilation matches $\dot V_{CO_2}$.

## Common errors

- Equating osmolarity with tonicity, or treating D5W as isotonic in effect.
- Losing the sign of $z$ in the Nernst equation, or thinking hyperkalemia hyperpolarizes.
- Using the full latency instead of the latency difference for conduction velocity.
- Mixing units in Fick, clearance or Poiseuille calculations.

@exam num
prompt: A 70 kg man has total body water of $42$ L and a plasma sodium of $140$ mEq/L. He drinks and retains $2$ L of pure water, and it distributes through all of his body water. Assuming no sodium or water is excreted, what is his new plasma sodium concentration in mEq/L, to one decimal place?
answer: 133.6
check: 140 * 42 / (42 + 2)
tol: 0.2
unit: mEq/L
hint: The total amount of sodium does not change; the volume of water in which it is distributed does.
hint: Plasma sodium concentration scales inversely with the volume of body water it is distributed in: $140 \times 42 / 44$.
solution: The effective solute is conserved and the added water equilibrates across all body water, so plasma $[\text{Na}^+]$ falls in proportion to the rise in TBW: $140 \times 42/(42+2) = 5{,}880/44 = 133.6$ mEq/L. (The $5{,}880$ is plasma $[\text{Na}^+]$ times TBW, a simplified index of effective solute, not the actual body sodium content, which is extracellular.)

prompt: A patient's serum potassium rises to $7.0$ mM. Using the Goldman equation with $[K]_i = 140$, $[Na]_o = 145$, $[Na]_i = 12$ mM, $P_{Na}/P_K = 0.04$ and $61.5\log_{10}$, calculate the resting membrane potential in mV (chloride ignored).
answer: -64
check: 61.5 * math.log10((7.0 + 0.04 * 145) / (140 + 0.04 * 12))
tol: 0.3
unit: mV
hint: Weight the sodium terms by $0.04$.
hint: Numerator $7 + 5.8 = 12.8$; denominator $140 + 0.48 = 140.48$.
solution: $V_m = 61.5\log(12.8/140.48) = 61.5 \times (-1.040) \approx -64$ mV, about 7 mV less negative than at a potassium of 4 mM ($-71$ mV).

@exam mc
prompt: A 31-year-old man with extensive burns sustained 12 days ago is intubated for surgery. Shortly after succinylcholine is given, his ECG shows peaked T waves, a widening QRS and then ventricular fibrillation. Which mechanism best explains this event?
choice: Succinylcholine blocks the Na$^+$/K$^+$-ATPase in cardiac muscle.
choice: Succinylcholine directly activates cardiac L-type calcium channels.
choice*: Upregulated extrajunctional nicotinic receptors are depolarized by succinylcholine, causing massive K$^+$ efflux and hyperkalemia.
choice: Acetylcholinesterase inhibition by succinylcholine produces a cholinergic crisis.
hint: Think of the receptors on denervated or burned muscle.
solution: After burns or denervation, muscle expresses extrajunctional (immature) nicotinic receptors across the sarcolemma. Succinylcholine depolarizes them all, releasing large amounts of K$^+$. The resulting hyperkalemia depolarizes the myocardial membrane (less negative $E_K$), producing peaked T waves, a wide QRS and arrhythmia.
