@article
The **autonomic nervous system (ANS)** controls smooth muscle, cardiac muscle and glands, using a two-neuron chain: a **preganglionic** neuron from the CNS synapses in a ganglion with a **postganglionic** neuron that innervates the target.

### Anatomy and transmitters

| Feature | Sympathetic | Parasympathetic |
|---|---|---|
| Outflow | thoracolumbar (T1-L2) | craniosacral (CN III, VII, IX, X; S2-S4) |
| Preganglionic fiber | short | long |
| Ganglia | near spinal cord (chain) | near or in target |
| Preganglionic transmitter | ACh (nicotinic N$_N$) | ACh (nicotinic N$_N$) |
| Postganglionic transmitter | norepinephrine (NE) | ACh (muscarinic) |

Exceptions: sympathetic postganglionic fibers to **sweat glands** release ACh (acting on muscarinic receptors); the **adrenal medulla** is innervated by preganglionic fibers and releases epinephrine (about 80%) and NE into blood; renal vasculature has dopamine D$_1$ receptors.

### Catecholamine synthesis and termination

Tyrosine $\to$ DOPA (**tyrosine hydroxylase**, rate-limiting) $\to$ dopamine $\to$ NE (dopamine $\beta$-hydroxylase) $\to$ epinephrine (PNMT, in the adrenal medulla, induced by cortisol). NE's action ends mostly by **reuptake** (NET, blocked by cocaine and tricyclics) and is degraded by **MAO** and **COMT**. Acetylcholine is hydrolyzed by acetylcholinesterase.

### Receptors

| Receptor | G protein | Key effects |
|---|---|---|
| $\alpha_1$ | Gq (IP$_3$/DAG, Ca$^{2+}\uparrow$) | vasoconstriction, mydriasis, sphincter contraction (bladder neck, GI), ejaculation |
| $\alpha_2$ | Gi (cAMP$\downarrow$) | decreased NE release (presynaptic), decreased insulin |
| $\beta_1$ | Gs (cAMP$\uparrow$) | heart rate, contractility, conduction velocity, renin release |
| $\beta_2$ | Gs | bronchodilation, skeletal-muscle vasodilation, uterine relaxation, glycogenolysis |
| $\beta_3$ | Gs | lipolysis, bladder detrusor relaxation |
| M$_1$, M$_3$ | Gq | M$_3$: pupil constriction (miosis), accommodation, bronchoconstriction, salivation and secretions, increased GI motility, bladder contraction, vasodilation via NO |
| M$_2$ | Gi | decreased heart rate and AV conduction |

### Predicting organ responses

**Sympathetic ("fight or flight"):** pupil dilation, increased heart rate and contractility, bronchodilation, vasoconstriction in skin and gut with vasodilation in skeletal muscle ($\beta_2$), inhibited digestion, glycogenolysis, lipolysis, renin release, relaxed detrusor with contracted internal sphincter, sweating.

**Parasympathetic ("rest and digest"):** miosis, decreased heart rate, bronchoconstriction and secretions, increased salivation and GI motility, bladder contraction, erection (NO-mediated vasodilation).

Most organs have dual innervation with opposing effects, but vessels and sweat glands are mostly sympathetic only, so vascular tone is controlled by changes in sympathetic firing.

### Reflexes and tone

The **baroreceptor reflex**: rising pressure stretches the carotid sinus (CN IX) and aortic arch (CN X); the nucleus tractus solitarius inhibits sympathetic outflow and increases vagal tone, lowering heart rate and resistance. Resting heart rate is dominated by **vagal tone**: the intrinsic sinus rate is higher (about 100/min), and atropine raises resting rate in a healthy person.

### Clinical pearls

Horner syndrome (loss of sympathetic supply to the face): ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis. **Anticholinergic toxicity:** "hot as a hare, dry as a bone, red as a beet, blind as a bat, mad as a hatter": fever, dry skin, flushing, mydriasis, delirium, tachycardia, urinary retention, ileus. Phenylephrine ($\alpha_1$ agonist) raises blood pressure and causes reflex bradycardia.

### Resistance and flow

Vessel resistance varies with the inverse fourth power of radius (Poiseuille): $R \propto 1/r^4$. Small changes in arteriolar radius from $\alpha_1$-mediated constriction therefore produce large changes in resistance and pressure.

@example
title: Predicting the response to phenylephrine
problem: A 45-year-old man under anesthesia is given an intravenous alpha$_1$ agonist (phenylephrine). Predict the changes in blood pressure and heart rate.
step: Alpha$_1$ receptors on arterioles (Gq) cause vasoconstriction, so total peripheral resistance and mean arterial pressure rise.
step: The baroreceptors sense the rise in pressure and increase vagal output via CN X and decrease sympathetic firing.
step: Phenylephrine has no direct beta$_1$ action, so there is no direct cardiac stimulation to oppose the reflex, and heart rate falls (reflex bradycardia).
step: Result: blood pressure up, heart rate down.

@example
title: Resistance after vasoconstriction
problem: An alpha$_1$ stimulus narrows an arteriole's radius by $20\%$. By what factor does its resistance change, assuming laminar flow?
step: New radius $= 0.8\,r$.
step: Resistance $\propto 1/r^4$, so the new resistance is $1/0.8^4$ times the old one.
step: $0.8^4 = 0.4096$, so the factor is $1/0.4096 = 2.44$.
step: A 20% narrowing more than doubles resistance.

@mistakes
- Saying the postganglionic sympathetic neuron always releases NE: sweat glands use ACh with muscarinic receptors.
- Forgetting the reflex: a pure vasoconstrictor lowers heart rate by baroreflex, while a mixed agonist may not.
- Mixing up receptors: $\beta_1$ is on the heart and $\beta_2$ on bronchi and skeletal-muscle vessels.

@quiz mc
