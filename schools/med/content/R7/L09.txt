@article
The emergency airway is a controlled procedure with a plan for failure. The goal is **oxygenation**, not intubation: if oxygenation is adequate and the cause is reversible, a noninvasive route may avoid the risks of a tube. Doses are typical adult doses for study.

### Is intubation needed?

Indications: failure to oxygenate or ventilate, failure to protect the airway (GCS of 8 or below with no gag), and anticipated clinical course (airway burns, expanding neck hematoma, worsening angioedema). **Noninvasive options:** high-flow nasal cannula, and BiPAP, which reduces intubation and mortality in hypercapnic COPD exacerbation and cardiogenic pulmonary edema. Contraindications to NIV include vomiting, severe encephalopathy and inability to protect the airway.

### Predicting difficulty

**LEMON:** Look externally, Evaluate 3-3-2 (mouth opening, mandible length, thyroid to floor of mouth), Mallampati score, Obstruction, Neck mobility. Predictors of difficult bag-mask ventilation: **MOANS** (Mask seal, Obstruction or obesity, Age over 55, No teeth, Stiff lungs). Use video laryngoscopy as the first-line device when available, with a bougie.

### Rapid sequence intubation (the seven Ps)

1. **Preparation:** equipment, suction, backup devices, drugs, plan A to D, monitoring.
2. **Preoxygenation:** 3 minutes of 100% oxygen by non-rebreather or BVM with PEEP valve, head of bed elevated, plus **apneic oxygenation** (nasal cannula at high flow during laryngoscopy).
3. **Pretreatment:** resuscitate first; give fluids or start a vasopressor in hypotensive patients, as induction agents and positive pressure ventilation lower blood pressure.
4. **Paralysis with induction:** an induction agent followed immediately by a paralytic.
   - Induction: **etomidate** 0.3 mg/kg (hemodynamically stable, brief adrenal suppression), **ketamine** 1 to 2 mg/kg (preserves blood pressure, bronchodilator; good in asthma and shock, at reduced dose in critical illness), **propofol** 1 to 2 mg/kg (hypotension).
   - Paralysis: **succinylcholine** 1 to 1.5 mg/kg (depolarizing, onset 45 to 60 seconds, duration under 10 minutes) or **rocuronium** 1 to 1.2 mg/kg (nondepolarizing, longer duration; reversed by sugammadex). Contraindications to succinylcholine: hyperkalemia, burns or crush injury beyond the first day or so, denervation or neuromuscular disease, history of malignant hyperthermia.
5. **Protection and positioning:** sniffing position; avoid cricoid pressure that obscures the view.
6. **Placement with proof:** direct laryngoscopy or video, then confirm with **waveform capnography** (the standard), chest rise, misting and auscultation; chest radiograph afterwards for depth.
7. **Post-intubation management:** sedation and analgesia, ventilator settings, orogastric tube, hemodynamics.

### The failed airway

If intubation fails, oxygenate between attempts with bag-mask or a supraglottic airway. In a **cannot intubate, cannot oxygenate** situation, perform a surgical airway (cricothyrotomy) immediately: scalpel, bougie, tube.

### Ventilator basics

Common modes are assist-control (volume or pressure) and pressure support. Parameters: tidal volume ($V_T$), respiratory rate, PEEP, $F_iO_2$, inspiratory flow. Initial settings: $V_T$ 6 to 8 mL/kg of **predicted body weight**, rate 12 to 20/min, PEEP 5 cm H$_2$O, $F_iO_2$ titrated for SpO$_2$ 92 to 96% (88 to 92% in chronic hypercapnic COPD).

$$\text{PBW (male)} = 50 + 2.3\,(\text{height in inches} - 60),\qquad \text{PBW (female)} = 45.5 + 2.3\,(\text{height in inches} - 60).$$
$$\text{Minute ventilation} = \text{RR}\times V_T,\qquad \text{Static compliance} = \frac{V_T}{P_{plat}-\text{PEEP}},\qquad \text{Driving pressure} = P_{plat} - \text{PEEP}.$$

### ARDS and lung-protective ventilation

The Berlin definition: onset within 1 week of an insult, bilateral opacities, respiratory failure not explained by cardiac failure or fluid overload, and a $PaO_2/F_iO_2$ ratio at or below 300 on PEEP of at least 5 cm H$_2$O; severity is mild (200 to 300), moderate (100 to 200), and severe (at or below 100). Lung-protective ventilation: **$V_T$ of 6 mL/kg PBW, plateau pressure at or below 30 cm H$_2$O**, higher PEEP in moderate to severe disease, conservative fluids, and **prone positioning for at least 12 hours per day** in severe ARDS ($PaO_2/F_iO_2$ below 150). Permissive hypercapnia is accepted.

### Troubleshooting

When a ventilated patient deteriorates, disconnect from the ventilator and bag by hand, and use **DOPE**: Displacement of the tube, Obstruction (mucus, kink, biting), Pneumothorax, Equipment failure. **High peak pressure with normal plateau pressure** indicates increased airway resistance (bronchospasm, secretions, kinked tube); **both elevated** indicates reduced compliance (pneumothorax, pulmonary edema, abdominal distension, endobronchial intubation).

In severe **asthma or COPD**, use a low rate, small tidal volume and a long expiratory time to avoid **auto-PEEP** (air trapping). Hypotension and rising pressures after intubation of a patient with asthma suggests dynamic hyperinflation: disconnect the circuit to allow exhalation and treat bronchospasm.

@example
title: Lung-protective tidal volume
problem: A man with ARDS is 72 inches tall. Find his predicted body weight and a 6 mL/kg tidal volume.
step: Male PBW = 50 + 2.3 x (72 - 60) = 50 + 27.6 = 77.6 kg.
step: Tidal volume at 6 mL/kg = 6 x 77.6 = 466 mL; round to about 470 mL.
step: Use predicted body weight, not actual weight, because lung size tracks height and sex rather than body fat.
step: Check plateau pressure; if it is above 30 cm H$_2$O, lower the tidal volume toward 4 mL/kg.

@mistakes
- Intubating a patient in shock without first resuscitating or reducing induction doses, causing peri-intubation arrest.
- Using succinylcholine in a patient with hyperkalemia or a burn or crush injury several days old.
- Using actual instead of predicted body weight to set tidal volume.
- Missing auto-PEEP in an intubated asthmatic who becomes hypotensive.
