@article
The first question for chest pain or dyspnea is not "what is the most likely cause?" but "which dangerous causes have I excluded?" Pain from acute coronary syndrome, pulmonary embolism (PE), aortic dissection, tension pneumothorax, tamponade and esophageal rupture must be considered in every adult.

### Acute coronary syndrome (ACS)

Obtain a 12-lead ECG within **10 minutes** of arrival. Plaque rupture with thrombus gives unstable angina, NSTEMI (troponin rise without persistent ST elevation) or STEMI (complete occlusion).

**STEMI criteria:** new ST elevation at the J point in at least two contiguous leads of at least 1 mm (0.1 mV) in all leads except V2-V3, where thresholds are at least 2 mm in men aged 40 or over, 2.5 mm in men under 40, and 1.5 mm in women. New left bundle branch block with a suggestive picture, posterior MI (ST depression V1-V3 with tall R waves, confirmed with posterior leads) and Sgarbossa criteria matter in special cases. Inferior STEMI (II, III, aVF) warrants **right-sided leads**; right ventricular infarction causes hypotension with clear lungs and elevated neck veins, and is **preload dependent**, so avoid nitroglycerin and give fluids.

Initial therapy: aspirin (typical adult dose 162 to 325 mg chewed), a P2Y12 inhibitor and anticoagulation per local protocol, oxygen only if SpO$_2$ is below about 90%, nitroglycerin for ongoing pain (contraindicated with hypotension, RV infarction or recent phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor use) and opioids sparingly. Reperfusion: **primary PCI with first-medical-contact-to-device time of 90 minutes or less** (120 minutes if transfer); if PCI cannot be achieved within 120 minutes, give fibrinolysis, with door-to-needle goal of 30 minutes. Troponin is serially measured (high-sensitivity assays at 0 and 1 to 3 hours) for non-ST-elevation presentations. The **HEART score** (History, ECG, Age, Risk factors, Troponin; each scored 0 to 2) of 0 to 3 identifies low risk for short-term major adverse cardiac events.

### Pulmonary embolism

Clinical presentation: dyspnea, pleuritic pain, tachycardia, hypoxia, unilateral leg swelling. Estimate pretest probability with the **Wells score**: clinical signs of DVT (3), PE the most likely diagnosis (3), heart rate above 100 (1.5), immobilization at least 3 days or surgery in the past 4 weeks (1.5), prior DVT/PE (1.5), hemoptysis (1), malignancy (1). Two-level interpretation: 4 or less "PE unlikely", above 4 "likely". In low-risk patients meeting all **PERC** criteria (age under 50, HR under 100, SpO$_2$ at least 95%, no hemoptysis, no estrogen use, no prior VTE, no unilateral leg swelling, no recent surgery or trauma), no testing is needed. For "unlikely" patients, a negative D-dimer excludes PE (use an age-adjusted cutoff of age $\times$ 10 ng/mL for patients over 50); for "likely" patients proceed straight to CT pulmonary angiography (or V/Q scan if contrast is contraindicated). Treat with anticoagulation; massive PE with shock needs systemic thrombolysis (typical alteplase 100 mg over 2 hours) or catheter or surgical embolectomy.

### Aortic dissection

Abrupt tearing pain radiating to the back, a pulse or blood-pressure differential greater than 20 mm Hg between arms, new aortic regurgitation murmur, widened mediastinum on chest radiograph. Diagnosis: CT angiography of chest, abdomen and pelvis (or transesophageal echo if unstable). Treat pain and **reduce shear stress** with IV beta-blocker (esmolol) targeting heart rate about 60 and systolic pressure 100 to 120 mm Hg; add a vasodilator only after beta-blockade. **Type A** (ascending aorta) needs emergency surgery; uncomplicated **type B** is managed medically. Avoid anticoagulants and thrombolytics.

### Pneumothorax and tamponade

**Tension pneumothorax** is a clinical diagnosis: respiratory distress, hypotension, distended neck veins, absent breath sounds on one side, tracheal deviation away. Do not wait for imaging: decompress with a large needle (in adults, the fourth or fifth intercostal space anterior to the midaxillary line per ATLS 10; the second intercostal space at the midclavicular line remains an option and is used in children) or finger thoracostomy, then place a chest tube. **Tamponade** gives Beck triad, pulsus paradoxus and low-voltage or alternating-amplitude ECG; confirm with echocardiography and drain by pericardiocentesis or surgery.

### Acute dyspnea

Examine for wheeze, crackles, focal findings and edema. Useful tests: pulse oximetry, ECG, chest radiograph, point-of-care ultrasound, BNP (above about 400 pg/mL favors heart failure; below 100 argues against), troponin, blood gas. Acute heart failure: sit upright, noninvasive ventilation, IV loop diuretic and nitroglycerin if hypertensive. Asthma and COPD: inhaled albuterol with ipratropium, systemic corticosteroids, magnesium in severe asthma, noninvasive ventilation for hypercapnic COPD. Pneumonia: antibiotics and oxygen.

### The A-a gradient

$$\text{PAO}_2 = F_iO_2\,(P_{atm}-47) - \frac{\text{PaCO}_2}{0.8},\qquad \text{A-a} = \text{PAO}_2-\text{PaO}_2.$$
At sea level on room air, $F_iO_2=0.21$ and $P_{atm}=760$. Normal A-a is about (age/4) + 4 mm Hg. A normal gradient with hypoxemia suggests hypoventilation; a wide gradient suggests V/Q mismatch, shunt or diffusion limitation.

@example
title: Risk stratifying a possible PE
problem: A 52-year-old woman 2 weeks after knee replacement has heart rate 112/min, SpO$_2$ 93% and a swollen left calf. She thinks she has pneumonia but no alternative diagnosis is more likely than PE on your assessment. Calculate the Wells score and choose the test.
step: Add points: clinical signs of DVT (3) + heart rate over 100 (1.5) + surgery within 4 weeks (1.5) = 6.
step: PE is also the most likely diagnosis (3 more points), so the total is 9. This is above 4, so PE is "likely".
step: In a likely patient D-dimer is not used to rule out; proceed to CT pulmonary angiography (renal function permitting) and start anticoagulation if there is a delay and low bleeding risk.
step: If she were hypotensive with a dilated right ventricle, she would have high-risk PE and need systemic thrombolysis or embolectomy.

@mistakes
- Giving nitroglycerin to a patient with inferior STEMI and hypotension without checking for right ventricular infarction.
- Ordering D-dimer in a high-pretest-probability patient and being reassured by a "normal" result.
- Needing a chest radiograph to confirm tension pneumothorax before decompressing.
- Anticoagulating before excluding aortic dissection in a patient with tearing pain.
