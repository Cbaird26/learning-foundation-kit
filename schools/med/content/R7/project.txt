Classify the type of shock from these data, calculate her MAP and the 30 mL/kg fluid volume, state the timing of antibiotics, name the first-line vasopressor and when to add a second, and write what you would do if her blood pressure remains low.

@review
### Week by week

**Week 1: the undifferentiated patient.** Treat the primary survey (airway, breathing, circulation, disability with glucose, exposure) in order. Calculate GCS, MAP = (SBP + 2 DBP)/3 and shock index = HR/SBP. Build a can't-miss differential, and update probability with likelihood ratios.

**Week 2: resuscitation.** Compressions at 100 to 120/min, at least 2 inches deep, 30:2 or one breath every 6 seconds with an advanced airway. VF and pulseless VT: shock, then epinephrine 1 mg every 3 to 5 minutes and amiodarone 300 mg then 150 mg. PEA and asystole: no shock, epinephrine early, look for the Hs and Ts. Post-arrest: ECG, avoid hypotension and hyperoxia, temperature control.

**Week 3: shock and sepsis.** Four shock types, neck veins and lungs as clues, SVR formula, 30 mL/kg crystalloid, antibiotics within 1 hour, norepinephrine first, IM epinephrine for anaphylaxis.

**Week 4: chest pain and dyspnea.** STEMI criteria and reperfusion times, right ventricular infarct, Wells score and PERC, D-dimer, aortic dissection and beta-blockade first, tension pneumothorax decompression, A-a gradient.

**Week 5: abdominal pain.** Visceral versus somatic pain; ruptured AAA, mesenteric ischemia, ectopic pregnancy, perforation, torsion, cholangitis, SBP (neutrophils at least 250/mm$^3$).

**Week 6: trauma.** xABCDE, hemorrhage classes, damage control resuscitation, massive hemothorax thresholds, FAST and CT decisions.

**Week 7: poisoning.** Toxidromes and antidotes, gaps (osmolar and anion), NAC, bicarbonate, fomepizole, atropine and pralidoxime.

**Week 8: environmental.** Heat stroke cooling, hypothermia rewarming, Parkland fluids, altitude illness, cyanide and carbon monoxide.

**Week 9: airway and ventilation.** Seven Ps, drug choices, succinylcholine contraindications, predicted body weight, ARDS lung-protective ventilation, DOPE.

**Week 10: critical care.** Vasopressor receptors, choice by shock type, infusion rates, analgesia-first light sedation, monitoring.

**Week 11: neurology.** Stroke windows and lysis criteria, ICH, SAH, status epilepticus sequence, meningitis therapy, CPP.

**Week 12: disposition.** ESI, EMTALA, capacity, documentation, I-PASS.

### Self-check

- I can run a VF arrest and a PEA arrest from memory, with doses.
- I can classify shock from vitals and neck veins.
- I can match each toxidrome to its antidote.
- I can choose a vasopressor for each shock type.
- I can give an I-PASS handoff.

### Common errors

Interrupting compressions, shocking PEA, delaying antibiotics, nitroglycerin in RV infarct, anticoagulating a dissection, imaging before decompressing a tension pneumothorax, using actual instead of predicted body weight, skipping the glucose, and forgetting return precautions.

prompt: A 80-kg man is in septic shock. Calculate (1) the 30 mL/kg crystalloid volume in mL, and (2) the pump rate in mL/h for norepinephrine at 0.15 mcg/kg/min using a bag of 8 mg in 250 mL. Enter the two values in that order.
answer: (2400, 22.5)
check: (30 * 80, 0.15 * 80 * 60 / (8000 / 250))
tol: 0.1
hint: Volume is 30 times the weight.
hint: The concentration is 8000 mcg / 250 mL = 32 mcg/mL.
solution: Fluids 30 x 80 = 2400 mL. Dose 0.15 x 80 = 12 mcg/min = 720 mcg/h; 720/32 = 22.5 mL/h.

@exam mc
prompt: A 40-year-old man with a stab wound to the chest is intubated for respiratory failure. Ten minutes later his blood pressure falls to 70/40 mm Hg, SpO$_2$ drops to 78%, peak airway pressure rises, breath sounds are absent on the right, neck veins are distended and the trachea deviates left. What is the best next step?
choice: Give a 2 L crystalloid bolus and repeat the chest radiograph
choice: Start CPR and give epinephrine
choice*: Perform needle or finger decompression of the right chest, then place a chest tube
choice: Increase PEEP and the tidal volume
hint: Positive pressure ventilation can convert a simple pneumothorax into a tension pneumothorax.
solution: This is tension pneumothorax after positive pressure ventilation. It is treated immediately by decompression and a chest tube, without waiting for imaging. Fluids and higher pressures would worsen the physiology.

@exam self
prompt: A 32-year-old man is found unresponsive with a respiratory rate of 4/min, pinpoint pupils and a pulse of 50/min; he then becomes pulseless with a rhythm that is not shockable. Write the critical actions in order, name the toxidrome and antidote and the reversible causes you will treat, and finish with a short I-PASS handoff to the ICU after return of circulation.
hint: Ventilation is the key intervention in opioid arrest; naloxone is not a substitute for CPR.
solution: Start CPR with ventilation (30:2 or bag-mask), attach the monitor and confirm PEA or asystole (no shock), establish IV or IO access, give epinephrine 1 mg every 3 to 5 minutes and consider naloxone (typical adult dose 0.4 to 2 mg, intranasal 4 mg) while ventilating. The toxidrome is opioid (miosis, respiratory depression); the dominant reversible cause is hypoxia, with the Hs and Ts reviewed (also hypoglycemia, co-ingestants). After ROSC: ECG, avoid hypotension and hyperoxia, glucose, temperature control if comatose, urine toxicology and level checks for acetaminophen and salicylate. I-PASS: illness severity unstable; summary of opioid arrest with PEA, shock-free ROSC time, naloxone and epinephrine given; action list of repeat gases, ECG, ICU bed, observation for recurrent sedation as naloxone wears off; contingency plan for rearrest or recurrent hypoventilation; receiver synthesizes.
rubric: Uses CPR with ventilation, no shock, and early epinephrine.
rubric: Names opioid toxidrome, naloxone, and hypoxia as the dominant cause.
rubric: Describes post-arrest care.
rubric: Gives an I-PASS handoff with contingency.
