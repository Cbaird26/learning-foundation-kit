@article
Neurologic emergencies reward speed: "time is brain." Start with ABCs, a bedside glucose, and a focused examination, and keep the time of last known well front and center. Doses are typical adult doses for study.

### Acute ischemic stroke

Sudden focal deficit (face droop, arm weakness, speech difficulty, visual loss, ataxia). Obtain **bedside glucose** first because hypoglycemia mimics stroke. Order a **noncontrast head CT** immediately to exclude hemorrhage, plus CT angiography of head and neck for large vessel occlusion. Score severity with the NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS).

**IV thrombolysis** (alteplase 0.9 mg/kg to a maximum of 90 mg: 10% as a bolus over 1 minute, the remainder over 60 minutes; tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg to a maximum of 25 mg as a single bolus is an accepted alternative) is given within **4.5 hours of last known well** in eligible patients. Contraindications include intracranial hemorrhage on CT, blood pressure persistently above 185/110 mm Hg despite treatment, glucose below 50 mg/dL, platelets below 100,000/microL, INR above 1.7, recent therapeutic-dose anticoagulant (such as a DOAC within 48 hours), recent major surgery or serious head trauma, and a history of intracranial hemorrhage. Lower blood pressure to below 185/110 before and below 180/105 for 24 hours after lysis. **Endovascular thrombectomy** is indicated for large-vessel occlusion of the anterior circulation within 6 hours, and up to 24 hours for selected patients on perfusion or advanced imaging, regardless of whether thrombolysis was given. Without lysis, permissive hypertension up to 220/120 mm Hg is allowed in the first hours. Give aspirin within 24 to 48 hours if not thrombolysed (after hemorrhage is excluded); dual antiplatelet therapy for minor stroke or high-risk TIA. Do not lower the glucose or blood pressure aggressively.

### Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH)

Hypertension is the commonest cause. CT shows hyperdensity. Management: airway protection, **intensive blood pressure control** (acutely lowering systolic pressure to about 140 mm Hg, avoiding drops below 130), reversal of anticoagulation (vitamin K and prothrombin complex concentrate for warfarin; idarucizumab for dabigatran; four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate for factor Xa inhibitors, since andexanet alfa was withdrawn from the U.S. market in December 2025), neurosurgical consultation for cerebellar hematomas with brainstem compression or hydrocephalus, and ICU care.

### Subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH)

**Thunderclap headache**, peaking within a minute ("worst of my life"), meningismus, and sometimes loss of consciousness. A noncontrast CT head within **6 hours** of onset on a modern scanner and read by a neuroradiologist is nearly 100% sensitive. If CT is negative and onset is beyond 6 hours or the study is questionable, perform **lumbar puncture** for xanthochromia (or CT angiography). Treat by securing the aneurysm (coiling or clipping), controlling blood pressure, giving **nimodipine** (typical adult dose 60 mg orally every 4 hours) to reduce delayed cerebral ischemia from vasospasm, and watching for rebleeding, hydrocephalus and hyponatremia.

### Status epilepticus

A seizure lasting 5 minutes or longer, or recurrent seizures without recovery. Check glucose, protect the airway, give oxygen, obtain IV access.
1. **First line (0 to 5 minutes):** a benzodiazepine: IM midazolam 10 mg (over 40 kg), IV lorazepam 4 mg (0.1 mg/kg), or IV diazepam (0.15 to 0.2 mg/kg, maximum 10 mg).
2. **Second line (5 to 20 minutes):** levetiracetam (60 mg/kg, maximum 4500 mg), fosphenytoin (20 mg phenytoin equivalents/kg, maximum 1500 mg), or valproate (40 mg/kg, maximum 3000 mg); these were comparable in trials.
3. **Third line:** refractory status epilepticus requires intubation and continuous infusion of midazolam or propofol with EEG monitoring.
Treat causes: hypoglycemia (glucose), eclampsia (magnesium sulfate), isoniazid toxicity (pyridoxine), alcohol withdrawal, infection and structural lesions.

### Meningitis

Fever, headache, neck stiffness and altered mentation (the full triad is present in a minority). Give empirical therapy **without waiting for imaging or lumbar puncture**: blood cultures, then **dexamethasone** (typical dose 10 mg IV every 6 hours, started before or with the first antibiotic dose) plus **ceftriaxone and vancomycin**, with **ampicillin** added for age over 50, immunocompromise or pregnancy to cover *Listeria*. Obtain CT before lumbar puncture in patients with immunocompromise, papilledema, focal deficit, new seizure or depressed consciousness. Bacterial CSF shows high neutrophil count, high protein and low glucose. Add acyclovir when herpes encephalitis is possible.

### Raised intracranial pressure

Signs: headache, vomiting, papilledema, declining consciousness, Cushing reflex (hypertension, bradycardia, irregular respiration), blown pupil. Cerebral perfusion pressure:
$$\text{CPP} = \text{MAP} - \text{ICP}.$$
The target CPP is generally 60 to 70 mm Hg. Treatment: elevate the head of bed to about 30 degrees, secure the airway and avoid hypoxia and hypotension, give osmotherapy (mannitol 0.25 to 1 g/kg or hypertonic saline), brief hyperventilation only as a bridge for impending herniation, and neurosurgical evaluation.

### Spinal emergencies

**Cord compression** (back pain, leg weakness, sensory level, urinary retention, often cancer) and **cauda equina syndrome** (saddle anesthesia, retention, bilateral radiculopathy) need urgent MRI and neurosurgical consultation; give dexamethasone for malignant cord compression. In **Guillain-Barre syndrome**, monitor forced vital capacity; intubate for FVC below about 20 mL/kg or inability to protect the airway.

@example
title: Alteplase dose
problem: A 90-kg man with a disabling stroke 2 hours after onset is eligible for thrombolysis. Calculate the alteplase dose.
step: Dose = 0.9 mg/kg x 90 kg = 81 mg, which is below the 90 mg maximum.
step: Bolus is 10% of the total: 8.1 mg over 1 minute.
step: The remainder, 72.9 mg, is infused over 60 minutes.
step: Keep blood pressure below 180/105 for 24 hours, and avoid antithrombotics and invasive procedures during that time.

@mistakes
- Skipping the bedside glucose in a patient with focal deficits.
- Delaying antibiotics in suspected meningitis for CT or lumbar puncture.
- Lowering blood pressure aggressively in ischemic stroke not treated with thrombolysis.
- Treating status epilepticus with an inadequate benzodiazepine dose and moving on too slowly.

@practice self
prompt: An 80-kg adult has been seizing continuously for 8 minutes. Write the sequence of treatment with doses, the checks you make, and the next step if seizures persist.
hint: Time-ordered: benzodiazepine, then second-line agent, then anesthetic infusion.
solution: Protect the airway and give oxygen, check glucose and place an IV. First-line: IV lorazepam 4 mg (repeat once) or IM midazolam 10 mg if no IV. If the seizure continues beyond 5 to 10 minutes, give a second-line agent: levetiracetam 60 mg/kg (maximum 4500 mg), fosphenytoin 20 mg PE/kg, or valproate 40 mg/kg. Look for causes (hypoglycemia, eclampsia, alcohol withdrawal, toxins such as isoniazid, infection, hemorrhage). If it persists after 20 to 40 minutes, intubate and begin continuous midazolam or propofol infusion with EEG monitoring and ICU admission.
