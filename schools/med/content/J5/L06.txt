@article
When you catch a ball, three systems cooperate: the **nervous system** senses and decides, the **muscular system** pulls, and the **skeletal system** gives the muscles something to pull on.

### The nervous system

Its working cells are **neurons**. A neuron has a cell body, short branches called **dendrites** that receive signals, and a long fiber called an **axon** that sends signals on. Signals are tiny electrical impulses.

- The **central nervous system (CNS)** is the brain and spinal cord.
- The **peripheral nervous system (PNS)** is the nerves that run between the CNS and the rest of the body.

A **reflex** is a fast, automatic response, such as pulling your hand off something hot. The path is a **reflex arc**: a sensory neuron carries the signal to the spinal cord, and a motor neuron carries the answer to a muscle. The brain gets the news a moment later.

### The skeletal system

An adult has 206 bones. The skeleton **supports** the body, **protects** organs (the skull protects the brain, the ribs protect the heart and lungs), allows **movement** at joints, **stores** minerals such as calcium, and makes blood cells in bone marrow. **Ligaments** connect bone to bone, and **cartilage** cushions joints.

### The muscular system

There are three muscle types: **skeletal** (attached to bones, voluntary), **smooth** (in the walls of organs such as the stomach, involuntary) and **cardiac** (in the heart, involuntary). Skeletal muscles are joined to bones by **tendons**. A muscle can only pull when it contracts, so muscles work in **pairs**: when the biceps contracts the elbow bends, and when the triceps contracts the elbow straightens.

### Oxygen's trip to a muscle cell

Air enters the nose, travels down the trachea and bronchi, and reaches the alveoli. Oxygen diffuses into capillaries and binds to hemoglobin in red blood cells. Blood returns to the left side of the heart and is pumped through arteries to the muscle. In muscle capillaries, oxygen diffuses into the muscle cell, where mitochondria use it to release energy from sugar. The waste carbon dioxide travels back the other way.

### Measuring reaction time at home

A friend holds a ruler, and you catch it between your fingers when they let go. The distance $d$ the ruler fell gives your reaction time $t$. Because a falling object follows $d = \tfrac{1}{2} g t^2$ with $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$,

$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2d}{g}}.$$

This is safe and gentle. Stop if anyone is tired or unwell.

@example
title: From ruler drop to reaction time
problem: A ruler falls 15 cm before it is caught. What is the reaction time? Use $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$.
step: Convert to meters: $15\ \text{cm} = 0.15\ \text{m}$.
step: Substitute into $t = \sqrt{2d/g}$: $t = \sqrt{2 \times 0.15 \div 9.8}$.
step: Compute $0.30 \div 9.8 \approx 0.0306$, and its square root is about $0.175$.
step: The reaction time is about $0.175$ s, or roughly $0.18$ s.

@mistakes
- Using centimeters instead of meters in the reaction-time formula, which gives a wrong answer.
- Thinking a muscle can push; a muscle only pulls, so a second muscle is needed to move the bone back.
- Mixing up tendons (muscle to bone) and ligaments (bone to bone).

@practice self
prompt: Trace one breath of oxygen from the air outside to a mitochondrion in a leg muscle cell. List each structure in order (at least 8) and say what happens to the oxygen at the key points.
solution: Air enters the nose or mouth, passes through the trachea, a bronchus and smaller tubes to an alveolus. Oxygen diffuses across the thin alveolar wall into a capillary and binds to hemoglobin in a red blood cell. Blood flows through the pulmonary vein to the left atrium and left ventricle, which pumps it into the aorta and arteries to the leg. In the muscle's capillaries, oxygen is released and diffuses into the muscle cell. In the mitochondria, oxygen is used with sugar to release energy, and carbon dioxide is made and carried away by the blood to the lungs.

rubric: Ends with oxygen entering the cell and being used in mitochondria.
