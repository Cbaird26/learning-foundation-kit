@article
Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is chronic, relapsing inflammation of the gut in genetically susceptible people (NOD2 variants in Crohn disease), driven by abnormal immune responses to the microbiota. The two main forms look similar in a patient with diarrhea but differ in almost everything pathologic.

### Crohn disease versus ulcerative colitis

| Feature | Crohn disease | Ulcerative colitis |
|---|---|---|
| Distribution | Any part from mouth to anus; terminal ileum and colon most often; **skip lesions** | Rectum always involved; **continuous** extension proximally; colon only |
| Depth | **Transmural** | Mucosa and submucosa |
| Histology | Noncaseating granulomas (in some), lymphoid aggregates | Crypt abscesses, crypt distortion, no granulomas |
| Gross | Cobblestone mucosa, creeping fat, linear ulcers, strictures | Friable mucosa, pseudopolyps, lead-pipe colon on imaging |
| Typical symptoms | Abdominal pain, diarrhea (often nonbloody), weight loss | Bloody diarrhea with urgency and tenesmus |
| Complications | Fistulae, abscess, strictures, perianal disease, malabsorption | Toxic megacolon, colorectal cancer |
| Smoking | Worsens disease | Associated with lower risk |
| Surgery | Not curative | Colectomy is curative for the colon |
| Antibodies | ASCA more often positive | p-ANCA more often positive |

Antibodies are not used alone to diagnose or separate the diseases. Terminal ileal Crohn disease impairs bile salt and B12 absorption (B12 deficiency, **gallstones**), and fat malabsorption leaves oxalate unbound to calcium, producing **calcium oxalate kidney stones**.

### Extraintestinal manifestations

Some track with bowel activity: erythema nodosum, aphthous stomatitis, episcleritis, and peripheral (type 1) arthritis. Others run independently: **primary sclerosing cholangitis** (more common with UC; cholestatic liver tests, beading on cholangiography, and risk of cholangiocarcinoma and colon cancer), uveitis, pyoderma gangrenosum (sometimes), sacroiliitis and ankylosing spondylitis.

### Complications

**Toxic megacolon** (colonic dilation above 6 cm with systemic toxicity: fever, tachycardia, leukocytosis) may complicate severe UC or infectious colitis. Management is IV corticosteroids, broad-spectrum antibiotics, bowel rest, serial abdominal imaging and early surgical consultation; avoid barium enema, colonoscopy and antimotility drugs. **Colorectal cancer risk** rises with disease duration and extent: surveillance colonoscopy begins about **8 years after diagnosis** of extensive colitis, and earlier in PSC, which is surveilled from diagnosis.

### Diagnosis

Exclude infection (stool culture, C. difficile testing). Colonoscopy with ileoscopy and biopsies establishes the diagnosis. Cross-sectional imaging (CT or MR enterography) shows small-bowel disease and complications. **Fecal calprotectin** (from neutrophils) and CRP distinguish IBD from irritable bowel syndrome and track activity. Truelove and Witts criteria define severe UC: at least 6 bloody stools per day plus systemic signs (heart rate above 90, temperature above 37.8 C, hemoglobin below 10.5 g/dL, ESR above 30 mm/h).

### Treatment

- **UC:** mild disease responds to **5-aminosalicylates (mesalamine)** by mouth or rectally (suppositories or enemas for proctitis). Moderate to severe disease requires induction with corticosteroids and advanced therapy: anti-TNF agents (infliximab, adalimumab), the anti-integrin **vedolizumab**, the IL-12/23 inhibitor ustekinumab, and JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, upadacitinib). Acute severe UC is treated in hospital with IV methylprednisolone; if there is no response by about day 3, rescue with infliximab or cyclosporine, or colectomy.
- **Crohn disease:** 5-ASA is not effective. Use budesonide for mild ileocecal disease and systemic steroids briefly for flares, but move early to **biologics** (anti-TNF with or without an immunomodulator such as azathioprine, or vedolizumab or ustekinumab) in moderate to severe or complicated disease. Surgery for strictures, fistulae and abscesses. Stop smoking.
- **Before biologics:** screen for latent tuberculosis (interferon-gamma release assay) and hepatitis B, and update vaccines. Anti-TNF risks include serious infection, TB reactivation, demyelination and lymphoma.

@example
title: Colonoscopy pattern
problem: A 24-year-old man has bloody diarrhea and tenesmus for 3 weeks. Colonoscopy shows continuous erythema and friability from the anal verge to the splenic flexure, with a normal terminal ileum. Biopsies show crypt abscesses without granulomas. Diagnosis and first-line therapy?
step: Continuous disease starting at the rectum and sparing the ileum, with mucosal inflammation and crypt abscesses, is ulcerative colitis.
step: Bloody diarrhea with extension to the splenic flexure is left-sided, moderately active disease. First-line therapy is oral plus rectal mesalamine.
step: If steroid or 5-ASA failure occurs, move to biologics or JAK inhibitors after TB and hepatitis B screening.

@example
title: How much does a positive calprotectin change the odds?
problem: Before testing, a patient with chronic diarrhea has a 30% probability of IBD. A fecal calprotectin test has a positive likelihood ratio of 5. What is the probability after a positive result?
step: Convert pretest probability to odds: 0.30 / 0.70 = 0.4286.
step: Multiply by the likelihood ratio: 0.4286 times 5 = 2.143.
step: Convert back to probability: 2.143 / 3.143 = 0.68. A positive calprotectin raises the probability from 30% to about 68%, which supports endoscopy.

@mistakes
- Saying 5-ASA drugs treat Crohn disease: they are the mainstay of mild UC but have little proven benefit in Crohn disease.
- Using colonoscopy or barium studies in toxic megacolon because of perforation risk.
- Thinking colectomy cures Crohn disease: it cures ulcerative colitis of the colon but Crohn disease can recur.
- Starting infliximab without screening for latent TB and hepatitis B.
