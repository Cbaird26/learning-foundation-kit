@article
### Classify first

Diarrhea is acute under 14 days, persistent 14 to 30 days, and chronic over 30 days. Most acute diarrhea is viral or foodborne and self-limited. Ask about **exposures** (travel, undercooked meat, eggs, seafood, untreated water, daycare, recent antibiotics or hospitalization), **blood, fever, volume status**, and immune status. Test stool (multiplex PCR or culture) when diarrhea is severe, bloody, febrile, persistent, in an immunocompromised host, or part of an outbreak.

- **Secretory/toxin:** large-volume watery stool, no blood or fever (cholera, ETEC, preformed toxins).
- **Inflammatory/invasive:** fever, blood or pus, tenesmus, fecal leukocytes or high lactoferrin or calprotectin (Shigella, Campylobacter, Salmonella, EHEC, C. difficile, Entamoeba).
- **Osmotic:** stops with fasting (lactose intolerance, laxatives).

### Pathogens by syndrome

| Pathogen | Exposure or clue | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Norovirus | Outbreaks (cruise ships, schools) | Vomiting and watery diarrhea 1 to 3 days |
| Rotavirus | Unvaccinated young children | Vaccine prevents severe disease |
| Staphylococcus aureus, Bacillus cereus (emetic) | Vomiting within 1 to 6 hours of eating (dairy or mayonnaise foods; reheated rice) | Preformed toxin |
| Clostridium perfringens | Reheated meat, onset 8 to 16 hours | Toxin; watery |
| ETEC | Traveler's diarrhea | Heat-labile toxin raises cAMP (like cholera); heat-stable toxin raises cGMP |
| Vibrio cholerae | Contaminated water in endemic areas | "Rice-water" stool, profound volume loss; toxin ADP-ribosylates Gs, raising cAMP |
| Campylobacter jejuni | Undercooked poultry | Bloody diarrhea; Guillain-Barre syndrome, reactive arthritis |
| Nontyphoidal Salmonella | Poultry, eggs, reptiles | Usually self-limited; treat high-risk hosts |
| Salmonella Typhi | Travel (South Asia) | Typhoid fever: stepwise fever, rose spots, relative bradycardia |
| Shigella | Low inoculum, daycare, dysentery | Tenesmus; Shiga toxin can cause hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS) |
| Shiga toxin-producing E. coli (O157:H7) | Undercooked ground beef | Bloody diarrhea, **little or no fever**; **HUS** (hemolytic anemia with schistocytes, thrombocytopenia, kidney injury) |
| Yersinia enterocolitica | Pork, cold storage | Pseudoappendicitis |
| Giardia lamblia | Streams, daycare | Foul gas, steatorrhea, weight loss; trophozoites or stool antigen |
| Entamoeba histolytica | Travel | Flask-shaped colon ulcers, bloody diarrhea; liver abscess |
| Cryptosporidium | Pools; immunocompromised | Watery diarrhea; acid-fast oocysts |

### Clostridioides difficile

Antibiotics (clindamycin, fluoroquinolones, cephalosporins), hospitalization, PPIs and older age predispose. Toxins A and B damage the colon (**pseudomembranes**). Test only patients with three or more unformed stools in 24 hours without laxatives, using a multistep test (glutamate dehydrogenase and toxin, with PCR as arbiter). Initial treatment for study: **fidaxomicin 200 mg orally twice daily for 10 days** (preferred by 2021 IDSA/SHEA guidance) or **vancomycin 125 mg orally four times daily for 10 days**. Fulminant disease (hypotension, ileus, megacolon): vancomycin 500 mg orally four times daily plus intravenous metronidazole, with surgical consultation. Recurrence is treated with extended-pulse fidaxomicin or vancomycin taper, and fecal microbiota-based therapy after repeated recurrences. Stop the inciting antibiotic if possible, avoid antimotility drugs, and use soap-and-water hand washing with contact precautions (alcohol does not kill spores).

### Treatment principles

1. **Rehydrate.** Oral rehydration solution works because the sodium-glucose cotransporter (SGLT1) remains intact even in cholera; reduced-osmolarity WHO solution contains about 75 mmol/L sodium and 75 mmol/L glucose. Use IV isotonic fluids for severe dehydration, shock or vomiting. Pediatric deficit: a 5% loss of body weight is about 50 mL/kg; replace ongoing losses at about 10 mL/kg per loose stool.
2. **Antimotility drugs** (loperamide) are acceptable in afebrile adults with nonbloody watery diarrhea; **avoid in bloody diarrhea, fever, suspected STEC or C. difficile**.
3. **Antibiotics** are not needed for most. Treat: severe traveler's diarrhea (azithromycin; typical adult single dose 1 g or 500 mg daily for 3 days, because fluoroquinolone resistance is common), dysentery with fever (azithromycin), shigellosis, typhoid, cholera (single-dose doxycycline or azithromycin as an adjunct), nontyphoidal Salmonella in infants, older adults with vascular disease or prostheses, and immunocompromised patients, plus C. difficile, giardiasis (tinidazole or metronidazole) and amebiasis (metronidazole followed by paromomycin). **Do not give antibiotics for STEC** (higher HUS risk).

@example
title: A traveler with fever and bloody diarrhea
problem: A 24-year-old returns from Southeast Asia with 4 days of fever, bloody diarrhea and tenesmus. How do you treat him?
step: Fever, blood and tenesmus suggest invasive disease: Shigella, Campylobacter or Salmonella. Send stool PCR or culture.
step: Rehydrate (oral if tolerated). Avoid loperamide because of fever and blood.
step: Give azithromycin empirically because fluoroquinolone resistance is common. Adjust to culture and susceptibilities.

@example
title: How much fluid does a dehydrated child need?
problem: A 15 kg child's weight has dropped to 14.1 kg during gastroenteritis. Estimate the percentage dehydration and the deficit.
step: Weight loss is 15 - 14.1 = 0.9 kg.
step: Percentage = 0.9 / 15 times 100 = 6%.
step: The deficit is about 0.9 L (900 mL), because 1 kg of body water is about 1 L. Replace it orally over 4 hours with ORS plus ongoing losses.

@mistakes
- Giving antibiotics or loperamide for suspected STEC: this increases the risk of HUS.
- Testing C. difficile in formed stool or in patients taking laxatives.
- Using alcohol-based sanitizer alone against C. difficile: spores need soap and water.
- Assuming oral rehydration fails in cholera: SGLT1 coupling keeps it effective.
