### Malabsorption by mechanism

Malabsorption means failure of nutrient digestion or absorption. Steatorrhea (bulky, foul, greasy, floating stool), weight loss and deficiencies are the typical consequences: **iron** (microcytic anemia), **folate and B12** (macrocytic anemia, neuropathy), **calcium and vitamin D** (osteomalacia, osteoporosis), **vitamin K** (prolonged INR), **vitamin A** (night blindness), **vitamin E** (neuropathy, hemolysis).

| Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|
| Luminal maldigestion (not enough enzymes) | Chronic pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis, pancreatic cancer |
| Bile acid deficiency or interrupted circulation | Cholestasis, ileal resection or Crohn ileitis, bacterial deconjugation |
| Mucosal disease (reduced surface or enzymes) | Celiac disease, tropical sprue, Whipple disease, giardiasis, lactase deficiency, short bowel |
| Lymphatic obstruction | Intestinal lymphangiectasia, lymphoma |
| Bacterial overgrowth | Blind loop, strictures, dysmotility, diverticula |

### Testing

- **Fecal fat:** Sudan III stain is a screen; a **72-hour collection** on a 100 g per day fat diet is abnormal above **7 g per day**. It cannot say why.
- **Fecal elastase** is low in exocrine pancreatic insufficiency.
- **D-xylose absorption** is a passive absorption test: **low in mucosal disease** (and SIBO) but **normal in pancreatic insufficiency**.
- **Breath tests:** hydrogen after lactose (lactose intolerance); after glucose or lactulose (SIBO).
- **Endoscopic duodenal biopsy** for celiac disease, Whipple disease and giardiasis.

### Stool osmotic gap

Plasma osmolality is about 290 mOsm/kg and stool osmolality equilibrates with it. If stool electrolytes explain the osmolality, the diarrhea is secretory; if unmeasured solutes (sugars, magnesium) explain it, it is osmotic. The stool osmotic gap is

$$\text{gap} = 290 - 2\,([\text{Na}^+] + [\text{K}^+]).$$

A gap **above 100** is osmotic (lactose intolerance, laxatives, magnesium); a gap **below 50** is secretory (cholera, VIPoma, bile acid diarrhea). Between 50 and 100 is indeterminate. Osmotic diarrhea stops with fasting; secretory diarrhea continues.

### Celiac disease

An immune-mediated reaction to gluten (gliadin) in genetically susceptible people with **HLA-DQ2 or HLA-DQ8**. Tissue transglutaminase deamidates gliadin, which is presented to T cells, producing inflammation of the proximal small bowel. Presentation ranges from classic malabsorption to iron deficiency anemia, osteoporosis, elevated transaminases, infertility, dermatitis herpetiformis (pruritic vesicles on extensor surfaces, with granular IgA at dermal papillae), and associations with type 1 diabetes and autoimmune thyroid disease.

Diagnosis: **IgA anti-tissue transglutaminase (tTG) antibody with total IgA** while the patient is **eating gluten**, followed by duodenal biopsy showing **villous atrophy, crypt hyperplasia and intraepithelial lymphocytes**. In IgA deficiency, use IgG-based tests. HLA-DQ2/DQ8 testing has a high negative predictive value but does not confirm disease. Treatment is a **lifelong gluten-free diet**. Refractory disease and complications include enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma and small-bowel adenocarcinoma.

### Other disorders

- **Lactose intolerance:** lactase deficiency (primary decline with age, or secondary to mucosal injury). Gas, bloating, osmotic diarrhea after dairy; stool pH is low; manage with avoidance or lactase.
- **Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO):** bloating, diarrhea, steatorrhea from bile salt deconjugation. Bacteria consume vitamin B12 and make folate, so **B12 is low and folate is normal or high**. Treat the cause plus rifaximin.
- **Whipple disease:** *Tropheryma whipplei*; middle-aged man with arthralgia, diarrhea, weight loss, sometimes neurologic and cardiac involvement; biopsy shows **PAS-positive foamy macrophages** in the lamina propria. Treat with prolonged antibiotics (induction with ceftriaxone, then trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for a year).
- **Abetalipoproteinemia:** MTP deficiency, absent chylomicrons, acanthocytes, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, neurologic disease.
- **Small bowel obstruction:** adhesions from prior surgery are the most common cause, then hernias and tumors. Colicky pain, vomiting, distension and obstipation; air-fluid levels on radiograph; CT confirms; manage with NPO, nasogastric decompression, IV fluids, and surgery for strangulation or failure of conservative management.
- **Acute mesenteric ischemia:** pain out of proportion to the abdominal examination in a patient with atrial fibrillation (SMA embolus), atherosclerosis or low-flow states; CT angiography; emergent revascularization and bowel resection if necrotic.

@example
title: Interpreting the stool osmotic gap
problem: A patient has chronic watery diarrhea. Stool sodium is 60 mmol/L and potassium is 30 mmol/L. Is the diarrhea osmotic or secretory, and what is the next question to ask?
step: Compute the electrolyte contribution: 2 times (60 + 30) = 180 mOsm/kg.
step: Gap = 290 - 180 = 110. This is above 100, so unmeasured osmoles are present: osmotic diarrhea.
step: Ask about lactose, sorbitol-containing sugar-free products, magnesium-containing antacids or laxatives, and whether the diarrhea stops with fasting.

@example
title: Evaluating suspected celiac disease
problem: A 34-year-old woman has iron deficiency anemia, bloating and elevated ALT. What are the next steps?
step: Confirm that she is eating gluten regularly; stopping gluten beforehand would cause false-negative serology and biopsy.
step: Order IgA anti-tTG with total IgA. If tTG is strongly positive, proceed to duodenal biopsies.
step: Biopsy shows villous atrophy with intraepithelial lymphocytes. Start a gluten-free diet, check ferritin, B12, folate, vitamin D, and bone density, and screen for thyroid disease and diabetes as appropriate.

@mistakes
- Stopping gluten before testing for celiac disease, which makes serology and biopsy unreliable.
- Using D-xylose to diagnose pancreatic insufficiency: it is normal there because absorption is passive and enzyme-independent.
- Forgetting the factor of 2 for Na and K in the stool osmotic gap (it accounts for accompanying anions).
- Assuming SIBO lowers folate: bacteria produce folate and consume B12.
