### Dysphagia first: where and for what?

**Oropharyngeal dysphagia** (difficulty initiating a swallow, coughing, nasal regurgitation, aspiration) suggests stroke, Parkinson disease, myasthenia or a Zenker diverticulum. **Esophageal dysphagia** is felt behind the sternum. If it affects **solids and liquids from the start**, think motility. If it affects **solids first and progresses**, think mechanical obstruction: stricture, ring, eosinophilic esophagitis or cancer.

- **Achalasia:** loss of inhibitory (nitric oxide and VIP) neurons of the myenteric plexus leaves the lower esophageal sphincter (LES) unable to relax, and the esophageal body aperistaltic. Patients have dysphagia to both solids and liquids, regurgitation of undigested food, and weight loss. Barium swallow shows a dilated esophagus with a "bird's beak." High-resolution manometry (elevated integrated relaxation pressure, absent peristalsis) is diagnostic, after endoscopy excludes pseudoachalasia from a tumor. Chagas disease (Trypanosoma cruzi) produces a similar picture. Treatment is pneumatic dilation, laparoscopic Heller myotomy or peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM); botulinum toxin is for patients who cannot have definitive therapy. Risk of squamous cell carcinoma is increased.
- **Diffuse esophageal spasm:** chest pain and dysphagia with "corkscrew" esophagus on barium; manometry shows premature contractions.
- **Systemic sclerosis:** smooth muscle atrophy gives a hypotensive LES, absent peristalsis, and severe reflux.

### GERD and its complications

Gastroesophageal reflux disease arises from transient LES relaxations, a weak LES, hiatal hernia, delayed gastric emptying and obesity. Typical symptoms are heartburn and regurgitation. A patient with typical symptoms and no alarm features can receive an empiric **8-week trial of a proton pump inhibitor**. **Alarm features** (dysphagia, odynophagia, weight loss, GI bleeding or iron deficiency anemia, persistent vomiting, age over about 60 with new symptoms) call for upper endoscopy. Complications are erosive esophagitis, peptic stricture and **Barrett esophagus**: replacement of distal squamous epithelium with columnar epithelium containing **goblet cells (intestinal metaplasia)**. Barrett esophagus is the precursor of **esophageal adenocarcinoma** through low- and high-grade dysplasia; it is managed with PPI, periodic surveillance endoscopy, and endoscopic eradication for dysplasia. Atypical presentations (chronic cough, hoarseness, asthma, dental erosions) should be investigated carefully before they are attributed to reflux.

**Eosinophilic esophagitis** is an allergic condition in young, often atopic, patients with solid-food dysphagia and food impaction. Endoscopy shows rings (a "trachealized" esophagus), furrows and white exudates; biopsy shows 15 or more eosinophils per high-power field. Treatment includes a PPI trial, swallowed topical corticosteroids (fluticasone or budesonide), dietary elimination, or dupilumab.

### Esophageal cancer and other lesions

Squamous cell carcinoma (upper and middle esophagus; smoking, alcohol, hot beverages, achalasia, caustic injury) and adenocarcinoma (lower esophagus; Barrett, chronic GERD, obesity) both present with progressive dysphagia and weight loss. **Mallory-Weiss tear:** partial-thickness mucosal tear at the gastroesophageal junction after retching, with hematemesis, usually self-limited. **Boerhaave syndrome:** transmural rupture of the distal esophagus after forceful vomiting, presenting with chest pain, subcutaneous emphysema and mediastinitis; it is a surgical emergency, diagnosed by CT or water-soluble contrast esophagram. **Infectious esophagitis** in immunocompromised patients: Candida (white plaques), HSV (small punched-out ulcers), CMV (large linear ulcers).

### Stomach: gastritis and cancer

| Feature | Type A (autoimmune) | Type B (H. pylori) |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Body and fundus | Antrum (and may spread) |
| Mechanism | Antibodies to parietal cells and intrinsic factor | H. pylori infection |
| Acid and gastrin | Achlorhydria, high gastrin | Variable; may be low or high |
| Associations | Pernicious anemia (B12), other autoimmune disease, ECL hyperplasia and gastric carcinoids | Peptic ulcers, MALT lymphoma, gastric adenocarcinoma |

Acute gastritis follows NSAIDs, alcohol and severe illness ("stress gastropathy"). **Gastric adenocarcinoma** occurs in an **intestinal type** (ulcerated mass; H. pylori, nitrosamines, smoked foods) and a **diffuse type** (signet-ring cells, linitis plastica, CDH1 mutation encoding E-cadherin). Spread to the left supraclavicular node is **Virchow node**, to the ovary is **Krukenberg tumor**, to the umbilicus is **Sister Mary Joseph nodule**. Gastric MALT lymphoma may regress after H. pylori eradication. **Ménétrier disease** is foveolar hyperplasia with protein-losing gastropathy.

title: Dysphagia to both solids and liquids
problem: A 38-year-old woman has had progressive trouble swallowing both solids and liquids for 8 months, regurgitates undigested food at night and has lost 6 kg. Endoscopy shows no mass; manometry shows incomplete LES relaxation with absent peristalsis. What is the diagnosis, mechanism and a definitive treatment?
step: Dysphagia to solids and liquids from the outset points to a motility disorder rather than a stricture.
step: Incomplete LES relaxation with absent peristalsis is achalasia. Inhibitory neurons (NO and VIP) in the myenteric plexus are lost, so the sphincter stays contracted.
step: Endoscopy has excluded a tumor mimicking achalasia. Barium swallow would show a dilated esophagus with a bird's beak tapering.
step: Definitive options are pneumatic dilation, Heller myotomy or POEM. Chagas serology is checked if she is from an endemic area.

@example
title: Number needed to treat for PPI therapy
problem: In a trial, 85% of patients with erosive esophagitis healed with a proton pump inhibitor at 8 weeks versus 60% with H2 blocker. What is the number needed to treat?
step: Absolute risk reduction is 0.85 - 0.60 = 0.25.
step: NNT is the reciprocal: 1 / 0.25 = 4. About 4 patients must be treated with the PPI rather than the H2 blocker for one additional patient to heal.

@mistakes
- Treating all dysphagia with a PPI trial: alarm features, progressive dysphagia or weight loss need endoscopy first.
- Saying Barrett esophagus is squamous metaplasia: it is columnar (intestinal) metaplasia with goblet cells.
- Mixing squamous cell carcinoma (upper and middle esophagus, smoking and alcohol) with adenocarcinoma (lower esophagus, Barrett).
- Confusing Mallory-Weiss (mucosal tear, bleeding) with Boerhaave (transmural rupture, mediastinitis).
