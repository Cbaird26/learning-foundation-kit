@article
The gut does three things in sequence: it moves food, it adds fluid and enzymes, and it absorbs nutrients and water. Disease is almost always a failure of one of these three jobs.

### Motility

Interstitial cells of Cajal generate **slow waves**, rhythmic oscillations of smooth muscle membrane potential (about 3 per minute in the stomach, about 12 per minute in the duodenum, falling to about 8 to 9 in the ileum). Whether a slow wave produces a contraction depends on whether the excitatory input (acetylcholine, substance P) pushes it over threshold. **Peristalsis** is a coordinated wave of contraction behind a bolus and relaxation ahead of it. **Segmentation** is mixing without net propulsion. Between meals the **migrating motor complex** sweeps undigested material from stomach to ileum every 90 to 120 minutes, triggered by motilin, and is abolished by feeding. **Receptive relaxation** of the gastric fundus (vagovagal reflex, nitric oxide and VIP) lets the stomach accommodate a meal. The **gastrocolic reflex** triggers colonic mass movements after eating.

### GI hormones

| Hormone | Source | Stimulus | Main actions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gastrin | G cells (antrum) | Peptides, amino acids, distension, vagal GRP; inhibited by pH below about 3 | Increases acid secretion, trophic to gastric mucosa |
| Cholecystokinin (CCK) | I cells (duodenum, jejunum) | Fatty acids, amino acids | Gallbladder contraction, sphincter of Oddi relaxation, pancreatic enzyme secretion; slows gastric emptying |
| Secretin | S cells (duodenum) | Acid in duodenum (pH below about 4.5) | Pancreatic and biliary bicarbonate; reduces gastric acid |
| GIP | K cells | Glucose, fat | Insulin release when glucose is present; inhibits acid |
| Somatostatin | D cells | Low pH | Inhibits nearly all GI secretion and hormones |

### Regulation of gastric acid secretion

The **parietal cell** secretes H+ into the lumen through the apical **H+/K+ ATPase** (the proton pump) in exchange for K+. Inside the cell, carbonic anhydrase converts CO2 and water to H+ and HCO3-. Bicarbonate leaves across the basolateral membrane in exchange for Cl-, so gastric venous blood is more alkaline after a meal (the **alkaline tide**). Cl- then moves into the lumen through channels. The result is gastric juice with H+ of about 100 to 150 mmol/L (pH about 1).

Three stimulatory signals converge on the parietal cell:
- **Acetylcholine** (vagus) acting on M3 receptors (Gq, raises intracellular Ca2+).
- **Gastrin** acting on CCK2 (CCK-B) receptors (Gq, raises Ca2+). Gastrin's main effect is indirect: it stimulates **ECL cells** to release histamine.
- **Histamine** from ECL cells acting on **H2 receptors** (Gs, raises cAMP). Histamine is the dominant stimulus, and it potentiates the other two.

Inhibition comes from **somatostatin** from D cells (Gi, lowers cAMP), acting directly on parietal cells and G cells and ECL cells, and from **prostaglandins E2 and I2** (Gi), secretin, CCK and GIP. Acid itself is the feedback brake: when antral pH falls below about 3, D cells release somatostatin that shuts off gastrin.

The three phases: **cephalic** (sight, smell, chewing; vagal; roughly 30%), **gastric** (distension and peptides; the largest share, roughly 60%) and **intestinal** (small, mostly inhibitory by acid, fat and hyperosmolarity entering the duodenum, via secretin, CCK and enterogastrones).

Other gastric products: **intrinsic factor** from parietal cells (needed for ileal vitamin B12 absorption), **pepsinogen** from chief cells (activated to pepsin by acid), and **mucus and bicarbonate** from surface mucous cells (the protective layer, stimulated by prostaglandins).

### Absorption

- **Carbohydrates:** salivary and pancreatic amylase, then brush-border enzymes (lactase, sucrase-isomaltase). Glucose and galactose enter by **SGLT1** (sodium-coupled), fructose by **GLUT5**, and all exit by **GLUT2**.
- **Protein:** pepsin, then pancreatic proteases (trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase, carboxypeptidase), then di- and tripeptides via **PepT1**. Enteropeptidase on the duodenal brush border activates trypsinogen.
- **Fat:** emulsified by bile salts, digested by pancreatic lipase and colipase, absorbed in **micelles**, repackaged as **chylomicrons** and carried by lymph (medium-chain fatty acids go straight to portal blood).
- **Iron:** duodenum (Fe2+ via DMT1, exported by ferroportin, which hepcidin blocks). **Calcium:** duodenum and jejunum, stimulated by calcitriol. **Folate:** jejunum. **Vitamin B12 and bile salts:** terminal ileum.
- **Water:** about 9 L enters the gut daily (about 2 L ingested plus about 7 L secreted), about 1 to 1.5 L reaches the colon, and about 100 to 200 mL leaves in stool. Most absorption occurs in the small intestine; the colon recovers sodium and water and secretes K+.

@example
title: Why does omeprazole raise serum gastrin?
problem: A 48-year-old man takes a proton pump inhibitor for years and a routine test shows serum gastrin above normal, with a normal fasting acid secretory test off the drug. Explain.
step: Normally acid in the antrum (pH below about 3) stimulates D cells to release somatostatin, which inhibits G cells. This is negative feedback on gastrin.
step: The proton pump inhibitor blocks the final step of acid secretion, so antral pH rises and somatostatin release falls.
step: G cells are released from inhibition, and gastrin rises. Gastrin is trophic, so long-term elevation may cause ECL hyperplasia and rebound acid hypersecretion on stopping the drug.

@example
title: How much of the fluid that enters the gut is absorbed?
problem: An adult drinks 2 L of fluid per day and the gut secretes 7 L. Stool water is 0.15 L. What percentage of the fluid entering the gut is absorbed?
step: Total fluid entering the lumen is 2 + 7 = 9 L.
step: Net absorbed volume is 9 - 0.15 = 8.85 L.
step: Percentage absorbed is 8.85 / 9 times 100, which is about 98.3%. Small changes in this fraction cause large changes in stool water: a 1% failure to absorb adds about 90 mL of stool.

@mistakes
- Saying gastrin acts mainly directly on the parietal cell: its main effect is through histamine release from ECL cells.
- Confusing secretin (bicarbonate, from S cells, stimulated by duodenal acid) with CCK (enzymes and gallbladder contraction, stimulated by fat and protein).
- Placing B12 and bile salt absorption in the jejunum: both are absorbed in the terminal ileum.
- Forgetting that acid stimulates somatostatin: raising gastric pH removes this brake and raises gastrin.

solution: In the cephalic phase, sight, smell and chewing activate the vagus, releasing acetylcholine directly on parietal cells (M3) and gastrin-releasing peptide on G cells.
