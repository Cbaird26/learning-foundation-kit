@article
### Viral hepatitis

- **Hepatitis A** (RNA, fecal-oral; outbreaks, travel, shellfish): acute self-limited illness; IgM anti-HAV; no chronic state; vaccine.
- **Hepatitis E** (RNA, fecal-oral): usually self-limited, but high mortality in pregnancy.
- **Hepatitis B** (DNA, blood, sexual, perinatal): chronic in about 90% of infected neonates but under 5% of adults. Chronic infection brings cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (even without cirrhosis). Treatment of chronic disease uses nucleos(t)ide analogs such as **entecavir or tenofovir**. Immunosuppressive therapy (for example rituximab) can reactivate it.
- **Hepatitis C** (RNA, blood, injection drug use): chronic in about 70% to 80%, often silent for decades. Direct-acting antivirals (for example sofosbuvir with velpatasvir for 12 weeks, or glecaprevir with pibrentasvir for 8 weeks in many patients) cure more than 95%, measured by sustained virologic response (undetectable RNA 12 weeks after treatment). Screen all adults at least once.
- **Hepatitis D** needs HBsAg to replicate (co-infection or superinfection).

| Pattern | HBsAg | anti-HBs | anti-HBc | HBeAg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acute infection | + | − | IgM | + |
| Window period | − | − | IgM | − |
| Chronic infection | + (over 6 months) | − | IgG | + or − |
| Resolved (immune) | − | + | IgG | − |
| Vaccinated | − | + | − | − |

### Other chronic liver diseases

- **Autoimmune hepatitis:** women, other autoimmune disease; ANA, anti-smooth muscle antibodies, high IgG, interface hepatitis; prednisone with azathioprine.
- **Hemochromatosis:** HFE (C282Y) mutation, high ferritin and transferrin saturation (above about 45%), cirrhosis, diabetes, skin pigmentation, arthropathy, cardiomyopathy, hypogonadism; phlebotomy.
- **Wilson disease:** ATP7B mutation; low ceruloplasmin, high urinary copper, Kayser-Fleischer rings, neuropsychiatric signs; chelators (trientine, penicillamine) and zinc.
- **Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency:** PAS-positive, diastase-resistant hepatocyte globules; liver disease plus panacinar emphysema.
- **Alcohol-associated and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD)** are the commonest causes of cirrhosis in the United States with hepatitis C.

### Cirrhosis

Chronic injury activates hepatic stellate cells, deposits collagen and produces regenerative nodules, raising intrahepatic resistance (**portal hypertension**) and reducing synthetic function. Findings include spider angiomata, palmar erythema, gynecomastia and testicular atrophy (estrogen excess), caput medusae, splenomegaly, ascites and asterixis. **Child-Pugh** (bilirubin, albumin, INR, ascites, encephalopathy; each scored 1 to 3 points) classifies severity: **A 5 to 6, B 7 to 9, C 10 to 15**. Cutoffs: bilirubin below 2 (1 point), 2 to 3 (2), above 3 (3); albumin above 3.5 (1), 2.8 to 3.5 (2), below 2.8 (3); INR below 1.7 (1), 1.7 to 2.3 (2), above 2.3 (3); ascites none (1), mild or controlled (2), moderate to severe (3); encephalopathy none (1), grade 1 to 2 (2), grade 3 to 4 (3).

### Complications

- **Ascites.** Diagnostic paracentesis for all new ascites and any hospitalization. **SAAG** (serum albumin minus ascitic fluid albumin) of **1.1 g/dL or more** means portal hypertension (cirrhosis, heart failure, Budd-Chiari); below 1.1 suggests nephrotic syndrome, carcinomatosis or tuberculous peritonitis. Treatment: sodium restriction (about 2 g per day), **spironolactone plus furosemide** (typical 100 mg and 40 mg per day; for study), large-volume paracentesis with albumin for tense ascites, avoidance of NSAIDs, and TIPS or transplant for refractory ascites.
- **Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP):** ascitic **PMN count of 250 per microliter or more** (PMN = total WBC times neutrophil fraction) without a surgical source. Fever, abdominal pain, encephalopathy or renal decline. Treat with **IV ceftriaxone** and **albumin** (typical 1.5 g/kg on day 1 and 1 g/kg on day 3), and give long-term antibiotic prophylaxis after an episode.
- **Variceal bleeding:** screen with endoscopy (or noninvasive criteria). Primary prophylaxis for medium or large varices uses a nonselective beta-blocker (propranolol, nadolol, or carvedilol) or band ligation. Acute management was covered in week 4; secondary prophylaxis is beta-blocker plus banding; TIPS for failure.
- **Hepatorenal syndrome (HRS-AKI):** acute kidney injury from renal vasoconstriction after splanchnic vasodilation, with bland urine and very low urine sodium, not responding to withdrawal of diuretics and albumin 1 g/kg for 2 days. Treat with albumin and a vasoconstrictor (terlipressin, or norepinephrine); transplant is definitive.
- **Hepatic encephalopathy:** ammonia and other toxins reach the brain through portosystemic shunting; confusion, asterixis, reversed sleep cycle. Look for precipitants (GI bleeding, infection, constipation, hypokalemia, sedatives, dehydration). **Lactulose** (titrated to 2 to 3 soft stools daily); add **rifaximin** to prevent recurrence.
- **Hepatocellular carcinoma:** ultrasound with or without AFP every 6 months. **Vaccinate** for hepatitis A and B. Avoid NSAIDs and aminoglycosides.

@example
title: Is it SBP?
problem: A patient with cirrhosis has fever and abdominal pain. Paracentesis shows 1,000 WBC per microliter, 40% neutrophils. Diagnosis and treatment?
step: PMN count = 1,000 times 0.40 = 400 per microliter, which is 250 or more.
step: This meets the criterion for spontaneous bacterial peritonitis.
step: Start IV ceftriaxone after culturing ascitic fluid in blood culture bottles, and give albumin (1.5 g/kg on day 1, 1 g/kg on day 3) to reduce the risk of hepatorenal syndrome. Long-term prophylaxis follows.

@example
title: Interpreting the SAAG
problem: Serum albumin is 3.2 g/dL and ascitic fluid albumin is 0.6 g/dL in a patient with ascites. What is the SAAG, and what does it suggest?
step: SAAG = 3.2 - 0.6 = 2.6 g/dL.
step: A SAAG of 1.1 or more indicates portal hypertension. With high protein fluid (above 2.5 g/dL) suspect heart failure or Budd-Chiari; low protein suggests cirrhosis.

@mistakes
- Treating anti-HBs with anti-HBc as vaccination: vaccination gives anti-HBs only, whereas resolved infection also has anti-HBc.
- Calling ascites a transudate versus exudate instead of using the SAAG.
- Using the total WBC rather than the PMN count to diagnose SBP.
- Giving large doses of diuretics and NSAIDs in cirrhosis, which precipitate AKI and hepatorenal syndrome.
