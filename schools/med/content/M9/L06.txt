@article
The **sympathetic** nervous system uses norepinephrine (and epinephrine from the adrenal medulla). Norepinephrine is made from tyrosine (tyrosine hydroxylase is rate-limiting), stored in vesicles, released, and cleared by reuptake (NET) and metabolism by MAO and COMT.

### Receptors

| Receptor | Signal | Main effects |
|---|---|---|
| $\alpha_1$ | $G_q$ | Vasoconstriction (raises BP), pupillary dilator contraction (mydriasis), bladder sphincter and prostate contraction |
| $\alpha_2$ | $G_i$ | Presynaptic inhibition of norepinephrine release; less sympathetic outflow (clonidine) |
| $\beta_1$ | $G_s$ | Heart rate, contractility and conduction up; renin release |
| $\beta_2$ | $G_s$ | Bronchodilation, skeletal-muscle vasodilation, uterine relaxation, glycogenolysis, potassium uptake into cells |
| $D_1$ | $G_s$ | Renal and mesenteric vasodilation |

### Agonists

- **Epinephrine** ($\alpha$, $\beta_1$, $\beta_2$): first-line treatment of anaphylaxis (typical adult dose 0.3 to 0.5 mg intramuscular into the anterolateral thigh, of the 1 mg/mL solution, for study; repeat as needed). Low doses favor $\beta$ effects, high doses add $\alpha$ vasoconstriction. Also used in cardiac arrest and added to local anesthetics.
- **Norepinephrine** ($\alpha_1 \gg \beta_1$): first-line vasopressor in septic shock. Raises blood pressure; heart rate may fall by reflex.
- **Phenylephrine** (pure $\alpha_1$): raises BP with **reflex bradycardia**; topical decongestant; mydriasis without cycloplegia.
- **Dobutamine** ($\beta_1$): inotrope for acute decompensated heart failure, and for pharmacologic stress testing.
- **Dopamine**: low dose $D_1$, medium dose $\beta_1$, high dose $\alpha_1$.
- **Isoproterenol** ($\beta_1 = \beta_2$): raises heart rate and lowers diastolic pressure; used for heart block and bradycardia in selected settings.
- **Albuterol, salmeterol, formoterol** ($\beta_2$): bronchodilators and tocolysis (terbutaline). Adverse effects: tremor, tachycardia, hypokalemia.
- **Clonidine, methyldopa** ($\alpha_2$): lower sympathetic outflow; methyldopa is used for hypertension in pregnancy. Abrupt clonidine withdrawal causes rebound hypertension.
- **Amphetamine, ephedrine, cocaine**: indirect (release or block reuptake).

### Reflex responses

A drug that raises blood pressure (phenylephrine) triggers the baroreceptor reflex, increasing vagal tone and so **slowing the heart**. A drug that lowers blood pressure by vasodilation (hydralazine, nifedipine) causes **reflex tachycardia**. The reflex is blunted by $\beta$-blockers.

### Blockers

- **Non-selective $\alpha$**: phentolamine (reversible; pheochromocytoma crisis, extravasation of vasopressors), phenoxybenzamine (irreversible). With $\alpha$ blockade, epinephrine's $\beta_2$ effect predominates and blood pressure falls: **epinephrine reversal**.
- **$\alpha_1$-selective**: prazosin, doxazosin, terazosin, tamsulosin (benign prostatic hyperplasia); adverse effects: first-dose orthostatic hypotension; intraoperative floppy iris syndrome (tamsulosin).
- **$\beta_1$-selective**: metoprolol, atenolol, bisoprolol, esmolol (ultra-short-acting IV).
- **Non-selective $\beta$**: propranolol, nadolol, timolol. **Carvedilol and labetalol** block $\alpha_1$ as well as $\beta$.

Uses: hypertension, angina, post-myocardial-infarction, heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol, carvedilol), arrhythmias, migraine prophylaxis and tremor, thyrotoxicosis symptoms. Adverse effects: bradycardia, AV block, fatigue, depression, bronchospasm (avoid non-selective agents in asthma), masking hypoglycemia symptoms, vasospasm (non-selective), and **rebound** after abrupt withdrawal. In pheochromocytoma, give an $\alpha$ blocker **before** a $\beta$ blocker; unopposed $\alpha$ stimulation can precipitate a hypertensive crisis.

### Predicting heart rate and pupils

Adrenergic agonist: pupil dilation (via $\alpha_1$) for epinephrine and phenylephrine; heart rate up with $\beta_1$ stimulation (isoproterenol, dobutamine, epinephrine), or down by reflex with pure $\alpha_1$ agonists. $\beta$ blockers slow the heart and do not change pupil size, while $\alpha_2$ agonists constrict pupils slightly (also seen with opioids) and lower heart rate.

### Calculations

**Mean arterial pressure**: $\text{MAP} = \text{DBP} + \tfrac{1}{3}(\text{SBP} - \text{DBP})$. **Infusion rate**: rate (mL/h) $=$ dose rate (mcg/min) $\times 60$ divided by concentration (mcg/mL). For weight-based doses multiply by the weight in kg first.

@example
title: Infusion pump rate for a vasopressor
problem: A patient in septic shock is to receive norepinephrine at 10 mcg/min (study example). The bag contains 8 mg in 250 mL. What pump rate in mL/h is needed? Predict the likely effect on mean arterial pressure and heart rate.
step: Concentration $= 8000\ \text{mcg}/250\ \text{mL} = 32$ mcg/mL.
step: Dose per hour $= 10 \times 60 = 600$ mcg/h.
step: Rate $= 600/32 = 18.75$ mL/h.
step: Norepinephrine acts mainly on $\alpha_1$, so mean arterial pressure rises; heart rate is little changed or slightly lower from baroreceptor reflex.
@mistakes
- Forgetting the reflex: a pure $\alpha_1$ agonist usually slows the heart.
- Mixing mg and mcg (1 mg = 1000 mcg) or minutes and hours in infusion calculations.
- Giving a $\beta$ blocker before an $\alpha$ blocker in pheochromocytoma.
- Assuming epinephrine always raises blood pressure; after $\alpha$ blockade it can lower it.

prompt: A patient's blood pressure is 120/60 mm Hg. What is the mean arterial pressure in mm Hg?

check: 60 + (120 - 60) / 3
unit: mm Hg
tol: 0.01
hint: $\text{MAP} = \text{DBP} + \frac{1}{3}(\text{SBP} - \text{DBP})$.
