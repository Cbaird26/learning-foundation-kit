@article
Questions about reasoning **within** the text ask what the passage itself commits its author to. The standard is strict: an answer must follow from the words on the page, not from your own medical knowledge, your opinions, or what seems likely.

### Stated, implied, unsupported

A **stated** claim appears in the passage. An **implied** claim must be true if the stated ones are. An **unsupported** claim may be plausible, even true in the real world, but the passage does not establish it. Suppose a passage says: "Every patient in the trial who developed the rash had taken the drug." That implies that no patient who avoided the drug developed the rash. It does **not** state that the drug causes the rash, nor that most patients who took the drug developed one.

Words of degree are a common trap. "Some" means at least one and possibly all; it does not mean "not all." "Most" means more than half. "May" asserts possibility, not frequency. An inference that quietly strengthens "some" to "most" or "may" to "does" fails.

### Conditional reasoning

Many arguments use **if-then** statements. In "If P, then Q," P is **sufficient** for Q (P guarantees Q), and Q is **necessary** for P (without Q, P cannot hold). From the statement "If P, then Q" four related forms can be built:

| Form | Statement | Follows? |
|---|---|---|
| Original | If P, then Q | given |
| Converse | If Q, then P | No |
| Inverse | If not P, then not Q | No |
| Contrapositive | If not Q, then not P | Yes |

Only the **contrapositive** is guaranteed to be equivalent to the original. Example: "If a patient is on the transplant list, then the patient has been evaluated by the committee." Contrapositive: if a patient has not been evaluated, the patient is not on the list. The converse (everyone evaluated is listed) does not follow, because some evaluated patients may be declined.

Two valid patterns are worth memorizing. **Affirming the antecedent**: If P then Q; P; therefore Q. **Denying the consequent**: If P then Q; not Q; therefore not P. Their invalid cousins, *affirming the consequent* (Q, therefore P) and *denying the antecedent* (not P, therefore not Q), are among the most common reasoning errors.

### The function of a sentence

Questions often ask why the author included a sentence. Name its job: it may give an example, concede an objection, define a term, supply evidence, state a premise, or state the conclusion. Always read it in context and ask what the paragraph would lose without it.

### A procedure for inference questions

1. Restate the passage in your own words, keeping the qualifiers.
2. For each answer, ask "must this be true if the passage is true?"
3. Reject any choice that adds a new fact, strengthens a qualifier, or reverses a conditional.

@example
title: What follows from a conditional passage
problem: (Invented passage.) "No patient was enrolled in the study unless a clinician had confirmed the diagnosis. All 120 enrolled patients completed the baseline survey." Which of these must be true? (a) Every patient with a confirmed diagnosis was enrolled. (b) A patient whose diagnosis was not confirmed was not enrolled. (c) All patients who completed the baseline survey were enrolled.
step: Translate: "No patient was enrolled unless the diagnosis was confirmed" means: if enrolled, then confirmed.
step: Statement (a) is the converse (if confirmed, then enrolled). It does not follow, because confirmed patients may have been left out for other reasons.
step: Statement (b) is the contrapositive (if not confirmed, then not enrolled). It must be true.
step: Statement (c) reverses the second sentence: the passage says enrolled patients completed the survey, not that everyone who completed it was enrolled. Only (b) must be true.

@mistakes
- Treating the converse of a conditional as if it were equivalent to the original.
- Using outside knowledge to approve an inference that the passage does not support.
- Upgrading "some" to "most," or "may" to "will," while paraphrasing.
- Choosing an answer because it sounds reasonable rather than because it must be true.

prompt: (Invented passage.) "If a wound is infected, the culture will grow bacteria. This culture grew no bacteria." What follows?
choice: The wound is infected.
choice*: The wound is not infected.
choice: The wound might be infected, but only mildly.
choice: A culture that grows bacteria means an infection.
hint: Use the pattern "if P then Q; not Q."
solution: Denying the consequent: if infected, then bacteria grow; no bacteria grew; therefore the wound is not infected (within the passage's logic).

@practice mc
prompt: A passage states: "Some of the residents who attended the workshop changed their charting habits." Which statement must be true?
choice: Most residents who attended changed their charting habits.
choice: Residents who did not attend did not change their habits.
choice*: At least one resident who attended changed charting habits.
choice: Fewer than half of attendees changed their habits.
hint: "Some" means at least one.
solution: "Some" guarantees at least one. It does not tell you the proportion, and it says nothing about non-attendees.

@practice num
prompt: A report says: "60 patients were enrolled. Every patient who dropped out was in the placebo arm. 12 patients dropped out. The placebo arm had 30 patients." How many placebo-arm patients did not drop out?
answer: 18
check: 30 - 12
hint: All dropouts came from one arm; subtract them from that arm.
solution: All 12 dropouts were placebo patients, so 30 - 12 = 18 stayed in.

@practice num
prompt: Consider the statement "If P, then Q." Four other statements are given: (1) If Q, then P. (2) If not Q, then not P. (3) If not P, then not Q. (4) If P and R, then Q. How many of these four must be true whenever the original statement is true?
answer: 2
check: sum([False, True, False, True])
hint: Check each: the converse and inverse do not follow; the contrapositive does; adding an extra condition to P keeps the guarantee.
solution: Statement (2) is the contrapositive and (4) strengthens the antecedent, so both follow. (1) is the converse and (3) is the inverse; neither follows. The answer is 2.

@practice self
prompt: (Invented passage.) "The department's discharge protocol requires that a pharmacist review every prescription before a patient leaves. This week, three patients left without pharmacist review. The department chair says the protocol was therefore violated three times." Is the chair's conclusion supported by the passage? Write three or four sentences explaining which inferences the passage supports and which it does not, including the form of the conditional used.
hint: Rewrite the protocol as "if a patient is discharged, then ...".
hint: Apply the contrapositive to the three patients; then ask whether the passage says anything about patients who did not need prescriptions.
solution: The protocol reads: if a patient leaves, then a pharmacist has reviewed every prescription. The contrapositive is: if a prescription was not reviewed, the patient should not have left. The three patients left without pharmacist review. If each had at least one prescription, each departure violated the protocol, so the chair is right. The passage does not say whether the three had prescriptions; a patient with no prescriptions would have nothing to review. The chair's claim is therefore supported only on the added assumption that all three patients had prescriptions.
rubric: Restates the protocol as an if-then statement.
rubric: Applies the contrapositive correctly.
rubric: Identifies the gap (whether the three patients had prescriptions).
rubric: Concludes with a qualified judgment tied to the text.

@quiz mc
prompt: (Invented passage.) "A drug is approved only if it passes two independent trials. Drug Z was approved." Which statement must be true?
choice: Drug Z passed exactly two trials.
choice*: Drug Z passed at least two independent trials.
choice: Every drug that passes two trials is approved.
choice: Drug Z is safer than drugs that were not approved.
hint: Translate "only if" as if approved, then passed.
solution: "Approved only if passed two trials" gives: if approved, then passed two. Z was approved, so it passed at least two. The converse (everything that passes two is approved) does not follow.

@quiz num
prompt: A report says: "Of the 80 patients on the ward, none of those over 65 received the new drug. 24 patients are over 65." What is the greatest possible number of patients on the ward who received the new drug?
answer: 56
check: 80 - 24
hint: Remove the group that definitely did not receive the drug.
solution: The 24 patients over 65 did not receive the drug, so at most 80 - 24 = 56 patients did.
