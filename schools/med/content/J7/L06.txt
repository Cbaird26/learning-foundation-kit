@article
**Weather** is the condition of the atmosphere at a certain place and time: temperature, air pressure, humidity, wind, clouds and precipitation.

### Layers and composition

Air is about **78% nitrogen**, **21% oxygen**, about 0.9% argon, and tiny amounts of carbon dioxide (about 0.04%), water vapor and other gases. The atmosphere has layers:

- **Troposphere** (surface to about 12 km): holds most of the air and nearly all weather. Temperature falls with height, about **6.5 °C per km**.
- **Stratosphere** (to about 50 km): contains the ozone layer, which absorbs harmful ultraviolet light, so the air warms with height.
- **Mesosphere** (to about 85 km): the coldest layer; most meteors burn up here.
- **Thermosphere** (above 85 km): very thin and very hot; auroras occur here.

### Pressure, wind and humidity

Air has weight. **Air pressure** is greatest at sea level and decreases as you go up. Air moves from **high pressure** to **low pressure**, which is wind. In a **high-pressure** area air sinks and warms, so skies are usually clear. In a **low-pressure** area air rises and cools, so clouds and precipitation are common. Warm air can hold more water vapor than cold air. When air cools to its **dew point**, vapor condenses into cloud droplets.

### Air masses and fronts

An **air mass** is a huge body of air with similar temperature and humidity. A **front** is the boundary where two different air masses meet. Because cold air is denser, it stays low, and warm air is forced up over it. Rising warm air cools and its water vapor condenses into clouds and rain.

- **Cold front** (blue line with triangles): cold air pushes under warm air, lifting it steeply and fast. Result: tall cumulonimbus clouds, a narrow band of heavy showers or thunderstorms, then cooler, clearer air.
- **Warm front** (red line with half circles): warm air slides gradually up and over retreating cold air. Result: widespread layered clouds and long, steady, lighter rain, followed by warmer weather.
- **Stationary front:** neither air mass advances, so cloudy, rainy weather can last for days.
- **Occluded front:** a cold front catches up to a warm front and lifts the warm air off the ground.

On a weather map, the symbols point in the direction the front is moving, and rain is usually found along and ahead of the front.

### Temperature with height

In the troposphere you can estimate temperature at altitude: $T = T_{\text{ground}} - 6.5 \times h$, with $h$ in km.

@example
title: Temperature high above the ground
problem: At the ground the temperature is 22 °C. Estimate the temperature at a height of 5 km in the troposphere, using a drop of 6.5 °C per km.
step: Find the total drop: $6.5 \times 5 = 32.5$ °C.
step: Subtract from the ground temperature: $22 - 32.5 = -10.5$ °C.
step: This is below freezing, which is why clouds at that height are often made of ice crystals.

@mistakes
- Thinking low pressure means calm, sunny weather. Low pressure usually brings clouds and rain.
- Mixing up the cold and warm front weather. Cold fronts are steep and fast with short, heavy storms; warm fronts are gradual with long, light rain.
- Confusing weather (now) with climate (long-term average).
- Believing that most of the atmosphere is oxygen. It is mostly nitrogen.

The condensing droplets grow and fall as heavy showers or thunderstorms along the front. After the front passes, cooler, drier air replaces the warm air: the temperature drops, pressure rises and the sky clears.
