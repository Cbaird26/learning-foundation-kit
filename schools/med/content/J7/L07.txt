@article
Weather is what the atmosphere is doing today. **Climate** is the **average pattern of weather over a long time**, usually at least 30 years, in one region. A single cold day does not disprove a warming climate, just as one free throw does not describe a player's season average.

### What controls climate

- **Latitude:** the tropics get more direct sunlight than the poles, so they are warmer.
- **Altitude:** air gets colder with height, so mountaintops are cold even near the equator.
- **Nearness to water:** water heats and cools slowly, so coastal places have milder temperatures than inland places at the same latitude.
- **Ocean currents:** warm currents such as the Gulf Stream warm the coasts they pass.
- **Mountains:** wind forced up the windward side of a mountain cools and drops rain. The leeward side is dry, a **rain shadow**.
- **Prevailing winds:** they bring in air with the properties of the region where it formed.

### The greenhouse effect

Earth is warmed by sunlight. The warm surface gives off infrared (heat) radiation. **Greenhouse gases**, such as water vapor, carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$), methane and nitrous oxide, absorb some of that radiation and send part of it back toward the surface. This natural **greenhouse effect** keeps the average temperature near 15 °C instead of about -18 °C. The problem is not the effect but that **human activities add extra greenhouse gases**, mainly by burning coal, oil and natural gas, clearing forests and farming. Before the Industrial Revolution the $\text{CO}_2$ level was about 280 parts per million (ppm); it is now above 420 ppm.

### Evidence of change

Global average surface temperature has risen by at least about 1.1 °C since the late 1800s (about 1.1 °C for 2011–2020 in the IPCC AR6 report, and recent years have been warmer). Other evidence includes shrinking glaciers and Arctic sea ice, rising sea level (from melting land ice and from water expanding as it warms), and gas bubbles trapped in ice cores that record past $\text{CO}_2$ levels. Scientists compare natural causes, such as changes in the Sun and volcanoes, with the observed pattern, and the warming matches the rise in greenhouse gases. Responses include cutting emissions (**mitigation**) and preparing for effects such as stronger heat waves and higher seas (**adaptation**).

### Using data

An average (mean) is the sum of the values divided by how many there are. A percent change is
$$\frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100\%.$$

@example
title: Averaging yearly temperatures
problem: A town's average annual temperatures over five years were 12.1, 12.4, 11.9, 12.6 and 12.3 °C. What is the five-year mean?
step: Add the values: $12.1 + 12.4 + 11.9 + 12.6 + 12.3 = 61.3$.
step: Divide by the number of values: $\frac{61.3}{5} = 12.26$.
step: The mean is about $12.3$ °C. Individual years differ from it, but the mean describes the typical climate.

@mistakes
- Using a cold day or a snowstorm as proof that climate is not warming. One day is weather, not climate.
- Saying the greenhouse effect is bad in itself. Without it Earth would be frozen; the concern is the extra gases.
- Confusing the ozone hole (a different problem involving ultraviolet light) with the greenhouse effect.
- Calculating a percent change relative to the new value instead of the old value.

@practice num
prompt: The atmospheric carbon dioxide level rose from 280 ppm before the Industrial Revolution to about 420 ppm. What is the percent increase?
answer: 50
check: (420 - 280) / 280 * 100
unit: %
tol: 1%
hint: Find the change first, then divide by the original value.
solution: Change $= 420 - 280 = 140$ ppm. Percent change $= \frac{140}{280} \times 100 = 50\%$.

@practice self
prompt: Explain in your own words why adding carbon dioxide to the atmosphere warms Earth, and why scientists say this is different from natural weather variation. Include the words absorb, infrared and average.
hint: Start with what happens to sunlight, then what the warm surface gives off, then what greenhouse gases do to it.
solution: Sunlight warms Earth's surface, and the surface gives off infrared radiation. Greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide absorb part of this infrared radiation and re-emit some of it back toward the surface, so less heat escapes to space. Adding more of these gases increases this effect, so the average temperature rises. Weather varies from day to day and year to year, but climate change is a long-term shift in the average that matches the rise in greenhouse gases and appears in many measurements (temperature, ice, sea level).

rubric: Connects more gas with a stronger effect and a higher average temperature.
rubric: Distinguishes a long-term average shift from day-to-day weather.

@quiz num
prompt: In 1958 the atmospheric carbon dioxide level was about 315 ppm. It is now about 420 ppm. By what percent did it increase?
answer: 33.3
check: (420 - 315) / 315 * 100
unit: %
tol: 1%
hint: Percent change is the change divided by the starting value, times 100.
solution: Change $= 105$ ppm. $\frac{105}{315} \times 100 = 33.3\%$.
