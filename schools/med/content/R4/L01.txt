@article
Prenatal care is a series of planned contacts whose purpose is to find and treat problems early: dating the pregnancy accurately, screening for conditions that change management, immunizing, and counseling. The structure is easy to learn if you tie each element to the **gestational age (GA)** at which it is useful.

### Dating the pregnancy

Gestational age is counted from the **first day of the last menstrual period (LMP)**, so at conception the pregnancy is already about 2 weeks old. A term pregnancy is 37 0/7 to 41 6/7 weeks; "full term" is 39 0/7 to 40 6/7.

**Naegele's rule** gives the estimated due date (EDD): add 7 days to the first day of the LMP, subtract 3 months, and add 1 year. It assumes a regular 28-day cycle.

The most accurate dating tool is **first-trimester ultrasound crown–rump length (CRL)**. Redate by ultrasound only if the discrepancy from LMP dating exceeds a threshold that grows with GA (ACOG): more than 5 days up to 8 6/7 weeks, more than 7 days from 9 0/7 to 15 6/7 weeks, more than 10 days from 16 0/7 to 21 6/7, more than 14 days from 22 0/7 to 27 6/7, and more than 21 days from 28 weeks. Once the due date is set, it is not changed again.

### Visit schedule

For an uncomplicated pregnancy the traditional schedule is:

| Gestational age | Visit frequency |
|---|---|
| Initial visit (ideally by 8-10 weeks) to 28 weeks | every 4 weeks |
| 28 to 36 weeks | every 2 weeks |
| 36 weeks to delivery | weekly |

Each visit records blood pressure, weight, urine protein if indicated, and fetal heart tones (Doppler is usually audible at 10-12 weeks). From about 20 to 36 weeks the **fundal height** in centimeters roughly equals the weeks of gestation (within 2 cm). Quickening, the first maternal perception of movement, occurs at about 18-20 weeks in a first pregnancy and a little earlier in later ones.

### Initial laboratory tests

Blood type, Rh status and antibody screen; CBC; rubella and varicella immunity; hepatitis B surface antigen; HIV; syphilis; urine culture (asymptomatic bacteriuria is treated in pregnancy because it can progress to pyelonephritis); chlamydia and gonorrhea testing; and hepatitis C screening in every pregnancy. Cervical cancer screening is given if due. Carrier screening (for example cystic fibrosis and spinal muscular atrophy) is offered, and early diabetes testing is done for those with risk factors such as obesity.

### Aneuploidy and anomaly screening

Every patient is offered screening or diagnostic testing regardless of age:
- **First-trimester combined screen** at 11-14 weeks: nuchal translucency, free beta-hCG and PAPP-A. In trisomy 21 hCG is high and PAPP-A low.
- **Cell-free DNA** from 10 weeks: the most sensitive screen for trisomy 21, 18 and 13, but it is still a screening test, so a positive result is confirmed by diagnostic testing.
- **Quad screen** at 15-22 weeks: AFP, hCG, unconjugated estriol and inhibin A. Trisomy 21 gives low AFP, high hCG, low estriol and high inhibin A; trisomy 18 gives all low. A high AFP suggests a neural tube or abdominal wall defect, multiple gestation or a dating error.
- **Diagnostic tests:** chorionic villus sampling at 10-13 weeks, amniocentesis from about 15 weeks.
- **Anatomy ultrasound** at 18-22 weeks.

### Later screening and prevention

- **Gestational diabetes** screening at 24-28 weeks (week 4).
- **Rh immune globulin** (typically 300 mcg IM) to Rh-negative, unsensitized patients at 28 weeks, within 72 hours of delivery of an Rh-positive infant, and after bleeding, abortion, ectopic pregnancy or invasive procedures.
- **Tdap** at 27-36 weeks of every pregnancy; **inactivated influenza** vaccine in any trimester during flu season; **RSV** vaccine at 32-36 weeks if the pregnancy falls in the late-summer to winter season. Live vaccines (MMR, varicella) are contraindicated; give them postpartum if the patient is not immune.
- **Group B streptococcus** rectovaginal culture at 36 0/7 to 37 6/7 weeks (week 2).
- **Low-dose aspirin** 81 mg daily, started between 12 and 28 weeks (ideally before 16), for patients at high risk of preeclampsia (week 3).

### Counseling

Folic acid 400 mcg daily (4 mg for a prior neural tube defect pregnancy), no alcohol or tobacco, limited caffeine (under 200 mg/day), avoiding high-mercury fish and unpasteurized dairy, and about 150 minutes of moderate exercise per week. Recommended total weight gain depends on prepregnancy BMI: underweight 28-40 lb, normal 25-35 lb, overweight 15-25 lb, obesity 11-20 lb.

title: Dating a pregnancy
problem: A patient's LMP began on August 12, 2025 and her cycles are regular. At her first visit a CRL scan shows 8 weeks 3 days on a day when LMP dating gives 7 weeks 4 days. What is her EDD, what was her GA by LMP on November 28, 2025, and which dating do you use?
step: Naegele's rule: add 7 days to August 12 to get August 19, subtract 3 months to get May 19, add 1 year. The EDD by LMP is May 19, 2026.
step: Count days from August 12 to November 28: 31 days to September 12, 30 more to October 12, 31 more to November 12 (92 days), then 16 more to November 28. The total is 108 days.
step: 108 days divided by 7 is 15 weeks with 3 days left over, so GA by LMP on November 28 is 15 weeks 3 days.
step: For the dating question, the scan GA is 8 weeks 3 days and the LMP GA is 7 weeks 4 days, a 6-day difference. Up to 8 6/7 weeks the threshold is more than 5 days, so 6 days exceeds it and the pregnancy is redated by the CRL.

@mistakes
- Counting gestational age from conception instead of from the first day of the LMP.
- Redating by ultrasound for any difference, instead of only when the discrepancy exceeds the threshold for that GA.
- Treating cell-free DNA as diagnostic: a positive result needs CVS or amniocentesis, and the test never replaces the anatomy ultrasound.
- Giving live vaccines (MMR, varicella) during pregnancy, or forgetting that Tdap is given in every pregnancy.

@quiz num
prompt: In a 30-year-old, cell-free DNA screening for trisomy 21 has a sensitivity of 99% and a specificity of 99.9%. The prevalence in her risk group is 0.2%. What is the positive predictive value of a positive result, as a percentage?
answer: 66.5
check: 100 * (0.002 * 0.99) / (0.002 * 0.99 + (1 - 0.002) * (1 - 0.999))
tol: 1%
unit: %
hint: Use a population of 100,000 and count the true positives and the false positives.
hint: True positives = 100,000 x 0.002 x 0.99; false positives = 100,000 x 0.998 x 0.001.
solution: True positives 198; false positives about 99.8. PPV = 198 / (198 + 99.8) = 66.5%. Even an excellent screen has a modest PPV when the condition is uncommon, which is why a positive result is confirmed with diagnostic testing.
