@article
Always begin bleeding in pregnancy with three questions: **how many weeks, is the patient stable, and is the pregnancy intrauterine?** Check blood type and Rh in every case, because Rh immune globulin is indicated for unsensitized Rh-negative patients with bleeding, abortion or ectopic pregnancy.

### Early pregnancy loss (before 20 weeks)

About 10-20% of recognized pregnancies end in miscarriage, most often from fetal aneuploidy (trisomy 16 is the commonest single abnormality). Classify by the cervix and the tissue passed:

| Term | Cervical os | Findings |
|---|---|---|
| Threatened | closed | bleeding, viable intrauterine pregnancy |
| Inevitable | open | bleeding, cramping, no tissue passed yet |
| Incomplete | open | some products passed, some retained |
| Complete | closed | all products passed, empty uterus on ultrasound |
| Missed | closed | nonviable embryo, often no symptoms |
| Septic | any | fever, foul discharge, uterine tenderness |

Ultrasound diagnoses failure when a crown-rump length of 7 mm or more has no cardiac activity, or a mean sac diameter of 25 mm or more has no embryo. Management options for nonviable pregnancy are expectant management, **misoprostol** (alone or after mifepristone), or uterine aspiration (dilation and curettage); the patient chooses, except that instability, infection or heavy bleeding requires evacuation. Septic abortion needs broad-spectrum antibiotics and evacuation. Recurrent loss (two or more) prompts evaluation for antiphospholipid syndrome, uterine anomalies, parental karyotype and thyroid disease. Painless second-trimester dilation suggests cervical insufficiency, treated with cerclage.

### Ectopic pregnancy

Implantation outside the uterine cavity, most often in the **ampulla of the fallopian tube**. Risk factors are prior ectopic, pelvic inflammatory disease, tubal surgery, assisted reproduction, smoking, and a pregnancy that occurs with an IUD in place. Classic presentation is amenorrhea, abdominal pain and vaginal bleeding; rupture causes hemoperitoneum, shock and shoulder pain. Every reproductive-aged patient with pain and bleeding gets a pregnancy test.

Diagnosis: transvaginal ultrasound plus serial quantitative beta-hCG. An intrauterine gestational sac should be visible when hCG exceeds the **discriminatory level** (about 1,500-3,500 mIU/mL, the conservative figure being 3,500). An empty uterus above that level, or a rise in hCG of less than the expected minimum (about 49% in 48 hours when starting under 1,500) suggests ectopic or failing pregnancy.

**Treatment:** a hemodynamically stable patient with an unruptured ectopic, no fetal cardiac activity, normal renal and hepatic function, and not breastfeeding may receive **methotrexate** 50 mg/m$^2$ IM (body surface area is used for dosing), with hCG on days 4 and 7 expecting a fall of at least 15% (otherwise repeat the dose or operate). A hemodynamically unstable or ruptured ectopic requires laparoscopic or open **salpingectomy** (or salpingostomy). Rh-negative patients receive Rh immune globulin.

### Gestational trophoblastic disease

A **complete mole** is 46,XX (rarely XY) with all chromosomes paternal, formed when an empty ovum is fertilized: no fetal tissue, diffuse villous swelling ("snowstorm" or "cluster of grapes" on ultrasound), markedly elevated beta-hCG, a uterus larger than dates, hyperemesis, early preeclampsia, hyperthyroidism (hCG cross-reacts with TSH receptors) and theca lutein cysts. A **partial mole** is triploid (69,XXY), with a fetus or fetal parts and a modest hCG rise. Treatment is suction curettage, with Rh immune globulin as indicated. Follow hCG weekly until undetectable and then monthly for several months, and use reliable contraception so that rising hCG is not confused with a new pregnancy. A plateau or rise indicates **gestational trophoblastic neoplasia** (invasive mole or choriocarcinoma), treated with methotrexate or dactinomycin; staging includes chest imaging because of lung metastases.

### Bleeding in the third trimester

| Feature | Placenta previa | Placental abruption |
|---|---|---|
| Pain | painless | painful |
| Blood | bright red | dark, may be concealed |
| Uterus | soft, relaxed | tender, rigid, hypertonic |
| Risk factors | prior cesarean, multiparity, prior previa | hypertension and preeclampsia, cocaine, tobacco, trauma, prior abruption |
| Diagnosis | ultrasound | clinical; ultrasound is insensitive |
| Complication | accreta spectrum, hemorrhage | DIC, fetal distress, fetal demise |

**Do not perform a digital vaginal exam** until previa is excluded by ultrasound. Previa with no bleeding is delivered by cesarean at 36 0/7 to 37 6/7 weeks; with active bleeding, stabilize and deliver. Abruption requires stabilization, blood products and delivery (vaginal if both are stable, cesarean for fetal compromise). **Vasa previa** causes painless bleeding at rupture of the membranes with fetal bradycardia (fetal blood loss). **Uterine rupture** causes sudden pain, loss of fetal station and fetal bradycardia, especially with a trial of labor after cesarean.

@example
title: Incomplete abortion
problem: A 27-year-old at 9 weeks has heavy bleeding and cramping and passed tissue at home. She is stable. The cervical os is open and ultrasound shows retained products. She is O-negative with a negative antibody screen. What do you do?
step: Open os with some tissue passed and some retained is an incomplete abortion.
step: She is stable, so options are misoprostol, expectant management or uterine aspiration; aspiration is quickest if bleeding is heavy.
step: Rh-negative status means she needs Rh immune globulin to prevent sensitization.
step: Counsel about follow-up, warning signs of infection or hemorrhage, and contraception.

@example
title: Ruptured ectopic pregnancy
problem: A 24-year-old with 7 weeks of amenorrhea has sudden lower abdominal pain, heart rate 124/min, blood pressure 82/50 mmHg, a positive pregnancy test and free fluid in Morison's pouch. What is the next step?
step: Pain, positive pregnancy test, shock and free fluid imply a ruptured ectopic until proven otherwise.
step: Instability excludes methotrexate; she needs resuscitation with two large-bore IV lines and blood products.
step: Proceed directly to surgery (laparoscopy or laparotomy) with salpingectomy; do not delay for further imaging.

@mistakes
- Performing a digital cervical exam in a patient with third-trimester bleeding before ultrasound has excluded placenta previa.
- Assuming absent abdominal pain excludes abruption; concealed hemorrhage can present with back pain or a rigid uterus alone.
- Giving methotrexate to a hemodynamically unstable patient or to a patient with an intrauterine pregnancy.
- Forgetting Rh immune globulin in an Rh-negative patient with bleeding, miscarriage or ectopic pregnancy.
