## Week-by-week key ideas

- **Week 1, prenatal care:** gestational age counts from the LMP; Naegele's rule (add 7 days, subtract 3 months, add 1 year); redate only if ultrasound differs beyond the threshold for that gestational age. Visits every 4, 2 and 1 weeks. Rh immune globulin at 28 weeks; Tdap at 27-36 weeks; GBS culture at 36 0/7-37 6/7; glucose test at 24-28 weeks; aspirin for high risk.
- **Week 2, labor:** three stages; cardinal movements; Bishop score; 200 Montevideo units; late, variable and early decelerations; Category I-III; arrest of labor definitions; GBS prophylaxis with penicillin G.
- **Week 3, hypertension:** definitions, severe features, magnesium sulfate, severe-range blood pressure treatment, delivery timing, no ACE inhibitors.
- **Week 4, medical problems:** hPL and insulin resistance, GDM screening and treatment, delivery timing, thyroid drugs, low-molecular-weight heparin instead of warfarin.
- **Week 5, bleeding:** miscarriage types, ectopic (methotrexate versus surgery), moles, previa versus abruption.
- **Week 6, preterm labor and PROM:** steroids, magnesium, tocolysis for 48 hours, latency antibiotics, delivery at 34 weeks for PPROM, chorioamnionitis requires delivery.
- **Week 7, postpartum:** the four Ts, uterotonic ladder and contraindications, endometritis, blues versus depression versus psychosis.
- **Week 8, menstrual disorders:** amenorrhea workup, PCOS, PALM-COEIN.
- **Week 9, contraception:** effectiveness tiers, estrogen contraindications, emergency contraception, Pearl Index.
- **Week 10, infertility and menopause:** semen analysis, HSG, letrozole, IVF; estrogen plus progestogen with a uterus.
- **Week 11, gynecologic cancers:** screening intervals, ASCCP follow-up, postmenopausal bleeding, tumor markers, RMI.
- **Week 12, pelvic pain and infection:** pregnancy test first, PID treatment, vaginitis, torsion, endometriosis.

## Common errors

Counting from conception; using proteinuria to define severity; giving tocolytics to patients with chorioamnionitis, abruption or severe preeclampsia; giving methylergonovine for hypertension or carboprost for asthma; forgetting a pregnancy test; a digital exam in third-trimester bleeding; prescribing estrogen-only therapy with a uterus; and overlooking Rh immune globulin.

@exam self
prompt: A 31-year-old G2P1 with severe preeclampsia (blood pressure 168/108 mmHg on admission) delivers vaginally at 36 weeks after a 20-hour oxytocin-augmented labor. She receives magnesium sulfate. Twenty minutes after delivery she has heavy bleeding, a boggy fundus, heart rate 122/min and blood pressure 90/52 mmHg. She has no asthma. Describe your stepwise management, justify each uterotonic you choose or avoid, and state two other causes to exclude if the bleeding continues despite a firm uterus.
hint: Use the four Ts and a systematic order: help, access, massage and oxytocin, then second-line drugs, tranexamic acid, inspection, and procedures.
hint: Think about which uterotonic is dangerous with hypertension and which with asthma; magnesium also relaxes the uterus.
solution: She has uterine atony (tone) with risk factors of prolonged augmented labor and magnesium use. Call for help, start the hemorrhage protocol, place two large-bore IV lines, send CBC, type and cross-match, fibrinogen and coagulation studies, empty the bladder, and begin quantitative blood loss and shock-index monitoring. Perform bimanual uterine massage and give oxytocin. If bleeding continues, give carboprost 250 micrograms IM (no asthma) or misoprostol 800-1,000 micrograms; avoid methylergonovine because of her severe hypertension. Give tranexamic acid 1 g IV. Examine for lacerations and retained tissue. If atony persists: balloon tamponade, embolization or surgery, and transfuse early with balanced products. Magnesium contributes to atony but is still needed for seizure prophylaxis for 24 hours postpartum, so continue it with monitoring for toxicity, especially while perfusion and urine output are reduced. If the uterus is firm, look for trauma (lacerations, hematoma, inversion) and tissue (retained placenta, accreta), and for thrombin disorders (DIC from preeclampsia or HELLP).
rubric: Starts with call for help, IV access, laboratory tests, massage and oxytocin before second-line drugs.
rubric: Chooses carboprost or misoprostol and explicitly avoids methylergonovine because of hypertension.
rubric: Includes tranexamic acid, inspection for lacerations and retained tissue, and escalation to tamponade, embolization or surgery with transfusion.
rubric: Names trauma, tissue and thrombin (for example DIC or HELLP) as causes to exclude when the uterus is firm.
