@article
Labor is the process by which regular contractions cause progressive cervical change and expulsion of the fetus. Three factors interact, the "three Ps": **power** (contractions and maternal pushing), **passenger** (fetal size, lie, presentation, position) and **passage** (the bony pelvis and soft tissues).

### Stages of labor

- **First stage:** onset of regular contractions to full dilation (10 cm). The **latent phase** involves slow dilation and effacement; the **active phase** is considered to start at about 6 cm (ACOG), after which dilation accelerates.
- **Second stage:** full dilation to delivery of the infant.
- **Third stage:** delivery of the infant to delivery of the placenta, normally within 30 minutes. Signs of separation are a gush of blood, lengthening of the cord and a firm, globular fundus. Oxytocin is given routinely after delivery to prevent hemorrhage.
- A fourth stage (the first 1-2 hours after delivery) is a period of close monitoring for bleeding.

**Station** is the position of the presenting part relative to the ischial spines, in centimeters, from -5 to +5; 0 means the head is engaged. **Effacement** is cervical thinning (percent); **dilation** is the opening in centimeters.

### Cardinal movements (vertex presentation)

In order: **engagement, descent, flexion, internal rotation, extension, restitution (external rotation), expulsion**. Flexion presents the smallest head diameter, internal rotation brings the occiput anterior under the pubic symphysis, extension delivers the head, restitution realigns the head with the shoulders, and the anterior then posterior shoulder deliver.

### Assessing the cervix and the contractions

The **Bishop score** (0-13) sums five items: dilation (0 cm = 0, 1-2 = 1, 3-4 = 2, 5 or more = 3), effacement (0-30% = 0, 40-50% = 1, 60-70% = 2, 80% or more = 3), station (-3 = 0, -2 = 1, -1 or 0 = 2, +1 or +2 = 3), consistency (firm 0, medium 1, soft 2) and position (posterior 0, midposition 1, anterior 2). A score of 8 or more predicts a successful induction; a low score suggests cervical ripening (for example prostaglandins or a balloon catheter) before oxytocin.

Contraction strength is quantified in **Montevideo units (MVU)**: for each contraction in a 10-minute window, subtract baseline uterine tone from the peak pressure, and add them up. At least 200 MVU is considered adequate labor.

### Fetal heart rate monitoring

Normal baseline is 110-160 beats per minute with **moderate variability** (6-25 bpm), a marker of an intact fetal autonomic nervous system and an acid-base status that is probably normal. Decelerations are classified by their relation to the contraction:

| Pattern | Shape | Mechanism | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early | gradual, mirrors the contraction | head compression | none needed |
| Variable | abrupt drop, variable timing | umbilical cord compression | reposition; amnioinfusion if recurrent |
| Late | gradual, nadir after the peak | uteroplacental insufficiency | lateral position, IV fluid, stop oxytocin |

Tracings are sorted into **Category I** (normal baseline, moderate variability, no late or variable decelerations), **Category III** (absent variability with recurrent late or variable decelerations or bradycardia, or a sinusoidal pattern; abnormal, needs prompt delivery if not corrected) and **Category II** (everything else, evaluated and resuscitated in utero).

### Abnormal labor

First-stage arrest is defined, once at least 6 cm dilated with ruptured membranes, as no change for 4 hours despite adequate contractions or 6 hours with oxytocin and inadequate contractions; cesarean is then indicated. The second stage is prolonged if it exceeds 3 hours in a nulliparous patient (4 with an epidural) and 2 hours in a multiparous patient (3 with an epidural), though continued progress allows longer.

**Shoulder dystocia** (the anterior shoulder lodges behind the symphysis; "turtle sign") is managed with the McRoberts maneuver and suprapubic pressure, then rotational maneuvers or delivery of the posterior arm. Perineal lacerations are graded first (skin), second (perineal muscle), third (anal sphincter) and fourth (rectal mucosa).

### Group B streptococcus

GBS colonizes the rectovaginal tract and can cause early-onset neonatal sepsis. Patients are screened by rectovaginal culture at 36 0/7 to 37 6/7 weeks. Intrapartum prophylaxis with IV **penicillin G** (cefazolin if allergy is low-risk) is given for a positive culture, GBS bacteriuria in this pregnancy, a prior infant with GBS disease, or unknown status with labor before 37 weeks, rupture of membranes of 18 hours or more, or intrapartum fever of 38.0 degrees C or higher. It is not needed for a scheduled cesarean with intact membranes and no labor.

title: Should this cervix be ripened before oxytocin?
problem: A 24-year-old nulliparous woman at 41 weeks is admitted for induction. Her cervix is 2 cm dilated, 40% effaced, station -2, firm and posterior. Calculate the Bishop score and decide on management.
step: Dilation 2 cm scores 1; effacement 40% scores 1; station -2 scores 1.
step: A firm cervix scores 0 and a posterior position scores 0.
step: The total is 1 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 0 = 3. A score below 6 (certainly below 8) is unfavorable.
step: An unfavorable cervix is first ripened with a prostaglandin or a mechanical balloon, then oxytocin is started once the cervix is favorable.

@mistakes
- Calling a deceleration "late" because it occurs late in the labor rather than because its nadir follows the contraction peak.
- Using the 4-cm threshold for the active phase and diagnosing arrest too early; arrest is not considered before 6 cm.
- Giving GBS prophylaxis to a patient who has a scheduled cesarean with intact membranes and no labor.
- Ordering a cesarean for a prolonged second stage while the patient is still making progress under a reassuring tracing.
