@article
For any woman of reproductive age with pelvic pain, the first test is a **pregnancy test**, because ectopic pregnancy is the diagnosis you cannot miss. Then separate gynecologic from non-gynecologic causes, acute from chronic, and infectious from structural.

### Acute pelvic pain

- **Ectopic pregnancy:** amenorrhea, pain, bleeding, positive hCG (week 5).
- **Pelvic inflammatory disease and tubo-ovarian abscess**
- **Ovarian torsion:** sudden severe unilateral pain with nausea and vomiting, usually with an ovarian mass (dermoid cyst, cystadenoma, stimulated ovaries). Doppler ultrasound may show normal flow; **suspicion alone justifies laparoscopy** to detorse and, where possible, preserve the ovary.
- **Ruptured or hemorrhagic corpus luteum cyst:** sudden pain mid or late cycle with free fluid.
- **Appendicitis, urolithiasis, diverticulitis**
- **Degenerating leiomyoma**, especially in pregnancy.

### Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)

PID is ascending infection of the upper genital tract (endometritis, salpingitis, tubo-ovarian abscess), caused by *Chlamydia trachomatis* and *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* with polymicrobial anaerobes. **Diagnosis is clinical**, because delayed treatment harms fertility: treat empirically a sexually active young woman with pelvic or lower abdominal pain and **cervical motion, uterine or adnexal tenderness**, when no other cause is found. Supporting findings are fever, mucopurulent discharge, white cells on wet mount, and elevated ESR or CRP. Test for gonorrhea and chlamydia, HIV and syphilis, and exclude pregnancy.

**Outpatient treatment** (CDC): ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once plus doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily for 14 days plus metronidazole 500 mg orally twice daily for 14 days (typical adult doses for study). **Hospitalize** for pregnancy, tubo-ovarian abscess, severe illness or inability to tolerate oral therapy, or failure of outpatient therapy; give IV cefotetan or cefoxitin plus doxycycline. Treat partners and do not remove an IUD unless there is no improvement. Complications: tubo-ovarian abscess, **Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome** (perihepatitis with right upper quadrant pain and violin-string adhesions), infertility from tubal scarring, ectopic pregnancy and chronic pelvic pain. Screen all sexually active women under 25 annually for chlamydia and gonorrhea.

### Vaginitis

| | Bacterial vaginosis | Candidiasis | Trichomoniasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discharge | thin, gray, fishy | thick, white, "cottage cheese" | frothy, yellow-green |
| Vaginal pH | above 4.5 | 4.5 or less (normal) | above 4.5 |
| Microscopy | clue cells (Amsel: 3 of 4: discharge, pH, clue cells, positive whiff test) | budding yeast and pseudohyphae on KOH | motile flagellated protozoa |
| Treatment | metronidazole | fluconazole 150 mg once or topical azole | metronidazole, with partner treatment |

Bacterial vaginosis results from a loss of lactobacilli and overgrowth of *Gardnerella* and anaerobes; it is a risk factor for preterm birth and is treated in symptomatic pregnant patients. Candida occurs with diabetes, antibiotics and immunosuppression. Trichomonas is sexually transmitted; "strawberry cervix" is characteristic though uncommon.

### Chronic pelvic pain and structural causes

**Endometriosis** is functional endometrial tissue outside the uterus (retrograde menstruation, coelomic metaplasia). It causes cyclic pelvic pain, dysmenorrhea, deep dyspareunia, dyschezia, infertility, uterosacral nodularity and ovarian **endometriomas** ("chocolate cysts"). Laparoscopy with visualization (and biopsy) is definitive, although empiric treatment is acceptable. Treatment: NSAIDs and hormonal suppression (continuous combined oral contraceptives, progestins, levonorgestrel IUD, GnRH agonists or antagonists), with excisional surgery for severe disease or infertility. **Adenomyosis** is endometrial tissue in the myometrium: a uniformly enlarged, boggy uterus with heavy painful menses in a multiparous woman in her 40s; hysterectomy is definitive. Functional ovarian cysts are common and usually resolve; a simple cyst under 5 cm in a premenopausal woman needs no follow-up. Other causes: adhesions, fibroids, interstitial cystitis, irritable bowel syndrome and pelvic floor dysfunction.

### Cervical screening reminder

Apply the intervals from week 11: age 21-29 cytology every 3 years; 30-65 cytology every 3 years, HPV alone every 5 years or cotesting every 5 years; stop after 65 with adequate negative history.

@example
title: Suspected PID
problem: A 21-year-old woman presents with 3 days of lower abdominal pain, fever of 38.4 degrees C and a mucopurulent cervical discharge. Cervical motion tenderness and bilateral adnexal tenderness are present. Urine hCG is negative. She is tolerating oral intake. What is the treatment?
step: She is sexually active with pelvic pain and cervical motion tenderness, and pregnancy is excluded, so PID is the working diagnosis. Start treatment without waiting for results.
step: She is stable and tolerating oral intake, so outpatient treatment is appropriate: ceftriaxone IM once, plus doxycycline and metronidazole for 14 days.
step: Test for gonorrhea, chlamydia, HIV and syphilis, treat partners and arrange follow-up in 3 days.
step: If she worsens or is not improving, admit and image for a tubo-ovarian abscess.

@example
title: Sudden unilateral pain with a mass
problem: A 25-year-old woman develops sudden severe right lower quadrant pain with vomiting. hCG is negative. Ultrasound shows a 7-cm right ovarian mass with arterial Doppler flow present. What is the next step?
step: Sudden severe pain and a large ovarian mass suggest torsion.
step: Normal Doppler flow does not exclude torsion because of the dual blood supply and partial twisting.
step: Proceed to laparoscopy to untwist (detorse) the ovary and treat the cause; avoid oophorectomy unless the tissue is clearly necrotic.

@mistakes
- Excluding ovarian torsion because Doppler flow is present.
- Delaying PID treatment until culture results are available.
- Removing an IUD routinely in uncomplicated PID.
- Omitting a pregnancy test before attributing pelvic pain to a gynecologic infection.
