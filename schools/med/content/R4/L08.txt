@article
A normal menstrual cycle is 24-38 days from the first day of one menses to the first day of the next, with bleeding lasting up to 8 days and a cycle-to-cycle variation of a few days. Understanding the hormones predicts every disorder.

### The cycle

In the **follicular phase** FSH recruits follicles; rising estradiol from the dominant follicle thickens the endometrium (proliferative phase). A sustained high estradiol triggers the **LH surge**, which causes ovulation about 36 hours later. In the **luteal phase** the corpus luteum secretes progesterone, which makes the endometrium secretory; without pregnancy the corpus luteum fails after about 14 days, hormones fall and menses begins. The luteal phase is constant at about 14 days, so ovulation occurs about 14 days before the next menses, whatever the cycle length.

### Amenorrhea

**Primary amenorrhea** is no menses by age 15 (or 13 without secondary sexual characteristics, or about 3 years after breast development began; some criteria use 5 years). **Secondary amenorrhea** is absence of menses for 3 months in a woman who was regular (6 months if previously irregular).

**Step one in either case is a pregnancy test.** For secondary amenorrhea then check TSH, prolactin and FSH:
- **Hyperprolactinemia** (pituitary adenoma, hypothyroidism, drugs): galactorrhea possible; MRI of the pituitary; treat with dopamine agonists (cabergoline).
- **Primary ovarian insufficiency** (menopause before age 40): high FSH on two measurements, low estradiol; evaluate with karyotype and fragile X premutation testing; give hormone therapy.
- **Hypothalamic amenorrhea** (stress, weight loss, excessive exercise, eating disorders): low FSH, LH and estradiol.
- **PCOS:** below.
- **Asherman syndrome** (intrauterine adhesions after curettage): normal hormones, no withdrawal bleeding after estrogen then progestin; diagnosed with hysteroscopy or saline sonography.
- **Sheehan syndrome:** pituitary necrosis after postpartum hemorrhage.

For primary amenorrhea, examine for breasts and a uterus:
- **Turner syndrome (45,X):** short stature, absent breast development, high FSH, streak gonads.
- **Müllerian agenesis (Mayer-Rokitansky-Kuster-Hauser):** normal breasts and pubic hair, 46,XX, blind vaginal pouch, absent uterus, normal testosterone.
- **Androgen insensitivity (46,XY):** breasts but sparse pubic and axillary hair, absent uterus, testes and male-range testosterone.
- **Imperforate hymen or transverse vaginal septum:** cyclic pain with bulging blue membrane and hematocolpos.
- **Kallmann syndrome:** low GnRH with anosmia.

### Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)

The Rotterdam criteria require two of three: oligo- or anovulation, clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism (hirsutism, acne, raised testosterone) and polycystic-appearing ovaries (20 or more follicles per ovary, or an elevated AMH where permitted), after excluding thyroid disease, hyperprolactinemia and nonclassic congenital adrenal hyperplasia (17-hydroxyprogesterone). Insulin resistance drives ovarian androgen production, so obesity, acanthosis nigricans, diabetes and dyslipidemia are common. Chronic anovulation causes unopposed estrogen and a risk of endometrial hyperplasia and cancer. Management: lifestyle and weight loss; **combined oral contraceptives** (cycle regulation, endometrial protection, treat hirsutism) with spironolactone for hirsutism; metformin for metabolic features; **letrozole** (first-line for ovulation induction in infertility, better than clomiphene).

### Abnormal uterine bleeding (AUB)

AUB is bleeding that is abnormal in frequency, regularity, duration or volume. **PALM-COEIN** separates structural causes (**P**olyp, **A**denomyosis, **L**eiomyoma, **M**alignancy and hyperplasia) from nonstructural ones (**C**oagulopathy, **O**vulatory dysfunction, **E**ndometrial, **I**atrogenic, **N**ot classified). Ovulatory dysfunction is the commonest cause in adolescents and perimenopausal women (anovulation leads to unopposed estrogen). In a patient with heavy bleeding from menarche, test for von Willebrand disease.

**Workup:** pregnancy test first, then CBC, TSH, and a transvaginal ultrasound. Perform **endometrial biopsy** for women aged 45 or older, and for younger women with risk factors for unopposed estrogen (obesity, PCOS), persistent bleeding or failed medical treatment. In postmenopausal bleeding, either transvaginal ultrasound (endometrium of 4 mm or less is reassuring) or biopsy is acceptable.

**Treatment** of a hemodynamically stable patient: levonorgestrel IUD, combined oral contraceptives, oral progestin or tranexamic acid; NSAIDs also decrease blood loss. Acute severe bleeding is treated with IV conjugated estrogens, high-dose combined oral contraceptives or tranexamic acid, and rarely uterine tamponade or curettage. Procedures (endometrial ablation, myomectomy, uterine artery embolization, hysterectomy) are used when medical therapy fails.

**Leiomyomas (fibroids)** cause heavy menses and bulk symptoms; they are estrogen-sensitive and shrink after menopause.

### Dysmenorrhea

**Primary dysmenorrhea** (no pelvic pathology) results from prostaglandin release; treat with NSAIDs and hormonal contraceptives. **Secondary dysmenorrhea** suggests endometriosis or adenomyosis (week 12).

@example
title: Secondary amenorrhea workup
problem: A 24-year-old marathon runner with a BMI of 17.5 has had no menses for 8 months. A urine pregnancy test is negative. TSH and prolactin are normal. FSH and LH are low and estradiol is low. What is the diagnosis and counseling?
step: Rule out pregnancy, thyroid disease and hyperprolactinemia first; here all are excluded.
step: Low FSH and LH with low estradiol, with energy deficiency, indicates hypothalamic amenorrhea (a functional suppression of GnRH).
step: Treatment is to restore energy balance with increased intake and reduced training; assess bone density because estrogen deficiency causes osteopenia.
step: Estrogen therapy alone does not treat the underlying problem.

@mistakes
- Forgetting a pregnancy test as the first step in amenorrhea and abnormal bleeding.
- Calling uterine bleeding in a 52-year-old "perimenopausal" without endometrial sampling when risk factors or persistence are present.
- Confusing Müllerian agenesis (46,XX, normal testosterone) with androgen insensitivity (46,XY, male-range testosterone).
- Treating PCOS-related anovulation with unopposed estrogen or no endometrial protection.

answer: 18
check: 32 - 14
tol: 0.01
unit: cycle day
hint: The luteal phase is fixed in length.
hint: Ovulation occurs 14 days before the next menses.
solution: The next menses begins on day 33 (day 1 of the next cycle). Fourteen days earlier is day 18 of the current cycle (32 - 14 = 18).
