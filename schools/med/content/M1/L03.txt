@article
The thoracic wall is a flexible cage that protects the heart and lungs and also pumps air. Most procedures here depend on one rule: **the neurovascular bundle runs along the lower edge of each rib**.

### Ribs and sternum

There are **12 pairs of ribs**. Ribs 1-7 are **true (vertebrosternal)** ribs attached directly to the sternum by their own costal cartilage; ribs 8-10 are **false (vertebrochondral)** ribs whose cartilages join the cartilage above; ribs 11-12 are **floating (vertebral)** ribs with no anterior attachment. Ribs 3-9 are "typical"; the 1st, 2nd, 10th, 11th and 12th are atypical. Between the ribs lie **11 intercostal spaces**.

The sternum has a **manubrium**, **body** and **xiphoid process**. The **sternal angle** (angle of Louis), the palpable ridge where manubrium meets body, lies at the level of the **2nd costal cartilage** and the **T4-T5 intervertebral disc**. At this level are the tracheal bifurcation (carina), the beginning and end of the aortic arch, and the azygos vein joining the superior vena cava. It is also the plane that separates the superior from the inferior mediastinum.

### Intercostal spaces

Each space contains three muscle layers: **external**, **internal** and **innermost** intercostals. The **neurovascular bundle** (vein, artery, nerve, "VAN" from superior to inferior) runs in the **costal groove**, between the internal and innermost intercostals along the **inferior border of the rib above**. For that reason a needle or chest tube is placed just **above the superior border of the rib below** the chosen space. Intercostal nerves T1-T11 also supply the skin and the parietal pleura, so chest wall and pleural disease causes sharp, localized pain.

### Diaphragm

The diaphragm is the main muscle of inspiration. It is supplied motor and sensory (central portion) by the **phrenic nerve (C3, C4, C5)**; "C3, 4, 5 keeps the diaphragm alive". Irritation of its central part is felt as pain in the **shoulder tip** (the C4 dermatome). Its three major openings:

| Level | Opening | Passes through |
|---|---|---|
| T8 | Caval opening | Inferior vena cava, right phrenic nerve branches |
| T10 | Esophageal hiatus | Esophagus, vagal trunks, esophageal branches of left gastric vessels |
| T12 | Aortic hiatus | Aorta, thoracic duct, azygos vein |

### Pleura

Each lung is covered by **visceral pleura**, with the **parietal pleura** lining the chest wall, diaphragm and mediastinum. The pleural cavity between them contains only a thin film of fluid. At the lower edges the pleura extends below the lung into the **costodiaphragmatic recess**, which fills when pleural fluid accumulates and is where a thoracentesis needle is aimed. Surface reflections: lung at the **6th rib** (midclavicular line), **8th** (midaxillary) and **10th** (paravertebral); pleura two ribs lower, at the **8th, 10th and 12th**.

### Lungs and bronchi

The **right lung** has three lobes (upper, middle, lower) separated by the oblique and horizontal fissures. The **left lung** has two lobes (upper and lower) separated by the oblique fissure; the left upper lobe contains the **lingula** and the **cardiac notch**. The right lung has 10 bronchopulmonary segments; the left has 8 to 10, depending on how segments are combined.

The **right main bronchus is wider, shorter and more vertical** than the left, so aspirated material and foreign bodies enter it more often, and an endotracheal tube advanced too far usually ends in the right main bronchus. At the hilum, remember **RALS**: on the **R**ight the pulmonary artery is **A**nterior to the bronchus; on the **L**eft the artery is **S**uperior. The pulmonary veins lie anterior and inferior.

### Procedures

For **thoracentesis**, the patient sits upright; the needle goes in posteriorly, typically near the midscapular line below the level of dullness, and passes just above the rib's upper border. For a **chest tube**, the "safe triangle" is bordered by the anterior edge of latissimus dorsi, the lateral edge of pectoralis major and a horizontal line at the nipple level (about the 5th intercostal space); the tube goes in at the 4th-5th space, mid-axillary line. For **tension pneumothorax**, needle decompression in adults is done in the 4th-5th intercostal space just anterior to the mid-axillary line (preferred in ATLS 10th edition) or in the 2nd intercostal space at the midclavicular line (the traditional site); both are accepted.

@example
title: Why the needle goes above the rib
problem: A thoracentesis needle is placed in the 8th intercostal space. Which part of the 9th rib should it pass over, and why?
step: The costal groove lies on the inferior border of each rib, so the neurovascular bundle of the 8th space runs under the 8th rib.
step: Entering just above the superior border of the 9th rib stays as far as possible from the 8th rib's lower border.
step: The needle then passes through the intercostal muscles and parietal pleura into the costodiaphragmatic recess, avoiding the vein, artery and nerve.

@mistakes
- Placing the needle below a rib's lower border, where the intercostal vein, artery and nerve run.
- Counting ribs from the first without palpating the sternal angle; the 2nd rib is the easiest starting point.
- Assuming the pleura and lung end at the same level inferiorly; the pleura extends two ribs lower, creating the recess.
- Saying the left main bronchus is the usual site for aspirated foreign bodies; it is the right.
