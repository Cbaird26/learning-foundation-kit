@article
The upper limb is one of the best systems for learning to predict function from structure: every deficit comes from a particular level of the brachial plexus or one of its branches.

### The brachial plexus

The plexus is formed by the anterior rami of **C5-T1**. Remember the sequence "Randy Travis Drinks Cold Beer": **roots** (C5-T1), **trunks** (upper C5-C6, middle C7, lower C8-T1), **divisions** (anterior and posterior) and **cords** (lateral, medial and posterior, named for their position relative to the axillary artery).

| Terminal nerve | Cord | Roots | Main actions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Musculocutaneous | Lateral | C5-C7 | Elbow flexion (biceps, brachialis) and coracobrachialis; sensation to lateral forearm |
| Axillary | Posterior | C5-C6 | Deltoid and teres minor; sensation over lateral shoulder |
| Radial | Posterior | C5-T1 | Extensors of elbow, wrist and fingers; brachioradialis; supinator |
| Median | Lateral and medial | C5-T1 (mainly C6-T1) | Forearm pronators, wrist and finger flexors (except FCU and ulnar half of FDP), thenar muscles, lateral two lumbricals |
| Ulnar | Medial | C8-T1 | FCU, ulnar half of FDP, hypothenar muscles, interossei, medial two lumbricals, adductor pollicis |

Important branches before the terminal nerves: **dorsal scapular** (C5, rhomboids), **long thoracic** (C5-C7, serratus anterior), **suprascapular** (from the upper trunk; supraspinatus and infraspinatus) and the **thoracodorsal** (latissimus dorsi).

### Injury patterns

- **Upper trunk (Erb palsy, C5-C6)**: shoulder adducted and medially rotated, elbow extended, forearm pronated ("waiter's tip"). Occurs with shoulder dystocia or falls on the neck and shoulder.
- **Lower trunk (Klumpke palsy, C8-T1)**: claw hand with intrinsic hand muscle weakness; Horner syndrome if T1 sympathetic fibers are involved. Occurs after forceful upward traction on the arm.
- **Long thoracic nerve**: **winged scapula** from serratus anterior paralysis (inability to push forward or raise the arm above the horizontal).
- **Axillary nerve**: surgical neck fracture or anterior shoulder dislocation; deltoid paralysis (abduction from 15 to 90 degrees is lost) and numbness over the lateral shoulder ("regimental badge").
- **Radial nerve**: midshaft humeral fracture at the spiral groove; **wrist drop**, with triceps spared because its branches leave before the groove. Sensory loss on the dorsum of the hand's lateral side.
- **Median nerve**: **carpal tunnel syndrome**: thenar wasting, weak thumb abduction and opposition, and sensory loss over the lateral three and a half digits' palmar surface (the palmar cutaneous branch spares the thenar skin). A proximal lesion (supracondylar fracture) causes the "hand of benediction" when trying to make a fist.
- **Ulnar nerve**: medial epicondyle fracture or hook-of-hamate fracture; **claw hand** (ring and little fingers), weak finger abduction and adduction, **Froment sign**, and sensory loss over the medial one and a half fingers.
- **Musculocutaneous nerve**: weak elbow flexion and supination, loss of sensation over the lateral forearm.

### Shoulder

The **rotator cuff** muscles (SITS) stabilize the glenohumeral joint: **s**upraspinatus (abduction, first 0-15 degrees; the most often torn), **i**nfraspinatus and **t**eres minor (external rotation), **s**ubscapularis (internal rotation). The **anterior** dislocation is most common, and the humeral head lies inferior and anterior to the glenoid.

### Arteries

The **subclavian** artery becomes the **axillary** at the lateral border of the first rib and the **brachial** at the inferior border of teres major. The **deep brachial** artery follows the radial nerve in the spiral groove. The brachial artery divides in the cubital fossa into the **radial** and **ulnar** arteries, which join to form the superficial and deep palmar arches.

### Cubital fossa, carpal tunnel and snuffbox

The **cubital fossa** contains, from lateral to medial, the biceps tendon, brachial artery and median nerve; the **median cubital vein** crosses it superficially, which is why it is used for venipuncture. The **carpal tunnel** holds the median nerve and nine tendons (four FDS, four FDP and FPL). The **anatomical snuffbox** is bounded by the EPL tendon medially and the APL and EPB tendons laterally; its floor is the **scaphoid**, so snuffbox tenderness suggests a scaphoid fracture. The scaphoid's blood supply enters distally and flows retrograde, so a waist fracture risks **avascular necrosis** of the proximal pole.

@example
title: Predicting a deficit from a plexus injury
problem: A 28-year-old man falls from a motorcycle and lands on his shoulder, forcing the head and neck away from the shoulder. His arm hangs adducted and medially rotated with the elbow extended. Which roots are affected, and which functions are lost?
step: The posture matches Erb palsy: upper trunk (C5-C6).
step: C5-C6 supply deltoid and rotator cuff (abduction, external rotation), biceps and brachialis (elbow flexion), and supinators.
step: Lost abduction and external rotation leave the arm adducted and medially rotated; lost flexion leaves the elbow extended; lost supination leaves the forearm pronated.
step: Sensation over the lateral arm and forearm is reduced; hand function is spared since C8-T1 is intact.

@mistakes
- Attributing wrist drop to a lesion that also paralyzes the triceps; a midshaft humeral fracture spares the triceps.
- Confusing Erb (C5-C6, "waiter's tip") with Klumpke (C8-T1, claw hand).
- Saying the ulnar nerve causes a claw in the index and middle fingers: those are median-innervated lumbricals.
- Placing the brachial artery lateral to the biceps tendon in the cubital fossa; the order from lateral to medial is biceps tendon, brachial artery, median nerve.
