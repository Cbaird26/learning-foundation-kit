@article
Anatomy is a descriptive science, so its first job is to remove ambiguity. "Left" means the patient's left, not the examiner's, and every description assumes the same reference posture.

### Anatomical position and directional terms

The **anatomical position** is a person standing upright, facing forward, feet together, arms at the sides with palms facing forward (forearms supinated). All terms below describe a structure relative to this posture, even when the patient is lying down or bent over.

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Superior / inferior (cranial / caudal) | toward the head / toward the feet | The heart is superior to the diaphragm. |
| Anterior / posterior (ventral / dorsal) | toward the front / back | The sternum is anterior to the heart. |
| Medial / lateral | toward / away from the midline | The ulna is medial to the radius in anatomical position. |
| Proximal / distal | nearer to / farther from the attachment or origin of a limb (or of a tube such as the gut) | The elbow is proximal to the wrist. |
| Superficial / deep | nearer to / farther from the body surface | The skin is superficial to muscle. |
| Ipsilateral / contralateral | same side / opposite side | A right-sided lesion causes ipsilateral weakness if the pathway is uncrossed. |

### Planes

Three cardinal planes are at right angles to one another.

- **Sagittal**: divides the body into right and left parts; the **midsagittal** (median) plane passes through the midline.
- **Coronal** (frontal): divides it into anterior and posterior parts.
- **Transverse** (axial, horizontal): divides it into superior and inferior parts. Axial CT slices are transverse.

By convention axial images are viewed **from the feet**, as if looking up from the foot of the bed, so the patient's right appears on the left of the screen.

### Movements and cavities

Flexion decreases and extension increases the angle at a joint (in the sagittal plane). Abduction moves a limb away from the midline and adduction toward it. Medial (internal) and lateral (external) rotation turn the anterior surface inward or outward. Pronation turns the palm posteriorly (in anatomical position, palm down when the elbow is flexed); supination turns it anteriorly. Dorsiflexion lifts the foot upward; plantarflexion points the toes down. Inversion and eversion turn the sole medially or laterally.

The **thoracic cavity** holds the two pleural cavities and the mediastinum. The **abdominopelvic cavity** is lined by peritoneum. Each serous cavity has a **parietal** layer lining the wall and a **visceral** layer covering the organs, with a thin film of fluid between.

### Imaging in brief

**Plain radiography** projects X-rays through the body onto a detector. Tissues that absorb more X-rays (bone, calcium, metal) appear **white**; air appears **black**; fat, water and soft tissue are shades of gray. Structures are superimposed, so two views at right angles are often needed.

**CT** reconstructs thin transverse slices. Each voxel gets a **Hounsfield unit (HU)** value defined relative to water:

$$\text{HU} = 1000 \times \frac{\mu_{\text{tissue}} - \mu_{\text{water}}}{\mu_{\text{water}}}$$

where $\mu$ is the linear attenuation coefficient. Water is $0$ HU, air about $-1000$, fat about $-100$, acute clotted blood roughly $+50$ to $+70$, and dense cortical bone several hundred to $1000$ or more. Intravenous iodinated contrast brightens vessels and enhancing tissue.

**MRI** uses a strong magnetic field and radiofrequency pulses and involves no ionizing radiation. On **T1-weighted** images fat is bright and fluid (such as CSF) is dark, which gives good anatomical detail. On **T2-weighted** images fluid is bright, and most pathology that contains water (edema, tumor, inflammation) is also bright. Cortical bone and air give almost no MRI signal.

**Ultrasound** uses reflected high-frequency sound. Fluid-filled structures (bladder, gallbladder) are anechoic (black); gas and bone reflect nearly all sound and cast shadows. It is real-time, portable and radiation-free, and is used to guide needles for procedures.

Choosing a modality is itself an anatomical decision: CT for bone, acute hemorrhage and speed; MRI for soft tissue, brain and spinal cord; ultrasound for gallbladder, pregnancy, vessels and bedside guidance.

@example
title: Hounsfield units from attenuation coefficients
problem: A voxel has a linear attenuation coefficient of $0.38\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. Water measures $0.19\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. What is the HU value, and which tissue does it suggest?
step: Write the definition: $\text{HU} = 1000\,(\mu - \mu_{\text{water}})/\mu_{\text{water}}$.
step: Substitute: $1000 \times (0.38 - 0.19)/0.19 = 1000 \times 1 = 1000$.
step: A value of about $1000$ HU is far above soft tissue, so it suggests dense bone (or calcification or metal). Check: water itself gives $1000 \times 0/0.19 = 0$.

@example
title: Reading an axial image
problem: On an axial CT slice displayed in the standard way, a mass lies on the left side of the screen, near the front of the abdomen. In which side and half of the patient is it?
step: Axial slices are viewed from the feet, so the left side of the screen is the patient's right.
step: Near the top of the image is anterior.
step: The mass is in the patient's right, anterior abdomen (for instance in the right lobe of the liver's anterior part or right anterior abdominal wall).

@mistakes
- Using the examiner's left and right instead of the patient's.
- Confusing proximal and distal in the gut or on a limb: proximal is always closer to the origin (shoulder, stomach), not "closer to the center of the body" in an absolute sense.
- Forgetting that axial CT is viewed from the feet, so the patient's right is on the viewer's left.
- Calling fluid "dark" on every MRI: it is dark on T1 and bright on T2.

choice*: coronal

answer: 52.6
check: 1000 * (0.20 - 0.19) / 0.19
tol: 0.5
unit: HU
hint: Use $\text{HU} = 1000(\mu - \mu_{\text{water}})/\mu_{\text{water}}$.
hint: The difference is $0.01$ and you divide it by $0.19$.
solution: $1000 \times 0.01/0.19 \approx 52.6$ HU, in the range of acute clotted blood.

@quiz mc
prompt: A 24-year-old man falls from a ladder. A supine axial CT slice shows a rounded air collection at the very top of the image within the chest, and the patient's left side is on the right side of the screen. Which statement is most accurate?

choice*: The air is anterior, which is where free pleural air rises in a supine patient.
