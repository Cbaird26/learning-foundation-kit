@article
The abdominal wall contains and protects the viscera, and it is where surgeons enter. Its layers, its weak points (the inguinal and femoral regions) and the peritoneum lining it explain hernias, ascites and the spread of infection.

### Layers of the anterior abdominal wall

From superficial to deep: **skin**, **Camper's fascia** (fatty superficial layer), **Scarpa's fascia** (membranous layer), then the muscles: **external oblique**, **internal oblique**, **transversus abdominis** (with **rectus abdominis** in the midline), then **transversalis fascia**, **extraperitoneal fat** and **parietal peritoneum**. The muscle fibers run in different directions (external oblique "hands in pockets", internal oblique at right angles, transversus transversely), which resists shearing. The **linea alba** is the midline fibrous raphe; the **semilunar line** marks the lateral edge of rectus.

Cutaneous landmarks: the **T10 dermatome** includes the umbilicus, and L1 lies at the inguinal ligament, so visceral and somatic pain can be related to levels.

### The rectus sheath

Above the **arcuate line** (about midway between umbilicus and pubic crest) the **anterior layer** is the external oblique aponeurosis plus the anterior lamina of the internal oblique aponeurosis, and the **posterior layer** is the posterior lamina of the internal oblique plus the transversus aponeurosis. Below the arcuate line all three aponeuroses pass **anterior** to rectus, so the posterior wall of rectus is only **transversalis fascia**. The **superior epigastric** (from the internal thoracic) and **inferior epigastric** (from the external iliac) arteries anastomose within the sheath, which provides collateral flow when the aorta is obstructed.

### The inguinal canal

The inguinal canal is a 4 cm oblique passage above the medial half of the inguinal ligament, carrying the **spermatic cord** in males and the **round ligament of the uterus** in females, plus the **ilioinguinal nerve**. It begins at the **deep inguinal ring** (an opening in transversalis fascia, lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels) and ends at the **superficial inguinal ring** (an opening in the external oblique aponeurosis).

- **Anterior wall**: external oblique aponeurosis (plus internal oblique laterally).
- **Posterior wall**: transversalis fascia (plus the conjoint tendon medially).
- **Roof**: arching fibers of internal oblique and transversus abdominis.
- **Floor**: inguinal ligament (and lacunar ligament medially).

### Hernias

**Hesselbach's (inguinal) triangle** is bordered by the **inferior epigastric vessels** laterally, the **lateral border of rectus** medially and the **inguinal ligament** inferiorly.

| Hernia | Relation to inferior epigastric vessels | Route | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indirect inguinal | Lateral | Through the deep ring, down the canal, often into the scrotum | Patent processus vaginalis; covered by all three spermatic fascia layers; commonest type overall |
| Direct inguinal | Medial | Through Hesselbach's triangle, a weak posterior wall | Acquired; exits at the superficial ring, not the deep ring |
| Femoral | Below and lateral to the pubic tubercle (below the inguinal ligament) | Through the femoral canal, medial to the femoral vein | More common in women; high risk of strangulation |

### The peritoneum

The **peritoneum** is a serous membrane: the **parietal** layer lines the wall; the **visceral** layer covers organs. The potential space between is the **peritoneal cavity**. Organs may be **intraperitoneal** (stomach, liver, spleen, jejunum, ileum, transverse and sigmoid colon, first part of the duodenum) suspended by mesenteries, or **retroperitoneal**, lying behind the peritoneum. A useful mnemonic for the primarily retroperitoneal organs is **SAD PUCKER**: **S**uprarenal glands, **A**orta and IVC, **D**uodenum (2nd to 4th parts), **P**ancreas (except the tail), **U**reters, **C**olon (ascending and descending), **K**idneys, **E**sophagus, **R**ectum.

The **greater omentum** hangs from the greater curvature of the stomach; the **lesser omentum** joins the lesser curvature and duodenum to the liver. Behind the stomach is the **lesser sac (omental bursa)**, communicating with the greater sac through the **epiploic foramen (of Winslow)**. The free edge of the lesser omentum, bounding the foramen anteriorly, contains the **portal triad** (hepatic portal vein, hepatic artery proper, bile duct); the IVC lies behind it. Compression of the free edge by hand is the **Pringle maneuver**.

**Dependent spaces**: in a supine patient the **hepatorenal pouch (Morison's pouch)** is the deepest recess, so free fluid or blood collects there first (seen on FAST ultrasound). In the erect position the pelvic recesses, the **rectovesical pouch** in men and **rectouterine pouch (of Douglas)** in women, are the lowest. The inferior surface of the diaphragm is covered by peritoneum, so subphrenic collections can irritate it.

@example
title: Direct versus indirect inguinal hernia
problem: During repair of a right inguinal hernia, the sac is found emerging lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels. What type is it, and through which structure does it enter the canal?
step: A sac lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels is an indirect hernia.
step: It enters through the deep inguinal ring, an opening in the transversalis fascia just lateral to those vessels.
step: It follows the spermatic cord inside the internal spermatic fascia, usually via a patent processus vaginalis, and may descend into the scrotum.
step: A direct hernia, by contrast, lies medial to the vessels and pushes through the posterior wall in Hesselbach's triangle.

@mistakes
- Reversing the relation to the inferior epigastric vessels (direct is medial, indirect is lateral).
- Placing the epiploic foramen anterior to the portal triad; the triad forms its anterior border and the IVC its posterior border.
- Treating the whole duodenum as retroperitoneal; only its second to fourth parts are.
- Forgetting that below the arcuate line the rectus has no posterior aponeurotic layer.
