@article
The abdominal viscera are organized by embryologic origin: **foregut** (to the second part of the duodenum), **midgut** (to the proximal two-thirds of the transverse colon) and **hindgut** (the rest). Arterial supply, venous drainage and pain pathways follow the same plan.

### Arteries from the aorta

| Artery | Level | Territory |
|---|---|---|
| **Celiac trunk** | T12 | Foregut: stomach, liver, gallbladder, spleen, upper duodenum, pancreas. Branches: left gastric, splenic, common hepatic |
| **Superior mesenteric (SMA)** | L1 | Midgut: lower duodenum, jejunum, ileum, appendix, ascending and proximal transverse colon |
| **Inferior mesenteric (IMA)** | L3 | Hindgut: distal transverse, descending and sigmoid colon, upper rectum |

The paired **renal arteries** arise at about L1-L2. The aorta bifurcates at **L4** and the IVC forms at **L5**. The **left renal vein** passes between the aorta and the SMA, and can be compressed there (nutcracker phenomenon). The left gonadal vein drains to the left renal vein, the right gonadal vein directly to the IVC, so varicocele is more common on the left.

### Portal system and anastomoses

The **hepatic portal vein** forms behind the neck of the pancreas (at about L1-L2) from the **superior mesenteric** and **splenic veins**, with the inferior mesenteric vein joining either. It runs in the free edge of the lesser omentum with the hepatic artery proper and common bile duct (the **portal triad**). In portal hypertension, blood reaches the systemic veins through four **portosystemic anastomoses**:

- **Esophageal** (left gastric to azygos): esophageal varices that can bleed massively.
- **Rectal** (superior rectal to middle and inferior rectal): hemorrhoids.
- **Paraumbilical** (paraumbilical veins to epigastric veins): caput medusae.
- **Retroperitoneal** (colonic veins to renal and lumbar veins).

### Liver, biliary tree and pancreas

The liver has anatomical right and left lobes (separated by the falciform ligament) with caudate and quadrate lobes, and functionally **eight Couinaud segments** based on portal and hepatic venous anatomy. Bile leaves via the **right and left hepatic ducts**, which join to form the **common hepatic duct**; with the **cystic duct** it forms the **common bile duct**, which joins the pancreatic duct at the **hepatopancreatic ampulla** (of Vater) opening on the **major duodenal papilla** in the second part of the duodenum, controlled by the **sphincter of Oddi**.

The **gallbladder** has a fundus, body, infundibulum with **Hartmann's pouch** (where stones lodge) and a neck leading to the cystic duct. The **cystic artery**, usually from the right hepatic artery, runs in **Calot's triangle** (cystic duct, common hepatic duct and inferior edge of the liver). Identifying the cystic duct and artery (critical view of safety) prevents bile duct injury in cholecystectomy.

The **pancreas** has a head (nestled in the duodenal C-loop, with the uncinate process behind the SMV), neck, body and tail (in the splenorenal ligament, near the splenic hilum). A tumor of the head can compress the common bile duct, causing painless jaundice. The **spleen** lies under ribs 9-11 on the left, so rib fractures there suggest splenic injury; it is intraperitoneal and fragile.

The **appendix** hangs from the cecum; its base lies at **McBurney's point**, one third of the way along the line from the right ASIS to the umbilicus. The tip is most often retrocecal.

### Referred pain: the appendix and the gallbladder

Visceral pain fibers travel with sympathetic nerves to the spinal cord and enter at levels fixed by the organ's embryologic origin; the brain places the pain in the matching dermatome, in the midline since organs develop bilaterally.

**Appendix (midgut)**: afferents run with the **lesser splanchnic nerve** to **T10**, so early appendicitis causes **dull periumbilical pain** (T10 dermatome). When inflammation spreads to the **parietal peritoneum** of the right iliac fossa, somatic nerves (T12 to L1) carry sharp, well-localized pain to **McBurney's point**.

**Gallbladder (foregut)**: afferents via the **greater splanchnic nerve** to **T7-T9** cause epigastric or right upper quadrant pain, sometimes felt under the right scapula. When the inflamed gallbladder irritates the diaphragm, the phrenic nerve (C3-C5) refers pain to the **right shoulder tip**. Contact of an inflamed gallbladder with the parietal peritoneum causes localized RUQ tenderness and **Murphy's sign** (inspiratory arrest during RUQ palpation).

### Kidneys and ureters

The kidneys lie retroperitoneally at about T12-L3, the right lower than the left because of the liver. Each ureter has three narrowings: the **ureteropelvic junction**, the **pelvic brim** (crossing the iliac vessels) and the **ureterovesical junction**. Stone pain starts in the flank and radiates to the groin as the stone descends (T10-L2 levels).

@example
title: Why does appendicitis pain move?
problem: A 19-year-old man has 12 hours of dull periumbilical pain followed by sharp pain at McBurney's point. Explain the anatomy.
step: Early inflammation distends the appendix, a midgut structure; visceral afferents travel with the lesser splanchnic nerve to T10 and are perceived in the T10 dermatome around the umbilicus, poorly localized.
step: As inflammation reaches the parietal peritoneum in the right iliac fossa, somatic afferents (T12-L1 via the iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal nerves, among others) are stimulated.
step: Somatic pain is sharp and localized to the overlying skin, so the patient points to McBurney's point.
step: Moving pain therefore reflects a shift from visceral to parietal peritoneal involvement.

@mistakes
- Believing visceral pain is well localized; it is dull, midline and referred according to embryologic origin.
- Mixing up the SMA and the celiac trunk territories; the celiac is T12 and foregut, the SMA is L1 and midgut.
- Forgetting that the portal vein forms behind the pancreatic neck from the SMV and splenic vein.
- Placing the gallbladder's referred shoulder pain in the left shoulder; it is right-sided via the phrenic nerve irritation.
