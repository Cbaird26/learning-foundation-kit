@article
The pelvis is a bony ring that supports the trunk, protects the pelvic viscera and forms the birth canal. Below it, the pelvic floor and perineum close the outlet and contain the external genitalia and anal canal.

### Bony pelvis

Each hip bone consists of the **ilium**, **ischium** and **pubis**, fused at the acetabulum. The two hip bones meet anteriorly at the **pubic symphysis** and articulate posteriorly with the **sacrum** at the sacroiliac joints. The **pelvic brim** (inlet) runs from the sacral promontory along the arcuate and pectineal lines to the pubic crest and symphysis; above it is the **greater (false) pelvis**, below it the **lesser (true) pelvis**. The **sacrotuberous** and **sacrospinous ligaments** convert the sciatic notches into the **greater and lesser sciatic foramina**.

The female pelvis has a wider, rounder inlet and a wider subpubic angle. Obstetric measurements: the **diagonal conjugate** (promontory to the lower border of the symphysis, measured by examination) is about 12.5 cm or more; the **obstetric conjugate**, the shortest anteroposterior diameter through which the fetal head must pass, is about 10 cm or more. It is estimated by **subtracting 1.5 to 2 cm** from the diagonal conjugate.

### Pelvic floor

The **pelvic diaphragm** is formed by **levator ani** (puborectalis, pubococcygeus, iliococcygeus) and **coccygeus**, supplied mainly by sacral branches (S3-S4) and the pudendal nerve. It supports the viscera and maintains continence; the puborectalis sling holds the anorectal angle. Weakness or birth injury of the floor contributes to prolapse and incontinence.

### The perineum

The **perineum** is the diamond-shaped region below the pelvic floor bounded by the pubic symphysis, ischiopubic rami, ischial tuberosities, sacrotuberous ligaments and coccyx. A line between the ischial tuberosities divides it into the anterior **urogenital triangle** and the posterior **anal triangle** (with the ischioanal fossae on each side of the anal canal).

The urogenital triangle has two pouches. The **superficial perineal pouch** lies between **Colles' fascia** (the membranous layer of superficial fascia) and the perineal membrane; it contains the root of the penis or clitoris, **ischiocavernosus**, **bulbospongiosus** and superficial transverse perineal muscles, and in females the greater vestibular glands. The **deep perineal pouch** lies above the perineal membrane and contains the **external urethral sphincter**, the deep transverse perineal muscle, the membranous urethra and (in males) the bulbourethral glands.

### Pudendal nerve

The **pudendal nerve (S2-S4)** is the main somatic nerve of the perineum: it supplies the external urethral and anal sphincters, perineal muscles and the skin of the perineum and external genitalia. It leaves the pelvis through the **greater sciatic foramen**, hooks around the **ischial spine** (and sacrospinous ligament) and re-enters through the **lesser sciatic foramen** into the pudendal canal. A **pudendal block** is given by injecting local anesthetic near the ischial spine, transvaginally or through the perineum. Autonomic control of erection is by **parasympathetic S2-S4** (pelvic splanchnic nerves); sympathetic fibers produce emission; somatic fibers via the pudendal nerve cause ejaculation and orgasmic contractions ("point and shoot": parasympathetic erection, sympathetic emission, somatic expulsion).

### The urethra and injury

The male urethra has **prostatic**, **membranous** and **spongy** (bulbar and penile) parts. A **straddle injury** can rupture the **bulbar urethra** below the perineal membrane: urine escapes into the superficial perineal pouch and is limited by Colles' fascia, so it fills the perineum, penis and scrotum and ascends in the anterior abdominal wall deep to Scarpa's fascia, but not into the thigh (fascia lata attaches) or anal triangle. In **pelvic fracture**, the membranous urethra may be torn above the perineal membrane, giving a high-riding prostate and extravasation into the pelvis.

### Pelvic organs

The **prostate** has a posterior peripheral zone (where most cancers arise and which is palpable on digital rectal examination) and a central transition zone surrounding the urethra (benign enlargement, causing obstruction). In females the **uterus** is typically anteverted and anteflexed. The **broad ligament** drapes the uterus, tubes and ovaries; the **round ligament of the uterus** runs to the labia majora via the inguinal canal; the **suspensory ligament of the ovary** carries the ovarian vessels; and the **cardinal ligament** carries the uterine vessels at the base of the broad ligament. The **ureter** passes beneath the uterine artery ("water under the bridge") and beneath the ductus deferens in males, so it can be injured in hysterectomy and other pelvic surgery. The **ampulla** is the commonest site of ectopic pregnancy.

### Anal canal

The **pectinate line** marks the embryologic junction of hindgut and ectoderm. **Above** it: columnar epithelium, visceral (autonomic) innervation, supplied by the superior rectal vessels, drained by the inferior mesenteric (portal) system and internal iliac nodes; **internal hemorrhoids** are painless. **Below** it: stratified squamous epithelium, somatic innervation (inferior rectal nerve, from the pudendal), drainage to superficial inguinal nodes; **external hemorrhoids** and anal fissures are painful. The internal sphincter is smooth muscle (involuntary); the external sphincter is skeletal muscle (pudendal nerve).

@example
title: Where does the urine go?
problem: A 22-year-old man falls astride a bicycle bar and develops a perineal hematoma with blood at the urethral meatus. Over hours the scrotum, penis and lower abdominal wall swell. Explain the anatomy.
step: The bulbar urethra lies in the superficial perineal pouch, just below the perineal membrane, where straddle forces compress it against the pubic arch.
step: A tear releases urine into the superficial pouch, which is enclosed by Colles' fascia and the perineal membrane.
step: Colles' fascia is continuous with the dartos fascia and the membranous layer of the abdominal wall (Scarpa's), so urine tracks into the scrotum, penis and abdominal wall.
step: It cannot enter the thigh (fascia lata attaches to the inguinal ligament) or the anal triangle (Colles' fascia fuses to the perineal body and membrane posteriorly).

@mistakes
- Believing urine from a bulbar urethral tear enters the thigh.
- Confusing the pudendal nerve's roots (S2-S4) with those of the sciatic nerve.
- Saying hemorrhoids above the pectinate line are painful; the visceral innervation above it is pain-insensitive to cutting.
- Mixing up the ureter and uterine artery relation: the ureter is under the artery.
