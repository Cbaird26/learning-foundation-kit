@article
Real motion often happens in a plane. The key idea of this lesson is that perpendicular directions are **independent**: you can analyze the $x$ and $y$ motions separately, each with the kinematic equations from week 1, and link them only through time.

### Vectors and components

A **vector** has magnitude and direction (displacement, velocity, acceleration, force). A **scalar** has only magnitude (distance, speed, time, mass). A vector of magnitude $A$ at angle $\theta$ above the $+x$ axis has components

$$A_x = A\cos\theta, \qquad A_y = A\sin\theta,$$

and from components you recover

$$A = \sqrt{A_x^2 + A_y^2}, \qquad \theta = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{A_y}{A_x}\right).$$

To add vectors, add their components separately: $R_x = A_x + B_x$ and $R_y = A_y + B_y$. Check the quadrant of the angle, because the calculator's $\tan^{-1}$ only returns angles between $-90^\circ$ and $90^\circ$.

### Projectile motion

A **projectile** moves under gravity alone (we ignore air resistance). Then:

- Horizontally, acceleration is zero, so $v_x$ is constant and $x = v_{0x}\,t$.
- Vertically, acceleration is $-g = -9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ (up positive), so $v_y = v_{0y} - gt$ and $y = v_{0y}t - \tfrac12 g t^2$.

For a launch at speed $v_0$ and angle $\theta$, the initial components are $v_{0x} = v_0\cos\theta$ and $v_{0y} = v_0\sin\theta$.

### Useful results for level ground

When the projectile lands at its launch height:

$$T = \frac{2v_{0y}}{g}, \qquad H = \frac{v_{0y}^2}{2g}, \qquad R = v_{0x}\,T = \frac{v_0^2\sin 2\theta}{g}.$$

At the top of the path $v_y = 0$ but $v_x$ is unchanged and the acceleration is still $g$ downward. The range is greatest at $45^\circ$, and two launch angles that add to $90^\circ$ (complementary angles) give the same range.

### Horizontal launch

An object launched horizontally from height $h$ has $v_{0y} = 0$, so its fall time is $t = \sqrt{2h/g}$, the same as if it were simply dropped. Its horizontal distance is then $v_{0x}t$.

### Method

Draw the path, choose axes, split the initial velocity into components, write the $x$ and $y$ equations, and solve for the shared time first. Never mix an $x$ quantity with a $y$ quantity in one equation, except time.

@example
title: A ball launched from level ground
problem: A ball is launched from level ground with initial velocity components $v_{0x} = 10\ \text{m/s}$ and $v_{0y} = 19.6\ \text{m/s}$. Using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, find its time of flight, maximum height and range.
step: The vertical motion alone sets the time. The ball returns to ground level when $y = 0$, so $T = 2v_{0y}/g = 2(19.6)/9.8 = 4\ \text{s}$.
step: Maximum height occurs where $v_y = 0$: $H = v_{0y}^2/(2g) = 19.6^2/19.6 = 19.6\ \text{m}$.
step: Horizontal velocity is constant, so $R = v_{0x}T = (10)(4) = 40\ \text{m}$.
step: Check the speed at launch: $\sqrt{10^2 + 19.6^2} \approx 22\ \text{m/s}$, a reasonable ball speed.

@mistakes
- Using the full speed, not the component, in the horizontal or vertical equation.
- Thinking the horizontal velocity decreases or that acceleration is zero at the top of the path.
- Setting calculator mode to radians when the angle is in degrees (or the reverse).
- Believing a heavier projectile falls faster or that a horizontally launched object takes longer to land than a dropped one.

@practice num
prompt: A boat's velocity has components $12\ \text{m/s}$ east and $5\ \text{m/s}$ north. What is its speed in m/s?
answer: 13
check: sqrt(12**2 + 5**2)
hint: Components at right angles combine with the Pythagorean theorem.
hint: $\sqrt{144 + 25}$.
solution: $v = \sqrt{12^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{169} = 13\ \text{m/s}$.
unit: m/s

@practice num
prompt: A ball rolls off the edge of a cliff $78.4\ \text{m}$ above the ground with a horizontal speed of $15\ \text{m/s}$. Using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ and ignoring air resistance, how far from the base of the cliff does it land, in meters?
answer: 60
check: 15 * sqrt(2 * 78.4 / 9.8)
tol: 0.01
hint: Find the fall time from the vertical motion only, with $v_{0y} = 0$.
hint: $t = \sqrt{2h/g} = 4\ \text{s}$, then multiply by the horizontal speed.
solution: $t = \sqrt{2(78.4)/9.8} = 4\ \text{s}$. Horizontal distance $= (15)(4) = 60\ \text{m}$.
unit: m

@practice mc
prompt: A soccer ball is kicked and follows a parabolic path. Ignoring air resistance, which statement is true at the highest point of its flight?
choice: Both components of velocity are zero.
choice: The acceleration is zero.
choice*: The vertical velocity is zero, the horizontal velocity is unchanged, and the acceleration is $9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ downward.
choice: The horizontal velocity is zero and the vertical acceleration is zero.
hint: Gravity acts the whole time, and no horizontal force acts.
solution: Gravity is the only force, so acceleration is $9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ downward everywhere. At the top only the vertical velocity is zero; the horizontal velocity stays constant.

@practice num
prompt: A projectile is launched from level ground at $40\ \text{m/s}$ at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal. Using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, find its range in meters, rounded to the nearest $0.1\ \text{m}$.
answer: 141.4
check: 40**2 * sin(2 * pi / 6) / 9.8
tol: 1%
hint: Use $R = v_0^2 \sin 2\theta / g$ and compute $2\theta = 60^\circ$.
hint: $\sin 60^\circ \approx 0.866$.
solution: $R = (1600)(0.866)/9.8 \approx 141.4\ \text{m}$.
unit: m

@practice num
prompt: A javelin is thrown from level ground at $19.6\ \text{m/s}$ at an angle of $30^\circ$ above the horizontal. Using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ and ignoring air resistance, how long is it in the air, in seconds?
answer: 2
check: 2 * 19.6 * sin(pi / 6) / 9.8
tol: 0.01
hint: Only the vertical component of the launch velocity matters for the time.
hint: $v_{0y} = 19.6 \sin 30^\circ = 9.8\ \text{m/s}$, and $T = 2v_{0y}/g$.
solution: $v_{0y} = 9.8\ \text{m/s}$, so $T = 2(9.8)/9.8 = 2\ \text{s}$.
unit: s

@quiz num
prompt: A ball is kicked from level ground at $20\ \text{m/s}$ at an angle of $30^\circ$ above the horizontal. Using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, what maximum height does it reach, in meters, rounded to the nearest $0.01\ \text{m}$?
answer: 5.1
check: (20 * sin(pi / 6))**2 / (2 * 9.8)
tol: 1%
hint: Find the vertical component of the launch velocity first.
hint: $v_{0y} = 10\ \text{m/s}$, then $H = v_{0y}^2/(2g)$.
solution: $v_{0y} = 20\sin 30^\circ = 10\ \text{m/s}$, so $H = 100/19.6 \approx 5.10\ \text{m}$.
unit: m

@quiz mc
prompt: A cannon at ground level fires a shell at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal, at a fixed muzzle speed. Ignoring air resistance, at which other launch angle with the same speed does the shell land at the same distance?
choice: $15^\circ$
choice: $45^\circ$
choice*: $60^\circ$
choice: $75^\circ$
hint: Range depends on $\sin 2\theta$, and $\sin x = \sin(180^\circ - x)$.
solution: $R \propto \sin 2\theta$. For $30^\circ$, $\sin 60^\circ$; $2\theta = 120^\circ$ gives the same sine, so $\theta = 60^\circ$. Complementary angles give equal ranges.
