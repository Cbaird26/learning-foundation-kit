@article
A **wave** is a disturbance that carries energy from place to place without carrying matter along with it. Sound, light, water ripples, and waves on a string all share the same mathematics.

### Describing a wave

- **Amplitude** $A$: the maximum displacement from equilibrium; it sets the energy.
- **Wavelength** $\lambda$: the distance over which the pattern repeats (crest to crest).
- **Frequency** $f$: the number of cycles per second, in hertz (Hz).
- **Period** $T = 1/f$: the time for one cycle.

The speed of a wave is set by the medium, and it ties these together:

$$v = f\lambda .$$

When a wave passes from one medium into another, its frequency stays the same (the source sets it) while its speed and wavelength change together.

### Transverse and longitudinal waves

In a **transverse** wave the medium moves perpendicular to the direction the wave travels (waves on a string, light). In a **longitudinal** wave the medium oscillates parallel to the travel direction, creating compressions and rarefactions (sound in air, which needs a medium).

### Sound

Sound is a longitudinal pressure wave. In air at $20^\circ\text{C}$ its speed is about $343\ \text{m/s}$, rising about $0.6\ \text{m/s}$ per degree Celsius; it is faster in liquids (about $1480\ \text{m/s}$ in water) and solids. **Pitch** corresponds to frequency, and **loudness** to amplitude and intensity. Humans hear roughly $20$ Hz to $20\,000$ Hz. Sound intensity level in decibels is $\beta = 10\log_{10}(I/I_0)$ with $I_0 = 10^{-12}\ \text{W/m}^2$, so each $10$ dB step is a factor of $10$ in intensity.

### Standing waves

When a wave reflects and overlaps itself, certain frequencies make a **standing wave** with fixed **nodes** and large-motion **antinodes**.

- String fixed at both ends, and pipe open at both ends: $f_n = n\dfrac{v}{2L}$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$
- Pipe closed at one end: $f_n = n\dfrac{v}{4L}$ for odd $n = 1, 3, 5, \dots$

The lowest frequency ($n=1$) is the **fundamental**; the others are **harmonics**.

### Beats

Two waves of nearly equal frequencies $f_1$ and $f_2$ interfere to produce a slow rise and fall of loudness at the **beat frequency** $f_{beat} = |f_1 - f_2|$. Musicians use this to tune instruments.

### The Doppler effect

When a source and an observer move relative to each other, the observed frequency changes: higher when they approach, lower when they separate. The wave speed in the medium is unchanged; what changes is how many crests arrive per second.

@example
title: A tone passing from air to water
problem: A $686\ \text{Hz}$ tone travels through air at $343\ \text{m/s}$ and then into water, where the speed of sound is $1480\ \text{m/s}$. Find the wavelength in each medium.
step: In air: $\lambda = v/f = 343/686$.
step: $\lambda_{air} = 0.50\ \text{m}$.
step: The frequency does not change at the boundary, so in water $\lambda = 1480/686$.
step: $\lambda_{water} \approx 2.16\ \text{m}$. The wave is faster in water, so with the same frequency its wavelength is longer, by the same factor as the speed.

@mistakes
- Believing that frequency changes when a wave enters a new medium; speed and wavelength change, frequency does not.
- Using kilohertz or centimeters without converting before applying $v = f\lambda$.
- Mixing up open and closed pipes; a closed pipe has only odd harmonics and a fundamental of $v/4L$.
- Thinking sound travels in a vacuum, or that a louder sound travels faster.

prompt: A tuning fork produces a $440\ \text{Hz}$ note. If the speed of sound in air is $343\ \text{m/s}$, what is the wavelength in meters, to the nearest $0.01\ \text{m}$?
answer: 0.78
check: 343 / 440
tol: 1%
hint: Use $v = f\lambda$.
hint: $\lambda = v/f$.
solution: $\lambda = 343/440 \approx 0.78\ \text{m}$.

prompt: A sound wave has a frequency of $50\ \text{Hz}$. What is its period in seconds?
answer: 0.02
check: 1 / 50
tol: 0.0001
hint: Period and frequency are reciprocals.
hint: $T = 1/f$.

@practice num
prompt: A hiker shouts toward a cliff and hears the echo $2.0\ \text{s}$ later. Taking the speed of sound as $343\ \text{m/s}$, how far away is the cliff, in meters?
answer: 343
check: 343 * 2.0 / 2
tol: 1
hint: The sound makes a round trip to the cliff and back.
hint: Total distance traveled is $vt$; the cliff is half of that.
solution: The sound travels $343 \times 2.0 = 686\ \text{m}$ in total, so the cliff is $686/2 = 343\ \text{m}$ away.
unit: m

@practice mc
prompt: An ambulance sounding a siren of constant frequency drives toward you. Compared with the frequency produced by the siren, how do you perceive it while the ambulance approaches?
choice*: Higher, because more wave crests reach you per second
choice: Lower, because the sound travels farther
choice: Unchanged, because the siren's frequency is fixed
choice: Higher, because the sound wave travels faster through the air
hint: The speed of sound in air is set by the air, not by the source's motion.
solution: The source moves toward you, so successive crests are emitted from positions nearer to you and arrive closer together in time. The observed frequency is higher, but the wave speed is unchanged.

@practice num
prompt: A pipe open at both ends is $0.50\ \text{m}$ long. Using $343\ \text{m/s}$ for the speed of sound, what is the frequency of its fundamental in hertz?
answer: 343
check: 343 / (2 * 0.50)
tol: 1
hint: For an open-open pipe the fundamental has a half wavelength equal to the pipe length.
hint: $f_1 = v/(2L)$.
solution: $f_1 = 343/(2 \times 0.50) = 343\ \text{Hz}$.
unit: Hz

@quiz num
prompt: A guitar string $0.65\ \text{m}$ long is fixed at both ends. Waves travel along the string at $260\ \text{m/s}$. What is the frequency of the fundamental in hertz?
answer: 200
check: 260 / (2 * 0.65)
tol: 1
hint: In the fundamental, the string length is half a wavelength.
hint: $\lambda = 2L$, then $f = v/\lambda$.
solution: $\lambda = 2(0.65) = 1.30\ \text{m}$, so $f_1 = 260/1.30 = 200\ \text{Hz}$.
unit: Hz

@quiz num
prompt: Two piano strings sound at $440\ \text{Hz}$ and $444\ \text{Hz}$ at the same time. How many loudness beats per second does a listener hear?
answer: 4
check: abs(444 - 440)
hint: The beat frequency equals the difference of the two frequencies.
hint: Subtract the smaller frequency from the larger.
solution: $f_{beat} = |444 - 440| = 4\ \text{Hz}$, so the listener hears four beats each second.
unit: Hz
