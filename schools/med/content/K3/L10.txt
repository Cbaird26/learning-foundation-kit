@article
**Volume** tells how much space a solid thing takes up. We measure it by counting **unit cubes**. A unit cube has sides of $1$ unit, for example a $1$ cm cube or a sugar cube.

### Fill a box with cubes

Imagine a box that is $4$ cubes long and $3$ cubes wide. The bottom layer has $4 \times 3 = 12$ cubes. If the box is $2$ cubes tall, you can stack a second layer. That is $12 + 12 = 24$ cubes.

So the volume is $24$ cubic units.

### The rule

For a box (a rectangular prism):

$$\text{volume} = \text{length} \times \text{width} \times \text{height}$$

Or in words: find the number of cubes in one layer (the base), then multiply by the number of layers (the height).

$$V = \text{base area} \times \text{height}$$

### Units for volume

Volume uses **cubic units**. If the sides are in centimeters, the volume is in cubic centimeters ($\text{cm}^3$). Area uses **square** units because it covers a flat shape. Volume uses **cubic** units because it fills a space.

### Check your answer

Picture the box. Estimate: a box $5$ long, $3$ wide and $2$ tall should hold about $5 \times 3 = 15$ cubes in a layer and two layers, so about $30$. If you get $10$, you forgot a side.

### Volume in the kitchen

Cups, pints and liters measure the volume of liquids and powders. A recipe that is made bigger needs more of every ingredient, and the units might need to change. For example, $12$ cups of flour is the same as $3$ quarts, because $4$ cups make $1$ quart.

Try this with your grown-up: fill a small box with sugar cubes or building blocks and count them. Then find the volume with the rule and compare.

@example
title: Volume of a box
problem: A box is $4$ cm long, $3$ cm wide and $2$ cm tall. What is its volume?
step: Find one layer: $4 \times 3 = 12$ cubes.
step: There are $2$ layers, so multiply by the height: $12 \times 2 = 24$.
step: The volume is $24$ cubic centimeters, written $24\ \text{cm}^3$.

@mistakes
- Adding the sides instead of multiplying. $4 + 3 + 2 = 9$ is not the volume.
- Writing square units for volume. Volume needs cubic units.
- Forgetting one of the three measurements.

check: 5 * 3 * 2

hint: Multiply length times width to find one layer.
hint: $5 \times 3 = 15$. Now multiply by the height.
solution: $5 \times 3 = 15$ and $15 \times 2 = 30$. The volume is $30$ cubic centimeters.

hint: Multiply the base area by the height.
solution: There are $12$ cubes in one layer and $4$ layers. $12 \times 4 = 48$ cubic centimeters.

@practice num

answer: 3
check: 3 * 4 / 4
unit: quarts
hint: First find how many cups of flour you need for $4$ loaves.
hint: $3 \times 4 = 12$ cups. Now change cups to quarts.
solution: $3 \times 4 = 12$ cups. Cups are smaller than quarts, so divide by $4$: $12 \div 4 = 3$ quarts.

@quiz num
