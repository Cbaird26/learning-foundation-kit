@article
Most depression and anxiety is diagnosed and treated in primary care. The USMLE and the clerkships both expect you to screen, diagnose, treat and follow up in an organized way.

### Screening and diagnosis

The **PHQ-2** (two items: depressed mood and anhedonia) screens; a positive result leads to the **PHQ-9**. Each of nine items is scored 0 to 3 (not at all to nearly every day), for a total of 0 to 27: **0 to 4** minimal, **5 to 9** mild, **10 to 14** moderate, **15 to 19** moderately severe, **20 to 27** severe. Item 9 asks about thoughts of death or self-harm and any positive response needs a direct suicide risk assessment. The **GAD-7** has seven items scored 0 to 3 (total 0 to 21): **5** mild, **10** moderate and **15** severe cut points, with a score of 10 or more suggesting generalized anxiety disorder.

**Major depressive disorder** requires at least **five of nine** symptoms for at least 2 weeks, nearly every day, including depressed mood or loss of interest or pleasure, with clinically significant distress or impairment: sleep change, interest loss, guilt, energy loss, concentration problems, appetite or weight change, psychomotor change, and suicidal ideation. Screens are not diagnoses; interview to confirm. Before starting treatment:

- Ask about **past mania or hypomania** (decreased need for sleep with high energy, impulsive spending, family history of bipolar disorder). An antidepressant alone can trigger mania in bipolar disorder.
- Exclude medical and substance causes: thyroid-stimulating hormone, complete blood count, B12, and other tests as indicated, alcohol and drug use, medications such as corticosteroids and beta-blockers, sleep apnea, and grief.
- Assess suicide risk and access to means, and document a plan for follow-up.

### Treatment of depression

Options are psychotherapy (cognitive behavioral therapy or interpersonal therapy), an antidepressant, or both for moderate to severe depression. First-line medications are **SSRIs**: sertraline, escitalopram, fluoxetine, citalopram, paroxetine. Typical adult starting doses for study (verify against current references): sertraline 50 mg daily, escitalopram 10 mg daily, fluoxetine 20 mg daily. Choose by adverse effects, comorbidity, prior response and cost.

- **Bupropion** (norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitor): little sexual dysfunction or weight gain; lowers seizure threshold, so avoid in seizure disorders, eating disorders and abrupt alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal.
- **Mirtazapine:** helps sleep and appetite; weight gain and sedation.
- **SNRIs** (venlafaxine, duloxetine): also treat neuropathic pain; can raise blood pressure.
- **Tricyclics:** effective but dangerous in overdose (arrhythmia, anticholinergic effects); **MAO inhibitors:** reserved for refractory depression because of hypertensive crisis and interaction risks.

Reassess in 1 to 2 weeks for tolerability and safety, then at 4 to 6 weeks at an adequate dose. If the response is partial, increase the dose; if still inadequate, switch drugs, add psychotherapy or augment. Continue treatment for **6 to 12 months** after remission (longer, or indefinitely, after recurrent episodes).

**Adverse effects to know:** nausea and diarrhea, headache and insomnia initially; sexual dysfunction (common); increased bleeding risk especially with NSAIDs or anticoagulants; **hyponatremia** from SIADH, especially in older adults in the first weeks; QT prolongation with citalopram (dose ceilings, lower in older adults); **discontinuation syndrome** (dizziness, flu-like symptoms, "electric shock" sensations) with abrupt stops, especially paroxetine. Antidepressants carry a boxed warning for increased suicidal thinking in people under 25, so see these patients more often early on. **Serotonin syndrome** (agitation, diaphoresis, tremor, **inducible clonus**, hyperreflexia, hyperthermia, diarrhea) follows combinations of serotonergic drugs (SSRIs with linezolid, MAO inhibitors, tramadol, triptans less commonly); stop the drugs, give supportive care and benzodiazepines, and cyproheptadine if needed.

### Anxiety disorders

**Generalized anxiety disorder** (excessive worry about multiple topics on most days for 6 months or longer with somatic symptoms): first-line CBT and an SSRI or SNRI; buspirone as an alternative. **Panic disorder** (recurrent unexpected attacks with fear of further attacks): CBT and an SSRI. Benzodiazepines act quickly but cause dependence, falls and cognitive effects, and are used briefly if at all. Rule out hyperthyroidism, caffeine, arrhythmia, pheochromocytoma and stimulant or withdrawal effects. Beta-blockers can help performance anxiety.

### Evidence arithmetic

If 52% respond to a drug and 37% to placebo, the absolute difference is 0.15 and the **number needed to treat** is the reciprocal, about 6.7.

@example
title: Scoring a PHQ-9
problem: A 46-year-old man answers the nine PHQ-9 items with scores of 1, 2, 2, 3, 1, 2, 1, 2 and 0. What is the total, how severe is the depression, and what is the plan?
step: Total = 1 + 2 + 2 + 3 + 1 + 2 + 1 + 2 + 0 = 14.
step: 10 to 14 is the moderate range. Item 9 is 0, but still ask about suicidal thoughts and means directly.
step: Confirm with an interview for at least five symptoms lasting 2 weeks and screen for past mania, substance use and medical causes (check TSH and a complete blood count).
step: Offer psychotherapy, an SSRI or both. Schedule follow-up within 1 to 2 weeks and reassess response at 4 to 6 weeks.

@mistakes
- Treating a positive PHQ-9 as the diagnosis without a clinical interview.
- Starting an antidepressant without asking about mania or hypomania.
- Judging an SSRI a failure at 1 week or at a subtherapeutic dose.
- Stopping an antidepressant abruptly, causing discontinuation symptoms.
