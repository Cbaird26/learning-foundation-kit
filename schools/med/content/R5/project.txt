### Week by week

- **Week 1, neurologic consult:** localize first (UMN versus LMN, crossed brainstem signs, visual fields); Glasgow Coma Scale; CPP = MAP - ICP.
- **Week 2, acute stroke:** last known well, glucose, CT; NIHSS 0 to 42; lysis within 4.5 hours with BP below 185/110; alteplase 0.9 mg/kg (max 90 mg) with 10% bolus; tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg (max 25 mg); thrombectomy up to 24 hours with imaging; ABCD2.
- **Week 3, seizures:** focal versus generalized; status epilepticus at 5 minutes: benzodiazepine, then levetiracetam, fosphenytoin or valproate, then anesthetic infusion; valproate and pregnancy.
- **Week 4, headache and dizziness:** SNOOP; thunderclap CT within 6 hours; giant cell arteritis steroids first; cluster oxygen; HINTS and Epley.
- **Week 5, weakness:** GBS (IVIG, 20/30/40 rule, no steroids), myasthenia (avoid fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, magnesium), Lambert–Eaton, botulism, cord compression.
- **Week 6, delirium and dementia:** CAM, avoid benzodiazepines and anticholinergics, dementia types, normal pressure hydrocephalus, GBS versus myasthenic crisis.
- **Week 7, psychiatric consult:** MSE, medical causes first, suicide risk factors, screening arithmetic.
- **Week 8, agitation and capacity:** de-escalate first, QTc, four abilities, surrogate, emergency exception.
- **Week 9, depression and anxiety:** PHQ-9 and GAD-7 cut points, screen for mania, SSRIs, 4 to 6 week response, adverse effects.
- **Week 10, psychosis and mania:** duration criteria, extrapyramidal syndromes, NMS, lithium, ANC, first antidepressant.
- **Week 11, withdrawal and overdose:** alcohol timeline, CIWA-Ar, thiamine, naloxone, buprenorphine timing, antidotes, osmolar gap.
- **Week 12, safety and law:** safety plan, 988, hold criteria, duty to protect.

### Common errors

- Naming a diagnosis before localizing; using found time as last known well.
- Delaying a benzodiazepine in status epilepticus; steroids in GBS.
- Diagnosing incapacity from a diagnosis or an unwise choice.
- Starting an antidepressant without asking about mania; relying on a no-suicide contract.

@exam self
prompt: A 70-year-old man with alcohol use disorder is admitted after a fall. On day 3 he is confused, febrile, tremulous and hallucinating, refuses all treatment and says he wants to leave. His heart rate is 124/min and his QT is 420 ms. He cannot say why he is in the hospital. Write a consult plan covering the diagnosis, treatment, capacity for refusing care, and what to do if he tries to leave.
solution: Diagnosis: delirium tremens (48 to 96 hours) with possible head injury, infection and electrolyte problems to exclude. Treat with symptom-triggered or scheduled benzodiazepines (CIWA-Ar is unreliable with delirium; use clinical judgment and escalate to ICU if needed), high-dose IV thiamine, folate, magnesium and potassium, fluids, CT head, infection work-up and ECG for QTc before any antipsychotic. Capacity: he lacks capacity for this decision because he cannot understand the situation (delirium); this is decision-specific and reversible, so reassess as delirium clears. Find a surrogate; if there is no surrogate and delay risks serious harm, treat under the emergency exception. Use de-escalation and one-to-one observation; use restraint only as a last resort per policy. If he tries to leave, he cannot safely do so while lacking capacity, so keep him under observation and follow hospital policy and state law.
