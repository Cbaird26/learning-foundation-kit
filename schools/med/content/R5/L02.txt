@article
"Time is brain": in a large-vessel stroke, millions of neurons die each hour that flow is not restored. The consult question is almost always the same: **can this patient receive reperfusion therapy, and is it safe?**

### The first minutes

1. Establish the **last known well** time (not when the symptoms were found).
2. Check a point-of-care **glucose**; hypoglycemia mimics stroke.
3. Get a **non-contrast head CT** at once to exclude hemorrhage, plus CT angiography of head and neck for a large-vessel occlusion.
4. Examine and score with the **NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS)**.

### The NIH Stroke Scale

The NIHSS has 11 items, 15 scored lines and a range of 0 to 42: level of consciousness (0 to 3), two orientation questions (0 to 2), commands (0 to 2), gaze (0 to 2), visual fields (0 to 3), facial palsy (0 to 3), each arm and each leg (0 to 4), limb ataxia (0 to 2), sensory (0 to 2), language (0 to 3), dysarthria (0 to 2) and extinction or inattention (0 to 2). Add the lines. As a rough guide, 1 to 4 is minor, 5 to 15 moderate, 16 to 20 moderate to severe, and above 20 severe. A high score with cortical signs (gaze deviation, aphasia or neglect) suggests a large-vessel occlusion.

### Intravenous thrombolysis

Alteplase or tenecteplase is given when symptom onset (or last known well) is within **4.5 hours** and a deficit is disabling. Beyond 4.5 hours, or when onset is unknown (wake-up stroke), lysis may still be considered when imaging selects the patient (for example an MRI diffusion-FLAIR mismatch, or salvageable penumbra on perfusion imaging, generally up to about 9 hours), according to local protocol; without such imaging, 4.5 hours is the limit. Before giving it:

- Blood pressure must be below **185/110 mm Hg** (labetalol or nicardipine to reach this) and kept below **180/105** for 24 hours afterward.
- Glucose must be at least 50 mg/dL.
- Exclusions include intracranial hemorrhage on CT, a recent ischemic stroke (within 3 months), major head trauma or intracranial surgery in 3 months, major surgery in 14 days, platelets below 100,000, INR above 1.7, and a direct oral anticoagulant dose in the past 48 hours (with normal renal function).
- There is no upper age limit.

**Typical adult doses for study (verify against current references before any clinical use):**

- Alteplase: **0.9 mg/kg** (maximum 90 mg); 10% as an IV bolus over 1 minute, the remaining 90% as an infusion over 60 minutes.
- Tenecteplase: **0.25 mg/kg** (maximum 25 mg) as a single bolus.

Withhold antithrombotics and invasive procedures for 24 hours afterward, repeat imaging before starting them, and watch for angioedema (especially with ACE inhibitor use) and symptomatic hemorrhage (neurologic worsening, headache, vomiting, hypertension; stop the infusion and image).

### Thrombectomy

Mechanical thrombectomy is added to lysis (or used alone when lysis is contraindicated) for an occlusion of the internal carotid or proximal middle cerebral artery. It is effective up to **6 hours** from onset, and up to **24 hours** with perfusion imaging showing salvageable tissue (DAWN and DEFUSE-3 criteria).

### When no reperfusion is given

Allow **permissive hypertension** up to 220/120 mm Hg in the first 24 to 48 hours. Give aspirin (typically 160 to 325 mg) within 24 to 48 hours; for a minor stroke (NIHSS 3 or lower) or high-risk TIA, dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin plus clopidogrel) for about 21 days is standard. Screen swallowing before any oral intake, maintain normothermia and normoglycemia, and start high-intensity statin therapy. Look for atrial fibrillation and carotid stenosis: carotid endarterectomy benefits symptomatic stenosis of 70 to 99%, ideally within 2 weeks.

### High-risk TIA

The **ABCD2** score estimates early stroke risk: Age 60 or older (1), Blood pressure at least 140/90 (1), Clinical features (unilateral weakness 2, speech disturbance without weakness 1), Duration (60 minutes or more 2, 10 to 59 minutes 1), Diabetes (1). Most centers now evaluate every TIA urgently rather than relying on the score alone.

### Intracerebral hemorrhage

CT shows blood, so no lysis. Lower systolic pressure toward 140 mm Hg, reverse anticoagulants (four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate plus vitamin K for warfarin; idarucizumab for dabigatran; four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate for factor Xa inhibitors, since andexanet alfa was withdrawn from the U.S. market in December 2025) and involve neurosurgery for cerebellar hemorrhage with brainstem compression or hydrocephalus.

@example
title: Decide about thrombolysis
problem: A 68-year-old woman is last known well 2 hours ago. She has right face, arm and leg weakness and aphasia. CT shows no hemorrhage; blood pressure is 196/108 mm Hg; glucose 112 mg/dL; she takes no anticoagulant. What do you recommend?
step: Within 4.5 hours, disabling deficit (aphasia and hemiparesis), no hemorrhage, normal glucose, no anticoagulant: she is a thrombolysis candidate.
step: Her blood pressure of 196/108 is above 185/110. Treat it first with IV labetalol or nicardipine, then recheck; do not withhold lysis, but do not give it until pressure is below the limit.
step: Order CT angiography at the same time; a large-vessel occlusion would add thrombectomy.
step: After lysis, keep blood pressure below 180/105 for 24 hours, make her nil by mouth until a swallow screen, and avoid antithrombotics for 24 hours.

@example
title: Weight-based alteplase
problem: Calculate the total dose, bolus and infusion amount of alteplase for an 83 kg patient.
step: Total dose = 0.9 mg/kg x 83 kg = 74.7 mg. This is below the 90 mg maximum, so no cap applies.
step: Bolus is 10% of the total: 7.47 mg over 1 minute.
step: The remaining 90% is 67.23 mg, infused over 60 minutes.

@mistakes
- Using the time the patient was found, not the last known well time, to decide eligibility.
- Treating blood pressure of 196/108 as a contraindication instead of lowering it below 185/110 first.
- Forgetting the 90 mg ceiling for alteplase in patients over 100 kg.
- Giving thrombolytics before a CT excludes hemorrhage, or giving heparin and aspirin within 24 hours after lysis.
