Script B (SPIKES). Patient: Ms. Anita Shah, 62, retired teacher who had 3 months of change in bowel habit and weight loss and underwent colonoscopy 5 days ago. The clinician (you) is meeting her with the results: biopsy of a colon mass shows adenocarcinoma; staging imaging is pending. Setting: she attends alone but says her daughter could come by phone if wanted. Perception if asked: "I think it's probably something like hemorrhoids, or maybe polyps. They took biopsies." Invitation if asked: she wants "the truth, but gently, please." Reaction: after the news she is silent, then says "My mother died of this. I'm scared." If given empathy she cries quietly, then asks "What happens now?" If you rush to the plan or reassure early, she says "I can't take this in." Strategy: next steps are staging CT scan and referral to a surgeon and an oncologist; she should leave with a follow-up date and a contact number. Check her understanding with teach-back at the end.
Script C (acute abdominal pain). Patient: Mr. Tom Okafor, 24, a graduate student. Opening statement: "My stomach really hurts." History to reveal: the pain began 20 hours ago around the belly button as a dull ache and has moved to the right lower abdomen, now sharp, 8 out of 10, worse with walking, coughing and bumps in the car, no relief from antacids. He has had no appetite since yesterday, vomited once, and has a low-grade fever. No diarrhea, no urinary symptoms, no testicular pain. PMH none, no medications, no allergies. Family history unremarkable. Social: occasional alcohol, no drugs, not sexually active currently. Findings if examined: T 37.9 °C, HR 104, BP 124/76, RR 18. He lies still and prefers not to move. Abdomen not distended, bowel sounds present, percussion tender in the right lower quadrant, tenderness maximal at McBurney point with guarding and rebound, Rovsing sign positive, psoas sign positive, no Murphy sign, no CVA tenderness, testicular examination normal. ## Week-by-week review

**Week 1, interview.** Open with an open-ended question, let the patient finish, then funnel to focused and closed questions. Characterize symptoms with OLDCARTS, then cover PMH, medications, allergies, family, social history and ROS. Pack-years $=$ (cigarettes per day $/20$) $\times$ years.

**Week 2, communication.** Empathy (NURSE), plain language, teach-back, qualified interpreters, and SPIKES: Setting, Perception, Invitation, Knowledge (warning shot), Emotions with empathy, Strategy and Summary.

**Week 3, vital signs.** Ranges: HR 60 to 100, RR 12 to 20, fever at 38.0 °C. 2017 ACC/AHA categories; correct cuff size; orthostatic drop of at least 20 systolic or 10 diastolic. MAP $=$ DBP $+$ (pulse pressure)/3; BMI; shock index $=$ HR/SBP.

**Week 4, HEENT.** RAPD, the LR6 SO4 rule, CN III palsy with pupil involvement (compressive), Weber and Rinne, otoscopy, fundoscopy and papilledema, qSOFA.

**Week 5, cardiovascular.** JVP $+ 5$ for RA pressure; S1, S2 splitting (physiologic, fixed, paradoxical), S3, S4; murmurs and how inspiration, Valsalva, squatting and handgrip change them.

**Week 6, respiratory.** Trachea, expansion, fremitus, percussion, auscultation; consolidation, effusion, pneumothorax, collapse and obstruction patterns; FEV$_1$/FVC below 0.70 means obstruction.

**Week 7, abdomen.** Inspect, auscultate, percuss, palpate; Murphy, McBurney, Rovsing, psoas and Courvoisier; SAAG $\geq 1.1$ g/dL means portal hypertension.

**Week 8, neurology.** UMN versus LMN, reflex roots, GCS, Romberg, cerebellar signs ipsilateral, Brown-Séquard, forehead sparing in UMN facial palsy.

**Week 9, musculoskeletal.** Look, feel, move; Lachman, McMurray, Phalen, straight leg raise, Ottawa ankle rules; inflammatory versus mechanical pain; cauda equina red flags.

**Week 10, skin and nodes.** Macule versus patch, papule versus plaque (1 cm), ABCDE, basal cell carcinoma, rule of nines, node characteristics and Virchow node.

**Week 11, presentations and notes.** One-liner, three-minute order, SOAP, avoid trailing zeros and "U".

**Week 12, reasoning.** Prioritized differential, can't-miss diagnoses, $\text{LR}^+ = \text{sens}/(1-\text{spec})$, odds update, PPV, cognitive biases.

## Self-check

- I can run a full interview and write a one-line summary.
- I can use SPIKES and NURSE statements.
- I can calculate MAP, BMI, shock index, SAAG, pack-years, LR and post-test probability.
- I can name the finding that distinguishes each pair: consolidation vs effusion, UMN vs LMN, conductive vs sensorineural loss.

## Common errors

Leading questions, premature reassurance, mis-sized cuff, calculating LR$^+$ without $1-\text{specificity}$, confusing Weber direction, putting symptoms under Objective, palpating before auscultating, skipping the diagnostic time-out.

(c) Acute coronary syndrome (supported by crescendo pattern, rest pain, diaphoresis and risk factors; S4 consistent with a stiff ventricle); aortic dissection (can't miss: tearing pain and blood pressure difference between arms would support it; the exertional prodrome opposes it); pulmonary embolism (pleuritic pain, hypoxemia, risk factors would support; the exertional pattern and clear oxygenation oppose); pericarditis or GERD (less likely: no positional or pleuritic pattern).
