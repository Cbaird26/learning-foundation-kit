@article
Environmental pathology covers disease caused by toxic exposures, physical agents and lifestyle; nutritional pathology covers too little or too much of what the body needs. Both build on the injury mechanisms from Lessons 1 and 2: hypoxia, free radicals, membrane damage and metabolic poisoning.

### Tobacco

Tobacco smoke is the leading preventable cause of death. It causes lung cancer (squamous and small cell are most strongly associated, though adenocarcinoma is now commonly seen), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, atherosclerosis and coronary disease, stroke, bladder, kidney, pancreatic, oral, laryngeal and esophageal cancers, and peptic ulcer. **Pack-years** equals packs per day times years smoked. In 2021, the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommended annual low-dose CT screening for adults aged 50 to 80 years with at least a 20 pack-year history who currently smoke or quit within the past 15 years.

### Alcohol

Ethanol is oxidized by alcohol dehydrogenase to acetaldehyde and then by aldehyde dehydrogenase to acetate, generating NADH. The excess NADH favors fatty acid synthesis and impairs fatty acid oxidation. Liver disease progresses: **steatosis** (reversible), **alcoholic hepatitis** (hepatocyte swelling, neutrophils, **Mallory–Denk bodies**, with AST greater than ALT, usually by more than 2 to 1), and **cirrhosis** (micronodular). Other effects: pancreatitis, cardiomyopathy, peripheral neuropathy, cerebellar degeneration, and the thiamine-deficiency syndromes **Wernicke encephalopathy** (confusion, ophthalmoplegia, ataxia) and Korsakoff syndrome (amnesia, confabulation). Give thiamine before glucose in patients at risk.

### Drugs and chemicals

**Acetaminophen** in overdose saturates glucuronidation and sulfation; more is oxidized by cytochrome P-450 to **NAPQI**, which depletes glutathione and injures hepatocytes, causing **centrilobular (zone 3) necrosis**. The antidote is **N-acetylcysteine**, which replenishes glutathione; the Rumack–Matthew nomogram guides treatment after a single acute ingestion at a known time.

**Lead** inhibits delta-aminolevulinic acid dehydratase and ferrochelatase in heme synthesis. Findings: microcytic anemia with **basophilic stippling**, abdominal pain and constipation, a **lead line** on the gums, wrist or foot drop (peripheral neuropathy), and in children developmental delay and encephalopathy; sources include old paint, contaminated water and some imported products. Treatment includes removing the source and chelation (succimer, edetate calcium disodium, dimercaprol for severe cases). The U.S. blood lead reference value for children is 3.5 micrograms/dL.

**Carbon monoxide** binds hemoglobin with about 200 times the affinity of oxygen and shifts the dissociation curve left, causing tissue hypoxia. Findings: headache, dizziness, confusion, cherry-red skin (seen late and unreliably), and a normal conventional pulse oximetry reading because it cannot distinguish carboxyhemoglobin from oxyhemoglobin; measure COHb co-oximetrically. Treatment is 100% oxygen, with hyperbaric oxygen for severe cases. Exposure sources: house fires, faulty heaters, car exhaust.

Other: **mercury** (neurotoxicity), **arsenic** (garlic odor, skin changes, bladder and skin cancer), **asbestos** (pleural plaques, asbestosis, mesothelioma), silica, coal dust and beryllium (pneumoconioses).

### Physical injury

**Thermal injury:** heat stroke (core temperature above about 40 °C with central nervous system dysfunction) and hypothermia; burns graded by depth (superficial, partial, full thickness), with the rule of nines for surface area, and complications of fluid loss, infection and shock. **Radiation** damages DNA, with rapidly dividing tissues (marrow, gut) injured first. **Electrical** injury produces deep tissue necrosis and arrhythmias.

### Nutrition

**Obesity** is a body mass index (BMI) of at least 30 kg/m$^2$, where BMI is weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared; overweight is 25 to 29.9. It increases the risk of type 2 diabetes, hypertension, steatotic liver disease, coronary disease, gallstones and some cancers.

**Protein-energy malnutrition:** **Marasmus** is total calorie deficiency: severe wasting, loss of subcutaneous fat, no edema, alert and hungry. **Kwashiorkor** is relatively greater protein deficiency despite some caloric intake: edema from low albumin, **fatty liver**, skin lesions, hair changes, apathy and a protuberant abdomen.

| Vitamin | Deficiency |
|---|---|
| **A** | Night blindness, xerophthalmia, Bitot spots, squamous metaplasia, immunodeficiency |
| **D** | Rickets in children (bowed legs, rachitic rosary), osteomalacia in adults |
| **C** | Scurvy: perifollicular hemorrhages, bleeding gums, poor wound healing (impaired collagen hydroxylation) |
| **K** | Bleeding with prolonged prothrombin time (factors II, VII, IX, X, proteins C and S) |
| **B1 (thiamine)** | Beriberi (dry: neuropathy; wet: high-output heart failure); Wernicke–Korsakoff |
| **B3 (niacin)** | Pellagra: dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia |
| **B12** | Megaloblastic anemia; subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord |
| **Folate** | Megaloblastic anemia; neural tube defects in pregnancy |

Iron deficiency causes microcytic anemia; iodine deficiency causes goiter.

@example
title: A nutritional diagnosis from the pattern of findings
problem: A 3-year-old child in a famine area has pitting edema of the feet, a swollen abdomen with a large soft liver, flaky skin with depigmentation and thin reddish hair. She is apathetic. What is the diagnosis, and why is she edematous?
step: Edema with an enlarged fatty liver and skin changes in a child eating mainly starchy food points to kwashiorkor, a deficiency of dietary protein with relatively preserved calories.
step: Low protein intake causes hypoalbuminemia, reducing plasma oncotic pressure, which allows fluid to move into the interstitium by Starling forces.
step: The fatty liver arises because lipoprotein synthesis requires protein, so triglycerides cannot be exported. In contrast, marasmus shows severe wasting without edema.

@mistakes
- Treating carbon monoxide poisoning as excluded by a normal pulse oximeter reading.
- Confusing marasmus (wasting, no edema, calories deficient) with kwashiorkor (edema, protein deficient).
- Giving glucose to an alcohol-dependent, malnourished patient before thiamine.
- Forgetting that acetaminophen toxicity is zone 3 (centrilobular) because that is where P-450 activity is highest.
