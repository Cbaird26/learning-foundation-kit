@article
**Neoplasia** means new growth: a clonal proliferation of cells that has escaped normal growth control and persists after the stimulus is gone. A **tumor** (or mass) is any swelling; a neoplasm is a tumor made of neoplastic cells. Cancer is fundamentally a **genetic disease of somatic cells**, though a minority arise from inherited mutations.

### Nomenclature and behavior

Benign tumors take the suffix **-oma** with a tissue prefix (adenoma, lipoma, leiomyoma). Malignant tumors of epithelium are **carcinomas** (adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma); of mesenchyme, **sarcomas** (osteosarcoma, liposarcoma). Misleading names: lymphoma, melanoma, seminoma and mesothelioma are malignant. A **teratoma** has tissue from all three germ layers; a **hamartoma** is a disorganized overgrowth of native tissue; a **choristoma** is normal tissue in an abnormal place.

| Feature | Benign | Malignant |
|---|---|---|
| Differentiation | Well differentiated | Variable; **anaplasia** (lack of differentiation) with pleomorphism, hyperchromatic nuclei, high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio, abnormal mitoses |
| Growth | Slow, expansile, often encapsulated | Faster, invasive, poorly demarcated |
| Spread | None | **Metastasis** |
| Recurrence after excision | Rare | Common |

**Dysplasia** with full-thickness involvement of the epithelium but an intact basement membrane is **carcinoma in situ**; invasion through the basement membrane defines invasive cancer.

### Invasion and metastasis

Steps: loss of cell-cell adhesion (reduced **E-cadherin**; epithelial-mesenchymal transition), degradation of the basement membrane and matrix (matrix metalloproteinases), attachment to matrix, migration, **intravasation**, survival in the circulation, arrest and **extravasation**, and growth in a distant organ. Routes: **lymphatic** (typical of carcinomas; first the sentinel node), **hematogenous** (typical of sarcomas, and of renal cell carcinoma and hepatocellular carcinoma), and **seeding** of body cavities (ovarian carcinoma in the peritoneum). Preferred destinations: lung, liver, bone and brain. Colon carcinoma metastasizes to the liver by the portal vein; prostate carcinoma to bone (osteoblastic).

### Genetics of cancer

**Proto-oncogenes** normally promote growth; mutation or overexpression converts them to **oncogenes** with a gain-of-function (one mutated allele is enough). **Tumor suppressor genes** restrain growth or repair DNA; loss of function requires both alleles to be inactivated (**Knudson two-hit hypothesis**). In hereditary syndromes the first hit is germline, so only one somatic hit is required, which explains early, multifocal, bilateral tumors.

Key oncogenes: *RAS* (GTPase; point mutations; the most common oncogene in human cancer), *MYC* (transcription factor; t(8;14) in Burkitt lymphoma), *BCR-ABL* (t(9;22), the Philadelphia chromosome, constitutive tyrosine kinase in chronic myeloid leukemia; imatinib), *HER2/ERBB2* (amplified in a subset of breast cancers; trastuzumab), *BRAF* V600E (melanoma, hairy cell leukemia), *RET* (MEN2), *KIT* (gastrointestinal stromal tumor), *BCL2* (t(14;18), follicular lymphoma), *ALK*.

Key tumor suppressors: **RB** (restrains the G1/S transition; retinoblastoma, osteosarcoma), **TP53** (guardian of the genome: arrests the cell cycle, repairs DNA or induces apoptosis; Li–Fraumeni syndrome; mutated in over half of cancers), *APC* (familial adenomatous polyposis), *BRCA1* and *BRCA2* (breast, ovarian cancer), *NF1*, *PTEN*, *VHL* (renal cell carcinoma and hemangioblastoma), *WT1*.

**DNA repair defects:** Lynch syndrome (hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer) from **mismatch repair** gene mutations (*MLH1*, *MSH2*, *MSH6*, *PMS2*), with **microsatellite instability**; **xeroderma pigmentosum** (nucleotide excision repair defect; skin cancer with UV exposure); ataxia-telangiectasia; BRCA defects impair homologous recombination.

Other hallmarks: **telomerase** reactivation (limitless replication), sustained **angiogenesis** (VEGF), evasion of apoptosis, **Warburg effect** (aerobic glycolysis), evasion of immune surveillance (for example PD-L1 expression), and a tumor microenvironment that supports growth.

### Carcinogens

- **Chemicals:** initiation (a permanent DNA change) followed by promotion (clonal expansion). Examples: polycyclic hydrocarbons and tobacco (lung, bladder), **aflatoxin B1** (hepatocellular carcinoma, TP53 mutation), vinyl chloride (angiosarcoma of liver), **asbestos** (mesothelioma and lung carcinoma), aromatic amines (bladder), alkylating agents.
- **Radiation:** UV light causes pyrimidine dimers (basal cell, squamous cell carcinoma, melanoma); ionizing radiation causes leukemias and thyroid cancer.
- **Oncogenic viruses:** **HPV** (E6 degrades p53, E7 inactivates RB; cervical, anal and oropharyngeal squamous carcinoma); **EBV** (Burkitt lymphoma, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, Hodgkin lymphoma); **HBV and HCV** (hepatocellular carcinoma via chronic inflammation); **HTLV-1** (adult T cell leukemia); **HHV-8** (Kaposi sarcoma); *Helicobacter pylori* (gastric adenocarcinoma and MALT lymphoma, a bacterium).

### Tumor growth kinetics

Clinical detection occurs at about $10^9$ cells (about 1 cm, 1 g) after about 30 doublings; the patient dies at about $10^{12}$ cells (1 kg), only about 10 doublings later. Most of a cancer's life history is therefore clinically silent. The number of doublings to reach $N$ cells from one cell is $\log_2 N$.

@example
title: How long does it take a tumor to become detectable?
problem: A tumor begins with one cell and its cells double every 2 months. How many doublings produce 10^9 cells, and how many months does this take, roughly?
step: The number of doublings is $\log_2(10^9) = 9 \times \log_2(10)$.
step: Since $\log_2(10)\approx 3.32$, this is $9 \times 3.32 \approx 29.9$, about 30 doublings.
step: At 2 months per doubling, the time is about $29.9 \times 2 \approx 60$ months, or 5 years, of silent growth before reaching about 1 cm.

@mistakes
- Calling every tumor named with "-oma" benign; lymphoma, melanoma, seminoma and mesothelioma are malignant.
- Saying that tumor suppressor loss needs only one hit. Both alleles must be lost (except for the germline first hit in hereditary cases).
- Assuming carcinomas spread mainly through blood. Carcinomas favor lymphatic routes; sarcomas favor hematogenous spread.
- Confusing carcinoma in situ (no invasion through the basement membrane) with invasive carcinoma.

prompt: A tumor grows from a volume of 0.5 cubic centimeters to 4 cubic centimeters over 9 months with exponential growth. What is the volume doubling time in months?
answer: 3
check: 9 / math.log2(4 / 0.5)
tol: 0.05

hint: Find how many times the volume doubled.
solution: 4 / 0.5 = 8, which is 3 doublings, so the doubling time is 9 / 3 = 3 months.
