@article
**Inflammation** is the host's protective response to infection and tissue damage. It delivers leukocytes and plasma proteins to the site, eliminates the cause, and starts repair. Acute inflammation begins within minutes, lasts hours to a few days, and is dominated by fluid, fibrin and **neutrophils**. The five cardinal signs are **rubor** (redness), **calor** (heat), **tumor** (swelling), **dolor** (pain) and **functio laesa** (loss of function).

### Vascular events explain the signs

After a brief arteriolar vasoconstriction, **vasodilation** of arterioles opens capillary beds. Histamine (from mast cells) and nitric oxide mediate it. The increased blood flow causes **rubor** and **calor**.

At the same time vessels become more permeable. The mechanisms are:

- **Endothelial contraction** in venules, triggered by histamine, bradykinin and leukotrienes C$_4$, D$_4$ and E$_4$. This is rapid and short-lived (15 to 30 minutes).
- **Cytoskeletal reorganization** driven by cytokines (TNF, IL-1), appearing after 4 to 6 hours and lasting more than a day.
- **Direct endothelial injury** (burns, toxins), which is immediate and may be prolonged.
- **Leukocyte-mediated injury** and **transcytosis** (VEGF) as additional routes.

Protein-rich fluid leaves the vessels and creates edema (**tumor**). By Starling forces, increased vascular hydrostatic pressure from vasodilation and loss of plasma oncotic gradient as protein escapes both drive fluid into the tissue. An inflammatory **exudate** is protein-rich (above about 3 g/dL) with high specific gravity (above 1.020) and often cells; a **transudate** is protein-poor (heart failure, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome). **Pus** is a purulent exudate of neutrophils, necrotic cells and fluid. **Pain** comes from bradykinin and prostaglandin E$_2$ acting on sensory nerve endings, plus pressure of edema.

### Cellular events: recruiting the neutrophil

1. **Margination.** Slowed flow lets leukocytes drift to the vessel wall.
2. **Rolling.** Weak, reversible binding of **selectins** to sialyl Lewis X on leukocytes. P-selectin (stored in Weibel–Palade bodies, mobilized by histamine) and E-selectin (induced by TNF and IL-1) are on endothelium; L-selectin is on leukocytes.
3. **Firm adhesion.** Cytokines raise endothelial **ICAM-1** and VCAM-1 and chemokines activate leukocyte **integrins** (LFA-1, Mac-1) to a high-affinity state.
4. **Transmigration (diapedesis)** between endothelial cells, mediated by **PECAM-1 (CD31)**.
5. **Chemotaxis** toward chemoattractants: bacterial formylated peptides, **C5a**, **leukotriene B$_4$**, and **IL-8 (CXCL8)**.

Neutrophils dominate for the first 6 to 24 hours; monocytes and macrophages follow at 24 to 48 hours.

### Phagocytosis and killing

Leukocytes recognize microbes directly or through **opsonins** (IgG, C3b). The microbe is engulfed into a phagosome that fuses with a lysosome. Killing uses **reactive oxygen species**: **NADPH oxidase** makes superoxide, which becomes hydrogen peroxide; **myeloperoxidase** converts H$_2$O$_2$ plus chloride into hypochlorous acid (bleach). Neutrophils also release **extracellular traps** (NETs).

### Chemical mediators

- **Histamine:** vasodilation, increased venular permeability (early).
- **Prostaglandins** (COX pathway): PGE$_2$ gives vasodilation, pain and fever; NSAIDs block COX.
- **Leukotrienes** (lipoxygenase): LTB$_4$ chemotaxis; LTC$_4$, D$_4$, E$_4$ bronchoconstriction and permeability.
- **Complement:** C3a and C5a (anaphylatoxins) release histamine; C5a is chemotactic; C3b opsonizes; the membrane attack complex lyses microbes.
- **Cytokines TNF, IL-1, IL-6:** endothelial activation, fever, and the **acute-phase response**: the liver makes C-reactive protein, fibrinogen, serum amyloid A and hepcidin. Fibrinogen causes red cells to form rouleaux, raising the **ESR**; hepcidin lowers iron availability (anemia of chronic disease). Leukocytosis with a left shift (more bands) accompanies bacterial infection.
- **Bradykinin:** pain and permeability.

### Inherited defects of leukocytes

- **Leukocyte adhesion deficiency type 1:** defect of the CD18 integrin subunit; delayed umbilical cord separation, recurrent infections without pus, marked neutrophilia.
- **Chronic granulomatous disease:** NADPH oxidase defect; no respiratory burst; recurrent infections with catalase-positive organisms (*Staphylococcus aureus*, *Aspergillus*, *Burkholderia cepacia*, *Serratia*); abnormal dihydrorhodamine test, formerly the nitroblue tetrazolium test.
- **Chediak–Higashi syndrome:** LYST gene defect; failed phagolysosome fusion, giant granules, partial albinism, neuropathy.

### Outcomes

Acute inflammation resolves completely, heals by fibrosis (if tissue is destroyed), forms an **abscess**, or progresses to chronic inflammation. Morphologic patterns: serous (blister), fibrinous (pericarditis, "bread and butter"), purulent, and ulcer.

@example
title: Explaining a red, swollen, painful cellulitis
problem: A 34-year-old woman has a warm, red, swollen, tender area on her shin 12 hours after a scrape. Explain each cardinal sign at the vascular or cellular level.
step: Redness and heat (rubor, calor) result from arteriolar vasodilation, caused by histamine and nitric oxide, which increases blood flow to the area.
step: Swelling (tumor) results from increased venular permeability (endothelial contraction by histamine and leukotrienes C4, D4, E4; later cytokine-driven changes) allowing protein-rich exudate to leave the vessel, together with increased hydrostatic pressure.
step: Pain (dolor) is caused by bradykinin and prostaglandin E2 on nerve endings plus stretching from edema. Loss of function follows from pain and swelling.
step: Neutrophils arrive by rolling on selectins, adhering through integrin binding to ICAM-1, crossing the venule through PECAM-1-mediated transmigration, and following chemoattractants such as C5a, LTB4 and IL-8 to the bacteria.

@mistakes
- Saying that vasodilation alone causes swelling; permeability change is required for exudate.
- Confusing rolling (selectins, reversible) with firm adhesion (integrins binding ICAM-1).
- Calling any edema fluid an exudate. A transudate has low protein and little inflammation.
- Linking NADPH oxidase to myeloperoxidase: NADPH oxidase makes superoxide; myeloperoxidase makes hypochlorous acid from hydrogen peroxide.
