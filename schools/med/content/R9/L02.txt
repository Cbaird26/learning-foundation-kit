@article
### Acute coronary syndromes

Plaque rupture with thrombus causes **STEMI** (complete occlusion, ST elevation or new left bundle branch block with a compatible story) or **NSTEMI/unstable angina** (partial occlusion, troponin elevated or not). For both, give **aspirin** and obtain an ECG within 10 minutes.

- **STEMI:** emergent reperfusion. Primary PCI with a door-to-balloon time of 90 minutes or less is preferred; if PCI cannot be done within 120 minutes of first medical contact, give **fibrinolysis** within 30 minutes of arrival (check contraindications: prior intracranial hemorrhage, recent stroke, active bleeding, suspected dissection, severe uncontrolled hypertension). Add a P2Y12 inhibitor, anticoagulation, a statin and a beta-blocker unless there is heart failure, low output or heart block. Inferior STEMI with right ventricular involvement is preload dependent: avoid nitrates and give fluids.
- **NSTEMI:** antiplatelets, anticoagulation, anti-ischemic therapy and risk-stratified invasive evaluation (urgent angiography if refractory pain, hemodynamic or electrical instability).

### Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction

Four drug classes reduce mortality: an **ARNI** (sacubitril-valsartan; or an ACE inhibitor or ARB), an evidence-based **beta-blocker** (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol), a **mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist** (spironolactone, eplerenone) and an **SGLT2 inhibitor**. Loop diuretics relieve congestion but do not improve survival. Avoid non-dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers. An implantable defibrillator is considered when ejection fraction stays 35% or less despite 3 months of therapy.

### Atrial fibrillation

Unstable (hypotension, ischemia, pulmonary edema): synchronized cardioversion. Stable: rate control (beta-blocker or diltiazem) or rhythm control. Stroke prevention is guided by **CHA2DS2-VASc**: congestive heart failure 1, hypertension 1, age 75 or older 2, diabetes 1, prior stroke/TIA/thromboembolism 2, vascular disease 1, age 65 to 74 1, female sex 1. Anticoagulation is recommended at a score of 2 or more in men and 3 or more in women; direct oral anticoagulants are preferred over warfarin except in moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis or a mechanical valve. AF of unknown duration or lasting over 48 hours needs anticoagulation (or transesophageal echocardiography) before elective cardioversion.

### Aortic dissection

Tearing chest or back pain, pulse or blood pressure differential, widened mediastinum. In an unstable patient, use transesophageal echocardiography; in a stable one, CT angiography. Lower shear stress first with an IV beta-blocker (esmolol or labetalol) to a heart rate near 60 and systolic pressure 100 to 120 mm Hg, adding a vasodilator only after beta-blockade. Type A (ascending) needs emergent surgery; uncomplicated type B is managed medically.

### Pulmonary embolism and the A-a gradient

A hemodynamically unstable PE (shock) receives systemic thrombolysis; a stable PE receives anticoagulation. The **alveolar-arterial gradient** separates hypoventilation from V/Q mismatch. At sea level on room air:

$$P_AO_2=F_iO_2\,(P_{atm}-47)-\frac{P_aCO_2}{0.8},\qquad A\text{-}a=P_AO_2-P_aO_2.$$

A normal gradient (about age/4 + 4) with hypoxemia and hypercapnia suggests hypoventilation; an elevated gradient suggests V/Q mismatch, shunt or diffusion defects.

### COPD and asthma

COPD exacerbation: inhaled short-acting bronchodilators, systemic corticosteroids for about 5 days, antibiotics when sputum purulence plus increased volume or dyspnea, and **noninvasive ventilation** for acute hypercapnic respiratory failure (pH below 7.35 with elevated PaCO2). Target oxygen saturation of 88 to 92%. Severe asthma: albuterol plus ipratropium, systemic steroids, magnesium for refractory cases; a "quiet chest" and normalizing PaCO2 signal impending failure.

@example
title: CHA2DS2-VASc and the anticoagulation decision
problem: A 76-year-old woman has new atrial fibrillation. She has hypertension and type 2 diabetes but no heart failure, stroke or vascular disease. Calculate her CHA2DS2-VASc score and decide whether to anticoagulate.
step: Age 75 or older: 2 points. Female sex: 1 point.
step: Hypertension: 1 point. Diabetes: 1 point. There is no heart failure, stroke or vascular disease.
step: Total: $2+1+1+1=5$.
step: The score exceeds the threshold for women (3 or more), so start long-term anticoagulation, preferably a direct oral anticoagulant, after checking bleeding risk.

@example
title: Calculating the A-a gradient
problem: On room air at sea level, a patient has PaO2 70 mm Hg and PaCO2 40 mm Hg. Calculate the A-a gradient.
step: $P_AO_2=0.21\times(760-47)-\frac{40}{0.8}=0.21\times 713-50$.
step: $0.21\times713=149.7$, so $P_AO_2=149.7-50=99.7$ mm Hg.
step: $A\text{-}a=99.7-70=29.7$ mm Hg, which is elevated for most ages and points to V/Q mismatch rather than pure hypoventilation.

@mistakes
- Giving a nitrate or large diuretic dose to a patient with an inferior STEMI and right ventricular infarction.
- Starting a vasodilator (nitroprusside) before a beta-blocker in aortic dissection, which causes reflex tachycardia and increases shear stress.
- Using an oxygen target of 100% in COPD with hypercapnia, or intubating before trying noninvasive ventilation when the patient is alert and has no contraindication.
- Forgetting that warfarin, not a direct oral anticoagulant, is used for mechanical valves and moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis.
