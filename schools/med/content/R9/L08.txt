@article
### Prenatal care and dating

**Naegele rule:** estimated due date is the first day of the last menstrual period minus 3 months plus 7 days (a 40-week gestation). Early ultrasonography corrects the date if it differs substantially. Screen for gestational diabetes at 24 to 28 weeks (50-g glucose challenge, then a 3-hour test if positive); give Tdap at 27 to 36 weeks of every pregnancy; screen for group B Streptococcus at 36 0/7 to 37 6/7 weeks and give intrapartum penicillin G to carriers. **Rh-negative** women with no anti-D antibodies receive Rh immune globulin (typical dose 300 micrograms IM) at 28 weeks and within 72 hours of delivering an Rh-positive infant or after bleeding or procedures. Offer cell-free DNA and nuchal translucency screening; low-dose aspirin starting at 12 to 28 weeks lowers the risk of preeclampsia in high-risk women.

### Bleeding in pregnancy

- **Ectopic pregnancy** (amenorrhea, pain, bleeding, hCG not rising appropriately, no intrauterine pregnancy on transvaginal ultrasonography): stable, unruptured, with reliable follow-up and no contraindications: **methotrexate**. Ruptured, unstable, or fetal cardiac activity: surgery.
- **Placenta previa:** painless bright red bleeding in the third trimester. Do **no digital cervical examination**; confirm with transabdominal ultrasonography. Cesarean delivery for complete previa.
- **Placental abruption:** painful bleeding with a tense, tender uterus (risk factors: hypertension, cocaine, trauma). It may cause DIC and fetal distress; stabilize and deliver.

### Hypertensive disorders

Preeclampsia is new hypertension of at least 140/90 mm Hg after 20 weeks with proteinuria or end-organ features. **Severe features:** BP of 160/110 mm Hg or higher, platelets below 100,000/mm3, doubled or elevated creatinine, transaminases twice normal, pulmonary edema, or new headache or visual symptoms. Treat with **IV magnesium sulfate** to prevent seizures, antihypertensives for severe-range pressure (labetalol, hydralazine, or oral nifedipine), and delivery at 34 weeks or beyond (immediately if unstable). Magnesium toxicity first appears as loss of deep tendon reflexes, then respiratory depression; treat with calcium gluconate. Avoid ACE inhibitors and ARBs in pregnancy. Eclampsia is a seizure and is managed with magnesium, then delivery.

### Labor complications

Preterm labor between 24 and 34 weeks: antenatal betamethasone, magnesium sulfate for neuroprotection before 32 weeks, and GBS prophylaxis; tocolysis is short-term to allow steroids. Preterm rupture of membranes: antibiotics (ampicillin and a macrolide) for latency. Fetal heart-rate tracing: variable decelerations from cord compression; recurrent late decelerations indicate uteroplacental insufficiency (reposition, treat hypotension, stop oxytocin, give oxygen if needed). **Postpartum hemorrhage** (4 Ts: tone, trauma, tissue, thrombin): uterine massage, oxytocin, then methylergonovine (avoid in hypertension), carboprost (avoid in asthma) or misoprostol, tranexamic acid, balloon tamponade, then surgery. Quantitative blood loss, for example weighing soaked sponges (1 g is about 1 mL), is more accurate than visual estimates.

### Gynecology

- **Abnormal uterine bleeding** (PALM-COEIN): pregnancy test first; endometrial sampling for age 45 or older, or younger with risk factors or failed therapy; hormonal treatment or tranexamic acid. Postmenopausal bleeding needs endometrial thickness measurement or biopsy.
- **Pelvic inflammatory disease:** cervical motion tenderness; outpatient ceftriaxone plus doxycycline plus metronidazole; admit for tubo-ovarian abscess or pregnancy.
- **Ovarian torsion:** acute unilateral pain with nausea; Doppler ultrasonography, then laparoscopy for detorsion.
- **Cervical cancer screening:** cytology every 3 years at 21 to 29; at 30 to 65, cytology every 3 years, primary HPV testing every 5 years, or cotesting every 5 years. Patient-collected HPV self-sampling in healthcare settings was approved in 2024 and incorporated into recent guidance.

@example
title: Estimating the due date
problem: A woman's last menstrual period began on August 10. Using the Naegele rule, what is her estimated due date?
step: Subtract 3 months from August 10: May 10.
step: Add 7 days: May 17.
step: The estimated due date is May 17 of the following year. Early ultrasonography changes this only if the discrepancy exceeds a set number of days for the gestational age.

@example
title: Rh immune globulin
problem: A 29-year-old woman is Rh(D)-negative, antibody-screen negative, and 28 weeks pregnant. What is the appropriate management?
step: She is at risk of alloimmunization from fetomaternal hemorrhage.
step: Give Rh immune globulin (typical dose 300 micrograms IM) at 28 weeks.
step: Give another dose within 72 hours of delivery if the newborn is Rh(D)-positive, and after bleeding episodes or procedures such as amniocentesis.

@mistakes
- Performing a digital cervical examination in a patient with painless third-trimester bleeding before ultrasonography excludes placenta previa.
- Using methylergonovine for postpartum hemorrhage in a hypertensive patient, or carboprost in an asthmatic patient.
- Giving magnesium sulfate to treat hypertension instead of to prevent seizures, or failing to check reflexes and respiratory rate during infusion.
- Using methotrexate in an unstable patient or when there is fetal cardiac activity.

prompt: A woman's last menstrual period began on January 15. On March 26 of the same (non-leap) year, how many completed weeks of gestation does she have by dates?
answer: 10
check: (16 + 28 + 26) // 7
hint: Count the days: rest of January after the 15th, all of February, then the days in March.
solution: From January 15 to January 31 is 16 days, February adds 28, and March adds 26, so $16+28+26=70$ days, which is exactly 10 weeks.

@practice self
prompt: A 31-year-old primigravida at 36 weeks has a headache and blurred vision. BP is 168/112 mm Hg on two measurements 15 minutes apart, platelets are 88,000/mm3 and AST is 120 U/L. Write your management plan and name the signs you will monitor during therapy.
hint: Identify the severe features present.
hint: Think of seizure prevention, blood pressure goals, delivery and toxicity monitoring.
solution: She has preeclampsia with severe features (severe-range BP, thrombocytopenia, elevated transaminases, neurologic symptoms), possibly HELLP syndrome. Admit and give IV magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis. Treat severe-range blood pressure promptly (within 30 to 60 minutes) with IV labetalol, IV hydralazine or oral nifedipine, aiming for roughly 140 to 150/90 to 100 mm Hg. At 36 weeks, plan delivery after maternal stabilization. Monitor deep tendon reflexes, respiratory rate, urine output and magnesium level if renal function is impaired; treat toxicity with calcium gluconate. Follow fetal status and labs, and continue magnesium for 24 hours postpartum.
