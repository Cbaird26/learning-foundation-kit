@article
### Cardiac arrest

Start high-quality CPR (rate 100 to 120/min, depth about 5 cm, full recoil, minimal interruptions) and attach a monitor. **Shockable** rhythms (ventricular fibrillation, pulseless ventricular tachycardia): defibrillate, resume CPR for 2 minutes, give epinephrine 1 mg IV every 3 to 5 minutes, and add amiodarone (300 mg, then 150 mg) after repeated shocks. **Nonshockable** rhythms (asystole, PEA): CPR and epinephrine as soon as possible, plus a search for reversible causes (hypovolemia, hypoxia, acidosis, hypo/hyperkalemia, hypothermia, tension pneumothorax, tamponade, toxins, thrombosis). The drug doses in this lesson are typical adult doses for study; check current references before any clinical use.

### Peri-arrest rhythms

- **Unstable tachycardia** (hypotension, ischemia, altered mentation, shock): synchronized cardioversion.
- **Stable narrow, regular tachycardia (SVT):** vagal maneuvers, then adenosine 6 mg rapid IV push, then 12 mg if needed. **Torsades de pointes:** IV magnesium.
- **Symptomatic bradycardia:** atropine 1 mg IV; if ineffective, transcutaneous pacing or infusion of dopamine or epinephrine. Mobitz II and third-degree block usually need pacing.

### Toxicology

| Poisoning | Key clues | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Acetaminophen | Asymptomatic early; liver injury later | N-acetylcysteine if above the treatment line on the Rumack-Matthew nomogram (level at 4 h or later) |
| Salicylates | Tinnitus, tachypnea, mixed respiratory alkalosis and anion gap acidosis | IV sodium bicarbonate; hemodialysis if severe |
| Opioids | Miosis, hypoventilation | Naloxone |
| Organophosphates | Salivation, lacrimation, bradycardia, miosis, fasciculations | Atropine plus pralidoxime |
| Carbon monoxide | Headache, confusion, normal SpO2 | 100% oxygen; consider hyperbaric oxygen |
| Tricyclic antidepressants | Wide QRS, seizures | Sodium bicarbonate |
| Methanol, ethylene glycol | Anion gap acidosis, vision changes or crystals | Fomepizole |
| Beta-blocker or calcium-channel blocker | Bradycardia, hypotension | Calcium, glucagon, high-dose insulin |
| Digoxin | Nausea, visual halos, arrhythmia | Digoxin-specific antibody fragments |

### Neurologic and allergic emergencies

**Ischemic stroke:** check glucose and obtain noncontrast head CT immediately. If no hemorrhage and last known well within 4.5 hours, give IV thrombolysis after excluding contraindications, with blood pressure below 185/110 mm Hg beforehand. CT angiography identifies large-vessel occlusion for thrombectomy, which extends to selected patients up to 24 hours. Withhold antiplatelets for 24 hours after thrombolysis. **Subarachnoid hemorrhage:** thunderclap headache; noncontrast CT within 6 hours is highly sensitive, otherwise lumbar puncture or CT angiography; give nimodipine. **Status epilepticus:** a benzodiazepine first (IV lorazepam or IM midazolam), then a second-line agent (levetiracetam, fosphenytoin or valproate). **Anaphylaxis:** epinephrine 0.3 to 0.5 mg of 1 mg/mL IM into the anterolateral thigh (typical adult dose for study), then fluids, airway support and adjuncts; antihistamines and steroids are secondary.

### Environmental

Heat stroke (temperature above 40 C with central nervous system dysfunction): rapid cooling by ice-water immersion. Severe hypothermia: gentle rewarming, and continue resuscitation until the patient is warm.

### Infusion arithmetic

Drug rate $=\frac{\text{dose}\ (\mu g/kg/min)\times\text{weight}\times60}{\text{concentration}\ (\mu g/mL)}$ in mL/h. Mean arterial pressure $=\frac{SBP+2\times DBP}{3}$.

@example
title: Ventricular fibrillation
problem: A 62-year-old man collapses in the emergency department. The monitor shows ventricular fibrillation and he is pulseless. After CPR is started, what is the next step, and what comes after that?
step: VF is a shockable rhythm, so the next step is immediate defibrillation.
step: Resume CPR for 2 minutes without checking a pulse, then reassess the rhythm.
step: If VF persists, shock again, give epinephrine 1 mg IV every 3 to 5 minutes, and add amiodarone after repeated shocks; search for reversible causes.

@example
title: Norepinephrine infusion rate
problem: A 70-kg patient in septic shock is to receive norepinephrine at 0.2 mcg/kg/min. The bag contains 8 mg in 250 mL. What pump rate in mL/h is needed?
step: Concentration $=8000\ \mu g/250\ mL=32\ \mu g/mL$.
step: Dose per hour $=0.2\times70\times60=840\ \mu g/h$.
step: Rate $=840/32=26.25$ mL/h.

@mistakes
- Checking a pulse or rhythm too soon after a shock instead of resuming CPR for a full 2-minute cycle.
- Giving flumazenil for benzodiazepine overdose (it can precipitate seizures) or waiting for imaging before treating suspected tension pneumothorax.
- Relying on SpO2 in carbon monoxide poisoning; pulse oximetry reads carboxyhemoglobin as oxyhemoglobin.
- Treating anaphylaxis with antihistamines first rather than epinephrine IM.

prompt: A patient weighing 80 kg receives norepinephrine at 0.1 mcg/kg/min from a bag containing 4 mg in 250 mL. What pump rate in mL/h is required?
answer: 30
check: 0.1 * 80 * 60 / (4000 / 250)
unit: mL/h
hint: Convert 4 mg in 250 mL to micrograms per mL.
solution: Concentration $=4000/250=16\ \mu g/mL$. Dose $=0.1\times80\times60=480\ \mu g/h$. Rate $=480/16=30$ mL/h.

unit: mm Hg
hint: Weight the diastolic pressure twice.
solution: $MAP=(84+2\times48)/3=180/3=60$ mm Hg, below the 65 mm Hg target.

prompt: A 68-year-old woman has sudden right hemiparesis and aphasia that began 90 minutes ago. Glucose is 110 mg/dL and BP is 168/94 mm Hg. Describe the order of evaluation and treatment, and list two contraindications that would stop thrombolysis.

hint: Think of the 4.5-hour window and a blood pressure threshold.
solution: Ensure airway and oxygenation, check glucose (done), obtain an immediate noncontrast head CT to exclude hemorrhage, and take a focused history for time last known well and anticoagulant use. If the CT shows no hemorrhage, she is within 4.5 hours and has no contraindications, give IV thrombolysis (blood pressure of 168/94 is below the 185/110 mm Hg threshold). Obtain CT angiography to look for a large-vessel occlusion and refer for thrombectomy if present. Avoid antiplatelets and anticoagulants for 24 hours after thrombolysis. Contraindications include intracranial hemorrhage on CT, recent major surgery or head trauma, a recent intracranial hemorrhage history, uncontrolled blood pressure above 185/110 mm Hg, active internal bleeding, platelets below 100,000/mm3, or therapeutic anticoagulation.
