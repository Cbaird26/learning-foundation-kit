title: Clinical-reasoning portfolio
overview: You will work through twenty supplied vignettes from the major Step 2 CK disciplines. For each, write the diagnosis, the best next step, and a brief reason why each of the main alternatives is wrong, then log your errors and repair your weakest area.
goal: Apply the six-step method (last line, stability, syndrome, pathway, prediction, elimination) consistently under realistic time pressure.
goal: Justify each answer and each rejected alternative with a specific reason (wrong patient, wrong time, wrong invasiveness, wrong question).
goal: Identify patterns in your errors and design a targeted repair plan.
step: Create a portfolio template with these columns for each case: case number, diagnosis, key clues (two or three), best next step, three alternatives with the reason each is wrong, and a confidence rating (1 to 3).
step: Complete cases 1 to 10 in one timed session of 15 minutes (90 seconds each) without notes; write short phrases, not paragraphs.
step: Complete cases 11 to 20 in a second timed session of 15 minutes, again without notes.
step: Compare each answer with the answer key provided in the materials. Mark each miss as a knowledge gap, reasoning error, reading error or test-taking error.
step: For every miss and every low-confidence correct answer, rewrite the case in your own words in two sentences and state the principle that would have led you to the right answer.
step: Count errors by category and by specialty, choose the weakest area, and write a one-week repair plan with specific topics, a number of practice questions and a re-test date.
step: Redo the missed cases after at least 2 days, then write a half-page reflection on how your method changed.

@review
### Week 1: Method
Read the last line first, triage stability, name the syndrome, find the step in the pathway, predict, then eliminate. Convert pretest probability to odds, multiply by the likelihood ratio, and convert back.

### Week 2: Cardiology and pulmonology
STEMI: primary PCI within 90 minutes or fibrinolysis if PCI is not available in 120 minutes. HFrEF: ARNI or ACE inhibitor, evidence-based beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2 inhibitor. CHA2DS2-VASc, aortic dissection (beta-blocker first), COPD exacerbation (noninvasive ventilation), the A-a gradient.

### Week 3: Gastroenterology, nephrology, endocrinology
Variceal bleeding (octreotide, ceftriaxone, banding), SBP (paracentesis, ceftriaxone, albumin), cholangitis, pancreatitis. Anion gap, FENa, corrected calcium, hyperkalemia (calcium first). DKA (potassium before insulin; 2024 consensus emphasizes ketones), adrenal crisis.

### Week 4: Infectious disease and hematology
Sepsis bundle, meningitis (antibiotics without delay), HIV prophylaxis, C. difficile, ANC and reticulocyte calculations, TTP (plasma exchange), HIT (stop heparin, non-heparin anticoagulant).

### Week 5: Surgery and trauma
ABCDE, tension pneumothorax, hemorrhage classes, FAST (unstable goes to the operating room), Parkland formula, mesenteric ischemia, postoperative fever timing.

### Week 6: Emergency medicine
ACLS (shock VF, CPR, epinephrine), unstable bradycardia (atropine, pacing), toxicology antidotes, stroke thrombolysis window and blood pressure limit, infusion calculations.

### Week 7: Pediatrics
Neonatal fever, pyloric stenosis, intussusception, malrotation, croup, Kawasaki disease, the 4-2-1 rule, milestones, abuse reporting.

### Week 8: Obstetrics and gynecology
Naegele rule, Rh immune globulin, ectopic pregnancy, previa versus abruption, preeclampsia with magnesium, postpartum hemorrhage, PID, cervical screening.

### Week 9: Neurology and psychiatry
Stroke prevention, GBS (IVIG, no steroids), giant cell arteritis (steroids first), thiamine before glucose, lithium toxicity, suicide assessment, GCS.

### Week 10: Preventive medicine and ethics
Screening ages, pack-years, immunizations, capacity, surrogate decisions, minors, confidentiality limits, error disclosure.

### Week 11: Biostatistics and patient safety
Sensitivity, specificity, PPV and prevalence, NNT, study designs, biases, Swiss cheese model and system fixes.

### Week 12: Strategy
Pacing at 90 seconds per question, elimination, error-log categories.

### Self-check
- I can state stable versus unstable and what that changes.
- I can calculate an anion gap, FENa, A-a gradient, Parkland rate and NNT without notes.
- I can explain why each distractor is wrong.

### Common errors
Treating the question as a diagnosis item, choosing definitive imaging in an unstable patient, giving the right drug at the wrong time, ignoring pregnancy or renal function, and mixing up relative and absolute risk.

prompt: A screening test has sensitivity 90% and specificity 80%. A patient has a pretest probability of disease of 20%. If the test is positive, what is the post-test probability, as a decimal rounded to three places?
answer: 0.529
check: (0.9*0.2) / (0.9*0.2 + (1-0.8)*(1-0.2))
tol: 0.005
hint: Compute the positive likelihood ratio, then use odds.
hint: $LR^+=0.90/0.20=4.5$; pretest odds are $0.25$.
solution: $LR^+=4.5$. Post-test odds $=0.25\times4.5=1.125$, so probability $=1.125/2.125=0.529$.

answer: 306
check: 2 * 128 + 720 / 18 + 28 / 2.8
tol: 1
unit: mOsm/kg
hint: Calculate each term separately.
hint: Sodium contributes $256$, glucose $40$.
solution: $2\times128=256$; $720/18=40$; $28/2.8=10$. The total is $306$ mOsm/kg.

@exam mc
prompt: A 31-year-old woman at 34 weeks of gestation with preeclampsia with severe features is receiving a magnesium sulfate infusion. Two hours into the infusion she is drowsy, her respiratory rate is 8/min, and her patellar reflexes are absent. Her BP is 148/92 mm Hg and oxygen saturation is 93%. What is the best next step?
choice: Increase the magnesium infusion rate
choice: Administer intravenous naloxone
choice*: Stop the magnesium infusion and give intravenous calcium gluconate while supporting ventilation
choice: Perform an immediate cesarean delivery without changing the infusion
hint: Which sign is the earliest marker of magnesium toxicity, and what is the antidote?
solution: Loss of deep tendon reflexes followed by respiratory depression indicates magnesium toxicity. Stop the infusion, support ventilation, check a magnesium level and renal function, and give calcium gluconate, the antagonist. Increasing the rate worsens toxicity, and naloxone does not reverse magnesium.
