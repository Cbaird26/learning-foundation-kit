@article
### Stroke and TIA

After acute treatment, secondary prevention for ischemic stroke or TIA includes antiplatelet therapy (short-term dual antiplatelet therapy for minor stroke or high-risk TIA, then single therapy), high-intensity statin, blood pressure control, diabetes management, and anticoagulation if atrial fibrillation is found. **Carotid endarterectomy** benefits symptomatic patients with 70 to 99% stenosis (and selected 50 to 69%). The **ABCD2** score (age at least 60: 1; BP at least 140/90: 1; unilateral weakness 2 or speech disturbance 1; duration at least 60 minutes 2 or 10 to 59 minutes 1; diabetes 1) is a historical risk tool; current practice evaluates every TIA urgently with vascular imaging, ECG and monitoring for atrial fibrillation.

### Seizures and headache

A first unprovoked seizure: EEG and MRI; treatment is started after a second seizure or when risk is high. Generalized tonic-clonic and focal seizures respond to levetiracetam, lamotrigine or valproate (avoid valproate in pregnancy because of neural tube defects); absence seizures use ethosuximide. **Headache:** migraine (triptans for acute, propranolol or topiramate for prevention); cluster (100% oxygen, sumatriptan); thunderclap onset means subarachnoid hemorrhage until proven otherwise; **giant cell arteritis** (age over 50, jaw claudication, ESR elevated, vision threat) is treated with high-dose glucocorticoids **before** temporal artery biopsy.

### Neuromuscular and spinal emergencies

- **Guillain-Barre syndrome:** ascending symmetric weakness and areflexia after infection; CSF shows albuminocytologic dissociation. Treat with IV immunoglobulin or plasma exchange; follow forced vital capacity and intubate for declining ventilation (FVC below about 20 mL/kg). Corticosteroids do not help.
- **Myasthenia gravis:** fatigable ptosis and diplopia; acetylcholine receptor antibodies; chest CT for thymoma; pyridostigmine; crisis needs IVIG or plasma exchange and ventilatory support.
- **Multiple sclerosis relapse:** high-dose IV methylprednisolone; MRI shows periventricular lesions.
- **Spinal cord compression** (cancer, back pain, weakness, sensory level): IV dexamethasone and urgent MRI of the whole spine, then radiation or surgery. **Cauda equina syndrome:** saddle anesthesia, urinary retention; emergent MRI and surgery.
- **Normal pressure hydrocephalus:** gait disturbance, urinary incontinence, dementia; ventriculomegaly; large-volume lumbar puncture trial, then shunt.

### Delirium, withdrawal and toxic syndromes

Delirium is acute, fluctuating inattention with an underlying medical cause; find and treat the cause and use nonpharmacologic measures. **Alcohol withdrawal:** tremor in 6 to 24 hours, seizures at 12 to 48, delirium tremens at 48 to 96 hours; treat with benzodiazepines (symptom-triggered using CIWA); give **thiamine before glucose** to prevent Wernicke encephalopathy (confusion, ophthalmoplegia, ataxia). **Serotonin syndrome:** clonus, hyperreflexia, hyperthermia after serotonergic drugs; stop the drug, give benzodiazepines, and cyproheptadine if needed. **Neuroleptic malignant syndrome:** lead-pipe rigidity and elevated creatine kinase after antipsychotics; stop the agent, cool, and use dantrolene or bromocriptine. **Lithium toxicity:** coarse tremor, diarrhea, confusion, often after dehydration, NSAIDs, thiazides or ACE inhibitors; stop lithium, check the level, give isotonic saline, and consider dialysis for severe cases.

### Psychiatry

Major depression: SSRI plus psychotherapy; electroconvulsive therapy for psychotic, catatonic or refractory depression or acute suicide risk. Bipolar disorder: lithium or valproate (mania), avoiding antidepressant monotherapy. Schizophrenia: second-generation antipsychotic with metabolic monitoring; clozapine for refractory illness with ANC monitoring. Opioid use disorder: buprenorphine or methadone (medications for opioid use disorder). **Suicide risk assessment:** ask directly about ideation, plan, intent and access to means; assess protective factors and past attempts; hospitalize or provide continuous observation for imminent risk; do not rely on a no-harm contract. **Glasgow Coma Scale:** eye opening 1 to 4, verbal 1 to 5, motor 1 to 6; a total of 8 or less generally prompts securing the airway.

title: Glasgow Coma Scale
problem: A patient opens his eyes only to pain (2), speaks inappropriate words (3), and localizes to pain (5). What is the GCS total, and what does it imply?
step: Add the three components: $2+3+5$.
step: The total is $10$, a moderate impairment of consciousness.
step: A total of 8 or less means severe impairment and usually requires airway protection; 10 requires close neurologic monitoring and a search for the cause.

@example
title: Headache with jaw pain
problem: A 72-year-old woman has 3 weeks of new temporal headache, jaw pain when chewing and 2 days of blurred vision in the right eye. ESR is 95 mm/h. What is the best next step?
step: The clinical picture suggests giant cell arteritis, and visual loss makes it an emergency.
step: Start high-dose glucocorticoids immediately; do not wait for pathology.
step: Arrange temporal artery biopsy within days; steroids do not obscure the biopsy appreciably in the first weeks.

@mistakes
- Treating Guillain-Barre syndrome with corticosteroids, or delaying intubation until respiratory failure rather than following vital capacity.
- Giving glucose before thiamine to an alcohol-dependent patient with confusion.
- Awaiting biopsy before starting steroids in suspected giant cell arteritis.
- Using antidepressant monotherapy in bipolar depression, or discharging a patient with active plan and intent because they "contracted for safety".

solution: NSAIDs, thiazides and ACE inhibitors reduce lithium clearance, producing toxicity. Stop lithium, measure the level and renal function, and give isotonic saline; hemodialysis is for severe cases. Naproxen is also an NSAID.

@practice self
prompt: A 17-year-old girl with depression tells her physician after a breakup that she has been thinking of overdosing on her mother's pills, has a plan, and the pills are at home. Describe how you would assess the risk and decide on the level of care.
hint: What must you ask directly, and what elements of the risk assessment determine disposition?
solution: Ask directly and calmly about suicidal ideation, plan, intent, preparatory behavior, past attempts, access to means, substance use, psychosis, impulsivity, hopelessness, and protective factors (family, reasons for living, future orientation). A specific plan with available means and intent indicates high risk. Do not leave her alone: arrange continuous observation and an emergency psychiatric evaluation, with admission given her plan, intent and available means (no-harm contracts are not reliable protection). Involve her parents or guardians because of her age, restrict access to means, treat the underlying depression, and arrange close follow-up if discharge is later deemed safe.
rubric: Asks directly about ideation, plan, intent and means.
rubric: Identifies a plan with available means as high risk and recommends emergency evaluation or admission.
rubric: Does not rely on a no-harm contract and arranges observation.
rubric: Addresses means restriction, family involvement and follow-up.
