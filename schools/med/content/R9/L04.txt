@article
### Sepsis

Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction from infection. Draw blood cultures and lactate, give broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour when shock or high likelihood is present, and give an initial crystalloid bolus (30 mL/kg is the usual suggestion for hypotension or lactate of 4 mmol/L or more; assess response). If mean arterial pressure stays below 65 mm Hg, start **norepinephrine**; add vasopressin or hydrocortisone for refractory shock. Control the source (drain an abscess, relieve an obstructed infected ureter, remove a line).

### Meningitis and encephalitis

Fever, headache, neck stiffness and altered mental status: draw blood cultures and **give empiric antibiotics without delaying for imaging**. In adults use ceftriaxone plus vancomycin, add ampicillin for age over 50 or immunocompromise (Listeria), and give dexamethasone with or before the first antibiotic dose. Obtain CT before lumbar puncture when there is papilledema, a focal deficit, seizure, severe altered mental status or immunocompromise. Add acyclovir when temporal-lobe signs or HSV encephalitis are suspected. Household contacts of meningococcal disease receive prophylaxis; the patient needs droplet precautions.

### Pneumonia, C. difficile and endocarditis

Community-acquired pneumonia in a healthy outpatient: amoxicillin or doxycycline (a macrolide only where local pneumococcal resistance is low). Admitted ward patients: a beta-lactam plus a macrolide, or a respiratory fluoroquinolone. **C. difficile** infection: fidaxomicin is preferred in current IDSA guidance, with oral vancomycin as an alternative; metronidazole is no longer first-line. **Infective endocarditis:** at least three sets of blood cultures before antibiotics, then echocardiography; vancomycin empirically in injection drug users (Staphylococcus aureus). Surgery is indicated for heart failure from valve dysfunction, abscess, persistent bacteremia or large vegetations with emboli.

### HIV and tuberculosis

Start antiretroviral therapy in everyone with HIV, regardless of CD4 count. Prophylaxis: trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for CD4 below 200/mm3 (Pneumocystis), which also covers toxoplasmosis when CD4 is below 100 and IgG is positive. Pre-exposure prophylaxis with a tenofovir-based regimen protects high-risk persons. **Active TB** is treated with isoniazid, rifampin, pyrazinamide and ethambutol for 2 months, then isoniazid and rifampin for 4 months, with pyridoxine to prevent isoniazid neuropathy. Latent TB uses short rifamycin-containing regimens in current guidance.

### Neutropenic fever

$ANC=\text{WBC}\times\frac{\%\text{segmented}+\%\text{bands}}{100}$. Fever with ANC below 500/mm3 (or expected to fall) needs an anti-pseudomonal beta-lactam such as cefepime within 60 minutes after cultures; add vancomycin only for specific indications.

### Hematology

- **Anemia:** microcytic: iron deficiency (low ferritin, high TIBC; in men and postmenopausal women look for GI blood loss with endoscopy), thalassemia, anemia of chronic disease. Macrocytic: B12 or folate deficiency. Hemolysis: high LDH and indirect bilirubin, low haptoglobin. The **corrected reticulocyte count** is $\text{retic}\%\times\text{Hct}/45$; above 2% suggests a marrow responding appropriately.
- **TTP:** fever, neurologic findings, renal injury, schistocytes, severe thrombocytopenia with normal PT/aPTT and low ADAMTS13. Treat with urgent plasma exchange and corticosteroids; avoid platelet transfusion unless life-threatening bleeding.
- **HIT:** platelet fall of more than 50% (or at least 30%) 5 to 10 days after heparin with thrombosis. Stop all heparin and start a non-heparin anticoagulant (argatroban or bivalirudin).
- **ITP:** isolated thrombocytopenia; treat with steroids or IVIG when bleeding or counts are very low.
- **Anticoagulation:** therapeutic enoxaparin is 1 mg/kg subcutaneously every 12 hours (typical adult dose for study; adjust for renal function). Life-threatening warfarin bleeding is reversed with four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate plus IV vitamin K. Restrictive transfusion (hemoglobin below 7 g/dL, below 8 with cardiovascular disease) is standard.

@example
title: Absolute neutrophil count
problem: A patient receiving chemotherapy has a WBC of 1,800/mm3 with 26% segmented neutrophils and 4% bands. Fever is 38.7 C. What is the ANC, and what does it mean?
step: Neutrophils (segs plus bands) are $26+4=30\%$.
step: $ANC=1800\times0.30=540$/mm3.
step: This is above 500 but may fall, and fever in a patient with an ANC below 1,000 and an expected decline is treated like neutropenic fever. Obtain cultures and start cefepime promptly.

@example
title: Corrected reticulocyte count
problem: A patient with anemia has a hematocrit of 27% and a reticulocyte percentage of 6%. Calculate the corrected reticulocyte count and interpret it.
step: Correct for the anemia: $6\%\times\frac{27}{45}$.
step: $27/45=0.6$, so the corrected count is $6\times0.6=3.6\%$.
step: A corrected count above 2% means the marrow is responding, favoring hemolysis or blood loss over underproduction.

@mistakes
- Delaying antibiotics in suspected bacterial meningitis for CT or lumbar puncture, or omitting dexamethasone.
- Transfusing platelets in TTP or continuing heparin in HIT; both can worsen thrombosis.
- Starting prophylaxis against the wrong organism (for instance, azithromycin alone for a CD4 of 85/mm3) or delaying antiretroviral therapy for unnecessary reasons.
- Using metronidazole as first-line therapy for C. difficile, or treating iron deficiency in an older adult without a source search.

@practice self
prompt: A 66-year-old man from a nursing home has fever, confusion, a respiratory rate of 26/min, BP 82/48 mm Hg, and a urinary catheter with purulent urine. Lactate is 5.1 mmol/L. Write your management plan for the first hour, including fluids, antibiotics, vasopressors and source control.
hint: What is done before antibiotics, and what MAP are you aiming for?
hint: Think of the sepsis bundle: cultures, lactate, antibiotics, fluids, pressors, source.
solution: This is septic shock, probably urosepsis. Within the first hour: obtain two sets of blood cultures and urine culture, measure lactate, then give broad-spectrum antibiotics covering resistant gram-negative organisms (for example an antipseudomonal beta-lactam, with local resistance considered) without delay. Give an initial crystalloid bolus (30 mL/kg suggested) and reassess perfusion; if mean arterial pressure remains below 65 mm Hg, start norepinephrine through a central or appropriate large vein. Replace or remove the infected catheter and image for obstruction or abscess if not improving. Repeat lactate and monitor urine output.
