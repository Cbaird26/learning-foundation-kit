@article
### The febrile infant

A fever of 38.0 C or higher in an infant 28 days old or younger is an emergency: full sepsis evaluation (blood, urine by catheter, and cerebrospinal fluid cultures), admission and empiric antibiotics (ampicillin plus gentamicin or a third-generation cephalosporin; ceftriaxone is avoided in the first weeks because of bilirubin displacement and calcium interactions), with acyclovir added when HSV is suspected. For infants 8 to 60 days old, the 2021 AAP guideline allows risk stratification using urinalysis, procalcitonin, absolute neutrophil count and CRP rather than automatic lumbar puncture in every well-appearing infant; the neonate under 28 days is still fully evaluated.

### Neonatal jaundice and heart disease

Jaundice in the first 24 hours is pathologic until proven otherwise (hemolysis from ABO or Rh incompatibility, G6PD deficiency); check total and direct bilirubin and a Coombs test, and treat with phototherapy according to age-in-hours nomograms; exchange transfusion for severe levels. Congenital heart disease: cyanosis that does not improve with oxygen suggests a cyanotic lesion; ductal-dependent lesions are kept open with **prostaglandin E1**; a closing PDA is closed with indomethacin or ibuprofen in preterm infants; transposition of the great arteries needs prostaglandin and atrial septostomy; "tet spells" in tetralogy of Fallot improve with knee-to-chest position, oxygen, fluids and morphine.

### Vomiting and abdominal emergencies

| Condition | Age and clues | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Pyloric stenosis | 2 to 8 weeks, projectile nonbilious vomiting, hungry afterward, palpable olive; hypochloremic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis | Correct fluids and electrolytes, then pyloromyotomy; ultrasonography if the examination is equivocal |
| Intussusception | 3 months to 3 years, colicky pain, currant-jelly stool, sausage mass; ultrasonography target sign | Air or contrast enema (surgery if peritonitis or perforation) |
| Malrotation with volvulus | Bilious emesis in an infant | Upper GI series if stable; emergent surgery (Ladd procedure) if unstable |
| Hirschsprung disease | No meconium in 48 hours, distension | Rectal suction biopsy |

### Respiratory emergencies

**Croup** (parainfluenza; barking cough, inspiratory stridor, steeple sign): a single dose of dexamethasone (0.6 mg/kg, typical for study), with nebulized racemic epinephrine for stridor at rest. **Bronchiolitis** (RSV, infants under 2): supportive care with oxygen, suction and hydration; no routine albuterol, steroids or antibiotics. **Foreign body aspiration:** sudden choking in a toddler, unilateral hyperinflation; rigid bronchoscopy. Epiglottitis is now rare after Hib vaccination; secure the airway first.

### Kawasaki disease and abuse

**Kawasaki disease:** fever for at least 5 days plus four of five features (bilateral nonexudative conjunctivitis, oral changes, cervical lymphadenopathy, rash, extremity changes). Treat with IV immunoglobulin and aspirin, and perform echocardiography for coronary aneurysms. Suspect **child abuse** when the injury does not fit the history or developmental stage (long-bone fractures in a non-walking infant, retinal hemorrhages, bruises on the torso, ears or neck in a young child). Obtain a skeletal survey for children under 2 years, and make a report to child protective services on reasonable suspicion; this report does not require proof or parental consent.

### Growth, development and drug dosing

Milestones (typical ages in classic teaching): social smile by 2 months; sits without support by about 6 months; walks by about 12 months; two-word phrases by 24 months; copies a circle by 3 years. The 2022 CDC checklists instead list the ages most children reach (for example sitting without support by 9 months and walking by 18 months), so a child is evaluated if a milestone is missed by those later ages. Maintenance fluids follow the **4-2-1 rule**: 4 mL/kg/h for the first 10 kg, 2 mL/kg/h for the next 10 kg, and 1 mL/kg/h for each kilogram above 20. Typical oral doses for study (check current references): acetaminophen 15 mg/kg per dose, ibuprofen 10 mg/kg per dose, amoxicillin for acute otitis media 80 to 90 mg/kg/day divided twice daily. Mild-to-moderate dehydration is treated with oral rehydration solution; shock receives 20 mL/kg isotonic boluses.

@example
title: The 4-2-1 rule
problem: A 25-kg child is NPO and needs maintenance IV fluids. What infusion rate in mL/h is required?
step: First 10 kg at 4 mL/kg/h: $10\times4=40$ mL/h.
step: Next 10 kg at 2 mL/kg/h: $10\times2=20$ mL/h.
step: Remaining 5 kg at 1 mL/kg/h: $5\times1=5$ mL/h.
step: Total $=40+20+5=65$ mL/h.

@example
title: Fever for five days
problem: A 3-year-old has had fever for 6 days with bilateral conjunctival injection without exudate, cracked red lips, a polymorphous rash and swollen hands. What is the diagnosis and the next step?
step: Fever of 5 days or more plus at least four of five principal criteria meets the definition of Kawasaki disease; here the conjunctivitis, oral changes, rash and extremity changes qualify.
step: The risk is coronary artery aneurysm, so treatment should begin within about 10 days of fever onset.
step: Give IV immunoglobulin (2 g/kg) with aspirin and obtain echocardiography.

@mistakes
- Using ceftriaxone for a jaundiced newborn in the first weeks of life, or skipping the lumbar puncture in a febrile neonate under 28 days.
- Operating on pyloric stenosis before correcting hypochloremic alkalosis and dehydration.
- Giving steroids and albuterol routinely for bronchiolitis, or antibiotics for croup.
- Waiting for parental consent or certainty before reporting suspected child abuse.
