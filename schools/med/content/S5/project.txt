### Week by week

**Week 1: principles.** Peptide hormones use surface receptors and have short half-lives; steroid and thyroid hormones use nuclear receptors and are carrier-bound. Free hormone is active. Feedback keeps a target hormone and its pituitary hormone in opposite directions: low-low or low-normal means central disease, low-high means primary disease. Prolactin is under inhibitory dopamine control.

**Week 2: pituitary.** Prolactinoma is treated with a dopamine agonist. Acromegaly: IGF-1 screen, glucose suppression test, surgery. Hypopituitarism: replace glucocorticoid before thyroid hormone. Central diabetes insipidus: urine osmolality rises more than 50% after desmopressin. SIADH: euvolemic hyponatremia with concentrated urine; fluid restriction.

**Week 3 and 4: thyroid.** NIS, TPO, coupling; T4 is a prohormone, T3 is active. TSH with free T4 is the screen. Hashimoto: anti-TPO; Graves: stimulating antibodies, high diffuse uptake; thyroiditis: low uptake. Myxedema coma: IV levothyroxine and hydrocortisone. Storm: beta-blocker, PTU, iodine later, steroids.

**Week 5 and 6: adrenal.** Zones GFR; rate-limiting step is StAR and P450scc. Cushing: confirm with salivary cortisol, urinary free cortisol or 1 mg dexamethasone, then ACTH. Adrenal insufficiency: cosyntropin test, and never delay hydrocortisone in crisis. CAH: 21-hydroxylase deficiency gives high 17-hydroxyprogesterone. Primary aldosteronism: high aldosterone-to-renin ratio.

**Week 7 and 8: calcium and pancreas.** Correct calcium for albumin. High PTH with high calcium: primary hyperparathyroidism or FHH (urine calcium). Low PTH: malignancy, vitamin D excess. Insulin release: GLUT2, glucokinase, K-ATP closure, calcium influx. High insulin with low C-peptide means exogenous insulin.

**Week 9 and 10: diabetes and emergencies.** ADA diagnostic thresholds; metformin first, SGLT2 inhibitor or GLP-1 agonist for cardiovascular, heart failure or kidney disease. DKA: fluids, check potassium, insulin infusion, add dextrose, continue until the gap closes, overlap subcutaneous insulin. HHS: fluids first. Hypoglycemia: 15 g carbohydrate, glucagon.

**Week 11 and 12: tumors and drugs.** Pheochromocytoma: metanephrines, alpha-blockade first. MEN1: menin; MEN2: RET. Carcinoid syndrome needs liver metastases. Compare diabetes drug classes by mechanism, hypoglycemia, weight and adverse effects.

### Common errors

- Reading TSH alone when free T4 is low.
- Using a dexamethasone test on a patient taking estrogen.
- Giving thyroid hormone before glucocorticoid in possible adrenal insufficiency.
- Starting insulin in DKA before checking potassium.
- Giving a beta-blocker before an alpha-blocker in pheochromocytoma.
