@article
### Insulins

| Type | Examples | Onset | Peak | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rapid-acting | lispro, aspart, glulisine | 5 to 15 min | 1 to 2 h | 3 to 5 h |
| Short-acting | regular | 30 to 60 min | 2 to 4 h | 5 to 8 h |
| Intermediate | NPH | 1 to 2 h | 4 to 10 h | 12 to 18 h |
| Long-acting | glargine, detemir, degludec | 1 to 2 h | minimal | 20 to 42 h |

These are approximate. Adverse effects are **hypoglycemia, weight gain** and injection-site lipohypertrophy. A typical starting basal dose in type 2 diabetes is 10 units a day or 0.1 to 0.2 units/kg/day (for study; follow current protocols).

### Non-insulin diabetes drugs

| Class (examples) | Mechanism | Hypoglycemia alone | Weight | Key adverse effects and notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Metformin** | decreases hepatic gluconeogenesis, activates AMPK | no | neutral or slight loss | GI upset, B12 deficiency, rare lactic acidosis (avoid at eGFR below 30) |
| **Sulfonylureas** (glipizide, glimepiride, glyburide) | close K-ATP channel, increase insulin secretion | **yes** | gain | hypoglycemia, especially in kidney disease; glyburide worst |
| **Meglitinides** (repaglinide) | close K-ATP channel, short-acting | yes | gain | hypoglycemia; taken with meals |
| **Thiazolidinediones** (pioglitazone) | PPAR-γ agonists, increase insulin sensitivity | no | gain | **fluid retention and heart failure**, fractures, edema |
| **DPP-4 inhibitors** (sitagliptin) | block incretin breakdown | no | neutral | pancreatitis (rare), joint pain, bullous pemphigoid |
| **GLP-1 receptor agonists** (semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide) | mimic GLP-1: glucose-dependent insulin, lower glucagon, slow gastric emptying, satiety | no | **loss** | nausea, vomiting, gallbladder disease, pancreatitis; **contraindicated with personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2**; cardiovascular benefit |
| **Tirzepatide** | dual GIP and GLP-1 agonist | no | marked loss | as for GLP-1 agonists |
| **SGLT2 inhibitors** (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin) | block proximal tubule glucose reabsorption: glucosuria | no | loss | genital mycotic infections, UTI, volume depletion, **euglycemic DKA**, Fournier gangrene; **heart failure and CKD benefit** |
| **α-Glucosidase inhibitors** (acarbose) | slow intestinal carbohydrate digestion | no | neutral | flatulence, diarrhea |
| **Pramlintide** | amylin analog, slows gastric emptying, lowers glucagon | with insulin | loss | nausea; used with mealtime insulin |

Choosing among them depends on cardiovascular, kidney and heart disease, cost and the patient's priorities. A drug's **absolute** effect on events is expressed as the number needed to treat: $\text{NNT} = 1/\text{ARR}$, where ARR is the absolute risk reduction.

### Glucocorticoids

Bind intracellular receptors, alter transcription, and are anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive. Adverse effects with prolonged use: **adrenal suppression** (do not stop abruptly; stress-dose in illness), Cushingoid features, hyperglycemia, hypertension, osteoporosis, cataract and glaucoma, **avascular necrosis**, myopathy, mood disturbance and psychosis, peptic ulcer risk (especially with NSAIDs), infection and poor wound healing. Protect bone with calcium, vitamin D and, if long-term use is expected, a bisphosphonate. Fludrocortisone is a mineralocorticoid used in primary adrenal insufficiency.

### Thyroid drugs

**Levothyroxine** (take on an empty stomach; absorption reduced by calcium, iron and proton pump inhibitors), **liothyronine** (short acting, rarely used alone), **methimazole and propylthiouracil** (block thyroid peroxidase; PTU also blocks T4 to T3 conversion and is preferred in thyroid storm and the first trimester), **propranolol** (β-blocker; also reduces T4 to T3 conversion), **iodine** (Wolff–Chaikoff effect; used before thyroidectomy and in storm after a thionamide) and radioactive iodine.

### Other agents

- **Desmopressin** (V2 agonist): central diabetes insipidus; hyponatremia is the main risk.
- **Octreotide and lanreotide** (somatostatin analogs): acromegaly, carcinoid, VIPoma; cause gallstones, bradycardia and glucose changes. **Pegvisomant** blocks the GH receptor.
- **Cabergoline and bromocriptine** (D2 agonists): prolactinoma; nausea, orthostasis, and cardiac valve fibrosis at high doses.
- **Bisphosphonates** (alendronate; inhibit osteoclasts): take upright with water; esophagitis, osteonecrosis of the jaw, atypical femur fracture. **Denosumab** (RANKL antibody): rebound fractures if stopped. **Teriparatide** (PTH analog): anabolic. **Cinacalcet** (CaSR agonist): hypocalcemia.
- **Tolvaptan** (V2 antagonist): SIADH; overly rapid sodium correction is the danger.

@example
title: Number needed to treat
problem: In a cardiovascular outcome trial in type 2 diabetes, the primary composite event occurred in 12% of the placebo group and 8% of the group given a drug over the study period. What are the absolute risk reduction and the NNT?
step: ARR $= 0.12 - 0.08 = 0.04$ (4 percentage points).
step: $\text{NNT} = 1/0.04 = 25$.
step: Interpretation: 25 patients must be treated for the study duration to prevent one event. The relative risk reduction (33%) sounds larger than the absolute effect, so report both.

@mistakes
- Choosing a sulfonylurea or insulin by weight alone and ignoring that they cause hypoglycemia and weight gain.
- Giving a GLP-1 receptor agonist to a patient with a family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2.
- Forgetting that SGLT2 inhibitors can cause euglycemic DKA, so ketones should be checked in unwell patients even when glucose is not very high.
- Stopping long-term glucocorticoids abruptly.
