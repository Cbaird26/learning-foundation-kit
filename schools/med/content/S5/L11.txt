@article
### Pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma

A catecholamine-secreting tumor of chromaffin cells in the adrenal medulla (**pheochromocytoma**) or of extra-adrenal sympathetic ganglia (**paraganglioma**). About a third to 40% are hereditary: **MEN2, von Hippel–Lindau (VHL), neurofibromatosis type 1 and SDHx mutations**. Classic features: episodic **headache, sweating and palpitations** with hypertension (sustained or paroxysmal), anxiety, pallor, and orthostasis. Triggers include exertion, anesthesia, and drugs such as metoclopramide and glucagon.

**Diagnosis**: **plasma free metanephrines** (best sensitivity; ideally drawn supine) or **24-hour urinary fractionated metanephrines**. A result more than about 3 times the upper limit is very likely a tumor. Imaging (CT or MRI of adrenals and abdomen; MIBG or somatostatin-receptor imaging for extra-adrenal or metastatic disease) follows biochemistry, not the reverse. **Preoperative preparation**: begin **alpha-blockade first** (phenoxybenzamine, or doxazosin) for 7 to 14 days with liberal salt and fluid intake, then add a **β-blocker** for tachycardia; **never give a β-blocker first**, because unopposed α-stimulation can cause a hypertensive crisis. Then perform surgical resection. Screen for genetic syndromes and follow lifelong.

### Adrenal incidentaloma

An adrenal mass found on imaging done for another reason. Ask two questions: is it malignant, and is it functional? Features of benign lesions: size under 4 cm, homogeneous, and **unenhanced CT attenuation of 10 Hounsfield units or less** (lipid-rich adenoma). Biochemical assessment: **1 mg dexamethasone suppression test** (for autonomous cortisol secretion), **plasma metanephrines** (if attenuation is above 10 HU, to avoid unexpected pheochromocytoma at biopsy or surgery), and aldosterone and renin if the patient is hypertensive or hypokalemic. Surgical resection is considered for functional tumors or tumors suspected of malignancy (large, heterogeneous or growing). A biopsy is generally avoided unless metastasis is suspected and pheochromocytoma has been excluded.

### Neuroendocrine tumors

**Carcinoid tumors** (usually small bowel, appendix or bronchus) secrete serotonin. **Carcinoid syndrome** (flushing, secretory diarrhea, wheezing, right-sided valvular fibrosis with tricuspid regurgitation and pulmonic stenosis) develops only when serotonin escapes hepatic metabolism, that is with **liver metastases** (or bronchial or ovarian primaries). Diagnose with **urinary 5-HIAA** (or plasma chromogranin A); treat with a **somatostatin analog** (octreotide, lanreotide) and surgery when possible. Niacin deficiency (pellagra) can result from tryptophan diversion.

**Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors:**

| Tumor | Hormone | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Insulinoma | insulin | fasting hypoglycemia, Whipple triad; high insulin, C-peptide and proinsulin |
| Gastrinoma (Zollinger–Ellison) | gastrin | refractory, multiple or distal peptic ulcers, diarrhea; high fasting gastrin; paradoxical rise in gastrin after **secretin**; treat with PPI and resection |
| Glucagonoma | glucagon | necrolytic migratory erythema, diabetes, weight loss, venous thrombosis |
| VIPoma | VIP | watery diarrhea, hypokalemia, achlorhydria (WDHA) |
| Somatostatinoma | somatostatin | diabetes, gallstones, steatorrhea |

### MEN syndromes (autosomal dominant)

| Syndrome | Gene | Components |
|---|---|---|
| **MEN1** (Wermer) | **MEN1** (menin, tumor suppressor, 11q13) | **Parathyroid** hyperplasia (usually the first, causing hypercalcemia), **pituitary** adenomas (prolactinoma), **pancreatic** endocrine tumors (gastrinoma, insulinoma) |
| **MEN2A** (Sipple) | **RET** (proto-oncogene, gain-of-function) | **Medullary thyroid carcinoma**, **pheochromocytoma**, parathyroid hyperplasia |
| **MEN2B** | **RET** (M918T) | Medullary thyroid carcinoma, pheochromocytoma, **mucosal neuromas**, marfanoid habitus; no parathyroid disease |

Medullary thyroid carcinoma arises from calcitonin-secreting C cells, has amyloid stroma and a **high serum calcitonin**; carriers of RET mutations are offered **prophylactic thyroidectomy** (very early in MEN2B). In a patient with MEN2, always exclude pheochromocytoma **before** thyroid or other surgery.

### Predictive value of a screening test

For a test with sensitivity $Se$ and specificity $Sp$ in a population with prevalence $p$:

$$\text{PPV} = \frac{Se \cdot p}{Se \cdot p + (1 - Sp)(1 - p)}.$$

Even an excellent test has a modest PPV when the disease is rare.

@example
title: Positive predictive value of metanephrine screening
problem: In a clinic, 200 patients with resistant hypertension are screened for pheochromocytoma; 20 truly have it. The test has sensitivity 98% and specificity 92%. What is the PPV?
step: True positives $= 0.98 \times 20 = 19.6$.
step: There are 180 patients without the tumor; false positives $= 0.08 \times 180 = 14.4$.
step: PPV $= 19.6/(19.6 + 14.4) = 19.6/34 \approx 0.58$.
step: About 58% of positive results are true tumors; confirmatory testing and imaging are needed. A positive result in a lower-prevalence group would have a lower PPV.

@mistakes
- Giving a β-blocker before an α-blocker in pheochromocytoma.
- Doing a biopsy of an adrenal mass without excluding pheochromocytoma first.
- Expecting carcinoid syndrome from a small bowel carcinoid without liver metastases.
- Confusing MEN1 (menin, parathyroid-pituitary-pancreas) with MEN2 (RET, medullary thyroid carcinoma-pheochromocytoma).

prompt: A screening test for pheochromocytoma has sensitivity 98% and specificity 92%. In 200 patients, 20 truly have the tumor. What is the positive predictive value, as a decimal (round to three decimal places)?
answer: 0.576
check: (0.98 * 20) / (0.98 * 20 + 0.08 * 180)
tol: 0.005
hint: Count true positives and false positives separately.
hint: There are 19.6 true positives and 14.4 false positives.
solution: PPV $= 19.6/(19.6 + 14.4) = 0.576$.

@practice num
prompt: A test has a sensitivity of 90% and a specificity of 95%. A patient tests negative. In a group of 1000 people with 100 truly diseased, what is the negative predictive value as a percentage (to one decimal)?
answer: 98.8
check: 900 * 0.95 / (900 * 0.95 + 100 * 0.10) * 100
tol: 0.1
unit: %
hint: True negatives are among the 900 without disease.
hint: There are 855 true negatives and 10 false negatives.
solution: True negatives $= 0.95 \times 900 = 855$; false negatives $= 0.10 \times 100 = 10$. NPV $= 855/865 = 0.988$, or 98.8%.
