@article
Sorting means putting things that are alike in the same group. You do it when you put toys in one box and books on one shelf.

### Sort by one thing

You can sort by **color**: all the red things together. You can sort by **size**: all the big things together and all the small things together. You can sort by **shape**: all the circles together.

Pick one way to sort and stick with it. If you sort by color, a big red block and a small red block go in the same group.

### Count each group

After you sort, count how many are in each group. Then you can say which group has more.

### Patterns

A **pattern** is something that repeats again and again. Red, blue, red, blue, red, blue is a pattern. The part that repeats is called the **core**. Here the core is red, blue.

To find what comes next, look for the part that repeats. Clap, stomp, clap, stomp, clap, what comes next? Stomp.

### Make your own

Use two colors of blocks to make a pattern. Say it out loud as you go. Ask your grown-up to guess what comes next.

@example
title: What comes next?
problem: A pattern goes: circle, square, circle, square, circle. What comes next?
step: Find the part that repeats: circle, square.
step: The last shape is a circle, so the next shape is the second part of the core, the square.
step: The next shape is a square.

@mistakes
- Sorting by color and size at the same time, so the groups get mixed up.
- Looking at only the last item and not the whole repeating part.
- Forgetting to count the items in each group.

@practice num
prompt: You sort 9 socks by color. 4 are white and the rest are gray. How many are gray?
answer: 5
check: 9 - 4
hint: Put 4 socks in the white group. How many are left over?
solution: 9 socks and 4 are white, so 5 are left. There are 5 gray socks.
