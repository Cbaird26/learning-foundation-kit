@article
You can add and subtract with numbers up to 20 too. Ten is a very helpful number.

### Make ten first

To add $8 + 5$, use the fact that $8 + 2 = 10$. Break the 5 into 2 and 3. Add 2 to the 8 to get 10. Then add the 3 that is left: $10 + 3 = 13$.

$$8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13$$

### Count on

For $9 + 4$, start at 9 and count on 4: 10, 11, 12, 13. The sum is 13.

### Doubles

Doubles are easy to remember: $6 + 6 = 12$, $7 + 7 = 14$, $8 + 8 = 16$, $9 + 9 = 18$.

### Subtract with ten

For $14 - 6$, first take away 4 to get to 10. Then take away 2 more: $10 - 2 = 8$. So $14 - 6 = 8$.

### Think of a friend fact

If you know $8 + 5 = 13$, then $13 - 5 = 8$. Adding facts can help you subtract.

### Practice with objects

Use 20 small objects. Make a group of 10 and a group for the rest. This helps you see tens and ones.

@example
title: Adding past ten
problem: What is $7 + 6$?
step: Break 6 into 3 and 3. Add 3 to 7 to make 10.
step: Add the other 3: $10 + 3 = 13$.
step: So $7 + 6 = 13$.

@example
title: Subtracting from 15
problem: What is $15 - 7$?
step: Take away 5 first to get to 10: $15 - 5 = 10$.
step: You still need to take away 2 more: $10 - 2 = 8$.
step: So $15 - 7 = 8$.

@mistakes
- Counting on one too many or one too few.
- Adding when the sign says to subtract.
- Forgetting that doubles like $8 + 8$ make 16, not 15 or 18.
