@article
Most reactions do not run to completion. They reach **dynamic equilibrium**, where the forward and reverse rates are equal and the concentrations stop changing, although both reactions continue. In the body, equilibria such as $\text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$ and oxygen binding to hemoglobin are never "finished": they respond continually to changing conditions.

### The equilibrium constant

For $a\text{A} + b\text{B} \rightleftharpoons c\text{C} + d\text{D}$ at equilibrium,

$$K_c = \frac{[\text{C}]^c[\text{D}]^d}{[\text{A}]^a[\text{B}]^b}.$$

Pure solids and pure liquids (including water as solvent) are omitted because their concentrations are constant. For gases you can use partial pressures, $K_p$, and the two constants are related by

$$K_p = K_c\,(RT)^{\Delta n},$$

where $\Delta n$ is moles of gaseous products minus moles of gaseous reactants, $R = 0.08206\ \text{L·atm/(mol·K)}$ and $T$ is in kelvin. Large $K$ ($\gg 1$) means products dominate; small $K$ ($\ll 1$) means reactants dominate. Reversing a reaction inverts $K$; multiplying all coefficients by $n$ raises $K$ to the power $n$; adding reactions multiplies their $K$ values. $K$ depends **only on temperature**.

### The reaction quotient

$Q$ has the same form as $K$ but uses the concentrations present *now*.

- $Q < K$: too many reactants, so the reaction proceeds to the **right**.
- $Q = K$: at equilibrium.
- $Q > K$: too many products, so the reaction proceeds to the **left**.

### Le Chatelier's principle

If a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts to partly counteract the disturbance.

- **Add reactant or remove product:** shifts right. Add product or remove reactant: shifts left. (Compare $Q$ with $K$ to see why.)
- **Compress a gas mixture** (raise pressure by decreasing volume): shifts toward the side with fewer moles of gas. If $\Delta n = 0$ there is no shift. Adding an inert gas at constant volume changes nothing, because partial pressures do not change.
- **Change temperature:** treat heat as a reactant or product. Raising $T$ favors the endothermic direction, and $K$ itself changes (an exothermic reaction's $K$ decreases when $T$ rises).
- **Catalyst:** no shift and no change in $K$; equilibrium is reached faster.

### ICE tables

To find equilibrium concentrations, set up **I**nitial, **C**hange and **E**quilibrium rows, with the change written in terms of an unknown $x$ scaled by the coefficients. Substitute the equilibrium row into $K$ and solve. If $K$ is small and the initial concentration is large, you may neglect $x$ relative to it (check that it is under about $5\%$); otherwise solve the quadratic.

### Clinical connection

In the lungs, high $P_{\text{O}_2}$ drives $\text{Hb} + \text{O}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{HbO}_2$ to the right, loading oxygen. In tissues, low $P_{\text{O}_2}$ and extra $\text{H}^+$ and $\text{CO}_2$ shift it left, unloading oxygen. This is Le Chatelier's principle at work in physiology.

@example
title: Compute K from equilibrium data
problem: For $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$ at equilibrium, $[\text{N}_2] = 0.10\ \text{M}$, $[\text{H}_2] = 0.20\ \text{M}$ and $[\text{NH}_3] = 0.040\ \text{M}$. Find $K_c$.
step: Write the expression: $K_c = [\text{NH}_3]^2/([\text{N}_2][\text{H}_2]^3)$.
step: Substitute: $(0.040)^2 = 1.6\times10^{-3}$ in the numerator, and $(0.10)(0.20)^3 = (0.10)(8.0\times10^{-3}) = 8.0\times10^{-4}$ in the denominator.
step: $K_c = 1.6\times10^{-3}/8.0\times10^{-4} = 2.0$.

@example
title: ICE table and a prediction
problem: For $\text{H}_2 + \text{I}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{HI}$, $K_c = 36$ at a certain temperature. Starting with $1.00\ \text{M}$ each of $\text{H}_2$ and $\text{I}_2$ and no HI, find $[\text{HI}]$ at equilibrium.
step: Let $x$ be the amount of $\text{H}_2$ that reacts. At equilibrium $[\text{H}_2] = [\text{I}_2] = 1.00 - x$ and $[\text{HI}] = 2x$.
step: $K_c = (2x)^2/(1.00 - x)^2 = 36$. Take the square root: $2x/(1.00 - x) = 6$.
step: $2x = 6 - 6x$, so $x = 0.75$.
step: $[\text{HI}] = 2x = 1.50\ \text{M}$ and $[\text{H}_2] = [\text{I}_2] = 0.25\ \text{M}$. Check: $(1.50)^2/(0.25)^2 = 36$.

@mistakes
- Including pure solids or liquids in $K$, or forgetting to raise concentrations to their coefficients.
- Using $Q$ when equilibrium concentrations are required, or the reverse.
- Claiming a catalyst or an inert gas at constant volume shifts the equilibrium.
- Forgetting that changing the temperature changes the value of $K$ itself.

@practice num
prompt: For $2\text{NO}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{N}_2\text{O}_4$ at equilibrium, $[\text{NO}_2] = 0.050\ \text{M}$ and $[\text{N}_2\text{O}_4] = 0.020\ \text{M}$. Find $K_c$.
answer: 8.0
check: 0.020 / 0.050**2
tol: 1%
hint: Products go in the numerator; each concentration is raised to its coefficient.
solution: $K_c = [\text{N}_2\text{O}_4]/[\text{NO}_2]^2 = 0.020/(0.050)^2 = 0.020/0.0025 = 8.0$.

@practice num
prompt: For $\text{N}_2\text{O}_4(g) \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NO}_2(g)$, $K_c = 0.0059$ at $298\ \text{K}$. Find $K_p$ ($R = 0.08206\ \text{L·atm/(mol·K)}$).
answer: 0.144
check: 0.0059 * (0.08206 * 298) ** (2 - 1)
tol: 1%
hint: Use $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}$ and count gas moles on each side.
solution: $\Delta n = 2 - 1 = 1$, so $K_p = 0.0059 \times (0.08206)(298) = 0.0059 \times 24.45 = 0.144$.

@practice num
prompt: For $\text{H}_2 + \text{I}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{HI}$ with $K_c = 36$, a mixture has $[\text{H}_2] = 0.50\ \text{M}$, $[\text{I}_2] = 0.50\ \text{M}$ and $[\text{HI}] = 2.0\ \text{M}$. Calculate $Q$. (Then decide which way the reaction will proceed.)
answer: 16
check: 2.0**2 / (0.50 * 0.50)
tol: 1%
hint: $Q$ has the same form as $K_c$, using the current concentrations.
solution: $Q = (2.0)^2/((0.50)(0.50)) = 4.0/0.25 = 16$. Since $Q < K_c = 36$, the reaction proceeds to the right (toward more HI).

prompt: The synthesis $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$ is exothermic ($\Delta H < 0$). What happens when the temperature of an equilibrium mixture is raised?
choice*: The equilibrium shifts left and $K$ decreases.
choice: The equilibrium shifts left and $K$ is unchanged.
choice: The equilibrium shifts right and $K$ increases.
choice: Nothing shifts, because temperature does not affect equilibrium.
hint: Treat heat as a product of an exothermic reaction.
solution: For an exothermic reaction, heat is a product. Adding heat shifts the system left, away from the added heat, and the value of $K$ decreases. Only a change in temperature changes $K$.

@practice num
prompt: For $\text{PCl}_5 \rightleftharpoons \text{PCl}_3 + \text{Cl}_2$, $K_c = 0.040$. Starting with $0.50\ \text{M}$ $\text{PCl}_5$ and no products, find the equilibrium $[\text{Cl}_2]$ in M. Solve the quadratic (do not neglect $x$).
answer: 0.123
check: (-0.040 + math.sqrt(0.040**2 + 4 * 0.040 * 0.50)) / 2
tol: 1%
unit: M
hint: Let $x$ be the amount that reacts: $K_c = x^2/(0.50 - x)$.
hint: Rearrange to $x^2 + 0.040x - 0.020 = 0$ and use the quadratic formula.
solution: $x^2/(0.50 - x) = 0.040$ gives $x^2 + 0.040x - 0.020 = 0$, so $x = [-0.040 + \sqrt{0.0016 + 0.080}]/2 = 0.123\ \text{M}$.

@quiz num
prompt: For $2\text{SO}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{SO}_3$, the equilibrium concentrations are $[\text{SO}_2] = 0.30\ \text{M}$, $[\text{O}_2] = 0.10\ \text{M}$ and $[\text{SO}_3] = 0.60\ \text{M}$. Find $K_c$.
answer: 40
check: 0.60**2 / (0.30**2 * 0.10)
tol: 1%
hint: Raise $[\text{SO}_2]$ and $[\text{SO}_3]$ to the second power.
solution: $K_c = (0.60)^2/((0.30)^2(0.10)) = 0.36/0.0090 = 40$.

@quiz mc
prompt: Helium gas is added to an equilibrium mixture of $\text{N}_2$, $\text{H}_2$ and $\text{NH}_3$ in a rigid container, so the total pressure rises but the volume is constant. What is the effect on the equilibrium?
choice: It shifts toward $\text{NH}_3$ because total pressure increased.
choice: It shifts toward $\text{N}_2$ and $\text{H}_2$ because the total pressure increased.
choice*: There is no shift, because the partial pressures of the reacting gases are unchanged.
choice: $K_p$ increases because pressure is higher.
hint: Equilibrium depends on the concentrations or partial pressures of the species in the expression.
solution: An inert gas at constant volume leaves the partial pressures and concentrations of the reactants and products unchanged, so $Q = K$ still holds and nothing shifts. $K$ depends only on temperature.
