@article
A **coordination complex** has a central metal ion bonded to **ligands**, species that donate a lone pair to the metal (a Lewis acid-base interaction). The number of donor atoms attached to the metal is its **coordination number** (commonly $6$, octahedral, or $4$, tetrahedral or square planar). Ligands that attach through one donor atom ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{Cl}^-$, $\text{CN}^-$, CO) are **monodentate**; those that attach through two or more are **chelating**, for example ethylenediamine (en, two donors), oxalate (two) and EDTA$^{4-}$ (six). Chelates are much more stable than comparable monodentate complexes (the **chelate effect**, mainly an entropy advantage).

### Oxidation state and d-electrons

The **oxidation state** of the metal is the charge it would carry if the ligands were removed with their electron pairs. Set the complex's charge equal to the metal's oxidation state plus the sum of ligand charges. In $[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-}$ the cyanides contribute $-6$, so $\text{Fe} = +2$. A neutral atom of Fe is $[\text{Ar}]4s^23d^6$; the ion $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ is $d^6$, and $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ is $d^5$. Transition-metal cations lose the $s$ electrons first.

### Crystal field theory

In an octahedral complex the five d orbitals split into a lower set of three ($t_{2g}$) and an upper set of two ($e_g$), separated by the **crystal field splitting** $\Delta_o$. The size of $\Delta_o$ depends on the ligand (the **spectrochemical series**: $\text{I}^- < \text{Cl}^- < \text{H}_2\text{O} < \text{NH}_3 < \text{en} < \text{CN}^- \approx \text{CO}$, weak field to strong field) and on the metal.

- If $\Delta_o$ is small relative to the electron-pairing energy, electrons occupy all orbitals singly first: **high spin**, more unpaired electrons, **paramagnetic**.
- If $\Delta_o$ is large, electrons fill the lower $t_{2g}$ set first: **low spin**, fewer unpaired electrons (zero for low-spin $d^6$, **diamagnetic**).

Octahedral $d^6$ is high spin $t_{2g}^4e_g^2$ with $4$ unpaired electrons, or low spin $t_{2g}^6$ with $0$. The color of a complex arises from absorption of a photon with energy equal to $\Delta_o$; the observed color is the complement of the absorbed one. The energy per mole for a photon of wavelength $\lambda$ is $E = hcN_A/\lambda$.

### Iron in hemoglobin

Heme is an $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ion held by the four nitrogen atoms of a porphyrin ring. A histidine side chain of the protein (the proximal histidine) is the fifth ligand, and the sixth site binds $\text{O}_2$. In **deoxyhemoglobin** the iron is high-spin $d^6$ and sits slightly out of the ring plane. Binding $\text{O}_2$ makes the iron low-spin and smaller, so it moves into the plane and pulls the histidine and the attached protein helix; this motion is the structural basis of **cooperativity** among the four subunits.

- **Carbon monoxide** binds the same $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ site far more tightly than $\text{O}_2$ and also raises the oxygen affinity of the remaining sites, so oxygen delivery falls. Treatment is high-concentration oxygen.
- **Oxidation to $\text{Fe}^{3+}$** (methemoglobin, caused by nitrites or some drugs) leaves a heme that cannot bind $\text{O}_2$ reversibly.
- **Cyanide** binds $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ in cytochrome $c$ oxidase and blocks the respiratory chain.

### Chelation and other metals

Chelating agents treat metal overload or poisoning: deferoxamine binds excess $\text{Fe}^{3+}$, and EDTA salts bind lead. Cisplatin, a square-planar $\text{Pt}^{2+}$ complex, binds DNA bases. Zinc, copper, iron and magnesium act as enzyme cofactors, and cobalt sits at the center of vitamin B$_{12}$.

@example
title: Oxidation state, d count and spin
problem: For $[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-}$, find the oxidation state of iron, the d-electron count, and the number of unpaired electrons. Cyanide is a strong-field ligand.
step: Six $\text{CN}^-$ ligands contribute $-6$. The complex charge is $-4$, so $\text{Fe} + (-6) = -4$ and $\text{Fe} = +2$.
step: $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ is $d^6$.
step: $\text{CN}^-$ is strong-field, so $\Delta_o$ is large and the electrons fill $t_{2g}$ completely: $t_{2g}^6e_g^0$. There are $0$ unpaired electrons, so the complex is diamagnetic.

@example
title: How the hemoglobin iron changes on oxygen binding
problem: Describe the iron in deoxyhemoglobin and oxyhemoglobin.
step: In both forms the iron is $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ($d^6$), bonded to four porphyrin nitrogens and one histidine nitrogen.
step: Deoxyhemoglobin has five ligands, and the iron is high-spin ($4$ unpaired electrons) and sits above the ring plane.
step: Binding $\text{O}_2$ as the sixth ligand raises $\Delta_o$, making the iron low-spin and smaller, so it moves into the porphyrin plane and pulls the histidine, which triggers the cooperative conformational change.

@mistakes
- Removing the $d$ electrons before the $s$ electrons when writing a transition-metal cation configuration.
- Counting a chelating ligand as one donor atom when computing the coordination number.
- Confusing a strong-field ligand with a high-spin complex; strong-field ligands give low spin.
- Saying CO oxidizes the iron; it binds Fe$^{2+}$, while nitrites oxidize it to Fe$^{3+}$.

@practice self
prompt: Explain, in terms of the iron atom, how hemoglobin binds oxygen. Include the oxidation state and spin state of iron in the deoxy and oxy forms, the ligands, and why binding at one subunit makes binding at the others easier.
hint: Think about the oxidation state, the five fixed ligands and the sixth site, then how spin state changes the size of the iron.
solution: In hemoglobin the heme iron is Fe$^{2+}$ ($d^6$), held by the four nitrogens of the porphyrin and a proximal histidine. In deoxyhemoglobin it is five-coordinate and high spin (four unpaired electrons), too large to fit in the ring plane, so it sits slightly above it. O$_2$ binds as the sixth ligand; the stronger ligand field makes the iron low spin (no unpaired electrons) and smaller, so it moves into the porphyrin plane and pulls the histidine and its helix. This shift is transmitted to neighboring subunits, shifting the tetramer toward the high-affinity conformation, which is cooperativity. Oxidized Fe$^{3+}$ (methemoglobin) cannot bind O$_2$ reversibly.

answer: 239.3
check: 6.626e-34 * 2.998e8 * 6.022e23 / 500e-9 / 1000
tol: 1%
