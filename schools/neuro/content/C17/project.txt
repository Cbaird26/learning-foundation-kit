@review
## Week-by-week review

**Week 1, classical conditioning.** The Rescorla–Wagner rule: $\Delta V_X = \alpha(\lambda - V_{\text{total}})$ for every present cue, with $\lambda = 1$ for a reinforced trial and 0 for an omitted one. It gives negatively accelerated acquisition, extinction by shrinkage, overshadowing and blocking, and misses latent inhibition and timing.

**Week 2, instrumental learning.** Reinforcement, punishment, extinction; FR, VR, FI and VI schedules; the matching law $B_1/(B_1+B_2) = R_1/(R_1+R_2)$; the delta rule $Q \leftarrow Q + \alpha(r - Q)$ and softmax choice $1/(1 + e^{-\beta\,\Delta Q})$.

**Week 3, reinforcement learning.** Return $G = \sum \gamma^k r_{t+k+1}$, TD error $\delta = r + \gamma V(s') - V(s)$, TD(0) update $V \leftarrow V + \alpha\delta$, Q-learning with $\max_{a'}$ and SARSA with the next action.

**Week 4, dopamine.** Phasic dopamine behaves like $\delta$: burst for unexpected reward, burst at the cue after learning, dip at an omission. Debates: novelty, aversive events, ramping, motivation and heterogeneity.

**Week 5, habits and goals.** Model-based $Q = \sum_o P(o|a)U(o)$ against cached model-free values; devaluation tests; mixture $wQ_{\text{MB}} + (1-w)Q_{\text{MF}}$; the direct and indirect pathways; striatal regions.

**Week 6, memory systems.** H.M., declarative and nondeclarative memory, working memory, z-scores and double dissociations.

**Week 7, hippocampus and consolidation.** The trisynaptic loop, standard and multiple-trace accounts, replay and ripples, the two-store model $h_{t+1} = (1-d_h)h_t$, $c_{t+1} = c_t + k h_t$.

**Week 8, spatial cells.** Place cells, grid cells, Gaussian fields, Poisson maximum-likelihood decoding $\sum_i [n_i\log f_i - f_iT]$, population vector, grid periods and least common multiples.

**Week 9, LTP and LTD.** Induction, cooperativity, associativity and specificity; the NMDA magnesium block $B(V)$; calcium thresholds for LTD and LTP; percent of baseline.

**Week 10, Hebb and STDP.** $\Delta w = \eta xy$ is unstable; Oja's rule $\eta y(\mathbf{x} - y\mathbf{w})$ finds the unit principal eigenvector; the STDP window with $\Delta t = t_{\text{post}} - t_{\text{pre}}$.

**Week 11, Hopfield networks.** $W_{ij} = \frac1N\sum_\mu \xi_i^\mu\xi_j^\mu$, zero diagonal, asynchronous updates, $E = -\frac12\mathbf{s}^\top\mathbf{W}\mathbf{s}$ never rises, capacity about $0.14N$.

**Week 12, lifespan.** Critical periods, infantile amnesia, aging and Alzheimer's disease, the forgetting curve $R = e^{-t/S}$, graceful degradation under connection loss.

## Common errors

Using separate errors for each cue; forgetting that a terminal state has value 0; mixing the sign of $\Delta t$ in STDP; updating a Hopfield network synchronously and expecting convergence; calling a single dissociation a double one; reading decoding accuracy as proof the brain decodes that way.

The two action values start at 0. On each trial, the probability of choosing action 1 is $1/(1 + e^{-\beta(Q_1 - Q_0)})$ and of choosing action 0 is one minus that; add the log of the probability of the chosen action, then update only the chosen action's value with $Q_c \leftarrow Q_c + \alpha(r - Q_c)$. `choices` is a list of 0s and 1s and `rewards` a list of rewards of the same length. model:
```python
import math


def choice_loglik(choices, rewards, alpha, beta):
    """Log-likelihood of bandit choices under delta-rule softmax learning."""
    Q = [0.0, 0.0]
    total = 0.0
    for c, r in zip(choices, rewards):
        p1 = 1.0 / (1.0 + math.exp(-beta * (Q[1] - Q[0])))
        total += math.log(p1 if c == 1 else 1.0 - p1)
        Q[c] += alpha * (r - Q[c])
    return total
```
hint: Compute the probability from the values before the update, add the log of the chosen action's probability, and only then update the chosen value.
solution: Taking the likelihood before the update is what makes this a prediction of the choice, not a description after the fact. With beta zero every choice has probability one half, so the log-likelihood is $n\ln 0.5$, which is lower than for a positive beta when the learner repeatedly chooses the action that has been rewarded. A fitting routine would maximize this function over alpha and beta on a grid.

prompt: A rat learns that a tone predicts a food pellet. Using the models of this course, describe a possible chain of mechanisms: how a prediction error is computed and signaled, how it could change synaptic strengths through Hebbian-style plasticity, and how the learned association could later be consolidated. For each link name the model or finding that supports it and one reason to be cautious. Then say what the tone's value would do over the first twenty trials in the Rescorla–Wagner model with a learning rate of 0.2.
hint: Link week 1 or 3 (prediction error), week 4 (dopamine), weeks 9 and 10 (LTP and plasticity rules), and week 7 (consolidation).
solution: In the Rescorla–Wagner or TD models the tone's value is updated by a prediction error $\delta$, large at first and shrinking as the pellet becomes predicted; in the tone-pellet task $\delta$ at the pellet fell to near zero as the value rose, and, with a delay, moved toward the tone. Phasic dopamine firing in midbrain neurons resembles this error (bursts for unpredicted reward, a dip for omission), but dopamine also responds to novelty and other events, so it is not the whole story. Dopamine released onto striatal synapses could gate Hebbian plasticity: cortical input representing the tone, active when the postsynaptic neuron is depolarized and dopamine arrives, strengthens through NMDA-dependent LTP, an idea supported by the induction rules of LTP and by three-factor extensions of STDP, though the in vivo rules are not settled. Later, hippocampal and cortical replay in slow-wave sleep could strengthen cortical traces (the two-store model), supported by the effects of disrupting ripples in rats and debated as to the extent for human memory. In the Rescorla–Wagner model with $\alpha = 0.2$ and $\lambda = 1$ the value after $n$ trials is $1 - 0.8^n$: 0.20, 0.36, 0.49, and so on, reaching $1 - 0.8^{20} = 0.988$ after twenty trials, a negatively accelerated curve.
