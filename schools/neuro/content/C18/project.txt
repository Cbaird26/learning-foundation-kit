material: **Starter code for a quantitative illustration (feedback loop; replace with your own choice and constants).**
```python
def settle(d, a, G, hours, dt=0.01):
    """Error e under de/dt = d - (a + G) * e, by Euler steps."""
    e = 0.0
    for _ in range(int(round(hours / dt))):
        e += dt * (d - (a + G) * e)
    return e
```

prompt: Write an integrated answer (about 300 words) on this question: how well do animal findings explain how stress changes learned fear in people? Use at least three course ideas (the HPA axis, fear conditioning and extinction, the amygdala and hippocampus, the evidence ladder) and conclude with what evidence would raise your confidence.
hint: Link cortisol effects on the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex to extinction, and grade each link on the evidence ladder.
solution: In rodents, stress activates the HPA axis through the paraventricular nucleus and raises corticosterone, and chronic stress shrinks hippocampal and prefrontal dendrites while enlarging amygdala dendrites; fear extinction relies on infralimbic prefrontal cortex and hippocampus acting on amygdala output, and stress impairs extinction learning in rodents. These are causal manipulations (rung 2). In people, imaging links reduced prefrontal and hippocampal engagement to poorer extinction recall in some groups, and hippocampal volume is reduced in post-traumatic stress disorder, but the twin study shows that small volume can pre-exist trauma, so causal direction is mixed (rung 3 to 4). The homology of infralimbic cortex and human ventromedial prefrontal cortex is a hypothesis, and lab fear conditioning is not clinical anxiety. Overall, the animal findings give a mechanistic hypothesis that fits human data, but they do not show that stress hormones cause the human changes. Better evidence would come from longitudinal human studies with pre-trauma baselines, pharmacological studies with ethics oversight, and replications across sexes and species.
