@article
An **empire** is a state that rules many peoples and territories under a single authority, usually by conquest. Between roughly 550 BCE and 500 CE, several empires grew so large that they had to invent lasting methods of administration. These are called **classical empires**.

### Persia

The Achaemenid Persian Empire began with conquests around 550 BCE and grew to stretch from Egypt and Anatolia to the Indus. It was divided into provinces called **satrapies**, each run by a **satrap** (governor) who collected tribute and kept order. Royal inspectors reported to the king. The **Royal Road**, with relay stations, carried messages and troops across thousands of kilometers. The empire generally allowed conquered peoples to keep their languages, laws and religions as long as they paid tribute. Persia fell to Alexander of Macedon's army in 330 BCE.

### India: the Mauryan Empire

The Mauryan Empire (c. 321 to 185 BCE) unified most of the Indian subcontinent. A large army, spies and regional governors held it together. Under the emperor Ashoka (reigned c. 268 to 232 BCE), inscribed edicts on rocks and pillars announced policies of moral conduct and tolerance, influenced by Buddhism.

### China: the Han dynasty

After the short Qin dynasty (221 to 206 BCE) unified China under a strict legal system, the Han dynasty (206 BCE to 220 CE) ruled through an emperor, a bureaucracy of officials, and commanderies and counties. The Han promoted Confucian ideas, which stress duty, family loyalty and rule by moral example, and trained officials in them. Recommendations and examinations selected officials, an early form of the civil-service system that later dynasties expanded. Han armies expanded into Central Asia, linking China to the Silk Road trade network.

### Rome

Rome began as a city-state and became a republic after expelling its kings (traditionally 509 BCE). The Republic had elected magistrates, a Senate and assemblies, but power was concentrated among wealthy families. After civil wars, Augustus became the first emperor in 27 BCE, keeping republican forms while holding real power. The **Pax Romana** (about 27 BCE to 180 CE) was a long period of relative internal peace. Rome governed through provinces with governors, a professional army, Roman law, roads and the extension of citizenship. The western half of the empire ended in 476 CE; the eastern half (the Byzantine Empire) continued until 1453.

### Comparing governments

Both Han China and Rome had an emperor, an army and a tax system, and both used roads and writing to administer distant regions. They differed in how officials were chosen: in Han China, the state trained and examined scholar-officials in Confucian learning, while in Rome, officials came largely from senatorial and equestrian families and army commanders played a large part in politics. The Han emperor ruled under the idea of a heavenly mandate, while Roman emperors held republican titles and, later, were sometimes honored as divine. Historians debate the relative importance of these differences, and it is always good practice to ask whose sources describe the government: court historians wrote for rulers.

### BCE and CE arithmetic

There is no year 0: 1 BCE is followed by 1 CE. So the span from 27 BCE to 476 CE is $27 + 476 - 1 = 502$ years.

@example
title: Length of the Han dynasty
problem: The Han dynasty is traditionally dated 206 BCE to 220 CE. About how many years did it last, and is that longer or shorter than the Western Roman Empire from 27 BCE to 476 CE?
step: Because there is no year 0, add the two year numbers and subtract 1: $206 + 220 - 1 = 425$ years.
step: The span from 27 BCE to 476 CE is $27 + 476 - 1 = 502$ years.
step: The Han lasted about 425 years, which is shorter than the roughly 502 years from the first emperor to the end of the western empire.
step: Both counts are approximate because the Han had an interruption (the Xin dynasty, 9 to 23 CE) and Roman dates depend on how you define the start.

@mistakes
- Subtracting BCE and CE years directly (for example $476 - 27$) and forgetting that there is no year 0.
- Describing the Roman Empire as ending in 476 CE everywhere; the eastern half continued.
- Assuming empires ruled by force alone; most also used roads, law, tax systems and local elites.
- Claiming Han China and Rome were "the same" kind of state; they shared features but chose officials differently.
