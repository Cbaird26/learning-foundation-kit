@article
A wiring diagram is the first thing many people want from a circuit: which regions project where, and which cells talk to which. Anatomy is the foundation for every perturbation study, because you need to know what you are manipulating and what lies downstream. It is also the source of many over-interpreted claims, since a line in a diagram can mean very different things.

### Tracing

**Anterograde tracers** are taken up near the cell body at the injection site and transported along axons, labeling projections out of a region. **Retrograde tracers** are taken up by axon terminals and carried back to cell bodies, labeling the neurons that project to the injected site. Genetically engineered viruses extend the toolkit: some label neurons of a defined type, and **transsynaptic** systems are designed to move across one synapse, so that cells connected to a starting population are labeled. All of these are indirect: what you see is where a marker ended up, not a synapse.

Artifacts to keep in mind:

- **Fibers of passage.** A tracer taken up by axons that merely pass through the injection site labels cells that do not project to the site.
- **Spread.** The injection may extend beyond the intended region, so labeled cells may project to a neighbor.
- **Tropism.** A virus may infect some cell types or some axons more readily than others, so absence of label is weak evidence.
- **Expression and efficiency.** The number of labeled cells depends on the amount of tracer, the survival time and expression, not only on the strength of the connection.

### Counting: fraction or density

Raw counts of labeled cells in each input region are easy to report and easy to misread. Two normalizations answer different questions.

The **fraction of inputs** from region $j$ is $n_j / \sum_k n_k$ and asks which regions supply the largest share of the labeled input. The **density** $n_j / V_j$, with $V_j$ the region's volume, asks how concentrated the projecting cells are. A large region can contribute the most cells without having the highest density, and a small region can be a dense source that contributes little in total. Neither is a measure of synaptic strength or of functional influence.

**Connection probability** from paired recordings is $k/m$, where $k$ of $m$ tested neuron pairs were connected, and it has sampling error: with $m = 40$ pairs and $k = 4$, the estimate $0.10$ has a standard error of about $\sqrt{0.1 \times 0.9 / 40} \approx 0.047$.

### Connectomics

Electron microscopy of serial sections of tissue can reconstruct neurons and identify synapses. White and colleagues (1986) reconstructed the nervous system of the nematode *C. elegans*, whose hermaphrodite nervous system has about 300 neurons and is nearly identical across animals. Larger reconstructions of fly and mouse tissue have followed, and they give the identity of presynaptic and postsynaptic partners and the number of synapses between them. What they do not give directly:

- **Sign and strength.** Whether a synapse excites or inhibits, and how strong it is, usually requires other evidence; synapse size or number is only a proxy.
- **Dynamics.** A connectome is a snapshot in one animal, and the same wiring supports different activity depending on neuromodulation and state.
- **Non-synaptic signaling.** Neuromodulators released at a distance and gap junctions are not captured in the same way as chemical synapses with clear morphology.
- **Variability.** Individuals differ, and a single reconstruction does not tell you how much.

Even the complete wiring of a small circuit has not by itself explained its function, which shows that structure constrains function without determining it.

### What a connectivity claim needs

A statement such as "region $A$ drives region $B$" has three parts: a **structural** claim (there are projections from $A$ to $B$), a **functional** claim (activity in $A$ changes activity in $B$) and a **behavioral** claim (that pathway is used for a behavior). Tracing supports the first, physiology with stimulation supports the second, and perturbation of the pathway during behavior supports the third. A strong paper says which of the three it has shown.

@example
title: Fraction or density?
problem: Retrograde tracing from a target site labels 150 cells in region A (volume 0.50 mm³), 90 in region B (0.10 mm³) and 60 in region C (0.60 mm³). Which region provides the largest fraction of inputs, and which has the highest density of labeled cells?
step: Total labeled cells: $150 + 90 + 60 = 300$. Fractions: A has $150/300 = 0.50$, B has $90/300 = 0.30$ and C has $60/300 = 0.20$.
step: Densities: A has $150/0.50 = 300$ cells per mm³, B has $90/0.10 = 900$ cells per mm³ and C has $60/0.60 = 100$ cells per mm³.
step: Compare: region A supplies the largest share of the input, but region B has the densest source, three times that of A.
step: Interpret: neither number says how strongly A or B drives the target. Both depend on tracer uptake and spread, and a conclusion that "B is the main input" would need an argument about why density, not share, matters.
answer: Region A has the largest fraction (0.50); region B has the highest density (900 cells per mm³).

@mistakes
- Treating labeled-cell counts as a measure of connection strength.
- Ignoring fibers of passage and injection spread when claiming a direct projection.
- Reading absent label as absent connection, when tropism or uptake can account for it.
- Treating a connectome as showing which synapses excite or inhibit, or how the circuit behaves.

@practice ms
prompt: A study traces inputs to a cortical site and finds no labeled cells in a candidate region. Which of these could explain the absence of label even if the projection exists?
choice*: The tracer or virus infects the relevant axon type inefficiently.
choice*: The injection missed the part of the site that receives the projection.
choice*: The survival time was too short for transport.
choice: Projections that exist always label with every tracer, so absence of label shows absence of a projection.
hint: Think of every step between the injection and a labeled cell body, and ask which could fail without the connection being absent.
solution: Tropism, a missed target zone and insufficient transport time all give a false negative. The claim that every projection labels with every tracer is false, which is why absence of label is weak evidence.

| P | 240 | 1.20 |

Evaluate the claim in about 150 words: compute the fraction and the density for each region, say which conclusions each supports, and name two additional pieces of evidence needed to support a claim about the strength of the input.

solution: Total labeled is 480. Fractions: P 0.50, Q 0.3125, R 0.1875. Densities: P 200, Q 500 and R 100 cells per mm³. @quiz ms
prompt: Which controls strengthen the claim that a retrograde label reflects a true projection from region $N$ to the target site $T$?

choice*: Replicating the label with a second tracer or a tracer with different uptake properties.
