@article
Every method in this course was once new, and a method's first results are rarely its last word. This lesson does not list the state of the art, which is changing and which you should read in recent reviews. It gives you tools to judge any new method or result: what question it answers, which rung of the evidence ladder it reaches, and what is still unknown.

### Recording at scale

Dense probes (Jun and colleagues, 2017) and two-photon imaging have been used to record from very large numbers of neurons at once, and the counts have grown over time. More neurons help in clear ways: you can read out single-trial population states, find rare cell types and estimate the structure of population activity. They do not by themselves answer the question of what is represented or used. A useful summary of population structure is its **dimensionality**. If the covariance of the population activity has eigenvalues $\lambda_k$, the **participation ratio** is

$$\text{PR} = \frac{\left(\sum_k \lambda_k\right)^2}{\sum_k \lambda_k^2},$$

which equals $N$ when $N$ directions carry equal variance and approaches 1 when one direction dominates. For eigenvalues $[6, 3, 2, 1]$, $\text{PR} = 12^2/(36 + 9 + 4 + 1) = 144/50 = 2.88$. Low dimensionality compared with the number of neurons is often reported, but the value depends on the task and its richness, on noise and on how the measure is defined, so comparisons need the same method and a task rich enough to expose higher dimensions. Data volume is itself a limit: imaging at 30 frames per second of 512 by 512 pixels at 2 bytes per pixel produces roughly 15.7 MB per second.

### Structure and function together

Connectome reconstructions give wiring and imaging gives activity; combining them in the same circuit lets models be constrained by both. The complete wiring diagram of a small nervous system (White and colleagues, 1986) constrained, but did not by itself explain, its function. Whether larger reconstructions will let activity be predicted from structure is open.

### Perturbation: precision and calibration

Newer perturbation tools aim at more precise patterns, at single cells or defined ensembles, at faster or more reversible control and at closed-loop designs that deliver the perturbation based on ongoing activity. The conceptual issues of lessons 5 and 6 remain: calibration of what was changed, off-target effects, compensation and a comparison with natural activity. A more precise tool narrows the possible explanations but does not remove the need for controls.

### Models as partners

Deep networks trained on tasks have been compared with neural activity: networks trained to recognize objects predict responses in the primate ventral visual stream better than earlier models (Yamins and colleagues, 2014; Khaligh-Razavi and Kriegeskorte, 2014), and a trained recurrent network reproduced population structure in prefrontal cortex (Mante and colleagues, 2013). These show that a model class can match data. They do not show that the brain implements the same mechanism: many different networks may predict the same activity, so the strongest tests are predictions of the effects of perturbations or of new stimuli. How to decide among models that fit equally well is open.

### Practice of the field

Preregistration, registered reports, sharing of data and code, power analysis (Button and colleagues, 2013) and reporting of all animals address the reliability problems that run through the methods lessons. Human neural data, such as intracranial and noninvasive recordings, raise additional issues: international bodies have begun to treat neural data as sensitive. The OECD adopted a Recommendation on Responsible Innovation in Neurotechnology in December 2019, and UNESCO's member states adopted a Recommendation on the Ethics of Neurotechnology in November 2025. Both are non-binding. Laws and guidelines are changing, so check the current versions.

### What is open

Whether recording everything would suffice to explain behavior; what counts as an explanation of a circuit (a model that predicts, a mechanism that can be manipulated, or both); how to link molecular, cellular and systems levels; and how to generalize across individuals, species and conditions. Your proposal will take a stand on the first parts of this list and should say so.

@example
title: Paired sample size by hand
problem: A pilot study of a perturbation found a mean paired difference of 3.0 units and a standard deviation of the differences of 6.0 units. How many animals are needed to detect a true difference of 3.0 with two-sided $\alpha = 0.05$ ($z = 1.96$) and power 0.80 ($z = 0.84$)?
step: The standardized paired effect is $d_z = 3.0/6.0 = 0.5$.
step: The formula $n = (z_\alpha + z_\beta)^2/d_z^2$ gives $2.8^2/0.25 = 7.84/0.25 = 31.36$.
step: Round up to 32 animals.
step: Caution: the pilot's $d_z$ is itself uncertain and tends to be optimistic when pilot samples are small, so plan with a conservative effect, the smallest one that would matter, and consider a margin for exclusions.
answer: 32 animals.

@mistakes
- Treating more neurons as more understanding: recording more units does not show which signals are used.
- Comparing dimensionality values across studies that used different tasks or definitions.
- Concluding that a model matching neural data reveals the mechanism, when many models can fit equally well.
- Treating the newest method's results as established because they are new, when they have not yet been replicated or tested with controls.

assert paired_n(-4.0, 6.0) == 18

model:
```python
import math


def paired_n(mean_diff, sd_diff, z_alpha=1.96, z_beta=0.84):
    """Animals needed for a paired comparison, rounded up."""
    if mean_diff == 0:
        raise ValueError("mean_diff must be nonzero")
    d_z = abs(mean_diff) / sd_diff
    return math.ceil(((z_alpha + z_beta) / d_z) ** 2)
```
hint: Compute the standardized effect first, raise the error for zero, and round up with `math.ceil`.
solution: The formula needs the standardized paired effect $d_z = |\delta|/s_d$, which is why a zero difference is an error and the sign does not matter. Rounding up, not to the nearest integer, keeps the power at or above the target; in the fourth test the exact value is $10.5$, which rounds up to 11.

prompt: Write the measurement and perturbation sections of a short proposal (about 250 words) to test whether a population of neurons in a region of your choice is needed for a specific behavior. Name the recording method and the perturbation method and why each fits the question, the controls for off-target effects and compensation, the sample size reasoning, and what each of three outcomes would show.
solution: Hypothesis: neurons of type $T$ in region $R$ carry the delayed cue during a memory-guided choice task and are needed for the correct choice. Measurement: two-photon calcium imaging of $T$ cells, chosen because cell-type labeling and tracking the same cells across sessions are needed, accepting that the signal is a slow proxy for spiking (so delay-period activity is analyzed with deconvolution and not for fine latency). Perturbation: optogenetic inhibition restricted to the delay, with randomized interleaved light trials (30%), because the claim concerns the delay and requires epoch specificity. Controls: opsin-free animals receiving the same light for heating and visibility; masking light; verification of reduced activity by imaging during light and histology; a light-power series; silencing at the cue and the response as controls for other epochs; a motor control task. Compensation is addressed by the acute design and by a second, different manipulation in a subset. Sample size: from pilot data the per-animal effect has $d_z = 0.8$, so $n = (2.8/0.8)^2 = 12.25$, rounded up to 13 animals, plus two for exclusions, analyzed with animal as the unit. Outcomes: (a) the delay population encodes the cue and silencing impairs choices only in the delay, supporting a necessary role of delay activity; (b) silencing impairs choices but the delay activity does not predict the cue, suggesting a role other than memory; (c) no behavioral effect despite verified silencing, indicating that this population is not necessary in this task, though redundancy or compensation cannot be excluded.
