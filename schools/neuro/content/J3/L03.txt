@article
A **linear equation** in one variable is an equation where the variable appears only to the first power, like $3x + 5 = 20$. You already know the main rule: whatever you do to one side of an equation, do to the other, so the two sides stay balanced. Multistep equations just take more than two moves.

### A routine that works

1. **Simplify each side.** Use the distributive property, $a(b + c) = ab + ac$, and combine like terms.
2. **Collect variable terms on one side** and constants on the other by adding or subtracting.
3. **Undo multiplication** by dividing both sides by the coefficient.
4. **Check** in the original equation.

Take $2(x + 3) = 5x - 9$. Distribute: $2x + 6 = 5x - 9$. Subtract $2x$ from both sides: $6 = 3x - 9$. Add $9$: $15 = 3x$. Divide by $3$: $x = 5$. Check: left side $2(8) = 16$, right side $25 - 9 = 16$.

### Fractions

When an equation has fractions, multiply every term by the least common denominator. In $\frac{x}{2} + \frac{x}{3} = 10$ the LCD is $6$, giving $3x + 2x = 60$, so $x = 12$.

### One solution, none, or all

Most linear equations have exactly one solution, but two other outcomes can happen after you simplify.

- If you reach a **false** statement like $3 = 7$, the equation has **no solution**. Example: $2x + 3 = 2x + 7$. Subtracting $2x$ leaves $3 = 7$, which no value of $x$ can fix.
- If you reach a **true** statement like $5 = 5$, the equation has **infinitely many solutions**; every real number works. Example: $2(x + 1) = 2x + 2$.

These cases happen when both sides have the same slope in $x$: parallel lines never meet, and identical lines meet everywhere. This idea returns when we study systems of equations.

### Keep the signs straight

The most common slips are with negatives. In $-(x - 4)$, the minus sign distributes to both terms: $-x + 4$. When you subtract a variable term from both sides, write it out so you do not lose the sign.

@example
title: Distributing and collecting
problem: Solve $4(x + 2) - 3 = 2x + 11$.
step: Distribute the $4$: $4x + 8 - 3 = 2x + 11$. Combine like terms on the left: $4x + 5 = 2x + 11$.
step: Subtract $2x$ from both sides: $2x + 5 = 11$.
step: Subtract $5$ from both sides: $2x = 6$.
step: Divide by $2$: $x = 3$.
step: Check: left side $4(3 + 2) - 3 = 17$, right side $2(3) + 11 = 17$. Both agree.

@mistakes
- Forgetting to distribute to every term inside the brackets, such as $3(x - 2) = 3x - 2$.
- Losing a negative sign when distributing, for example $-(x + 4) = -x + 4$.
- Doing an operation to only one side of the equation.
- Declaring "no solution" when the result is $0 = 0$; that means infinitely many solutions.

prompt: Solve for $x$: $5x - 3 = 2x + 12$.
answer: 5
check: root(lambda x: (5*x - 3) - (2*x + 12), -100, 100)
hint: Subtract $2x$ from both sides to collect the $x$ terms on the left.
hint: You get $3x - 3 = 12$.
solution: $3x - 3 = 12$, so $3x = 15$ and $x = 5$. Check: $5(5) - 3 = 22$ and $2(5) + 12 = 22$.

answer: 13
check: root(lambda x: 3*(x - 2) + 4 - (2*(x + 1) + 9), -100, 100)
hint: Distribute on both sides and combine like terms.
hint: The left side becomes $3x - 2$ and the right side becomes $2x + 11$.
solution: $3x - 2 = 2x + 11$, so $x = 13$. Check: $3(11) + 4 = 37$ and $2(14) + 9 = 37$.

answer: 5

hint: $8x - 4 = 3x + 21$.
solution: $5x = 25$, so $x = 5$. Check: $4(9) = 36$ and $15 + 21 = 36$.

@quiz num
prompt: Solve for $x$: $5 - 2(x - 3) = x - 7$.
answer: 6
check: root(lambda x: 5 - 2*(x - 3) - (x - 7), -100, 100)
hint: The minus sign in front of $2$ applies to both $x$ and $-3$.
hint: The left side simplifies to $11 - 2x$.
solution: $11 - 2x = x - 7$, so $18 = 3x$ and $x = 6$. Check: $5 - 2(3) = -1$ and $6 - 7 = -1$.
