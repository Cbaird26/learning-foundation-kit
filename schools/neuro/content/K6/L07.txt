@article
Words do different jobs in a sentence. The jobs are called **parts of speech**.

### The main jobs

- A **noun** names a person, place, thing or idea: *girl, park, ball.*
- A **verb** tells an action or a state: *run, jump, is.*
- An **adjective** describes a noun: *red ball, tall girl.*
- An **adverb** describes a verb and often tells how: *ran quickly, spoke softly.*
- A **pronoun** takes the place of a noun: *he, she, it, they.*

Try it: "The small dog barked loudly." *Dog* is a noun. *Small* is an adjective. *Barked* is a verb. *Loudly* is an adverb.

### Agreement

The **subject** is who or what the sentence is about. The **verb** must **agree** with the subject.

- One subject (singular) usually takes a verb with an **s**: "The dog **runs**."
- More than one (plural) takes a verb without the s: "The dogs **run**."

Here are more pairs:

| One | More than one |
|---|---|
| The bird sings. | The birds sing. |
| She is tall. | They are tall. |
| He has a pen. | We have pens. |

### Pronoun agreement

A pronoun must match the noun it replaces.

- "Mia lost her hat." (Mia is one girl, so her.)
- "The boys lost their hats." (Boys are more than one, so their.)

### Read it aloud

If a sentence sounds wrong when you say it, check the verb. "The cats runs" sounds wrong. "The cats run" sounds right.

@example
title: Fix the agreement
problem: Fix this sentence: "The two frogs jumps into the pond."
step: Find the subject. It is "frogs," and there are two of them, so it is plural.
step: The verb "jumps" is for one. A plural subject needs "jump."
step: Write the fix: "The two frogs jump into the pond."

@mistakes
- Using an s on the verb when the subject is plural.
- Mixing up adjectives and adverbs: "ran quick" should be "ran quickly."
- Mixing up "their", "there" and "they're".
