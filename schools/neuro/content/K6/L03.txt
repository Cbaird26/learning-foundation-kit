@article
Every story is told by someone. That someone is the **narrator**. The narrator's **point of view** is the way the story is seen and told.

### First person

In **first-person** point of view, the narrator is a character in the story. The narrator says "I," "me," "my," "we" and "our."

"I picked up my bat and swung hard. I missed!"

You know what the narrator thinks and feels, because "I" is telling you.

### Third person

In **third-person** point of view, the narrator is outside the story. The narrator says "he," "she," "they" and uses names.

"Lucas picked up his bat and swung hard. He missed!"

The narrator tells about the characters from the outside.

### Why it matters

The point of view changes what you learn. A first-person narrator may tell only what he or she saw and felt. Another person who was there may remember it differently.

### Comparing two accounts

Sometimes two people tell about the **same event**. To compare them, ask:

- What is the **same** in both?
- What is **different**?
- Why might each person tell it that way?

Here are two accounts of a rainy picnic.

Mara: "I was sad when the rain came. Our sandwiches got wet!"

Mara's dad: "The rain came, so we ran to the car and ate our sandwiches there. We laughed the whole time."

Both tell about rain and sandwiches. Mara tells about feeling sad. Her dad tells about laughing in the car. They saw the same event and told it in different ways.

@example
title: Name the point of view
problem: Read: "I held the puppy gently. It licked my chin, and I giggled." Is this first person or third person? How do you know?
step: Look at the pronouns. The words are "I," "my" and "I."
step: "I" means the narrator is a character in the story.
step: So this is first person. If it said "She held the puppy," it would be third person.

@mistakes
- Thinking that any story with a name in it is third person. Check for "I" and "my."
- Saying two accounts are the same just because they tell about the same event.
- Forgetting to say what is the same and what is different when you compare.
