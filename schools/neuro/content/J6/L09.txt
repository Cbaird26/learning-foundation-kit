@article
All matter is made of particles that are always moving. **Temperature** is a measure of the **average kinetic energy** of the particles in a substance. Hotter means the particles move faster on average. **Thermal energy** is the total kinetic energy of all the particles, so it depends on the amount of matter as well as the temperature. A bathtub of warm water has more thermal energy than a cup of hot water, even though the cup has the higher temperature.

**Heat** is the transfer of thermal energy from a warmer object to a cooler one. Heat always flows from hot to cold, never the other way, until both reach the same temperature (thermal equilibrium).

### Temperature scales

The Celsius scale sets water's freezing point at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and boiling point at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The Fahrenheit scale uses $32\,^\circ\mathrm{F}$ and $212\,^\circ\mathrm{F}$. The Kelvin scale starts at **absolute zero**, where particle motion is at a minimum. Conversions:

$$T_\mathrm{C} = \tfrac{5}{9}(T_\mathrm{F} - 32), \qquad T_\mathrm{K} = T_\mathrm{C} + 273.$$

### Specific heat and $Q = mc\Delta T$

Different materials need different amounts of energy to warm up. The **specific heat** $c$ is the energy needed to raise the temperature of $1\ \mathrm{g}$ of a substance by $1\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Water has a high specific heat, $c = 4.18\ \mathrm{J/(g\,^\circ C)}$. The heat absorbed or released is

$$Q = mc\,\Delta T,$$

with $m$ in grams and $\Delta T = T_\text{final} - T_\text{initial}$. This is why water heats and cools slowly and is used in cooling systems.

### Three ways heat moves

- **Conduction:** transfer by direct contact between particles, such as a metal spoon warming in hot soup. Metals are good conductors.
- **Convection:** transfer by the movement of a fluid (liquid or gas). Warm fluid rises and cool fluid sinks, setting up a circulating current, as in boiling water or a room heater.
- **Radiation:** transfer by electromagnetic waves, which need no matter. This is how the Sun warms Earth.

### Insulators

A material that conducts heat poorly is an **insulator**. Air trapped in foam, wool or fiberglass is a good insulator because gases conduct heat poorly and the trapped air cannot circulate easily. Insulators slow heat flow; they do not make something warm by themselves. A foam cup keeps hot drinks hot and cold drinks cold by slowing the transfer in both directions.

@example
title: Heat needed to warm water
problem: How much heat is needed to raise the temperature of $200\ \mathrm{g}$ of water from $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $45\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$? Use $c = 4.18\ \mathrm{J/(g\,^\circ C)}$.
step: Find the temperature change: $\Delta T = 45 - 20 = 25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.
step: Use $Q = mc\Delta T = 200 \times 4.18 \times 25$.
step: $Q = 20{,}900\ \mathrm{J}$, which is $20.9\ \mathrm{kJ}$.

@mistakes
- Saying that "cold" flows into a warm object. Heat always flows from the warmer object to the cooler one.
- Thinking temperature and heat are the same thing. Temperature is an average; heat is a transfer of energy.
- Using the final temperature instead of the change in temperature $\Delta T$ in $Q = mc\Delta T$.
- Forgetting to convert Fahrenheit to Celsius before using $c$ in $\mathrm{J/(g\,^\circ C)}$.

@practice self
prompt: A pan of water is heated on a stove, the metal handle becomes hot, and you feel warmth from the burner when you hold your hand beside it. Identify the type of heat transfer in each of the three cases and explain each in a sentence.
solution: Water in the pan: convection. Hot water near the bottom rises and cooler water sinks, setting up a circulating current. Metal handle: conduction. Fast-moving particles pass energy to neighboring particles by direct contact. Warmth beside the burner: radiation. The hot burner gives off electromagnetic waves that travel through the air and warm your hand without contact.
hint: Ask whether energy moves by contact, by moving fluid, or through empty space.

rubric: Names conduction for the handle and mentions contact between particles.
rubric: Names radiation for the warmth beside the burner and says it needs no medium.
rubric: Each explanation is one clear sentence tied to the situation.
