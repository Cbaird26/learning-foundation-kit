@lesson
title: Main idea and key details
summary: Find what a passage is mostly about and the details that support it, and say the main idea in your own words.
objective: Tell the difference between the topic and the main idea.
objective: Pick out key details that support the main idea.
objective: Read a passage aloud and state its main idea.
reading: ReadWorks, free reading passages: choose a short nonfiction passage and say its main idea after reading.

@article
Every passage is about something. What it is about, in one or two words, is the **topic**. The **main idea** is the most important thing the author says about the topic. It is a whole sentence.

### Topic and main idea

Here is a short passage: "Bees help flowers. A bee drinks sweet nectar. Pollen sticks to its legs. It carries the pollen to the next flower. This helps new seeds grow."

- The **topic** is bees.
- The **main idea** is "Bees help flowers make seeds."

### Key details

**Key details** are the small facts that prove or explain the main idea. In the bee passage, the key details are that bees drink nectar, pollen sticks to them, and they carry pollen to other flowers.

### How to find the main idea

1. Read the whole passage first.
2. Ask: "Who or what is it about?" That is the topic.
3. Ask: "What is the most important thing the author says?"
4. Check: do the other sentences explain that idea? If yes, you found it.

The main idea is often in the first or last sentence, but not always. A title can also give a clue.

### Say it in your own words

Try saying the main idea without looking back. Use your own words. If you can, you understood the passage.

@example
title: Finding the main idea
problem: Read: "Owls hunt at night. They have big eyes to see in the dark. Their soft feathers make no noise. Their ears hear tiny sounds." What is the main idea?
step: The topic is owls.
step: Each sentence tells how owls are good at hunting at night: big eyes, quiet feathers, good ears.
step: The main idea is "Owls have body parts that help them hunt at night."

@mistakes
- Picking one small detail as the main idea.
- Writing only the topic, like "owls", instead of a full sentence about it.
- Choosing a sentence that is true but not from the passage.

@practice mc
prompt: Read: "Dogs need care. They need food and fresh water each day. They need walks to stay strong. They need a warm place to sleep." What is the main idea?
choice*: Dogs need care to stay healthy.
choice: Dogs like to sleep.
choice: Water is wet.
choice: Walks are short.
hint: Ask what all the sentences are explaining about dogs.
solution: Food, water, walks and a bed are all kinds of care. The main idea is that dogs need care to stay healthy.

@practice ms
prompt: Read: "Rain is good for gardens. It gives plants water to drink. It washes dust off the leaves. It helps seeds grow." Which are key details?
choice*: Rain gives plants water to drink.
choice*: Rain washes dust off the leaves.
choice: Rain is good for gardens.
choice: Gardens are always green.
hint: The main idea is the big sentence. Key details are the small facts that explain it.
solution: Water to drink and washing leaves are details. "Rain is good for gardens" is the main idea, and "Gardens are always green" is not in the passage.

@practice num
prompt: Read: "Ants work together. Some ants find food. Some ants guard the nest. Some ants take care of baby ants." How many kinds of ant jobs does the passage list?
answer: 3
check: len(["find food", "guard the nest", "take care of baby ants"])
hint: Look at the sentences after the first one. Each one tells a job.
solution: The jobs are finding food, guarding the nest and taking care of baby ants. That is 3 jobs.

@practice self
prompt: Read this passage out loud to your grown-up at a steady pace. Then say the main idea. "Trees give us many things. They make shade on a hot day. They give us fruit to eat. Birds build nests in them." Say the main idea in your own sentence.
solution: A good main idea is "Trees give people and animals many good things." The details are shade, fruit and places for nests.
rubric: You read at a steady pace and stopped at each period.
rubric: Your main idea is a whole sentence.
rubric: Your main idea is about all three details, not only one.
hint: Count the things trees give in the passage, then think of one sentence that covers all of them.
