@lesson
title: Complete sentences and punctuation
summary: Write complete sentences that start with a capital letter and end with the right mark, and use commas in a list.
objective: Tell a complete sentence from a fragment.
objective: Use capital letters and end marks (period, question mark, exclamation mark).
objective: Use commas to separate items in a list.
reading: ReadWorks, free reading passages: read a short passage and notice the capital letters and end marks.

@article
A **complete sentence** tells a whole idea. It has a **who or what** (the subject) and a **what happened** (the action). "The dog ran." is a complete sentence. "The big brown dog" is not, because it does not say what the dog did. That is a **fragment**.

### Capital letters

Start every sentence with a capital letter. Also use a capital for names of people and places (Maya, Texas) and for the word I.

### End marks

- A **period** (.) ends a telling sentence: "I like pears."
- A **question mark** (?) ends an asking sentence: "Do you like pears?"
- An **exclamation mark** (!) ends a sentence with strong feeling: "I love pears!"

### Commas in a list

When you name three or more things, put a **comma** between them: "I packed an apple, a book, and a towel." The comma helps the reader see where one thing ends and the next begins.

### Check your sentence

Ask yourself four questions.
1. Does it start with a capital letter?
2. Who or what is it about?
3. What did that person or thing do?
4. Does it end with the right mark?

If you can say yes to all four, your sentence is complete. Reading your sentence out loud to your grown-up helps you hear if something is missing.

@example
title: Fixing a fragment
problem: Make a complete sentence from the fragment "ran down the hill".
step: The fragment says what happened, but not who did it. We need a who.
step: Add a who: "The girl ran down the hill".
step: Add a capital letter at the start and a period at the end: "The girl ran down the hill."

@mistakes
- Forgetting the capital letter at the start or the end mark at the end.
- Writing a fragment that is missing who or what happened.
- Leaving out the commas in a list of three or more things.

@practice num
prompt: Count the sentences. "I woke up. The sun was bright! Did you see it? We went outside." How many sentences are there?
answer: 4
check: len([s for s in "I woke up. The sun was bright! Did you see it? We went outside.".replace("!", ".").replace("?", ".").split(".") if s.strip()])
hint: Each sentence ends with one end mark. Count the end marks.
solution: There is a period after up, an exclamation mark after bright, a question mark after it and a period after outside. That is 4 sentences.
