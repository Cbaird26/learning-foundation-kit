@lesson
title: Writing a paragraph
summary: Write a paragraph with a topic sentence, three details and an ending sentence.
objective: Write a topic sentence that tells the main idea.
objective: Add three details that stay on topic.
objective: Finish with an ending sentence.
reading: ReadWorks, free reading passages: find a one-paragraph passage and point to its topic sentence and ending.

@article
A **paragraph** is a group of sentences about one idea. The first line is pushed in a little, or you start on a new line. Think of a paragraph like a sandwich.

### The parts

- **Topic sentence (top bun):** tells the main idea. "My cat Milo is a good friend."
- **Details (the filling):** three sentences with facts or examples that explain the main idea. "He purrs when I am sad. He plays ball with me. He sleeps at the end of my bed."
- **Ending sentence (bottom bun):** wraps up the idea. "I am glad Milo is my cat."

### Staying on topic

Every detail must be about the topic. If you are writing about Milo being a good friend, do not add "The grocery store sells fish." That does not belong.

### Make it better

Use time words like first, next and also. Use describing words, like soft or tiny. Ask yourself: "Does every sentence help my main idea?"

### Plan before you write

Make a quick plan with four boxes. Write the topic in the first box, then a detail in each of the next three, then write the ending. Then turn each box into a sentence. After writing, read it to your grown-up. Fix any missing capital letters or end marks.

@example
title: A paragraph plan
problem: Plan a paragraph about "Why I like recess".
step: Topic sentence: "Recess is my favorite part of school."
step: Details: I play tag with my friends. I swing high on the swings. I get fresh air.
step: Ending: "That is why I love recess."

@mistakes
- Writing a topic sentence that is too small, like "I have a dog", when the paragraph is about many things.
- Adding a detail that is not about the topic.
- Stopping without an ending sentence.

@practice mc
prompt: Which sentence is the best topic sentence for a paragraph about the beach?
choice*: The beach is a fun place to visit.
choice: I have a red towel.
choice: Sand is yellow.
choice: We went home.
hint: A topic sentence tells the big idea that all the details can explain.
solution: "The beach is a fun place to visit" is a big idea. Details can tell what is fun there.

solution: The pizza shop has nothing to do with the dog, so it does not belong.

@practice num
prompt: A paragraph has the topic sentence "My cat is a good friend." Here are four more sentences. 1: She purrs when I am sad. 2: The mailman wears a blue hat. 3: She plays ball with me. 4: She sleeps on my bed. How many of the four sentences are details that belong in the paragraph?
answer: 3
check: 4 - 1
hint: A detail must be about the cat being a good friend. Check each sentence.
hint: Sentence 2 is about the mailman, not the cat.
solution: Sentences 1, 3 and 4 are about the cat being a good friend. Sentence 2 is about the mailman, so it does not belong. That makes 3 details.

solution: Example: Pizza is my favorite food. It has hot, melty cheese. I like the crunchy crust. I can eat it with my family. I could eat pizza every day.
rubric: The first sentence tells the main idea.
rubric: There are three details about the food.
rubric: There is an ending sentence.
rubric: Sentences start with capital letters and end with the right mark.
hint: Write the food in your topic sentence first, then think of how it tastes, looks and when you eat it for the details.
