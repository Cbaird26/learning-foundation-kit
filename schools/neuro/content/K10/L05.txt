@article
A **bug** is a mistake in a set of instructions. The instructions do not do what we wanted.

Finding and fixing bugs is called **debugging**. Every programmer, even a grown-up expert, makes bugs. Fixing them is a normal part of the job.

### Three steps to debug

1. **Run** the instructions, one step at a time, just as the computer would.
2. **Find** the step where it goes wrong.
3. **Fix** that step. Then run the instructions again to check.

### An example

Say we want the robot to go from A1 to C3. Here are the instructions:

```text
→ → ↑
```

Run them. The robot goes to B1, then C1, then C2. It stops on C2. But we wanted C3! The bug is that one ↑ is missing. We fix it by adding another ↑:

```text
→ → ↑ ↑
```

Run it again. The robot goes B1, C1, C2, C3. It works!

### Bugs in everyday steps

Bugs show up in ordinary steps too. Look at these steps for getting dressed: put on shoes, put on socks. The order is wrong. The fix is to swap them.

Grown-up helper: make a bug on purpose in a set of steps and ask your child to find it. Children love catching a grown-up's mistake!

@example
title: Fixing a bug
problem: The robot starts on A1 and should end on B3. The instructions are → ↑ ↑ ↑. Find the bug and fix it.
step: Run the steps. → goes to B1. Then ↑ goes to B2, B3 and B4.
step: The robot ended on B4, not B3. It went up one square too many.
step: Fix it by taking away one ↑. The new instructions are → ↑ ↑.
step: Run again: B1, B2, B3. It works!
answer: Take away one ↑.

@mistakes
- Guessing where the bug is without running the steps. Run them one at a time.
- Fixing the wrong step, so the instructions still do not work. Always run again after a fix.
- Thinking a bug means you are bad at this. Bugs are normal, and finding them is a skill.

@practice text
prompt: The robot starts on A1 and should end on C3. The instructions are → → ↑. Which square does it really end on? Type the square name.

```text
4 . . . .
3 . . . .
2 . . . .
1 R . . .
  A B C D
```
answer: C2
accept: c2
check: run("d={'R':(1,0),'L':(-1,0),'U':(0,1),'D':(0,-1)}\nx,y=0,1\nfor m in 'RRU':\n    x+=d[m][0]\n    y+=d[m][1]\nprint('ABCD'[x]+str(y))\n")
hint: Do the arrows one at a time. Two → go right, and one ↑ goes up.
solution: The robot goes B1, then C1, then up to C2. It ends on C2, one square short of C3.
