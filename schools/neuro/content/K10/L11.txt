@article
A computer is a machine that follows instructions. A list of instructions for a computer is called a **program**.

### Exactly, one at a time

A computer does what the program says. It does not do what you **meant**. If the program has a bug, the computer follows the bug, step by step.

The computer does one step, then the next step. It is very, very fast. It never gets bored or tired. But it never guesses, either.

### Input and output

A computer needs ways to get information in and ways to show it back to us.

- **Input** is information going **into** the computer. A keyboard, a mouse and a microphone are for input.
- **Output** is information coming **out** of the computer. A screen and a speaker are for output.

A program often works like this: input comes in, the computer follows the steps, and output goes out.

### A tiny program

Here is a tiny program:

```text
1. Start with 0.
2. Add 3.
3. Add 4.
4. Say the number.
```

Follow it like a computer. Start with 0. Add 3 and you have 3. Add 4 and you have 7. Say the number: **7**.

### Memory

A computer also has **memory**, where it keeps the program and the numbers it works with. It remembers what you stored, as long as it has power.

Grown-up helper: play "human computer". Give your child a list of steps on paper. Your child must follow the steps exactly and say the answer out loud.

@example
title: Being the computer
problem: Follow this program. What does the computer say at the end?

```text
1. Start with 10.
2. Add 5.
3. Take away 3.
4. Say the number.
```
step: Step 1: start with 10.
step: Step 2: add 5. Now the number is 15.
step: Step 3: take away 3. Now the number is 12.
step: Step 4: say the number. The computer says 12.
answer: 12

@mistakes
- Thinking the computer will guess what you meant. It only does what the steps say.
- Skipping a step, or doing the steps in a different order.
- Mixing up input and output. Input goes in, and output comes out.

@practice self
prompt: Play "human computer". Ask your grown-up to write a list of four steps for drawing a smiley face, such as "draw a circle" and "draw two dots". Follow the steps exactly as written, even if you can guess what is missing. What does your drawing look like? Where do you need better steps?
hint: Take it one step at a time, and ask your grown-up to check each part with you.
solution: If the steps say "draw two dots" but not where, the computer may put them anywhere. A good fix is to write exact steps, such as "draw two dots near the top of the circle". Computers need exact steps, so people must be careful when they write them.
rubric: The child followed the steps exactly and did not add anything that was missing.
rubric: The child can say one step that was not exact enough.
rubric: The child or grown-up wrote a better, more exact step.
