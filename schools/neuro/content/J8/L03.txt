@article
### Egypt: the gift of the Nile

Egypt lies in a desert, but the Nile floods each year and leaves rich soil. Egyptians organized their calendar and farming around this flood. About 3100 BCE Upper and Lower Egypt were united into one kingdom. The ruler, called the **pharaoh**, was seen as a divine king who kept order (called *ma'at*) in the world. Officials called **scribes** kept records and collected taxes. Egyptians wrote in **hieroglyphs** and wrote on **papyrus**, a paper-like material made from a river plant. They built **pyramids** as royal tombs, especially in the Old Kingdom (about 2700 to 2200 BCE), and practiced **mummification** because they believed in an afterlife.

### The Indus Valley

The Indus Valley civilization (about 2600 to 1900 BCE) had cities such as Harappa and Mohenjo-daro. Archaeologists found streets laid out in grids, standardized fired bricks, wells, and drains. Its seals carry symbols, but the **script has not been deciphered**, so historians do not know how it was governed or what its people believed. Why the cities declined is debated; changes in rivers and climate are among the proposed explanations. Later, in India, the **Maurya Empire** (about 321 to 185 BCE) united much of the subcontinent, and the **Gupta period** (about 320 to 550 CE) is known for advances in mathematics, including the use of zero as a number in the decimal place-value system.

### China: from dynasties to empire

Chinese history is often described as a series of **dynasties**, ruling families. The **Shang** (about 1600 to 1046 BCE) left writing on animal bones and turtle shells ("oracle bones") and bronze vessels. The **Zhou** claimed the **Mandate of Heaven**: the idea that a ruler governs rightly only while he rules justly. In 221 BCE the **Qin** ruler unified China under a strong central government, standardized writing, weights and measures. The **Han dynasty** (206 BCE to 220 CE) kept the empire, built a government staffed by educated officials, and expanded trade on the **Silk Road**. Chinese inventions include silk cloth, paper (dated by tradition to about 105 CE), the compass and, later, printing and gunpowder.

### Comparing civilizations

When you compare two civilizations, use the same questions for each:

| Question | Egypt | China (Han) |
|---|---|---|
| Who rules? | A pharaoh, seen as divine | An emperor, whose rule depended on the Mandate of Heaven |
| Who runs the government? | Scribes and officials | Educated officials in a large bureaucracy |
| Key writing | Hieroglyphs on papyrus | Characters on bamboo, silk and later paper |
| Famous works or inventions | Pyramids, mummification | Paper, silk, Great Wall construction |

Comparing is not ranking. Look for similarities (both used officials) and differences (the source of the ruler's authority).

title: Comparing two governments
problem: Write two sentences comparing how the rulers of ancient Egypt and Han China justified their authority.
step: First name each idea. Egyptian pharaohs were seen as divine kings who upheld ma'at. Han emperors were said to rule with the Mandate of Heaven.
step: Find the similarity: in both, rulers claimed a link to the sacred or the heavens.
step: Find the difference: the pharaoh was considered divine, while the Mandate of Heaven said rule could be lost if a ruler governed badly.
step: Combine: "Both Egyptian and Chinese rulers linked their power to the sacred. However, a pharaoh was seen as divine, while a Chinese emperor was said to hold a mandate that could be lost through unjust rule."

@mistakes
- Saying the Indus script is understood; it has not been deciphered.
- Treating Egypt, India and China as if they existed at exactly the same time or in the same way.
- Listing facts about two civilizations side by side without actually comparing them.
- Presenting a tradition (such as the date of paper's invention) as a precise, certain fact.
