### Greece: many city-states

Greece is mountainous and surrounded by sea, so communities developed as independent **city-states**, each called a **polis**. Greeks shared a language, religion and festivals such as the Olympic Games, but city-states often competed and fought.

**Athens** developed a form of **direct democracy** in the 500s BCE: eligible citizens voted in an assembly on laws and policies, and many officials were chosen by lot. But citizenship was limited to free adult men born to Athenian parents. Women, enslaved people and foreign-born residents could not vote. **Sparta**, in contrast, was an oligarchy (rule by a few) that trained its male citizens as soldiers and relied on a large enslaved population, the helots, to farm.

The Greek city-states joined to resist Persian invasions (the **Persian Wars**, 490 to 479 BCE). Later, Athens and Sparta fought the **Peloponnesian War** (431 to 404 BCE), which weakened Greece. Greek achievements include theater, philosophy, history writing, and geometry. Later, Alexander of Macedon's conquests spread Greek culture across Southwest Asia and Egypt, in what is called the **Hellenistic** period.

### Rome: from republic to empire

According to Roman tradition, the **Roman Republic** began in 509 BCE, when the Romans ended rule by kings. In a **republic**, leaders are chosen by citizens or their representatives rather than inheriting power. Rome's government had three parts. Two **consuls** were elected each year to lead the government and army. The **Senate**, made up of powerful men, advised and made decisions. **Tribunes** protected the interests of ordinary citizens (plebeians). Roman law was posted publicly in the **Twelve Tables** (about 450 BCE).

Rome conquered Italy and then fought Carthage in the **Punic Wars** (264 to 146 BCE), winning control of the western Mediterranean. Wealth and conquest, along with conflict over land and power, led to civil wars. In 27 BCE **Augustus** became the first emperor, beginning the **Roman Empire**. The empire's roads, aqueducts, arches, domes and concrete connected and supplied its cities. In 476 CE the last emperor of the **Western** Roman Empire was removed; the **Eastern** Roman (Byzantine) Empire lasted until 1453.

### Comparing governments

| Feature | Athens | Roman Republic |
|---|---|---|
| Who made decisions? | Citizens voting directly | Elected officials and the Senate |
| Who was a citizen? | Free adult men of Athenian birth | Free men of Roman status (citizenship expanded over time) |
| Excluded | Women, enslaved people, foreigners | Women, enslaved people, and many conquered peoples for a long time |
| Legacy | Democracy, jury trials | Republic, written law, representation |

Both societies relied on slavery, and both limited political rights to a minority of the population.

@mistakes
- Saying Athenian democracy included everyone; most residents could not vote.
- Calling Rome a democracy; it was a republic that became an empire.
- Mixing up the fall of Rome: the Western Empire ended in 476 CE, but the Eastern Empire continued for nearly a thousand years more.
- Treating Athens and Sparta as the same kind of society.
