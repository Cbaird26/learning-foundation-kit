@article
Substance use disorders (SUDs) are chronic, relapsing conditions of the brain's reward and self-control circuits. Drugs of misuse increase dopamine in the nucleus accumbens (mesolimbic reward pathway). Repeated use produces tolerance, craving and withdrawal, and weakens prefrontal control. They are medical illnesses; use person-first language such as "a person with opioid use disorder," and avoid stigmatizing labels.

### Diagnosis

DSM-5-TR uses one diagnosis for each substance, based on **11 criteria** in 12 months: taking more or for longer than intended; unsuccessful efforts to cut down; much time spent obtaining, using or recovering; **craving**; failure to meet role obligations; continued use despite social or interpersonal problems; giving up activities; use in hazardous situations; continued use despite physical or psychological harm; **tolerance**; **withdrawal**. Severity: **2 to 3 criteria mild, 4 to 5 moderate, 6 or more severe**. Tolerance and withdrawal do not count when a medication is taken under appropriate medical supervision.

**Screening:** SBIRT (screening, brief intervention, referral to treatment). **AUDIT-C** has three items scored 0 to 4 (total 0 to 12); a positive screen is 4 or more in men and 3 or more in women. CAGE (cut down, annoyed, guilty, eye-opener) is positive at 2 or more. CRAFFT is used in adolescents.

### Alcohol

Intoxication: disinhibition, slurred speech, ataxia, nystagmus. **Withdrawal** follows reduction in a dependent drinker: tremor and anxiety (6 to 24 hours), **seizures** (12 to 48 hours), alcoholic hallucinosis (12 to 48 hours, with clear sensorium), and **delirium tremens** (48 to 96 hours: confusion, autonomic hyperactivity, fever, hallucinations; a medical emergency with substantial mortality if untreated). Treat with **benzodiazepines**, ideally symptom-triggered guided by the CIWA-Ar score (lorazepam or oxazepam in liver disease). Give **thiamine before or with glucose** to prevent **Wernicke encephalopathy** (confusion, ophthalmoplegia, ataxia), which can progress to **Korsakoff syndrome** (anterograde amnesia with confabulation). Treat electrolytes, particularly magnesium and potassium.

Medications for alcohol use disorder: **naltrexone** (opioid receptor antagonist; reduces craving and heavy drinking; contraindicated with opioid use or acute hepatitis), **acamprosate** (modulates glutamate, renally cleared; supports abstinence), **disulfiram** (inhibits aldehyde dehydrogenase, causing flushing, nausea and tachycardia with alcohol; requires motivation). Gabapentin and topiramate are used off label.

### Opioids

Intoxication: **miosis, respiratory depression, decreased consciousness**. Treat with **naloxone** (short-acting; repeat dosing may be needed because many opioids outlast it). Withdrawal is unpleasant but rarely life threatening: mydriasis, rhinorrhea, lacrimation, yawning, piloerection, diarrhea, myalgias. **Medications for opioid use disorder** reduce mortality and are first-line: **methadone** (full agonist, dispensed through certified programs, QT prolongation), **buprenorphine** (high-affinity partial agonist; start when the person is already in mild to moderate withdrawal to avoid precipitated withdrawal; ceiling effect on respiratory depression), and **extended-release naltrexone** (requires 7 to 10 days opioid-free). Offer naloxone to all patients at risk.

### Stimulants, cannabis, sedatives, nicotine

**Cocaine and amphetamines:** tachycardia, hypertension, mydriasis, agitation, paranoia, hyperthermia, chest pain; "crash" with hypersomnia, depression and hunger. Treat agitation with benzodiazepines; there is no approved medication for stimulant use disorder, and **contingency management** is the best-supported behavioral treatment. **Cannabis:** conjunctival injection, increased appetite, dry mouth, impaired coordination; cannabinoid hyperemesis with chronic use. **Benzodiazepine and barbiturate withdrawal** can cause seizures and requires a gradual taper. **Phencyclidine** causes agitation, nystagmus and violent behavior. **Tobacco:** combination nicotine replacement, varenicline, and bupropion double or triple quit rates.

### Substance use with psychosis: comparing antipsychotics

Patients with SUD and psychosis often take antipsychotics. Compare key side effects: **olanzapine and clozapine** cause the most weight gain and metabolic syndrome; **risperidone, paliperidone and haloperidol** cause the most hyperprolactinemia and extrapyramidal symptoms; **ziprasidone and haloperidol** prolong the QT most; **aripiprazole and lurasidone** are relatively weight-neutral; **clozapine** alone causes agranulocytosis and sialorrhea; low-potency agents (chlorpromazine) are sedating, anticholinergic and cause orthostasis.

@example
title: Grading severity from the criteria
problem: A 38-year-old man drinks more than he intends, has tried twice to cut down, craves alcohol, has missed work because of drinking, and continues to drink despite his physician telling him alcohol caused his gastritis. He also needs more alcohol for the same effect. How many criteria are met and what is the severity?
step: List: use in larger amounts than intended (1), unsuccessful efforts to cut down (2), craving (3), failure to fulfill work obligations (4), continued use despite physical harm (5), tolerance (6).
step: Six criteria are present.
step: Six or more criteria is severe alcohol use disorder.
step: Initial management includes assessing withdrawal risk and offering naltrexone or acamprosate with counseling.

@mistakes
- Giving glucose before thiamine in a person at risk for Wernicke encephalopathy.
- Starting buprenorphine in someone still using full opioids and not yet in withdrawal, which precipitates withdrawal.
- Giving naltrexone to someone using opioids, or expecting naloxone to last as long as the opioid.
- Counting tolerance and withdrawal toward the diagnosis when the medication is prescribed and supervised.

choice: Disulfiram

solution: Delirium tremens is treated with benzodiazepines and thiamine, with supportive care. Antipsychotics alone lower seizure threshold and do not treat the underlying withdrawal.
