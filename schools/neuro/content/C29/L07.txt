@article
A **personality disorder** is an enduring pattern of inner experience and behavior that deviates markedly from cultural expectations, is **pervasive and inflexible** across personal and social situations, begins by **adolescence or early adulthood**, is stable over time, and causes distress or impairment. It is not better explained by another mental disorder, substance use or a medical condition. Traits are often **ego-syntonic**: the person experiences them as part of who they are, which is why many people seek care only for depression, anxiety or substance use, or because of a crisis. Personality disorders raise the risk of mood disorders, substance use, and suicide. DSM-5-TR retains the categorical system and lists an alternative dimensional model (impairment in self and interpersonal functioning plus pathological trait domains) in Section III; ICD-11 is dimensional.

### Cluster A: odd and eccentric

- **Paranoid:** pervasive distrust and suspicion that others' motives are malevolent; no psychosis.
- **Schizoid:** detachment from social relationships and restricted range of emotional expression; prefers solitary activities, little desire for relationships.
- **Schizotypal:** acute discomfort in close relationships with cognitive or perceptual distortions and eccentric behavior: ideas of reference, magical thinking, odd beliefs, unusual perceptual experiences; part of the schizophrenia spectrum with increased family risk.

### Cluster B: dramatic, emotional, erratic

- **Antisocial:** pervasive disregard for the rights of others since age 15; the person is at least **18** years old and had **conduct disorder before age 15**. Deceitfulness, impulsivity, irritability, recklessness, lack of remorse.
- **Borderline:** instability of relationships, self-image and affect, and marked impulsivity, beginning by early adulthood. Features: frantic efforts to avoid abandonment; unstable intense relationships alternating between idealization and devaluation (**splitting**); identity disturbance; impulsivity in at least two potentially harmful areas; recurrent suicidal behavior or self-harm; affective instability; chronic emptiness; inappropriate intense anger; transient stress-related paranoia or dissociation.
- **Histrionic:** excessive emotionality and attention seeking, inappropriate seductiveness, shifting shallow emotions.
- **Narcissistic:** grandiosity, need for admiration, lack of empathy, entitlement, fragile self-esteem.

### Cluster C: anxious and fearful

- **Avoidant:** social inhibition, feelings of inadequacy and hypersensitivity to criticism, with desire for relationships (contrast schizoid).
- **Dependent:** pervasive need to be taken care of, submissive and clinging, difficulty making decisions.
- **Obsessive-compulsive personality disorder (OCPD):** preoccupation with orderliness, perfectionism and control at the expense of flexibility. It is ego-syntonic and has no true obsessions or compulsions, unlike OCD.

### Defense mechanisms

Defense mechanisms are unconscious strategies that manage conflict and anxiety.
- **Immature:** **splitting** (seeing people as all good or all bad), **projection** (attributing one's own unacceptable feelings to others), **projective identification**, **denial**, **acting out**, **regression**, **passive aggression**.
- **Neurotic:** **repression**, **displacement**, **reaction formation**, **intellectualization**, **rationalization**, **isolation of affect**, **undoing**.
- **Mature:** **sublimation** (channeling impulses into acceptable activity), **altruism**, **humor**, **suppression** (conscious), **anticipation**.

### Treatment

**Psychotherapy is the mainstay.** For borderline personality disorder, **dialectical behavior therapy (DBT)** — individual therapy plus skills training in mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation and interpersonal effectiveness — reduces self-harm and hospitalization. Mentalization-based therapy and transference-focused psychotherapy are also effective. Medications treat target symptoms or comorbid depression, anxiety or psychosis, but no drug is approved for borderline personality disorder; avoid polypharmacy and benzodiazepines because of misuse risk. Maintain a consistent, boundaried therapeutic approach: set limits, validate emotions, and watch for splitting among team members. Patients with personality disorders can have suicidal behavior; assess risk at each visit (Lesson 10).

### Likelihood ratios

A positive likelihood ratio for a screening instrument is $LR+ = \frac{\text{sensitivity}}{1 - \text{specificity}}$. Higher values shift post-test probability more strongly toward the disorder.

@example
title: Cluster identification by vignette
problem: A 29-year-old woman has a history of intense, short-lived relationships in which she idealizes partners and then abruptly condemns them. She cuts her arms when she fears being left, describes constant emptiness, and has had episodes of angry outbursts since her late teens. Which disorder and which cluster fit, and what is first-line management?
step: Instability of relationships (idealization-devaluation), fear of abandonment, self-harm, emptiness and intense anger since adolescence define borderline personality disorder.
step: It belongs to cluster B (dramatic, emotional, erratic).
step: First-line treatment is psychotherapy, especially DBT. Medication addresses comorbidity only.
step: Assess suicide and self-harm risk, develop a safety plan and engage supports.

@mistakes
- Diagnosing a personality disorder during an acute mood or psychotic episode without evidence of an enduring pattern since early adulthood.
- Confusing schizoid (little desire for relationships) with avoidant (desires relationships but fears rejection).
- Confusing OCD with OCPD: OCD has obsessions and compulsions that cause distress; OCPD is ego-syntonic.
- Relying on medication alone for borderline personality disorder or giving benzodiazepines.

@practice num
prompt: A screening questionnaire for borderline personality disorder has a sensitivity of 0.80 and specificity of 0.90. What is the positive likelihood ratio?
answer: 8
check: 0.80 / (1 - 0.90)
tol: 0.05
hint: LR+ = sensitivity / (1 - specificity).
solution: LR+ = 0.80 / 0.10 = 8.

prompt: Describe the three personality disorder clusters. For each cluster, list its disorders, one defining feature of each, and one pair of disorders that are commonly confused with each other and how to tell them apart.
hint: Organize as odd/eccentric, dramatic/emotional, anxious/fearful.
solution: Cluster A (odd, eccentric): paranoid (distrust), schizoid (detached, restricted affect, no desire for closeness), schizotypal (eccentric, magical thinking, ideas of reference); schizoid versus schizotypal is distinguished by cognitive and perceptual distortions. Cluster B (dramatic): antisocial (disregard for others' rights, conduct disorder before 15), borderline (unstable relationships, self-image and affect, fear of abandonment), histrionic (attention seeking), narcissistic (grandiosity, lack of empathy); histrionic versus borderline is distinguished by the self-harm, identity disturbance and intense anger in borderline. Cluster C (anxious): avoidant (fears rejection but desires relationships), dependent (needs to be cared for), obsessive-compulsive (perfectionism, rigid control); OCD versus OCPD is distinguished by distress and true obsessions and compulsions in OCD.

rubric: Gives an accurate defining feature for each disorder.
