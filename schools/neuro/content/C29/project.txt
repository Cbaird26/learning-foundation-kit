@project
title: Psychiatric interview and plan
overview: You will read three short interview transcripts, write a mental status examination for each, reach a diagnosis with reasoning, and write a safety-focused treatment plan. The cases are invented and written out below.
goal: Describe a patient's presentation in precise mental status examination language, separating mood from affect and process from content.
goal: Apply DSM-5-TR criteria to reach a primary diagnosis with a differential and needed workup.
goal: Formulate suicide risk and write a collaborative safety plan and a treatment plan with monitoring.
step: Read Transcript A. A 34-year-old woman, tearful, says: "I have felt empty for two months. I cannot enjoy anything, even my daughter. I wake at 4 every morning. I have lost 12 pounds. Sometimes I think it would be better if I did not wake up, but I have no plan and I would never leave my daughter." She is oriented, attends to serial sevens, and says "I know I need help." No past manic episodes. TSH normal.
step: Read Transcript B. A 23-year-old man, brought by his roommate, talks rapidly and cannot be interrupted: "I have not slept in five days and I do not need to. I am designing a machine that will fix the economy; the president will want to hear it. I spent my rent money on equipment, but it is an investment." He jumps between topics linked by rhymes. Denies voices. He has been unable to work for a week, and is irritable when questioned. He is oriented but distractible. Urine drug screen is negative.
step: Read Transcript C. A 28-year-old woman is calm, but guarded: "People at work are watching me. I hear them whispering about me on the floor and in my apartment. A voice tells me what I am doing, 'she is going to the kitchen.' It has been like this for seven months. I stopped going to work four months ago. My mood is fine." She is oriented and denies thoughts of harming herself or others. Her drug screen is negative and her TSH is normal.
step: For each transcript, write a complete MSE with the ten domains (appearance and behavior may be inferred from the description; say so where data are missing).
step: For each, give the most likely diagnosis with the DSM-5-TR criteria met, two differential diagnoses, and the medical workup you would obtain.
step: For each, state suicide and violence risk with risk and protective factors, then write a safety plan outline (warning signs, coping strategies, contacts, professionals including the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, and making the environment safer).
step: For each, give a treatment plan: first-line medication or psychotherapy, monitoring of serious adverse effects, and follow-up timing.
step: Review your work against the rubric, then compare your diagnoses and plans with a peer or tutor.
deliverable: Three MSEs, one for each transcript, using precise terms.
deliverable: A table of diagnosis, criteria met, differential and workup for each case.
deliverable: A risk formulation, safety plan, and treatment plan for each case.
rubric: Mood is quoted and affect is described separately; thought process and thought content are not confused.
rubric: Diagnoses cite specific DSM-5-TR criteria and duration thresholds and exclude substance and medical causes.
rubric: Risk formulation names factors, a reasoned level and a disposition; the safety plan has all six parts and includes 988.
rubric: Treatment choices are first-line and include monitoring for serious adverse effects.
safety: This project is an educational exercise with invented cases. If you or someone you know is thinking of suicide or in crisis, call or text 988 (the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline in the United States) or your local emergency number.
tutor: Check my MSE for Transcript B: what is the thought process and how is it different from the content?
tutor: Why is Transcript C not schizophreniform disorder, and what duration changes the label?
tutor: What are the monitoring needs for the medication I chose in Transcript A or C?

@review
**Week 1:** History domains, collateral, medical mimics. The MSE: mood is the patient's words, affect is your observation; process versus content; delirium (acute, inattention) versus dementia. **Week 2:** MDD needs 5 of 9 symptoms for 2 weeks with depressed mood or anhedonia and no mania; persistent depressive disorder 2 years; PHQ-9 bands (5, 10, 15, 20); SSRIs first-line, 4 to 6 weeks, continue 6 to 12 months or more; ECT for severe or psychotic illness. **Week 3:** mania 1 week (or hospitalization, psychosis) versus hypomania 4 days; bipolar I, II, cyclothymia; lithium, valproate and antipsychotics for mania; avoid antidepressant monotherapy; lithium toxicity and its interactions. **Week 4:** GAD 6 months; panic disorder; PTSD more than 1 month with four clusters; OCD (ERP and SSRIs); avoid benzodiazepines in PTSD. **Week 5:** psychosis by duration (brief, schizophreniform, schizophrenia 6 months); dopamine pathways; EPS, NMS, metabolic and QT effects; clozapine for resistance. **Week 6:** 11 DSM criteria (2 to 3 mild, 4 to 5 moderate, 6 or more severe); alcohol withdrawal and thiamine; opioid agonist therapy; naloxone; antipsychotic side-effect comparisons. **Week 7:** personality disorder clusters A, B, C; defense mechanisms; DBT. **Week 8:** anorexia severity by BMI, refeeding hypophosphatemia, bulimia treatment (avoid bupropion), CBT-I and sleep disorders. **Week 9:** ADHD (6 symptoms, before age 12, two settings), autism, Tourette, DMDD, mandated reporting. **Week 10:** risk and protective factors, safety plan, 988, serotonin syndrome versus NMS, capacity. **Week 11:** receptor profiles, washouts, TCA overdose, benzodiazepines. **Week 12:** CBT, IPT, DBT, conditioning schedules, stages of change, MI.

**Self-check:** Can you write a one-paragraph MSE from a transcript? Distinguish mania from hypomania and name three first-line treatments? Explain why duration separates psychotic disorders? List the six parts of a safety plan? Match 12 drugs to a use and a serious effect?

**Common errors:** confusing mood and affect; diagnosing bipolar disorder as depression and starting an antidepressant alone; ignoring duration thresholds; giving glucose before thiamine; starting buprenorphine too early; missing NMS or serotonin syndrome; relying on a promise instead of a risk assessment; omitting means safety; skipping baseline metabolic monitoring.

choice: Lithium is safe with NSAIDs and ACE inhibitors because it is metabolized by the liver.
choice*: NSAIDs and ACE inhibitors raise lithium levels; monitor levels, renal function, and watch for tremor, vomiting and ataxia
choice: Lithium requires monitoring of absolute neutrophil count
choice: Lithium should be stopped if he gets a rash
hint: Lithium is cleared by the kidneys.
solution: Lithium is renally cleared; NSAIDs and ACE inhibitors reduce clearance and can cause toxicity. Neutrophil monitoring is for clozapine and rash concern is for lamotrigine.

@exam self
prompt: A 31-year-old man with schizophrenia taking a long-acting injectable antipsychotic is brought to the emergency department after saying he has "no reason to go on." He has a history of an earlier attempt, has been drinking more, and says he has a plan and intent. Describe your risk assessment, the factors that raise and lower risk, the disposition, and the medical and pharmacologic issues for his care.
hint: Use the structure: screen, characterize, risk and protective factors, formulate, disposition, safety plan, treatment.
solution: Screen directly with the C-SSRS; characterize ideation, plan, intent and preparatory behavior; ask about means access. Risk factors: schizophrenia, prior attempt, alcohol use, hopelessness, plan and intent. Protective factors depend on supports, treatment engagement and reasons for living. Formulate acute risk as high given plan and intent; chronic risk is elevated. Disposition: psychiatric admission (voluntary if possible, otherwise involuntary under state law), constant observation. Evaluate for intoxication and withdrawal and treat as needed (benzodiazepines, thiamine). Review adherence, consider clozapine, which reduces suicide risk in schizophrenia (ANC monitoring remains recommended per labeling and practice), and assess metabolic and QT monitoring. Prepare a safety plan before discharge: warning signs, coping, contacts, professionals including 988, and means safety, with follow-up within days.
rubric: Performs structured assessment with plan, intent, means, risk and protective factors, and a reasoned acute and chronic risk level.
rubric: Chooses an appropriate high-risk disposition and addresses alcohol use.
rubric: Mentions treatment optimization including clozapine and a safety plan with 988 and means safety.
