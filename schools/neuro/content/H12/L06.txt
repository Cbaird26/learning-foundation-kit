@article
The 1920s brought rapid economic growth, mass-produced goods and a stock market boom, but wealth was unevenly shared and many farmers were struggling. Stock prices collapsed in October 1929. The crash did not alone cause the Depression. Historians point to the combination of bank failures, a shrinking money supply, falling demand, high debts, weak farm income and international trade problems.

### The human toll

By 1933 about a quarter of workers were unemployed, thousands of banks had closed and many families lost savings. Black Americans, Mexican Americans and other minorities often faced the worst job losses and discrimination in relief. In the Plains, drought and poor farming practices created the Dust Bowl, pushing many families to migrate. Many people turned to charity, local relief and shantytowns. President Hoover favored voluntary cooperation and limited federal action, such as loans to banks and businesses; critics said this was too little, and supporters said it avoided harmful intervention.

### The New Deal

In 1932 voters elected Franklin D. Roosevelt, who promised a "new deal." Historians group its programs into the "three Rs."

**Relief** (immediate help):
- The Civilian Conservation Corps and the Works Progress Administration gave jobs on public projects.
- The Federal Emergency Relief Administration provided aid to states.

**Recovery** (restoring the economy):
- The Agricultural Adjustment Act paid farmers to cut production in order to raise prices.
- The National Industrial Recovery Act set up industry codes; the Supreme Court struck it down in 1935.
- The Tennessee Valley Authority built dams and provided electricity.

**Reform** (preventing future crises):
- The **Banking Act of 1933** created the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, which insures bank deposits.
- The **Securities and Exchange Commission** (1934) regulates stock markets.
- The **Wagner Act** (1935) protected workers' rights to organize and bargain collectively.
- The **Social Security Act** (1935) created retirement benefits for many workers, unemployment insurance and aid to some mothers and children. Agricultural and domestic workers, many of them Black or Mexican American, were initially excluded.
- The **Fair Labor Standards Act** (1938) set a minimum wage and a maximum workweek and limited child labor.

### Food and drug regulation

In 1937 a liquid medicine made with a poisonous solvent killed more than a hundred people, many of them children. Because the 1906 law did not require safety testing, public pressure rose for a stronger law. In 1938 Congress passed the **Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act**, which required manufacturers to show that new drugs were safe before marketing them, extended regulation to cosmetics and medical devices, and set standards for food. This extended the 1906 laws from preventing false labels to requiring premarket safety review.

### The Supreme Court and the limits of federal power

Courts struck down several early New Deal laws as exceeding the federal commerce power. In 1937 the president proposed enlarging the Court. Congress rejected the plan, and the Court soon upheld key laws such as the Wagner Act and Social Security.

### Judging the New Deal

Historians and citizens disagree. Supporters say it provided relief, stabilized banks and established protections such as deposit insurance and Social Security. Critics from one side argued that it expanded federal power and debt and slowed recovery; critics from another side argued that it did too little for the poorest and left racial inequality in place. Unemployment remained high until the economic mobilization for World War II. Many New Deal institutions remain, and Americans still debate the proper size and role of the federal government.

@example
title: Sorting programs into relief, recovery and reform
problem: Classify (a) the Civilian Conservation Corps, (b) the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation and (c) the Agricultural Adjustment Act as relief, recovery or reform.
step: The Civilian Conservation Corps gave young men paid work on conservation projects, a direct and immediate help to the unemployed. That is relief.
step: The FDIC insures bank deposits so that bank failures will not wipe out savings and cause panics. It addresses a structural weakness. That is reform.
step: The Agricultural Adjustment Act paid farmers to reduce output and raise farm prices, aiming at economic restoration. That is recovery.
step: Many programs fit more than one category, so state your reasoning rather than only the label.

@mistakes
- Saying the stock market crash alone caused the Depression.
- Treating the New Deal as a single plan; programs were created over several years and some were ended by the Supreme Court.
- Presenting the New Deal as universally praised or condemned; opinions have always differed.
