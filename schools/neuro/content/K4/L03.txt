@article
Reading starts with listening. In this lesson you only use your ears.

### Rhymes

Words **rhyme** when they end with the same sound. Cat, hat and mat rhyme. They all end in "-at". Rhyming words can start with different letters.

Here is a little rhyme to say with your grown-up:

The cat sat on a mat. The cat wore a hat.

Cat, sat, mat and hat all rhyme. Dog does not rhyme with cat, because it ends with a different sound.

### Syllables

A **syllable** is one beat in a word. You can clap each beat.

- cat: clap once (1 syllable)
- ta-ble: clap twice (2 syllables)
- ba-na-na: clap three times (3 syllables)

Every syllable has a vowel sound. A fun trick: put your hand under your chin and say the word. Your chin drops once for each syllable.

### Try it with your grown-up

Clap your names. Clap the names of the people in your family. Then clap the names of three foods, like "ap-ple" and "wa-ter-mel-on".

@example
title: Clap a word
problem: How many syllables are in "butterfly"?
step: Say the word slowly: but-ter-fly.
step: Clap once for each part: but (clap), ter (clap), fly (clap).
step: You clapped 3 times, so "butterfly" has 3 syllables.

@mistakes
- Thinking words rhyme because they start the same. Cat and cup start the same, but they do not rhyme.
- Clapping each letter instead of each beat. Clap the sound beats you hear.
- Saying the word too fast to hear the beats. Say it slowly.
