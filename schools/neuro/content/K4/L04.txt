@lesson
title: Blending sounds into words
summary: Push single sounds together to read three-sound words.
objective: Say the sounds in a short word one at a time.
objective: Blend three sounds into a word.
objective: Count the sounds in a word.
reading: Reading Rockets, "Phonics and decoding": look up the topic "blending" under decoding.

@article
To read a word, you say each sound, then **blend** the sounds together. Blending means gliding the sounds into one word.

### Note for the grown-up

Point to each letter and say its sound. Then slide your finger under the whole word and say it fast.

- c, a, t: /k/ /a/ /t/. Blend: cat.
- s, u, n: /s/ /u/ /n/. Blend: sun.
- m, a, p: /m/ /a/ /p/. Blend: map.

### Stretch it

Some sounds can be stretched, so the blend is easier: mmm-aaa-p. Start with those: m, s, f, n, l, r. Sounds like /t/, /p/ and /b/ are quick taps, so keep them short.

### Break it apart

You can also go the other way. Say a word slowly and count the sounds. The word "dog" has 3 sounds: /d/ /o/ /g/. Hold up one finger for each sound.

### Try it with your grown-up

Your grown-up says the sounds of a word one at a time, like /p/ /i/ /g/. You push the sounds together and say the word. Then switch jobs.

@example
title: Blend a word
problem: Blend the sounds /h/ /a/ /t/ into a word.
step: Say the first sound: /h/. Then the next sound: /a/. Say them together: ha.
step: Add the last sound: /t/. Glide it all together.
step: The word is "hat".

@mistakes
- Saying the sounds with extra "uh" sounds, like "huh-a-tuh". Keep the sounds clean and short.
- Stopping between sounds. Slide them together like a train.
- Guessing from the first letter only. Look at every letter.
