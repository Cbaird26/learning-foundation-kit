@article
**Dementia** is acquired, progressive decline in two or more cognitive domains that impairs independence, with preserved alertness. **Delirium** is an acute (hours to days), fluctuating disturbance of attention and awareness caused by a medical condition, drug or withdrawal. Delirium is a medical emergency and the two often coexist: dementia is the main risk factor for delirium.

### Delirium

The **Confusion Assessment Method (CAM)** requires acute onset with fluctuating course and inattention, plus either disorganized thinking or altered level of consciousness. Causes are remembered with a checklist: infection (urinary, pneumonia), withdrawal (alcohol, benzodiazepines), metabolic disturbance (sodium, calcium, glucose, uremia, hepatic), drugs (anticholinergics, benzodiazepines, opioids, steroids), hypoxia, stroke, urinary retention and constipation. Management is to **treat the cause**, review medications, and use nonpharmacologic measures (reorientation, glasses and hearing aids, sleep hygiene, early mobilization). Reserve low-dose antipsychotics (haloperidol) for dangerous agitation; avoid benzodiazepines except in alcohol or sedative withdrawal.

### Evaluating dementia

Screen with the **Montreal Cognitive Assessment** (MoCA; score of 25 or less out of 30 is abnormal) or the Mini-Mental State Examination (23 or less out of 30 is abnormal). Exclude reversible causes with TSH, vitamin B12, CBC, metabolic panel (calcium, glucose, renal and liver function), HIV and syphilis testing when indicated, depression screening and medication review. Obtain MRI (or CT) of the brain.

### The major dementias

- **Alzheimer disease:** most common (60 to 70%). Insidious memory loss, then language and visuospatial decline. Pathology: extracellular **amyloid-$\beta$ plaques** and intracellular **neurofibrillary tangles** (hyperphosphorylated tau), with atrophy of the hippocampus and entorhinal cortex and loss of cholinergic neurons in the nucleus basalis of Meynert. Risk: age, **APOE $\epsilon$4**; early-onset familial forms involve *APP* (chromosome 21), *PSEN1* (chromosome 14) and *PSEN2* (chromosome 1); Down syndrome (trisomy 21) carries early risk. Treatment: **cholinesterase inhibitors** (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine) for mild to moderate disease, **memantine** (NMDA antagonist) for moderate to severe disease; anti-amyloid antibodies (lecanemab, donanemab) in early disease with a risk of amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (edema and microhemorrhage).
- **Vascular dementia:** stepwise decline with focal signs and vascular risk factors; multiple infarcts on MRI; manage risk factors.
- **Dementia with Lewy bodies:** fluctuating cognition, well-formed **visual hallucinations**, parkinsonism, REM sleep behavior disorder and severe sensitivity to antipsychotics. If dementia precedes or arises within a year of parkinsonism, it is DLB; later is Parkinson disease dementia.
- **Frontotemporal dementia:** onset at 50 to 65 with personality change, disinhibition, apathy or progressive aphasia and relatively preserved memory; frontal and temporal atrophy; tau (Pick bodies) or TDP-43 pathology.
- **Normal pressure hydrocephalus:** triad of gait disturbance (magnetic gait), urinary incontinence and dementia ("wet, wobbly, wacky"); ventriculomegaly out of proportion to atrophy; gait improves after large-volume lumbar puncture, and a ventriculoperitoneal shunt treats it.
- **Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease:** rapidly progressive dementia with myoclonus over weeks to months; misfolded prion protein (PrP$^{Sc}$, $\beta$-sheet rich); EEG periodic sharp wave complexes, MRI cortical ribboning on diffusion imaging, and positive RT-QuIC in CSF; fatal.

### Screening-test statistics

For a screening test, **sensitivity** $= \frac{TP}{TP + FN}$, **specificity** $= \frac{TN}{TN + FP}$, **positive predictive value** $= \frac{TP}{TP + FP}$. Predictive values depend on prevalence; sensitivity and specificity do not.

@example
title: Evaluating a cognitive screening test
problem: A screening test is given to 200 older adults, of whom 80 have dementia. The test is positive in 68 of the 80 with dementia and in 12 of the 120 without dementia. Calculate sensitivity, specificity and PPV.
step: Sensitivity $= 68/80 = 0.85$.
step: True negatives $= 120 - 12 = 108$, so specificity $= 108/120 = 0.90$.
step: Positives total $68 + 12 = 80$, so PPV $= 68/80 = 0.85$.
step: In a population with a lower prevalence the PPV would be lower even though sensitivity and specificity are unchanged.

@mistakes
- Diagnosing dementia in an acutely confused patient before searching for delirium and its causes.
- Using benzodiazepines to sedate delirium.
- Giving antipsychotics for hallucinations in Lewy body dementia, which risks severe reactions.
- Ignoring depression ("pseudodementia") and medications such as anticholinergics as reversible causes.

@quiz num
prompt: A screening test is positive in 30 of 40 people with a disease and in 6 of 60 people without it. Calculate the positive predictive value.
answer: 5/6
check: 30 / (30 + 6)
hint: PPV is true positives divided by all positives.
solution: Total positives $= 30 + 6 = 36$, so PPV $= 30/36 = 5/6 \approx 0.83$.
