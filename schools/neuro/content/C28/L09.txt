@article
The meninges, CSF and brain can be infected by bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites. The classic triad of meningitis is fever, neck stiffness and altered mental status; headache is the most common symptom and fewer than half of patients have all three. Kernig and Brudzinski signs are specific but insensitive.

### Bacterial meningitis

Organisms depend on age: neonates, group B streptococcus, *Escherichia coli* and *Listeria*; children and adults, *Streptococcus pneumoniae* and *Neisseria meningitidis* (petechial rash, adrenal hemorrhage in Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome); over 50 or immunocompromised, add *Listeria monocytogenes*. Prompt treatment is critical.

- **Blood cultures, then empiric antibiotics immediately.** Do not delay antibiotics for imaging. Typical regimen: ceftriaxone plus vancomycin, with ampicillin added for age over 50 or immunocompromise to cover *Listeria*.
- **Dexamethasone** given before or with the first antibiotic dose reduces hearing loss and mortality in pneumococcal meningitis.
- **CT before lumbar puncture** if there is focal deficit, papilledema, new seizure, severely altered consciousness or immunocompromise. Give antibiotics before CT in those cases.
- Close contacts of meningococcal disease receive prophylaxis (rifampin, ciprofloxacin or ceftriaxone), and droplet precautions apply.

### Interpreting CSF

| Finding | Bacterial | Viral | Tuberculous or fungal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening pressure | High | Normal or mildly high | High |
| WBC (cells/$\mu$L) | Often over 1000, neutrophils | 10 to 500, lymphocytes | 100 to 500, lymphocytes |
| Protein | High (over 100 mg/dL) | Mild increase | Very high |
| Glucose | Low (CSF:serum ratio below 0.4) | Normal | Low |

If a traumatic tap contaminates CSF with blood, the corrected CSF white cell count is
$$\text{WBC}_{\text{corrected}} = \text{WBC}_{\text{CSF}} - \frac{\text{WBC}_{\text{blood}} \times \text{RBC}_{\text{CSF}}}{\text{RBC}_{\text{blood}}}.$$
Viral meningitis (enteroviruses, HSV-2) is usually self-limited. PCR panels help identify the pathogen.

### Encephalitis and other infections

- **Herpes simplex encephalitis:** fever, confusion, seizures and temporal lobe signs; MRI shows temporal lobe changes, CSF has lymphocytes and red cells, and HSV PCR is positive. Start **IV acyclovir** (10 mg/kg every 8 hours, typical dose for study) without waiting for results.
- **Brain abscess:** ring-enhancing lesion with surrounding edema, from contiguous sinus or ear infection or hematogenous spread; lumbar puncture is contraindicated; treat with drainage and antibiotics.
- **Toxoplasmosis** (HIV with CD4 below 100/$\mu$L): multiple ring-enhancing lesions, treated with pyrimethamine and sulfadiazine; **primary CNS lymphoma** (EBV-associated) is the main alternative.
- **Cryptococcal meningitis:** HIV with CD4 below 100/$\mu$L; India ink, cryptococcal antigen; amphotericin B plus flucytosine.
- **Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy:** JC virus reactivation causing multifocal white matter lesions in immunosuppression or natalizumab therapy.
- **Neurocysticercosis:** *Taenia solium* cysts, a leading cause of adult-onset seizures worldwide.

### Brain tumors

**Metastases** (lung, breast, melanoma, kidney, colon) are the most common intracranial tumors in adults; they are multiple, at the gray-white junction. Primary tumors:

| Tumor | Key features |
|---|---|
| Glioblastoma | Most common primary malignant tumor in adults; "butterfly" lesion crossing the corpus callosum; pseudopalisading necrosis; GFAP positive |
| Meningioma | Extra-axial, dural tail, psammoma bodies; slow growing, women |
| Vestibular schwannoma | CN VIII at the cerebellopontine angle; bilateral in neurofibromatosis type 2 |
| Pilocytic astrocytoma | Child, cerebellum, Rosenthal fibers, good prognosis |
| Medulloblastoma | Child, cerebellar vermis, small blue cells with rosettes, hydrocephalus |
| Oligodendroglioma | Frontal lobe, fried-egg cells, calcification, 1p/19q codeletion |
| Craniopharyngioma | Child, from Rathke pouch, suprasellar calcification, bitemporal hemianopia |
| Pituitary adenoma | Prolactinoma most common (cabergoline); bitemporal hemianopia |

Dexamethasone reduces vasogenic edema from tumors. New seizures, progressive headache and focal deficits are the usual presentations.

@example
title: Interpreting CSF and choosing treatment
problem: A 22-year-old college student has fever, headache and neck stiffness. CSF: opening pressure 32 cm H$_2$O, 2400 WBC/$\mu$L (90% neutrophils), protein 220 mg/dL, glucose 20 mg/dL; serum glucose 100 mg/dL. Interpret and treat.
step: High pressure, neutrophilic pleocytosis, high protein and low glucose point to bacterial meningitis.
step: CSF:serum glucose ratio is $20/100 = 0.20$, below 0.4.
step: Start ceftriaxone and vancomycin plus dexamethasone immediately, after drawing blood cultures; she has no focal deficit, so CT is not required before the lumbar puncture.
step: Add droplet precautions and arrange contact prophylaxis if Gram stain or culture shows meningococcus.

@mistakes
- Delaying antibiotics to obtain CT or the lumbar puncture.
- Forgetting ampicillin for Listeria in patients over 50 or immunocompromised.
- Reading low CSF glucose alone without comparing with serum glucose.
- Calling a ring-enhancing lesion in HIV a tumor without considering toxoplasmosis.
