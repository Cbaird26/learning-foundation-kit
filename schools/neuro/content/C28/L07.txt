@article
The basal ganglia (caudate, putamen, globus pallidus, subthalamic nucleus, substantia nigra) do not generate movement; they select and scale movements initiated by the cortex. Two loops from the striatum act like an accelerator and a brake. The **direct pathway** (D1 receptors) facilitates movement; the **indirect pathway** (D2 receptors) suppresses it. Dopamine from the substantia nigra pars compacta stimulates the direct and inhibits the indirect pathway, so both effects promote movement. Loss of dopamine causes **hypokinesia**; loss of striatal indirect-pathway neurons (Huntington disease) or the subthalamic nucleus (hemiballismus) causes **hyperkinesia**.

### Parkinson disease

Degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta, with intraneuronal Lewy bodies composed of $\alpha$-synuclein. Diagnosis is clinical: **bradykinesia** plus at least one of **resting tremor** (4 to 6 Hz, pill-rolling), **rigidity** (cogwheel or lead-pipe) or postural instability, with masked facies, micrographia, shuffling gait, and often anosmia, REM sleep behavior disorder and constipation preceding motor signs. Symptoms begin asymmetrically.

Treatment:
- **Levodopa with carbidopa** is the most effective drug. Carbidopa inhibits peripheral dopa decarboxylase, so more levodopa reaches the brain with less nausea and hypotension; about 70 to 100 mg of carbidopa daily is needed to saturate peripheral decarboxylase. Long-term use causes motor fluctuations ("wearing off") and dyskinesias.
- **Dopamine agonists** (pramipexole, ropinirole): useful early or as adjuncts; adverse effects include impulse control disorders, sleep attacks and hallucinations.
- **MAO-B inhibitors** (selegiline, rasagiline) and **COMT inhibitors** (entacapone) prolong dopamine action; **amantadine** reduces dyskinesias; anticholinergics (benztropine) help tremor but are avoided in older patients because of confusion.
- **Deep brain stimulation** of the subthalamic nucleus or globus pallidus interna for fluctuations in selected patients.

**Atypical parkinsonism:** poor levodopa response, early falls and symmetry. **Dementia with Lewy bodies** (dementia within a year of motor signs, visual hallucinations); **progressive supranuclear palsy** (vertical gaze palsy, early backward falls); **multiple system atrophy** (early autonomic failure, cerebellar signs).

### Other movement disorders

- **Huntington disease:** autosomal dominant CAG trinucleotide expansion in *HTT* on chromosome 4 with **anticipation** (especially paternal transmission); 40 or more repeats gives full penetrance. Onset at 30 to 50 years with chorea, psychiatric symptoms and dementia; caudate atrophy on imaging. Chorea is treated with VMAT2 inhibitors (tetrabenazine, deutetrabenazine).
- **Essential tremor:** bilateral postural and action tremor, often familial and improved by alcohol; treated with propranolol or primidone.
- **Wilson disease:** autosomal recessive *ATP7B* mutation (chromosome 13) with copper accumulation in liver, basal ganglia and cornea. Young patient with liver disease, tremor, dysarthria or psychiatric symptoms; **Kayser-Fleischer rings**, low serum ceruloplasmin and high 24-hour urine copper. Treat with chelation (penicillamine or trientine) and zinc.
- **Dystonia:** sustained involuntary contractions causing twisting postures; botulinum toxin injection for focal dystonia.
- **Tardive dyskinesia:** repetitive orofacial movements after months to years of dopamine antagonists; treated with VMAT2 inhibitors (valbenazine) and by changing the antipsychotic.
- **Hemiballismus:** violent flinging of one side from a contralateral subthalamic nucleus lesion, often a lacunar infarct.

### Drug-induced emergencies

| Feature | Neuroleptic malignant syndrome | Serotonin syndrome |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Dopamine antagonists | Serotonergic drugs (SSRIs with MAO inhibitors, linezolid, tramadol) |
| Onset | Days | Hours |
| Tone | Lead-pipe rigidity | Hyperreflexia and clonus |
| Other | Fever, autonomic instability, elevated creatine kinase | Diarrhea, mydriasis, tremor, agitation |
| Treatment | Stop drug, cooling, supportive care, dantrolene or bromocriptine | Stop drugs, benzodiazepines, cyproheptadine |

Treat rhabdomyolysis with fluids.

@example
title: Interpreting a CAG repeat result
problem: A man whose father has Huntington disease is tested and has 41 CAG repeats. What is his prognosis and the chance that a future child inherits the expansion?
step: Repeat length is 41, at or above the full penetrance threshold of 40, so he will develop disease if he lives long enough.
step: The gene is autosomal dominant; each child has a 50% chance of inheriting the expanded allele.
step: Repeats may expand further in the next generation, especially with paternal transmission (anticipation), so a child might have earlier onset.

@mistakes
- Treating resting tremor as essential tremor; essential tremor is a postural and action tremor.
- Withholding levodopa for fear of toxicity; delaying treatment gives no neuroprotection and worsens function.
- Using haloperidol for psychosis in Parkinson disease (quetiapine or clozapine are preferred, along with pimavanserin).
- Mistaking serotonin syndrome for NMS: clonus and hyperreflexia point to serotonin.

@practice self
prompt: A 70-year-old woman on haloperidol develops fever of 40 °C, lead-pipe rigidity, confusion and tachycardia, with creatine kinase elevated. Name the diagnosis, explain the mechanism and give management, then say how serotonin syndrome differs.
solution: This is neuroleptic malignant syndrome from dopamine D2 blockade in the nigrostriatum and hypothalamus, causing rigidity, heat production and autonomic instability. Stop the antipsychotic, give IV fluids and external cooling, admit to monitored care and treat severe cases with dantrolene or bromocriptine. Serotonin syndrome follows serotonergic drugs, begins within hours, and shows clonus, hyperreflexia, diarrhea and mydriasis rather than lead-pipe rigidity; it is treated with benzodiazepines and cyproheptadine.
hint: Think about which neurotransmitter is blocked.
rubric: Names neuroleptic malignant syndrome and dopamine blockade.
rubric: Lists stopping the drug, cooling, fluids and dantrolene or bromocriptine.
rubric: Distinguishes serotonin syndrome by clonus and rapid onset.
