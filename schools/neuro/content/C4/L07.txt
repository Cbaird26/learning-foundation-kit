@article
A **titration** measures an unknown concentration by adding a solution of known concentration (the **titrant**, usually from a buret) until the reaction is exactly complete. The **equivalence point** is when moles of titrant added equal the moles required by the reaction stoichiometry. The **endpoint** is where an indicator changes color; a well-chosen indicator makes the endpoint close to the equivalence point.

### Stoichiometry at equivalence

For a monoprotic acid and a strong base, $M_aV_a = M_bV_b$ at equivalence. For a diprotic acid such as $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ (both protons titrated), moles of base $= 2\times$ moles of acid. Always work in **moles**, since volumes change as titrant is added.

### Strong acid with strong base

For $\text{HCl}$ titrated with $\text{NaOH}$:

- **Before equivalence:** the pH is set by the excess acid, $[\text{H}^+] = (\text{mol acid} - \text{mol base})/V_{\text{total}}$.
- **At equivalence:** only $\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{Cl}^-$ remain, so $\text{pH} = 7.00$ (at $25\ \text{°C}$).
- **After equivalence:** the pH is set by the excess $\text{OH}^-$.

The curve is nearly flat, rises almost vertically through pH $7$, and flattens again.

### Weak acid with strong base

The curve has four distinct regions:

1. **Start:** a weak-acid problem (pH from $K_a$ and $C$).
2. **Buffer region:** both HA and $\text{A}^-$ are present, so use Henderson-Hasselbalch with the moles left over after the reaction.
3. **Half-equivalence point:** exactly half the acid has been converted, so $[\text{HA}] = [\text{A}^-]$ and $\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a$. This is how p$K_a$ is read from a curve.
4. **Equivalence point:** only $\text{A}^-$ remains, a weak base, so the pH is **above $7$**. Find it from $K_b = K_w/K_a$, with $[\text{A}^-] = \text{mol acid}/V_{\text{total}}$ and $[\text{OH}^-] = \sqrt{K_b[\text{A}^-]}$.

After equivalence, excess strong base controls the pH. The vertical rise is shorter than in a strong-acid titration. A weak base titrated with strong acid mirrors this, with an equivalence pH **below $7$**.

### Indicators

An **indicator** is a weak acid whose conjugate forms differ in color; it changes color over about one pH unit on each side of its own p$K_a$. Pick one whose transition range lies on the steep part of the curve: methyl orange or methyl red for titrations whose equivalence point is acidic, phenolphthalein (range about $8.2$–$10$) for a weak acid with a strong base. A pH meter gives the whole curve instead.

### Polyprotic acids and clinical use

A polyprotic acid gives a curve with one equivalence point per proton when the p$K_a$ values are well separated. Titration principles also underlie lab measurements of drug purity and titratable acidity of gastric samples, and the buffer region you read on a curve is exactly the bicarbonate-style behavior from last week.

@example
title: pH before the equivalence point (strong acid)
problem: $25.00\ \text{mL}$ of $0.100\ \text{M}$ HCl is titrated with $0.100\ \text{M}$ NaOH. Find the pH after $20.00\ \text{mL}$ of NaOH has been added.
step: Moles of acid: $0.02500\ \text{L} \times 0.100 = 2.500\times10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$. Moles of base added: $0.02000 \times 0.100 = 2.000\times10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$.
step: Excess acid: $2.500\times10^{-3} - 2.000\times10^{-3} = 5.0\times10^{-4}\ \text{mol}$.
step: Total volume is $25.00 + 20.00 = 45.00\ \text{mL}$, so $[\text{H}^+] = 5.0\times10^{-4}/0.04500 = 0.01111\ \text{M}$.
step: $\text{pH} = -\log(0.01111) = 1.95$.

@example
title: pH at the equivalence point (weak acid)
problem: $25.0\ \text{mL}$ of $0.100\ \text{M}$ acetic acid ($K_a = 1.8\times10^{-5}$) is titrated with $0.100\ \text{M}$ NaOH. Find the pH at the equivalence point.
step: Equivalence needs $2.50\times10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$ of base, which is $25.0\ \text{mL}$. The total volume is $50.0\ \text{mL}$.
step: All the acid is now acetate: $[\text{A}^-] = 2.50\times10^{-3}/0.0500 = 0.0500\ \text{M}$.
step: $K_b = (1.0\times10^{-14})/(1.8\times10^{-5}) = 5.56\times10^{-10}$, so $[\text{OH}^-] = \sqrt{(5.56\times10^{-10})(0.0500)} = 5.27\times10^{-6}\ \text{M}$.
step: $\text{pOH} = 5.28$, so $\text{pH} = 14.00 - 5.28 = 8.72$, above $7$ as expected.

@mistakes
- Using volumes instead of moles when finding the excess acid or base, or forgetting that the total volume grows.
- Assuming the equivalence point is always at pH $7$; this is true only for strong acid with strong base.
- Reading p$K_a$ at the equivalence point instead of at the half-equivalence point.
- Forgetting that a diprotic acid consumes two moles of hydroxide per mole.

@practice num
prompt: What volume of $0.200\ \text{M}$ NaOH is needed to neutralize $20.0\ \text{mL}$ of $0.150\ \text{M}$ HCl? Give the volume in mL.
answer: 15.0
check: 0.150 * 20.0 / 0.200
tol: 1%
unit: mL
hint: At equivalence, moles of acid equal moles of base for HCl and NaOH.
solution: $M_aV_a = M_bV_b$, so $V_b = (0.150)(20.0)/0.200 = 15.0\ \text{mL}$.

@practice num
prompt: A $25.0\ \text{mL}$ sample of $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ requires $30.0\ \text{mL}$ of $0.100\ \text{M}$ NaOH to reach the point where both protons are neutralized. What is the molarity of the acid?
answer: 0.0600
check: (0.100 * 0.0300 / 2) / 0.0250
tol: 1%
unit: M
hint: Find moles of NaOH, then use the stoichiometric ratio of base to acid.
hint: Each mole of $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ consumes two moles of $\text{OH}^-$.
solution: Moles NaOH $= 0.100 \times 0.0300 = 3.00\times10^{-3}$. Moles acid $= 1.50\times10^{-3}$. Molarity $= 1.50\times10^{-3}/0.0250 = 0.0600\ \text{M}$.

@practice num
prompt: A weak acid with $K_a = 1.8\times10^{-5}$ is titrated with strong base. What is the pH at the half-equivalence point?
answer: 4.745
check: -log10(1.8e-5)
tol: 0.02
hint: At half-equivalence, $[\text{HA}] = [\text{A}^-]$.
solution: With equal HA and $\text{A}^-$, $\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a = -\log(1.8\times10^{-5}) = 4.74$.

@practice num
prompt: $20.0\ \text{mL}$ of $0.100\ \text{M}$ HCl is titrated with $0.100\ \text{M}$ NaOH. Find the pH after $10.0\ \text{mL}$ of NaOH is added.
answer: 1.477
check: -log10((0.0200 * 0.100 - 0.0100 * 0.100) / (0.0200 + 0.0100))
tol: 0.01
hint: Find the moles of excess HCl and divide by the total volume.
solution: Excess $\text{H}^+ = 2.00\times10^{-3} - 1.00\times10^{-3} = 1.00\times10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$ in $30.0\ \text{mL}$, so $[\text{H}^+] = 0.0333\ \text{M}$ and $\text{pH} = 1.48$.

@quiz num
prompt: $25.0\ \text{mL}$ of $0.100\ \text{M}$ formic acid (HCOOH, $K_a = 1.8\times10^{-4}$) is titrated with $0.100\ \text{M}$ NaOH. Find the pH after $10.0\ \text{mL}$ of NaOH has been added.
answer: 3.569
check: -log10(1.8e-4) + log10((0.0100 * 0.100) / (0.0250 * 0.100 - 0.0100 * 0.100))
tol: 0.02
hint: Find moles of HA and $\text{A}^-$ after the reaction, then use Henderson-Hasselbalch.
hint: $\text{A}^- = 1.00\times10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$ and $\text{HA} = 1.50\times10^{-3}\ \text{mol}$.
solution: $\text{p}K_a = 3.74$ and the ratio is $1.00/1.50$, so $\text{pH} = 3.74 + \log(0.667) = 3.74 - 0.18 = 3.57$.
