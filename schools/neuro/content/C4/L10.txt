@article
An **oxidation-reduction (redox)** reaction transfers electrons: **oxidation** is loss of electrons, **reduction** is gain (remember "LEO the lion says GER"). In a **galvanic (voltaic) cell**, the two half-reactions occur in separate compartments joined by a wire and a salt bridge, so the electrons flow through the wire and do work.

### Parts of a galvanic cell

- The **anode** is where oxidation occurs; it is the negative terminal.
- The **cathode** is where reduction occurs; it is the positive terminal.
- Electrons flow through the external wire from the anode to the cathode.
- The **salt bridge** supplies ions to balance charge, so the circuit is complete. Anions drift toward the anode and cations toward the cathode.

The cell is written in shorthand as anode $|$ anode solution $\|$ cathode solution $|$ cathode, for example $\text{Zn}\,|\,\text{Zn}^{2+}\,\|\,\text{Cu}^{2+}\,|\,\text{Cu}$.

### Standard cell potential

Tables list **standard reduction potentials** $E^\circ$ (volts, $1\ \text{M}$ solutions, $1\ \text{atm}$ gases, $25\ \text{°C}$, relative to the standard hydrogen electrode, which is $0$). A more positive $E^\circ$ means a stronger tendency to be reduced. Then

$$E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}}.$$

Do **not** multiply $E^\circ$ by the stoichiometric coefficients; potential is intensive. A positive $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ means a spontaneous (galvanic) cell. A common table shows $\text{Zn}^{2+}/\text{Zn}$ at $-0.76\ \text{V}$, $\text{Cu}^{2+}/\text{Cu}$ at $+0.34\ \text{V}$, and $\text{Ag}^+/\text{Ag}$ at $+0.80\ \text{V}$.

### Potential, free energy and K

With $n$ moles of electrons transferred and the Faraday constant $F = 96\,485\ \text{C/mol}$,

$$\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ_{\text{cell}}, \qquad E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = \frac{RT}{nF}\ln K = \frac{0.05916\ \text{V}}{n}\log K\ \ (25\ \text{°C}).$$

Positive $E^\circ$ corresponds to negative $\Delta G^\circ$ and $K > 1$. Electrical work is $w_{\max} = -nFE$.

### The Nernst equation

Away from standard conditions,

$$E = E^\circ - \frac{RT}{nF}\ln Q = E^\circ - \frac{0.05916}{n}\log Q \quad (25\ \text{°C}).$$

As a cell discharges, $Q$ rises toward $K$ and $E$ falls to zero at equilibrium: a "dead" battery is one at equilibrium ($E = 0$, $\Delta G = 0$). A **concentration cell** uses the same electrode material in both half-cells ($E^\circ = 0$) with different concentrations; the potential comes entirely from $Q$.

### Biological connection

Cells maintain ion gradients, and the membrane potential obeys a Nernst-type relation for each ion, which is why nerve and muscle cells behave like concentration cells. Electron transport in mitochondria is a chain of redox couples of increasing reduction potential ending at oxygen, releasing free energy in small steps. Batteries and pH electrodes use the same ideas.

@example
title: Cell potential, free energy and K
problem: Find $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$, $\Delta G^\circ$ and $K$ for $\text{Zn} + \text{Cu}^{2+} \to \text{Zn}^{2+} + \text{Cu}$.
step: Zinc is oxidized (anode) and copper(II) is reduced (cathode): $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = 0.34 - (-0.76) = 1.10\ \text{V}$. Two electrons are transferred, so $n = 2$.
step: $\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ = -(2)(96\,485)(1.10) = -2.12\times10^{5}\ \text{J} = -212\ \text{kJ}$.
step: $\log K = nE^\circ/0.05916 = (2)(1.10)/0.05916 = 37.2$, so $K \approx 1.5\times10^{37}$, essentially complete reaction.

@example
title: Nernst equation
problem: The cell $\text{Zn} + \text{Cu}^{2+} \to \text{Zn}^{2+} + \text{Cu}$ ($E^\circ = 1.10\ \text{V}$, $n = 2$) has $[\text{Zn}^{2+}] = 1.0\ \text{M}$ and $[\text{Cu}^{2+}] = 0.010\ \text{M}$. Find $E$ at $25\ \text{°C}$.
step: $Q = [\text{Zn}^{2+}]/[\text{Cu}^{2+}] = 1.0/0.010 = 100$.
step: $E = 1.10 - (0.05916/2)\log(100) = 1.10 - (0.02958)(2)$.
step: $E = 1.10 - 0.059 = 1.04\ \text{V}$. Lower $[\text{Cu}^{2+}]$ makes the cell less strongly spontaneous.

@mistakes
- Multiplying a half-cell potential by its stoichiometric coefficient when balancing.
- Subtracting in the wrong order; it is cathode minus anode.
- Using $Q$ with products and reactants inverted in the Nernst equation, or forgetting $n$.
- Saying electrons flow through the salt bridge; they flow through the wire, and ions move in the bridge.

@practice num
prompt: The cell $2\text{Ag}^+ + \text{Cu} \to 2\text{Ag} + \text{Cu}^{2+}$ has $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = 0.46\ \text{V}$. Find $\Delta G^\circ$ in kJ ($F = 96\,485\ \text{C/mol}$).
answer: -88.8
check: -2 * 96485 * 0.46 / 1000
tol: 1%
unit: kJ
hint: Determine $n$ from the balanced equation: how many electrons are transferred?
solution: Two electrons are transferred, so $\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ = -(2)(96\,485)(0.46) = -88\,766\ \text{J} = -88.8\ \text{kJ}$.

@practice num

@practice num
prompt: A copper concentration cell has $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ at $0.10\ \text{M}$ in one half-cell and $0.0010\ \text{M}$ in the other, with copper electrodes. Find $E$ in volts at $25\ \text{°C}$ ($n = 2$, $E^\circ = 0$).
answer: 0.0592
check: 0.05916 / 2 * log10(0.10 / 0.0010)
tol: 1%
unit: V
hint: $Q$ is the ratio of the dilute to the concentrated concentration, and $E = -(0.05916/n)\log Q$.
solution: $Q = 0.0010/0.10 = 0.010$, so $E = -(0.05916/2)\log(0.010) = (0.02958)(2) = 0.0592\ \text{V}$.

@practice mc
