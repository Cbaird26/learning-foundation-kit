title: Comparing numbers
summary: Tell which group has more, fewer or the same, and which of two numbers is bigger.
objective: Match things one to one to see which group has more.
objective: Use the words more, fewer and the same.
objective: Say which of two numbers up to 20 is greater.
reading: Khan Academy, Early math review: the topic "Comparing numbers".

@article
Sometimes we want to know which group has **more**. Maybe you and a friend each have some crackers. Who has more?

### Match them up

Line up the crackers in two rows. Put one cracker from your row next to one cracker from your friend's row. If one row has crackers left over, that row has **more**. The other row has **fewer**. If nothing is left over in either row, the two rows are **the same**.

### Use the numbers

You can also count each group. Say you have 5 crackers and your friend has 3. The number 5 is **greater than** 3. So you have more.

The number that comes later when you count is the bigger number. You say 3 before you say 5. So 5 is bigger.

### The words to know

- **more**: a bigger number
- **fewer**: a smaller number
- **the same**: equal numbers

### Numbers up to 20

It works the same way for bigger numbers. 12 is greater than 9, because you say 12 after you say 9.

@example
title: Who has more?
problem: Mia has 6 grapes. Sam has 4 grapes. Who has more?
step: Count each group. Mia has 6. Sam has 4.
step: You say 4 before 6 when you count, so 6 is the bigger number.
step: Mia has more grapes. Sam has fewer.

@mistakes
- Thinking a group that is spread out wide has more. Spreading things out does not change how many there are.
- Mixing up more and fewer.
- Comparing the size of the things instead of counting how many things there are.

prompt: Dan has 3 blocks. Eve has 8 blocks. Who has more blocks?
choice: Dan
choice*: Eve
choice: They have the same number.
hint: Which number do you say later when you count, 3 or 8?
solution: 8 is greater than 3, so Eve has more blocks.

@practice num
prompt: Which number is greater, 9 or 14? Type the greater number.
answer: 14
check: max(9, 14)
hint: Count from 9. Do you reach 14 after 9?
solution: You say 14 after 9, so 14 is greater.

hint: The bigger number tells the group with more.
solution: 12 is greater than 7, so the basket has more. It has 12 apples.

@practice self
prompt: Pick two groups of things at home, like spoons and forks, or socks and shoes. Count each group to 20 or less. Tell your grown-up which group has more and which has fewer.
hint: Say the last number of each group, then pick the bigger one.
solution: Count each group, for example 9 spoons and 6 forks. Then say that 9 is more than 6, so there are more spoons and fewer forks.
rubric: You counted each group and said both numbers.
rubric: You said which group has more.
rubric: You said which group has fewer.
