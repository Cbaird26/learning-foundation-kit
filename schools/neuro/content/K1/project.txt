@project
title: My counting and shapes book
overview: With your grown-up, make a small book about numbers. It has a page for each number from 1 to 20, a shape hunt, and three story problems you made up.
goal: Count objects from 1 to 20 and show each number on a page.
goal: Find and name shapes around the house.
goal: Make up and solve three story problems with a number sentence.
step: Fold 10 sheets of paper in half to make a book. Write your title on the front.
step: Make a page for each number from 1 to 20. On each page, write the number and draw that many dots, stars or hearts. Count them with your grown-up.
step: Make a shape page. Find five shapes around the house, such as a circle, square, triangle and rectangle. Draw each one and write where you found it.
step: Make a sorting page. Sort some small things by color or size, draw the groups, and write which group has more.
step: Make three story problems. Use adding and taking away. Draw a picture for each one and write the number sentence and the answer.
step: Add one page that shows how you measured a toy with paper clips, and one page with a clock that shows a time of day you like.
step: Read your book aloud to your grown-up.
deliverable: A book with a page for each number from 1 to 20.
deliverable: A shape hunt page with five shapes.
deliverable: Three story problems with pictures, number sentences and answers.
rubric: Each number page has the right number of things drawn and you can count them aloud.
rubric: The shapes are named correctly.
rubric: Each story problem has a number sentence that matches the story.
rubric: You can say one big idea from the course in your own words.
safety: Use safe, blunt scissors with your grown-up. Use only paper, crayons and household items.
tutor: Can you check my counting from 1 to 20?
tutor: How do I know if my number sentence matches my story?
tutor: Can you help me think of a good story problem?

@review
### Week by week

- **Counting to 20:** Say the numbers in order. The last number tells how many.
- **Comparing numbers:** More, fewer and the same. The bigger number is said later when counting.
- **Shapes and positions:** Circle, square, triangle and rectangle. Above, below, next to and between.
- **Patterns and sorting:** Sort by color, size or shape. Find the part that repeats.
- **Adding within 10:** Putting together. $3 + 2 = 5$.
- **Subtracting within 10:** Taking away. $5 - 2 = 3$.
- **Numbers to 100:** Count by tens: 10, 20, 30 and so on.
- **Tens and ones:** 34 is 3 tens and 4 ones.
- **Adding and subtracting within 20:** Make ten first.
- **Story problems:** Read, draw, write a number sentence.
- **Measuring length:** Units end to end with no gaps.
- **Telling time:** On the hour and half past.

### Check yourself

- I can count to 20 and to 100.
- I can tell which group has more.
- I can name four shapes.
- I can add and subtract small numbers.
- I can read a clock to the hour and half hour.

### Common mistakes

Skipping or double counting things. Adding when you should take away. Mixing up the tens and ones digits. Leaving gaps when measuring.

@exam num
prompt: A shelf has 5 tens of crayons and 3 loose crayons. Then 4 loose crayons are taken away. How many crayons are on the shelf now?
answer: 49
check: 5 * 10 + 3 - 4
hint: 5 tens and 3 ones make 53.
hint: Then take away 4.
solution: 5 tens and 3 ones is 53. Then $53 - 4 = 49$. There are 49 crayons.
