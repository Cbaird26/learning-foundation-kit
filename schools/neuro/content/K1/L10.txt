@lesson
title: Story problems
summary: Read a short story, draw it, and write a number sentence to find the answer.
objective: Tell whether a story is about putting together or taking away.
objective: Draw a picture and write a number sentence for a story.
objective: Solve story problems with numbers up to 20.
reading: Khan Academy, Early math review: the topic "Word problems".

@article
A **story problem** tells a little story with numbers in it. Your job is to find the answer.

### Read, think, draw

First, read the story slowly. Ask your grown-up to read it aloud. Next, ask: What is the story about? What do I need to find? Then draw a picture with dots or boxes.

### Putting together

Some words tell you to add: **in all**, **altogether**, **more**. A story like "Ben has 4 toy cars and gets 3 more" makes you add. Write $4 + 3 = 7$.

### Taking away

Some words tell you to subtract: **left**, **fly away**, **eat**, **give away**. A story like "There are 9 birds and 4 fly away" makes you take away. Write $9 - 4 = 5$.

### Write a number sentence

Put the numbers from the story into a number sentence. Then write the answer and say it in a sentence: "Ben has 7 cars."

### Check your answer

Does the answer make sense? If you add, the answer should be bigger. If you take away, the answer should be smaller.

### Make your own

Make up a story about your toys and have your grown-up solve it. Then switch.

@example
title: A story with adding
problem: Ava has 6 shells. She finds 5 more. How many shells does she have in all?
step: The words "finds more" and "in all" tell you to add.
step: Write the number sentence: $6 + 5 = ?$
step: $6 + 4 = 10$ and $1$ more is $11$. Ava has 11 shells.

@example
title: A story with taking away
problem: There are 12 ducks on a pond. 4 swim away. How many ducks are left?
step: The words "swim away" and "left" tell you to take away.
step: Write the number sentence: $12 - 4 = ?$
step: Counting back 4 from 12 gives 11, 10, 9, 8. There are 8 ducks left.

@mistakes
- Adding when the story says take away, or the other way around.
- Writing the answer without checking that it makes sense.
- Using a number that is not in the story.

@practice num
prompt: Tom has 5 red balloons and 4 blue balloons. How many balloons does he have in all?
answer: 9
check: 5 + 4
hint: "In all" tells you to put the groups together.
solution: $5 + 4 = 9$. Tom has 9 balloons.

@practice self
prompt: Make up one adding story and one taking-away story about your toys. Draw a picture for each one and write each number sentence. Tell the stories to your grown-up.
hint: Pick small numbers, like numbers that are 10 or less.
solution: For example, "I have 6 cars and get 3 more. How many in all?" gives $6 + 3 = 9$. "I have 8 blocks and give away 5. How many are left?" gives $8 - 5 = 3$.
rubric: Each story has numbers and a question.
rubric: Each picture matches its story.
rubric: Each number sentence matches its story and has the right answer.

@quiz num
prompt: Sam has 14 grapes. He eats 6 of them. How many grapes does he have left?
answer: 8
check: 14 - 6
hint: Eating means taking away.
hint: Take away 4 to get to 10, then 2 more.
solution: $14 - 6 = 8$. Sam has 8 grapes left.
