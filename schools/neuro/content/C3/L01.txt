@article
Every chemical calculation you will do in this course begins with two questions: what is the atom made of, and how many atoms are we talking about? This lesson answers both. The answer to the second question is the **mole**, the bridge between the invisible world of atoms and the grams you can weigh on a balance.

### Inside the atom

An atom has a tiny, dense **nucleus** containing **protons** (charge $+1$) and **neutrons** (charge $0$), surrounded by **electrons** (charge $-1$). Three numbers describe it:

- The **atomic number** $Z$ is the number of protons. It defines the element.
- The **mass number** $A$ is protons plus neutrons.
- The **charge** equals protons minus electrons. A neutral atom has equal numbers of each.

The notation ${}^{A}_{Z}\text{X}$ packs this together: ${}^{23}_{11}\text{Na}$ has $11$ protons and $23 - 11 = 12$ neutrons. The sodium ion $\text{Na}^+$ has lost one electron, so it has $10$ electrons.

**Isotopes** are atoms of the same element (same $Z$) with different numbers of neutrons, such as ${}^{12}\text{C}$ and ${}^{14}\text{C}$. They behave almost identically in chemistry but differ in mass, and some are radioactive.

### Atomic mass and the unit u

Masses of atoms are expressed in **unified atomic mass units** (u), defined so that one atom of ${}^{12}\text{C}$ has a mass of exactly $12$ u. The mass on the periodic table is a **weighted average** over the naturally occurring isotopes:

$$\bar{m} = \sum_i f_i\, m_i$$

where $f_i$ is the fractional abundance of isotope $i$ (the percent divided by $100$) and $m_i$ its mass. Chlorine is about $75.8\%$ ${}^{35}\text{Cl}$ and $24.2\%$ ${}^{37}\text{Cl}$, so its average mass is near $35.45$ u and no single chlorine atom actually has that mass.

### The mole

Counting atoms one by one is impossible, so chemists count in bundles. One **mole** is exactly $6.02214076 \times 10^{23}$ elementary entities, the value of **Avogadro's constant** $N_A$. We use $6.022 \times 10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$.

The **molar mass** $M$ of a substance is the mass of one mole, in g/mol. It is numerically equal to the atomic or formula mass in u. For a compound, add the atomic masses of all atoms in the formula: for $\text{CO}_2$, $M = 12.011 + 2(15.999) = 44.009$ g/mol.

Three conversions cover most of what you will do:

$$n = \frac{m}{M}, \qquad N = n\,N_A, \qquad m = nM.$$

Always carry units through the calculation: grams cancel grams, and what remains is moles. This habit makes every later topic, from stoichiometry to solutions, much easier.

### Counting atoms inside molecules

The subscripts in a formula tell you how many atoms are in each formula unit. One mole of $\text{CO}_2$ contains one mole of C atoms and two moles of O atoms. Convert to moles of the compound first, then multiply by the subscript.

@example
title: Average atomic mass of chlorine
problem: Chlorine has two stable isotopes: ${}^{35}\text{Cl}$ with mass $34.969$ u and abundance $75.77\%$, and ${}^{37}\text{Cl}$ with mass $36.966$ u and abundance $24.23\%$. Find the average atomic mass.
step: Convert percents to fractions: $0.7577$ and $0.2423$. They should sum to $1$.
step: Multiply each mass by its fraction: $0.7577 \times 34.969 = 26.50$ u and $0.2423 \times 36.966 = 8.957$ u.
step: Add the contributions: $26.50 + 8.957 = 35.45$ u.
step: Sanity check: the answer lies between $34.969$ and $36.966$ and is closer to the more abundant isotope, as it must be.

@example
title: From grams to molecules
problem: How many molecules are in $9.00$ g of glucose, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$, and how many carbon atoms do they contain?
step: Molar mass: $6(12.011) + 12(1.008) + 6(15.999) = 180.16$ g/mol.
step: Moles: $n = 9.00\ \text{g} / 180.16\ \text{g/mol} = 0.04996$ mol.
step: Molecules: $N = 0.04996 \times 6.022\times10^{23} = 3.009 \times 10^{22}$.
step: Each molecule has $6$ carbon atoms, so there are $6 \times 3.009\times10^{22} = 1.81 \times 10^{23}$ carbon atoms.

@mistakes
- Averaging isotope masses without weighting by abundance, or using percents instead of fractions.
- Forgetting to multiply by the subscript when counting atoms of one element inside a molecule.
- Subtracting the charge the wrong way: a cation has fewer electrons than protons, an anion has more.
- Using the mass number as the atomic mass in molar-mass calculations instead of the periodic-table value.

@practice num
prompt: How many neutrons are in an atom of ${}^{63}\text{Cu}$ (atomic number $29$)?
answer: 34
check: 63 - 29
hint: Neutrons equal the mass number minus the atomic number.
hint: Mass number is $63$ and the number of protons is $29$.
solution: Neutrons $= A - Z = 63 - 29 = 34$.

@practice num
prompt: How many moles of NaCl are in $11.69$ g of sodium chloride? Use $M(\text{Na}) = 22.990$ and $M(\text{Cl}) = 35.45$ g/mol. Give your answer to three decimal places.
answer: 0.200
check: 11.69 / (22.990 + 35.45)
tol: 0.002
unit: mol
hint: First find the molar mass of NaCl by adding the two atomic masses.
hint: Moles equal mass divided by molar mass.
solution: $M = 22.990 + 35.45 = 58.44$ g/mol. $n = 11.69 / 58.44 = 0.2000$ mol.

@practice num
prompt: Boron has two stable isotopes: ${}^{10}\text{B}$ (mass $10.013$ u, abundance $19.9\%$) and ${}^{11}\text{B}$ (mass $11.009$ u, abundance $80.1\%$). Calculate the average atomic mass of boron in u, to two decimal places.
answer: 10.81
check: 0.199*10.013 + 0.801*11.009
tol: 0.01
unit: u
hint: Convert each percent to a fraction.
hint: Multiply each mass by its fractional abundance and add.
solution: $0.199(10.013) + 0.801(11.009) = 1.993 + 8.818 = 10.81$ u.

@quiz num
prompt: How many oxygen atoms are in $4.50$ g of carbon dioxide, $\text{CO}_2$? Use $M(\text{C}) = 12.011$, $M(\text{O}) = 15.999$ g/mol and $N_A = 6.022\times10^{23}$.
answer: 1.23e23
check: 4.50 / (12.011 + 2*15.999) * 2 * 6.022e23
tol: 1%
hint: Convert grams of $\text{CO}_2$ to moles, then to molecules.
hint: Each molecule of $\text{CO}_2$ contains two oxygen atoms.
solution: $M = 44.009$ g/mol, so $n = 4.50/44.009 = 0.1023$ mol. Molecules: $0.1023 \times 6.022\times10^{23} = 6.16\times10^{22}$. Oxygen atoms: $2 \times 6.16\times10^{22} = 1.23\times10^{23}$.

@quiz num
prompt: How many electrons are in one ion of ${}^{56}\text{Fe}^{3+}$? The atomic number of iron is $26$.
answer: 23
check: 26 - 3
hint: A neutral iron atom has as many electrons as protons.
hint: A $3+$ charge means three electrons have been removed.
solution: Neutral Fe has $26$ electrons. Removing three gives $26 - 3 = 23$ electrons.
