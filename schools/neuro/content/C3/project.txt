title: Solution-preparation plan for saline and dextrose
overview: You are writing a preparation sheet for a teaching laboratory that makes intravenous-style saline and dextrose solutions for a simulation. For each solution you compute the masses and dilutions needed, state the concentration in several units, and check every value by an independent calculation. This is a calculation exercise for study only: it is not a procedure for preparing solutions for human use.
goal: Convert between percent weight per volume, molarity, molality and osmolarity for NaCl and glucose solutions.
goal: Plan a dilution from a concentrate using $M_1V_1 = M_2V_2$ and verify it with a mass balance.
goal: Compare each solution's osmolarity with plasma (about $285$ to $295$ mOsm/L) and justify a tonicity label.
step: Use these constants: $M(\text{NaCl}) = 58.44$ g/mol, $M(\text{glucose}) = 180.16$ g/mol (anhydrous), $K_f = 1.86\,^\circ\text{C kg/mol}$ for water. Treat NaCl as fully dissociated ($i = 2$) and glucose as undissociated ($i = 1$). Assume $1.00$ g/mL for dilute aqueous solutions.
step: Solution A, $0.9\%$ w/v saline, $1.00$ L: compute the mass of NaCl, the molarity, the osmolarity, and the approximate molality (assume the solvent mass is about $1.00$ kg per liter). State whether it is isotonic.
step: Solution B, $5\%$ w/v dextrose, $500.$ mL: compute the mass of glucose, the molarity and the osmolarity. Then repeat the osmolarity for the monohydrate form, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6\cdot\text{H}_2\text{O}$ with $M = 198.17$ g/mol, which is what commercial dextrose actually is, and explain the difference in two sentences.
step: Solution C, a dilution: a stock of $23.4\%$ w/v NaCl is on the shelf. Compute its molarity, then the volume of stock needed to make $500.$ mL of $0.9\%$ w/v saline, and confirm by checking that the grams of NaCl before and after dilution are equal.
step: Solution D, mixed solution, $1.00$ L containing $0.45\%$ w/v NaCl and $5\%$ w/v dextrose: compute the mass of each solute and the total osmolarity, and classify it relative to plasma.
step: Estimate the freezing point of Solution A from $\Delta T_f = iK_f m$ and say why the measured value ($-0.52\,^\circ\text{C}$) is slightly less negative than your estimate.
step: Assemble a one-page preparation sheet: a table of every solution with mass, volume, molarity, molality, osmolarity and tonicity, plus a short check column showing the independent calculation.
deliverable: A one-page preparation sheet with the table for solutions A through D and all work shown with units.
deliverable: A short written explanation (150 to 250 words) of why osmolarity, not mass percent, determines whether a solution is isotonic.

rubric: Tonicity labels follow from comparing osmolarity with plasma, and the glucose versus dextrose-monohydrate difference is explained correctly.

@review
### Week by week

- **Week 1, atoms and the mole:** $Z$, $A$, charge and isotopes; average atomic mass is a weighted average; $n = m/M$ and $N = nN_A$.
- **Week 2, stoichiometry:** gram to mole to mole to gram; the limiting reagent has the smallest moles divided by coefficient; percent yield is actual over theoretical.
- **Week 3, aqueous reactions:** solubility rules, net ionic equations, oxidation numbers, $M = n/V$, $M_1V_1 = M_2V_2$ and titration.
- **Week 4, gases:** $PV = nRT$ with kelvin and matching units; $M = dRT/P$; Dalton's law; alveolar gas equation with water vapor subtracted.
- **Week 5, thermochemistry:** $q = mc\Delta T$; exothermic means $\Delta H < 0$; reverse and scale equations in Hess's law; products minus reactants for formation enthalpies.
- **Week 6, quantum theory:** $E = hc/\lambda$; quantum numbers; Aufbau, Pauli, Hund; exceptions Cr and Cu; $4s$ electrons leave first.
- **Week 7, periodic trends:** $Z_{\text{eff}}$ and shell number explain radius, ionization energy, electronegativity; watch the dips at Be to B and N to O.
- **Week 8, Lewis structures:** count valence electrons, formal charge, resonance, octet exceptions, bond enthalpies (broken minus formed).
- **Week 9, VSEPR:** count electron groups, name the molecular geometry, and check whether dipoles cancel.
- **Week 10, hybridization and MO theory:** groups give hybridization; bond order is half of bonding minus antibonding electrons; $\text{O}_2$ is paramagnetic.
- **Week 11, intermolecular forces:** hydrogen bonding, dipole-dipole and dispersion; Clausius-Clapeyron needs kelvin.
- **Week 12, solutions:** molarity, molality, mole fraction, osmolarity $= iM$, colligative properties, Henry's law.

### Self-check

- Can you convert grams to moles to particles without notes?
- Can you pick the limiting reagent and defend the choice?
- Can you draw a Lewis structure, assign formal charges, name the shape and decide polarity?
- Can you rank the boiling points of a set of compounds and explain each step?
- Can you compute osmolarity from a mass percent?

### Most common errors

- Using Celsius instead of kelvin, or mixing units of pressure and $R$.
- Comparing grams rather than moles over coefficients for the limiting reagent.
- Forgetting the van't Hoff factor $i$.
- Counting a double bond as two electron groups, or ignoring lone pairs in shape and hybridization.
- Subtracting $4s$ and $3d$ electrons in the wrong order for transition-metal ions.
- Reversing the sign of $\Delta H$ for a reversed equation incorrectly, or computing bond-energy changes as formed minus broken.

prompt: Calcium carbonate reacts with hydrochloric acid by $\text{CaCO}_3 + 2\,\text{HCl} \to \text{CaCl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2$. A tablet containing $5.00$ g of $\text{CaCO}_3$ is dropped into excess acid, and all the $\text{CO}_2$ is collected as an ideal gas at $1.00$ atm and $37\,^\circ$C ($310.15$ K). What volume of $\text{CO}_2$, in liters, forms? Use $M(\text{Ca}) = 40.078$, $M(\text{C}) = 12.011$, $M(\text{O}) = 15.999$ g/mol and $R = 0.082057$ L atm/(mol K).
answer: 1.27
check: 5.00 / (40.078 + 12.011 + 3*15.999) * 0.082057 * 310.15 / 1.00
tol: 1%
unit: L
hint: Convert the mass of $\text{CaCO}_3$ to moles, then use the $1:1$ ratio to $\text{CO}_2$.
hint: Then apply $V = nRT/P$ with the temperature in kelvin.
solution: $M(\text{CaCO}_3) = 100.087$ g/mol, so $n = 5.00/100.087 = 0.04996$ mol, which equals the moles of $\text{CO}_2$. $V = nRT/P = (0.04996)(0.082057)(310.15)/1.00 = 1.27$ L.

prompt: The combustion of glucose, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\,\text{O}_2 \to 6\,\text{CO}_2 + 6\,\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$, has $\Delta H = -2803$ kJ per mole of glucose. A calorimeter holds $1000.$ g of water ($c = 4.184$ J g$^{-1}\,^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$). If $10.0$ g of glucose ($M = 180.16$ g/mol) burns completely and all the heat goes into the water, by how many $^\circ$C does the water temperature rise?
answer: 37.2
check: 10.0 / 180.16 * 2803 * 1000 / (1000. * 4.184)
tol: 1%
unit: °C
hint: Find the heat released by $10.0$ g of glucose in joules.
hint: Use $q = mc\Delta T$ and solve for $\Delta T$.
solution: Moles: $10.0/180.16 = 0.05551$ mol. Heat: $0.05551 \times 2803 = 155.6$ kJ $= 1.556\times10^5$ J. $\Delta T = q/(mc) = 1.556\times10^5/(1000.\times4.184) = 37.2\,^\circ\text{C}$.

prompt: Select all statements that are true of xenon tetrafluoride, $\text{XeF}_4$.
choice*: Its molecular geometry is square planar.
choice*: It is nonpolar even though each Xe–F bond is polar.
choice*: Xenon is $sp^3d^2$ hybridized.
choice: It has a net dipole moment because of the two lone pairs on xenon.
choice: Xenon obeys the octet rule in this molecule.
hint: Count the electron groups around xenon, including lone pairs.
hint: Six electron groups give an octahedral electron geometry; place the two lone pairs opposite each other.
solution: Xe has four bonds and two lone pairs, so six electron groups, octahedral electron geometry, $sp^3d^2$ hybridization, and a square planar shape with the lone pairs on opposite sides. The bond dipoles cancel by symmetry, so the molecule is nonpolar. Xe has $12$ electrons around it, an expanded octet.
