@lesson
title: Using your five senses
summary: Use sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch safely to describe things you observe.
objective: Name the five senses and the body part for each one.
objective: Describe an object with words from your senses.
objective: Know that you never taste or smell an unknown thing without a grown-up.
reading: NASA Space Place: look up "What is a scientist?" or any picture story about observing, and ask your grown-up to read it with you.

@article
Scientists learn by **observing**. To observe means to look closely and notice things. You have five **senses** that help you observe.

- **Sight:** you see with your eyes. You can see color, shape and size.
- **Hearing:** you hear with your ears. Sounds can be loud or quiet.
- **Smell:** you smell with your nose. A flower may smell sweet.
- **Taste:** you taste with your tongue. Lemon tastes sour. Honey tastes sweet.
- **Touch:** you feel with your skin, mostly your hands. Things can be soft, rough, smooth, hot or cold.

Use describing words. Do not just say "a rock." Say "a gray, rough, hard rock."

### Stay safe

Always ask your grown-up first. Never taste or smell something unless your grown-up says it is food or is safe. Never touch something hot or sharp.

@example
title: Observe an orange
problem: Your grown-up gives you an orange. What can you observe with your senses?
step: Look at it. It is round and orange. That is sight.
step: Hold it. The peel is bumpy and a little cool. That is touch.
step: Your grown-up peels it. It smells fresh and sweet. That is smell. Taste a piece: it is juicy and a little sour. That is taste.

@mistakes
- Tasting something before your grown-up says it is safe.
- Saying only "nice" or "good" instead of a describing word such as "smooth" or "loud."
