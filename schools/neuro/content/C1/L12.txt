@article
The **central dogma** states that information flows from DNA to RNA to protein: **transcription** copies a gene into RNA and **translation** reads mRNA to make a polypeptide.

### Transcription
**RNA polymerase** binds a **promoter**, unwinds DNA, and builds RNA 5' to 3' using the **template (antisense) strand**; the RNA is therefore identical in sequence to the coding (sense) strand, with U for T. Bacteria use one polymerase with a sigma factor. Eukaryotes use RNA polymerase II for mRNA, with general transcription factors that bind the TATA box. Termination follows a terminator sequence.

### Eukaryotic RNA processing
The primary transcript is modified before leaving the nucleus: a **5' cap** (7-methylguanosine), a **poly-A tail** at the 3' end and **splicing** by the spliceosome, which removes **introns** and joins **exons**. Alternative splicing lets one gene encode several proteins. Bacterial mRNA is not spliced, and translation begins before transcription ends.

### The genetic code
Codons are triplets of mRNA nucleotides. There are $4^3 = 64$ codons: 61 specify amino acids and 3 are stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA). **AUG** codes for methionine and is the usual start codon. The code is **degenerate** (several codons per amino acid), nearly universal and non-overlapping. Some codons used below: AUG Met, UUU and UUC Phe, GGC Gly, AAA and AAG Lys, UGC Cys, GAG and GAA Glu, GUG Val, UAA stop.

### Translation
Ribosomes (large and small subunits plus rRNA) read mRNA in the 5' to 3' direction. **Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases** attach amino acids to their tRNAs; each tRNA's **anticodon** pairs with a codon. The ribosome has **A** (aminoacyl), **P** (peptidyl) and **E** (exit) sites. In **initiation**, the small subunit finds the start codon and the initiator tRNA binds; **elongation** brings each aminoacyl-tRNA into the A site, forms a peptide bond and translocates the ribosome; **termination** occurs when a stop codon is reached and release factors free the chain. The polypeptide is made from N terminus to C terminus. A protein of $n$ amino acids needs a coding sequence of $3n + 3$ nucleotides including the stop codon.

### Mutations
- **Silent:** a codon changes but codes for the same amino acid (often the third position).
- **Missense:** a different amino acid. Sickle cell disease: GAG (Glu) to GUG (Val) in $\beta$-globin.
- **Nonsense:** a codon becomes a stop codon, truncating the protein.
- **Frameshift:** insertion or deletion of nucleotides not a multiple of 3 shifts the reading frame, usually producing a garbled, short protein.
- **In-frame insertion or deletion** of a multiple of 3 adds or removes amino acids (cystic fibrosis $\Delta$F508 deletes three nucleotides, removing one phenylalanine).

**To predict the effect of a mutation on a protein:** write the mRNA, split it into codons from the start codon, apply the change, translate both versions, and classify the result.

### Regulating gene expression
In bacteria, genes with related functions form an **operon**. In the **lac operon**, the repressor (encoded by *lacI*) binds the operator and blocks transcription when lactose is absent; allolactose binds the repressor and releases it. Even then, transcription is high only when glucose is low, because cAMP-CAP activates the promoter. Loss of the repressor's DNA binding gives **constitutive** expression.

Eukaryotes add more layers: **transcription factors** and **enhancers** (bound by activators that loop to the promoter), **chromatin structure** (histone acetylation opens chromatin and activates genes; DNA methylation at CpG islands silences genes), alternative splicing, **microRNAs** and siRNA that bind mRNA to block translation or trigger degradation, and control of mRNA and protein stability.

@example
title: Predicting the effect of point mutations
problem: A short mRNA reads 5'-AUG UUU GGC AAA UGA-3'. Predict the effect of (a) GGC to UGC, (b) AAA to UAA, and (c) deletion of the first U of UUU.
step: Translate the normal mRNA: AUG (Met), UUU (Phe), GGC (Gly), AAA (Lys), then UGA (stop): Met-Phe-Gly-Lys.
step: (a) GGC to UGC changes Gly to Cys: a missense mutation, one residue different, effect depends on the role of that residue.
step: (b) AAA to UAA creates a stop at codon 4: a nonsense mutation giving the truncated peptide Met-Phe-Gly.
step: (c) Deleting one nucleotide shifts the frame: AUG UUG GCA AAU GA... gives Met-Leu-Ala-Asn and the original stop is no longer read in frame. This frameshift usually destroys protein function.
@mistakes
- Using the template strand sequence as if it were the mRNA; the mRNA matches the coding strand with U for T.
- Calling any single-base change a frameshift; only insertions or deletions that are not multiples of 3 shift the frame.
- Forgetting to count the stop codon when converting coding length to amino acids.
- Thinking that every point mutation changes the protein; silent mutations do not.

@practice self
prompt: An mRNA reads 5'-AUG GAG AAA UUU UAA-3'. Translate it, then predict and classify the effect on the protein of: (a) GAG to GUG, (b) GAG to GAA, (c) AAA to UAA, (d) insertion of one extra G immediately after the A of the start codon.
hint: Translate the normal mRNA first (AUG Met, GAG Glu, AAA Lys, UUU Phe, UAA stop), then re-read each mutated version from the start codon.
solution: Normal: Met-Glu-Lys-Phe. (a) GUG is Val: missense; Glu replaced by Val. (b) GAA is also Glu: silent; protein unchanged. (c) UAA is a stop at codon 3: nonsense; truncated peptide Met-Glu. (d) Inserting a G after the first nucleotide gives AGU GGA GAA AUU UUA A: this shifts the frame (Ser-Gly-Glu-Ile-Leu and so on), so it is a frameshift and the start codon is also destroyed, so the normal protein is not made.
rubric: Gives the correct normal translation Met-Glu-Lys-Phe.
rubric: Classifies (a) missense, (b) silent, (c) nonsense and (d) frameshift.
rubric: States the consequence for the protein in each case (changed residue, unchanged, truncated, scrambled).
