@article
All cells have a plasma membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes and DNA. **Prokaryotes** (bacteria and archaea) lack a membrane-bound nucleus and have a nucleoid, 70S ribosomes and usually a cell wall. **Eukaryotes** compartmentalize their work in membrane-bound **organelles**, and their ribosomes are 80S. Compartments let a cell keep incompatible reactions apart, such as digestion of macromolecules and synthesis of proteins.

### Why cells are small
A cell exchanges materials across its surface, but its needs scale with its volume. For a cube of side $L$, surface area is $6L^2$ and volume is $L^3$, so
$$\frac{\text{SA}}{V} = \frac{6}{L}.$$
Doubling a cell's linear size halves the ratio. Large cells cope by folding membranes (intestinal microvilli) or by being elongated or flat.

### The nucleus and ribosomes
The **nucleus** is surrounded by a double membrane, the nuclear envelope, with nuclear pores that regulate traffic. Chromatin (DNA plus histones) is inside, and the **nucleolus** makes rRNA and assembles ribosomal subunits. The nuclear lamina, made of lamins, supports the envelope; mutations in lamin A cause progeria. Ribosomes, free or bound to the rough ER, translate mRNA.

### The endomembrane system
- **Rough ER:** ribosomes make proteins destined for secretion, membranes or lysosomes; folding is monitored by quality control, and misfolded proteins are sent for degradation. Smooth ER makes lipids, detoxifies drugs and stores Ca$^{2+}$.
- **Golgi apparatus:** modifies, sorts and ships proteins. It adds mannose-6-phosphate to lysosomal enzymes so they are routed to lysosomes.
- **Lysosomes:** acidic (about pH 5) organelles with hydrolases that digest macromolecules and worn-out organelles.
- **Vesicles** carry cargo between compartments and to the plasma membrane.

A secreted protein travels ribosome, rough ER, Golgi, secretory vesicle, plasma membrane.

### Energy and detox organelles
**Mitochondria** have an outer and a highly folded inner membrane (cristae), carry their own circular DNA and ribosomes, and make most ATP (week 5). They are inherited maternally. **Peroxisomes** break down very-long-chain fatty acids and detoxify hydrogen peroxide with catalase. Chloroplasts of plants and algae carry out photosynthesis (week 6). Mitochondria and chloroplasts are explained by **endosymbiosis**: they descend from engulfed bacteria, which is why they have double membranes and their own DNA.

### The cytoskeleton
Three polymers give shape and movement: **microfilaments** (actin; cell crawling, cleavage furrow), **intermediate filaments** (keratin, lamins; tensile strength) and **microtubules** (tubulin; mitotic spindle, vesicle transport by kinesin and dynein, cilia and flagella with a 9+2 arrangement).

### Organelle failure and disease
| Organelle | Failure | Disease |
|---|---|---|
| Lysosome | Missing hexosaminidase A | Tay-Sachs disease |
| Golgi/lysosomal targeting | Enzymes not tagged with mannose-6-phosphate | I-cell disease |
| Peroxisome | Failure to import enzymes or oxidize very-long-chain fatty acids | Zellweger syndrome, X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy |
| Mitochondrion | mtDNA mutations | MELAS, Leber hereditary optic neuropathy |
| Cilium (axoneme) | Defective dynein arms | Primary ciliary dyskinesia (Kartagener syndrome) |
| Nuclear lamina | Lamin A mutation | Hutchinson-Gilford progeria |
| Endoplasmic reticulum | Misfolded CFTR retained and degraded | Cystic fibrosis ($\Delta$F508) |

@example
title: Surface area to volume as a cell grows
problem: A cubic cell has a side of 2 $\mu$m. It grows until its side is 4 $\mu$m. By what factor do its surface area, volume and SA/V ratio change?
step: Surface area is $6L^2$. It goes from $6(2)^2 = 24\ \mu\text{m}^2$ to $6(4)^2 = 96\ \mu\text{m}^2$, a factor of $4$.
step: Volume is $L^3$. It goes from $8\ \mu\text{m}^3$ to $64\ \mu\text{m}^3$, a factor of $8$.
step: The ratio goes from $24/8 = 3$ to $96/64 = 1.5\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$, so it halves. Volume outruns surface, which limits cell size.
@example
title: Following a secreted protein
problem: Insulin is made by pancreatic beta cells and released into blood. List the organelles it passes through in order.
step: Synthesis begins on free ribosomes, but the signal sequence directs the ribosome to the rough ER, where the protein enters the lumen.
step: Vesicles carry the folded protein to the Golgi apparatus, which modifies and sorts it.
step: Secretory vesicles bud from the Golgi and fuse with the plasma membrane (exocytosis), releasing insulin. Lysosomes are not on this route.
@mistakes
- Saying prokaryotes have no ribosomes; they have 70S ribosomes, and eukaryotes have 80S.
- Placing lysosomal enzyme tagging in the lysosome; the Golgi adds the mannose-6-phosphate tag.
- Assuming a larger cell is better at exchange; SA/V falls as size grows.
- Mixing up microtubules (tubulin, spindle, cilia) with intermediate filaments (keratin, lamins).

@practice self

hint: Start from what each organelle normally does, then ask which disease arises when that job fails.
solution: (a) Mitochondrion: MELAS or Leber hereditary optic neuropathy, from mutations in mitochondrial DNA that impair oxidative phosphorylation. (d) Golgi apparatus: I-cell disease, in which lysosomal enzymes lack the mannose-6-phosphate tag and are secreted instead of reaching lysosomes. (e) Cilium: primary ciliary dyskinesia (Kartagener syndrome), from defective dynein arms so cilia cannot beat.
