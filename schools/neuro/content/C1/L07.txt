@article
Cells signal to coordinate growth, metabolism and survival. By distance, signals are **autocrine** (acting on the secreting cell), **paracrine** (nearby cells), **endocrine** (hormones through the blood) or by **direct contact** (gap junctions, or membrane-bound ligands acting on a neighbor, called juxtacrine signaling). Signaling has three stages: **reception** (a ligand binds a receptor), **transduction** (a relay inside the cell) and **response** (a change in enzyme activity, gene expression or behavior).

### Receptors
- **Intracellular receptors** bind lipid-soluble ligands that cross the membrane (steroid hormones, thyroid hormone, vitamin D). The complex acts as a transcription factor, so the response is slow (hours).
- **Ligand-gated ion channels** open when a ligand binds (the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor); the response takes milliseconds.
- **G-protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs)** have seven transmembrane helices and are the largest receptor family and a major drug target.
- **Receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs)** dimerize and autophosphorylate on binding growth factors; they feed the Ras pathway you will trace in week 8.

### Receptor binding
For a ligand $L$ binding one receptor site with dissociation constant $K_d$, the fraction of occupied receptors is
$$\text{fraction bound} = \frac{[L]}{K_d + [L]}.$$
A low $K_d$ means high affinity; half the receptors are occupied when $[L] = K_d$.

### GPCR signaling
Ligand binding changes the receptor so it acts as a guanine nucleotide exchange factor for a heterotrimeric **G protein**. The G$\alpha$ subunit swaps GDP for GTP and dissociates from G$\beta\gamma$; it is active until its intrinsic GTPase hydrolyzes GTP back to GDP.
- **G$_s$** activates **adenylyl cyclase**, making **cAMP** from ATP; cAMP activates **protein kinase A (PKA)**, which phosphorylates target proteins (in liver, epinephrine through PKA promotes glycogen breakdown). G$_i$ inhibits adenylyl cyclase.
- **G$_q$** activates **phospholipase C**, which cleaves PIP$_2$ into **IP$_3$** and **DAG**. IP$_3$ opens Ca$^{2+}$ channels on the endoplasmic reticulum, raising cytosolic Ca$^{2+}$ (which acts through calmodulin); DAG activates **protein kinase C**.

cAMP, IP$_3$, DAG and Ca$^{2+}$ are **second messengers**: small molecules that spread the signal inside the cell.

### Amplification
Each step in a cascade is catalytic: one active enzyme makes many products. So a few ligand molecules can produce a huge response. The total amplification is the product of the multiplication at each step.

### Termination
Signals must be switched off: G$\alpha$ hydrolyzes its GTP; **phosphodiesterase** converts cAMP to AMP (caffeine inhibits it); **phosphatases** reverse kinases; receptors are internalized or desensitized. Toxins show what happens when termination fails: **cholera toxin** locks G$_s$ in the active state (persistent cAMP in intestinal cells causes secretory diarrhea); **pertussis toxin** blocks G$_i$.

@example
title: Receptor occupancy
problem: A hormone receptor has $K_d = 5$ nM. What fraction of receptors is occupied at a free hormone concentration of 15 nM?
step: Use fraction bound $= [L]/(K_d + [L])$.
step: Substitute: $15/(5 + 15) = 15/20$.
step: The fraction is $0.75$, so 75% of receptors are occupied. Check: at $[L] = K_d$ it would be $0.5$, and more ligand gives more occupancy.
@example
title: Tracing and amplifying a G$_s$ signal
problem: Epinephrine binds a $\beta$-adrenergic receptor on a liver cell. Describe the sequence to the response, and find the amplification if one receptor activates 10 G proteins and each G protein's adenylyl cyclase makes 100 cAMP before shutoff.
step: Epinephrine binds the receptor, which activates G$_s$ by GDP/GTP exchange.
step: G$\alpha_s$-GTP activates adenylyl cyclase, which makes cAMP; cAMP activates PKA, which phosphorylates enzymes to mobilize glucose.
step: Amplification at these two steps is $10 \times 100 = 1000$ cAMP per activated receptor, before the kinase steps multiply it further.
@mistakes
- Saying second messengers are the ligands; the ligand is the "first messenger," and the second messenger forms inside the cell.
- Confusing G$_s$ (stimulates adenylyl cyclase) with G$_i$ (inhibits it).
- Forgetting that signal termination (GTP hydrolysis, phosphodiesterase, phosphatases) is as important as activation.
- Assuming all hormones need a membrane receptor; steroid hormones act on intracellular receptors.

@practice self
prompt: Trace the pathway from a G$_q$-coupled receptor ligand to a rise in cytosolic Ca$^{2+}$, then name three ways such a signal is switched off.
hint: Start with the receptor and G protein, then phospholipase C and PIP$_2$.
solution: The ligand activates the receptor, which exchanges GDP for GTP on G$\alpha_q$. G$\alpha_q$-GTP activates phospholipase C, which cleaves PIP$_2$ into IP$_3$ and DAG. IP$_3$ binds channels on the ER and releases stored Ca$^{2+}$ into the cytosol; DAG and Ca$^{2+}$ activate protein kinase C. Termination: G$\alpha$ hydrolyzes GTP to GDP and reassociates with G$\beta\gamma$; Ca$^{2+}$ is pumped back into the ER or out of the cell; IP$_3$ is dephosphorylated; the receptor is phosphorylated and internalized; phosphatases reverse the kinases.

rubric: Identifies IP$_3$ as acting on the ER and DAG as activating protein kinase C.
rubric: Names at least three correct termination mechanisms.
