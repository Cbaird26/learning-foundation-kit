@article
Angles are measured in degrees. A full turn is $360^\circ$, a straight line is $180^\circ$, and a right angle is $90^\circ$. This lesson shows how to find a missing angle from the ones you know.

### Pairs of angles

- **Complementary** angles add up to $90^\circ$. If one is $35^\circ$, the other is $90 - 35 = 55^\circ$.
- **Supplementary** angles add up to $180^\circ$. If one is $128^\circ$, the other is $180 - 128 = 52^\circ$. Two angles that form a straight line (a **linear pair**) are supplementary.
- **Vertical** angles are the opposite angles formed when two lines cross. They are **equal**. If one is $70^\circ$, the angle across from it is also $70^\circ$, and the two angles next to them are each $110^\circ$.

### Angles in a triangle

The three angles inside any triangle add up to $180^\circ$:

$$a + b + c = 180^\circ.$$

You can see why by tearing off the three corners of a paper triangle and putting them side by side: they form a straight line. If two angles are $48^\circ$ and $67^\circ$, the third is $180 - 48 - 67 = 65^\circ$.

### Special triangles

- An **isosceles** triangle has two equal sides, and the angles opposite them are equal.
- An **equilateral** triangle has three equal sides and three $60^\circ$ angles.
- A triangle has at most one right angle or one angle larger than $90^\circ$, since two such angles would already use $180^\circ$.

### Angle equations

Often an angle is given as an expression like $3x + 10$. Use the relationship to write an equation, then solve it with the two-step methods you learned earlier. If vertical angles are $3x + 10$ and $70$, then $3x + 10 = 70$, so $x = 20$.

### Can three lengths make a triangle?

The **triangle inequality** says that any two sides together must be longer than the third. Sides $3$, $4$, $5$ work because $3 + 4 > 5$. Sides $4$, $5$, $10$ fail since $4 + 5 = 9$, which is less than $10$: the two short sides cannot reach each other. You only need to test the two shorter sides against the longest.

### Drawing with given conditions

If you know three side lengths that satisfy the triangle inequality, there is one possible triangle shape. If you know three angles, there are many triangles of different sizes. Always check that the angles add to $180^\circ$.

@example
title: Finding a base angle
problem: An isosceles triangle has a top (apex) angle of $44^\circ$. Find the two equal base angles.
step: The angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$, so the two base angles together measure $180 - 44 = 136^\circ$.
step: The base angles are equal, so each one is $\frac{136}{2} = 68^\circ$.
step: Check: $44 + 68 + 68 = 180$.

@mistakes
- Mixing up complementary ($90^\circ$) and supplementary ($180^\circ$).
- Thinking vertical angles are supplementary. They are equal.
- Testing the triangle inequality with the wrong pairs, for example checking $4 + 10 > 5$ only. Always compare the two shorter sides with the longest.

@practice num
prompt: Two lines cross. One of the angles measures $(3x + 10)^\circ$ and the angle vertical to it measures $70^\circ$. Find $x$.
answer: 20
check: root(lambda x: 3*x + 10 - 70, -100, 100)
hint: Vertical angles are equal, so set the two expressions equal.
solution: $3x + 10 = 70$, so $3x = 60$ and $x = 20$. Check: $3(20) + 10 = 70$.

@quiz num
prompt: The angles of a triangle measure $x^\circ$, $2x^\circ$ and $3x^\circ$. Find $x$.
answer: 30
check: 180 / (1 + 2 + 3)
hint: Write an equation that says the three angles add to $180$.
solution: $x + 2x + 3x = 6x = 180$, so $x = 30$. The angles are $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $90^\circ$.

@quiz num
prompt: Two supplementary angles measure $(2x + 20)^\circ$ and $(x + 10)^\circ$. Find $x$.
answer: 50
check: root(lambda x: (2*x + 20) + (x + 10) - 180, -100, 100)
hint: Supplementary angles add to $180^\circ$. Write and solve the equation.
solution: $3x + 30 = 180$, so $3x = 150$ and $x = 50$. The angles are $120^\circ$ and $60^\circ$.
