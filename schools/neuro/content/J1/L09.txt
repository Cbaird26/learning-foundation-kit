@article
**Area** measures how much flat surface a shape covers, in square units such as $\text{cm}^2$ or $\text{ft}^2$. A rectangle with length $l$ and width $w$ has area $A = lw$. Everything else in this lesson comes from that fact.

### Parallelograms

A **parallelogram** has two pairs of parallel sides. Its **height** is the perpendicular distance between the parallel bases. It is **not** the slanted side. Cut a right triangle off one end and slide it to the other end, and the parallelogram becomes a rectangle with the same base and height. So

$$A = bh.$$

### Triangles

Two copies of any triangle fit together to make a parallelogram with the same base and height. So a triangle has half the area:

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2}bh.$$

Again, the height must be perpendicular to the base. For a triangle with base $10$ and height $7$, the area is $\frac{1}{2}(10)(7) = 35$ square units.

### Composite shapes

A **composite shape** is made of simpler shapes. Two strategies:

- **Add:** split the shape into rectangles and triangles, find each area, and add.
- **Subtract:** find the area of a larger shape and subtract any piece that was cut out.

Always label each piece and write its area, so that you can check your work.

### Working backward

If you know the area and one dimension, solve for the other. A triangle of area $24$ with base $8$ satisfies $\frac{1}{2}(8)h = 24$, so $4h = 24$ and $h = 6$.

### Units

Area is in **square** units. A square with sides of $5$ cm has area $5 \times 5 = 25\ \text{cm}^2$, because both dimensions are measured in centimeters. Make sure all lengths use the same unit before multiplying.

@example
title: A house-shaped composite figure
problem: A shape is a rectangle $12$ cm wide and $7$ cm tall with a triangle on top. The triangle has base $12$ cm (the top side of the rectangle) and height $5$ cm. Find the total area.
step: Rectangle: $12 \times 7 = 84\ \text{cm}^2$.
step: Triangle: $\frac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 5 = 30\ \text{cm}^2$.
step: Add the pieces: $84 + 30 = 114$. The area is $114\ \text{cm}^2$.

@mistakes
- Using the slanted side of a parallelogram or triangle as the height instead of the perpendicular height.
- Forgetting the factor $\frac{1}{2}$ for a triangle.
- Adding the area of a cut-out piece instead of subtracting it.

@practice num
prompt: A parallelogram has base $9$ cm and height $4$ cm. What is its area in square centimeters?
answer: 36
check: 9 * 4
unit: cm^2
hint: Area of a parallelogram is base times height.
hint: Multiply $9$ by $4$.
solution: $A = bh = 9 \times 4 = 36\ \text{cm}^2$.

@practice num
prompt: A triangle has base $10$ m and height $7$ m. What is its area in square meters?
answer: 35
check: 0.5 * 10 * 7
unit: m^2
hint: Use $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$.
hint: $10 \times 7 = 70$. Take half of it.
solution: $A = \frac{1}{2}(10)(7) = 35\ \text{m}^2$.

unit: ft^2
hint: Find the area of each rectangle separately.
hint: $8 \times 5 = 40$ and $3 \times 4 = 12$.

@quiz num
prompt: A $10$ m by $6$ m rectangular garden has a $4$ m by $3$ m rectangular pond cut out of it. What is the area of the garden that remains, in square meters?
answer: 48
check: 10 * 6 - 4 * 3
unit: m^2
hint: Find the area of the whole rectangle, then remove the pond.
hint: $10 \times 6 = 60$ and $4 \times 3 = 12$.
solution: $60 - 12 = 48\ \text{m}^2$.
