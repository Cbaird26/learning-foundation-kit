### Week by week

- **Weeks 1–2: equilibrium and resting potentials.** The Nernst equation $E = (RT/zF)\ln([\text{out}]/[\text{in}])$ (26.7 mV for $RT/F$ at 37 °C). The Goldman–Hodgkin–Katz voltage equation weights each ion by its permeability, with the anion's inside concentration on top. The resting potential is a conductance-weighted mean, $V = \sum g_i E_i/\sum g_i$.
- **Weeks 3–4: passive membrane and cable.** $\tau\,dV/dt = -(V - E_L) + RI$ with $\tau = RC$; exponential approach; low-pass filter with $f_c = 1/(2\pi\tau)$; $R = R_m/A$, $C = C_m A$. The cable equation has $\lambda = \sqrt{R_m d/(4R_i)}$ and steady-state decay $e^{-x/\lambda}$; compartment models with Euler or tridiagonal solutions.
- **Weeks 5–6: Hodgkin–Huxley and the spike.** $g_\text{Na} = \bar g m^3 h$, $g_\text{K} = \bar g n^4$, $x_\infty = \alpha/(\alpha + \beta)$, $\tau_x = 1/(\alpha + \beta)$. Threshold, all-or-none firing, refractoriness from $h$ and $n$, propagation by local circuit currents.
- **Weeks 7–8: myelin and synapses.** $v \propto \sqrt{d}$ without myelin and $\propto d$ with it; safety factor $A e^{-L/\lambda}/\theta$. Quantal release: binomial $\mu = npq$, $\sigma^2 = np(1-p)q^2$; method of failures $m = \ln(N/N_0)$; variance–mean $\sigma^2 = q\mu - \mu^2/n$.
- **Weeks 9–10: integration and modulation.** Double-exponential EPSP, temporal summation, sublinear summation of conductances, shunting inhibition; G-protein cascades, Hill responses, effects of a closed K+ conductance on $R$ and $\tau$.
- **Weeks 11–12: molecules and disease.** $I = Np\gamma(V - E)$, Boltzmann gating with $k = RT/(z_gF)$, pumps and KCC2 (setting $E_\text{Cl}$), expression as production and decay; channelopathies, Hill block and use-dependent block.

### Common errors

Sign of $z$ for anions; MΩ × pA giving µV; $\tau$ and cell size; $d$ in cm in $\lambda$; singular points of the rate functions; updating variables one after another; dividing by $n-1$ or mixing seconds and milliseconds; confusing inactivation with closing by repolarization; calling a reduced response presynaptic without checking $q$.

model:
```python
import math


def expected_epsp(n, p, q, x_um, lam_um):
    """Mean somatic EPSP and CV of a binomial synapse on a passive dendrite."""
    if p == 0:
        return 0.0, None
    mean_local = n * p * q
    mean_soma = mean_local * math.exp(-x_um / lam_um)
    cv = math.sqrt((1.0 - p) / (n * p))
    return mean_soma, cv
```
hint: Combine the binomial mean $npq$ with the steady-state attenuation $e^{-x/\lambda}$; the CV depends only on $n$ and $p$.
solution: The binomial mean is $npq$ and attenuation is a multiplicative factor, so the somatic mean is $npq\,e^{-x/\lambda}$. Because the CV is the ratio of the standard deviation to the mean and both are multiplied by the same attenuation factor, it is unchanged. This ties week 4 (cable) to week 8 (quantal analysis): a distant synapse looks smaller at the soma without looking more variable.

unit: mV

@exam mc
prompt: A squid-axon preparation is voltage-clamped at $0$ mV from a holding potential of $-65$ mV, and the current is recorded as a function of time. Tetrodotoxin, which blocks voltage-gated Na+ channels, is then added. Which part of the current record is abolished?
choice*: The early transient inward current, leaving a delayed outward K+ current.
choice: The late outward current, leaving a transient inward Na+ current.
choice: The capacitive current at the start of the step, leaving both ionic currents.
choice: All of the current, because no ionic current flows without Na+ channels.
hint: Which conductance activates quickly and inactivates, and which one activates with a delay and stays?
solution: Na+ current is carried by $m^3h$: it activates within a fraction of a millisecond and then inactivates, producing an early inward current (at $0$ mV, $E_\text{Na}$ is $+50$ mV so Na+ flows in). The K+ current, carried by $n^4$, activates with a delay and persists, so it remains after tetrodotoxin. The capacitive transient is not ionic and persists too. Blocking K+ channels with tetraethylammonium would remove the late outward current instead.
