@article
Most theories of consciousness start from the brain and ask what it does when we are conscious. **Integrated information theory** (IIT) starts from experience itself. Introduced by Tononi (2004) and developed through versions 3.0 (Oizumi, Albantakis and Tononi, 2014) and 4.0 (Albantakis and colleagues, 2023), it proposes to identify, from the essential properties of every experience, the properties a physical system must have to support it.

### The method and the identity claim

IIT begins with axioms about experience, claimed to be self-evident: experience exists intrinsically, for itself; it is structured; it is specific, being this experience and not another; it is unified, not reducible to independent parts; and it is definite, with borders. From these it derives **postulates** about the physical substrate, stated in terms of **cause-effect power**: the system must have a repertoire of causes and effects over its own states (information), which is irreducible to that of its parts (integration), and the relevant system is the one for which this irreducibility is maximal (exclusion). The quantity measuring irreducibility is $\Phi$.

The theory's central claim is an **identity**: an experience is identical to the cause-effect structure of a physical substrate that is maximally irreducible, and its quality is that structure's form. Consciousness is not computed by the system or correlated with it but is its intrinsic causal structure. Several consequences follow.

- Consciousness depends on physical causal structure, not on the function or computation carried out. A system with the same input-output behavior but a feedforward structure has $\Phi = 0$. A digital simulation of a brain on a conventional computer is predicted to have very little consciousness, whatever it simulates.
- The substrate can be small: a complex within the brain, not the whole brain. The cerebellum, though it holds most of the brain's neurons, has a largely modular, feedforward-like organization and is predicted to contribute little; this fits the observation that cerebellar damage does not abolish experience.
- Consciousness varies in degree with $\Phi$ and with the richness of the structure, which is meant to explain why sleep and anesthesia reduce it.

### A toy integration measure

Computing $\Phi$ for realistic systems is intractable because the number of partitions grows exponentially, and the full definition uses cause and effect repertoires and a search for the minimum-information partition. To see the idea, consider a simplified measure for two binary units A and B whose next states are determined by their current states.

The **effective information** of the whole is the mutual information, in bits, between the current state, drawn uniformly from all four states, and the next state; because the update is deterministic, it is the entropy of the next-state distribution. For a part, say A, replace the input from B by independent noise and compute the mutual information between A's current and next state. The **toy integration** is $\mathrm{EI}_{\text{whole}} - \mathrm{EI}_A - \mathrm{EI}_B$. This is not $\Phi$, and it is not the quantity IIT 4.0 defines; it only shows how a whole can carry more causal information than its parts do separately.

| system | whole EI (bits) | $\mathrm{EI}_A$ | $\mathrm{EI}_B$ | toy integration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| swap: A$'$ = B, B$'$ = A | 2 | 0 | 0 | 2 |
| independent copies: A$'$ = A, B$'$ = B | 2 | 1 | 1 | 0 |

In the swap system each unit is determined entirely by the other, so cutting the link destroys all the information; in the independent system the whole is just the sum of its parts.

### Evidence and its limits

Evidence for IIT is largely indirect: the cerebellum, sleep and anesthesia fit its predictions, and the perturbational complexity index (Casali and colleagues, 2013), which measures the complexity of the brain's response to a magnetic pulse, was developed on ideas that include integration and differentiation and separates conscious from unconscious states in several conditions. Critics note that other theories predict some of these as well. In the adversarial collaboration reported in 2025 (week 10), IIT predictions about sustained synchronization within posterior cortex were not supported; what this shows for the theory is debated, with proponents arguing that the tested predictions were not central.

### Criticisms and replies

1. **Intractability and approximation.** $\Phi$ cannot be computed for brains, so tests use proxies, whose relation to the theory is debated.
2. **Counterintuitive implications.** Simple systems with the right structure, such as certain lattices, would have high $\Phi$, and a precise copy of a brain on a digital computer would not be conscious. Defenders accept these as consequences of a theory that starts from experience, and they argue that intuitions should not override a derivation; critics take them as reductios.
3. **The axioms.** Critics doubt that the axioms are self-evident or that physical postulates follow from them; defenders say that this is the strength of a principled theory.
4. **Scientific status.** In September 2023 an open letter signed by more than a hundred researchers called IIT pseudoscience, arguing that the claims about its empirical support were overstated and that its core identity claim cannot be tested. Many other researchers rejected that label, pointing to its testable predictions and to the publication of adversarial tests. The disagreement concerns where the line lies between a testable core, an untestable core and testable auxiliary predictions.

@example
title: Toy integration of a system with a shared XOR
problem: Two binary units update as A$'$ = A XOR B and B$'$ = B. Compute the whole effective information, the part effective informations, and the toy integration.
step: Whole: the current states (A,B) = (0,0), (0,1), (1,0), (1,1) go to the next states (0,0), (1,1), (1,0), (0,1). These are four different next states, so the next-state entropy is $\log_2 4 = 2$ bits.
step: Part A: with B replaced by uniform noise, A$'$ = A XOR noise is uniform whatever A is, so A$'$ carries no information about A: $\mathrm{EI}_A = 0$.
step: Part B: B$'$ = B copies B, so with A as noise it is a perfect copy of a uniform bit: $\mathrm{EI}_B = 1$ bit.
step: Combine: $2 - 0 - 1 = 1$ bit.
answer: Whole 2 bits, parts 0 and 1 bit, toy integration 1 bit.

@mistakes
- Identifying the toy measure with $\Phi$. The theory's quantity involves cause and effect repertoires, a search over partitions of the whole system and the identification of a maximum, not a single bipartition of effective information.
- Saying that IIT holds consciousness to be computation. It holds that consciousness is intrinsic cause-effect structure, so identical computation on different hardware can differ.
- Reporting the 2023 letter as the consensus of the field. Many researchers signed it and many others rejected its label.

@practice self
prompt: In about 250 words, explain why IIT predicts that a feedforward network and a recurrent network with identical input-output behavior differ in consciousness, state the strongest defense of this consequence, and the strongest objection.
hint: Link the prediction to the identity claim about cause-effect structure, not to behavior.
solution: IIT identifies consciousness with the intrinsic cause-effect structure of a physical system, and integration requires that the system's parts constrain each other in both directions. In a purely feedforward network each layer's causes lie in the previous layer and its effects in the next, so any partition of the system into parts leaves the cause-effect structure unchanged and $\Phi$ is zero; a recurrent network with the same input-output mapping can have irreducible, maximally integrated structure and a positive $\Phi$. Because the identity claim concerns structure, not function, behavior cannot reveal the difference. The strongest defense is that a theory that starts from the essential properties of experience should be allowed to have implications that depart from functionalist intuition, just as physics departed from common sense; it also gives a principled reason why the cerebellum, which processes much information, contributes little, and why a simulation of a brain differs from a brain, as a simulated storm does not get anything wet. The strongest objection is that this makes consciousness undetectable by any behavioral or functional test: two systems alike in every way that can be observed from outside would differ in whether anything is experienced, so evidence for the theory could come only from correlates in humans, from which the extension to other systems is an extrapolation depending on the theory itself. Critics add that the axioms and the derivation of the postulates are then doing work that experiments cannot check.

@quiz self
prompt: Give, in about 250 words, the strongest case that IIT's core claims are scientifically testable and the strongest case that they are not, taking into account the 2023 open letter and the 2025 adversarial collaboration, without taking a side.
hint: Separate the identity claim, the axioms, and the predictions about neural correlates.
solution: The case that IIT is testable is that the theory has produced predictions about neural correlates and levels of consciousness: that experience tracks posterior cortical structure with high integration, that feedforward systems are not conscious, and that measures of integration and differentiation in neural responses should drop in unconscious states. Some of these have been tested, including in a preregistered adversarial collaboration published in 2025 whose results challenged a prediction about sustained synchronization within posterior cortex, and a theory that can be challenged by data is testable in the ordinary sense. The case that its core is not testable is that the axioms are offered as self-evident and the identity of experience with cause-effect structure cannot be checked by observation, since the only direct evidence of experience is a subject's report; $\Phi$ cannot be computed for brains, so experiments use proxies; and when a prediction fails, the theory can say that the proxy or the prediction was not central. In September 2023 an open letter signed by more than a hundred researchers called IIT pseudoscience on grounds of this kind, and many other researchers rejected the label, saying that every theory of consciousness has a core that is not tested directly and that IIT's testable predictions are what matter. The sharpest way to put the disagreement is to ask which results would count against the theory, and to ask proponents and critics to state this in advance. Where they agree on such a list, the theory is testable, and where they do not, the dispute is about methodology and not about the data.
