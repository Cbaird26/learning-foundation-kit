@article
A **fluid** is a liquid or gas: a substance that flows and takes the shape of its container. Fluids at rest are described by density and pressure.

### Density and pressure

**Density** is mass per volume, $\rho = m/V$, in kg/m$^3$. Fresh water is $1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$, seawater about $1025\ \text{kg/m}^3$, mercury $13\,600\ \text{kg/m}^3$, blood about $1060\ \text{kg/m}^3$ and ice $917\ \text{kg/m}^3$. **Pressure** is force per unit area perpendicular to a surface, $P = F/A$, in pascals ($1\ \text{Pa} = 1\ \text{N/m}^2$). Fluid pressure acts in all directions, not only downward.

Common units: standard atmospheric pressure is $P_{\text{atm}} = 1.013 \times 10^5\ \text{Pa}$, which equals $760\ \text{mmHg}$ (mm of mercury), so $1\ \text{mmHg} = 133.3\ \text{Pa}$. Blood pressure is reported in mmHg, usually as **gauge pressure**, meaning pressure above atmospheric.

### Pressure versus depth

In a fluid of constant density $\rho$, pressure increases with depth $h$:

$$P = P_0 + \rho g h,$$

where $P_0$ is the pressure at the surface and $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$. The term $\rho g h$ is the **gauge pressure** from the fluid. Pressure depends only on depth, not on the shape of the container or the amount of fluid. This explains why an IV bag hung higher above the patient pushes fluid in at a greater pressure, and why the pressure in your feet exceeds that at your head when standing.

### Pascal's principle

A pressure change applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished to every part of the fluid and to the container walls. For a hydraulic lift with input piston area $A_1$ and output piston area $A_2$:

$$\frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2}.$$

A small force on a small piston yields a large force on a large piston, but the small piston moves farther: work is conserved, $F_1 d_1 = F_2 d_2$.

### Buoyancy

An object in a fluid feels an upward **buoyant force** equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces (**Archimedes' principle**):

$$B = \rho_{\text{fluid}}\,V_{\text{displaced}}\,g.$$

A fully submerged object rises if its average density is less than the fluid's and sinks if greater. A floating object is in equilibrium with $B = W$, so the fraction submerged is

$$\frac{V_{\text{sub}}}{V} = \frac{\rho_{\text{object}}}{\rho_{\text{fluid}}}.$$

This is the principle behind hydrostatic weighing to estimate body fat (fat is less dense than lean tissue) and why ice floats with about $92\%$ of its volume below the surface of water. Use $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ and the densities above throughout.

@example
title: Pressure at depth
problem: Find the gauge pressure and absolute pressure at a depth of $10\ \text{m}$ in fresh water ($\rho = 1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$). Take $P_{\text{atm}} = 1.013 \times 10^5\ \text{Pa}$ and $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$.
step: Gauge pressure is the fluid term only: $\rho g h = (1000)(9.8)(10) = 9.8 \times 10^4\ \text{Pa}$.
step: Absolute pressure adds the atmosphere above the surface: $P = 1.013 \times 10^5 + 0.98 \times 10^5 = 1.993 \times 10^5\ \text{Pa}$.
step: Compare: this is about twice atmospheric pressure, so every $10$ m of water adds roughly one atmosphere.

@mistakes
- Using the absolute pressure when a problem asks for gauge pressure, or forgetting to add the atmosphere for absolute.
- Thinking pressure depends on the width or total volume of the container instead of just depth.
- Using the object's density rather than the fluid's in $B = \rho g V$.
- Confusing the mass of the displaced fluid with the mass of the object when the object floats.

@practice num
prompt: What is the gauge pressure at a depth of $2.0\ \text{m}$ in fresh water ($\rho = 1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$)? Use $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$. Give pascals.
answer: 19600
check: 1000*9.8*2.0
unit: Pa
hint: Gauge pressure is $\rho g h$.
solution: $P = (1000)(9.8)(2.0) = 19\,600\ \text{Pa}$.

@practice num
prompt: An IV bag of saline (density $1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$) hangs $1.0\ \text{m}$ above the patient's vein. Using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ and $1\ \text{mmHg} = 133.3\ \text{Pa}$, what gauge pressure does the height of fluid create at the vein, in mmHg? Give one decimal place.
answer: 73.5
check: 1000*9.8*1.0/133.3
tol: 0.2
unit: mmHg
hint: Find $\rho g h$ in pascals, then convert.
solution: $\rho g h = 9800\ \text{Pa}$; $9800/133.3 = 73.5\ \text{mmHg}$.

@practice mc
prompt: An object of density $917\ \text{kg/m}^3$ floats in fresh water. Which statement is correct?
choice: The buoyant force is greater than the object's weight
choice*: The buoyant force equals the object's weight and about 92% of the object is submerged
choice: The buoyant force equals the weight of the whole object's volume of water
choice: Less than half the object is submerged because it floats
hint: A floating object is in equilibrium.
solution: Floating means $B = W$. The fraction submerged equals $\rho_{\text{object}}/\rho_{\text{fluid}} = 0.917$.

@practice num
prompt: In a hydraulic press, a $200\ \text{N}$ force is applied to a piston of area $2.0\ \text{cm}^2$. The large piston has area $100\ \text{cm}^2$. What force does it exert, in newtons?
answer: 10000
check: 200*100/2.0
unit: N
hint: Pressure is the same on both pistons.
solution: $F_2 = F_1(A_2/A_1) = 200 \times 50 = 10\,000\ \text{N}$.

@practice num
prompt: A solid object of volume $2.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3$ is fully submerged in fresh water ($\rho = 1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$). Using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, what is the buoyant force on it, in newtons?
answer: 19.6
check: 1000*2.0e-3*9.8
tol: 0.05
unit: N
hint: Archimedes' principle: weight of the displaced fluid.
solution: $B = \rho V g = (1000)(2.0 \times 10^{-3})(9.8) = 19.6\ \text{N}$.

@quiz num
prompt: A diver is $15\ \text{m}$ below the surface of the sea (density $1025\ \text{kg/m}^3$). Using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, what is the gauge pressure from the water at that depth, in kPa? Give one decimal place.
answer: 150.7
check: 1025*9.8*15/1000
tol: 0.2
unit: kPa
hint: Use $\rho g h$ and convert pascals to kilopascals.
solution: $(1025)(9.8)(15) = 150\,675\ \text{Pa} = 150.7\ \text{kPa}$.

@quiz num
prompt: A blood pressure of $120\ \text{mmHg}$ (gauge) is equivalent to the pressure at the bottom of a column of water of what height? Use $1\ \text{mmHg} = 133.3\ \text{Pa}$, water density $1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$ and $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$. Give metres to two decimal places.
answer: 1.63
check: 120*133.3/(1000*9.8)
tol: 0.01
unit: m
hint: Convert to pascals first, then solve $P = \rho g h$ for $h$.
solution: $P = 120 \times 133.3 = 15\,996\ \text{Pa}$; $h = P/(\rho g) = 15\,996/9800 = 1.63\ \text{m}$.
