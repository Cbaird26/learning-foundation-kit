@project
title: Biomechanics report: forces in the elbow and lower back, and flow through a narrowed vessel
overview: You will write a short quantitative report that applies torque balance to a lifting task at the elbow and at the lower back, and then applies Poiseuille's law to estimate how a narrowing changes blood flow. All data are supplied below; use $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, blood viscosity $\eta = 3.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ and blood density $1060\ \text{kg/m}^3$.
goal: Compute muscle and joint forces for the elbow and lower back from free-body diagrams and torque balance.
goal: Compare the forces with the weight lifted and explain what the lever arms imply for injury risk.
goal: Use Poiseuille's law and continuity to predict the change in flow and speed when a vessel narrows.
step: Elbow data. A person holds an $8.0\ \text{kg}$ mass in the hand, forearm horizontal. The hand is $0.35\ \text{m}$ from the elbow, the forearm has mass $1.5\ \text{kg}$ with its centre of mass $0.15\ \text{m}$ from the elbow, and the biceps pulls vertically $0.04\ \text{m}$ from the elbow. Draw a free-body diagram of the forearm.
step: Write the torque equation about the elbow and solve for the biceps force. Then write the vertical force balance and solve for the joint force from the humerus. Express both as multiples of the $78.4$ N weight of the held mass.
step: Lower back data. A person bends forward and lifts a $15\ \text{kg}$ box held $0.35\ \text{m}$ horizontally from the lumbar pivot. The trunk, head and arms have mass $38\ \text{kg}$ with their centre of mass $0.20\ \text{m}$ from the pivot. The back muscles act $0.05\ \text{m}$ from the pivot. Treat all forces as vertical. Compute the muscle force, then repeat with the box held $0.15\ \text{m}$ from the pivot, and compare.
step: Vessel data. An artery segment has radius $2.0\ \text{mm}$, length $0.10\ \text{m}$ and a pressure difference of $600\ \text{Pa}$ along it. Compute the flow rate (in mL/s) and the mean speed. Then suppose atherosclerosis narrows the radius to $1.5\ \text{mm}$ with the same pressure difference and length; compute the new flow rate, the percent change in flow, and the new mean speed.
step: Compute the Reynolds number $\rho v D/\eta$ for the narrowed artery and state whether the flow is likely to be laminar. Note the limitations of the model (rigid straight tube, steady flow, newtonian fluid).
step: Write the report with a title, a diagram for each system, a table of results with units, and a paragraph of interpretation for each of the three parts.
deliverable: A written report of two to four pages with free-body diagrams, equations, substituted numbers and units.
deliverable: A table summarising muscle force, joint force, the vessel flow rates and the percent change.
deliverable: A half-page discussion of the clinical meaning: why lifting close to the body matters and why a small narrowing has a large effect on flow.
rubric: Free-body diagrams show every force with its point of application and chosen pivot.
rubric: Torque and force balance equations are correct and the numbers are correctly substituted with units.
rubric: The Poiseuille calculations use the radius to the fourth power and consistent SI units.
rubric: Results are checked for reasonableness (for example, comparing forces with body weight).
rubric: The discussion states the modelling assumptions and their limits.
tutor: Why is the biceps force so much larger than the weight in the hand?
tutor: How does moving a load closer to the spine change the torque the back muscles must provide?
tutor: How would the flow change if the pressure difference rose to compensate for the narrowing?

@review
### Review guide

**Week 1, kinematics.** Constant-acceleration equations ($v = v_0 + at$, $\Delta x = v_0t + \tfrac12at^2$, $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\Delta x$); free fall with $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$; projectile motion as independent horizontal (constant speed) and vertical (free fall) motions.

**Week 2, forces.** $\sum F = ma$ for each body; free-body diagrams show forces *on* one body; weight, normal, tension and friction $f_k = \mu_kN$; apparent weight in an elevator; two connected bodies share $a$.

**Week 3, energy.** $W = Fd\cos\theta$, $KE = \tfrac12mv^2$, $PE = mgh$, $PE_s = \tfrac12kx^2$, $P = W/t$; conservation of energy for falls.

**Week 4, momentum.** $\vec p = m\vec v$; impulse $= \Delta p$; momentum is conserved in all collisions without external forces, kinetic energy only in elastic ones.

**Week 5, rotation.** $\tau = rF\sin\theta$; equilibrium needs $\sum F = 0$ and $\sum\tau = 0$; $\tau = I\alpha$, $I = \sum mr^2$.

**Week 6, biomechanics.** The forearm is a third-class lever; the biceps force is large because its lever arm is short; the joint force follows from force balance.

**Week 7, fluids at rest.** $P = P_0 + \rho gh$; $1\ \text{mmHg} = 133.3\ \text{Pa}$; Pascal's principle; $B = \rho Vg$.

**Week 8, fluid flow.** Continuity $A_1v_1 = A_2v_2$; Bernoulli; Poiseuille $Q = \pi r^4\Delta P/(8\eta L)$; Reynolds number.

**Week 9, heat.** $Q = mc\Delta T$, $Q = mL$; calorimetry; four routes of heat transfer.

**Week 10, thermodynamics.** $\Delta U = Q - W$; efficiency; heat of exercise; sweat needed $= Pt/L_v$.

**Week 11, oscillations.** $T = 2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$, $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$; $v_{\max} = A\omega$; resonance.

**Week 12, waves and sound.** $v = f\lambda$; $\beta = 10\log_{10}(I/I_0)$; echo depth $vt/2$; Doppler shift.

### Self-check

- Can you draw a free-body diagram and write the equations without looking?
- Can you say which conservation law applies in a collision and why?
- Can you convert every quantity to SI units before substituting?

### Common errors

Using diameter instead of radius; forgetting the fourth power; applying energy conservation across an inelastic collision; mixing Celsius and kelvin in radiation; forgetting the factor of two in echo depth; mixing gauge and absolute pressure.

@exam num
prompt: A person holds a $6.0\ \text{kg}$ mass in the hand, $0.35\ \text{m}$ from the elbow, with the forearm horizontal. The forearm has mass $1.5\ \text{kg}$ with its centre of mass $0.15\ \text{m}$ from the elbow, and the biceps pulls vertically $0.04\ \text{m}$ from the elbow. Using $g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$, what is the biceps force in newtons?
answer: 569.6
check: (6.0*9.8*0.35 + 1.5*9.8*0.15)/0.04
tol: 1
unit: N
hint: Take torques about the elbow, including both the held mass and the forearm.
hint: $F(0.04) = (6.0)(9.8)(0.35) + (1.5)(9.8)(0.15)$.
solution: $F(0.04) = 20.58 + 2.205 = 22.785$, so $F = 569.6\ \text{N}$.

@exam num
prompt: An artery of radius $2.0\ \text{mm}$ and length $0.10\ \text{m}$ carries blood of viscosity $3.0 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ under a pressure difference of $600\ \text{Pa}$. Atherosclerosis narrows the radius to $1.5\ \text{mm}$ with the same pressure difference and length. Using Poiseuille's law, what is the new flow rate in mL/s? ($1\ \text{mL} = 10^{-6}\ \text{m}^3$.) Give three significant figures.
answer: 3.98
check: math.pi*(1.5e-3)**4*600/(8*3.0e-3*0.10)*1e6
tol: 1%
unit: mL/s
hint: Use SI units for every quantity, with the radius to the fourth power.
hint: $Q = \pi r^4\Delta P/(8\eta L)$ with $r = 1.5 \times 10^{-3}$ m.
solution: $Q = \pi(1.5 \times 10^{-3})^4(600)/(8 \times 3.0 \times 10^{-3} \times 0.10) = 3.98 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m}^3/\text{s} = 3.98\ \text{mL/s}$, which is $(0.75)^4 = 32\%$ of the original $12.6$ mL/s.

@exam num
prompt: A $65\ \text{kg}$ runner (body specific heat $3500\ \text{J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$) generates $800\ \text{W}$ of heat for $30$ minutes while losing $500\ \text{W}$ of heat by evaporation of sweat and other routes. If no other heat is lost, by how many kelvin does the body temperature rise? Give two decimal places.
answer: 2.37
check: (800 - 500)*30*60/(65*3500)
tol: 0.02
unit: K
hint: Find the net heat stored, in joules, over $1800$ s.
hint: Then use $\Delta T = Q/(mc)$.
solution: Net power $= 300$ W, so $Q = 300 \times 1800 = 5.4 \times 10^5$ J. $\Delta T = 540\,000/(65 \times 3500) = 2.37\ \text{K}$.
