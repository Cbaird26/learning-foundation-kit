@article
**Temperature** measures the average random kinetic energy of the particles of a substance. **Heat** is energy transferred because of a temperature difference; it flows from hot to cold. Heat is measured in joules (J); the food Calorie (kilocalorie) is $1\ \text{kcal} = 4184\ \text{J}$.

### Temperature scales

The Celsius and kelvin scales have the same degree size: $T(\text{K}) = T(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15$. Fahrenheit is related by $T_F = \tfrac{9}{5}T_C + 32$. Normal core body temperature is about $37\ ^\circ\text{C}$ ($310\ \text{K}$, $98.6\ ^\circ\text{F}$). Gas-law and radiation formulas need kelvin; temperature *differences* are the same in K and $^\circ$C.

### Specific heat

To change the temperature of a mass $m$ by $\Delta T$ requires

$$Q = mc\,\Delta T,$$

where $c$ is the **specific heat capacity**, in J/(kg$\cdot$K). Useful values: water $4186$, ice $2090$, aluminium $900$, copper $390$, and the human body about $3500$. Water has an unusually large specific heat, so the body, which is mostly water, warms and cools slowly, and large water bodies moderate climate.

### Phase changes and latent heat

During melting or boiling, heat goes into changing the phase and the temperature stays constant:

$$Q = mL.$$

For water, the latent heat of fusion is $L_f = 3.34 \times 10^5\ \text{J/kg}$, and the latent heat of vaporisation is $L_v = 2.26 \times 10^6\ \text{J/kg}$ at $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$. At skin temperature, evaporating sweat absorbs about $2.42 \times 10^6\ \text{J/kg}$. For problems with several stages (for example ice at $-10\ ^\circ\text{C}$ to steam), compute each stage's heat separately: warm the ice, melt it, warm the water, boil it.

### Calorimetry

In an insulated system, heat lost by hotter parts equals heat gained by cooler parts:

$$\sum Q = 0 \quad\text{or}\quad Q_{\text{gained}} = |Q_{\text{lost}}|.$$

Set up each object's $mc(T_f - T_i)$ with the final temperature $T_f$ unknown (a shared value at equilibrium) and solve. Check that $T_f$ lies between the initial temperatures, unless a phase change is involved.

### Heat transfer

There are four ways the body exchanges heat with its surroundings:

- **Conduction:** direct contact between molecules (touching a cold floor).
- **Convection:** movement of a fluid (air or blood) carrying heat; wind chill increases it.
- **Radiation:** infrared emission, which does not need a medium. The skin radiates strongly.
- **Evaporation:** sweat absorbing latent heat as it vaporises. In a hot environment or during hard exercise this is the main way to lose heat, which is why humid air, which slows evaporation, is dangerous.

@example
title: Heating water
problem: How much heat is needed to warm $0.50\ \text{kg}$ of water from $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$ to $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$? Use $c = 4186\ \text{J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$.
step: Temperature change: $\Delta T = 80 - 20 = 60\ \text{K}$ (a difference, so no need to convert to kelvin).
step: $Q = mc\,\Delta T = (0.50)(4186)(60)$.
step: $Q = 1.26 \times 10^5\ \text{J}$, which is $30$ kcal, since $125\,580/4184 = 30.0$.

@mistakes
- Using Celsius temperatures instead of kelvin in radiation and gas-law formulas, or adding $273$ to a temperature difference.
- Forgetting that the temperature stays constant during a phase change and using $mc\Delta T$ there.
- Mixing the sign of $\Delta T$ in calorimetry: use $T_f - T_i$ for each object and let the signs sum to zero.
- Mixing joules with kilocalories (the food Calorie is a kilocalorie).
