@article
A digit is one of the number symbols 0 to 9. Where a digit sits tells us how much it is worth. This is called **place value**.

### Ones, tens and hundreds

Picture small blocks. One small block is **1 one**. A stick of 10 blocks is **1 ten**. A flat square of 10 sticks is **1 hundred**, because 10 tens make 100.

The number 253 has three digits:

- The 2 is in the hundreds place. It means 2 hundreds, or 200.
- The 5 is in the tens place. It means 5 tens, or 50.
- The 3 is in the ones place. It means 3 ones, or 3.

We can write it as $200 + 50 + 3 = 253$. This is called **expanded form**.

### Zero holds a place

In 406 there are 4 hundreds, 0 tens and 6 ones. The 0 shows that there are no tens. Without the 0 we would write 46, which is a very different number.

### Which number is bigger?

Compare the hundreds first. 512 is more than 498 because 5 hundreds is more than 4 hundreds. If the hundreds are the same, look at the tens. If the tens are the same too, look at the ones.

We use signs to show this. The open mouth of the sign points to the bigger number: $512 > 498$ and $498 < 512$. If both numbers are the same, we use $=$.

### Counting by 100 and 10

Add 1 hundred and only the hundreds digit goes up by one: 253 becomes 353. Add 1 ten and only the tens digit goes up by one: 253 becomes 263.

The biggest three-digit number is 999. One more makes 1,000, which is 10 hundreds.

@example
title: Build a number from blocks
problem: You have 2 flats (hundreds), 5 sticks (tens) and 3 small blocks (ones). What number is that? Write it in expanded form.
step: Count the value of each kind of block. 2 hundreds is 200. 5 tens is 50. 3 ones is 3.
step: Put the parts together in order from the biggest to the smallest: $200 + 50 + 3$.
step: Add them up. The number is 253.

@example
title: Compare two numbers
problem: Which is bigger, 347 or 351?
step: Look at the hundreds first. Both numbers have 3 hundreds, so they match.
step: Now look at the tens. 347 has 4 tens and 351 has 5 tens. 5 tens is more than 4 tens.
step: So 351 is bigger. We write $351 > 347$.

@mistakes
- Forgetting the zero that holds a place, such as writing 46 when you mean 406.
- Looking at the ones digit first when comparing. Always start with the hundreds.
- Mixing up the hundreds and tens places, so 3 hundreds and 4 tens turns into 34 instead of 340.
