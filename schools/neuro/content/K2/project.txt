@project
title: Our pretend store
overview: With your grown-up, run a pretend store at home. You will price items, add up totals, make change and record a week of sales in a bar graph. You will use place value, adding and subtracting, money, multiplication and graphs.
goal: Add and subtract with money to find totals and change.
goal: Use multiplication for equal groups, such as 3 pencils at 5 cents each.
goal: Make a bar graph from the sales you record and read it.
step: Choose 6 things from around the house to sell, such as a pencil, an eraser, a sticker, a toy car, a book and a ball. Do not use anything that belongs to someone else without asking.
step: Make a price tag for each item. Use prices of 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 or 50 cents. Write them on small pieces of paper.
step: Set out play money or real coins with your grown-up. Practice counting a handful of coins in the order quarters, dimes, nickels, pennies.
step: Be the shopper and the shopkeeper. Buy 3 items whose prices add up to 100 cents or less. Add up the total cost. Then pay with 1 dollar (100 cents) and work out the change by counting up and by subtracting.
step: Try a multiplication sale. Buy 4 stickers that each cost 5 cents. Write the number sentence $4 \times 5 = 20$.
step: Play store for 5 days. After each day, write how many items were sold with tally marks.
step: Make a bar graph of the sales for the 5 days. Give it a title, labels and a number line up the side.
step: Look at your graph. Which day sold the most? How many more items were sold on that day than the day with the fewest? Write the answer in a sentence.
deliverable: A price list with 6 items and prices.
deliverable: Three sales showing the total and the change, with number sentences.
deliverable: A bar graph of 5 days of sales with a title and labels, and two true sentences about it.
rubric: Prices are written in cents and the totals are added correctly.
rubric: Change is found correctly and checked with the other operation.
rubric: The bar graph has a title, labels, a number line and bars of the right height.
rubric: The multiplication sale shows equal groups and a correct number sentence.
safety: Use only safe household items and play money, and do the project with a grown-up.
tutor: How can I check that my change is right?
tutor: How do I decide how tall to draw each bar?
tutor: Can you give me one more equal-groups store story to solve?

@review
### Week by week

- **Week 1: Place value.** 10 ones make a ten and 10 tens make a hundred. In 253 the 2 is 200, the 5 is 50 and the 3 is 3.
- **Week 2: Add and subtract to 1,000.** Work ones, tens, hundreds. Regroup when you need to. Check with the other operation.
- **Week 3: Money and time.** Penny 1, nickel 5, dime 10, quarter 25. On a clock, count by 5s for the minutes.
- **Week 4: Measurement and data.** Start a ruler at 0. A bar graph uses bar heights. Subtract to find how many more.
- **Week 5: Meaning of multiplication.** Equal groups, repeated addition and arrays. $3 \times 5$ means 3 groups of 5.
- **Week 6: Facts.** 2s are even, 5s end in 0 or 5, 10s end in 0. You can turn a fact around.
- **Week 7: Division.** Share equally or make equal groups. Think of the matching multiplication.
- **Week 8: Word problems.** Know two of groups, size and total, then find the third.
- **Week 9: Fractions.** Equal parts. The bottom number tells how many parts in all.
- **Week 10: Number line.** Mark equal parts from 0 to 1. A bigger bottom number means a smaller piece.
- **Week 11: Area and perimeter.** Area counts squares inside. Perimeter adds the sides around.
- **Week 12: Two steps.** Find the hidden first number, then do the second step.

### Self-check

- I can tell the hundreds, tens and ones digits of a number.
- I can add and subtract three-digit numbers and check them.
- I can count coins and tell time to 5 minutes.
- I know my 2s, 5s and 10s facts.
- I can name a fraction from a picture.
- I can find the area and perimeter of a rectangle.

### Common mistakes

Mixing up area and perimeter, forgetting to regroup, comparing from the ones digit, and thinking a bigger bottom number is a bigger fraction.

@exam num
prompt: At a pretend store, a pencil costs 25 cents and an eraser costs 10 cents. You buy 3 pencils and 1 eraser and pay with \$1 (100 cents). How many cents of change do you get back?
answer: 15
check: 100 - (3 * 25 + 10)
hint: First find the cost of 3 pencils. Then add the eraser.
hint: Subtract the total cost from 100 cents.
solution: 3 pencils cost $3 \times 25 = 75$ cents. With the eraser the total is $75 + 10 = 85$ cents. The change is $100 - 85 = 15$ cents.

@exam num
prompt: A rectangle room on grid paper is 6 squares wide and 5 squares tall. Each square is 1 foot. What is the perimeter of the room in feet?
answer: 22
check: 6 + 5 + 6 + 5
hint: The sides of a rectangle come in two equal pairs.
hint: Add 6, 5, 6 and 5.
solution: The sides are 6, 5, 6 and 5 feet. $6 + 5 + 6 + 5 = 22$. The perimeter is 22 feet.

@exam self
prompt: A shop sold 6 apples on Monday, 4 on Tuesday and 5 on Wednesday. Make a bar graph of the sales. Then write how many apples were sold in all and how many more were sold on Monday than on Tuesday.
solution: The graph has a title such as "Apples Sold" and labels for Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday. The bars are 6, 4 and 5 tall. In all: $6 + 4 + 5 = 15$ apples. Monday had $6 - 4 = 2$ more than Tuesday.
hint: Draw one bar for each day. Make each bar as tall as its number.
hint: Add the three numbers for the total. Subtract to find how many more.
rubric: The graph has a title, labels and a number line.
rubric: The bars are 6, 4 and 5 tall.
rubric: The total is 15 and the difference is 2.
