@article
A neurologist taps the tendon below your kneecap and your leg kicks before you can decide anything. The delay is about 20 ms, far too short for the brain to be involved. The reflex happens in the spinal cord, and tracing it shows the logic of the whole motor system: a sensory neuron, a motor neuron and, usually, interneurons between them.

### Segments, roots and gray matter

The spinal cord has **31 pairs of spinal nerves**: 8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral and 1 coccygeal. Each segment has a **dorsal root**, carrying sensory axons whose cell bodies lie in the **dorsal root ganglion**, and a **ventral root**, carrying motor axons from cells inside the cord (the **Bell–Magendie law**). The two fuse into a mixed spinal nerve. The adult cord ends at about vertebral level L1 to L2 as the conus medullaris, and lower roots descend as the **cauda equina**. Enlargements at about C5 to T1 and L1 to S3 serve the limbs.

In cross section, gray matter forms a butterfly. The **dorsal horn** receives sensory input; the **intermediate zone** includes the lateral horn (T1 to L2: sympathetic preganglionic neurons; S2 to S4: parasympathetic); the **ventral horn** holds **alpha motor neurons**, arranged so that medial cells supply proximal and axial muscles and lateral cells supply distal limb muscles. White matter surrounds the gray matter in dorsal, lateral and ventral columns (funiculi), which carry the tracts of weeks 7 and 8.

Landmarks to know: sensory **dermatomes** (C6 thumb, C7 middle finger, T4 nipple line, T10 umbilicus, L5 dorsum of the foot and great toe, S1 lateral foot) and reflex roots (biceps C5 to C6, triceps C7, patellar L3 to L4, Achilles S1). Neighboring dermatomes overlap, so one cut root rarely leaves a patch fully numb.

### The stretch reflex

A tap stretches a muscle and its **muscle spindles**, whose sensory endings give rise to **Ia afferents** (large, myelinated, about 70 to 120 m/s). Each Ia fiber enters the dorsal root and synapses directly on alpha motor neurons of the same muscle, which contract (a **monosynaptic** reflex, one synapse in the cord). The same Ia fibers also excite synergist motor neurons and, through an **Ia inhibitory interneuron**, inhibit the antagonist: **reciprocal inhibition** (disynaptic). **Gamma motor neurons** keep spindles taut during contraction (alpha–gamma coactivation). The **Golgi tendon organ**, which reports muscle force through **Ib afferents**, acts through an inhibitory interneuron to reduce its own muscle's activity (**autogenic inhibition**). **Renshaw cells** provide recurrent inhibition of motor neurons.

### The flexion withdrawal reflex

A painful stimulus to the foot excites nociceptors (A-delta and C fibers). Polysynaptic pathways through interneurons excite flexor motor neurons and inhibit extensors in the stimulated limb, and the **crossed extension** component does the opposite on the other side, supporting body weight. Because it uses interneurons, the reflex is slower than the stretch reflex and can be modulated by descending input.

### Reflex latency

Total latency is the sum of conduction times and synaptic delays (central delay of about 0.5 to 1 ms per synapse):

$$t = \frac{d_{\text{aff}}}{v_{\text{aff}}} + \frac{d_{\text{eff}}}{v_{\text{eff}}} + n\,\delta,$$

ignoring neuromuscular transmission and muscle activation, with distances in meters, velocities in m/s, and $\delta$ in ms. Multiply the conduction terms by 1000 to get ms.

### Upper versus lower motor neurons

**Lower motor neurons** (alpha motor neurons and their axons) lesions cause weakness with flaccid tone, atrophy, fasciculations and reduced or absent reflexes. **Upper motor neuron** lesions (cortex or its descending axons) cause weakness with spasticity, exaggerated reflexes and an extensor plantar response (Babinski sign). Immediately after an acute severe cord injury, there is **spinal shock** with flaccid areflexia below the level, which later gives way to spasticity.

### Evidence and limits

The Bell–Magendie law and the circuits above come from animal physiology and human electrophysiology; the human H-reflex is an electrical analogue of the stretch reflex. Spinal circuits are not fixed wiring: descending commands and sensory feedback continuously modulate reflex gain.

title: Latency of a stretch reflex
problem: In a tap-evoked stretch reflex the afferent path is 0.6 m at 80 m/s, the efferent path is 0.6 m at 60 m/s, and there is one synapse with a delay of 1 ms. What is the latency?
step: Afferent conduction: $0.6 / 80 = 0.0075$ s $= 7.5$ ms.
step: Efferent conduction: $0.6 / 60 = 0.010$ s $= 10$ ms.
step: Central delay: one synapse, 1 ms.
step: Total: $7.5 + 10 + 1 = 18.5$ ms, which is far shorter than a voluntary response and consistent with a spinal loop.
answer: 18.5 ms.

@example
title: Tracing a withdrawal reflex
problem: A person steps on a tack with the right foot. Which muscles act in which leg?
step: Nociceptor afferents enter the lumbosacral cord and excite interneuron chains.
step: On the right, flexor motor neurons are excited and extensor motor neurons inhibited: the foot lifts.
step: Crossed branches excite left extensors, so the left leg takes the weight.
answer: Right leg flexors contract and extensors relax; left leg extensors contract.

@mistakes
- Calling the stretch reflex polysynaptic: the direct Ia to alpha motor neuron link is monosynaptic; the interneuron branches are extra.
- Saying that the dorsal root is motor: the dorsal root is sensory (cell bodies in the ganglion) and the ventral root is motor.
- Mixing upper and lower motor neuron signs: hyperreflexia and spasticity are upper; flaccidity, atrophy and fasciculations are lower.
- Forgetting units in latency: meters divided by m/s gives seconds, so multiply by 1000 for ms.

model:
```python
def fastest_reflex(arcs):
    """Name of the arc with the smallest total latency (ms), or None."""
    best_name, best_t = None, None
    for name, aff_m, aff_v, eff_m, eff_v, n in arcs:
        t = aff_m / aff_v * 1000 + eff_m / eff_v * 1000 + 1.0 * n
        if best_t is None or t < best_t:
            best_name, best_t = name, t
    return best_name
```
hint: Compute each arc's latency in ms, then keep the smallest, updating only on a strictly smaller value so ties keep the first.
solution: Convert each conduction time to ms, add the synaptic delays, and track the minimum. The second test shows why the synapse count matters: arc A has shorter conduction time but five synapses, so B is faster overall.
