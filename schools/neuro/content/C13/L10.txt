@article
In 1953 a surgeon removed the medial temporal lobes on both sides of a young man's brain to control his seizures. Afterward the patient, known in the literature as H.M., kept his intelligence and his language, could hold a conversation, and could remember events from his childhood, but he could not form new lasting memories of the people he met and the events he lived (Scoville and Milner, 1957). The case showed that memory depends on particular structures, and put the **hippocampus** at the center of memory research.

### The limbic system

The **limbic system** is a set of connected structures around the medial edge of the hemisphere and diencephalon: the hippocampal formation, the amygdala, the cingulate and parahippocampal gyri, the septal nuclei, the mammillary bodies, the anterior thalamic nucleus, and parts of the hypothalamus and ventral striatum. The idea of a unified emotional "limbic system" is historical; no sharp boundary exists, and the same structures serve memory, motivation and emotion.

The **Papez circuit** (Papez, 1937) links memory-related structures in a loop: the hippocampus projects through the **fornix** to the **mammillary bodies**, which send the **mammillothalamic tract** to the **anterior thalamic nucleus**, which projects to the **cingulate gyrus**, and the cingulum carries signals to the parahippocampal gyrus and entorhinal cortex, which feed the hippocampus. Damage to the mammillary bodies and thalamus, as in thiamine deficiency (Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome), causes profound amnesia.

### The hippocampal formation

The hippocampal formation lies in the medial temporal lobe. It includes the **dentate gyrus**, the **hippocampus proper** (areas CA3, CA2, CA1) and the **subiculum**. It is allocortex, with three layers, and it receives its main input from the **entorhinal cortex**, which connects it to association cortex. The principal circuit is **trisynaptic**:

1. Entorhinal cortex layer II axons (the **perforant path**) synapse on dentate granule cells.
2. Granule cell **mossy fibers** synapse on CA3 pyramidal neurons.
3. CA3 neurons send **Schaffer collaterals** to CA1 neurons.
4. CA1 projects to the subiculum and back to the entorhinal cortex; output also leaves through the fornix.

Entorhinal layer III also projects directly to CA1.

### What H.M. and the hippocampus show

H.M. had severe **anterograde amnesia** for new facts and events, with a temporally graded loss of some memories before surgery, normal digit span and preserved skill learning, such as mirror drawing (Milner). This dissociation supports separate systems for declarative memory and procedural skill. **Long-term potentiation** (LTP), a lasting increase in synaptic strength after high-frequency stimulation, was first reported by Bliss and Lømo (1973) in the rabbit perforant path to dentate gyrus synapse, and is a leading candidate cellular mechanism of memory. **Place cells** (O'Keefe and Dostrovsky, 1971) fire when an animal is in a specific location, linking the hippocampus to spatial representation. The CA1 region is especially vulnerable to ischemia.

### The amygdala

The **amygdala** is an almond-shaped group of nuclei in the anterior medial temporal lobe, in front of the hippocampus. The **basolateral complex** (lateral, basal and accessory basal nuclei) receives sensory input from thalamus and cortex and is connected to the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex; the **central nucleus** is the main output to the hypothalamus and brainstem, driving autonomic, endocrine and defensive responses (for example freezing, via the periaqueductal gray); the corticomedial group is linked to olfaction. Output pathways include the **stria terminalis** and the ventral amygdalofugal pathway.

In **fear conditioning**, a neutral tone paired with a shock comes to evoke freezing; studies in rats by LeDoux and colleagues showed that the lateral amygdala receives the tone from thalamus and auditory cortex and that the central nucleus produces the response. In humans, a patient with bilateral amygdala damage from Urbach–Wiethe disease (S.M.) showed impaired recognition of fear in faces and reduced fear conditioning. **Klüver–Bucy** syndrome, first described in monkeys after bilateral temporal lobe removal (Klüver and Bucy, 1939), includes placidity, hyperorality and visual agnosia.

### Evidence and limits

The amygdala is not simply a "fear center": it also processes reward and salience, and fear involves wider circuits. Findings in rodents may not map exactly to humans; adult hippocampal neurogenesis is established in rodents and debated in humans. Patient data are rare and lesions are seldom confined to one structure.

step: Increase: $0.84 - 0.60 = 0.24$ mV/ms.

@example
title: Following the trisynaptic circuit
problem: List the order of excitatory synapses from entorhinal cortex to CA1 in the trisynaptic circuit.
step: Entorhinal layer II to dentate granule cells (perforant path).
step: Dentate granule cells to CA3 (mossy fibers).
step: CA3 to CA1 (Schaffer collaterals).
answer: Entorhinal cortex, dentate gyrus, CA3, CA1.

@mistakes
- Saying H.M. lost all memory: he lost new declarative memory and kept skills, intelligence and many old memories.
- Calling the amygdala the "fear center": it contributes to fear learning but also to reward, salience and social processing.
- Mixing up the Papez circuit and the trisynaptic circuit: Papez loops from the hippocampus through the fornix to the cingulate, while the trisynaptic circuit lies inside the hippocampus.
- Treating the limbic system as a sharply defined organ: its boundaries are historical.
