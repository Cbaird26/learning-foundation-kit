@article
The adult human brain holds roughly 86 billion neurons, yet it begins, about two and a half weeks after fertilization, as a flat patch of ectoderm on the back of the embryo. How does a sheet of cells become a tube, and a tube become a brain whose parts you can name and find? The answer is a sequence of folds, swellings and bends that fixes the plan you will use for the rest of this course.

### From plate to tube

Signals from the underlying **notochord** induce the overlying ectoderm to thicken into the **neural plate**. Its edges rise as **neural folds**, which meet and fuse in the midline during **neurulation** (about days 21 to 28 after fertilization). Fusion starts in the future neck region and proceeds both rostrally and caudally, like a zipper. The two open ends, the **anterior** and **posterior neuropores**, close at about day 25 and day 28. The result is the **neural tube**, which becomes the central nervous system, and its hollow core becomes the ventricular system and the central canal of the spinal cord.

Cells at the crest of each fold, the **neural crest**, detach and migrate. They form the peripheral nervous system's sensory neurons (dorsal root ganglia), the postganglionic neurons of the autonomic ganglia, Schwann cells, the chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla and, outside the nervous system, pigment cells. The CNS (brain and spinal cord) comes from the neural tube; most of the PNS comes from the crest.

**Neural tube defects** are failures of closure. If the anterior neuropore stays open, the forebrain fails to form properly (**anencephaly**); if the posterior neuropore stays open, the lumbosacral cord and its coverings are exposed (**spina bifida** in its open form, myelomeningocele). Large studies show that folic acid taken before conception and in early pregnancy lowers the risk of these defects substantially; the mechanism is not fully understood, and many cases are not preventable this way.

### Vesicles: the plan of the brain

By the fourth week the rostral tube swells into **three primary vesicles**, and by the fifth week into **five secondary vesicles**. Bends (flexures) appear: the cephalic flexure at the midbrain and the cervical flexure at the brain–cord junction, which is why the long axis of the human brain turns by about 90 degrees.

| primary vesicle | secondary vesicle | adult structures | ventricle |
|---|---|---|---|
| prosencephalon (forebrain) | telencephalon | cerebral cortex, white matter, basal ganglia, hippocampus, amygdala | lateral ventricles |
| | diencephalon | thalamus, hypothalamus, epithalamus, subthalamus, retina and optic nerve | third ventricle |
| mesencephalon (midbrain) | mesencephalon | tectum, tegmentum, cerebral peduncles | cerebral aqueduct |
| rhombencephalon (hindbrain) | metencephalon | pons, cerebellum | upper fourth ventricle |
| | myelencephalon | medulla | lower fourth ventricle |

The caudal neural tube becomes the **spinal cord** with its **central canal**.

### Alar and basal plates

In the walls of the tube, a groove, the **sulcus limitans**, divides a dorsal **alar plate** from a ventral **basal plate**. The alar plate gives rise mainly to sensory relay neurons (the dorsal horn of the spinal cord and the sensory nuclei of the brainstem); the basal plate gives rise to motor neurons (the ventral horn and the motor nuclei of cranial nerves). This dorsal–ventral pattern is set by opposing signals: Sonic hedgehog from the notochord and floor plate promotes ventral (motor) fates, and bone morphogenetic proteins from the roof plate promote dorsal (sensory) fates. The pattern persists in the adult brainstem, where motor nuclei lie medial to sensory nuclei.

### Making and placing neurons

Neurons are born from dividing stem cells lining the ventricle, the **ventricular zone**. Early divisions are mostly symmetric (one progenitor makes two progenitors, doubling the pool); later divisions are often asymmetric (one progenitor and one neuron or intermediate progenitor). **Radial glia** serve both as progenitors and as scaffolds along which young neurons migrate outward. In the cerebral cortex, layers form **inside-out**: the earliest-born neurons settle in the deepest layers, and later neurons migrate past them to occupy superficial layers. Axon growth, synapse formation and a wave of cell death then sculpt connectivity, and **myelination** continues well after birth, through adolescence in some tracts.

### Evidence and limits

The vesicle plan and the neural-crest fate map come from embryology and from lineage tracing in animals, and from human fetal anatomy and the clinical spectrum of defects. Cortical inside-out layering was shown by labeling cells born on different days in rodents and primates. Whether new neurons are added to the adult human hippocampus is still debated; adult neurogenesis is well established in rodents.

problem: A patient has a tumor in the thalamus and loss of vision from damage to the optic nerve. Name the secondary vesicle that gave rise to both, and the ventricle that is its cavity.
step: Place the thalamus in the table: the thalamus is a major derivative of the diencephalon.

@example
title: Growing a progenitor pool
problem: A patch of cortex begins with 200 progenitors. For 5 rounds every progenitor divides symmetrically into two progenitors. Then, for 10 rounds, every progenitor divides asymmetrically into one progenitor and one neuron. How many progenitors and how many neurons are there at the end?
step: Symmetric rounds double the pool each time: $200 \times 2^5 = 6400$ progenitors.
step: An asymmetric division keeps the number of progenitors the same (one in, one out) and adds one neuron per progenitor per round.
step: Neurons made: $6400 \times 10 = 64{,}000$.
step: Check: the pool is still 6400, so total cells are $6400 + 64{,}000 = 70{,}400$.
answer: 6400 progenitors and 64,000 neurons.

@mistakes
- Treating the neural crest as part of the CNS: the CNS comes from the neural tube; dorsal root ganglion neurons, Schwann cells and adrenal medulla cells come from the crest.
- Confusing the cavity with the tissue: the ventricles are the leftover lumen of the tube (the lateral ventricles belong to the telencephalon, the third to the diencephalon, the aqueduct to the midbrain, the fourth to the hindbrain).
- Reversing the plates: the alar plate is dorsal and mostly sensory; the basal plate is ventral and mostly motor.
- Saying cortical layers form outside-in: the deep layers are born first, and later neurons migrate past them.
