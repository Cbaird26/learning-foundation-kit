@article
After a stab wound that cuts half of the spinal cord, a patient can feel a pinprick on the left leg but not the right, yet cannot feel the position of the left foot or the vibration of a tuning fork on it. The two kinds of sensation take different routes and cross at different levels, so a hemisection splits them. Tracing the routes explains the puzzle and lets you predict the deficits.

### Receptors and fibers

Touch is carried by large myelinated **A-beta** fibers (about 35 to 75 m/s) from mechanoreceptors: Merkel cells (sustained pressure, edges), Meissner corpuscles (light touch, flutter), Ruffini endings (skin stretch) and Pacinian corpuscles (vibration). **Proprioception** comes from muscle spindles (Ia and II fibers), Golgi tendon organs (Ib) and joint receptors. **Pain and temperature** come from free nerve endings: thinly myelinated **A-delta** fibers (about 5 to 35 m/s) give a fast, sharp "first pain" and cold, while unmyelinated **C fibers** (about 0.5 to 2 m/s) give slow, burning "second pain", warmth and itch. All first-order cell bodies lie in the dorsal root ganglia (or the trigeminal ganglion for the face).

### Dorsal column–medial lemniscal pathway (fine touch, vibration, proprioception)

1. First-order neurons (dorsal root ganglion) send axons up the **dorsal column on the same side**: the **fasciculus gracilis** (lower body, below about T6, medial) and **fasciculus cuneatus** (upper body, lateral).
2. They synapse in the **nucleus gracilis and nucleus cuneatus** in the caudal medulla.
3. Second-order axons arc ventrally and cross the midline as the **internal arcuate fibers** (this is the level of **decussation**) and ascend as the **medial lemniscus** through the medulla, pons and midbrain.
4. They end in the **ventral posterolateral (VPL) nucleus** of the thalamus.
5. Third-order neurons project through the **posterior limb of the internal capsule** to the primary somatosensory cortex (areas 3, 1, 2), with the leg medial and the face lateral.

### Spinothalamic (anterolateral) pathway (pain, temperature, crude touch)

1. First-order A-delta and C neurons enter the cord and synapse in the **dorsal horn** (laminae I, II and V) on the same side, often after rising or falling a segment or two in Lissauer's tract.
2. Second-order neurons cross the midline in the **anterior white commissure** within one to two segments of entry.
3. They ascend in the **contralateral anterolateral funiculus**, with sacral fibers lateral and cervical fibers medial.
4. They reach the VPL (and other thalamic nuclei, including the intralaminar group) and project to the somatosensory cortex, and also to the insula and cingulate cortex, which contribute to the emotional side of pain.

### The face and unconscious proprioception

Face touch ends in the principal sensory trigeminal nucleus (pons), and face pain and temperature in the **spinal trigeminal nucleus** (which extends from the pons into upper cervical cord); second-order axons cross and reach the **VPM** of the thalamus and then face cortex. Unconscious proprioception travels in the **spinocerebellar tracts** to the cerebellum (dorsal spinocerebellar tract uncrossed; ventral spinocerebellar tract crossed twice).

### Localizing lesions

| lesion | deficit |
|---|---|
| spinal cord hemisection (Brown-Séquard) | ipsilateral loss of vibration and proprioception below the lesion; ipsilateral upper motor neuron weakness below; contralateral loss of pain and temperature beginning one to a few levels below |
| central cord (syringomyelia) | bilateral loss of pain and temperature across the shoulders and arms (cape), with touch and vibration spared, because crossing spinothalamic fibers are cut and the dorsal columns are not |
| medial lemniscus (brainstem) | contralateral loss of vibration and proprioception |
| lateral medulla | ipsilateral face and contralateral body pain and temperature loss |
| VPL or VPM thalamus | contralateral loss, possibly with later thalamic pain |

Pain is modulated: the gate control theory (Melzack and Wall, 1965) proposed that large-fiber input and descending signals can dampen pain transmission at the dorsal horn; descending pathways from the periaqueductal gray through the rostral ventromedial medulla modulate it, and opioid signaling is involved. The details of the original gate model have been revised, but the principle of dorsal horn modulation is well established.

### Evidence and limits

Pathway maps rest on animal tracing, lesion cases and human imaging. The anterolateral system is more diffuse than textbook diagrams suggest, and some pain input travels in other routes, which is why chordotomy sometimes fails to abolish pain over time.

@example
title: First pain and second pain
problem: A stimulus applied to a point 1.2 m from the spinal cord excites A-delta fibers (20 m/s) and C fibers (1.0 m/s). How much later does the C-fiber signal reach the cord?
step: A-delta time: $1.2/20 = 0.060$ s = 60 ms.
step: C-fiber time: $1.2/1.0 = 1.2$ s = 1200 ms.
step: Difference: $1200 - 60 = 1140$ ms, about 1.1 s later, which explains the delayed burning after a sharp first sting.
answer: About 1140 ms later.

@example
title: Following a left Brown-Séquard lesion
problem: The left half of the cord is cut at T8. Describe the sensory loss.
step: Dorsal column fibers ascend uncrossed, so vibration and proprioception are lost on the left below T8.
step: Spinothalamic fibers from the left side crossed at or just above entry, so the left half of the cord holds fibers from the right body: pain and temperature are lost on the right, beginning one to two segments below T8.
answer: Left loss of vibration and proprioception below T8; right loss of pain and temperature beginning about T9 to T10.

@mistakes
- Assuming that all sensory pathways cross in the cord: the dorsal column pathway stays uncrossed until the caudal medulla.
- Mixing up where each crosses: spinothalamic fibers cross within a segment or two of entry, while dorsal column fibers cross as internal arcuate fibers in the medulla.
- Forgetting that a hemisection gives opposite sides for touch/proprioception and pain/temperature.
- Treating one tract as one modality: the anterolateral system also carries crude touch, and the dorsal column pathway also carries two-point discrimination.
