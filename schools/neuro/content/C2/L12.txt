@article
Sexual reproduction in humans requires **gametogenesis** (formation of haploid gametes by meiosis), fertilization and development. Hormones from the hypothalamus, pituitary and gonads coordinate every stage.

### Gametogenesis and male reproduction

**Spermatogenesis** occurs in the seminiferous tubules and produces four sperm per primary spermatocyte; it takes about $64$ to $74$ days and continues from puberty. **Sertoli cells** nourish developing sperm and respond to FSH; **Leydig cells** in the interstitium make testosterone in response to LH. Testosterone feeds back negatively on the hypothalamus and pituitary. Sperm mature and are stored in the epididymis.

### Oogenesis and the menstrual cycle

**Oogenesis** begins before birth. Oocytes arrest in prophase I until puberty; at ovulation, the secondary oocyte arrests in metaphase II and completes meiosis only if fertilized. Each oogonium yields one egg and polar bodies. The cycle averages $28$ days and has two ovarian phases:

- **Follicular phase**: FSH stimulates follicles; the dominant follicle secretes estrogen. Estrogen drives the **proliferative** phase of the endometrium. When estrogen is high and sustained, it switches to positive feedback, causing the **LH surge**, which triggers **ovulation** about $36$ hours later.
- **Luteal phase**: the follicle becomes the **corpus luteum**, which secretes progesterone (and estrogen), maintaining the **secretory** endometrium. The luteal phase is fairly constant at about $14$ days. If there is no pregnancy, the corpus luteum degenerates, hormones fall and the endometrium sheds (menses).

Since the luteal phase is fixed, **ovulation occurs about $14$ days before the next menses**, so $\text{day of ovulation} \approx \text{cycle length} - 14$. Hormonal contraceptives work mainly by suppressing the LH surge and ovulation through negative feedback by synthetic estrogen and progestin.

### Fertilization and early development

Fertilization usually occurs in the **ampulla** of the uterine tube. The sperm's acrosomal enzymes penetrate the zona pellucida; fusion triggers the cortical reaction, which blocks polyspermy. The zygote undergoes **cleavage**, forming a morula and then a **blastocyst**, which **implants** in the endometrium about $6$ to $10$ days after fertilization. The blastocyst's trophoblast makes **hCG**, which maintains the corpus luteum and is the basis of pregnancy tests. **Gastrulation** forms the three germ layers: **ectoderm** (skin epidermis, nervous system), **mesoderm** (muscle, bone, heart, blood, kidney) and **endoderm** (gut and respiratory linings, liver, pancreas). **Neurulation** forms the neural tube, and incomplete closure causes neural tube defects, which folic acid supplementation reduces. Pregnancy lasts about $40$ weeks from the last menstrual period, divided into trimesters. Gestational age is counted from the last menstrual period.

### Comparing innate and adaptive immunity

Use this checklist to compare them: speed (innate within hours, adaptive days), specificity (pattern-based versus antigen-specific), memory (absent versus present), key cells (phagocytes, NK cells versus B and T cells), key molecules (complement, cytokines versus antibodies and T-cell receptors) and how they interact (innate cells present antigen and secrete cytokines that activate adaptive responses).

@example
title: Estimating the day of ovulation
problem: A woman has a regular cycle of $32$ days. Estimate the day of the cycle on which she ovulates, counting the first day of menses as day $1$.
step: The luteal phase is fairly constant at about $14$ days, so ovulation occurs about $14$ days before the next menses.
step: Subtract the luteal phase from the cycle length, using the usual convention: $32 - 14 = 18$. (For a $28$-day cycle this gives day $14$.)
step: The longer follicular phase explains why cycles of different length differ mainly before ovulation.
step: Ovulation is estimated at about day $18$.

@mistakes
- Assuming ovulation always occurs on day $14$; it depends on cycle length, because the luteal phase is about $14$ days.
- Mixing up the cells: Sertoli cells respond to FSH, and Leydig cells respond to LH and make testosterone.
- Thinking the egg completes meiosis before ovulation; the secondary oocyte arrests in metaphase II until fertilization.
- Placing the heart or muscle in ectoderm; they are mesoderm, and the nervous system is ectoderm.

solution: Innate immunity responds within minutes to hours, recognizes conserved microbial patterns, has no memory, and uses neutrophils, macrophages, NK cells, complement and cytokines. Adaptive immunity takes days on first exposure, is antigen-specific, has memory, and uses B cells (antibodies) and T cells (CD4 helper, CD8 cytotoxic). Cooperation: macrophages engulf bacteria and present peptides on MHC class II to CD4 T cells, which help B cells make antibodies; the antibodies then opsonize bacteria, making phagocytosis and complement more effective.
