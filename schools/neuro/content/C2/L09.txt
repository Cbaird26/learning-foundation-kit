@article
The circulatory and respiratory systems deliver oxygen and nutrients and remove carbon dioxide and wastes. Together they connect the lungs, where gas exchange occurs, to every tissue.

### The heart and the two circuits

The heart is a double pump. The **right heart** (right atrium and right ventricle) receives deoxygenated blood from the body and pumps it to the lungs through the **pulmonary circuit**. The **left heart** receives oxygenated blood from the lungs and pumps it through the **systemic circuit**. The path of one red blood cell returning from the body is:

$$\text{vena cava} \to \text{right atrium} \to \text{tricuspid valve} \to \text{right ventricle} \to \text{pulmonary valve} \to \text{pulmonary arteries} \to \text{lungs} \to \text{pulmonary veins} \to \text{left atrium} \to \text{mitral valve} \to \text{left ventricle} \to \text{aortic valve} \to \text{aorta}.$$

The pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood and the pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood; arteries and veins are defined by direction relative to the heart, not oxygen content. The **sinoatrial (SA) node** is the pacemaker; the impulse passes to the **AV node**, then through the bundle of His and Purkinje fibers. The ECG shows this activity: the P wave is atrial depolarization, the QRS complex is ventricular depolarization, and the T wave is ventricular repolarization.

### Hemodynamics

Per beat, the ventricle ejects the **stroke volume** (SV $=$ EDV $-$ ESV), and the **ejection fraction** is SV/EDV. **Cardiac output** is
$$\text{CO} = \text{HR} \times \text{SV}.$$
A typical resting adult has a CO of about $5$ L/min. **Mean arterial pressure** (MAP) is estimated as
$$\text{MAP} \approx \text{DBP} + \tfrac{1}{3}(\text{SBP} - \text{DBP}),$$
because diastole lasts longer than systole. Blood flows through arteries, arterioles (the main site of resistance), capillaries (exchange) and veins (which hold most of the blood volume). Starling forces move fluid out of capillaries at the arterial end and back at the venous end; edema results when this balance is disturbed.

### Ventilation and gas exchange

Air moves in when the diaphragm contracts, and the thoracic volume increases, lowering alveolar pressure below atmospheric pressure. Gas exchange occurs by diffusion across the thin alveolar-capillary membrane, driven by partial pressure differences. Alveolar oxygen is estimated with the alveolar gas equation:
$$P_AO_2 = F_iO_2\,(P_{atm} - 47) - \frac{P_aCO_2}{0.8},$$
where $47$ mmHg is the water vapor pressure at body temperature. The **A–a gradient** is $P_AO_2 - P_aO_2$; it is normally small (about $5$–$15$ mmHg in young adults, increasing with age) and widens in ventilation-perfusion mismatch, shunt and diffusion defects, whereas hypoventilation alone has a normal A–a gradient.

### Oxygen transport

Most oxygen is carried by **hemoglobin**, which binds up to four O$_2$ molecules cooperatively, giving a sigmoid **oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve**. A **right shift** (lower affinity, easier unloading) occurs with increased CO$_2$, H$^+$ (lower pH, the Bohr effect), temperature and 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate. Fetal hemoglobin has a higher affinity, a left shift, to take O$_2$ from maternal blood. CO$_2$ is carried mostly as bicarbonate, formed by carbonic anhydrase in red blood cells, plus some bound to hemoglobin and some dissolved. Breathing rate is controlled mainly by medullary centers responding to CO$_2$ and pH through central chemoreceptors, with peripheral chemoreceptors in carotid and aortic bodies responding to low O$_2$.

@example
title: Stroke volume, ejection fraction and cardiac output
problem: A patient's left ventricle has an end-diastolic volume of $120$ mL and an end-systolic volume of $50$ mL. The heart rate is $75$ beats/min. Find the stroke volume, ejection fraction and cardiac output.
step: Stroke volume is $\text{EDV} - \text{ESV} = 120 - 50 = 70$ mL.
step: Ejection fraction is $\text{SV}/\text{EDV} = 70/120 \approx 0.58$, or $58\%$, which is normal.
step: Cardiac output is $\text{HR} \times \text{SV} = 75 \times 70 = 5250$ mL/min.
step: In liters, $\text{CO} = 5.25$ L/min.

@mistakes
- Saying pulmonary veins carry deoxygenated blood; arteries and veins are named by direction, and pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood.
- Using MAP as the simple average of systolic and diastolic pressure.
- Forgetting to subtract the water vapor pressure of $47$ mmHg in the alveolar gas equation.
- Thinking a right shift means hemoglobin binds oxygen more tightly; it binds less tightly and unloads more readily.
