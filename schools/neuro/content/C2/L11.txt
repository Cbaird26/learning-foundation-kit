@article
The immune system protects against pathogens while tolerating the body's own cells. It has two linked parts: **innate immunity**, which is fast and non-specific, and **adaptive immunity**, which is slower, specific and has memory.

### Innate immunity

The first line of defense is physical and chemical barriers: skin, mucus, cilia, stomach acid, lysozyme in tears and saliva, and the normal microbiota. If a pathogen enters, **phagocytes** (neutrophils and macrophages) recognize conserved microbial patterns through **pattern recognition receptors** such as Toll-like receptors, and engulf the microbe. **Natural killer (NK) cells** kill virus-infected and tumor cells that lack MHC class I. The **complement system**, a cascade of plasma proteins, promotes **opsonization**, inflammation and direct lysis. **Cytokines** produced by macrophages cause **inflammation** (redness, heat, swelling, pain) and fever, and **interferons** limit viral replication. Innate responses begin within minutes to hours and do not improve with repeated exposure.

### Adaptive immunity

Adaptive immunity depends on **lymphocytes**. Each B or T cell carries a receptor made by random gene rearrangement, giving a huge repertoire of specificities. According to **clonal selection**, an antigen activates only the cells whose receptors fit it, and these expand into **effector cells** and **memory cells**.

**Antigen-presenting cells** (dendritic cells, macrophages, B cells) display peptides on MHC molecules. **MHC class I** is on all nucleated cells and presents intracellular peptides to **CD8$^+$ cytotoxic T cells**, which kill infected cells. **MHC class II** is on antigen-presenting cells and presents ingested peptides to **CD4$^+$ helper T cells**, which release cytokines that direct both B-cell and CD8 responses. **B cells** differentiate into **plasma cells**, which secrete **antibodies**. Antibodies neutralize toxins and viruses, opsonize bacteria and activate complement. The first antibody class made is **IgM**; after class switching, **IgG** dominates (and crosses the placenta), **IgA** protects mucosal surfaces and breast milk, and **IgE** mediates allergy and parasite defense. The **primary response** takes about $1$ to $2$ weeks to peak and is mostly IgM; the **secondary response** is faster, larger and mostly IgG, because of memory cells.

### Vaccines, tolerance and disease

**Vaccination** exposes the immune system to antigen without disease, generating memory. When enough people are immune, an infection cannot spread efficiently: **herd immunity**. The basic reproduction number $R_0$ is the average number of secondary cases from one case in a fully susceptible population. The threshold fraction that must be immune is
$$1 - \frac{1}{R_0}.$$
Failure of **self-tolerance** causes autoimmune disease, such as type 1 diabetes and rheumatoid arthritis. **Hypersensitivity** reactions are overreactions; type I (IgE-mediated) causes anaphylaxis, for which the first-line treatment is intramuscular epinephrine (a typical adult dose for study is $0.3$ to $0.5$ mg of the $1$ mg/mL solution, to be checked against current references before any clinical use). **HIV** infects CD4$^+$ T cells and, untreated, causes AIDS with opportunistic infections. **Immunodeficiency** can be primary or secondary.

@example
title: Herd immunity threshold
problem: An infection has $R_0 = 3$. What fraction of the population must be immune to prevent sustained spread, assuming a fully mixing population and a perfect vaccine?
step: Each case produces $3$ new cases in a fully susceptible population. To halt spread, the average must fall to $1$.
step: If a fraction $f$ is immune, new cases per case are $R_0(1 - f)$. Set $R_0(1 - f) = 1$.
step: Solve: $f = 1 - 1/R_0 = 1 - 1/3 = 2/3$.
step: About $67\%$ of the population must be immune. An imperfect vaccine would require a higher coverage.

@mistakes
- Saying innate immunity has memory; memory is a hallmark of adaptive immunity.
- Confusing which T cell recognizes which MHC: CD8 with class I, CD4 with class II.
- Believing the first exposure to a pathogen leads to the highest and fastest antibody response; the secondary response is faster and larger.
- Using $R_0$ as a fixed property of a pathogen; it depends on the population and behavior.
