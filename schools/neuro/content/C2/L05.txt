@article
The human body is organized from cells to **tissues**, **organs**, **organ systems** and the whole organism. Every level depends on conditions inside the body staying within a narrow range. This stability is **homeostasis**.

### The four tissue types

- **Epithelial tissue** covers surfaces and lines cavities and glands. Cells sit on a basement membrane and are classified by shape (squamous, cuboidal, columnar) and layers (simple or stratified). Simple squamous epithelium is thin, which suits gas exchange in alveoli. Stratified squamous epithelium resists abrasion in skin. Epithelia form barriers, absorb, secrete and sense.
- **Connective tissue** has cells scattered in an extracellular matrix. It includes loose and dense connective tissue, adipose tissue, cartilage, bone and blood. Fibroblasts make collagen. Blood is a connective tissue with a fluid matrix (plasma).
- **Muscle tissue** contracts: skeletal (voluntary, striated), cardiac (striated, involuntary, with intercalated discs) and smooth (involuntary, non-striated).
- **Nervous tissue** consists of neurons, which conduct signals, and glial cells, which support them.

### Homeostasis and feedback

A **set point** is the target value of a variable such as body temperature (about $37\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$), blood pH, glucose or osmolarity. Maintaining it generally needs four components:

1. a **sensor** (receptor) that detects the variable,
2. a **control center** (integrator) that compares it with the set point,
3. an **effector** that produces a response,
4. the response that changes the variable.

In **negative feedback**, the response opposes the original change, bringing the variable back toward the set point. Most homeostatic loops are negative feedback. In **positive feedback**, the response amplifies the change, driving the process toward completion, and it is not a stabilizing mechanism. Examples include uterine contractions during labor stimulated by oxytocin, platelet plug formation and the rising phase of the action potential.

### Thermoregulation as an example

The temperature sensors are thermoreceptors in skin and the hypothalamus. The **hypothalamus** is the control center. If the body is too warm, effectors include **vasodilation** of skin vessels (more heat radiated), **sweating** (evaporation removes heat) and behavior. If the body is too cold, **vasoconstriction** reduces heat loss, **shivering** and nonshivering thermogenesis by brown fat produce heat, and **piloerection** traps air. Fever is a regulated rise in the hypothalamic set point, usually due to prostaglandin E$_2$ released in response to pyrogens, not a failure of the system. Animals that regulate temperature with their own metabolism are **endotherms**; those that rely mainly on the environment are **ectotherms**.

### Size and heat

Heat is exchanged across the body surface, but it is produced by the volume. A shape with a larger **surface area to volume ratio** loses heat more quickly. For a cube with side $L$, surface area is $6L^2$ and volume is $L^3$, so
$$\frac{\text{SA}}{V} = \frac{6}{L}.$$
Smaller animals therefore lose heat faster per unit mass and need higher metabolic rates per gram, and neonates are more susceptible to hypothermia than adults.

@example
title: Tracing a feedback loop
problem: On a hot day, core temperature rises above the set point. Identify the parts of the negative feedback loop and the response.
step: Stimulus: core temperature rises above about $37\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The sensors are thermoreceptors in skin and the hypothalamus.
step: Control center: the hypothalamus compares the input with the set point and signals effectors.
step: Effectors: sweat glands increase secretion and skin arterioles dilate.
step: Response: evaporation and increased radiation lose heat, so temperature falls back toward the set point, reducing the stimulus. Because the response opposes the change, this is negative feedback.

@mistakes
- Describing negative feedback as "bad" feedback; it is the stabilizing kind, and the word negative refers to the response opposing the change.
- Listing the hypothalamus as the effector; it integrates and sends commands, while sweat glands, vessels and muscles are the effectors.
- Calling fever a malfunction of homeostasis; it is an elevated, regulated set point.
- Confusing connective tissue with the tissues it supports; blood, bone and cartilage are all connective tissue.
