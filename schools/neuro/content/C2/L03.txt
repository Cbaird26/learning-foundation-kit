@article
Most of the diversity of life is microbial. Biologists group living things into three **domains**: **Bacteria**, **Archaea** and **Eukarya**. The first two are prokaryotic; the third includes protists, fungi, plants and animals. Prokaryotes also make up much of the human microbiome, and a few cause serious infection.

### Prokaryotic structure

Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. The chromosome is usually a single circular DNA molecule in the **nucleoid**, often accompanied by small circular **plasmids**. Prokaryotes have **70S ribosomes** (a 30S and a 50S subunit), unlike the 80S ribosomes of eukaryotic cytoplasm. Most bacteria have a cell wall of **peptidoglycan**, a mesh of sugar chains cross-linked by short peptides; archaea have walls without true peptidoglycan. Many bacteria have flagella for motility, pili for attachment and gene exchange, and some form a protective **capsule** or a dormant **endospore**.

### The Gram stain

The **Gram stain** divides most bacteria into two groups by wall structure. **Gram-positive** bacteria have a thick peptidoglycan layer that retains crystal violet and appears purple. **Gram-negative** bacteria have a thin peptidoglycan layer under an **outer membrane** containing lipopolysaccharide (LPS, an endotoxin); they lose the crystal violet and stain pink with the counterstain. The outer membrane makes Gram-negative bacteria less permeable to many antibiotics.

### Growth and genetic exchange

Bacteria divide by **binary fission**. With generation time $g$, a population of $N_0$ cells grows after time $t$ to
$$N = N_0 \cdot 2^{t/g}.$$
Growth in a closed culture has a lag phase, an exponential (log) phase, a stationary phase and a death phase.

Bacteria are asexual, yet they exchange genes by **horizontal gene transfer**: **transformation** (uptake of free DNA), **transduction** (transfer by a bacteriophage) and **conjugation** (transfer of a plasmid through a pilus). Resistance genes on plasmids can spread between species this way, which is why resistance can appear quickly in hospitals.

### Protists

**Protists** are a diverse, non-monophyletic group of mostly unicellular eukaryotes. Medically important examples include *Plasmodium* (malaria, transmitted by *Anopheles* mosquitoes, with a cycle in liver cells and red blood cells), *Giardia*, *Entamoeba histolytica*, *Trypanosoma* and *Toxoplasma*. Many protists have complex life cycles, with different stages in different hosts.

### Fungi

**Fungi** are eukaryotic heterotrophs that digest food externally and absorb the nutrients. Their cell walls contain **chitin**, and their cell membranes contain **ergosterol** rather than the cholesterol of animal cells. Molds grow as multicellular filaments (**hyphae**, collectively a **mycelium**), and yeasts are unicellular. Reproduction is by spores. Fungi cause superficial infections such as tinea and candidiasis, and in immunocompromised patients, invasive disease with *Aspergillus*, *Cryptococcus* or *Pneumocystis jirovecii*.

@example
title: Exponential growth of bacteria
problem: A culture begins with $200$ bacteria that divide every $30$ minutes. How many bacteria are present after $2$ hours, assuming continuous exponential growth?
step: Convert to the same units: $t = 120$ minutes and $g = 30$ minutes.
step: The number of generations is $t/g = 120/30 = 4$.
step: Apply $N = N_0 \cdot 2^{t/g} = 200 \cdot 2^4 = 200 \cdot 16$.
step: $N = 3200$ bacteria.

@mistakes
- Saying Gram-positive bacteria have an outer membrane; it is the Gram-negative bacteria that do.
- Calling mitochondria, a nucleus or membrane-bound organelles features of bacteria; these are eukaryotic.
- Confusing generation time with the time for the whole culture to reach a given size; the exponent is the number of doublings.
- Treating protists as a single evolutionary group; the term is a convenience for eukaryotes that are not plants, animals or fungi.

prompt: A Gram stain of a bacterium isolated from a wound shows purple cocci in clusters. Which statement about this organism's cell envelope is most likely correct?
choice*: It has a thick peptidoglycan layer that retained the crystal violet.
choice: It has an outer membrane containing lipopolysaccharide.
choice: It has no cell wall.
choice: Its wall is made of chitin.
hint: Purple means the stain was retained.
solution: Purple (Gram-positive) cells have thick peptidoglycan and no outer membrane. Chitin is a fungal wall component, and a cell without a wall would not hold the stain in a defined shape.

answer: 512
check: 2**(180/20)
hint: Convert $3$ hours to minutes and divide by the generation time.
hint: There are $9$ generations. Compute $2^9$.
solution: $t/g = 180/20 = 9$, so $N = 1 \cdot 2^9 = 512$.

choice: Transduction
choice*: Conjugation
choice: Binary fission

solution: Conjugation transfers DNA, often a plasmid, between cells in contact through a pilus. Transformation takes up free DNA and transduction uses a phage; binary fission is simple division.

@quiz num
prompt: A culture of $50$ bacteria divides every $30$ minutes. How many bacteria are present after $4$ hours of continuous exponential growth?
answer: 12800
check: 50 * 2**(240/30)
hint: Count the number of generations in $4$ hours.
hint: There are $8$ generations. Multiply $50$ by $2^8$.
solution: $t/g = 240/30 = 8$, so $N = 50 \cdot 256 = 12{,}800$.
