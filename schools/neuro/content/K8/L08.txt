@article
**Electricity** is energy that flows through wires. It lights lamps and runs toys.

### Circuits
A **circuit** is a path that electricity can go around. A simple circuit needs a battery, wires and something to power, such as a small bulb. The path must be a closed loop. If you open it with a switch or a gap, the electricity stops and the bulb goes out.

### Conductors and insulators
A **conductor** lets electricity pass easily. Metals such as copper and steel are conductors. An **insulator** does not. Plastic, rubber, wood and glass are insulators. Wires are copper inside and plastic outside, which keeps us safe.

**Safety:** only use a small battery with a grown-up. Never put anything in a wall outlet. Outlets are dangerous.

### Magnets
A **magnet** pulls on things made of iron or steel, such as a paper clip. It does not pull wood, plastic or paper. Every magnet has two **poles**, north and south. Opposite poles attract. Like poles repel (push away).

### A fair test with a ramp
A scientist changes only one thing. This is the **variable**. Try a ramp for a toy car. Change the ramp height. Keep the same car, the same ramp and the same floor. Measure how far the car rolls after the ramp, and try each height 3 times. A table might look like this:

| Ramp height | Distance rolled |
|---|---|
| 10 cm | 40 cm |
| 20 cm | 80 cm |
| 30 cm | 120 cm |

A higher ramp gives the car more motion energy, so it goes farther.

@example
title: Will the bulb light?
problem: A battery, a bulb and two wires are joined in a loop, but one wire is not touching the bulb. Will it light?
step: A circuit must be a closed loop.
step: The loose wire leaves a gap, so electricity cannot flow.
step: The bulb will not light. Touch the wire to the bulb to close the loop.

@example
title: Average distance
problem: A car rolls 58 cm, 60 cm and 62 cm in 3 tries. What is the average?
step: Add the distances: $58 + 60 + 62 = 180$.
step: Divide by the number of tries: $180 \div 3 = 60$ cm.

@mistakes
- Thinking a bulb lights with only one wire. A closed loop is needed.
- Thinking that magnets attract everything. They only pull on some metals, such as iron and steel.
- Changing two things at once in a test, so you do not know what caused the result.

@quiz num
