@article
### Factors

A **factor** of a number is a number that divides it with nothing left over. Factors come in pairs that multiply to make the number.

The factors of $12$ are $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12$. The pairs are $1 \times 12$, $2 \times 6$ and $3 \times 4$.

To find all the factors, start at $1$ and test each counting number. Ask, "Does it go in evenly?" Stop when the pairs start to repeat.

### Multiples

A **multiple** of a number is what you get when you multiply it by $1, 2, 3, 4, \dots$. It is the same as skip counting.

The multiples of $5$ are $5, 10, 15, 20, 25, \dots$. They go on forever. Factors are small and few. Multiples are big and endless.

### Prime and composite

A **prime number** has exactly two factors: $1$ and itself. The first primes are $2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23$.

A **composite number** has more than two factors. $12$ is composite because it has six factors.

The number $1$ is special. It has only one factor, so it is neither prime nor composite. The number $2$ is the only even prime. Every other even number can be shared into groups of two, so it has $2$ as a factor.

### A quick test

To see if a number is prime, try dividing by small primes such as $2, 3, 5$ and $7$. If none go in evenly, the number is prime (for numbers up to $100$).

Look at the number $21$. It is odd, but $3 \times 7 = 21$. So $21$ is composite. Many people think it is prime. Always test it.

@example
title: Factors of 20
problem: List all the factors of $20$ and say whether $20$ is prime or composite.
step: Test each number from $1$. $1 \times 20 = 20$, so $1$ and $20$ are factors. $2 \times 10 = 20$, so $2$ and $10$ are factors.
step: $3$ does not go in evenly. $4 \times 5 = 20$, so $4$ and $5$ are factors. Next is $5$, which is already in a pair, so we can stop.
step: The factors are $1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20$. That is more than two factors, so $20$ is composite.

@mistakes
- Thinking $1$ is a prime number. It has only one factor.
- Thinking every odd number is prime. Numbers such as $9$, $15$ and $21$ are odd but composite.
- Mixing up factors and multiples. Factors divide the number. Multiples are what you get by skip counting.

@practice num
prompt: How many factors does $18$ have?
answer: 6
check: len([i for i in range(1, 19) if 18 % i == 0])
hint: Find pairs that multiply to make $18$. Start with $1 \times 18$.
hint: The pairs are $1 \times 18$, $2 \times 9$ and one more. Count every number in the pairs.
solution: The pairs are $1 \times 18$, $2 \times 9$ and $3 \times 6$. The factors are $1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18$. That is $6$ factors.

answer: 42

hint: $7, 14, 21, 28, 35, \dots$ Keep going until you have said six numbers.
solution: Skip counting by sevens gives $7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42$. The $6$th multiple is $42$.

@practice num
prompt: What is the smallest prime number that is bigger than $20$?
answer: 23
check: next(n for n in range(21, 100) if all(n % d for d in range(2, n)))
hint: Test $21$, $22$, $23$ one at a time. Is each one divisible by something besides $1$ and itself?
hint: $21 = 3 \times 7$ and $22 = 2 \times 11$. Try the next number.
solution: $21 = 3 \times 7$ and $22 = 2 \times 11$ are composite. $23$ has only the factors $1$ and $23$, so it is prime.

@practice num
prompt: How many factor pairs does $36$ have? Count $6 \times 6$ as one pair.
answer: 5
check: sum(1 for i in range(1, 37) if 36 % i == 0 and i * i <= 36)
hint: List the pairs in order, starting with $1 \times 36$.
hint: After $1 \times 36$ come $2 \times 18$, $3 \times 12$ and $4 \times 9$. What comes next?
solution: The pairs are $1 \times 36$, $2 \times 18$, $3 \times 12$, $4 \times 9$ and $6 \times 6$. That is $5$ pairs.

@quiz num
prompt: What is the biggest number that is a factor of both $12$ and $30$?
answer: 6
check: math.gcd(12, 30)
hint: List the factors of $12$, then the factors of $30$.
hint: Look for the numbers that appear in both lists and pick the biggest.
solution: The factors of $12$ are $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12$. The factors of $30$ are $1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30$. The biggest in both lists is $6$.
