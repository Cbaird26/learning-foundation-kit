@article
A linear function adds the same amount at every step. An **exponential function** multiplies by the same factor at every step:

$$f(x) = a\,b^x, \qquad a \neq 0,\ b > 0,\ b \neq 1.$$

Here $a$ is the **initial value** (the output at $x = 0$) and $b$ is the **growth factor**: each time $x$ increases by $1$, the output is multiplied by $b$.

### Growth, decay and percent change

- If $b > 1$ the function grows. If $0 < b < 1$ it decays.
- A change of $r$ per step (written as a decimal) gives $b = 1 + r$ for growth and $b = 1 - r$ for decay. A $5\%$ increase has $b = 1.05$; a $20\%$ loss has $b = 0.8$.
- Percentages **compound**: two years of $10\%$ growth multiply by $1.1^2 = 1.21$, which is $21\%$ and not $20\%$.

For $a > 0$ the graph has domain all reals, range $(0, \infty)$, $y$-intercept $a$ and the horizontal asymptote $y = 0$. It gets as close to the axis as you like but never touches it. A shifted version $f(x) = a\,b^{x-h} + k$ has horizontal asymptote $y = k$.

### Doubling time and half-life

If a quantity doubles every $T$ units of time, then $N(t) = N_0 \cdot 2^{t/T}$. If it halves every $T$ units (half-life), then $N(t) = N_0 \cdot \left(\tfrac12\right)^{t/T}$. The exponent counts how many doublings or halvings have happened, so keep $t$ and $T$ in the same units.

### Compound interest and the number $e$

Invest principal $P$ at annual rate $r$ compounded $n$ times per year for $t$ years:

$$A = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}.$$

As $n$ grows without bound, $\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n$ approaches the irrational number $e \approx 2.71828$, and the formula becomes **continuous compounding**:

$$A = Pe^{rt}.$$

### Finding $a$ and $b$ from two points

If $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ lie on $y = ab^x$, divide the equations to cancel $a$:

$$b^{\,x_2 - x_1} = \frac{y_2}{y_1} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad b = \left(\frac{y_2}{y_1}\right)^{1/(x_2 - x_1)},\qquad a = \frac{y_1}{b^{x_1}}.$$

Take the positive root: $b$ must be positive. Always finish by checking both points in your model.

@example
title: Fit a model to two measurements
problem: A culture has $480$ cells after $1$ hour and $1080$ cells after $3$ hours. Model it as $N(t) = ab^t$, then find the count after $5$ hours and the percent growth per hour.
step: Divide the two equations: $b^{3-1} = \dfrac{1080}{480} = 2.25$, so $b^2 = 2.25$ and $b = 1.5$ (positive root).
step: Find $a$ from the first point: $a \cdot 1.5 = 480$, so $a = 320$. The model is $N(t) = 320 \cdot 1.5^t$.
step: Check the second point: $320 \cdot 1.5^3 = 320 \cdot 3.375 = 1080$. It matches.
step: After $5$ hours: $N(5) = 320 \cdot 7.59375 = 2430$ cells.
step: Since $b = 1.5 = 1 + 0.5$, the culture grows by $50\%$ per hour.

@example
title: Compounding quarterly versus continuously
problem: Invest \$2000 at $4\%$ per year for $5$ years. Compare quarterly compounding with continuous compounding.
step: Quarterly: $n = 4$, so $A = 2000\left(1 + \frac{0.04}{4}\right)^{20} = 2000(1.01)^{20}$.
step: Since $1.01^{20} \approx 1.22019$, the balance is $A \approx \$2440.38$.
step: Continuous: $A = 2000\,e^{0.04 \cdot 5} = 2000\,e^{0.2} \approx 2000(1.221403) \approx \$2442.81$.
step: Continuous compounding is slightly larger. More frequent compounding always helps, but the gain levels off quickly.

@mistakes
- Using the rate itself as the base: $5\%$ growth means $b = 1.05$, not $b = 0.05$.
- Adding percentages over several periods instead of multiplying the factors.
- Mixing units, such as a half-life in days with a time in hours, or a monthly rate with a time in years.
- Expecting an exponential to reach its asymptote. It approaches the line without ever touching it.

@practice num
prompt: A sample of an isotope has a half-life of $8$ days. Starting with $80$ g, how many grams remain after $24$ days?
answer: 10
check: 80 * 0.5**(24/8)
unit: g
hint: Count how many half-lives fit into $24$ days.
hint: There are $3$ half-lives, so the mass is halved three times.
solution: $24/8 = 3$ half-lives, so the mass is $80 \cdot (1/2)^3 = 80/8 = 10$ g.

@practice num
prompt: A town of $12000$ people grows by $3\%$ per year. How many people live there after $4$ years, rounded to the nearest whole person?
answer: 13506
check: 12000 * 1.03**4
tol: 0.5
hint: A $3\%$ increase multiplies the population by $1.03$ each year.
hint: Raise $1.03$ to the number of years and multiply by $12000$.
solution: $P = 12000(1.03)^4 = 12000(1.12550881) \approx 13506.1$, which rounds to $13506$.

@practice mc
prompt: A machine loses $20\%$ of its value each year. If it starts at value $V_0$, which formula gives its value after $t$ years?
choice*: $V_0(0.8)^t$
choice: $V_0(0.2)^t$
choice: $V_0(1.2)^t$
choice: $V_0 - 0.2t$
hint: Losing $20\%$ means keeping what fraction?
hint: Each year the value is multiplied by the fraction it keeps. A fixed multiplier means an exponential model, not a linear one.
solution: Each year the machine keeps $80\%$ of its value, so it is multiplied by $0.8$ every year: $V_0(0.8)^t$. The base $0.2$ would keep only $20\%$, $1.2$ would be growth, and $V_0 - 0.2t$ subtracts a fixed amount rather than a fixed fraction.

@practice tuple
prompt: For $g(x) = 4\cdot 3^x - 5$, find the horizontal asymptote $y = k$ and the $y$-intercept. Enter them as an ordered pair $(k,\ y\text{-intercept})$.
answer: (-5, -1)
check: (lambda g: (g(-1000.0), g(0)))(lambda x: 4*3**x - 5)
hint: As $x \to -\infty$, what does $3^x$ approach?
hint: The $y$-intercept is the value at $x = 0$, and $3^0 = 1$.
solution: As $x \to -\infty$, $3^x \to 0$, so $g(x) \to -5$ and the asymptote is $y = -5$. At $x = 0$: $g(0) = 4 \cdot 1 - 5 = -1$.

@practice num
prompt: A colony grows by $2\%$ per month. What is the equivalent annual growth rate, in percent, rounded to two decimal places?
answer: 26.82
check: (1.02**12 - 1) * 100
tol: 0.01
unit: %
hint: After $12$ months the colony has been multiplied by the monthly factor twelve times.
hint: The annual factor is $1.02^{12}$. Subtract $1$ to turn a factor into a rate.
solution: $1.02^{12} \approx 1.26824$, so the colony grows by about $26.82\%$ per year, which is more than $12 \times 2\% = 24\%$ because of compounding.

@quiz num
prompt: Invest \$1500 at $6\%$ annual interest compounded monthly. Find the balance after $3$ years, to the nearest cent.
answer: 1795.02
check: 1500 * (1 + 0.06/12)**(12*3)
tol: 0.01
unit: dollars
hint: Monthly compounding means $n = 12$ and the rate per period is $0.06/12$.
hint: Use $A = P(1 + r/n)^{nt}$ with $nt = 36$.
solution: $A = 1500(1.005)^{36} \approx 1500(1.19668) \approx 1795.02$ dollars.

@quiz expr
prompt: A sample has mass $40$ g at $t = 0$ hours and $10$ g at $t = 6$ hours, and it decays exponentially. Write its mass $m(t)$ in grams.
vars: t
lead: $m(t) =$
answer: 40*2^(-t/3)
check: lambda t: 40 * (10/40)**(t/6)
hint: The mass falls to a quarter in $6$ hours. How many half-lives is that?
hint: A quarter is two halvings, so the half-life is $3$ hours.
solution: A drop from $40$ to $10$ is two halvings in $6$ hours, so the half-life is $3$ hours and $m(t) = 40 \cdot 2^{-t/3}$. Check: $m(6) = 40 \cdot 2^{-2} = 10$.
