@article
Lesson 4 gave us axioms with frame conditions. Adding axioms to K produces stronger logics, and the three most famous are named by the conditions they impose: reflexivity, then transitivity, then symmetry.

### The systems

Each system below contains K and is closed under modus ponens and necessitation. They differ in the further axioms:

| system | axioms added to K | frames for which it is sound and complete |
|---|---|---|
| T | $\Box A \to A$ | reflexive frames |
| S4 | T and $\Box A \to \Box\Box A$ | reflexive and transitive frames |
| S5 | T and $\Diamond A \to \Box\Diamond A$ | reflexive and Euclidean frames, i.e. equivalence relations |

"Sound and complete for a class" means exactly: a formula is a theorem of the system if and only if it is valid on every frame in the class. Soundness comes from the correspondences of Lesson 4, completeness from the canonical model construction. S5 is also the logic of the **universal** frames, those in which every world sees every world: a formula's truth at a world depends only on the worlds reachable from it, and in an equivalence frame that is just its own class, which is a universal frame. S5 can equally be axiomatized as S4 plus B, or as T plus B plus 4. In S5, B and 4 are theorems; Lesson 6 proves this.

### The hierarchy is strict

Every theorem of K is a theorem of T, of T is a theorem of S4, and of S4 is a theorem of S5. The inclusions are proper:

- T does not prove $\Box A \to \Box\Box A$. Take $W = \{1, 2, 3\}$ and $R = \{(1,1), (2,2), (3,3), (1,2), (2,3)\}$, which is reflexive but not transitive, with $P$ true at 1 and 2 only. At 1, $\Box P$ is true (1 and 2 make $P$ true), but $\Box\Box P$ is false, since 1 sees 2 and 2 sees 3, where $P$ fails.
- S4 proves neither $A \to \Box\Diamond A$ nor $\Diamond A \to \Box\Diamond A$. Take $W = \{1, 2\}$, $R = \{(1,1), (1,2), (2,2)\}$ (reflexive and transitive, but not symmetric) with $P$ true at 1 only. At 1, $P$ and $\Diamond P$ are true, but 2 sees only itself, so $\Diamond P$ is false at 2, and $\Box\Diamond P$ is false at 1.
- Without 4, reflexive and symmetric frames give only the system KTB (usually just called B): the relation on $\{1, 2, 3\}$ in which each world sees itself and 1 and 3 each see 2 (and 2 sees both) is reflexive and symmetric but not transitive.

### Reductions of modalities

The more axioms, the fewer genuinely different modalities. In **S4**, $\Box\Box A \leftrightarrow \Box A$ and $\Diamond\Diamond A \leftrightarrow \Diamond A$, so repeated operators collapse. In **S5**, even mixed strings collapse to the last operator: $\Diamond\Box A \leftrightarrow \Box A$ and $\Box\Diamond A \leftrightarrow \Diamond A$. Consequently, every formula of S5 is equivalent to one with no nested modal operators. Whatever is possibly necessary is necessary, and whatever is necessarily possible is possible: **there is no higher-order contingency in S5**.

### Which system for which necessity?

The answer depends on what $\Box$ means.

- *Metaphysical necessity*: S5 is the usual choice in the literature on possible worlds, and it is the logic of Lewis's modal realism, where every world is possible relative to every other. Some, like Salmon, have argued that axiom 4 fails (Lesson 4).
- *Knowledge*: S4 and S5 are both used. Axiom 4 is positive introspection (if Ana knows, she knows that she knows); axiom 5 is negative introspection, which many philosophers reject for knowledge of humans. Models of idealized agents often use S5.
- *Belief*: the system KD45 (D, 4 and 5 without T) is standard, because belief need not be true.
- *Obligation*: not T (Lesson 10); the standard system adds only D.
- *Time*: for "it will always be", reflexivity depends on whether "always" includes now.

Here is the argument for S5 as the logic of metaphysical necessity, in standard form:

| P1 | Metaphysical necessity is truth at all metaphysically possible worlds, and every such world is possible relative to every other: accessibility is universal. |
| P2 | A formula is valid on every frame with universal accessibility if and only if it is a theorem of S5. |
| C | The logic of metaphysical necessity is S5: a formula is valid for metaphysical necessity exactly when it is a theorem of S5. |

The argument is valid. P2 is a theorem of modal logic and can be accepted by everyone; the substantive claim is P1. Its best defense is that a "possible world" is just a complete way things could have been, and what could have been is not relative to the world we start from. The best objection is that possibility itself may depend on how things are: Salmon's table argument suggests that a world reachable by a series of small changes is not thereby possible from the start, and then accessibility is not transitive. A reply defends origin essentialism and says that the chain premises are the weak spot.

@example
title: Separating S4 from S5
problem: Show that $P \to \Box\Diamond P$ is not a theorem of S4, but is a theorem of S5 (by soundness and completeness).
step: S4 is sound for reflexive and transitive frames. If there is a reflexive transitive frame on which the formula is invalid, the formula is not a theorem of S4.
step: Take $W = \{1, 2\}$, $R = \{(1,1), (1,2), (2,2)\}$. This is reflexive, and transitive (the only chains end in $(2, 2)$ or repeat a loop), and not symmetric. Let $P$ be true at 1 only.
step: At 1, $P$ is true. At 2, $\Diamond P$ needs a successor with $P$; the only successor is 2, which lacks $P$. So $\Diamond P$ is false at 2, and since $1R2$, $\Box\Diamond P$ is false at 1. The formula fails at 1.
step: In S5 the formula is valid on every equivalence frame: if $P$ holds at $w$ and $wRv$, then $vRw$ by symmetry, so $\Diamond P$ holds at $v$. By completeness it is a theorem of S5.
answer: $P \to \Box\Diamond P$ (axiom B) is not a theorem of S4, because of the reflexive transitive countermodel above, but it is a theorem of S5.

@example
title: The collapse of $\Diamond\Box A$ in S5
problem: Show that $\Diamond\Box A \leftrightarrow \Box A$ is valid on every equivalence frame.
step: ($\to$) Suppose $\Diamond\Box A$ is true at $w$: there is $v$ with $wRv$ and $\Box A$ true at $v$. Let $u$ be any world with $wRu$.
step: By symmetry $vRw$, and by transitivity $vRw$ and $wRu$ give $vRu$. Since $\Box A$ is true at $v$, $A$ is true at $u$.
step: As $u$ was arbitrary, $\Box A$ is true at $w$.
step: ($\leftarrow$) Suppose $\Box A$ is true at $w$. By reflexivity $wRw$, and $\Box A$ is true at $w$, so $\Diamond\Box A$ is true at $w$.
answer: The formula is valid on equivalence frames, so by completeness $\Diamond\Box A \leftrightarrow \Box A$ is a theorem of S5.

@mistakes
- Thinking that an S5 frame must be universal: equivalence relations can have several classes, and S5 is complete for both kinds of frames only because truth at a world depends on its own class.
- Believing that T already contains 4, or that reflexive and symmetric suffices for S5: a reflexive symmetric frame need not be transitive.
- Taking S5 to say that everything is necessary: it only collapses *iterated* modalities. $P$ can be true while $\Box P$ is false.
- Treating the choice of system as a purely formal matter: which axioms hold depends on how $\Box$ is read.

model:
```python
def systems(W, R):
    """Systems for which the frame (W, R) is a frame of the system."""
    reflexive = all((w, w) in R for w in W)
    symmetric = all((b, a) in R for (a, b) in R)
    transitive = all((a, c) in R for (a, b) in R for (b2, c) in R
                     if b == b2)
    found = ['K']
    if reflexive:
        found.append('T')
        if transitive:
            found.append('S4')
            if symmetric:
                found.append('S5')
    return sorted(found)
```
