@article
Think of a cell as a tiny factory. Different workers do different jobs, and each job happens in a part called an **organelle** ("little organ").

### Parts every cell has

- **Cell membrane**: a thin, flexible boundary that controls what enters and leaves the cell.
- **Cytoplasm**: the jelly-like fluid inside the cell where the organelles float.
- **Ribosomes**: tiny structures that build proteins.

### Parts in plant and animal cells

- **Nucleus**: holds the cell's DNA, the instructions for making proteins, and directs the cell's activities.
- **Mitochondria**: release usable energy from food in a process called cellular respiration. Both plant and animal cells have them.
- **Vacuoles**: storage bubbles for water, food and wastes. A plant cell usually has one large central vacuole. Animal cells have small ones.
- **Endoplasmic reticulum** and **Golgi apparatus**: help make, fold, package and ship proteins and other materials.

### Parts found only in plant cells

- **Cell wall**: a stiff layer outside the membrane made mostly of cellulose. It gives the cell support and a fixed shape.
- **Chloroplasts**: contain green chlorophyll and carry out **photosynthesis**, which uses light, water and carbon dioxide to make sugar and oxygen.

Animal cells lack a cell wall and chloroplasts, so they are usually rounder and more flexible, and they must get food from outside.

### Comparing the two

| Feature | Plant cell | Animal cell |
|---|---|---|
| Cell wall | Yes | No |
| Chloroplasts | Yes | No |
| Central vacuole | Large | Small or none |
| Mitochondria | Yes | Yes |
| Typical shape | Boxy | Round or irregular |

### Making a good diagram

Use a ruler, draw large, and label with straight lines that touch the part. Give the drawing a title and say which cell it shows. If your diagram has a scale bar, you can measure the cell: if a bar labeled 10 µm is 20 mm long, then 1 mm of drawing is 0.5 µm.

@example
title: Using a scale bar
problem: In a drawing of a plant cell, a scale bar labeled 10 µm is 25 mm long. The cell in the drawing is 100 mm long. How long is the real cell?
step: Work out how many micrometers one millimeter of drawing stands for: $10\ \mu\text{m} \div 25\ \text{mm} = 0.4\ \mu\text{m/mm}$.
step: Multiply by the length of the cell in the drawing: $100 \times 0.4 = 40\ \mu\text{m}$.
step: Check the answer in another way: the cell is $100 \div 25 = 4$ scale bars long, and $4 \times 10 = 40\ \mu\text{m}$.

@mistakes
- Saying animal cells have no energy-making parts; they have mitochondria, just like plant cells.
- Saying the cell wall and the cell membrane are the same thing; the membrane is inside the wall and controls what passes.
- Believing plant cells do not need mitochondria because they have chloroplasts; chloroplasts make sugar and mitochondria release energy from it.
