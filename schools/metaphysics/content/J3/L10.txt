@article
In a **right triangle**, the side opposite the right angle is the **hypotenuse**, the longest side. The other two sides are the **legs**. The **Pythagorean theorem** says
$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2,$$
where $a$ and $b$ are the legs and $c$ is the hypotenuse. It applies **only** to right triangles.

### Finding the hypotenuse

If the legs are $6$ and $8$: $c^2 = 36 + 64 = 100$, so $c = \sqrt{100} = 10$. When $c^2$ is not a perfect square, leave the answer as a radical, such as $\sqrt{34}$, or estimate it.

### Finding a leg

Rearrange: $a^2 = c^2 - b^2$. With hypotenuse $13$ and one leg $5$: $a^2 = 169 - 25 = 144$, so $a = 12$. Subtract, never add, when the unknown is a leg.

### Pythagorean triples

Whole-number solutions are called **Pythagorean triples**: $(3, 4, 5)$, $(5, 12, 13)$, $(8, 15, 17)$ and $(7, 24, 25)$. Any multiple, such as $(6, 8, 10)$, is again a triple.

### The converse

If the side lengths of a triangle satisfy $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ with $c$ the longest side, then the triangle **is** a right triangle. If $a^2 + b^2 \neq c^2$, it is not. For sides $4, 5, 6$: $16 + 25 = 41$ but $6^2 = 36$, so it is not a right triangle.

### Distance in the coordinate plane

The distance between $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose legs are the horizontal and vertical changes:
$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}.$$
From $(2, 1)$ to $(5, 5)$ the legs are $3$ and $4$, so $d = 5$.

### Combining with similar triangles

Real problems often use similarity first and the theorem second. A $1.8$ m person casts a $2.4$ m shadow. The person, shadow and sun's ray form a right triangle, with legs $1.8$ and $2.4$ and hypotenuse $\sqrt{1.8^2 + 2.4^2} = 3$ m. A tree in the same light is similar (the right angle and the sun angle match, so AA applies), which gives the tree's height from its shadow length, and the theorem then gives the distance along the ray from the treetop to the tip of its shadow.

@example
title: Finding a leg
problem: A right triangle has a leg of length $7$ and a hypotenuse of length $25$. Find the other leg.
step: Write the theorem with the unknown leg $a$: $a^2 + 7^2 = 25^2$.
step: Isolate $a^2$: $a^2 = 625 - 49 = 576$.
step: Take the positive square root: $a = 24$.
step: Check: $49 + 576 = 625 = 25^2$.

@mistakes
- Adding the squares when the unknown side is a leg; you must subtract.
- Forgetting to take the square root at the end and reporting $c^2$.
- Applying the theorem to a triangle that is not a right triangle.
- Using the converse with a shorter side as $c$; $c$ must be the longest side.

@practice self
prompt: A $1.8$ m person casts a $2.4$ m shadow. At the same moment a tree casts a $10$ m shadow. Explain why the two triangles formed are similar, find the tree's height, and then find the distance from the top of the tree to the tip of its shadow.
solution: Each object is vertical, so each triangle has a right angle, and the sun's rays hit the ground at the same angle for both; two equal angles make the triangles similar by AA. The scale factor from the person's shadow to the tree's shadow is $\frac{10}{2.4}$, so the height is $1.8 \times \frac{10}{2.4} = 7.5$ m. The distance along the ray is the hypotenuse: $\sqrt{7.5^2 + 10^2} = \sqrt{156.25} = 12.5$ m.
rubric: Names the two equal angle pairs (right angle and sun angle) and concludes similarity by AA.
rubric: Sets up the proportion $\frac{h}{10} = \frac{1.8}{2.4}$ and finds $h = 7.5$ m.
rubric: Applies the Pythagorean theorem with legs $7.5$ and $10$ to get $12.5$ m.
hint: Draw both triangles and label the equal angles.
hint: Set up a proportion of height to shadow length.
hint: For the last part, the ray is the hypotenuse of the tree's right triangle.
