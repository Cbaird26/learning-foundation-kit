objective: Solve a volume formula for a missing radius or height.

@article
**Volume** measures how much space a solid takes up, in cubic units such as cm$^3$ or m$^3$. The three round solids of 8th grade each have a formula that you can understand rather than only memorize.

### Cylinders

A cylinder is a stack of circles. The area of one circular layer is $\pi r^2$, and the stack has height $h$, so
$$V = \pi r^2 h.$$
For $r = 3$ and $h = 5$: $V = \pi (9)(5) = 45\pi \approx 141.4$ cubic units. Leave the answer in terms of $\pi$ for an exact value.

### Cones

A cone has the same base and height as a cylinder but holds exactly **one third** as much. So
$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h.$$
You can check this by filling a cone with sand and pouring it into a cylinder of the same base and height: it takes three cones to fill it.

### Spheres

A sphere of radius $r$ has volume
$$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3.$$
The radius is **cubed**, not squared, because volume is three-dimensional. For $r = 3$: $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi (27) = 36\pi$.

### Radius or diameter?

Formulas use the **radius**, half the diameter. A cylinder with diameter $10$ has radius $5$. Reading the diameter as the radius is a very common error.

### Working backwards

To find a missing dimension, substitute what you know and solve. A cone has radius $5$ and volume $50\pi$. Then $50\pi = \frac{1}{3}\pi (25) h$. Cancel $\pi$ and multiply by $3$: $150 = 25h$, so $h = 6$.

### How volume scales

Doubling the radius of a sphere multiplies the volume by $2^3 = 8$. Doubling the radius of a cylinder (height fixed) multiplies its volume by $2^2 = 4$, but doubling its height only doubles the volume.

### Units

Volume is in cubic units. If the dimensions are in meters, the volume is in cubic meters. One liter equals $1000$ cm$^3$, which is useful for liquids.

@example
title: Volume of a cylinder
problem: A can has radius $4$ cm and height $9$ cm. Find its volume in terms of $\pi$.
step: Write the formula: $V = \pi r^2 h$.
step: Substitute $r = 4$ and $h = 9$: $V = \pi (4^2)(9)$.
step: Compute: $4^2 = 16$ and $16 \cdot 9 = 144$, so $V = 144\pi$ cm$^3$.
step: As a decimal, $144\pi \approx 452.4$ cm$^3$.

@mistakes
- Using the diameter in place of the radius.
- Forgetting the factor $\frac{1}{3}$ in the cone formula.
- Squaring the radius in the sphere formula instead of cubing it.
- Writing area units (cm$^2$) instead of volume units (cm$^3$).

@practice num
prompt: A cylinder has radius $3$ and height $5$. Find its volume. Give an exact answer in terms of $\pi$.
answer: 45pi
check: pi * 3**2 * 5
hint: Use $V = \pi r^2 h$.
hint: $r^2 = 9$.
solution: $V = \pi (9)(5) = 45\pi$.

@practice num
prompt: A cone has radius $6$ and height $5$. Find its volume. Give an exact answer in terms of $\pi$.
answer: 60pi
check: pi * 6**2 * 5 / 3
hint: A cone is one third of the cylinder with the same base and height.
hint: $\frac{1}{3} \cdot 36 \cdot 5$.
solution: $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi (36)(5) = 60\pi$.

@practice num
prompt: A sphere has radius $3$. Find its volume. Give an exact answer in terms of $\pi$.
answer: 36pi
check: 4/3 * pi * 3**3
hint: Use $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$ and cube the radius.
hint: $3^3 = 27$.
solution: $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi (27) = 36\pi$.

@practice num
prompt: A cone has radius $5$ and volume $50\pi$. Find its height.
answer: 6
check: 3 * 50 / 5**2
hint: Substitute into $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$ and cancel $\pi$.
hint: $50 = \frac{1}{3}(25)h$.
solution: $150 = 25h$, so $h = 6$.

@practice mc

choice: It doubles.
choice: It is multiplied by $4$.
choice*: It is multiplied by $8$.
choice: It is multiplied by $6$.
hint: Volume depends on the cube of the radius.
solution: $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$, so replacing $r$ with $2r$ gives $\frac{4}{3}\pi (8r^3)$, which is $8$ times the original.

@quiz num
prompt: A cylindrical tank has diameter $10$ m and height $4$ m. Find its volume in cubic meters, in terms of $\pi$.
answer: 100pi
check: pi * (10/2)**2 * 4
hint: The formula uses the radius, which is half the diameter.
hint: $r = 5$.
solution: $V = \pi (5^2)(4) = 100\pi$ m$^3$.

@quiz num
prompt: A sphere has volume $288\pi$ cubic centimeters. Find its radius in centimeters.
answer: 6
check: round((288 * 3 / 4) ** (1/3))
unit: cm
hint: Set $288\pi = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$ and solve for $r^3$.
hint: $r^3 = 288 \cdot \frac{3}{4}$.
solution: $r^3 = 216$, so $r = 6$ cm since $6^3 = 216$.
