@lesson
title: Changing your mind for a good reason
summary: Tell a good reason for changing your mind from a poor one, and tell about a time you changed your mind and why.
objective: Say what it means to change your mind.
objective: Tell a good reason for changing your mind from a poor one.
objective: Tell about a time you changed your mind and why.
reading: Teaching Children Philosophy (Prindle Institute for Ethics): the pages about open-minded discussion and changing your mind.

@article
Grown-up helper: read aloud, and share a small example of your own, like trying a food you did not like at first.

Have you ever thought, "I do not like that food," and then tried it and found you did like it? You changed your mind.

**Changing your mind** means thinking something different from before. It is not weak or silly. It is what good thinkers do when they learn something new.

But the reason matters. A **good reason** helps you see something true or helpful. A **poor reason** does not.

Here are some good reasons to change your mind:
- You learn a new fact. ("I thought the bowl was empty, but I looked, and it has berries.")
- You hear a good reason from someone. ("Mia said that sharing makes the game more fun, and that makes sense.")
- You try something and see for yourself.

Here are some poor reasons:
- Someone is louder than you.
- Someone says, "Everyone thinks so," but gives no reason.
- You are tired of talking.

When you change your mind, you can say, "I used to think... but now I think... because..." This tells people your reason.

It is also fine to keep your mind the same, if the reasons you hear are not better than yours. You can say, "I heard your reason, and I still think the same, because..."

The best thinkers listen and think about reasons. They are not afraid to say, "I changed my mind."

Today's job: tell about a time you changed your mind and why. A little example is perfect, such as a game, a food or a book.

@example
title: Good reason or poor reason?
problem: Ana thinks the zoo is a good place for animals. Her friend shows her a fact: the tigers have a small cage. Ana changes her mind. Another time Ana's friend just shouts "You are wrong!" and Ana changes her mind. Which time was the reason good?
step: In the first case, Ana learned something new. The size of the cage is a fact that helps her think.
step: In the second case, the friend only shouted. Shouting does not give a reason about animals or zoos.
step: So the first time was a good reason, and the second was a poor one.
answer: Learning about the small cage was a good reason. Being shouted at was a poor reason.

@mistakes
- Thinking you must never change your mind. Good thinkers change their minds when they have a good reason.
- Changing your mind just because someone is louder or bigger.
- Forgetting to say your reason. Say "I changed my mind because..."

@practice mc
prompt: Which is a good reason to change your mind?
choice: Someone shouted louder than you.
choice*: You learned a new fact that you had not known.
choice: You were tired of talking.
choice: Someone said "Everyone says so" and gave no reason.
hint: A good reason helps you see something true.
solution: A new fact is a good reason. Loudness, tiredness and "everyone says so" do not tell you anything about the question.

@practice mc
prompt: Which sentence says why you changed your mind?
choice: I changed my mind.
choice*: I used to think the soup was yucky, but now I like it because I tried it.
choice: Soup is hot.
choice: I like soup.
hint: Look for "I used to think... but now... because..."
solution: The sentence with "used to think... but now... because" tells what changed and why.

@practice ms
prompt: Which are good reasons to change your mind? Pick all that are good.
choice*: A friend gives a reason that makes sense.
choice: A friend yells at you.
choice*: You try something and see it for yourself.
choice*: You read a new fact in a book.
hint: A good reason helps you see what is true.
solution: A reason that makes sense, trying it yourself and learning a new fact are good reasons. Yelling is not.

@practice num
prompt: A class of 10 children votes on a game. At first 7 children want tag. Then 2 of them hear a good reason and change their minds. How many children now want tag?
answer: 5
unit: children
check: 7 - 2
hint: Take away the children who changed their minds.
solution: 7 children wanted tag, and 2 changed their minds, so 7 minus 2 leaves 5 children who want tag.

@quiz mc
prompt: Jo says "Cats are always mean." Then Jo meets a gentle cat and changes her mind. Why is this a good reason?
choice*: Jo saw something new for herself.
choice: Someone shouted at Jo.
choice: Jo was bored.
choice: Jo wanted to be like everyone else.
hint: Seeing something yourself gives you information.
solution: Meeting a gentle cat gave Jo new information. The other choices are not good reasons.

@quiz mc
prompt: Someone gives you a reason, but you still think the same. What can you say?
choice*: I heard your reason, and I still think the same because...
choice: You are wrong and silly.
choice: I will not listen.
choice: Be quiet.
hint: Say that you listened and give your own reason.
solution: Listening and giving your own reason is a good way to keep your mind. The others are unkind.
