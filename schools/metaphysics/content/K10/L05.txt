@lesson
title: What is real? Dreams, pretend and make-believe
summary: Tell things you can touch and see from things in stories, pretend play and dreams, and wonder about what makes something real.
objective: Say what pretend and make-believe mean.
objective: Tell something you can touch from something in a story.
objective: Wonder about a big question about what is real and give a reason.
reading: Teaching Children Philosophy (Prindle Institute for Ethics): the pages about using picture books to talk about real and pretend.

@article
Grown-up helper: read aloud, and let the child act out the pretend parts.

Look at the spoon on the table. You can touch it, hold it and feel that it is hard. A spoon is **real** in a way you can check with your hands and eyes.

Now think about a dragon in a storybook. You can see a picture of a dragon, but you cannot touch a dragon. The dragon was made up by the person who wrote the story. That is **make-believe**.

When you play **pretend**, you decide to imagine something. A box can be a boat, and a stick can be a magic wand. The box is really a box, and you are really playing. The boat is only pretend.

What about **dreams**? When you sleep, you may see pictures in your mind, like flying or talking animals. When you wake up, you cannot touch what was in the dream. But the dream was something that happened to you, and you may remember it.

So here is a big question: "Is a dream real?" Some people say yes, because you really had the dream. Some people say no, because you cannot touch what was in it. Both answers can come with reasons. What do you think, and why?

Another big question: "Is a pretend friend real?" Think about it with your helper. There is no wrong way to wonder.

Remember: asking what is real is a big question, so give your reason.

@example
title: Real, pretend or both?
problem: Your helper shows you three things: a toy car you can hold, a talking rabbit in a story, and the idea of a boat made from a cardboard box. Which can you touch and which are made up?
step: The toy car can be held and felt, so you can touch it.
step: The talking rabbit is in a story. It is a made-up character, so you cannot touch it, although the book it is in is real.
step: The boat made from a box is two things. The box is real, and "boat" is what you are pretending.
answer: You can touch the toy car and the cardboard box. The talking rabbit and the pretend boat are made up.

@mistakes
- Thinking pretend is a bad word. Pretend and make-believe are a good way to think and play.
- Thinking a big question like "Is a dream real?" has one right answer. People give different reasons.
- Mixing up the book and the character. A book is a real thing you can hold, but a dragon in it is made up.

hint: Think about what you could hold in your hand.
solution: You can touch a spoon. A dragon in a story, a pretend friend and a flying horse in a dream are made up in the mind or the story.

@practice mc
prompt: A child plays that a cardboard box is a boat. What is really true?
choice: The box really is a boat.
choice*: The box is really a box, and the boat is pretend.
choice: The box is a dragon.
choice: The child is not playing.
hint: Remember that pretend means imagining something.
solution: In pretend play, the box is still a box. The child imagines it is a boat.

@practice ms
prompt: Which of these are things made up in a story? Pick all that are made up.
choice*: A dragon who cooks soup
choice: A real bird in the garden
choice*: A mouse who rides a bus and speaks
choice: A book on the shelf
hint: A made-up thing is one that someone imagined.
solution: A cooking dragon and a talking mouse on a bus are made up. A real bird and a book exist and you can touch them.

@practice num
prompt: At a pretend tea party you set out 4 toy cups for your friends and 3 more for stuffed animals. How many toy cups are on the table in all?
answer: 7
unit: cups
check: 4 + 3
hint: Put the two groups together.
solution: 4 cups and 3 cups make 4 plus 3, which is 7 cups. The cups are real toys even though the tea is pretend.

@practice self
prompt: Think about this big question with your helper: "Is a dream real?" Give your answer and one reason.
hint: You can say "yes", "no" or "a little", and then say why.
solution: One answer: "Yes, a dream is real because it happened in my mind and I remember it." Another answer: "No, because I cannot touch what was in it." Both are fine because they come with reasons.
rubric: The child gives an answer to the question and a reason.
rubric: The helper listens to the reason and asks "Why?" kindly.
rubric: Any answer is accepted as long as it has a reason.

@quiz mc

choice: A pencil on the desk
choice*: A wizard who lives on the moon in a story
choice: A pet fish in a tank
choice: A chair
hint: One of these is a character that someone imagined.
solution: The wizard on the moon is made up. The pencil, fish and chair are real things.

@quiz mc
prompt: What is pretend?
choice: Telling something that is a lie to hurt people.
choice*: Imagining that something is something else, as a game.
choice: Counting to ten.
choice: Being asleep.
hint: Think about a stick that becomes a magic wand in a game.
solution: Pretend means imagining something on purpose, as a game. It is not a lie told to hurt anyone.

@quiz self
prompt: Pick a pretend game you like. Tell your helper what is real in it and what is pretend.
hint: A box, a blanket or a stick can be real, with something pretend added.
solution: Example: "A blanket is a cave. The blanket is real, and the cave is pretend." Any game with a clear real part and a pretend part works.
rubric: The child names something real in the game.
rubric: The child names something that is pretend.
rubric: The helper listens and enjoys the game.
