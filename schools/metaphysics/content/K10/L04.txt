@lesson
title: Agreeing, disagreeing and listening
summary: Listen to someone else, agree or disagree kindly, and give one reason for and one reason against an answer.
objective: Listen to someone and say back what you heard.
objective: Disagree in a kind way and give a reason.
objective: Give one reason for and one reason against an answer to a big question.
reading: Teaching Children Philosophy (Prindle Institute for Ethics): the pages about listening and responding in a class discussion.

@article
Grown-up helper: take turns with the child, and practice the listening steps together.

In a philosophy circle, people take turns. One person talks while the others **listen**. Listening means looking at the speaker, keeping quiet and thinking about what they say.

After someone talks, you can show you listened by saying it back: "I heard you say that dogs can be sad because they cry." Then they know you understood.

Then you can **agree** or **disagree**. To agree means you think the same. To disagree means you think something different. Disagreeing is not mean. It is how we find out more.

Here are kind ways to disagree: "I see it a different way." "I think something else, because..." "That is a good reason, but I have another one." Notice that each one gives a reason and does not say "You are wrong" or "That is silly."

Take the big question, "Should the biggest piece of cake go to the youngest child?" One reason for yes: "The youngest is smallest and might need to grow." One reason against: "Everyone should get the same, so it is fair." Both are reasons. You can think about both.

When you hear a reason, you can also say, "That makes me think..." You might even learn something new.

Practice: say one reason for and one reason against an answer to a big question. It is like looking at a coin on both sides.

@example
title: One reason for, one reason against
problem: The big question is "Should a class pet live in the classroom all the time?" Say one reason for yes and one reason against.
step: Think of the animal. A reason for yes: "The children can look after it every day."
step: Now think about the other side. A reason against: "The classroom is quiet at night and the pet might be lonely."
step: Check that each one is a reason about the question, and that you could say it with "because".
answer: For: the children can look after it each day. Against: it might be lonely at night in a quiet room.

@mistakes
- Saying "That is silly" or "You are wrong" instead of giving your own reason.
- Talking while someone else is talking. Wait for your turn.
- Thinking that disagreeing means you are not friends. Friends can think differently.

@practice mc
prompt: Which is a kind way to disagree?
choice: That is a silly idea.
choice*: I see it a different way, because the park is better for running.
choice: You are wrong.
choice: I am not listening.
hint: A kind way to disagree gives your own reason.
solution: "I see it a different way, because..." gives your own reason and respects the other person. The other sentences are unkind or do not give a reason.

@practice mc
prompt: Sam says, "Frogs are the best because they can jump high." Which sentence says back what Sam said?
choice: Frogs are boring.
choice*: You said frogs are the best because they can jump high.
choice: I like cats more.
choice: Jumping is easy.
hint: Saying back means telling what you heard, in your own words, without changing it.
solution: Saying back means repeating what Sam said. The other choices give a different opinion.

@practice ms
prompt: Which of these show good listening? Pick all that do.
choice*: Looking at the person who is talking.
choice: Talking while someone else talks.
choice*: Waiting for your turn.
choice*: Saying back what you heard.
hint: Think about what you would like someone to do when you talk.
solution: Looking, waiting and saying back show that you are listening. Talking over someone does not.

@practice num
prompt: In a circle, 8 children listen to a big question. 5 children agree with one answer and the rest disagree. How many children disagree?
answer: 3
unit: children
check: 8 - 5
hint: Take away the children who agree from all the children.
solution: There are 8 children and 5 agree, so 8 minus 5 is 3 children who disagree.

@practice self
prompt: Pick this big question: "Is it always good to tell the truth?" Say one reason for yes and one reason for no, or "it depends".
hint: Think about a time when telling the truth is kind and a time when it could hurt feelings.
solution: Example: "For yes: telling the truth helps people trust you. Against: if I tell my friend her drawing is ugly, that would hurt her feelings." Any two reasons, one on each side, are fine.
rubric: The child gives one reason on one side of the question.
rubric: The child gives one reason on the other side, or says what it depends on.
rubric: The helper listens to both reasons and does not choose a winner.

@quiz mc
prompt: Which sentence disagrees in a kind way?
choice: Everybody knows you are wrong.
choice*: That is a good reason, but I think something different because it is not fair to the others.
choice: Be quiet.
choice: That is a baby idea.
hint: Choose the one that says your own reason.
solution: The kind disagreement thanks the other person's reason and gives a new one. The other sentences are unkind.

@quiz mc
prompt: In a circle, what should you do while a friend is talking?
choice*: Listen and wait for your turn.
choice: Tell a different story at the same time.
choice: Turn your back.
choice: Say "That is wrong" right away.
hint: Think about how you would like to be treated.
solution: Listening and waiting is the way. Interrupting or turning away makes it hard for the friend to share.

@quiz self
prompt: Think of a big question you like. Say one reason for an answer and one reason against it.
hint: Say "One reason for is... One reason against is..."
solution: Example: "Should animals live in a zoo? For: they get food and a safe home. Against: they may miss running in the wild." Any big question with a reason on each side works.
rubric: The child gives a reason for an answer.
rubric: The child gives a reason against that answer.
rubric: The helper listens and says back what they heard.
