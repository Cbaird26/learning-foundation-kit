@lesson
title: Giving reasons: because
summary: Tell a reason from a plain statement and give a reason with the word because.
objective: Say what a reason is.
objective: Find the sentence that gives a reason.
objective: Give a reason with "because" for an answer you choose.
reading: Teaching Children Philosophy (Prindle Institute for Ethics): the pages about asking for reasons in a discussion.

@article
Grown-up helper: read aloud, and pause to let the child finish each "because".

Suppose a friend says, "That is the best game." You might ask, "Why?" Your friend says, "Because you can play it with everyone." That second part is a **reason**.

A reason helps other people see why you think something. "I like the red cup" just says what you think. "I like the red cup because it is my favorite color" says what you think and gives a reason.

The little word **because** is a clue. When you hear "because", a reason is coming.

Reasons can be small or big. "I wore a coat because it was cold" is a small reason. "It is fair to let everyone have a turn because everyone wants to play" is a bigger reason about fairness.

A good reason is **about** the answer. If you say, "The cat is happy because it is purring," the purring really is connected to being happy. If you said, "The cat is happy because my shoes are blue," your friend would say, "What do blue shoes have to do with it?"

Two people can give different reasons. That is fine. When you hear a reason, you can think: Does it make sense? Is it about the answer? Do I have a different reason?

You can practice at home. When someone asks, "Which snack do you want?" say your answer and add "because".

@example
title: Finding the reason
problem: A child says: "I think the turtle should win the race. The turtle is slow. The turtle keeps going because it never gives up." Which sentence is the reason?
step: Look for the word "because". It is in the last sentence.
step: What comes after "because"? "It never gives up." That says why the turtle keeps going.
step: "The turtle is slow" only says how the turtle is. It does not say why the turtle should win.
answer: The reason is "it never gives up", and the word "because" points to it.

@mistakes
- Saying the answer again instead of giving a reason. "It is good because it is good" gives no reason.
- Giving a reason that has nothing to do with the answer, like blue shoes for a happy cat.
- Thinking a reason has to be long. A short reason can be a good one.

prompt: Which sentence gives a reason?
choice: I like the swing.
choice*: I like the swing because it goes high.
choice: The swing is in the park.
choice: Swings are red.
hint: Look for the word "because".
solution: "I like the swing because it goes high" gives a reason after "because". The other sentences just say something about the swing.

@practice mc
prompt: Mia says, "We should clean up the toys because someone could trip on them." What is Mia's reason?
choice*: Someone could trip on them.
choice: We should clean up.
choice: The toys are on the floor.
choice: Mia is talking.
hint: The reason comes right after "because".
solution: The reason is "someone could trip on them". The part before "because" is what Mia thinks should happen.

@practice ms
prompt: Which of these sentences give a reason? Pick all that do.
choice*: I put on a hat because the sun was bright.
choice: I have a hat.
choice*: The dog is sleeping because it is tired.
choice: The sun is bright.
hint: Look for the sentences that say why something is true.
solution: The sentence about the hat and the sentence about the sleeping dog each say why with "because". The sentences "I have a hat" and "The sun is bright" are plain statements.

@practice num
prompt: Your helper asks for reasons. You say 4 reasons for one answer on Monday and 3 reasons for another answer on Tuesday. How many reasons did you give in all?
answer: 7
unit: reasons
check: 4 + 3
hint: Put the two groups of reasons together.
solution: 4 reasons and 3 more reasons make 4 plus 3, which is 7 reasons.

@practice self
prompt: Your helper asks, "Which would you rather have: a long walk or a long nap?" Say your answer and give a reason using the word because.
hint: Start with "I would rather have... because..."
solution: Example: "I would rather have a long walk because I can see birds." Any answer works if it has a reason that is about the answer.
rubric: The child says an answer and uses "because" to give a reason.
rubric: The reason is about the answer, even if it is small or silly.
rubric: The helper listens and does not say the answer is wrong.

@quiz mc
prompt: Which reason fits best? "The dog is happy because..."
choice: my shoes are blue.
choice*: it is wagging its tail and running to greet me.
choice: the clock is on the wall.
choice: it is Tuesday.
hint: A good reason is about the answer.
solution: Wagging and running to greet someone is connected to being happy. Blue shoes, the clock and the day of the week have nothing to do with the dog.

@quiz self
prompt: Say something you think about a game or toy and give a reason with "because".
hint: Try "I think... because..."
solution: Example: "I think blocks are the best toy because I can build anything." Any honest answer with a reason that is about it works.
rubric: The child says what they think.
rubric: The child gives a reason that uses "because".
rubric: The helper listens and may ask "Why?" kindly.
