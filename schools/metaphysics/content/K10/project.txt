@project
title: Big-question circle
overview: Choose a picture book, write three big questions about it with your helper, and hold a philosophy circle where everyone gives a reason. Then draw or tell what you think now and one good reason someone gave that was different from yours.
goal: Ask big questions about a story.
goal: Give reasons and listen to other reasons.
goal: Say what you think now and why.
step: Choose a picture book that you and your helper like. Read it together.
step: Write or say three questions about the book with your helper. Use who, what, where, why and how.
step: Circle the big questions, the ones where people could give different answers with reasons.
step: Pick one big question for the circle. Sit in a circle with your helper and anyone else who wants to join.
step: Take turns. Each person says an answer and a reason with the word because. Listen and say back what you heard.
step: Say one reason for and one reason against the answer you like best.
step: Draw or tell what you think now. Say one good reason someone gave that was different from yours.
material: Question starters you can use: "Why did the character...?" "What would you do if...?" "Is it fair that...?" "Was that kind? Why?" "I wonder if..."
deliverable: Three big questions about the picture book, written or said.
deliverable: A drawing or a spoken story of what you think now.
deliverable: One good reason someone gave that was different from yours.
rubric: The three questions are big questions about the book, with more than one possible answer.
rubric: The child gives at least one reason with "because".
rubric: The child listens and says back at least one different reason.
rubric: The helper accepts any answer that has a reason and listens kindly.
safety: Keep the circle friendly. Nobody has to answer a question about their home or family. Choose a story with gentle questions. If someone feels upset, stop and choose a different question.
tutor: What makes a question a big question?
tutor: Can you help me think of a reason for my answer?
tutor: How can I disagree kindly?

@review
### Key ideas, week by week
1. Wondering: a big question has many answers and each can have a reason. An easy question has one answer you can find.
2. Good questions: who, what, where, when, why and how. Add "why" to make a yes-or-no question bigger.
3. Reasons: "because" points to a reason.
4. Listening: look, wait, say back; disagree kindly with your own reason.
5. Real, pretend and dreams: what you can touch and what is made up.
6. Same and different: say in what way. Many things change when you grow.
7. Fair and unfair: equal shares, turns, one cuts and one picks.
8. Friends and kindness: kind acts help or care for someone.
9. Animals: clues help us guess what animals feel. Say "I think".
10. Numbers, names and ideas: things you can touch and things you cannot.
11. Time: yesterday, today, tomorrow; before and after.
12. Changing your mind: good reasons are new facts, good reasons from others, or trying it yourself.

### Self-check
- I can say if a question is easy or big.
- I can give a reason with "because".
- I can listen and say back what I heard.
- I can tell a good reason for changing my mind from a poor one.

### Common mistakes
- Thinking a big question has one right answer.
- Saying "same" without saying in what way.
- Changing your mind because someone is louder.

prompt: Which is a big question?
choice: How many chairs are at the table?
choice*: Can a toy ever feel lonely?
choice: What color is a lemon?
choice: How many fingers are on both hands?
hint: A big question has more than one answer, and each answer can have a reason.
solution: "Can a toy ever feel lonely?" has many answers that come with reasons. The other questions have one answer that you can find by counting or looking.

@exam num
prompt: A class has 18 stickers to share in equal shares among 6 children. How many stickers does each child get?
answer: 3
unit: stickers
check: 18 // 6
hint: Share one sticker at a time to each child until none are left.
solution: 18 stickers in 6 equal shares is 3 each, because 3 times 6 is 18.

@exam self
prompt: Pick a big question, such as "Can a pet be happy?" Say your answer and a reason with "because". Then say one reason someone might give for a different answer, and tell whether it would make you change your mind and why.
hint: Say "I think... because..." and then "Someone might say... because..."
solution: Example: "I think a pet can be happy because it jumps when it sees me. Someone might say it cannot because it cannot tell us. That is a good reason to be careful, but I still think it can be happy, because I see clues. If someone showed me a new fact, I would think again." Any answer with reasons and listening is good.
rubric: The child gives an answer and a reason with "because".
rubric: The child says a different reason that someone else might give.
rubric: The child says whether it changes their mind and gives a reason.
