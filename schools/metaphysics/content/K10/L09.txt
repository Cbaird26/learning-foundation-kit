@lesson
title: Do animals think? Minds and feelings
summary: Say what clues tell us about animals' feelings and thinking, and give reasons for your ideas about what animals feel.
objective: Say what a clue is and use clues to guess what an animal feels.
objective: Tell what we can see from what we can only wonder about.
objective: Give a reason for an idea about animals' minds.
reading: Teaching Children Philosophy (Prindle Institute for Ethics): the pages about animals, minds and feelings in picture-book discussions.

@article
Grown-up helper: read aloud, and let the child tell about any animal they know or have seen in a book.

A dog wags its tail when you come home. A cat purrs on a soft blanket. A bird sings in the morning. What are they thinking or feeling?

We cannot see inside an animal's mind. We can only see what the animal does. What we see are **clues**. A clue is a sign that helps us guess.

The tail wag is a clue. Many people think a wagging tail may mean the dog is excited or happy. Another clue is when a dog runs to its toy and plays. A different clue is when a dog hides and shakes. That may mean the dog is scared.

Clues help, but they do not tell us everything. Can a dog be happy? Can a cat be sad? Can a bird think about tomorrow? These are big questions. Scientists study them, and people have different ideas and reasons.

One way to wonder: "What would I look like if I felt that way?" Another way: "What does the animal do, and what does that remind me of?"

A good thinker is careful. We can say, "I think the dog is happy because it is wagging its tail and jumping." We should not say, "I know for sure." Saying "I think" and giving a clue is a good way to wonder.

Here is one more big question: "Do animals think?" Try asking it about your favorite animal and say why.

@example
title: Using clues
problem: A cat is lying in the sun with its eyes half closed, purring. What might it feel? What is the clue?
step: Look at what the cat does: it lies still in the warm sun, purrs and closes its eyes.
step: Think about what you do when you feel cozy. You also relax and make soft sounds.
step: Say it carefully: "I think the cat feels comfortable, because it is purring and relaxed."
answer: The cat may feel comfortable or happy. The clues are the purring and the relaxed lying down.

@mistakes
- Saying "I know for sure" about what an animal feels. We can only use clues, so we say "I think".
- Thinking an animal that cannot talk has no feelings. Talking is only one way to show feelings.
- Forgetting to give a clue or a reason. Say what you saw.

@practice mc
prompt: A dog runs in circles, wags its tail and brings you a ball. Which is the best clue that it may be happy and playful?
choice: The dog's fur is brown.
choice*: It wags its tail and brings a ball.
choice: The dog is a mammal.
choice: The dog has four legs.
hint: A clue is something the animal does that shows a feeling.
solution: Wagging its tail and bringing a ball are things the dog does that show it may be playful. Fur color and legs say nothing about feelings.

@practice mc
prompt: Which sentence is a careful way to say what a pet feels?
choice: I know for sure the cat is happy.
choice*: I think the cat is happy because it is purring.
choice: The cat feels nothing.
choice: Cats never feel anything.
hint: A careful sentence says "I think" and gives a clue.
solution: "I think... because..." is careful, and it gives a clue. The other sentences claim to know more than anyone can see.

@practice ms
prompt: Which of these are clues that a dog may be scared? Pick all that are clues.
choice*: It hides under a table.
choice: It has a brown coat.
choice*: It shakes and tucks its tail.
choice: It is bigger than a cat.
hint: Think about what a scared animal does.
solution: Hiding, shaking and tucking the tail are things a scared dog may do. Coat color and size are not clues about fear.

@practice num
prompt: At the animal shelter, 3 dogs each get 2 treats for being good. How many treats is that in all?
answer: 6
unit: treats
check: 3 * 2
hint: Count the treats in 3 groups of 2.
solution: 3 groups of 2 treats make 3 times 2, which is 6 treats.

@practice self
prompt: Wonder with your helper: "Can a dog be happy?" Give your answer and one clue or reason.
hint: Think about what a dog does when it is excited.
solution: Example: "Yes, because dogs wag their tails and jump when you come home." Another: "I am not sure, because a dog cannot tell us in words." Any answer with a reason is good.
rubric: The child gives an answer or says "I am not sure".
rubric: The child gives a clue or reason from what an animal does.
rubric: The helper listens to other ideas and does not claim the only right answer.

@quiz mc
prompt: A bird sits fluffed up and still, and does not sing. Which sentence is a careful guess?
choice: I know the bird is sad.
choice*: Maybe the bird is tired or not feeling well, because it is sitting still.
choice: Birds never feel anything.
choice: The bird is a statue.
hint: A careful guess uses the word "maybe" or "I think".
solution: "Maybe... because..." gives a clue and does not claim to know everything.

@quiz self

solution: Example: "I think a horse feels calm when it eats grass, because it moves slowly." Any animal and reason works.

rubric: The child says what it might feel with "I think" or "maybe".
rubric: The helper listens and accepts any careful answer.
