@lesson
title: Friends and kindness
summary: Say what makes someone a good friend, tell kind acts from unkind ones, and give reasons for your ideas.
objective: Name some things that kind friends do.
objective: Tell a kind act from an unkind one.
objective: Wonder about what a friend is and give a reason.
reading: Teaching Children Philosophy (Prindle Institute for Ethics): the pages about friendship and kindness in picture-book discussions.

@article
Grown-up helper: read aloud, and let the child tell about friends in a picture book rather than from real life if they would like to.

What is a friend? A friend is someone you like to be with, someone who is kind to you and whom you are kind to. Friends can play together, help each other and listen.

Here is a big question: "What makes a good friend?" Some people say a good friend shares. Others say a good friend listens. Others say a good friend is honest. Everyone can give a reason.

**Kindness** means doing something to help or care for someone else. Some kind acts are small: holding a door, saying "thank you", or helping to pick up dropped crayons. Some are bigger: sitting with someone who feels sad.

Some acts are **unkind**: laughing at someone who has fallen, taking a toy without asking, or leaving someone out on purpose.

Kindness can feel good for both people. The friend who is helped feels cared for, and the helper may feel happy too.

Another big question: "Can you be kind to someone who is not your friend?" Think about it. Some say yes, because kindness is for everyone. Others say it is easier to be kind to friends. Both have reasons.

Remember to give a reason for what you think, and listen to your helper's reason too.

Today's job: wonder together about picture-book friends, such as two animals who help each other, and ask, "Was that kind? Why?"

@example
title: Was it kind?
problem: In a picture book, a bunny sees a squirrel drop all its acorns. The bunny helps to pick them up. Was this kind? Say why.
step: Ask what the bunny did. The bunny helped the squirrel pick up the acorns.
step: Ask how the squirrel might feel. The squirrel may feel glad to have help.
step: Say the reason: "It was kind because the bunny helped someone who needed it."
answer: Yes, it was kind, because the bunny helped the squirrel when it needed help.

@mistakes
- Thinking that only big acts are kind. A smile or a "thank you" can be kind.
- Thinking a friend has to agree about everything. Good friends can disagree and still be kind.
- Forgetting to listen to how the other person feels.

@practice mc
prompt: Which of these is a kind act?
choice: Taking a toy without asking
choice*: Helping a friend pick up dropped crayons
choice: Leaving a child out of a game on purpose
choice: Laughing when someone falls
hint: A kind act helps or cares for someone.
solution: Helping a friend pick up crayons is kind. The other acts hurt or leave someone out.

@practice mc
prompt: Which sentence gives a reason why Leo is a good friend?
choice: Leo is a boy.
choice*: Leo is a good friend because he listens when I talk.
choice: Leo has a red bike.
choice: Leo is in my class.
hint: Look for the "because" and a reason about friendship.
solution: Listening when a friend talks is a reason to call someone a good friend. The other sentences are just facts about Leo.

@practice ms
prompt: Which of these are unkind acts? Pick all that are unkind.
choice*: Leaving someone out of a game on purpose
choice: Saying "thank you"
choice*: Taking a friend's toy without asking
choice: Holding the door for someone
hint: An unkind act hurts someone or leaves someone out.
solution: Leaving someone out on purpose and taking a toy without asking are unkind. Saying thank you and holding a door are kind.

@practice num
prompt: A class makes 15 kind cards. They give the same number of cards to each of 5 neighbors. How many cards does each neighbor get?
answer: 3
unit: cards
check: 15 // 5
hint: Deal the cards out one at a time to each neighbor.
solution: 15 cards in 5 equal groups is 3 each, because 3 times 5 is 15.

@practice self
prompt: Wonder about this big question with your helper: "What makes a good friend?" Give your answer and a reason.
hint: Think about a friend in a book or a game, and what they do.
solution: Example: "A good friend shares, because sharing makes both people happy." Another: "A good friend is honest, because then I can trust them." Any answer with a reason is good.
rubric: The child gives an idea of what makes a friend good.
rubric: The child gives a reason for that idea.
rubric: The helper shares a different idea, and both listen.

@quiz mc
prompt: A child sees another child sitting alone and asks, "Do you want to play with us?" Which word best describes this?
choice*: Kind
choice: Unfair
choice: Loud
choice: Pretend
hint: The child is helping someone feel included.
solution: Asking someone to play is a kind act. It helps the other child feel included.

@quiz mc
prompt: Which of these does a kind friend do?
choice: Ignore you when you are sad.
choice*: Listen when you tell them something.
choice: Take your snack without asking.
choice: Say a mean name.
hint: Think about caring for someone.
solution: A kind friend listens. Ignoring, taking and name-calling are unkind.

@quiz self
prompt: Tell about a kind thing a character in a book, a show or a game does, or a kind thing you could do, and say why it is kind.
hint: Say "That is kind because..."
solution: Example: "A bear shares his honey with a bird. That is kind because the bird was hungry." Any act with a reason works.
rubric: The child names a kind act.
rubric: The child gives a reason it is kind.
rubric: The helper listens and does not ask about private family matters.
