@lesson
title: Fair and unfair
summary: Tell a fair way to share from an unfair one, work out equal shares, and act out both ways.
objective: Say what fair and unfair mean.
objective: Work out how many equal shares a fair split gives.
objective: Act out a fair and an unfair way to share and give a reason.
reading: Teaching Children Philosophy (Prindle Institute for Ethics): the pages about fairness and sharing in picture-book discussions.

@article
Grown-up helper: have some small objects, such as blocks or paper cookies, to share while you read.

You and a friend have a plate with four cookies. How do you share them? One way is to give two cookies to you and two to your friend. Each person gets the same, so many people say that is **fair**.

Now suppose one person takes all four cookies and the other gets none. Many people say that is **unfair**.

Fair does not always mean "exactly the same". Imagine one child is very hungry and the other just ate. Some people say it is fair to give more to the hungry one, because the hungry one needs it. Others say giving equal shares is fairest. This is a big question: "What is fair?"

Here are some ways to share:
- **Equal shares:** everyone gets the same amount.
- **Taking turns:** everyone gets a go on the swing, one at a time.
- **One cuts, one picks:** one child cuts the cake into two pieces and the other child picks first, so the cutter wants the pieces to be equal.

When you say that something is fair or unfair, give a reason. "It is unfair because only one child got a turn."

To work out equal shares, you divide. If 12 blocks are shared between 4 children, each gets 3, because 3 and 3 and 3 and 3 make 12.

Your job this week: with your helper, act out one fair way to share and one unfair way. Say why each one is fair or unfair.

@example
title: Equal shares
problem: There are 10 grapes to share between 2 children in equal shares. How many does each child get, and is it fair?
step: Share them one at a time. Give one grape to the first child, one to the second, and keep going until none are left.
step: Each child ends up with 5 grapes, and 5 plus 5 is 10.
step: Both children get the same number, so this is an equal-shares way of being fair. A child who is very hungry might want more, but that is a separate big question.
answer: Each child gets 5 grapes, an equal and fair share.

@mistakes
- Thinking fair always means exactly the same. Sometimes people disagree, so give a reason.
- Thinking unfair just means "I did not get what I wanted". Unfair means something is not right for someone.
- Forgetting to count how many are left over. Check that all the shares add up to the whole.

@practice mc
prompt: Four children want to use one swing. Which way is fair?
choice: The tallest child always goes first and stays on.
choice*: Everyone takes a turn, one at a time.
choice: One child keeps the swing all day.
choice: Only the children with red shoes can swing.
hint: A fair way gives everyone a chance.
solution: Taking turns gives everyone a chance. The other ways leave some children out for reasons that do not matter.

@practice mc
prompt: Which sentence gives a reason that something is unfair?
choice: That is unfair.
choice*: That is unfair because only one child got a turn.
choice: That is a swing.
choice: I like turns.
hint: Look for the word "because".
solution: The sentence with "because" gives a reason. The others only say the word or something else.

@practice ms
prompt: Which of these are ways to share that many people call fair? Pick all that are fair.
choice*: Equal shares
choice*: Taking turns
choice: The loudest child gets everything
choice*: One cuts the cake and the other picks first
hint: A fair way does not leave someone out for no reason.
solution: Equal shares, taking turns, and one cuts and the other picks are all ways people use to be fair. Giving everything to the loudest child leaves others out.

@practice num
prompt: 12 stickers are shared in equal shares between 3 children. How many stickers does each child get?
answer: 4
unit: stickers
check: 12 // 3
hint: Share one sticker at a time to each child until none are left.
hint: Count how many stickers each child has when the 12 are gone.
solution: 12 shared into 3 equal shares is 4 each, because 4 plus 4 plus 4 is 12.

@practice self
prompt: With your helper, act out two ways to share 6 pretend cookies: one fair way and one unfair way. Say why each way is fair or unfair.
hint: Try giving out the cookies in two different ways, and each time say "This is fair because..." or "This is unfair because...".
solution: Fair: share 3 and 3, because both people get the same. Unfair: one person takes all 6 and the other gets 0, because only one person gets any. Another fair way: give 4 to a child who has had nothing all day and 2 to the other, because the first one needed more. People can disagree, so give a reason.
rubric: The child acts out one way that is fair and one that is unfair.
rubric: The child gives a reason for each, such as "because everybody got the same".
rubric: The helper listens, and any reason is accepted.

@quiz num
prompt: 20 pretend coins are shared in equal shares among 5 friends. How many coins does each friend get?
answer: 4
unit: coins
check: 20 // 5
hint: Give one coin to each friend, over and over.
solution: Each friend gets 4 coins, because 4 times 5 is 20.

@quiz mc
prompt: Which is a fair way to choose who goes first in a game?
choice: The oldest child always goes first.
choice*: Everyone takes a turn first on different days.
choice: The child who yells goes first.
choice: Only one child ever goes first.
hint: Look for the way that gives everyone a chance.
solution: If each child gets to go first on a different day, everyone has a chance. The other ways give the chance to only some children.

@quiz self
prompt: Tell about a way you or your friends could share something fairly, such as a game, a turn or a snack. Say why it is fair.
hint: Say "We could... because..."
solution: Example: "We could use a sand timer to take turns on the tablet, because each person gets the same time." Any fair way with a reason works.
rubric: The child describes a way of sharing.
rubric: The child gives a reason that it is fair.
rubric: The helper listens and does not choose one right answer.
