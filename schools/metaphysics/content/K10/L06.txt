@lesson
title: Same and different: what makes you you?
summary: Say how two things are the same or different in a stated way, and draw yourself as a baby and now to say what stayed the same.
objective: Say how two things are the same and how they are different.
objective: Name things about you that change as you grow.
objective: Draw yourself as a baby and now and say what stayed the same.
reading: Teaching Children Philosophy (Prindle Institute for Ethics): the pages about same and different in picture-book discussions.

@article
Grown-up helper: read aloud, and have paper and crayons ready.

Look at two apples. One is red and one is green. They are **different** in color. Both are round, and both are fruit. They are the **same** in shape and kind.

When you say "same" or "different", you should say **in what way**. Two cups can be the same in size and different in color. If you do not say the way, the question is not clear.

Now think about you. When you were a baby, you were small and could not walk. Now you are bigger and you can run. Many things change as you grow: your height, your shoes, your teeth and what you can do.

But you are still you. Some things may stay the same. Maybe you still smile in the same way, or you still love a certain song. Maybe you still laugh at the same jokes.

This is a big question: "What makes you you?" Is it your body, your name, your memories, how you laugh, or something else? People give different reasons.

Here is your drawing job. Draw yourself as a baby, and draw yourself now. Look at the two drawings. Say one thing that is different. Then say one thing that stayed the same, and say why you think it stayed the same.

You do not need a perfect picture. Stick people are fine!

@example
title: Same and different in a stated way
problem: Look at a kitten and a puppy. Say one way they are the same and one way they are different.
step: Pick a way to compare. For example, "number of legs".
step: Both a kitten and a puppy have four legs, so they are the same in number of legs.
step: Now pick another way, such as "the sound they make". A kitten says meow and a puppy says woof, so they are different in sound.
answer: They are the same in number of legs (four) and different in the sound they make.

@mistakes
- Saying two things are "the same" without saying in what way. Always say "the same in..."
- Thinking that changing means you are not you anymore. Many things change while you are still you.
- Feeling that the drawing must look real. A simple drawing is fine.

@practice mc
prompt: A red ball and a blue ball are both round. In which way are they the same?
choice: Their color
choice*: Their shape
choice: Their name
choice: Their price
hint: Look for what both balls have in common.
solution: Both balls are round, so they are the same in shape. They are different in color, because one is red and one is blue.

@practice mc
prompt: Two cups are the same size but one is blue and one is yellow. In which way are they different?
choice: Size
choice*: Color
choice: Both are cups
choice: Both can hold water
hint: Think about the ways the story says they are not the same.
solution: The cups are different in color. They are the same in size, and both are cups that hold water.

@practice ms
prompt: Which of these usually change as a child grows up? Pick all that change.
choice*: How tall you are
choice*: The size of your shoes
choice: The number of ears you have
choice*: What you can do, like reading
hint: Think about which things are different for a baby and for a big child.
solution: Height, shoe size and what you can do all change as you grow. The number of ears stays the same.

@practice num
prompt: You have 6 toy animals. 4 of them are brown and the rest are not brown. How many are different from the brown ones in color?
answer: 2
unit: animals
check: 6 - 4
hint: Take away the brown animals from all of the animals.
solution: There are 6 animals and 4 are brown, so 6 minus 4 is 2 animals that are a different color.

@practice self
prompt: Draw yourself as a baby and yourself now. Then say one thing that is different and one thing that stayed the same, and give a reason.
hint: Think about what you can do, what you look like and what you like.
solution: Example: "As a baby I was small and could not walk. Now I am bigger and can run. What stayed the same: I still like to sing, because singing makes me happy." Any honest answer is good.
rubric: The child makes two drawings, or says what the two pictures would be.
rubric: The child says one thing that changed and one thing that stayed the same.
rubric: The child gives a reason, and the helper listens without correcting.

@quiz mc
prompt: A leaf and a feather both weigh very little, so both are light in weight. In which way are they the same?
choice*: Both weigh very little.
choice: Both are green.
choice: Both fly like birds.
choice: Both are from a tree.
hint: Use what the question says.
solution: The question says both weigh very little, so they are the same in weight. A leaf may be green, but a feather may not be, and neither is certain to be the other things.

@quiz mc
prompt: When you say two things are the same, what should you add?
choice: How much you like them.
choice*: In what way they are the same.
choice: Who owns them.
choice: How old they are.
hint: Remember the words "in what way".
solution: You should say in what way they are the same, such as the same color or shape. Without that, nobody knows what you mean.

@quiz self
prompt: Think of two things you own or see, such as two toys. Say one way they are the same and one way they are different.
hint: Say "the same in..." and "different in...".
solution: Example: "My ball and my balloon are the same in shape, because both are round. They are different in what they are made of."
rubric: The child names two real things.
rubric: The child says one way they are the same and names the way.
rubric: The child says one way they are different.
