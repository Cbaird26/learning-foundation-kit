@lesson
title: Time: yesterday, today and tomorrow
summary: Put days in order, say what yesterday, today and tomorrow mean, and wonder about what time is.
objective: Use the words yesterday, today and tomorrow correctly.
objective: Put events in order of before and after.
objective: Wonder about a big question about time and give a reason.
reading: Teaching Children Philosophy (Prindle Institute for Ethics): the pages about time in picture-book discussions.

@article
Grown-up helper: read aloud, and use a calendar or the days of the week as you go.

Think about your morning. First you woke up. Next you got dressed. Then you ate breakfast. These things happened one after another, in order.

**Today** is the day we are in right now. **Yesterday** is the day before today. **Tomorrow** is the day after today. If today is Wednesday, then yesterday was Tuesday and tomorrow will be Thursday.

We also say **before** and **after**. Brushing your teeth comes before bed, and breakfast comes after waking up. Putting things in order is how we tell a story.

Now for the wondering. Yesterday already happened. Tomorrow has not happened yet. Today is happening now. So a big question is: "Where does yesterday go?" Another is: "Is tomorrow real yet?"

Some people say yesterday is still real because we remember it. Others say it is only a memory. Both have reasons. You can say your idea and why.

Another big question: "Does time go faster when you have fun?" Sometimes a fun afternoon feels very short, and a boring wait feels long. But a clock ticks at the same speed. So what is "time feeling long"? Think about it with your helper.

Try this: with your helper, list three things you did today in order, and then say what you will do tomorrow.

@example
title: Yesterday, today, tomorrow
problem: Today is Friday. What day was yesterday, and what day will tomorrow be?
step: Yesterday is the day before today. The day before Friday is Thursday.
step: Tomorrow is the day after today. The day after Friday is Saturday.
step: Check by counting: Thursday, Friday, Saturday, one after the other.
answer: Yesterday was Thursday and tomorrow will be Saturday.

@mistakes
- Mixing up yesterday and tomorrow. Yesterday is behind us and tomorrow is ahead.
- Thinking "Is yesterday real?" has one right answer. People give different reasons.
- Saying "before" when you mean "after". Say the events in order, one at a time.

@practice ms

choice*: Yesterday was Friday.
choice*: Tomorrow is Sunday.
choice: Yesterday was Sunday.
choice: Tomorrow was Friday.
hint: Yesterday is before today and tomorrow is after today.
solution: The day before Saturday is Friday and the day after is Sunday. So the sentences about Friday as yesterday and Sunday as tomorrow are true.

@practice num
prompt: There are 7 days in a week. How many days are in 2 weeks?
answer: 14
unit: days
check: 7 * 2
hint: Count 7 days, and then 7 more.
solution: 7 days and 7 more days make 7 plus 7, which is 14 days.

@practice self
prompt: List three things you did today in the order they happened, using the words first, next and then. Then say one thing you want to do tomorrow.
hint: Start with waking up, and then say what came next.
solution: Example: "First I woke up. Next I ate breakfast. Then I played with blocks. Tomorrow I want to go to the park." Any true list in order is fine.
rubric: The child tells three things in the order they happened.
rubric: The child says something they want to do tomorrow.
rubric: The helper listens and does not correct the order if it is a true memory.

@quiz self
prompt: Wonder about this with your helper: "Is yesterday still real?" Give your idea and one reason.
hint: Think about remembering and about things that are gone.
solution: Example: "Yes, because I remember what I did yesterday." Another: "No, because it is over and I cannot go there." Any answer with a reason is good.
rubric: The child gives an idea about yesterday.
rubric: The child gives a reason.
rubric: The helper listens to other ideas.
