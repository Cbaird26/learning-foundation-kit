@lesson
title: Numbers, names and things you cannot touch
summary: Tell things you can touch from things you can only think about, like numbers, names and ideas, and talk about whether a pet can be happy.
objective: Tell a thing from its name.
objective: Name some things you cannot touch, like ideas and numbers.
objective: Talk about whether a pet can be happy and give a reason.
reading: Teaching Children Philosophy (Prindle Institute for Ethics): the pages about ideas, numbers and words in picture-book discussions.

@article
Grown-up helper: read aloud, and have a few small objects on the table to point to.

Hold up three crayons. You can touch each crayon. Now think about the number **three**. Can you touch the number three itself? You can touch three crayons, but where is "three"? Is it on the table? Is it in your head?

This is a big question: "Are numbers real?" Some people say numbers are real because we use them every day. Others say they are ideas in our heads. Both can give reasons.

Now think about **names**. A name is a word we use for a person, pet or thing. Your dog is a real animal. "Rex" is its name. You can pat the dog, but you cannot pat the name. A name is not the same as the thing it names. If you changed the dog's name, it would still be the same dog.

There are other things you cannot touch: **ideas** like fairness, kindness and fun. You cannot hold fairness in your hand, but you can see fair acts and talk about them.

So we have two kinds of things:
- things you can touch, like a crayon, a spoon or a dog;
- things you can only think or talk about, like three, a name or fairness.

Both matter. A good thinker notices which is which.

This week's job: talk about whether a pet can be happy, and say why. If you do not have a pet, think about a pet in a picture book.

@example
title: Thing or name?
problem: Your friend has a cat called Pepper. Which can you pat, the cat or the name "Pepper"?
step: The cat is an animal. You can pat it.
step: "Pepper" is a word that people say or write to talk about the cat. You can say or write the word, but you cannot pat a word.
step: If the cat were called a different name, the cat would still be the same cat.
answer: You can pat the cat. The name is a word we use for the cat.

@mistakes
- Mixing up a thing and its name. A dog is not a word.
- Thinking that things you cannot touch are not important. Ideas like fairness matter a lot.
- Thinking numbers have only one right answer to "Are they real?" People give different reasons.

@practice num
prompt: You hold up 2 crayons in one hand and 4 crayons in the other. How many crayons do you have in all?
answer: 6
unit: crayons
check: 2 + 4
hint: Put the two groups together and count.
solution: 2 and 4 make 6 crayons. You can touch the crayons, but the number six is something you can only think or talk about.

@practice self
prompt: Talk with your helper about this: "Can a pet be happy?" Give your answer and one reason.
hint: Think about a pet you know or one in a picture book, and what it does.
solution: Example: "Yes, a pet can be happy, because my friend's dog jumps when it sees her." Another: "I think so, but I am not sure, because it cannot tell us." Any answer with a reason is good.
rubric: The child gives an answer or says they are not sure.
rubric: The child gives a reason, such as something a pet does.
rubric: The helper listens to other ideas.
