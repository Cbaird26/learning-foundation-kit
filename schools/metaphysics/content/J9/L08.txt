@article
A weather app says there is a 30% chance of rain. Does that mean it will rain 30% of the day? No. It means that in situations like today's, it rains about 3 times out of 10. Probability is how we put numbers on uncertainty.

### Probability

The **probability** of an event is a number from 0 to 1 (or 0% to 100%). A probability of 0 means the event cannot happen, and 1 means it is certain. When all outcomes are **equally likely**,

$$\text{probability} = \frac{\text{number of outcomes where the event happens}}{\text{number of all possible outcomes}}.$$

A fair die has six equally likely faces. The probability of rolling an even number is 3 out of 6, which is $1/2$.

### The complement rule

The chance that an event does **not** happen is $1$ minus the chance that it does. If the chance of rain is 30%, the chance of no rain is 70%.

### Counting outcomes

For two fair coins, the equally likely outcomes are HH, HT, TH and TT. The probability of at least one head is 3 out of 4, which is 0.75, since only TT has no head.

### Independent events and the gambler's fallacy

Two events are **independent** when one does not affect the other. Coin flips are independent. A fair coin that has landed heads five times still has a probability of $1/2$ of landing heads on the sixth flip, since the coin has no memory. Thinking that tails is "due" is the **gambler's fallacy**. A run of five heads is unusual, but unusual things happen; what matters for the next flip is the coin, not the run.

### Expected counts

If the probability of an event is 0.25 and you try 200 times, you expect about $0.25 \times 200 = 50$ successes. You will rarely get exactly 50, but over many tries the share gets closer to 25%.

### Everyday cautions

Small chances add up: a 1% chance each day over a long time becomes likely at some point. And "unlikely" is not "impossible": someone wins every lottery. Also, a probability applies to a **kind** of situation, not as a promise about one case.

@example
title: Counting outcomes
problem: A bag has 5 red, 3 blue and 2 green marbles. You pick one without looking. What is the probability that it is blue?
step: Count all the marbles: $5 + 3 + 2 = 10$ equally likely picks.
step: Count those that are blue: 3.
step: The probability is $3/10 = 0.3$.
answer: 0.3, or 30%.

@example
title: Is tails due?
problem: A fair coin has landed heads four times in a row. Is tails more likely next?
step: Flips are independent: the coin does not remember what happened before.
step: On each flip, heads and tails are equally likely for a fair coin, so the probability of tails is still 1/2.
step: Believing otherwise is the gambler's fallacy. (If you saw a hundred heads in a row, you might doubt that the coin is fair, but that is a different question.)
answer: No. The chance of tails on the next flip is still 1/2.

@mistakes
- Thinking that after a streak the opposite result is "due". Independent events do not remember.
- Adding up the wrong totals. Count all possible outcomes, not only the ones you care about.
- Treating a 30% chance of rain as meaning rain for 30% of the day.
- Treating "very unlikely" as "impossible".

answer: 0.4
check: (3 + 1) / (6 + 3 + 1)

solution: There are 6 + 3 + 1 = 10 marbles, and 3 + 1 = 4 are not red. So the probability is 4/10 = 0.4.

@practice num
prompt: Two fair dice are rolled. What is the probability that the numbers add up to 7? Give your answer as a decimal rounded to two places.
answer: 0.17
tol: 0.01
check: sum(1 for a in range(1, 7) for b in range(1, 7) if a + b == 7) / 36
hint: There are 6 times 6 equally likely pairs. List the pairs that add to 7.
solution: The pairs adding to 7 are (1,6), (2,5), (3,4), (4,3), (5,2) and (6,1): 6 pairs out of 36. The probability is 6/36 = 1/6, about 0.17.

@quiz num
prompt: A fair spinner has 5 equal sections, and one of them is red. You spin it 300 times. About how many red results do you expect?
answer: 60
unit: spins
check: 300 * 1 / 5
hint: Find the probability of red on one spin, then multiply by the number of spins.
solution: The probability of red is 1/5, and 300 times 1/5 is 60. You would not expect to get exactly 60, but about that many.
