@article
"Medieval" or "Middle Ages" is a European label for roughly 500 to 1500 CE. In this period, regions around the world were developing in different ways, and long-distance trade connected many of them.

### Medieval Europe

After the Western Roman Empire ended in 476 CE, power in western Europe became local. **Feudalism** was a system of mutual obligations: a king granted land (a fief) to nobles in exchange for military service, and lesser knights served nobles. Most people were peasants, many of them **serfs** bound to the land. The **manor** was the economic unit: a lord's estate worked by peasants who owed labor and a share of the crop. Many manors used a **three-field system**, planting two fields and leaving one fallow each year to restore the soil. The Catholic Church was the major institution, running monasteries, schools and, later, universities.

### The Byzantine and Islamic worlds

The Byzantine Empire preserved Roman law and Greek learning from its capital Constantinople until 1453. In the Islamic world, the Abbasid caliphate (from 750 CE) had its capital at Baghdad. Scholars in the **House of Wisdom** and elsewhere translated Greek, Persian and Indian works into Arabic and added their own work in mathematics, astronomy, optics and medicine. The Canon of Medicine, an Arabic medical encyclopedia finished around 1025, was used in Europe and the Middle East for centuries. Hospitals (bimaristans) in cities such as Baghdad and Cairo treated patients and trained physicians.

### China

During the Tang (618 to 907) and Song (960 to 1279) dynasties, China saw major advances: woodblock and movable-type printing, gunpowder, the magnetic compass and paper money. Song cities were large and commercial. Confucian civil-service examinations became a main path to office.

### West Africa

Trans-Saharan caravans carried salt from the desert and gold from the savanna. The Mali Empire (c. 1235 to 1600) controlled gold-producing regions and trade towns such as Timbuktu, which became a center of Islamic scholarship. A Mali ruler's pilgrimage to Mecca in 1324 brought attention to the empire's wealth.

### The Mongols

In the 1200s the Mongols created the largest contiguous land empire in history, stretching across Eurasia. Their conquests caused great destruction, but afterward, a relatively safe period for travelers, sometimes called the **Pax Mongolica**, helped trade, technology and ideas move. Historians still debate how to weigh the violence against the connections.

### The Black Death

The **Black Death** reached the Mediterranean in 1347 and spread across Europe by 1351. It is generally identified with plague caused by the bacterium *Yersinia pestis*, spread by fleas on rodents, and in some cases person to person. Estimates of mortality vary by region; many historians estimate that Europe lost roughly one-third to one-half of its population. Effects included labor shortages, higher wages for survivors in many regions, peasant unrest, persecution of minority groups who were wrongly blamed, and new public-health measures such as quarantine. The plague also struck the Islamic world and Asia.

@example
title: The three-field system
problem: A manor has 600 acres of arable land farmed with the three-field system, with one-third fallow each year. How many acres are planted in a given year, and what is the purpose of the fallow field?
step: One-third of $600$ is $200$ acres fallow.
step: The planted area is $600 - 200 = 400$ acres.
step: Leaving a field fallow lets the soil recover nutrients, and a rotation means each field is fallow once every three years.

@mistakes
- Assuming the whole world was in a "Dark Age" while Europe was; the Islamic world, China and West Africa were centers of learning and trade.
- Treating feudalism as a uniform system; it varied by region and century.
- Blaming the Black Death on specific groups; contemporaries who did so were mistaken, and historians reject the accusations.
- Giving a single exact death toll for the Black Death; estimates range widely and vary by region.
