@article
After 1945 most colonial empires ended, and the world divided into rival blocs led by the United States and the Soviet Union. These two processes overlapped and influenced each other.

### Decolonization

World War II weakened the European powers and strengthened nationalist movements. Routes to independence varied:
- **Negotiated or largely nonviolent:** India and Pakistan became independent in 1947, with the partition of British India causing mass migration and violence; Ghana in 1957 was the first sub-Saharan colony to gain independence from Britain.
- **Armed struggle:** Indonesia declared independence in 1945 and won recognition in 1949; Vietnam fought France until 1954; Algeria's war against France ended in independence in 1962.
- **Wave of independence:** In 1960 seventeen African states became independent.

Leaders of new states faced borders drawn by colonial powers, limited industry, and ethnic and regional divisions. Some pursued **nonalignment**: the Bandung Conference (1955) of Asian and African states promoted independence from both blocs.

### The Cold War (1947 to 1991)

The **Cold War** was a rivalry between the United States and its allies and the Soviet Union and its allies that did not become direct large-scale war between the two superpowers. Key events:
- **1947:** The Truman Doctrine pledged support to countries resisting communist takeover; the Marshall Plan gave economic aid to Western Europe.
- **1949:** NATO formed; the People's Republic of China was founded. The Soviet Union formed the Warsaw Pact in 1955.
- **1950 to 1953:** Korean War.
- **1961:** The Berlin Wall was built; it was opened in 1989.
- **1962:** The Cuban Missile Crisis brought the superpowers close to nuclear war.
- **1955 to 1975:** Vietnam War.
- **1991:** The Soviet Union dissolved.

Historians have offered differing explanations of how the Cold War began. Traditional accounts stress Soviet expansion; revisionist accounts stress American economic and strategic goals; post-revisionist accounts emphasize mutual fear and misperception. The competition took the form of **proxy wars** in Korea, Vietnam, Angola, Afghanistan and elsewhere, fought by local forces backed by the superpowers, and these wars had heavy consequences for local peoples. Nuclear arsenals created **deterrence**: the idea that each side would not attack because retaliation would be devastating.

### Health cooperation

Smallpox, which had killed many millions for centuries, was targeted by the World Health Organization in an intensified global campaign beginning in 1967. It used surveillance and containment, vaccinating the contacts of each case. The Soviet Union and the United States both contributed vaccine and personnel. The last natural case was in Somalia in 1977, and the WHO declared smallpox eradicated in 1980. It remains the only human disease eradicated so far.

### Reading primary sources

Cold War documents include speeches, treaties, memoirs and intelligence reports. Ask: who wrote this, for whom, and what did the writer want?

@mistakes
- Saying the Cold War involved no fighting; it produced major wars fought through proxies.
- Treating decolonization as a single event; routes and dates varied widely.
- Treating India's independence as only peaceful; partition brought great violence.
- Presenting one explanation of Cold War origins as the only one.
