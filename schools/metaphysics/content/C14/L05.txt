@article
Everyone agrees that the dinosaurs are gone and that tomorrow's weather has not happened. Philosophers disagree about whether the dinosaurs *exist*, in the widest sense of "exist". Special relativity gave this old debate a new argument, because it shows that there is no frame-independent fact about which distant events are happening "now".

### Four views about what exists

You met the A-theory and B-theory in C11 (Metaphysics II); here we need the ontological versions.

- **Presentism**: only present things and events exist. Past and future events do not.
- **Eternalism** (the **block universe** view): past, present and future events all exist, and none is metaphysically privileged by being "present"; "now" is indexical, like "here".
- **Growing block**: past and present events exist; future events do not, and the block grows as time passes.
- **Moving spotlight**: all events exist, as in eternalism, but exactly one time is absolutely present, and presentness moves along.

Presentism, the growing block and the moving spotlight are **A-theories**, which hold that there is an absolute, objective distinction between past, present and future. In the 2020 PhilPapers Survey, 27% of respondents accepted or leaned toward the A-theory of time and 38% the B-theory; the share who hold a view is not evidence that it is true.

### The argument from relativity

The argument is usually associated with Putnam (1967), following an earlier version by Rietdijk (1966). Its setting is Minkowski spacetime, the four-dimensional structure of special relativity. Take an event $O$, "here-now" for an observer. The **simultaneity hypersurface** through $O$ of an inertial frame is the set of events simultaneous with $O$ in that frame. For presentism to make sense, it has to say which events are present with $O$, so that they, but not the past and the future, exist.

| P1 | If presentism is true, then the events that exist are exactly those on one simultaneity hypersurface through $O$, the hypersurface of some inertial frame $F$. |
| P2 | If the existing events are exactly those on the simultaneity hypersurface of $F$ through $O$, then $F$ is physically privileged: inertial frames in relative motion have different simultaneity hypersurfaces through $O$. |
| P3 | No inertial frame is physically privileged. |
| C | So presentism is false. |

The argument is valid: from P1 and P2, presentism implies that some frame is privileged, and P3 denies that. P2 is justified by the Lorentz transformation: for $O = (0, 0)$ and an event $E$ at $(t, x)$, frame $F$ with velocity $v$ counts $E$ as simultaneous with $O$ when $t' = \gamma(t - vx) = 0$, i.e. $t = vx$ ($c = 1$). Different frames give different $t$ for the same $x$. Every event with $|t| < |x|$ (spacelike from $O$) is simultaneous with $O$ in some inertial frame.

### Replies

- **Deny P3.** Maybe one frame is privileged after all. Some presentists (and some physicists working on interpretations of quantum mechanics with a preferred foliation) accept a **neo-Lorentzian** picture: a preferred frame exists but is not detectable, perhaps the frame of the cosmic microwave background or one fixed by quantum nonlocality. The cost: a feature of reality that plays no role in experiments, and a tension with the spirit of the relativity principle.
- **Deny P1.** Presentism may be relativized: the existing events are those at $O$ only (**point presentism**, or here-now presentism), or those on or in the past light cone of $O$ (a view related to proposals of Stein, 1968, on what has become definite relative to an event). These avoid any privileged frame, but existence becomes relative to an event, and two observers at different places disagree about what exists. Point presentism seems to deprive every observer but one point of a world.
- **Accept the conclusion.** Embrace eternalism: events exist tenselessly in Minkowski spacetime, and relativity is its natural home, because the invariant structure (the interval, worldlines) is four-dimensional. The cost for A-theorists is giving up objective becoming; defenders of the block say that the experience of passage needs an explanation but not an extra ontology.

The argument applies equally to the growing block and the moving spotlight, since each needs an objective boundary or an objective present that must be a hypersurface of some frame; the argument does not by itself favor eternalism over a relativized A-theory.

@example
title: Two observers at one event
problem: Alice is at rest in frame $S$. Bob passes Alice at $O = (0, 0)$ with velocity $v = 0.6$ in the $+x$ direction ($c = 1$; years and light-years). Event $E$ is at $(t, x) = (0, 5)$ in $S$, so it is simultaneous with $O$ in Alice's frame. Find the coordinates of $E$ in Bob's frame and say what Bob thinks about the order of $O$ and $E$.
step: $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - 0.36} = 1.25$.
step: Bob's time for $E$: $t' = \gamma(t - vx) = 1.25 \times (0 - 0.6 \times 5) = -3.75$ years.
step: Bob's position for $E$: $x' = \gamma(x - vt) = 1.25 \times 5 = 6.25$ light-years.
step: For Bob, $E$ happened 3.75 years before $O$. So if "what exists now" is what is simultaneous with $O$, Alice's present includes $E$ and Bob's present does not (it includes events that are in Alice's future).
answer: In Bob's frame $E$ is at $(-3.75, 6.25)$: 3.75 years before $O$, so Alice and Bob would count different distant events as present at $O$.

@example
title: How wide is the "elsewhere"?
problem: For an event $O$ at the origin, consider events at $x = 5$ light-years. For which times $t$ (in years, $c = 1$) is the event $(t, 5)$ simultaneous with $O$ in some inertial frame with $|v| < 1$?
step: A frame with velocity $v$ counts $(t, 5)$ as simultaneous with $O$ if $t' = \gamma(t - 5v) = 0$, so $t = 5v$.
step: As $v$ ranges over $-1 < v < 1$, $t$ ranges over $-5 < t < 5$.
step: So the events at that distance that are "present" in some frame form a window 10 years wide. Equivalently these are exactly the events spacelike separated from $O$ at this distance.
answer: All events at $x = 5$ light-years with $-5 < t < 5$ years, a window of 10 years.

@mistakes
- Saying that relativity proves eternalism. The argument shows that presentism, on its simplest form, needs a privileged frame; replies deny premises P1 or P3, and a relativized or neo-Lorentzian presentism remains an option.
- Confusing presentism with the A-theory. Presentism is one A-theory; the growing block and the moving spotlight are others, with different ontologies.
- Thinking that the relativity of simultaneity is about what observers can see. It concerns which distant events are simultaneous after correcting for the time light takes to arrive.
- Forgetting that point presentism or cone presentism is a revision of presentism, not a way of keeping the usual view that everything happening "now" exists.

prompt: In the argument from relativity against presentism, premise P2 says that if the existing events are exactly those on the simultaneity hypersurface of one inertial frame $F$ through $O$, then $F$ is physically privileged. Which fact of special relativity supports P2?
choice: Time runs slower in a moving frame than in a frame at rest.
choice: Nothing can travel faster than light.
choice*: Inertial frames in relative motion have different simultaneity hypersurfaces through the same event.
choice: The interval between two events is the same in all inertial frames.
hint: P2 turns on the fact that choosing one frame's slice rules out the others'.
solution: Different frames through $O$ count different distant events as simultaneous with $O$ (the relativity of simultaneity). So if existence is fixed by one frame's slice, the others' slices are not the existing events, and that frame is singled out. Time dilation, the speed limit and the invariance of the interval are real features of relativity but do not make the slices differ.
