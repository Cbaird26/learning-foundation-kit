## Course review

### Key ideas by week

- **Weeks 1 and 2.** Substantivalism (space as an entity) against relationism (space as relations among bodies). Leibniz's shift argument: P1 substantivalism implies distinct shifted worlds, P2 they agree in all relations among bodies, P3 such worlds are not distinct. Newton's bucket argument and the Machian reply (denying that the only options are the bucket and absolute space). Galilean relativity: $x' = x - vt$; velocity is frame-relative and acceleration is not.
- **Weeks 3 and 4.** The postulates; $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}$; $t' = \gamma(t - vx/c^2)$; relativity of simultaneity; the interval $s^2 = \Delta t^2 - \Delta x^2$ and its classification. Time dilation $\Delta t = \gamma\,\Delta\tau$, length contraction $L = L_0/\gamma$, the twin scenario ($v = 0.8c$, 4 ly: 10 and 6 years) and why it is asymmetric.
- **Weeks 5 and 6.** Presentism, eternalism, the growing block and the moving spotlight. The argument from relativity against presentism and the replies (privileged frame, relativized presentism, accept eternalism). Boltzmann entropy $S = k_B\ln W$, Loschmidt's objection, the retrodiction problem and the Past Hypothesis.
- **Weeks 7 and 8.** States, the Born rule $\lvert\langle\phi\vert\psi\rangle\rvert^2$, superposition against mixture, the collapse postulate, the measurement problem as an inconsistent triad, decoherence.
- **Weeks 9 and 10.** The singlet, $E = -\cos\theta$, no-signaling, the EPR argument, the CHSH bound $\lvert S\rvert \le 2$ and the quantum value $2\sqrt{2}$.
- **Weeks 11 and 12.** Collapse, pilot-wave, many-worlds, QBism and relational views; costs of each; Wigner's friend and the claim each interpretation gives up.

### Key distinctions and formulas

- Validity and soundness; which premise a reply denies.
- Superposition against mixture; amplitude against probability.
- Locality, measurement independence and single outcomes as assumptions of Bell's theorem.
- No-signaling against locality: nonlocal correlations do not permit signals.
- The speed of light is exactly 299,792,458 m/s; the age of the universe on the standard model is about 13.8 billion years.

### Self-check

- Can you state the shift argument, the bucket argument, the argument against presentism, the measurement problem, Bell's argument and the Wigner's friend argument in standard form and name the premise each reply denies?
- Can you compute $\gamma$, a dilated time, a contracted length, the twins' ages, a spin probability, a CHSH value and a branch weight?
- Can you say, for each interpretation, what exists, what is given up and what the main cost is?

### Common errors

- Treating relativity as "everything is relative" and forgetting the invariants.
- Saying that a violation of the CHSH bound proves nonlocality or permits signaling.
- Saying that decoherence solves the measurement problem alone.
- Presenting one interpretation as established; the interpretation of quantum mechanics is disputed.
- Forgetting the units and conventions in a physics calculation.

@exam num
prompt: In a Bell test, Alice's and Bob's measurements are simultaneous in the laboratory frame $S$, at stations 2.5 km apart on the $x$-axis (SI units, $c = 299{,}792{,}458$ m/s). A frame $S'$ moves at $v = 0.6c$ in the $+x$ direction. Using $\Delta t' = \gamma\,(\Delta t - v\,\Delta x/c^2)$, find the magnitude of the time difference between the two measurements in $S'$, in microseconds to two decimal places.
answer: 6.25
unit: microseconds
tol: 0.01
check: (1 / math.sqrt(1 - 0.6 ** 2)) * 0.6 * 2500 / 299792458 * 1e6
hint: With $\Delta t = 0$, the magnitude is $\gamma v \Delta x/c^2$.
solution: $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - 0.36} = 1.25$, so $\lvert\Delta t'\rvert = 1.25 \times 0.6 \times 2500/299{,}792{,}458 = 6.25\times 10^{-6}$ s, which is 6.25 microseconds. The events are spacelike separated (light needs 8.34 microseconds to cross 2.5 km), so their order is frame-dependent, as the calculation shows.

@exam mc
prompt: Which pairing of an interpretation with its response to Bell's theorem is correct?
choice: Many-worlds gives up locality, and pilot-wave theory gives up single outcomes.
choice*: Many-worlds aims to keep locality by giving up single outcomes, and pilot-wave theory gives up locality.
choice: Superdeterminism gives up locality, and collapse theories give up measurement independence.
choice: All three interpretations keep locality and give up the experimental violation of the CHSH bound.
hint: Recall which assumption of the theorem (locality, measurement independence, single outcomes) each interpretation denies.
solution: Many-worlds drops the assumption that each run has one outcome, and its defenders argue that its dynamics is local; pilot-wave theory is nonlocal. Superdeterminism denies measurement independence, and collapse theories are nonlocal. No interpretation denies the experimentally observed violation, which is a result that every interpretation must reproduce.

@exam self
prompt: Does special relativity refute presentism, and does the nonlocality found in some interpretations of quantum mechanics bear on the answer? Write an essay of about 300 words that sets out the argument from relativity against presentism in standard form, names the premise denied by each of two replies, and says how a preferred foliation in pilot-wave theory relates to one of the replies.
hint: Recall that the neo-Lorentzian reply needs a privileged frame, and ask what pilot-wave theory needs for its guidance equation.
solution: The argument: P1, if presentism is true, the existing events are exactly those on one simultaneity hypersurface through here-now $O$, the hypersurface of some inertial frame $F$; P2, if so, $F$ is physically privileged, since inertial frames in relative motion have different hypersurfaces through $O$; P3, no inertial frame is physically privileged; C, presentism is false. The argument is valid, so a reply must deny a premise. A neo-Lorentzian reply denies P3: a preferred frame exists even if experiments do not detect it. A relativized presentism, such as point presentism, denies P1: only the here-now exists, which needs no privileged frame, at the price of a nearly empty ontology. Pilot-wave theory bears on the first reply: its guidance equation is nonlocal, so the standard version needs a preferred foliation, which supplies the structure the neo-Lorentzian wants and shows that a preferred frame can play a role in physics, but only at the cost of Lorentz invariance of the fundamental laws; critics reply that the foliation is undetectable and so is surplus structure. So relativity does not strictly refute presentism, but it makes the defense costly: presentism requires either hidden structure or a drastically revised ontology, and the choice depends on one's view of the relation between physical theory and metaphysics.
rubric: States the argument in standard form so that it is valid, with P2 supported by the differing simultaneity hypersurfaces.
rubric: Names the premise denied by each of two replies correctly (P3 for the privileged frame, P1 for the relativized view).
rubric: Connects pilot-wave theory's preferred foliation to the privileged-frame reply and states a cost.
