@article
A proof system should never lead from truths to a falsehood. **Soundness** says that it does not. It is the easy half of the link between proof and truth; the hard half, completeness, is next week. Soundness is also a working tool: to show that an argument has *no* derivation, you cannot search through all derivations, but you can exhibit a countermodel.

### Two relations

Fix a first-order language. A **structure** $\mathcal{M}$ gives a nonempty domain and interprets each symbol; an **assignment** $s$ gives variables values in the domain; $\mathcal{M}, s \vDash A$ says that $A$ is true in $\mathcal{M}$ under $s$. For a set of formulas $\Gamma$ and a formula $A$:

- $\Gamma \vDash A$ (**semantic consequence**) means: for every $\mathcal{M}$ and $s$, if $\mathcal{M}, s \vDash B$ for all $B \in \Gamma$, then $\mathcal{M}, s \vDash A$.
- $\Gamma \vdash A$ (**derivability**) means that there is a derivation, a finite object, whose conclusion is $A$ and whose undischarged premises are all in $\Gamma$. We use Fitch-style natural deduction with the rules of the course: $\wedge$I/E, $\vee$I/E, $\to$I/E, $\neg$I/E, IP, X, the quantifier rules $\forall$I/E and $\exists$I/E, and the identity rules.

$\vDash$ quantifies over all structures, an infinite and unsurveyable totality. $\vdash$ is about finite symbol manipulation that a program can check. That the two coincide is the content of soundness and completeness.

**Soundness Theorem.** If $\Gamma \vdash A$, then $\Gamma \vDash A$.

**Corollary.** If $\Gamma$ has a model (is satisfiable), then $\Gamma \nvdash \bot$, so $\Gamma$ is consistent. Equivalently, to show $\Gamma \nvdash A$ it suffices to find a structure and assignment that make all of $\Gamma$ true and $A$ false.

### The proof

The idea: assign to each line of a derivation its **context**, the set $\Delta$ of premises and open assumptions in force at that line (those whose subproofs have not been closed). We prove an invariant about every line by induction on the line number.

| P1 | At every line that is a premise or an assumption, the context entails the line. |
| P2 | For each rule: if the lines it cites satisfy "context entails line", so does the line it yields. |
| P3 | Every derivation is a finite sequence of lines, each a premise, an assumption, or obtained by a rule from earlier lines. |
| C | So at every line of every derivation, the context entails the line. |

Valid by induction on the line number, using P3 to say that nothing else can occur. The soundness theorem follows: if $\Gamma \vdash A$, take a derivation with conclusion $A$ at the last line, outside all subproofs. Its context is a set $\Gamma' \subseteq \Gamma$ of premises, so $\Gamma' \vDash A$, and then $\Gamma \vDash A$ because any model of $\Gamma$ is a model of $\Gamma'$.

We check the rules. Throughout, assume $\mathcal{M}, s$ satisfies the context $\Delta$ of the new line, and remember that $\bot$ is true in no structure.

- **Premise or assumption** (P1). The line is in its own context, so any $\mathcal{M}, s$ satisfying the context satisfies it.
- **$\to$E.** Lines $A \to B$ and $A$ are in scope, so their contexts are contained in $\Delta$ and $\mathcal{M}, s$ satisfies them. By hypothesis $A \to B$ and $A$ are true, so $B$ is true.
- **$\to$I.** A subproof assumes $A$ and ends with $B$, with context $\Delta \cup \{A\}$ at the last line; hypothesis: $\Delta \cup \{A\} \vDash B$. Let $\mathcal{M}, s$ satisfy $\Delta$. If $A$ is false there, $A \to B$ is true. If $A$ is true, $\mathcal{M}, s$ satisfies $\Delta \cup \{A\}$, so $B$ is true, and $A \to B$ is true.
- **$\neg$I.** A subproof assumes $A$ and ends with $\bot$: $\Delta \cup \{A\} \vDash \bot$. Since nothing makes $\bot$ true, no structure satisfies $\Delta \cup \{A\}$. So if $\mathcal{M}, s$ satisfies $\Delta$, then $A$ is false there, and $\neg A$ is true.
- **IP.** Same argument with $\neg A$ in place of $A$: no structure satisfies $\Delta \cup \{\neg A\}$, so every model of $\Delta$ makes $\neg A$ false, hence $A$ true. This is where *classical* semantics (every sentence is true or false) enters the proof.
- **$\vee$E.** Lines $A \vee B$ and subproofs from $A$ to $C$ and from $B$ to $C$: $\Delta \vDash A \vee B$, $\Delta \cup \{A\} \vDash C$, $\Delta \cup \{B\} \vDash C$. If $\mathcal{M}, s$ satisfies $\Delta$, then $A$ or $B$ is true; in the first case $\mathcal{M}, s$ satisfies $\Delta \cup \{A\}$ and so $C$, and in the second case likewise.
- **$\forall$I.** From $A(c)$ infer $\forall x\,A(x)$, where the constant $c$ occurs in no member of $\Delta$ and not in $\forall x\,A(x)$. Hypothesis: $\Delta \vDash A(c)$. Let $\mathcal{M}$ satisfy $\Delta$ and let $d$ be any element of the domain. Let $\mathcal{M}_d$ be like $\mathcal{M}$ except that $c$ denotes $d$. Because $c$ does not occur in $\Delta$, $\mathcal{M}_d$ still satisfies $\Delta$, so it satisfies $A(c)$, which says that $A$ holds of $d$. As $d$ was arbitrary, $\forall x\,A(x)$ is true in $\mathcal{M}$.
- **$\exists$E.** From $\exists x\,A(x)$ and a subproof assuming $A(c)$ and ending with $C$, where $c$ occurs in no premise, in no open assumption, in $\exists x\,A(x)$ or in $C$: $\Delta \vDash \exists x\,A(x)$ and $\Delta \cup \{A(c)\} \vDash C$. Let $\mathcal{M}$ satisfy $\Delta$. Some $d$ satisfies $A$; let $\mathcal{M}_d$ make $c$ denote $d$. It still satisfies $\Delta$ (no $c$), and $A(c)$, so it satisfies $C$; and $C$ does not contain $c$, so $C$ is true in $\mathcal{M}$.

The remaining rules ($\wedge$I/E, $\vee$I, X, $\exists$I, $\forall$E, $=$I, $=$E) go the same way and are easier. The side conditions on $\forall$I and $\exists$E are essential: without them the rules are unsound (from $Pc$ derive $\forall x\,Px$).

### What soundness gives

Soundness shows that nothing false is derivable from truths, and that $\Gamma \nvdash A$ whenever there is a countermodel. It does not show the converse, that every valid argument has a derivation. It also is proved in a metatheory that uses logic, so it certifies the system relative to our reasoning about structures. It cannot give an independent guarantee.

@example
title: A derivation and its invariant
problem: Here is a derivation of $P \wedge Q \vdash Q \wedge P$. Give the context at each line and check the invariant at the last line.

```text
1 | P ∧ Q        PR
  |---
2 | P            ∧E 1
3 | Q            ∧E 1
4 | Q ∧ P        ∧I 3, 2
```
step: Every line is in the scope of the single premise, so the context at each line is $\Delta = \{P \wedge Q\}$.
step: Line 1: $\Delta \vDash P \wedge Q$ trivially, as the line is its own context.
step: Lines 2 and 3: if $P \wedge Q$ is true then each conjunct is, so $\Delta \vDash P$ and $\Delta \vDash Q$.
step: Line 4: if $\Delta$ is satisfied then $Q$ and $P$ are both true by lines 3 and 2, so $Q \wedge P$ is true: $\Delta \vDash Q \wedge P$, which is the soundness claim for this derivation.
answer: The context is $\{P \wedge Q\}$ throughout, and $P \wedge Q \vDash Q \wedge P$, as soundness predicts.

@example
title: Using soundness to refute derivability
problem: Show that $P \to Q,\ \neg P \nvdash \neg Q$.
step: By soundness, if $P \to Q, \neg P \vdash \neg Q$ then $P \to Q, \neg P \vDash \neg Q$. So it is enough to find a valuation that makes both premises true and $\neg Q$ false.
step: Take $P$ false and $Q$ true. Then $P \to Q$ is true (false antecedent) and $\neg P$ is true.
step: But $\neg Q$ is false under this valuation.
step: So $P \to Q, \neg P \nvDash \neg Q$, and by the contrapositive of soundness, no derivation exists. (The argument "denying the antecedent" is invalid and not derivable.)
answer: No derivation exists: the valuation $P = F$, $Q = T$ is a countermodel.

@mistakes
- Reading soundness as "every true sentence is provable". Soundness says that provable sentences are true (valid arguments' conclusions follow when the premises are true); the converse is completeness.
- Forgetting the side conditions on $\forall$I and $\exists$E: the proof of soundness for these rules uses exactly that the constant $c$ is new.
- Concluding $\Gamma \nvdash A$ from a failed search for a derivation. A failed search proves nothing; only a countermodel (via soundness) does.
- Checking the invariant only at the last line. The induction must treat every line, including those inside subproofs, where the context contains the open assumptions.

@practice self
prompt: Prove by induction on formulas that every formula built from atoms using only $\wedge$ and $\vee$ is true under the valuation that makes every atom true. Deduce that no such formula is unsatisfiable, so no such formula is refutable by derivation from no premises.
hint: Induct on the formation of the formula: base case for atoms, one case for each of $\wedge$ and $\vee$.
hint: For the deduction, a formula true under one valuation is satisfiable.
solution: Let $v$ make every atom true. Claim: every formula $A$ with only $\wedge$ and $\vee$ has $\bar v(A) = T$. Base case: an atom is true under $v$ by definition. Inductive step for $(A \wedge B)$: by the induction hypothesis $\bar v(A) = T$ and $\bar v(B) = T$, so $\bar v(A \wedge B) = T$. For $(A \vee B)$: $\bar v(A) = T$ by the hypothesis, so the disjunction is true. There is no other formation rule in this class. So every such formula is true under $v$, hence satisfiable. If $A \vdash \bot$ held for such $A$, soundness would give $A \vDash \bot$, so no valuation could make $A$ true; but $v$ does. Hence $A \nvdash \bot$.

rubric: Uses the induction hypothesis on both immediate parts where needed.

@quiz num
prompt: How many valuations of $P, Q, R, S$ make all of $P \vee Q$, $\neg P \vee R$ and $\neg Q \vee S$ true?
answer: 5
check: count_models(lambda p, q, r, s: (p or q) and ((not p) or r) and ((not q) or s))
hint: Consider the cases for $(P, Q)$ in turn: each true letter forces another.
solution: If $P$ is true then $R$ is true; if $Q$ is true then $S$ is true; at least one of $P$, $Q$ is true. The case $P = T$, $Q = F$ forces $R = T$ and leaves $S$ free: $2$ valuations. The case $P = F$, $Q = T$ forces $S = T$ and leaves $R$ free: $2$. The case $P = T$, $Q = T$ forces $R = S = T$: $1$. Total $5$. (Every one of them also makes $R \vee S$ true, as soundness requires, since $R \vee S$ is derivable from these premises.)

@quiz mc
prompt: Which of these follows directly from the Soundness Theorem?
choice*: If a set $\Gamma$ has a model, then $\Gamma \nvdash \bot$.
choice: If $\Gamma \nvdash \bot$, then $\Gamma$ has a model.
choice: Every valid argument has a derivation.
choice: The relation $\Gamma \vdash A$ is decidable.
hint: Soundness takes you from derivations to truth; the corollary is its contrapositive applied to $\bot$.
solution: If $\Gamma \vdash \bot$ then, by soundness, $\Gamma \vDash \bot$, and no structure satisfies $\Gamma$. Contrapositively, a set with a model is consistent. The second and third statements are completeness (model existence, and derivability of valid arguments). Soundness says nothing about decidability.
