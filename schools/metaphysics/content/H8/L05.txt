@article
Names and formulas are the shared language of chemistry. A formula says which elements are present and in what ratio; a name lets you say it aloud and unambiguously.

### Ions you should know

Main-group elements form predictable ions. Group 1 metals give $1+$, Group 2 gives $2+$, aluminum gives $3+$; Group 17 gives $1-$, Group 16 gives $2-$, Group 15 gives $3-$. Many transition metals form more than one ion (iron gives $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ and $\text{Fe}^{3+}$; copper gives $\text{Cu}^{+}$ and $\text{Cu}^{2+}$), and silver is $\text{Ag}^{+}$ and zinc is $\text{Zn}^{2+}$.

**Polyatomic ions** are charged groups of atoms that behave as one unit:

| Name | Formula | Name | Formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| ammonium | $\text{NH}_4^{+}$ | hydroxide | $\text{OH}^{-}$ |
| nitrate | $\text{NO}_3^{-}$ | sulfate | $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ |
| carbonate | $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ | phosphate | $\text{PO}_4^{3-}$ |
| acetate | $\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^{-}$ | bicarbonate (hydrogen carbonate) | $\text{HCO}_3^{-}$ |

### Writing ionic formulas

An ionic compound is electrically neutral, so the positive and negative charges must cancel. Find the smallest whole-number counts that do this (the "criss-cross" shortcut: the magnitude of each charge becomes the subscript of the other ion), and **put parentheses around a polyatomic ion if you need more than one**. Aluminum ($3+$) and sulfate ($2-$): two $\text{Al}^{3+}$ give $+6$, three $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ give $-6$, so the formula is $\text{Al}_2(\text{SO}_4)_3$. Subscripts multiply everything in the parentheses, so that formula contains $2$ Al, $3$ S and $12$ O atoms.

### Naming ionic compounds

Name the cation, then the anion. Monatomic anions end in **-ide** (chloride, oxide, sulfide); polyatomic anions keep their names. If the metal can have several charges, give the charge in Roman numerals in parentheses: $\text{FeCl}_2$ is iron(II) chloride and $\text{FeCl}_3$ is iron(III) chloride. To find the charge, use the anion's charge and the formula: in $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ three oxide ions give $-6$, so each of two iron ions is $+3$, and the name is iron(III) oxide.

### Binary molecular compounds

For two nonmetals, use Greek prefixes for the number of atoms: mono (1), di (2), tri (3), tetra (4), penta (5), hexa (6), hepta (7), octa (8). Omit "mono" on the first element. The second element ends in -ide: $\text{CO}_2$ is carbon dioxide, $\text{CO}$ is carbon monoxide, $\text{N}_2\text{O}_5$ is dinitrogen pentoxide.

### Acids

Binary acids are named hydro-(stem)-ic acid: $\text{HCl}(aq)$ is hydrochloric acid. Oxyacids come from oxyanions: anions ending in -ate give -ic acids ($\text{HNO}_3$, nitric acid; $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, sulfuric acid), and -ite anions give -ous acids ($\text{HNO}_2$, nitrous acid).

@example
title: From name to formula
problem: Write the formula for magnesium nitrate.
step: Magnesium is in Group 2, so the ion is $\text{Mg}^{2+}$. Nitrate is $\text{NO}_3^{-}$.
step: One $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ gives $+2$, and each nitrate gives $-1$, so two nitrate ions are needed to balance.
step: Put the polyatomic ion in parentheses before the subscript: $\text{Mg(NO}_3)_2$.

@example
title: From formula to name
problem: Name $\text{CuSO}_4$.
step: The anion is sulfate, $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$, so the compound is neutral only if copper is $2+$.
step: Copper can form more than one ion, so the charge is included in the name: copper(II).
step: Name: copper(II) sulfate.

@mistakes
- Omitting parentheses around a polyatomic ion that needs a subscript, such as writing $\text{MgNO}_{3\,2}$ for magnesium nitrate instead of $\text{Mg(NO}_3)_2$.
- Using Roman numerals for metals with only one common charge (it is "sodium chloride", not "sodium(I) chloride") or leaving them out for iron or copper.
- Using Greek prefixes for ionic compounds ("magnesium dichloride") or leaving them out for molecular ones.
- Changing the ion's subscript to balance charge; subscripts in the polyatomic ion are fixed.

@practice mc
prompt: What is the name of $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$?
choice: Iron(II) oxide
choice*: Iron(III) oxide
choice: Diiron trioxide
choice: Iron oxide(III) trioxide
hint: Oxide is $\text{O}^{2-}$; find the total negative charge first.
hint: Three oxide ions give $-6$ shared by two iron ions.
solution: The $-6$ total from three oxide ions must be balanced by $+6$ from two iron ions, so each iron is $3+$. The name is iron(III) oxide.

@practice num
prompt: How many atoms are in one formula unit of calcium nitrate, $\text{Ca(NO}_3)_2$?
answer: 9
check: 1 + 2 * (1 + 3)
hint: The subscript $2$ outside the parentheses multiplies everything inside.
hint: Count $1$ Ca, then $2$ N and $6$ O.
solution: $1\ \text{Ca}+2\ \text{N}+2\times3=6\ \text{O}$, for a total of $9$ atoms.

@practice num
prompt: How many atoms are in one formula unit of ammonium phosphate, $(\text{NH}_4)_3\text{PO}_4$?
answer: 20
check: 3 * (1 + 4) + 1 + 4
hint: Ammonium is $\text{NH}_4^{+}$, and there are three of them.
hint: Each ammonium has $5$ atoms; phosphate has $5$ atoms.
solution: Three ammonium ions contribute $3\times5=15$ atoms, and one phosphate contributes $1\ \text{P}+4\ \text{O}=5$ atoms. Total: $20$.
