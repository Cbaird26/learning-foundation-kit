@article
All ordinary matter is built from atoms, and atoms are built from three particles. The picture you need comes from a few key experiments: cathode-ray work showed that atoms contain tiny negative **electrons**, and Rutherford's gold-foil scattering showed that most of an atom's mass and all of its positive charge sit in a tiny dense **nucleus**, with the electrons occupying the space around it.

### The three particles

| Particle | Relative charge | Approximate mass (u) | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| proton | $+1$ | $1.007$ | nucleus |
| neutron | $0$ | $1.009$ | nucleus |
| electron | $-1$ | $0.00055$ | around the nucleus |

Here $1\ \text{u}$ (atomic mass unit) is defined as one twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom. Electrons contribute almost nothing to mass, so an atom's mass comes essentially from its protons and neutrons.

### Atomic number, mass number, isotopes

The **atomic number** $Z$ is the number of protons. It defines the element: every atom with $Z=17$ is chlorine. The **mass number** $A$ is protons plus neutrons, so

$$N = A - Z.$$

In the symbol ${}^{A}_{Z}\text{X}^{q}$ the superscript on the left is $A$, the subscript is $Z$, and $q$ is the charge. **Isotopes** are atoms of the same element (same $Z$) with different numbers of neutrons, so different $A$. Carbon-12 and carbon-14 both have $6$ protons; they have $6$ and $8$ neutrons.

### Ions

A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons. An **ion** has gained or lost electrons; the nucleus does not change:

$$\text{charge} = \text{protons} - \text{electrons}.$$

Losing electrons gives a positive **cation** (the Mg atom becomes $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ after losing two); gaining electrons gives a negative **anion** ($\text{S}^{2-}$ has two extra electrons).

### Average atomic mass

The atomic mass on the periodic table is a weighted average over the naturally occurring isotopes:

$$\bar{m}=\sum_i f_i\, m_i,$$

where $f_i$ is the fractional abundance and $m_i$ the isotope's mass in u. Chlorine is about $75.8\%$ chlorine-35 and $24.2\%$ chlorine-37, so its average is close to, but not equal to, $35$ and is nowhere near a whole number.

### Atomic-scale units

Atomic sizes are tiny, so chemists use the picometer ($1\ \text{pm}=10^{-12}\ \text{m}$) and the angstrom ($1\ \text{\AA}=10^{-10}\ \text{m}=100\ \text{pm}$). Dimensional analysis from last week handles every conversion: write the factor so the unwanted unit cancels.

@example
title: Particles in an atom and an ion
problem: How many protons, neutrons and electrons are in ${}^{25}\text{Mg}$, and how many electrons are in the ion ${}^{25}\text{Mg}^{2+}$?
step: Magnesium has atomic number $Z=12$, so every magnesium atom has $12$ protons.
step: Neutrons: $N=A-Z=25-12=13$.
step: A neutral atom has as many electrons as protons, so ${}^{25}\text{Mg}$ has $12$ electrons.
step: The $2+$ ion has lost two electrons: $12-2=10$ electrons. The nucleus is unchanged, still $12$ protons and $13$ neutrons.

@example
title: Converting a length
problem: The radius of a sodium atom is about $186\ \text{pm}$. Express it in meters, with scientific notation.
step: Use $1\ \text{pm}=10^{-12}\ \text{m}$ to build the factor $\dfrac{10^{-12}\ \text{m}}{1\ \text{pm}}$.
step: Multiply: $186\ \text{pm}\times\dfrac{10^{-12}\ \text{m}}{1\ \text{pm}}=186\times10^{-12}\ \text{m}$.
step: Write in scientific notation with three significant figures: $1.86\times10^{-10}\ \text{m}$.

@mistakes
- Using the atomic mass (a decimal) as the mass number; the mass number of an isotope is always a whole number.
- Changing the number of protons when forming an ion; only electrons are gained or lost.
- Getting the sign of an ion's charge backwards: more electrons than protons means a negative charge.
- Calling isotopes different elements; they have the same $Z$ and differ only in neutrons.

@practice num
prompt: Chlorine has two main isotopes: ${}^{35}\text{Cl}$ with mass $34.969\ \text{u}$ and abundance $75.77\%$, and ${}^{37}\text{Cl}$ with mass $36.966\ \text{u}$ and abundance $24.23\%$. Calculate the average atomic mass in u, to two decimal places.
answer: 35.45
check: 0.7577 * 34.969 + 0.2423 * 36.966
tol: 0.01
unit: u
hint: Convert each percentage to a fraction and multiply by that isotope's mass.
hint: Add $0.7577\times34.969$ and $0.2423\times36.966$.
solution: $0.7577\times34.969=26.496$ and $0.2423\times36.966=8.957$. The sum is $35.45\ \text{u}$.

@quiz num

answer: 146
check: 238 - 92
hint: Subtract the number of protons from the mass number.

@quiz num
prompt: Boron has two stable isotopes: ${}^{10}\text{B}$ (mass $10.013\ \text{u}$, abundance $19.9\%$) and ${}^{11}\text{B}$ (mass $11.009\ \text{u}$, abundance $80.1\%$). Find the average atomic mass in u, to two decimal places.
answer: 10.81

hint: $0.199\times10.013+0.801\times11.009$.
