@article
Many quantities change together. When air is pumped into a balloon, its radius, surface area and volume all grow at once, tied by formulas such as $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$. If you know how fast one quantity changes, the formula tells you how fast the others change. These are **related rates** problems, and the tool is the chain rule from last week with **time** $t$ as the independent variable.

### The key idea

If $V$ and $r$ both depend on $t$, differentiate $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$ with respect to $t$:

$$\frac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi r^2\,\frac{dr}{dt}.$$

The factor $\frac{dr}{dt}$ appears for the same reason $y'$ appeared in implicit differentiation: $r$ is a function of $t$.

### A six-step method

1. **Draw a diagram.** Label every quantity that changes with a letter. Quantities that never change (a ladder's length) get numbers.
2. **List the givens and the unknown** as rates with units, for example $\frac{dx}{dt} = 0.5$ m/s. An increasing quantity has a positive rate and a decreasing one a negative rate.
3. **Find an equation** linking the variables: the Pythagorean theorem, similar triangles, or an area or volume formula.
4. **Differentiate with respect to $t$**, using the chain rule on every changing quantity.
5. **Substitute** the values at the instant in question. Find any missing variable from the equation of step 3.
6. **Solve, attach units, and interpret the sign.**

The order of steps 4 and 5 is the heart of the matter. Quantities such as $x$ and $y$ keep changing, so you may not substitute their instantaneous values until after you have differentiated. A quantity that never changes can be substituted at any time.

### Useful relationships

| Situation | Relationship |
|---|---|
| right triangle, legs $x$, $y$, hypotenuse $s$ | $x^2 + y^2 = s^2$ |
| circle | $A = \pi r^2$ |
| sphere | $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$, $A = 4\pi r^2$ |
| cone | $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$ |
| similar triangles | corresponding sides are proportional |
| angle in a right triangle | $\tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$ |

Keep the units visible all the way through: a rate of change of area has units such as $\text{m}^2/\text{s}$. If two lengths are linked by similar triangles, as the radius and depth of water in a cone are, use that link to reduce the formula to a single variable before differentiating. Angles must be in radians whenever you differentiate trigonometric functions.

@example
title: A sliding ladder
problem: A 10 m ladder leans against a vertical wall. The foot of the ladder is pulled away from the wall at $0.5$ m/s. How fast is the top of the ladder sliding down the wall at the moment the foot is $6$ m from the wall?
step: Diagram and variables: let $x(t)$ be the distance in metres from the wall to the foot, and $y(t)$ the height in metres of the top. The ladder length, $10$ m, is constant. Given $\frac{dx}{dt} = 0.5$ m/s; want $\frac{dy}{dt}$ when $x = 6$.
step: The ladder, wall and ground form a right triangle: $x^2 + y^2 = 100$.
step: Differentiate with respect to $t$: $2x\,\frac{dx}{dt} + 2y\,\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$, so $\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{x}{y}\,\frac{dx}{dt}$.
step: Find the missing value at the instant: when $x = 6$, $y = \sqrt{100 - 36} = 8$.
step: Substitute: $\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{6}{8}(0.5) = -0.375$ m/s. The negative sign means $y$ is decreasing: the top slides down at $0.375$ m/s.

@example
title: An inflating balloon
problem: A spherical balloon is inflated at $100\ \text{cm}^3/\text{s}$. How fast is its radius increasing when the radius is $5$ cm?
step: Variables: $V(t)$ in $\text{cm}^3$ and $r(t)$ in cm. Given $\frac{dV}{dt} = 100\ \text{cm}^3/\text{s}$; want $\frac{dr}{dt}$ when $r = 5$ cm.
step: Relate them: $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$. Differentiate with respect to $t$: $\frac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi r^2\,\frac{dr}{dt}$.
step: Solve for the unknown: $\frac{dr}{dt} = \frac{1}{4\pi r^2}\,\frac{dV}{dt}$.
step: Substitute $r = 5$ and $\frac{dV}{dt} = 100$: $\frac{dr}{dt} = \frac{100}{4\pi \cdot 25} = \frac{1}{\pi}$ cm/s, about $0.32$ cm/s. The radius grows more slowly as the balloon gets bigger, because the same volume spreads over a larger surface.

@mistakes
- Substituting an instantaneous value, such as $x = 6$, before differentiating. Then you differentiate a constant and get $0$.
- Dropping signs. A decreasing quantity has a negative rate, so say which way each variable moves.
- Forgetting the factor $\frac{dr}{dt}$ (or $\frac{dx}{dt}$) when differentiating, which means differentiating with respect to $r$ instead of $t$.
- Mixing units, such as centimetres with metres or minutes with seconds, or leaving the units off the final answer.

@practice num
prompt: The radius of a circular oil slick grows at $2$ m/min. How fast is the area of the slick increasing at the moment the radius is $10$ m? Give an exact value.
answer: 40pi
unit: m^2/min
check: nderiv(lambda t: pi*(10 + 2*t)**2, 0)
hint: Relate area and radius with $A = \pi r^2$, then differentiate with respect to $t$.
hint: $\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r\,\frac{dr}{dt}$. Now substitute $r = 10$ and $\frac{dr}{dt} = 2$.
solution: $\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r\,\frac{dr}{dt} = 2\pi(10)(2) = 40\pi\ \text{m}^2/\text{min}$.

@practice num
prompt: The edge of a cube is increasing at $3$ cm/s. How fast is the volume increasing at the moment the edge is $5$ cm?
answer: 225
unit: cm^3/s
check: nderiv(lambda t: (5 + 3*t)**3, 0)
hint: The volume of a cube with edge $x$ is $V = x^3$.
hint: $\frac{dV}{dt} = 3x^2\,\frac{dx}{dt}$. Substitute $x = 5$ and $\frac{dx}{dt} = 3$.
solution: $\frac{dV}{dt} = 3x^2\,\frac{dx}{dt} = 3(25)(3) = 225\ \text{cm}^3/\text{s}$.

@practice num
prompt: At a certain instant, car A is $3$ km north of an intersection and driving north, away from it, at $60$ km/h. Car B is $4$ km east of the same intersection and driving east, away from it, at $80$ km/h. How fast is the distance between the two cars increasing at that instant?
answer: 100
unit: km/h
check: nderiv(lambda t: sqrt((4 + 80*t)**2 + (3 + 60*t)**2), 0)
hint: Let $x$ be car B's distance east, $y$ car A's distance north and $s$ the distance between the cars. How are $x$, $y$ and $s$ related?
hint: From $s^2 = x^2 + y^2$ you get $s\,\frac{ds}{dt} = x\,\frac{dx}{dt} + y\,\frac{dy}{dt}$. Find $s$ at the instant first.
solution: $s^2 = x^2 + y^2$ gives $s\,s' = x\,x' + y\,y'$. At the instant, $s = \sqrt{16 + 9} = 5$ km, so $5\,s' = 4(80) + 3(60) = 500$ and $s' = 100$ km/h.

@practice num
prompt: Water is poured at $3\ \text{m}^3/\text{min}$ into a conical tank with its vertex pointing down. The tank is $4$ m deep and has a top radius of $2$ m. How fast is the water level rising at the moment the water is $2$ m deep? Give an exact value.
answer: 3/pi
unit: m/min
check: nderiv(lambda t: (12*(pi/12*2**3 + 3*t)/pi)**(1/3), 0)
hint: The water forms a smaller cone with radius $r$ and depth $h$. Similar triangles give $\frac{r}{h} = \frac{2}{4}$, so $r = \frac{h}{2}$.
hint: Substitute into $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$ to get $V = \frac{\pi}{12}h^3$, then differentiate with respect to $t$.
solution: $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi\left(\frac{h}{2}\right)^2 h = \frac{\pi}{12}h^3$, so $\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{\pi}{4}h^2\,\frac{dh}{dt}$. With $h = 2$ and $\frac{dV}{dt} = 3$: $3 = \pi\,\frac{dh}{dt}$, so $\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{3}{\pi}$ m/min, about $0.95$ m/min.

@practice self
prompt: A $1.8$ m tall person walks directly away from a $6$ m tall lamp post at $1.2$ m/s. Set up a related-rates problem for the rate at which the length of the person's shadow is increasing: draw and label a diagram, name every variable with its units, state the given and wanted rates, write the equation that links the variables, differentiate with respect to time, and solve.
hint: Draw the lamp, the person and the shadow as two similar right triangles. Let $x$ be the person's distance from the lamp post and $s$ the shadow length.
hint: Similar triangles give $\frac{6}{x + s} = \frac{1.8}{s}$. Solve this for $s$ in terms of $x$ before differentiating.
solution: Let $x(t)$ be the person's distance from the lamp post in metres and $s(t)$ the shadow length in metres. Given $\frac{dx}{dt} = 1.2$ m/s; want $\frac{ds}{dt}$. Similar triangles give $\frac{6}{x + s} = \frac{1.8}{s}$, so $6s = 1.8x + 1.8s$ and $s = \frac{3}{7}x$. Differentiating, $\frac{ds}{dt} = \frac{3}{7}\cdot 1.2 = \frac{18}{35} \approx 0.51$ m/s. The shadow lengthens at a constant rate, independent of how far the person has walked.
rubric: The diagram shows the lamp (6 m), person (1.8 m), distance $x$ and shadow $s$, with units labelled.
rubric: States the given rate $\frac{dx}{dt} = 1.2$ m/s (positive) and the wanted rate $\frac{ds}{dt}$ in m/s.
rubric: Writes the similar-triangle relation before differentiating and does not substitute changing values early.
rubric: Differentiates with respect to $t$ and reaches $\frac{ds}{dt} = \frac{18}{35}$ m/s with units.

@quiz num
prompt: A spherical snowball melts so that its radius decreases at $0.5$ cm/min. How fast is its surface area $A = 4\pi r^2$ changing at the moment the radius is $8$ cm? A decrease should be entered as a negative number. Give an exact value.
answer: -32pi
unit: cm^2/min
check: nderiv(lambda t: 4*pi*(8 - 0.5*t)**2, 0)
hint: Differentiate $A = 4\pi r^2$ with respect to $t$, using the chain rule.
hint: $\frac{dA}{dt} = 8\pi r\,\frac{dr}{dt}$. The radius is decreasing, so $\frac{dr}{dt} = -0.5$.
solution: $\frac{dA}{dt} = 8\pi r\,\frac{dr}{dt} = 8\pi(8)(-0.5) = -32\pi\ \text{cm}^2/\text{min}$.

@quiz num
prompt: An observer stands $100$ m from the base of a launch pad and watches a rocket rise vertically at $50$ m/s. Let $\theta$ be the angle of elevation of the line of sight to the rocket. How fast is $\theta$ increasing at the moment the rocket is $100$ m above the ground?
answer: 1/4
unit: rad/s
check: nderiv(lambda t: arctan((100 + 50*t)/100), 0)
hint: Let $h$ be the rocket's height. Which trigonometric function relates $\theta$, $h$ and the fixed distance $100$ m?
hint: Use $\tan\theta = \frac{h}{100}$ and differentiate both sides with respect to $t$. You will need $\sec^2\theta$.
solution: $\sec^2\theta\,\frac{d\theta}{dt} = \frac{1}{100}\,\frac{dh}{dt}$. At $h = 100$, $\theta = 45^\circ$ and $\sec^2\theta = 2$. So $2\,\frac{d\theta}{dt} = \frac{50}{100}$ and $\frac{d\theta}{dt} = \frac{1}{4}$ rad/s.
