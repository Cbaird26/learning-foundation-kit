@project
title: My animal booklet
overview: You will write and illustrate a short nonfiction booklet about an animal you like. It will have headings, a glossary and a labeled picture. Work with your grown-up over two weeks.
goal: Use nonfiction text features: headings, a glossary and labels.
goal: Write paragraphs with a topic sentence, details and an ending.
goal: Use complete sentences with capital letters and end marks.
step: Pick an animal. Look at a library book or a safe kids' website with your grown-up, and write down five true facts in your own words.
step: Sort your facts into three groups, like "Where it lives", "What it eats" and "Babies". These will be your headings.
step: Write one short paragraph under each heading. Each paragraph needs a topic sentence, three details and an ending sentence.
step: Draw a big picture of your animal. Add at least four labels with lines pointing to its body parts, like beak, wing, fur or tail.
step: Pick three bold words from your booklet, like habitat or nectar. Make a glossary that lists them in ABC order with a short meaning for each.
step: Add a title and a cover picture. Read the whole booklet to your grown-up at a steady pace and fix any missing capitals or end marks.
deliverable: A booklet with a title, three headings and a paragraph under each.
deliverable: A labeled picture with at least four labels.
deliverable: A glossary with three words in ABC order.
rubric: Each paragraph has a topic sentence, details and an ending sentence.
rubric: All facts are true and in your own words.
rubric: The booklet has headings, labels and a glossary.
rubric: Sentences are complete, start with capitals and end with the right marks.
rubric: You read the booklet aloud smoothly and said its main idea.
safety: Use child-safe scissors, glue and crayons or markers with your grown-up. Use the internet only together with your grown-up.
tutor: Can you help me make my topic sentence clearer?
tutor: How can I tell if a fact is true or an opinion?
tutor: How do I write a short meaning for a hard word?

@review
### Week by week

- **Weeks 1 and 2:** Vowel teams (ai, ee, oa, oi) make one sound. R-controlled vowels (ar, or, er, ir, ur) change the vowel sound. Count syllables by clapping, and split words between two consonants or between small words.
- **Weeks 3 and 4:** Fluency means accurate, steady, expressive reading. Follow punctuation. The topic is what a passage is about; the main idea is a whole sentence about it; key details support it.
- **Weeks 5 and 6:** Stories have characters, a setting, a problem and a solution. Use first, next, then and last for order. A cause is why; an effect is what happened.
- **Weeks 7 and 8:** Headings, captions, labels, bold words and a glossary help you find facts. Use context clues to figure out new words.
- **Weeks 9 and 10:** A complete sentence has a who and a what happened. Use capitals, end marks and commas in lists. A paragraph has a topic sentence, three details and an ending.
- **Weeks 11 and 12:** A story has a beginning, middle and end with a problem and solution. An opinion piece states what you think and gives reasons.

### Self-check

- I can read two-syllable words by splitting them.
- I can say the main idea in my own words.
- I can tell cause from effect.
- I can write a paragraph and a short story.

### Common mistakes

- Mixing up the topic and the main idea.
- Writing fragments or forgetting end marks.
- Giving an opinion with no reasons.

@exam mc
prompt: Read: "Frogs start life as eggs in water. The eggs hatch into tadpoles. Tadpoles grow legs and lose their tails. Then they are frogs." Which is the best main idea?
choice*: Frogs change in steps as they grow.
choice: Frogs live in water.
choice: Tails are long.
choice: Eggs are small.
hint: Find the sentence that covers eggs, tadpoles and frogs.
solution: The passage tells the steps of a frog's life, so the main idea is that frogs change in steps as they grow.

prompt: A paragraph has a topic sentence, three details and an ending sentence. Every sentence has 20 words. Mia reads it aloud at a steady pace of 40 words per minute. How many minutes does it take her to read the whole paragraph?
answer: 2.5
check: (1 + 3 + 1) * 20 / 40
tol: 0.01
hint: First find how many sentences there are. Then find how many words in all.
hint: Divide the total words by the words she reads each minute.
solution: There are 5 sentences, so 5 times 20 is 100 words. At 40 words per minute, 100 divided by 40 is 2.5 minutes.

@exam self
prompt: Read this sentence: "The kitten was timid, so it hid under the bed." Tell your grown-up what timid means and why the kitten hid (the cause and the effect). Then write two complete sentences about a pet, with one question mark.
solution: Timid means shy or easily scared. The cause is that the kitten was timid, and the effect is that it hid under the bed. Example sentences: My dog likes to dig. Does your cat like fish?
rubric: You said timid means shy or scared, using the clue.
rubric: You named the cause and the effect correctly.
rubric: Both sentences are complete with capitals and end marks.
rubric: One sentence ends with a question mark.
hint: Look at what the kitten did after the comma to find the meaning, then ask why it did it.
