summary: Break long words into syllables so you can read them one small part at a time.
objective: Count the syllables in a word by listening for vowel sounds.
objective: Split two-syllable words into parts to read them.
objective: Sort two-syllable words by their vowel pattern.

@article
A **syllable** is one beat in a word. Each syllable has one vowel sound. Clap as you say a word and you can feel the beats. The word "cat" has one clap. The word "rabbit" has two claps: rab-bit. The word "banana" has three: ba-na-na.

### Count the beats

Put your hand under your chin and say the word. Your chin drops once for each syllable. Try it with "pillow". Your chin drops two times: pil-low.

### Split a long word

Long words look scary, but small parts are easy. Here are three tricks.

- **Two words in one.** A compound word is made of two small words. Split between them: sun-set, rain-bow, tea-pot.
- **Two consonants in the middle.** When you see two consonants between two vowels, split between the consonants: rab-bit, nap-kin, pic-nic.
- **Look for teams.** Keep a vowel team or an r-controlled vowel together. Do not split it. Rain-bow, not ra-in-bow. Far-mer, not fa-rmer.

### Read it, then put it together

Read each syllable by itself. Say it slowly. Then say the parts faster until they join into a word. Then ask yourself, "Does that sound like a real word?"

### Sort by vowel pattern

You can sort words by the vowel sound in the first syllable. In **rainbow**, the first syllable has a vowel team. In **farmer**, the first syllable has an r-controlled vowel. Sorting helps your brain notice patterns.

@example
title: Splitting the word "napkin"
problem: How can you read the word napkin?
step: Find the vowels: a and i. That means two syllables.
step: There are two consonants in the middle, p and k. Split between them: nap-kin.
step: Read each part, nap and kin, then blend them: napkin.

@example
title: Splitting the word "teapot"
problem: How can you read the word teapot?
step: Look for small words. You can see tea and pot.
step: The letters ea are a vowel team, so keep them together: tea-pot.
step: Blend the parts: teapot.

@mistakes
- Counting letters instead of beats. The word "bake" has four letters but only one syllable.
- Splitting a vowel team in half, like ra-in-bow.
- Reading the word too fast to check it. Read each part slowly first.
