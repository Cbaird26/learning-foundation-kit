@article
A **ratio** compares two quantities. If a fruit bowl holds 3 apples and 2 oranges, the ratio of apples to oranges is $3 : 2$, read "3 to 2". You can also write it as the fraction $\frac{3}{2}$. Order matters: the ratio of oranges to apples is $2 : 3$, which is a different statement.

### Equivalent ratios

Two ratios are **equivalent** when one is made from the other by multiplying (or dividing) both parts by the same nonzero number. Doubling the bowl gives 6 apples and 4 oranges, so $3 : 2$ and $6 : 4$ are equivalent. They describe the same mix.

To simplify a ratio, divide both parts by a common factor: $12 : 18$ becomes $2 : 3$ after dividing by $6$.

### Ratio tables

A **ratio table** lists equivalent ratios in columns. Each column is the same recipe made bigger or smaller.

| Cups of flour | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cups of sugar | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |

To find a missing entry, find the number you multiply by to get from one column to another, and use it on both rows.

### Rates and unit rates

A **rate** is a ratio of two quantities with different units, such as $180$ miles in $4$ hours. A **unit rate** has $1$ in the second part. Divide to find it:

$$\frac{180 \text{ miles}}{4 \text{ hours}} = 45 \text{ miles per hour}.$$

When the quantity is money, the unit rate is a **unit price**, such as dollars per apple. Unit prices let you compare deals fairly: the lower price per item is the better buy.

### Using a unit rate

Once you know a unit rate, any amount is one multiplication. If a car travels $45$ miles per hour, then in $3$ hours it travels $45 \times 3 = 135$ miles.

@example
title: Finding a missing amount with a ratio table
problem: A paint mix uses 2 cans of blue for every 5 cans of white. How many cans of white go with 6 cans of blue?
step: Start a ratio table with the known column: blue $2$, white $5$.
step: Find the multiplier from $2$ blue to $6$ blue: $6 \div 2 = 3$.
step: Multiply the white row by the same number: $5 \times 3 = 15$.
step: Check: $6 : 15$ divided by $3$ gives $2 : 5$. The answer is $15$ cans of white.

@mistakes
- Adding the same number to both parts instead of multiplying: $2 : 5$ is not equivalent to $4 : 7$.
- Writing a ratio in the wrong order, such as blue to white when the question asks for white to blue.
- Dividing the wrong way when finding a unit rate; "per hour" means divide by the hours.

@practice num
prompt: A recipe uses 3 cups of flour for every 2 cups of sugar. How many cups of flour go with 8 cups of sugar?
answer: 12
check: 3 * 8 / 2
hint: Make a ratio table with flour $3$ and sugar $2$.
hint: What do you multiply $2$ by to get $8$?
solution: $8 \div 2 = 4$, so multiply flour by $4$: $3 \times 4 = 12$ cups.

check: 4.5 / 6

choice*: $2 : 3$
choice: $6 : 4$
choice: $8 : 10$
choice: $4 : 10$
hint: Divide or multiply both parts by the same number.
hint: Try dividing both $4$ and $6$ by $2$.
solution: Dividing both parts by $2$ gives $2 : 3$. The ratio $6 : 4$ is reversed, and $8 : 10$ and $4 : 10$ come from adding, not multiplying.

@practice num
prompt: Store A sells 5 notebooks for \$8.75. Store B sells 8 notebooks for \$13.60. What is the lower price per notebook, in dollars?
answer: 1.7
check: min(8.75 / 5, 13.60 / 8)
tol: 0.005
hint: Find the unit price at each store.
hint: Store A is $8.75 \div 5$. Store B is $13.60 \div 8$. Pick the smaller one.
solution: Store A: $8.75 \div 5 = 1.75$. Store B: $13.60 \div 8 = 1.70$. The lower price is \$1.70 per notebook.

@quiz num
prompt: A car uses 7 gallons of gas to travel 224 miles. At the same rate, how many miles can it travel on 11 gallons?
answer: 352
check: 224 / 7 * 11
hint: First find miles per gallon.
hint: $224 \div 7$ is the unit rate. Multiply it by $11$.
solution: $224 \div 7 = 32$ miles per gallon, and $32 \times 11 = 352$ miles.
