@article
The **coordinate plane** is made from two number lines that cross at right angles. The horizontal one is the **x-axis**, the vertical one is the **y-axis**, and they meet at the **origin** $(0, 0)$.

### Ordered pairs

A point is named by an **ordered pair** $(x, y)$. The first number says how far to move right (positive) or left (negative) from the origin. The second says how far to move up (positive) or down (negative). Order matters: $(2, 5)$ and $(5, 2)$ are different points.

### The four quadrants

The axes divide the plane into four **quadrants**, numbered counterclockwise starting at the top right.

| Quadrant | Signs of $(x, y)$ |
|---|---|
| I | $(+, +)$ |
| II | $(-, +)$ |
| III | $(-, -)$ |
| IV | $(+, -)$ |

So $(-3, 4)$ is left $3$ and up $4$, which is in Quadrant II. Points on an axis are not in any quadrant.

### Reflections

To **reflect** across the x-axis, keep $x$ and change the sign of $y$: $(3, -2)$ goes to $(3, 2)$. To reflect across the y-axis, change the sign of $x$ and keep $y$: $(-4, 5)$ goes to $(4, 5)$. The reflected point is the same distance from the axis, on the other side.

### Distances on a grid

When two points have the same $x$-coordinate, they sit on a vertical line, and the distance is the difference of the $y$-coordinates, found with absolute value. When they have the same $y$-coordinate, use the $x$-coordinates.

From $(2, 5)$ to $(2, -3)$: $|5 - (-3)| = 8$. This is the same idea as distance on a number line: add the absolute values when the numbers are on opposite sides of zero.

### Shapes on the plane

Plotting the corners of a rectangle and finding side lengths with absolute value lets you compute its perimeter and area from coordinates alone.

@example
title: Plotting points in four quadrants and measuring
problem: Name the quadrant of $A(3, 2)$, $B(-3, 2)$, $C(-3, -4)$ and $D(3, -4)$, then find the distance from $B$ to $C$.
step: $A$ has signs $(+, +)$, so it is in Quadrant I. $B$ has $(-, +)$, Quadrant II. $C$ has $(-, -)$, Quadrant III. $D$ has $(+, -)$, Quadrant IV.
step: $B$ and $C$ share the $x$-coordinate $-3$, so the segment is vertical.
step: Use the $y$-coordinates $2$ and $-4$. They are on opposite sides of zero: $2 + 4 = 6$, or $|2 - (-4)| = 6$. The distance is $6$ units.

@mistakes
- Reversing the coordinates and plotting $(y, x)$ instead of $(x, y)$.
- Changing the wrong sign when reflecting: the x-axis reflection changes the sign of $y$.
- Subtracting coordinates without absolute value and getting a negative distance.

@practice num
prompt: What is the horizontal distance between $(-6, 1)$ and $(4, 1)$?
answer: 10
check: abs(4 - (-6))
hint: The points share a $y$-coordinate, so use the $x$-coordinates.
hint: $-6$ and $4$ are on opposite sides of zero.
solution: $|4 - (-6)| = 6 + 4 = 10$ units.

@practice self
prompt: Plot $A(-2, 3)$, $B(4, 3)$, $C(4, -1)$ and $D(-2, -1)$ on a coordinate grid and connect them in order. Name the shape, give each side length, and find the perimeter.
hint: Sides that share an $x$- or $y$-coordinate are vertical or horizontal; use absolute value for their lengths.
solution: The shape is a rectangle. Side $AB$ has the same $y$, so its length is $|4 - (-2)| = 6$. Side $BC$ has the same $x$, so its length is $|3 - (-1)| = 4$. The opposite sides are equal, so the perimeter is $2(6 + 4) = 20$ units.
rubric: All four points are plotted correctly, with the right sign for each coordinate.
rubric: The shape is named as a rectangle, with a reason such as equal opposite sides and right angles.
rubric: Side lengths $6$ and $4$ are found using absolute value or counting.
rubric: The perimeter is given as $20$ units.
