@article
Right now you are choosing to keep reading. You could have stopped. Or could you? If everything that happens is the result of earlier events, were you ever free to do anything other than what you did?

### Determinism

**Determinism** is the view that every event is the result of earlier events together with the laws of nature. In 1814 the scientist Pierre-Simon Laplace described an imaginary super-mind that knew the position of every particle and all the laws: for that mind, he said, nothing would be uncertain, and the future would be as clear as the past. This imagined mind is known as Laplace's demon. Physics has not settled whether determinism is true: physicists disagree about how to interpret quantum mechanics, so the question remains open.

### The argument for a problem

Many philosophers have argued that if determinism is true, then no one has free will:

| P1 | If determinism is true, then every choice you make is the result of events before you were born together with the laws of nature. |
| P2 | You have no control over events before you were born or over the laws of nature. |
| P3 | If your choices are the result of things you have no control over, then your choices are not up to you. |
| C | So if determinism is true, your choices are not up to you. |

### Three responses

**Libertarianism** (nothing to do with the political view) says that we have free will and that determinism is false, so that some choices are not fully fixed by earlier events. A challenge: if a choice is not fixed by anything, is it just luck, and does that make it more up to you?

**Compatibilism** says that free will and determinism can both be true. The compatibilist rejects premise P3. Being free, they say, is not about the causes going back to the Big Bang; it is about acting from your own desires and reasons without being forced or blocked. If you choose to read because you want to, and nobody is making you, you are free, even if your wants were caused. A challenge: if your wants were fixed long ago, is that really *your* freedom?

**Hard determinism**, and related views that deny free will, say that determinism is true, that free will needs more than compatibilists say, and so we do not have it. A challenge: it seems to leave no room for praise and blame, though its defenders say we can still have good reasons to treat people kindly and fairly.

### Where things stand

In the 2020 PhilPapers Survey, most professional philosophers accepted or leaned toward compatibilism (about 59%), roughly one in five libertarianism, and about one in ten no free will. That tells you where opinion is, not which view is true. Whichever you prefer, the question matters for how we think about praise, blame and responsibility. It is also a question about robots: if a robot's every action follows from its programming and the world around it, would it be free?

@example
title: Which view is it?
problem: Sam says: "Everything I do is caused by earlier events, but when I choose pizza because I like it and nobody forces me, I am acting freely." Which view is this, and which premise of the argument does it reject?
step: Sam accepts that everything is caused by earlier events, which is determinism.
step: Sam says that he is still free, because he acts on his own wants without being forced. Saying that free will and determinism fit together is compatibilism.
step: The argument's premise P3 says that if choices come from things you do not control, they are not up to you. Sam denies it.
answer: Compatibilism, which rejects premise P3.

@example
title: Does the libertarian accept determinism?
problem: A libertarian says: "My choice to raise my hand was not fixed by anything before it. It was up to me." Which premise of the argument does the libertarian reject, if any?
step: The libertarian says that choices are not fully fixed by earlier events, which is a denial of determinism.
step: The argument says "if determinism is true...". The libertarian accepts this conditional claim (that determinism would rule out free will) and denies its starting point.
answer: The libertarian accepts that determinism would remove free will, and so concludes that determinism is false, at least for free choices.

@mistakes
- Confusing determinism with fate. Determinism says that your choices are among the causes of what happens, and not that things happen whatever you choose.
- Thinking that compatibilists deny determinism. They accept that it may be true and say free will can survive it.
- Thinking that libertarianism (the free will view) has to do with politics. It is a view about free will.
- Treating the number of philosophers who hold a view as proof that the view is correct.

solution: I think a robot could think, but only if it did more than imitate answers. By "think" I mean to understand what the words one uses are about and to reason from reasons. My first reason is that nothing in the idea of thinking says that it must be done by a brain.
