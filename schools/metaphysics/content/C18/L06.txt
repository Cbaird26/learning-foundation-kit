@article
Most of the time you are busy: answering messages, planning the week, doing as people do. Occasionally something stops you, such as a loss, a diagnosis you read about, or an unexplained feeling that nothing in particular matters. Heidegger takes such moments as disclosing something about the structure of existence that ordinary busyness conceals. This lesson treats death with calm attention: it is a topic in the structure of a life, not a prediction about anyone.

### Care and temporality

Heidegger names the being of Dasein **care** (*Sorge*) and gives it a three-part structure: Dasein is always **ahead of itself** (projecting possibilities), **already in** a world it did not choose (its **facticity**), and **alongside** the entities it deals with. Each part corresponds to a temporal dimension: the future, having-been, and the present. **Temporality** (*Zeitlichkeit*) is the unity of these three, with the future given a certain priority: Dasein is first of all its possibilities, and it understands the past and the present in light of them. This **originary time** is contrasted with **ordinary** (Heidegger says "vulgar") **time**, the sequence of equal "nows" measured by clocks. Heidegger holds that ordinary time is derived from originary time. It is worth comparing this with Husserl's retention and protention (week 3) and, in week 10, with Bergson's duration.

### Anxiety, the they, and authenticity

In **fear**, we are afraid of a particular thing in the world. In **anxiety** (*Angst*) nothing in particular threatens: what discloses itself is the world as such and one's own being-in-it, so everyday meanings recede. Anxiety individualizes: it confronts Dasein with itself as the one who must be.

In everyday life Dasein is absorbed in **das Man**, "the they" or "one": the anonymous public that settles what one does, thinks and says. This gives security but flattens possibilities. Heidegger calls the mode of living that is dominated by it **inauthentic** and the mode in which Dasein owns its possibilities **authentic** (*eigentlich*, from *eigen*, "own"). He insists that these are not moral praise and blame, and it is debated whether he can keep the terms purely descriptive. **Resoluteness** (*Entschlossenheit*) is the authentic stance of owning one's situation and acting on a chosen possibility; Heidegger links it to **conscience**, which calls Dasein back to itself from the they.

### Being-toward-death

Heidegger separates **perishing** (the end of an organism), **demise** (the biological ending of a human life) and **dying** in his existential sense, the way Dasein relates to its own end throughout its life. Death is a **possibility** of Dasein, and Heidegger characterizes it by five features: it is **ownmost** (no one can take over my dying for me, though others can die for me in the sense of sacrifice), **non-relational** (it undoes my relations to others and individualizes me), **not to be outstripped** (no other possibility goes beyond it), **certain**, and, as such, **indefinite** as to when. He calls it the possibility of the impossibility of existing at all. **Being-toward-death** is relating to it as a possibility, which Heidegger calls **anticipation**. It is not brooding on death or wishing for it: it is the freedom to live one's finitude as one's own. The reasoning behind it can be set out as follows:

| P1 | Dasein can be understood as a whole, including its end. |
| P2 | Dasein cannot grasp its end as an event that it experiences, because when the end arrives Dasein no longer is. |
| P3 | If Dasein's end can be grasped at all, it can be grasped either as an experienced event or as a possibility that Dasein relates to while it exists. |
| C | So Dasein's end can be grasped only as a possibility that Dasein relates to while it exists: being-toward-death. |

The argument is valid. It is a case where the premises carry the weight: P1 sets the aim, P2 rules out one route, and P3 is an either-or. **Jean-Paul Sartre** objected, in *Being and Nothingness*, that death is not my possibility but the end of all my possibilities, an external fact that I cannot make my own or build my life around. A philosopher who accepts P2 and P3 and holds that death cannot be a possibility one relates to must therefore deny P1: the end cannot be integrated into an understanding of Dasein as a whole. Other critics hold that finitude does not alone give life meaning. A defender of Heidegger replies that the possibility in question is the possibility of no longer being able to exist, and that one can relate to this while alive, as the anxiety and anticipation described above indicate.

### Readiness-to-hand again

Readiness-to-hand and temporality connect. Equipment is encountered in a world that is ahead of itself in projects, and the anticipatory stance is the opposite of the mere busyness of the they, where tools become means to ends that no one has examined. A careful treatment of the readiness-to-hand requires three features: use, withdrawal from attention, and embedding in a totality of involvements. @example
title: Matching the features of death
problem: Match each feature to the right description: (a) ownmost, (b) non-relational, (c) not to be outstripped, (d) indefinite. Descriptions: (i) it is certain that it will come, but when is not known; (ii) it cannot be left behind by any other possibility; (iii) it is mine, and no one can take it over for me; (iv) when it arrives, ties to others are cut and Dasein stands alone.
step: (a) The ownmost possibility is the one that belongs to me and cannot be represented by another: (iii).
step: (b) Non-relational means that death individualizes: it cuts ties with others, which is (iv).
step: (c) Not to be outstripped means that no other possibility goes beyond it: (ii).
step: (d) Indefinite refers to the unknown time, even though death is certain: (i).
answer: (a)-(iii); (b)-(iv); (c)-(ii); (d)-(i).

@example
title: Locating Sartre's objection
problem: Which premise of the argument for being-toward-death is denied by someone who agrees that no one can experience their own death and that death can be grasped only experientially or as a lived possibility, but says death is only a fact that ends possibilities and cannot be a lived possibility?
step: The person accepts P2 (death is not an experienced event) and P3 (the only options are an experienced event or a lived possibility).
step: The person also denies that the second option is available: death is not a possibility one can relate to.
step: Then neither route grasps the end, so Dasein's end cannot be grasped. P1 says that Dasein can be understood as a whole including its end, so P1 must be false. That is the premise denied.
answer: P1: Dasein cannot be understood as a whole including its end.

@mistakes
- Treating "authentic" and "inauthentic" as moral praise and blame. Heidegger says they are ways of being, though readers disagree about whether the terms are really neutral.
- Reading being-toward-death as preoccupation with dying or as a wish to die. It is a way of relating to the possibility of one's own end as part of existing.
- Confusing anxiety with fear. Fear has a particular object in the world; anxiety does not, and it discloses the world as such.
- Treating Heidegger's temporality as the clock time of physics. Originary time is the lived unity of future, having-been and present.

solution: Sartre says that death is not one of my possibilities but the end of all possibilities, an external fact that I cannot assume or give meaning to.
