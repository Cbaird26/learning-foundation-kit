@lesson
title: Pushes and pulls
summary: Show how pushes and pulls change how things move, and test what floats.
objective: Say that a push moves something away and a pull moves it closer.
objective: Say that pushes and pulls can start, stop or change motion.
objective: Test which objects float or sink in water, with a grown-up.
reading: NASA Space Place: look up "What is gravity?" or ask your grown-up to read a picture book about how things move.

@article
A **push** moves something away from you. A **pull** moves something toward you. Pushes and pulls are kinds of **force**.

- You push a swing to make it go.
- You pull a wagon to bring it to you.
- You push a door open and pull a drawer open.

A force can **start** something moving, **stop** it, make it go **faster or slower**, or change its **direction**.

A **strong** push makes a ball roll far. A **gentle** push makes it roll a short way.

### Float or sink

Things float or sink in water. A **float** stays on top. A **sink** goes down. A cork floats. A stone sinks. Gravity pulls things down, and water pushes some things up so they float.

### Try it

With your grown-up, fill a bowl with water. Guess if each safe object will float or sink. Then test it. A grape, a coin and a wooden spoon are good choices.

@example
title: Roll a ball
problem: You give a ball a gentle push, then a strong push. What is different?
step: The gentle push makes the ball roll a short way.
step: The strong push makes it roll farther. A bigger push makes a bigger change in motion.

@mistakes
- Mixing up push and pull. A push goes away from you. A pull comes toward you.
- Thinking big things always sink. A big wooden log floats, and a small stone sinks.

@practice mc
prompt: You open a drawer by bringing it toward you. What did you do?
choice: Push
choice*: Pull
choice: Float
choice: Sink
hint: The drawer came toward you.
solution: Bringing something toward you is a pull.

@practice mc
prompt: A ball stops rolling when it hits a wall. Which sentence is true?
choice*: The wall pushes on the ball and stops it
choice: Nothing touches the ball
choice: The ball is alive and chooses to stop
choice: The ball floats
hint: Forces can stop things as well as start them.
solution: The wall pushes back on the ball and stops it.

@practice num
prompt: You push a toy car 3 times. Then you push it 2 more times. How many pushes did you make?
answer: 5
check: 3 + 2
hint: Start at 3 and count up 2 more.
solution: 3 + 2 = 5 pushes.

@practice num
prompt: You put 6 objects in water. 4 of them sink. How many float?
answer: 2
check: 6 - 4
hint: The ones that do not sink float. Take 4 away from 6.
solution: 6 - 4 = 2 objects float.

@practice self
prompt: With your grown-up, fill a bowl with water. Pick three safe objects. Guess float or sink, then test each. Tell if your guess was right.
solution: I guessed a grape would sink and it sank. I guessed a spoon would float and it floated, so I was right twice. I guessed a coin would float but it sank.
rubric: You guessed before you tested each object.
rubric: You tested all three in the water.
rubric: You said which guesses were right or wrong.
hint: Hold each object in your hand and think about whether it feels heavy for its size before you drop it in.
