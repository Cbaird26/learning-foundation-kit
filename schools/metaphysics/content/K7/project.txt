@project
title: My nature journal
overview: For two weeks you will watch one plant, the weather and one animal. Each day you make a drawing and write one sentence for each. A grown-up helps you look, write and stay safe.
goal: Observe a plant, the weather and an animal with your senses.
goal: Record what you see each day with a drawing and a sentence.
goal: Notice how things change over two weeks.
step: With your grown-up, pick one plant to watch. A bean in a cup, a houseplant or a tree outside all work.
step: Pick one animal to watch, such as a bird at a window, an ant, a pet or a squirrel. Watch from far away and never touch a wild animal.
step: Fold paper into a journal with one page for each day. Put the date at the top.
step: Each day, look at the weather out of the window. Draw it and write a weather word.
step: Each day, draw your plant. Write one sentence about it, such as "My bean has a new leaf."
step: Each day you can, draw your animal and write one sentence about what it did.
step: After one week, count how many days were sunny, cloudy or rainy.
step: On day 14, read your journal to your grown-up. Tell one thing that changed and one thing you learned.
deliverable: A journal with a drawing and a sentence each day for the plant, the weather and the animal.
deliverable: A weather count that tells how many days were sunny, cloudy and rainy.
deliverable: A spoken story about one thing that changed.
rubric: Your journal has dated pages for about two weeks.
rubric: Each page has a drawing and a sentence for the plant, the weather and the animal.
rubric: Your sentences use words from your senses, such as green, soft, loud or cold.
rubric: You told your grown-up one change you saw.
safety: Never touch or feed a wild animal, and wash your hands after touching plants or soil. Never taste a plant or look straight at the Sun. Stay with your grown-up when you are outside.
tutor: How can I tell if my plant is growing?
tutor: What do animals need that I can look for?
tutor: What are some good describing words for the weather?

@review
### Big ideas

- **Week 1, senses.** Sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch help us observe.
- **Week 2, living things.** Living things grow, need food and water, and have babies. Nonliving things do not.
- **Week 3, plants.** Plants need water, light, air and space.
- **Week 4, animals.** Animals need food, water, air and shelter. A habitat is where they live.
- **Week 5, weather.** Weather changes every day. The four seasons repeat in order.
- **Week 6, sky.** The Sun gives light. The Moon shines by sunlight, and its shape seems to change.
- **Week 7, materials.** Materials have properties such as hard, soft, rough, smooth and waterproof.
- **Week 8, forces.** A push moves away and a pull moves toward. Some things float and some sink.
- **Week 9, sound and light.** Sound comes from vibrations. Objects that block light make shadows.
- **Week 10, Earth.** Earth has land and water.
- **Week 11, body.** Each body part has a job, and they work as a team.
- **Week 12, habits.** Washing, eating well, sleeping and playing keep you healthy.

### Self-check

- I can name my five senses.
- I can tell living from nonliving.
- I can name what a plant needs.
- I can name the four seasons in order.

### Common errors

- Thinking things that move are always alive.
- Mixing up weather and seasons.
- Mixing up push and pull.

hint: Think about the four things a plant needs.

@exam num
prompt: A weather chart for one week shows 3 sunny days, 2 cloudy days and the rest rainy. A week has 7 days. How many days are rainy?
answer: 2
check: 7 - 3 - 2
hint: Add the sunny and cloudy days first.
solution: 3 + 2 = 5, and 7 - 5 = 2 rainy days.
