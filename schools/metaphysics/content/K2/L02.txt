@article
You can add and subtract big numbers by working one place at a time: ones, then tens, then hundreds.

### Adding

To add 138 + 247, add the ones, then the tens, then the hundreds.

- Ones: 8 + 7 = 15. That is 1 ten and 5 ones.
- Tens: 3 + 4 = 7, plus the 1 ten we made, is 8 tens.
- Hundreds: 1 + 2 = 3.

The answer is 385. When the ones make 10 or more, we **regroup**: 10 ones become 1 ten and move to the tens place.

### Another way: break apart

You can also break a number into parts. 138 + 247 is the same as 138 + 200 = 338, then 338 + 40 = 378, then 378 + 7 = 385. Same answer, a different path.

### Subtracting

To subtract 352 - 129, start with the ones. You cannot take 9 from 2, so trade 1 ten for 10 ones. Now you have 12 ones and 4 tens.

- Ones: 12 - 9 = 3.
- Tens: 4 - 2 = 2.
- Hundreds: 3 - 1 = 2.

The answer is 223.

### Check with the other operation

Addition and subtraction undo each other. If 352 - 129 = 223, then 223 + 129 must give 352. If it does not, look for a mistake. If 138 + 247 = 385, then 385 - 247 must give 138.

@example
title: Add with regrouping
problem: What is 138 + 247? Check your answer.
step: Add the ones: 8 + 7 = 15. Write 5 and carry 1 ten.
step: Add the tens: 3 + 4 + 1 = 8. Add the hundreds: 1 + 2 = 3. The sum is 385.
step: Check with subtraction: 385 - 247 = 138. That matches the number we started with.

@example
title: Subtract with trading
problem: What is 352 - 129? Check your answer.
step: In the ones place, 2 is too small to take away 9. Trade 1 ten for 10 ones. Now it is 12 - 9 = 3 ones, and 4 tens are left.
step: Tens: 4 - 2 = 2. Hundreds: 3 - 1 = 2. The answer is 223.
step: Check with addition: 223 + 129 = 352. That matches the number we started with.

@mistakes
- Forgetting to add the ten you carried into the tens place.
- Subtracting the smaller digit from the bigger one even when the bigger digit is on the bottom, such as writing 9 - 2 = 7 in the ones place.
- Not checking. A quick check with the other operation catches many mistakes.
