@article
Some stories need **two steps**. You cannot answer the question until you find a hidden number first.

### Find the hidden step

Read this story: Kim has 14 crayons. She gets 2 boxes with 4 crayons in each box. How many crayons does she have now?

The question asks for the total. But we do not yet know how many crayons are in the new boxes. That is the first step.

- Step 1: $2 \times 4 = 8$ new crayons.
- Step 2: $14 + 8 = 22$ crayons in all.

### A plan

1. Read the whole story. What is the question?
2. Ask: "What do I need to know first?"
3. Do the first step and write the answer.
4. Use that answer to do the second step.
5. Check: does the answer make sense?

### Draw it

A quick picture or a bar helps. Draw groups for multiplying. Draw a bar of the total and mark what is taken away.

### Area and perimeter of a room

You can draw a room on grid paper. Each square can stand for 1 foot. A room that is 6 squares wide and 4 squares tall has area $4 \times 6 = 24$ square feet and perimeter $6 + 4 + 6 + 4 = 20$ feet. Area tells how much carpet covers the floor. Perimeter tells how much trim runs along the wall.

@example
title: Multiply, then add
problem: Kim has 14 crayons. She gets 2 boxes with 4 crayons in each box. How many crayons does she have now?
step: Find the hidden number first: how many crayons are in the 2 new boxes? $2 \times 4 = 8$.
step: Add them to what she had: $14 + 8 = 22$.
step: Kim has 22 crayons.

@example
title: Multiply, then subtract
problem: A baker makes 3 trays with 6 rolls on each tray. She sells 9 rolls. How many rolls are left?
step: First find how many rolls she made: $3 \times 6 = 18$.
step: Take away the rolls she sold: $18 - 9 = 9$.
step: There are 9 rolls left.

@mistakes
- Doing only the first step and stopping.
- Using the wrong operation for a step. Ask what is happening: groups, putting together, or taking away.
- Using the numbers in the order they appear in the story instead of thinking about what each one means.

answer: 18
check: 8 + 2 * 5
hint: First find how many marbles are in the 2 bags.
hint: Then add them to the 8 marbles Ben already had.
solution: $2 \times 5 = 10$, and $8 + 10 = 18$. Ben has 18 marbles.

@quiz num
prompt: Mom buys 2 packs with 6 juice boxes in each pack and 3 more single juice boxes. She shares all the juice boxes equally among 3 children. How many does each child get?
answer: 5
check: (2 * 6 + 3) / 3
hint: First find how many juice boxes there are in all.
hint: Then share that number among 3 children.
solution: $2 \times 6 = 12$, and $12 + 3 = 15$ juice boxes. $15 \div 3 = 5$. Each child gets 5.
