@article
Native peoples have lived in North America for thousands of years. Each **nation** has its own name, language, stories, leaders and ways of life. Native nations are not a thing of the past. Many Native people live in the United States today, and many nations have their own governments.

### Land shapes life

People used what was near them. In different places they built different homes, ate different foods and made different tools.

### The Haudenosaunee (Eastern Woodlands)

The Haudenosaunee (say "hoe-den-oh-SHOW-nee") live in what is now New York State and nearby areas, where there are forests. Their name means "People of the Longhouse." Long ago, families lived in **longhouses** made from wood and bark. They grew corn, beans and squash, called the "Three Sisters," and hunted and fished. Five nations joined together in a group called the Haudenosaunee Confederacy, and a sixth nation joined later, so it has six nations today. Their Great Law of Peace guides the Confederacy. Some people call them the Iroquois, a name given by outsiders.

### The Lakota (Great Plains)

The Lakota are a Plains nation. Long ago they lived on the Great Plains, a wide grassland, and many Lakota people still live there today. They hunted bison, which gave them meat, hides and bones to use. They used **tipis**, tents made of hides on poles, that could be moved. Lakota people used horses for travel and hunting after horses came to the Plains from the Spanish.

### Other nations

The Pueblo peoples of the Southwest have built homes of adobe (clay) for a long time. Many Alaska Native peoples, like the Inupiat, have hunted whales and seals in the cold north. The Cherokee, from the Southeast, lived in towns, farmed and made their own writing system for their language.

### Telling the story fairly

Different Native nations have their own stories about their history. It is best to say the nation's name, rather than one word for all, and to listen to what Native people say about themselves.

@example
title: Compare two nations
problem: Why did the Haudenosaunee build longhouses and the Lakota use tipis?
step: The Haudenosaunee lived in forests, so they had wood and bark to build with.
step: The Lakota followed bison across the plains, so they used homes that could be moved.
step: Each nation used the land around it and how it lived.

@example
title: Count the Sisters
problem: The Three Sisters are corn, beans and squash. If you plant 2 rows of each, how many rows is that?
step: There are 3 sisters.
step: Plant 2 rows of each: 2 + 2 + 2.
step: That is 6 rows.

@mistakes
- Talking about all Native people as if they were one group. There are many nations with different ways of life.
- Thinking Native nations only lived long ago. Native people and nations are part of the United States today.
- Saying every nation lived in tipis. Different nations built different homes.
