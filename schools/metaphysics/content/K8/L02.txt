@lesson
title: Traits and inheritance
summary: Tell inherited traits from learned ones and compare the life cycles of a plant, an insect and a mammal.
objective: Say that a trait is a feature of a living thing.
objective: Tell inherited traits from learned traits and from changes caused by the environment.
objective: Explain why young are alike but not exactly the same as their parents.
reading: NASA Space Place: look up the topic "what is DNA" or "genes" in a kids' science resource for the idea that living things pass on traits; also look at an animal and its young.

@article
A **trait** is a feature of a living thing, like the color of a cat's fur, the shape of a leaf, or how tall a plant grows.

### Inherited traits
Young living things get traits from their parents. This is called **inheritance**. Kittens may have the same fur color as a parent. Sunflower seeds grow into sunflowers, never into roses. Eye color, hair color and the shape of your ears are inherited traits too.

### Alike but not the same
Young are **like** their parents, but not exactly the same. A cat can have a litter with gray kittens and orange kittens. This difference between living things of the same kind is called **variation**. No two are exactly alike.

### Learned traits
Some things are not inherited. You learn them. Riding a bike, reading, and playing a song are **learned**. A puppy can learn to sit. Babies are not born knowing how to do these things.

### Changes from where you live
The environment can change a living thing. A plant in a sunny window grows green and strong. The same kind of plant in a dark closet grows pale and weak. A scar from a scrape is also not passed to babies.

### Comparing life cycles
In week 1 you learned three life cycles. They are alike in some ways and different in others:

| | Plant | Butterfly | Dog (mammal) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starts as | seed | egg | born alive |
| Young stage | seedling | caterpillar | puppy |
| Feeds on | water and sunlight | leaves | mother's milk |

All three grow, become adults and make young like themselves.

@example
title: Inherited or learned?
problem: Is "having brown eyes" inherited or learned? Is "playing the drum" inherited or learned?
step: Ask: did you get it from your parents' bodies, or did you practice it?
step: Brown eyes come from parents, so it is inherited.
step: Drumming is practiced, so it is learned.

@example
title: Counting kittens
problem: A cat has 5 kittens. 2 are gray and 1 is black. The rest are orange. How many are orange?
step: Add the gray and black ones: $2 + 1 = 3$.
step: Take that from all the kittens: $5 - 3 = 2$.

@mistakes
- Thinking that learned skills, like reading, are passed down in the body.
- Thinking that young must look exactly like their parents.
- Forgetting that the environment, like light or food, can change how a living thing grows.
