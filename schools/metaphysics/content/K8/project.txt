@project
title: A fair-test science fair project
overview: Pick a question you can test, change only one thing, and measure what happens. Record your results in a table and a graph, then tell what you found.
goal: Ask a question that can be tested by changing one thing.
goal: Keep everything else the same so the test is fair.
goal: Record results in a table and a bar graph and write a conclusion.
step: Pick a question with your grown-up, such as "Does a higher ramp make a toy car roll farther?" or "Which paper towel soaks up the most water?" or "Does salt dissolve faster in lukewarm tap water or cold water?"
step: Write what you think will happen. This is your prediction.
step: Decide the one thing you will change (the variable). List the things you will keep the same (the car, the floor, the cups, the amount of water).
step: Make a table with a row for each try. Test each setting 3 times.
step: Do the test and measure with a ruler, a cup or a scale. Write each number down right away.
step: Find the average of the 3 tries for each setting. Draw a bar graph with the settings along the bottom and the averages going up.
step: Write your conclusion. Say whether your prediction was right, and use your numbers.
step: Show your project to your grown-up as a short talk, a poster or a drawing.
deliverable: A table of your results with 3 tries for each setting.
deliverable: A bar graph of the averages.
deliverable: A short conclusion that uses your numbers, told or written.
rubric: The question can be tested.
rubric: Only one thing was changed and the rest was kept the same.
rubric: Each setting was tried 3 times and the results are in a table.
rubric: The graph matches the table.
rubric: The conclusion says what happened using the numbers.
safety: Use only safe things from the kitchen or toy box, and always work with a grown-up. No fire, no hot water, and no wall outlets.
tutor: How can I make my test fair?
tutor: What could I measure with a ruler, a cup or a scale?
tutor: How do I know if my results are good enough to answer my question?

@review
**Week 1 and 2: Living things.** A life cycle goes: start, grow, adult, make young. A butterfly has 4 stages: egg, larva, pupa, adult. A frog goes from egg to tadpole to froglet to adult. Mammals are born alive and drink milk. Traits can be inherited or learned.

**Week 3 and 4: Ecosystems.** Producers make food. Consumers eat. Decomposers break down dead things. Arrows in a food chain point to the eater. Adaptations such as fur, webbed feet and camouflage help living things survive.

**Week 5 and 6: Matter.** Matter has mass and takes up space. Solid, liquid and gas are states. Mass stays the same when salt dissolves. Evaporation brings the salt back.

**Week 7 and 8: Energy.** A force is a push or a pull. Gravity pulls down and friction slows things. Speed is distance divided by time. A circuit needs a closed loop. Opposite magnet poles attract.

**Week 9 and 10: Earth.** Weathering breaks rocks. Erosion moves pieces. The water cycle is evaporation, condensation, precipitation and collection.

**Week 11 and 12: Space and body.** Earth spins for day and night and orbits the Sun for a year. The tilt makes seasons. Food goes mouth, esophagus, stomach, small intestine and large intestine.

**Self-check:** Can I name the stages of a life cycle? Can I draw a food web? Can I tell why a test is fair?

**Common errors:** mixing up weathering and erosion, drawing food web arrows backward, and changing two things in one test.

@exam mc
prompt: A child tests which paper towel soaks up the most water. Which plan is a fair test?
choice: Use a different amount of water for each towel
choice*: Use the same amount of water for each towel and change only the brand
choice: Use a different cup each time and change the water
choice: Test only one towel
hint: Change only one thing.
solution: A fair test changes only one thing (the brand). The amount of water stays the same.

@exam num
prompt: A toy car rolls 40 cm, 44 cm and 48 cm down a ramp in 3 tries. Next, a higher ramp makes it roll 70 cm, 74 cm and 78 cm. How many cm farther is the average roll on the higher ramp?
answer: 30
check: (70 + 74 + 78) / 3 - (40 + 44 + 48) / 3
unit: cm
tol: 0.01
hint: Find each average by adding the three tries and dividing by 3.
solution: Low ramp: $(40 + 44 + 48) \div 3 = 44$. High ramp: $(70 + 74 + 78) \div 3 = 74$. Difference: $74 - 44 = 30$ cm.

@exam mc
prompt: Grass is eaten by a grasshopper. A frog eats the grasshopper. A heron eats the frog. If all the frogs go away, what is most likely to happen?
choice*: There will be more grasshoppers and the heron will have less food
choice: There will be fewer grasshoppers
choice: Nothing will change
choice: The grass will disappear
hint: Think about who eats the frogs and what the frogs eat.
solution: With no frogs, fewer grasshoppers are eaten, so there will be more of them, and the heron loses a food source.
