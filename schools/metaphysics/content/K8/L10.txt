@article
**Weather** is what the air is like outside right now or today: sunny, rainy, windy or snowy, hot or cold. **Climate** is the usual weather in a place over many years. A desert has a dry climate. A rainforest has a wet climate.

### The water cycle
Water on Earth moves in a never-ending loop called the **water cycle**. The Sun's heat powers it.

1. **Evaporation**: the Sun warms water in oceans, lakes and puddles. Some becomes water vapor, a gas, and rises.
2. **Condensation**: high in the sky it is cold. Water vapor cools and turns into tiny drops that make clouds.
3. **Precipitation**: when drops get big and heavy, they fall as rain, snow, sleet or hail.
4. **Collection**: water runs into rivers, lakes and the ocean, or soaks into the ground. Then it can evaporate again.

The water is never used up. It is the same water moving around.

### Measuring weather
- A **thermometer** measures temperature in degrees Celsius (°C).
- A **rain gauge** is a tall, open cup with marks. It measures rainfall in centimeters.
- A **wind vane** shows which way the wind blows.

### Keeping a record
Write measurements in a table each day. A table helps you see patterns, such as which day was the warmest.

### Air masses and storms
Warm air rises and cool air sinks, and moving air is wind. Big storms like thunderstorms can bring heavy rain, and weather forecasters watch the sky and use tools to predict them.

### Draw it
To explain the water cycle, draw the Sun, an ocean, a cloud and a mountain. Add arrows and label evaporation, condensation, precipitation and collection.

@example
title: Put the cycle in order
problem: Which comes first: condensation or evaporation?
step: Water must become a gas before it can cool and make clouds.
step: Evaporation comes first, then condensation.

@example
title: Total rainfall
problem: It rained 3 cm on Monday, 2 cm on Tuesday and 4 cm on Wednesday. How much rain fell in all?
step: Write the numbers: $3$, $2$ and $4$.
step: Add them: $3 + 2 + 4 = 9$ cm.

@mistakes
- Mixing up weather (today) with climate (the usual weather for years).
- Thinking clouds are made of smoke. Clouds are made of tiny drops of water.
- Thinking that rain water is used up. It stays in the water cycle.
