@article
A **polygon** is a closed figure made of straight segments that meet only at their endpoints. A polygon with $n$ sides is an **$n$-gon**. It is **regular** if all sides are equal and all angles are equal.

### Angle sums

Pick one vertex of an $n$-gon and draw all diagonals from it. This splits the polygon into $n - 2$ triangles, each with angle sum $180^\circ$. So

$$\text{sum of interior angles} = (n - 2)\cdot 180^\circ.$$

A quadrilateral gives $360^\circ$, a pentagon $540^\circ$, a hexagon $720^\circ$. In a regular $n$-gon each interior angle is $\dfrac{(n-2)\cdot 180^\circ}{n}$.

At each vertex, the **exterior angle** (formed by extending one side) is supplementary to the interior angle. Walking around any convex polygon you turn through a full circle, so the **exterior angles sum to $360^\circ$**, whatever $n$ is. A regular $n$-gon therefore has exterior angles of $\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$ each.

### The quadrilateral family

A **parallelogram** has two pairs of parallel sides. Picture it as a slanted rectangle with vertices $A, B, C, D$ in order. Its properties:

- opposite sides are equal,
- opposite angles are equal,
- consecutive angles are supplementary,
- the diagonals **bisect each other**.

Special parallelograms add more:

- **Rectangle:** four right angles; diagonals are equal.
- **Rhombus:** four equal sides; diagonals are **perpendicular** and bisect the angles.
- **Square:** both: four equal sides and four right angles. It is a rectangle and a rhombus at once.

A **trapezoid** has exactly one pair of parallel sides (the bases); some books say "at least one." Its **midsegment**, joining the midpoints of the two legs, is parallel to the bases and has length equal to half the sum of the bases. A **kite** has two pairs of adjacent equal sides; its diagonals are perpendicular.

### Proving a quadrilateral is a parallelogram

It is enough to show **any one** of the following:

1. both pairs of opposite sides are parallel (the definition),
2. both pairs of opposite sides are equal,
3. one pair of opposite sides is both parallel and equal,
4. both pairs of opposite angles are equal,
5. the diagonals bisect each other.

Be careful: one pair of equal sides alone is not enough, and neither is one pair of parallel sides (that could be a trapezoid).

### Classifying

Think of the family as a hierarchy: every square is a rectangle and a rhombus, every rectangle and rhombus is a parallelogram, and every parallelogram is a quadrilateral. A statement about parallelograms holds for all of the special cases, but not the other way around.

@example
title: Side length of a rhombus from its diagonals
problem: A rhombus has diagonals of length $10$ and $24$. Find its side length and its perimeter.
step: The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other and are perpendicular, so they cut the rhombus into four congruent right triangles.
step: Each right triangle has legs equal to half of each diagonal: $5$ and $12$.
step: The side of the rhombus is the hypotenuse: $\sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{169} = 13$.
step: All four sides are equal, so the perimeter is $4 \times 13 = 52$.

@mistakes
- Using $n \cdot 180^\circ$ or $(n-1)\cdot 180^\circ$ instead of $(n-2)\cdot 180^\circ$ for the interior angle sum.
- Believing that one pair of parallel sides (or one pair of equal sides) makes a quadrilateral a parallelogram.
- Thinking a rectangle's diagonals are perpendicular; that is true for a rhombus, not for a general rectangle.
- Reversing the hierarchy: not every rectangle is a square.

@practice num
prompt: In parallelogram $ABCD$, $m\angle A = (3x + 10)^\circ$ and $m\angle C = (5x - 30)^\circ$. Find $m\angle B$ in degrees.
answer: 110
check: 180 - (3 * ((10 + 30) / 2) + 10)
hint: Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal.
hint: Solve $3x + 10 = 5x - 30$, then use the fact that consecutive angles are supplementary.
solution: $40 = 2x$, so $x = 20$ and $m\angle A = 70^\circ$. Angle $B$ is consecutive to $A$, so $m\angle B = 180 - 70 = 110^\circ$.

@quiz num
prompt: Four interior angles of a pentagon measure $100^\circ$, $110^\circ$, $95^\circ$ and $120^\circ$. Find the fifth angle in degrees.
answer: 115
check: (5 - 2) * 180 - (100 + 110 + 95 + 120)
hint: First find the sum of the interior angles of a pentagon.
solution: The sum is $3 \cdot 180 = 540^\circ$. The four given angles total $425^\circ$, so the fifth is $540 - 425 = 115^\circ$.
