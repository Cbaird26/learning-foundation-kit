## Week-by-week review

1. **The new science.** The mechanical philosophy replaced substantial forms and final causes with matter in motion, and it created three problems: the foundation of knowledge, the place of mind, and God and necessity. The primary/secondary distinction was made standard by Locke, though the idea is earlier.
2. **Method and doubt.** Descartes seeks an indubitable foundation. Three stages: sensory error, dreaming (which spares arithmetic and geometry) and the deceiver (which does not).
3. **Cogito and clear and distinct ideas.** I exist while I think; Descartes calls the cogito an intuition, not a syllogism. Clear means present to the attentive mind, and distinct means containing only what is clear. The truth rule is not yet secure.
4. **God and the circle.** The trademark argument uses formal and objective reality and causal adequacy. The ontological argument says existence belongs to the nature of a supremely perfect being. Arnauld's circle objection and the reply from present perception and memory.
5. **Real distinction.** Mind (thought) and body (extension) are really distinct if each can exist apart. Arnauld's objection: incomplete understanding.
6. **Elisabeth.** How can an unextended soul move a body? Replies: primitive notion of union, deny the contact premise, or abandon interaction or dualism.
7. **Malebranche.** God is the only true cause because only God's will has a perceivable necessary connection with its effects.
8. **Spinoza on substance.** From definitions to one substance, God or Nature; everything else is a mode.
9. **Spinoza on mind, body and freedom.** One thing under two attributes; no interaction; no absolute free will; freedom as self-determination.
10. **Leibniz on monads.** Simple, windowless substances mirroring the universe; pre-established harmony.
11. **PSR and the best world.** Truths of reasoning and of fact; arguments from contingency and for the best world; modal collapse.
12. **Identity of indiscernibles and space.** PII is the converse of Leibniz's Law; the shift argument; Clarke, the bucket and Black's spheres.

### Distinctions to master

- Validity versus soundness; exegesis versus evaluation.
- Formal versus objective reality; clear versus distinct; true cause versus occasional cause.
- Substance, attribute and mode (Spinoza); truths of reasoning and truths of fact (Leibniz); moral versus metaphysical necessity.
- Leibniz's Law versus the identity of indiscernibles; interaction versus occasionalism versus harmony.

### Common errors

Confusing the method of doubt with skepticism; saying that the cogito proves immateriality; confusing occasionalism with harmony; saying that Spinoza makes the mind move the body; treating Leibniz as a necessitarian; mixing up PII and Leibniz's Law.

@exam self
prompt: Compare Descartes and Spinoza on the relation of mind and body. In about 300 words, explain (a) how Elisabeth's objection arises for Descartes, (b) how Spinoza's metaphysics avoids it, and (c) one cost of Spinoza's solution.
hint: Use the real distinction, the problem of interaction, Spinoza's claim about attributes of one substance, and the denial of mind-body causation.
solution: (a) Descartes argues that mind and body are really distinct substances, one thinking and unextended and the other extended and non-thinking, yet he holds that they interact. Elisabeth asks how an unextended thing can move a body when moving requires contact, and Descartes's reply that the union is a primitive notion does not provide an account. (b) Spinoza holds that there is only one substance, of which thought and extension are attributes, so that the mind is the idea of the body and is the same thing under another attribute. Since causation holds only within an attribute, there is no mind-body interaction to explain, and the correspondence of their orders is explained by the identity of the thing. (c) One cost is that the view makes the mental either identical with the physical or explanatorily isolated from it, and it brings with it Spinoza's necessitarianism and his denial of absolute free will. A critic may add that the view attributes mentality to all individual things in some degree.
rubric: States the real distinction and Elisabeth's objection accurately.
rubric: Explains how monism with two attributes and the denial of cross-attribute causation avoid the problem.
rubric: States a genuine cost of Spinoza's solution.
