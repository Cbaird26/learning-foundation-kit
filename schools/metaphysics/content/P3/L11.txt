@article
General relativity describes gravity as the curvature of spacetime and is confirmed in many regimes, including the direct detection of gravitational waves in 2015. Quantum field theory describes the other interactions and is confirmed to extraordinary precision. There is **no accepted theory of quantum gravity**: no theory that combines the two in a way that is both mathematically complete and empirically confirmed, and no experiment has yet probed the regime in which the combination is needed. Whether spacetime is fundamental or **emergent** from something non-spatiotemporal is open. Your task as a philosopher is to say precisely what the question means and what would count as an answer, without claiming more than the physics supports.

### Why the problem arises

Three considerations are standard. Treating gravity as a quantum field on a fixed background gives a theory that is **perturbatively non-renormalizable**: it needs infinitely many parameters at high energies, so it works as an effective theory but not as a fundamental one. The **thermodynamics of black holes** assigns an entropy to a horizon, the **Bekenstein-Hawking entropy**
$$S = \frac{k_B\,c^3 A}{4\hbar G} = \frac{k_B A}{4\,\ell_P^2},\qquad \ell_P = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^3}},$$
with $A$ the horizon area and $\ell_P \approx 1.616 \times 10^{-35}$ m the **Planck length** (with $\hbar = 1.054571817 \times 10^{-34}$ J s, $G = 6.67430 \times 10^{-11}$ m$^3$ kg$^{-1}$ s$^{-2}$ and $c = 299{,}792{,}458$ m/s). The **Planck time** is $t_P = \ell_P/c \approx 5.39 \times 10^{-44}$ s. And in the **canonical** approach, a theory with diffeomorphism invariance has a Hamiltonian that is a constraint, and the **Wheeler-DeWitt equation** $\hat H \Psi = 0$ has no time variable: this is the **problem of time**.

### Research programs

None of the following is confirmed.

- **String theory** began as perturbative strings moving in a background spacetime. Its most discussed non-perturbative result is the **AdS/CFT correspondence** (Maldacena, 1997): a conjectured duality between a quantum theory of gravity in anti-de Sitter space of $d+1$ dimensions and a conformal field theory without gravity on its $d$-dimensional boundary. Ryu and Takayanagi (2006) related the entanglement entropy of a boundary region to the area of a minimal surface in the bulk, which suggests a way in which bulk geometry is encoded in boundary entanglement. The correspondence concerns a spacetime with a negative cosmological constant, whereas observations favor a positive one.
- **Loop quantum gravity** quantizes general relativity in a background-independent way. Its states are spin networks, and the operators for area and volume have discrete spectra.
- **Causal set theory** (Sorkin and collaborators) takes spacetime to be fundamentally a discrete partially ordered set, summed up in the slogan "order plus number equals geometry".

### Three senses of "spacetime emerges"

The programs differ in what is said to emerge from what. In AdS/CFT the bulk geometry is encoded in a boundary theory; in loop quantum gravity, classical space is to emerge from spin network states in a semiclassical limit; in causal set theory, a continuum spacetime approximates a discrete order. Philosophers distinguish emergence as **derivation in a limit**, as **duality** (two descriptions of one thing, which raises the question of which is the more fundamental) and as **functional realization**: Lam and Wüthrich argue that something counts as spacetime if it plays the functional roles of spacetime, so spacetime need not be fundamental to be real.

### Empirical coherence

Huggett and Wüthrich ("Emergent spacetime and empirical (in)coherence", 2013) pose a problem for theories whose fundamental ontology is not spatiotemporal. Our evidence is gathered by observers at places and times.

| P1 | If a quantum gravity theory with a non-spatiotemporal fundamental ontology is empirically confirmable, then it accounts for how spatiotemporal observations arise from that ontology. |
| P2 | If such a theory accounts for how spatiotemporal observations arise from its fundamental ontology, then it supplies a derivation or explanation of approximate spacetime structure from that ontology. |
| C | So if such a theory is empirically confirmable, it supplies a derivation or explanation of approximate spacetime structure from its fundamental ontology. |

It is valid (hypothetical syllogism). The authors do not conclude that emergence is impossible; they say that the burden is to show how, in a given theory, spacetime emerges. A reply against **P1**: the evidential connection can run through a limiting theory, general relativity and quantum field theory, which the new theory is shown to recover at low energy, so the theory need not itself describe observers in its fundamental terms. The cost, say the critics, is that confirmation then attaches to the limit and not to what is distinctive of the new theory.

### Where the work is

@example
title: Computing the Planck length
problem: Using $\hbar = 1.054571817 \times 10^{-34}$ J s, $G = 6.67430 \times 10^{-11}$ m$^3$ kg$^{-1}$ s$^{-2}$ and $c = 299{,}792{,}458$ m/s, compute $\ell_P = \sqrt{\hbar G/c^3}$ and state its units.
step: The numerator is $\hbar G \approx 1.0546 \times 10^{-34} \times 6.6743 \times 10^{-11} \approx 7.039 \times 10^{-45}$ in SI units (J s m$^3$ kg$^{-1}$ s$^{-2}$).
step: The denominator is $c^3 \approx 2.6944 \times 10^{25}$ m$^3$ s$^{-3}$, so $\hbar G/c^3 \approx 2.612 \times 10^{-70}$ m$^2$.
step: Taking the square root gives $\ell_P \approx 1.616 \times 10^{-35}$ m, in meters because the units combine to m$^2$ under the root.
answer: $\ell_P \approx 1.62 \times 10^{-35}$ m.

@example
title: Locating the reply to empirical coherence
problem: A physicist says: "Our confidence in a quantum gravity theory will come from its recovering general relativity and the standard model at accessible energies, not from describing observers in its own fundamental terms." Which premise of the standard-form argument does this reply deny, and what is the objection to it?
step: Find what is granted. The physicist grants that observations are spatiotemporal and does not deny the validity of the argument.
step: Find what is denied. The reply says that confirmation can be mediated by a limit theory, so the theory need not itself account for the observations. That denies P1, the claim that confirmability requires the theory to account for how spatiotemporal observations arise.
step: The objection: if confirmation attaches only through the limit, the evidence supports the limit theory and may not discriminate the new theory from rivals that recover the same limit. So the reply leaves open whether the distinctive claims about non-spatiotemporal ontology are confirmed at all.
answer: It denies P1. The objection is that evidence then confirms the limit and not what is distinctive of the new theory.

@mistakes
- Saying that string theory, loop quantum gravity or any other approach has been confirmed. None has experimental confirmation, and none is accepted as the theory of quantum gravity.
- Saying that AdS/CFT shows that our spacetime is emergent. It concerns anti-de Sitter space, which is not the geometry that observations favor for our universe.
- Treating "spacetime emerges" as a single claim. It differs between programs, and the sense (limit, duality, functional) matters.
- Reading the empirical coherence argument as showing that emergent spacetime is incoherent. Its conclusion is a burden: the theory must show how spacetime arises.

@practice self
prompt: In 200 words, explain why the Wheeler-DeWitt equation is said to leave no room for time, and give one response to the problem of time.
hint: Start with diffeomorphism invariance and the Hamiltonian constraint, then say what a relational response says.
solution: In general relativity, the theory is invariant under diffeomorphisms, including those that reparametrize time. In the canonical formulation this shows up as constraints: the Hamiltonian is not a generator of evolution in an external time but a constraint that must vanish on physical states. Quantizing gives the Wheeler-DeWitt equation $\hat H \Psi = 0$, in which no time parameter appears, so the wave function of the universe seems to be static: the problem of time. One response is relational: time is not an external parameter but is defined by correlations between physical subsystems, so one asks for the state of the universe conditional on a clock variable reading a given value, as in the conditional-probability proposal of Page and Wootters. Evolution then appears as a relation within a static state, not as change of the whole. The cost is that clocks are approximate and no single choice of clock works globally in general, and critics say it leaves open how the apparent flow of time is accounted for. Another response is to say that time emerges only at a semiclassical level.

@quiz self
prompt: Compare AdS/CFT and causal set theory on the question what, in each, spacetime is said to emerge from, and what kind of emergence (limit, duality or functional) is at issue. Write 150 to 200 words.
hint: State what the fundamental description is in each program, and then how spacetime geometry is related to it.
solution: In AdS/CFT, the fundamental description, on the duality reading, is a conformal field theory on a boundary, without gravity; the bulk geometry is encoded in the boundary state, for example through entanglement entropy relating to minimal surface area. The emergence is by duality: two descriptions of the same physics, one of which has a geometry in a higher-dimensional space and one of which does not. Whether the boundary theory is more fundamental than the bulk is itself a question, since a duality gives equivalence and not an obvious ordering. In causal set theory, the fundamental description is a discrete partially ordered set, whose order encodes causal relations and whose number of elements encodes volume. Spacetime emerges as a continuum approximation: a causal set approximates a Lorentzian manifold if it can be embedded in it in a suitable way. The emergence is by limit or approximation, with a continuum description recovered at large scales. Both involve non-spatiotemporal or pre-geometric fundamental structure, but they differ in the kind of relation between the structure and the geometry, and the empirical coherence problem takes a different form in each.
