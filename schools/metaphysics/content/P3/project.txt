@review
### Key ideas, week by week

1. **Naturalized metaphysics.** Quine's criterion; Ladyman and Ross's primacy of physics and naturalistic closure; Maudlin's interpretive view; van Fraassen's empirical stance. The argument for constraint is valid (modus tollens); replies deny P1 or P2; underdetermination is the main pressure.
2. **Spacetime.** Substantivalism and relationalism; the shift argument (denied at P1 by anti-haecceitists, at P3 by those who reject the principle); Newton's globes; Galilean spacetime has absolute acceleration and no absolute velocity; the Minkowski interval $I^2 = c^2\Delta t^2 - \Delta x^2$ with signature $(+,-,-,-)$.
3. **The hole argument.** Diffeomorphism invariance, hole diffeomorphisms, determinism. Replies: relationalism, sophisticated substantivalism, metric essentialism, mathematical practice, accepting indeterminism.
4. **Laws, symmetries and structure.** Best system versus non-Humean laws; the explanatory argument; Noether's theorem; the Poincaré group has 10 generators; epistemic versus ontic structural realism and the relations-without-relata objection.
5. **Chance.** The Principal Principle and admissibility; undermining; the incompatibility argument and the Mentaculus reply; counting microstates.
6. **The arrow of time.** Reversibility, retrodiction and the past hypothesis; $S = k_B \ln W$; Price's double-standard objection; Earman's objection from general relativity.
7. **Wave function realism and primitive ontology.** Maudlin's inconsistent triad; the recovery argument; Bohmian mechanics, GRW flashes and matter density; the nomological view.
8. **Many worlds.** Decoherence and emergent branches; incoherence and quantitative problems; self-locating and decision-theoretic replies; Born weights versus branch counting.
9. **Nonlocality.** CHSH bound 2 versus $2\sqrt{2}$; Bell's argument and three assumptions; parameter and outcome independence; the foliation argument.
10. **Emergence.** Weak and strong emergence; Kim's exclusion argument; the thermodynamic limit, universality and the Butterfield and Batterman dispute.
11. **Quantum gravity.** No accepted theory; research programs; Planck units; empirical coherence.
12. **Writing.** Scoped theses, physical accuracy, scope arguments, talks and referee reports.

### Common errors

Treating a reconstruction as an author's own words; confusing no-signaling with locality; saying that experiments refute quantum mechanics; saying that any quantum gravity program is confirmed; treating a conditional conclusion (a burden) as a refutation; omitting the signature or the units; and generalizing from a model to a theory without saying so.

@exam num
prompt: A spin state has amplitudes $\sqrt{0.1}$ for "up" and $\sqrt{0.9}$ for "down", and the same preparation is measured three times in succession. What is the total Born weight, as a percent to one decimal place, of the branches in which exactly one outcome is "up"?
answer: 24.3
unit: %
tol: 0.1
check: comb(3, 1) * 0.1 * 0.9 ** 2 * 100
hint: There are three branches with exactly one "up", each with the same weight.
hint: Each such branch has weight $0.1 \times 0.9 \times 0.9$.
solution: Each branch with one "up" has weight $0.1 \times 0.9^2 = 0.081$, and there are 3 of them, so the total is $0.243$, or 24.3%. Branch counting would give 3 of 8 branches, or 37.5%, which is why the quantitative problem arises.

@exam ms
prompt: Which of the following pairings of a position with the premise or assumption it denies are correct? Select all that apply.
choice*: A sophisticated substantivalist denies the premise of the hole argument that substantivalism implies that the two diffeomorphic models represent distinct possibilities.
choice*: A defender of the Mentaculus denies the premise of the incompatibility argument that if the laws and the complete state entail an event or its negation, the chance of the event is 1 or 0.
choice*: A defender of the self-locating reply denies the premise of the incoherence argument that if every outcome occurs in some branch there is nothing to be uncertain about.
choice: Everettian quantum mechanics denies factorizability in Bell's argument.
choice: A superdeterminist denies the premise that the CHSH quantity for local hidden-variable theories is at most 2.
hint: Match each position with the premise or assumption it actually targets.
solution: The first three pairings are correct. Everettians reject the assumption that each run has a single outcome, not factorizability, and superdeterminism denies measurement independence, not the mathematical bound that follows from the assumptions.

@exam self
prompt: The hole argument, the incoherence argument for many worlds and the exclusion argument for emergence each have the form of a conditional chain that ends in a conclusion about what a formalism or theory requires. Choose two of these three arguments, state each in standard form (valid as written), and compare them: in each case, which premise is the most contested, why, and what does the choice show about the limits of reading metaphysics directly off physics (lesson 1)? Write about 400 words.
hint: Pick two arguments, state each with numbered premises, then compare the contested premises and tie them to the naturalism debate.
solution: I take the hole argument and the incoherence argument. Hole argument. P1: If manifold substantivalism is true, then the models $(M,g,T)$ and $(M,d^*g,d^*T)$ represent distinct possibilities. P2: Both models solve Einstein's equations, are identical on a spacelike surface before the hole, and differ at some points in it. P3: If those models are distinct possibilities and agree on the surface but differ in the hole, general relativity is indeterministic. P4: General relativity is not indeterministic in that way. C: So manifold substantivalism is false. Incoherence argument. P1: If Everettian quantum mechanics is true, every outcome occurs in some branch. P2: If every outcome occurs in some branch, there is nothing to be uncertain about. P3: If there is nothing to be uncertain about, probability cannot play its ordinary role as a measure of uncertainty. C: So if Everettian quantum mechanics is true, probability cannot play its ordinary role. The most contested premise of the first is P1, since it depends on whether points have an identity that outruns their role in the structure (haecceitism); the formalism is silent, because the mathematics of the models is the same on either view. The most contested premise of the second is P2, because whether there is something to be uncertain about depends on whether uncertainty can be self-locating, again not settled by the equations. In both cases the physics supplies the premises that are not in dispute (diffeomorphism invariance, the unitary evolution and the branch structure) and the dispute is about a premise concerning identity or the role of a concept, which is what a metaphysician contributes. This supports the interpretive naturalist against a crude reading of the constraint view: physics constrains the options, but the choice among them uses considerations (unity, the role of a concept, cost) that physics does not itself supply. It does not support the view that the physics is irrelevant, since each premise is defended or attacked by reference to what the formalism and practice make available.
